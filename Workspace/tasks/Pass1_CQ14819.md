# Pass-1 Transformation Report: CQ14819

## Final Question
A wealthy Christian couple, who were both major donors to a domestic mission organization, were killed in a small plane crash while returning from a ministry project. A dispute subsequently arose in a federal diversity action over a $5 million bequest to the mission organization, which was contingent upon the husband surviving the wife. The presiding judge must determine whether to apply a specific state-law presumption that, in the event of a simultaneous death or a common accident where sequence cannot be determined, the husband is deemed to have survived the wife. 

Under the Federal Rules of Evidence, the federal court's application of this presumption is determined according to:

## Distilled Core Question
In a federal diversity action, does state or federal law govern the effect of a presumption when the underlying claim is based on state law?

## Final Choices (Post-Shuffle)
(A) Federal statutory law as provided in the Federal Rules of Evidence general presumption rule.
(B) Federal common law principles established under the *Erie* doctrine to ensure national uniformity.
(C) The law of the state whose substantive law is applied to the claim or defense.
(D) Traditional common law principles regarding survivorship in common accidents.

## Correct Answer
**Correct Answer: C** (Original Key: C)

## Right-Answer Explanation
**C is correct.** Under Federal Rule of Evidence 302, in a civil case, state law governs the effect of a presumption regarding a claim or defense for which state law supplies the rule of decision. In a diversity action where the underlying claim (the bequest/contract) is governed by state substantive law, the federal court must apply the state law's presumptions. This codifies the *Erie* doctrine's requirement that federal courts sitting in diversity apply state substantive law to prevent forum shopping and inequitable administration of the laws.

## Wrong-Answer Explanations
- **(A) is incorrect.** While the Federal Rules of Evidence generally apply in federal court, FRE 301 (the general rule for presumptions) explicitly yields to FRE 302 when state law provides the rule of decision for the underlying claim.
- **(B) is incorrect.** This is a "bait" answer. While the *Erie* doctrine is the underlying constitutional principle, it mandates the application of *state law* for substantive issues like presumptions, rather than the creation of a "federal common law" for such matters.
- **(D) is incorrect.** Traditional common law might have provided the basis for the state's rule, but the federal court's authority to apply it comes from the state law itself (via FRE 302), not from a general reliance on ancient common law principles.

## Black-Letter Verification
- **Authority:** Federal Rule of Evidence (FRE) 302.
- **Rule:** "In a civil case, state law governs the effect of a presumption regarding a claim or defense for which state law supplies the rule of decision."
- **Verification:** The item involves a federal diversity action (where state law typically provides the rule of decision) regarding a bequest (a matter of state substantive law). Therefore, under FRE 302, the state's presumption law must be applied.

**Rigor: Core.** This is a foundational *Erie* / FRE interaction question. The difficulty lies in resisting the "federal law applies in federal court" heuristic and correctly identifying the specific exception in FRE 302.
