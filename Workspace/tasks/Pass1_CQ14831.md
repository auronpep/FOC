# Pass-1 Transformation Report: CQ14831

## Final Question
A church janitor is charged with arson for allegedly setting fire to a mission fellowship hall after attempting to steal church offerings. The prosecution calls as a witness the four-year-old daughter of the janitor, who was present at the mission that night and whose hand was burned by the fire. The prosecutor aims to have the child testify that she saw her father start the blaze in the fellowship hall. The prosecutor has presented evidence that the child has personal knowledge of the incident, understands the difference between telling the truth and lying, and can understand the duty to tell the truth.

The child's testimony should be:

## Distilled Core Question
What are the fundamental requirements for a child to be deemed competent to testify under the Federal Rules of Evidence?

## Final Choices (Post-Shuffle)
(A) Admitted, because the prosecution has sufficiently established that the child has personal knowledge and understands her duty to testify truthfully.
(B) Admitted, because the child’s cognitive ability and age are sufficient to establish her competency to testify.
(C) Excluded, because the testimony is more unfairly prejudicial to the defendant (her father) than probative.
(D) Excluded, because it is insufficiently probative due to the child's tender age.

## Correct Answer
**Correct Answer: A** (Original Key: B)

## Letter Map
- Original A -> Variant B
- Original B -> Variant A
- Original C -> Variant D
- Original D -> Variant C

## Right-Answer Explanation
**A is correct.** Under FRE 601, every person is competent to be a witness, provided they have personal knowledge of the matter (FRE 602) and understand the duty to testify truthfully (FRE 603). There is no "minimum age" requirement for competency. A child is competent if they have sufficient recollection and the ability to distinguish truth from falsehood and understand the duty to tell the truth under oath or affirmation. The prosecution has established these foundational requirements here.

## Wrong-Answer Explanations
- **(B) is incorrect.** This is a "bait" answer. While the child's cognitive ability might be relevant, age and general cognitive ability are not the *legal tests* for competency. The legal tests are personal knowledge and the understanding of the obligation to testify truthfully.
- **(C) is incorrect.** While testimony from a child against a parent is emotionally charged, it is not "unfairly prejudicial" under FRE 403. Relevant, probative evidence—even if damaging—is admissible.
- **(D) is incorrect.** Age is not a barrier to probative value. Testimony that a child witnessed the crime is highly probative, regardless of the child's age.

## Black-Letter Verification
- **Authority:** Federal Rule of Evidence (FRE) 601, 602, 603.
- **Rule:** Every person is competent to be a witness unless otherwise provided; competence requires personal knowledge and an understanding of the duty to testify truthfully.
- **Verification:** The child has personal knowledge of the events and an understanding of the truth-telling obligation; therefore, she is competent under the FRE.

## Rigor/Difficulty Note
**Rigor: Core.** This tests the threshold for witness competency. The difficulty lies in overcoming the common (but legally incorrect) notion that there is a strict age limit for child testimony in federal court.
