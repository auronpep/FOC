# Pass-1 Transformation Report: CQ22161

## Final Question
A church van driver was charged with reckless driving and speeding through a residential neighborhood while transporting students back from a youth retreat. At trial, the prosecution called a witness who serves on the church's volunteer safety and security team. The witness testified that she was a retired office manager, that she lived on the street where the incident occurred, and that for the past twenty years she had driven that same route daily and had extensive experience observing the flow and speed of neighborhood traffic. She stated that she was sitting on her porch when she saw the mission van drive by at what appeared to be an excessive rate of speed.

When asked by the prosecutor whether she formed an opinion of the van's speed at that time, she replied that she had. When the prosecutor asked her to state that opinion, the driver's attorney objected.

Should the objection be sustained?

## Distilled Core Question
Is a lay witness's opinion regarding the speed of a moving vehicle admissible if they have personal perception and the experience to form such an opinion, even if they are not an expert?

## Final Choices (Post-Shuffle)
(A) Yes, because the witness was not qualified as an expert in traffic reconstruction or vehicle speed estimation.
(B) Yes, because a proper foundation for the witness's expertise was not established by the prosecution.
(C) No, because a lay witness may testify to their opinion of a vehicle's speed if it is based on their own perception.
(D) No, because members of a church security team are automatically qualified as experts on safety matters including vehicle speed.

## Correct Answer
**Correct Answer: C** (Original Key: D)

## Right-Answer Explanation
**C is correct.** Under Federal Rule of Evidence 701, a lay witness may testify in the form of an opinion if it is (a) rationally based on the witness’s perception; (b) helpful to clearly understanding the witness’s testimony or to determining a fact in issue; and (c) not based on scientific, technical, or other specialized knowledge. Opinion as to the speed of a moving vehicle is a classic example of a matter within the common knowledge of laypeople. The witness here established that she observed the van and had significant experience judging speeds in her neighborhood, providing a sufficient foundation for her lay opinion.

## Wrong-Answer Explanations
- **(A) is incorrect.** An expert qualification is not required for testimony regarding the speed of a vehicle; such observations are within the competence of ordinary laypeople.
- **(B) is incorrect.** While a foundation is required, it was laid here by showing the witness's personal observation and her experience living on and driving that specific street for twenty years. No "expert" foundation is necessary.
- **(D) is incorrect.** Being on a church security team does not automatically grant expert status. More importantly, expert status is unnecessary for this type of testimony.

## Black-Letter Verification
- **Authority:** Federal Rule of Evidence (FRE) 701.
- **Rule:** A lay witness may testify to an opinion that is rationally based on their perception and helpful to the fact-finder.
- **Verification:** Speed of a vehicle is one of the most commonly recognized subjects for lay opinion testimony. The witness had personal perception (saw the van drive by) and foundation (20 years of experience on that street).

## Rigor/Difficulty Note
**Rigor: Core.** This tests the fundamental understanding of FRE 701 and the specific, high-frequency "lay opinion" categories like speed, weight, and intoxication. The difficulty lies in the student's potential instinct to require "expert" status for anything involving "estimation."
