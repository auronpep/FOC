## 7. Right-Answer Explanation

**Gold Key GK-EVIDENCE-PHONE-AUTH-01:** When someone calls YOU (incoming call) and announces "I'm Peter," that self-identification alone cannot authenticate the call against Peter — any caller can claim a name. A telephone call is authenticated when the caller demonstrates knowledge of a private fact that only a very limited group of people would have.

Here, Lydia's dock-office number was unlisted and known only to Peter and two other dock workers — three people total. A caller who knew that number almost certainly belonged to that small group. Choice B provides the strongest foundation for a judge to find the call is what it purports to be.

**Silver Key SK-EVIDENCE-PHONE-AUTH-01:** Lock the call as "which fact best narrows the universe of who could have placed this call," then test B vs. D on that single axis. B does (only those with Lydia's unlisted number could have called it); D does not (anyone can announce "I'm Peter").

## 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice A** — Prior testimony from Lydia against Peter in a prior case feels like it matters: she hurt him once, so he'd want to silence her now. But this explains *why* someone would threaten Lydia, not *who* was actually on the line. The authentication gate is identity, not motive. Choice A is not responsive to the call. NOT_RESPONSIVE.

**Choice C** — Lydia's personal belief that Peter is "the kind of person" who makes threats sounds like direct testimony about him. But this is a character-propensity judgment — it answers "would he do this?" not "was it him on the phone?" **Gold Key GK-EVIDENCE-PHONE-AUTH-01** applies: character/capability belief does not narrow the universe of possible callers. NOT_RESPONSIVE.

**Choice D** — Sixty-one percent of students chose D because "the caller identified himself as Peter" sounds definitive. But **Gold Key GK-EVIDENCE-PHONE-AUTH-01** defeats this: on an INCOMING call, any caller can say "I'm Peter." Self-identification alone does not authenticate the call against the speaker. Compare: if Lydia had dialed Peter's registered number and a voice answered "This is Peter," the outgoing-call rule would authenticate that conversation. Here the call came to Lydia; the rules are reversed. NOT_TRUE — flat_misstatement.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing authority:** FRE 901(a) requires authentication — evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims. FRE 901(b)(4) allows authentication by distinctive characteristics and circumstances, including a caller's demonstrated knowledge of facts known to only a limited number of people. FRE 901(b)(6) specifically addresses telephone-call authentication (outgoing calls: caller dials a known number, recipient answers and is identified by number assignment + circumstances).

**Why B is correct:** Under FRE 901 and common-law authentication doctrine, an incoming telephone call may be authenticated circumstantially by showing that the caller had knowledge of a private fact — such as an unlisted number — that only a limited group possessed. With Lydia's number known to only three people including Peter, Choice B provides the strongest circumstantial authentication. Advisory Committee Notes to FRE 901 and McCormick on Evidence §226 confirm that self-identification on an incoming call (unlike an outgoing call where the recipient's number is independently verified) is insufficient alone to authenticate the call against the claimed speaker.

**Credited outcome unchanged:** The rule (authentication by private-fact knowledge), the tested issue (which ground best authenticates an incoming threatening call), and the credited answer (caller's knowledge of unlisted number) are identical in the transform and the original.

**Doctrinal currency:** FRE 901 was not among the December 2023 amendments. FRE 702 (expert testimony) was amended Dec 2023 but is not implicated here. No doctrinal-currency hotlist items apply.

**Lawyer confirmation only.** Authority: FRE 901(a), FRE 901(b)(4), FRE 901(b)(6); McCormick on Evidence §226. No fabricated case names or citations used. Attorney review recommended for jurisdiction-specific application.
