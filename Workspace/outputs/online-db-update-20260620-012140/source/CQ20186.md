## 9. Black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

The governing authority is **Trump v. Mazars USA, LLP**. The Supreme Court held that courts must take account of separation-of-powers concerns when congressional subpoenas seek the President’s personal information; courts consider whether the legislative purpose warrants involving the President and his papers, whether the subpoena is no broader than reasonably necessary, the evidence supporting the legislative purpose, and the burdens imposed on the President. ([Justia Law][1])

The broader congressional-investigation baseline is that congressional subpoenas are valid when they fall within a legitimate legislative sphere and concern a subject on which legislation could be had; Congress does not have a general power to expose private affairs for exposure’s sake. ([Justia Law][2])
