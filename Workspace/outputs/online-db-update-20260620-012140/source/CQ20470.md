# CQ20470 - Evidence - Divergent Variant

## Pass-1 Report

**Final Question:**
In a federal fraud trial, a man denies signing a church donation pledge card. The church administrator, who worked with the man for six years on the stewardship committee and frequently saw him sign committee commitment forms, is asked whether the signature on the pledge card appears to be the man's. The man objects that the administrator is not a handwriting expert. How should the court rule?

**Distilled Core Question:**
Can a lay witness provide an opinion on handwriting authenticity based on pre-litigation familiarity?

**Final Choices (Post-Shuffle):**
A: Sustain the objection because handwriting identification requires an expert.
B: Overrule the objection because the administrator's opinion is based on familiarity acquired prior to the litigation.
C: Sustain the objection unless the administrator personally witnessed the man sign this specific pledge card.
D: Overrule the objection only if the jury is not permitted to compare the signature itself.

**Correct Answer:** B

**Letter Map:**
A: Distractor (Mechanic: Misstates evidentiary requirement for expert)
B: Correct
C: Distractor (Mechanic: Invented foundational requirement)
D: Distractor (Mechanic: Misstates jury's role/authentication rules)

**Right-Answer Explanation:**
Under FRE 701 and 901, a lay witness with familiarity with a person's handwriting, provided that familiarity was not acquired for the purposes of litigation, may provide an opinion identifying the handwriting.

**Wrong-Answer Explanations:**
A: Handwriting identification by lay witnesses based on familiarity is permitted.
C: Personal observation of the specific document's signing is not required.
D: Lay testimony and jury comparison are independent, non-exclusive methods of authentication.

**Black-Letter Verification with Authority:**
FRE 701 (Lay Opinion) and FRE 901(b)(2) (Authentication by non-expert with familiarity).

**Rigor/Difficulty Note:**
Core. Tests the fundamentals of lay opinion testimony and handwriting authentication.

**C3 Walkthrough:**
*   **CUT:** The core legal issue is the admissibility of lay opinion regarding handwriting authenticity.
*   **CLASH:** The clash is between the defendant's demand for an "expert" and the permitted use of pre-litigation familiarity to authenticate a document.
*   **CALL:** The call requires ruling on the objection based on FRE 901(b)(2).

**Divergence Audit:**
*   Original Stem Summary: Man denies signing receipt, witness familiar with handwriting asked to identify signature.
*   Variant Stem Summary: Man denies signing church pledge, witness familiar with handwriting asked to identify signature.
*   Divergence: Contextual (Church stewardship committee vs workplace delivery forms).

**Review Truth:**
Pre-litigation familiarity with an individual's handwriting provides a sufficient foundation for a lay witness to authenticate a document.
