## 1. Final Question

Martha owns two adjoining tracts. One tract has a small Christian retreat inn called Morning Light Inn. The other tract is a vacant hillside meadow. Eighteen years ago, Martha conveyed the hillside tract to Daniel by a deed that Martha signed but Daniel did not. ## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**A is correct.** Lydia is likely bound by Daniel's covenant, so Stephen is likely to prevail and may enjoin construction. The deed covenant is an equitable servitude/restrictive covenant that runs with the burdened hillside tract because it was created by a sufficient written instrument, it was intended to bind Daniel's heirs and assigns, it touches and concerns the land by preserving a view from the adjoining inn and restricting use of the hillside, and Lydia had record notice through the properly recorded deed.

Equitable servitudes do not require horizontal or vertical privity in the real-covenant sense. The burden of a restrictive covenant can run to successors, including devisees, when the successor takes with notice. Daniel's death does not extinguish the servitude; it passes with the burdened land. And Daniel's failure to sign the deed does not defeat the covenant where he accepted and recorded the deed containing the restriction. The key is not who built first; it is whether the recorded covenant binds Lydia.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**B. "Lydia, because Daniel did not sign the deed."** ⚠️ DOMINANT TRAP  
Fabricated-rule trap. There is no MBE rule that a grantee's covenant in a recorded deed is unenforceable merely because the grantee did not personally sign the deed. Daniel accepted and recorded the deed containing the restriction. The restriction is enforceable against him and against a successor who takes with notice.

**C. "Stephen, because Morning Light Inn was constructed before Lydia began construction."**  
Wrong-element trap. The timing of construction is not the operative rule. Morning Light Inn's existence helps show that the covenant benefits an adjoining parcel, but Stephen prevails because the covenant is enforceable against Lydia, not because the inn was built first.

**D. "Lydia, because an equitable servitude does not survive the death of the promisor."**  
Flat misstatement. A restrictive covenant/equitable servitude is a property burden, not a merely personal obligation that dies with the promisor. The burden can run to heirs, assigns, and devisees when the successor takes with notice.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing rule:** An equitable servitude/restrictive covenant is enforceable against a successor to the burdened land when the covenant is in writing, intended to run, touches and concerns the land, and the successor has notice. Privity of estate is not required for an equitable servitude.

**Authority:** The traditional rule is illustrated by *Tulk v. Moxhay*, 41 ER 1143 (Ch. 1848), which recognized that a restrictive covenant may be enforced in equity against a successor with notice. The modern Restatement framework treats such restrictions as servitudes; the American Law Institute's *Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes* covers creation, validity, succession to benefits and burdens, and enforcement of servitudes, including succession rules such as §§ 5.1–5.2 for appurtenant burdens running to successors.

**Application:** Daniel's covenant not to build on the hillside tract was in the recorded deed, expressly bound his heirs and assigns, benefited Martha's adjoining inn parcel by preserving the sunrise view, and burdened Daniel's hillside tract. Stephen owns the benefited parcel, and Lydia owns the burdened parcel with record notice. Lydia is therefore bound, and Stephen may enjoin construction.

**Credited outcome confirmed:** Legal issue (equitable servitude binding successor), tested rule (recorded restrictive covenant runs with burdened land), and credited outcome (benefited-owner plaintiff prevails) unchanged from original.
