**D is correct.** Lydia's statement to John is an admission by a party-opponent and therefore not hearsay under FRE 801(d)(2). **Gold Key:** FRE 409 makes evidence of furnishing, promising, or offering to pay medical, hospital, or similar expenses resulting from an injury inadmissible to prove liability for that injury—even if the offer would otherwise be admissible as a party admission.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**B is incorrect.** This choice conflates FRE 408 (compromise and settlement offers) with FRE 409 (offers to pay medical expenses). ## 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule:** Federal Rule of Evidence 409 provides: "Evidence of furnishing, promising to pay, or offering to pay medical, hospital, or similar expenses resulting from an injury is not admissible to prove liability for the injury."

**Authority:** Fed. R. Evid. 409; see also Advisory Committee Notes to FRE 409 (rule aims to encourage humanitarian gestures by excluding benevolent offers from use as evidence of liability).

**Right-answer reasoning:** Lydia's statement to John—"I want to cover all your medical expenses from this"—is a naked offer to pay medical expenses resulting from an injury. Under FRE 409, such evidence is inadmissible to prove liability. The rule applies even though the statement would otherwise qualify as a party-opponent admission under FRE 801(d)(2). ### wrong_answer_recovery_paths
**Path A (statement against interest):**
- Why a student picks this: They remember statement-against-interest is a hearsay exception and select it without verifying unavailability.
- Skipped move: Check whether the declarant is unavailable before invoking the statement-against-interest exception.
- Recovery step: When you see "statement against interest," immediately scan the facts for unavailability; if absent, cut the choice.

**Path B (offer to compromise):**
- Why a student picks this: They confuse any benevolent payment offer with a settlement offer governed by FRE 408.
- Skipped move: Verify that the facts show a disputed claim and negotiation before labeling an offer as a compromise.
- Recovery step: Distinguish FRE 408 (compromise of a disputed claim) from FRE 409 (naked offer to pay medical expenses).

**Path C (party admission):**
- Why a student picks this: They correctly identify the party admission but stop at hearsay analysis and miss the policy exclusion.
- Skipped move: After resolving hearsay, check for independent policy exclusions (FRE 407, 408, 409, 410, 411) before calling admissible.
- Recovery step: When a party makes a post-injury offer, run the policy-exclusion checklist before selecting an admissibility answer.

### outline_mastery_map
**Placement:** 35030305 — Privileges and Public Policy Exclusions > Public Policy Exclusions > Payment of medical expenses

**This item teaches:** FRE 409 excludes offers to pay medical expenses to prove liability, even if the offer is a party admission.

**Fills:**
- Distinguishing FRE 409 from FRE 408
- Recognizing that party admissions can still be excluded by policy rules

**Adjacent to master:**
- 35030302 (Settlement negotiations and offers of compromise)
- 35030304 (Liability insurance)
- 33040202 (Statement by opposing party)

### crossover_intersection_map
- **Hearsay Exemptions (33040202):** Lydia's statement is a party-opponent admission (nonhearsay), which is why C is attractive.
- **Hearsay Exceptions — Unavailability (33040301):** A's misapplication of statement-against-interest requires unavailability.

### review_truth
Under FRE 409, evidence that a person offered to pay an injured party's medical, hospital, or similar expenses is inadmissible to prove liability for the injury.
