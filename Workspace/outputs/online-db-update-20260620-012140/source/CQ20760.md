### 1. Final question

At Daniel’s federal mail-fraud trial over forged Bible-study supply invoices, the prosecutor offers a rubber stamp marked “PAID” that was found in a clearance bin at Lydia’s Christian Bookshop twenty miles from the post office used in the alleged scheme. At a federal trial, the prosecution offers a physical object with no connection to the defendant, the charged event, or any relevant issue. Should it be admitted?

### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.** The stamp is a physical object, but the stem gives no link between the stamp and Daniel, any invoice, or the charged mailing. That means the object has no fact-path into the case. It is not enough that mail-fraud schemes can involve office supplies. The object must help prove something that matters.

**Silver Key:** Start with connection and purpose before object-category or prejudice. If the stem gives no bridge between the thing and the charged event, the “this kind of object fits this kind of crime” answer is bait.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells a safe-sounding alternate use: if the stamp cannot be real evidence, maybe it can at least show the jury what rubber stamps look like. The breaker is the offered-purpose move: the prosecutor offered this stamp as evidence, and the stem supplies no issue that needs a generic stamp demonstration.

**B.** This choice is attractive because it reaches the same outcome as the credited answer: exclusion. The breaker is the reason. “Only because” and “as a matter of law” jump to a prejudice frame when the visible defect is more basic: no connection, no probative value.

**C.** This is the dominant trap candidate. It treats the category as enough. Stamps can appear in fraud cases, but “all office stamps are relevant” says too much. **Silver Key:** Do not let the crime label do the connection work. The object needs its own bridge to a charged event or relevant issue.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The source row tests real-evidence connection and materiality under FRE 401 and FRE 901, with the official key excluding an unconnected weapon-like object.  The variation preserves that rule: a stamp-like object, like a weapon-like object, is not admissible merely because it belongs to a category that could matter in some case.

FRE 401 defines relevant evidence as evidence that has any tendency to make a fact more or less probable and where that fact is of consequence. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) FRE 402 makes irrelevant evidence inadmissible. ([Legal Information Institute][2]) FRE 901 also requires the proponent to produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that an item is what the proponent claims it is, including through witness knowledge or distinctive characteristics. ([Legal Information Institute][3]) Here, no witness links the stamp to Daniel, the invoices, or the charged mailing, so the stamp does not clear the basic relevance/connection layer. The credited outcome is unchanged.
