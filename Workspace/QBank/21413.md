Outline: Attempt
Question: Milo points a revolver at Dana from close range, intending to kill her, and pulls the trigger. Unknown to Milo, the revolver is unloaded. Dana is unharmed. Milo is charged with attempted murder. Is the unloaded gun a defense?
Answer A: Yes, because an unloaded gun cannot cause death
Answer B: No, because Milo intended to kill and took an act that would have killed if the gun were loaded
Answer C: Yes, because attempted murder requires an actual injury
Answer D: No, but only because Milo was standing close to Dana
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Milo had the specific intent to kill and took a direct act toward that killing. The factual circumstance preventing completion does not defeat attempt.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The unloaded gun is factual impossibility. If the facts were as Milo believed, pulling the trigger would have been murder. C: Attempted murder does not require injury; it requires intent to kill plus a sufficient act. D: The key is intent plus pulling the trigger under facts Milo believed would kill. The doctrine does not turn solely on distance.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant who intends to kill and pulls the trigger believing the gun is loaded may be guilty of attempted murder even if the gun is actually unloaded.
