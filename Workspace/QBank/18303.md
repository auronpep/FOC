Outline: Initial disclosures
Question: After the Rule 26(f) conference in a federal negligence case, the defendant refuses to identify potential witnesses, relevant documents, insurance coverage, or its damages-related information. The defendant says it will respond only if the plaintiff serves formal discovery requests. Is the defendant's position correct?
Answer A: Yes, because federal discovery begins only after interrogatories or document requests are served
Answer B: No, because initial disclosures generally must be made without awaiting a request
Answer C: Yes, because insurance agreements are privileged until liability is established
Answer D: No, but only damages computations must be disclosed without a request
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 26(a)(1) requires core disclosures such as likely witnesses, documents, damages computations, and insurance agreements.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Initial disclosures are required without awaiting a discovery request unless an exception or order applies. C: Insurance agreements are part of the required initial disclosure categories. D: The required disclosures also include likely witnesses, relevant documents, and insurance agreements.
lb_rule_reference: Unless exempted or otherwise ordered, a party must make initial disclosures without awaiting a discovery request, including likely witnesses, relevant documents, damages computations, and insurance agreements.
