MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Consent
Question: Oskar, a California resident, filed a complaint in a Nevada federal court against TerraView Corp., a Nevada corporation. TerraView's attorney reviewed the complaint and, believing there was no viable 12(b)(2) personal jurisdiction challenge (TerraView is a Nevada corporation), filed a pre-answer Rule 12 motion asserting only (1) insufficient service of process under Rule 12(b)(5) and (2) failure to state a claim under Rule 12(b)(6). The court denied both grounds. TerraView then filed its answer, which for the first time asserted failure to state a claim under Rule 12(b)(6). Six months into discovery, TerraView's new counsel reviewed the record and determined that Oskar's complaint contains a defective allegation that could support a Rule 12(b)(6) motion distinct from the one already ruled on — specifically, a new theory that the complaint fails to plead an essential element. Which of the following best describes TerraView's ability to raise the new 12(b)(6) argument at this stage of the litigation?
Answer A: TerraView has waived the new 12(b)(6) argument because it omitted it from its first pre-answer Rule 12 motion
Answer B: TerraView may raise the new 12(b)(6) argument, but only if it files a second pre-answer motion before filing its answer
Answer C: TerraView may raise the new 12(b)(6) argument through a Rule 12(c) motion for judgment on the pleadings or at trial, because 12(b)(6) is not subject to the consolidation waiver that applies to 12(b)(2)–(5)
Answer D: TerraView cannot raise any Rule 12 argument at this stage because it has filed an answer — all 12(b) motions must be made before the answer
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 12(h)(2) expressly preserves the failure-to-state-a-claim defense: it may be raised in a pleading (the answer did assert it), in a motion for judgment on the pleadings under Rule 12(c), or at trial. The consolidation requirement under Rule 12(g)(2) and the waiver rule under Rule 12(h)(1) apply only to the 12(b)(2)–(5) defenses. TerraView did not waive its 12(b)(6) argument by omitting a specific theory from its pre-answer motion. It may pursue the new 12(b)(6) theory through a Rule 12(c) motion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 12(h)(1)(A) waiver applies only to the 12(b)(2)–(5) defenses — personal jurisdiction, venue, process, and service. Rule 12(b)(6) failure to state a claim is specifically preserved by Rule 12(h)(2), which permits a 12(b)(6) defense to be raised in any pleading permitted or ordered under Rule 7, in a motion for judgment on the pleadings, or at trial. It is not subject to consolidation waiver. TerraView's new 12(b)(6) theory may still be raised. B: Rule 12(g)(2) prohibits a second pre-answer motion raising defenses available but omitted from the first motion. And TerraView has already filed its answer, so the pre-answer window has closed. Neither a second pre-answer motion (barred by 12(g)(2)) nor a new pre-answer motion after the answer (procedurally unavailable) is the correct path. The correct vehicle for a 12(b)(6) challenge at this stage is a Rule 12(c) motion for judgment on the pleadings or, if the case involves a sufficiency question that cannot be resolved on the pleadings, a Rule 56 summary-judgment motion. D: This overstates the rule. While several 12(b) defenses are waived if not raised before the answer (12(b)(2)–(5)), other 12(b) defenses specifically survive the answer: (b)(6) may be raised in the answer, in a 12(c) MJOP, or at trial; (b)(1) (SMJ) is never waivable and may be raised at any time. "No Rule 12 motions after the answer" is a categorical over-statement that Rule 12(h)(2) and 12(b)(1) both contradict.
lb_rule_reference: Under FRCP 12(g)(2) and 12(h)(1)(A): if a party makes a pre-answer Rule 12 motion and omits a defense of personal jurisdiction (12(b)(2)), improper venue (12(b)(3)), insufficient process (12(b)(4)), or insufficient service (12(b)(5)), that defense is waived. By contrast, 12(b)(6) (failure to state a claim) is not waived by omission from a pre-answer motion; it may be raised later in any pleading or motion. 12(b)(1) (lack of SMJ) is never waivable.
