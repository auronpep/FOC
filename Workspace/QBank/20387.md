Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police arrested Martin in his yard without a warrant or probable cause. They found no evidence during the arrest and obtained no statement from him. Weeks later, prosecutors indicted Martin based on surveillance video and eyewitness testimony that were already in police files before the arrest. Martin moves to dismiss the indictment solely because his arrest was unlawful. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant the motion, because an unlawful arrest voids any later indictment
Answer B: Grant the motion unless the prosecutor proves the arresting officers acted in good faith
Answer C: Deny the motion, because an unlawful arrest does not require dismissal when the prosecution rests on evidence independent of the arrest
Answer D: Deny the motion only if Martin was released and rearrested with a valid warrant
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The indictment rests on preexisting video and witnesses. Because Martin seeks dismissal rather than suppression of tainted evidence, the motion should be denied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: An unlawful arrest does not void an otherwise valid prosecution. The remedy is suppression of tainted evidence, if any, not dismissal of charges supported by independent evidence. B: The issue is not whether to admit evidence from a defective warrant. No tainted evidence is being offered, and dismissal is not the remedy. D: The prosecution can proceed on independent evidence regardless of whether police repeat the arrest process. The suppression inquiry focuses on tainted evidence.
lb_rule_reference: The remedy for an unlawful arrest is suppression of evidence obtained through the illegality when applicable, not automatic dismissal of charges or invalidation of an indictment.
