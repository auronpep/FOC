Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A patient loudly discussed her medical billing dispute on a hospital's open public lobby phone. A visitor standing nearby heard the conversation without using any device, trick, or hidden access. The patient sued for intrusion upon seclusion. Is the visitor likely liable for intrusion?
Answer A: No, because the visitor merely overheard a conversation exposed in a public lobby
Answer B: Yes, because the topic was medical and therefore always secluded
Answer C: Yes, because intrusion requires no publication
Answer D: No, but only because the visitor did not repeat the conversation
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Intrusion requires invasion of seclusion or private affairs; ordinary overhearing in a public area is not enough.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Sensitive subject matter matters, but the plaintiff exposed it in a public setting. C: It is true that publication is unnecessary, but there still must be a highly offensive intrusion. D: Lack of publication is not the key. The problem is lack of intrusion into seclusion.
lb_rule_reference: Intrusion upon seclusion requires intrusion into a private place, conversation, or matter; observing or hearing what is exposed in a public setting usually is not intrusion.
