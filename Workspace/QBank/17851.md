Question: In a federal civil trial, nine-year-old Samuel is called to testify about a fall he saw in a store. On voir dire, Samuel says he knows the difference between telling the truth and lying, promises to tell the truth, and can describe where he was standing. He cannot state the exact date of the accident. The store objects that Samuel is incompetent. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a child under ten is automatically incompetent.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because Samuel cannot give the exact date.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Samuel can understand the truth duty and has a basis for testimony; weaknesses affect weight.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Samuel's testimony is corroborated by an adult witness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Witness competency is broad. A child witness may testify if capable of understanding the duty to tell the truth and providing testimony based on perception. Inability to state the exact date affects credibility or weight, not automatic competency.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because age alone is not automatic incompetency. B: B is wrong because imperfect memory does not necessarily destroy competency. D: D is wrong because corroboration by an adult is not a competency prerequisite.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 601 and 603 / broad competency and oath
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests competence-versus-credibility confusion.
