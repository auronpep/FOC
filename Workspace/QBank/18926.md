Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: A city denies a parade permit for a specific holiday parade. The parade date passes before trial, and the organizers no longer seek an injunction for that event. Their complaint also requests nominal damages for the alleged past violation of their First Amendment rights. The city argues the case is moot because the parade date has passed. What is the best response?
Answer A: The case is not moot because a live damages claim can provide effective relief for the alleged past violation
Answer B: The case is moot because injunctive relief for the parade is no longer possible
Answer C: The case is moot unless the city promises to deny future permits
Answer D: The case is unripe because the parade date has already passed
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Although event-specific injunctive relief is unavailable, the nominal-damages claim remains a live request for relief.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Mootness asks whether any effective relief remains, and damages can keep the controversy live. C: A damages claim for the past denial is independently live. D: Ripeness concerns premature disputes; a passed event raises mootness, subject to remaining remedies.
lb_rule_reference: A case is not moot if a live claim for damages or other effective relief remains, even if injunctive relief is no longer available.
