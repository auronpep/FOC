Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: In a federal assault trial, Ezra is accused of striking Joseph outside a theater on Friday night. Ezra denies the assault. Joseph offers evidence that Ezra rented an apartment three miles from the theater eight years earlier. Ezra objects. There is no evidence that Ezra still lived there or used the apartment on Friday. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because any past connection to the neighborhood is relevant to identity.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because, without a current connection to the place or event, the old apartment rental does not make Ezra's presence at the theater more probable.
Answer C: Admit the evidence because remoteness affects only sentencing, not admissibility.
Answer D: Exclude the evidence only if Ezra proves he moved away.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Relevance requires a logical connection to a fact of consequence. An eight-year-old rental, with no current connection to the theater or the event, does not tend to prove Ezra was present on Friday night.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A overstates identity relevance. C is wrong because remoteness can defeat or weaken relevance depending on the logical link. D is wrong because Joseph bears the burden of offering a relevant connection. C: C is wrong because remoteness can defeat or weaken relevance depending on the logical link. D: D is wrong because Joseph bears the burden of offering a relevant connection.
