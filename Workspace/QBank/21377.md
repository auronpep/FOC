Outline: Motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law
Question: At the start of a jury trial, before the plaintiff calls any witness, the defendant asks the judge to enter judgment as a matter of law because the plaintiff will not be able to prove causation. The plaintiff objects that she has not yet presented evidence. How should the court treat the Rule 50(a) request?
Answer A: Grant it because JMOL may be entered whenever the judge predicts the plaintiff will lose
Answer B: Grant it only if the complaint was also defective under Rule 12(b)(6)
Answer C: Deny it because Rule 50 motions are available only after judgment
Answer D: Deny it as premature because the plaintiff has not been fully heard on causation
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The defendant is trying to use JMOL before the plaintiff has presented evidence on the challenged element.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 50(a) requires that the party be fully heard on the issue before JMOL is considered. B: JMOL is not tied to pleading sufficiency. It tests trial evidence after the party has been heard. C: Rule 50(a) is available before submission to the jury, but only after the party has been fully heard.
lb_rule_reference: A Rule 50(a) motion may be made before the case is submitted to the jury only after the opposing party has been fully heard on the issue.
