Outline: Renewed Motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law
Question: Before the jury received the case, defendant moved under Rule 50(a) only on damages. The court denied the motion. After the jury found for plaintiff, defendant filed a Rule 50(b) motion arguing for the first time that plaintiff failed to prove causation. May the court grant renewed JMOL on the new causation ground?
Answer A: Yes, because any Rule 50(a) motion preserves every possible Rule 50(b) ground
Answer B: Yes, because causation is more important than damages
Answer C: No, because defendant did not preserve the causation ground in its Rule 50(a) motion
Answer D: No, because Rule 50(b) is available only to plaintiffs
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The post-verdict motion cannot introduce a new legal-sufficiency ground not raised before submission.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Renewed JMOL is limited to grounds fairly raised pre-verdict. B: Importance does not substitute for preservation. D: Any party meeting Rule 50 requirements may seek JMOL.
lb_rule_reference: A Rule 50(b) motion renews, rather than invents, a Rule 50(a) legal-sufficiency challenge.
