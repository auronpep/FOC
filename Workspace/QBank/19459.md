MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Tara and Luis conspire to submit false invoices to a city agency. During a lunch break unrelated to the scheme, Luis gets into a personal argument with a neighbor and assaults him. The assault does not help the invoice scheme and was not discussed or foreseeable from the plan. Prosecutors charge Tara with the assault under Pinkerton. Is Tara liable for Luis's assault under Pinkerton?
Answer A: Yes, because Tara was still a member of the conspiracy when Luis committed the assault
Answer B: No, because the assault was a personal detour outside the conspiracy's scope and not foreseeable
Answer C: Yes, because any crime by any conspirator is automatically foreseeable
Answer D: No, but only because the conspiracy was nonviolent
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Luis's assault had no connection to advancing the false-invoice conspiracy and was not a natural consequence of it. Pinkerton does not impute that offense to Tara.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Pinkerton is limited to crimes in furtherance of the conspiracy and reasonably foreseeable. Luis's personal assault was outside the invoice scheme. C: The doctrine asks whether this kind of crime was a reasonably foreseeable consequence of the agreed venture. D: The reason is not a categorical nonviolent-conspiracy rule. The reason is lack of furtherance and foreseeability on these facts.
lb_rule_reference: Pinkerton liability does not extend to a co-conspirator's crime that is outside the conspiracy's objective, not in furtherance of it, or not reasonably foreseeable.
