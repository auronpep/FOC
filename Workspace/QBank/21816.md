Question: A buyer brings a diversity products-liability action in federal court eight years after a machine was first sold. The governing state statute says no products-liability action may be brought more than six years after first sale, regardless of when injury occurs. How should the federal court treat the repose rule?
Answer A: Ignore it because filing deadlines are always federal procedure
Answer B: Apply it and dismiss if the state repose period expired
Answer C: Apply only the federal four-year limitations period for all civil actions
Answer D: Let the jury decide whether the delay was reasonable
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: State repose rules are substantive for Erie purposes because they define the existence and reach of the state-law claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A statute of repose is part of the state-created right and can extinguish the claim itself. It is not mere federal docket management. C: There is no general federal limitations period that replaces state repose rules in an ordinary diversity products case. D: Expiration of a repose period is a legal bar for the court to apply.
lb_rule_reference: A federal court sitting in diversity applies the forum state's substantive limitations and repose rules selected by the governing choice-of-law analysis.
