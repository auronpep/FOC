Question: Lydia writes to a bank, “If you extend a $10,000 line of credit to my brother Aaron and notify me in writing by Monday that you have done so, I will guarantee his repayment.” The bank extends the credit on Friday but forgets to notify Lydia. Aaron defaults. Is Lydia bound as guarantor?
Answer A: Yes, because notice is never required when a lender accepts by extending credit.
Answer B: Yes, because Aaron received the benefit of the loan.
Answer C: No, because the bank did not satisfy the express notice condition attached to Lydia's guaranty offer.
Answer D: No, but only because Lydia would have been free to revoke after Aaron defaulted.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Lydia's offer expressly conditioned her guaranty on written notice by Monday after the bank extended credit. The bank extended credit but failed to satisfy the notice condition, so Lydia is not bound on that offer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an offer can require notice, and the fact pattern expressly did so. B: B is wrong because benefit to Aaron does not satisfy Lydia's notice condition. D: D is wrong because the issue is failure of an express notice condition, not revocation after default.
lb_rule_reference: surety/common_law
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treated performance as always enough for a unilateral guaranty, ignoring an express notice condition.
