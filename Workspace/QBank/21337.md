Question: A state agency terminates a recipient's subsistence cash benefits without prior notice or a chance to respond. The agency says the recipient can file an appeal after benefits stop, but the recipient depends on the benefits for food and rent. What is the best procedural due process answer?
Answer A: Post-termination review is always enough because benefits are statutory
Answer B: Due process likely requires notice and a meaningful chance to respond before termination
Answer C: No process is due because welfare benefits are not property
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies because poverty is a suspect classification
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The private interest is extremely high, and erroneous termination would impose immediate hardship.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A statutory entitlement can be protected property, and subsistence benefits often require meaningful pre-termination procedure. C: A legitimate entitlement to benefits can be a protected property interest. D: The claim is procedural due process, not equal protection suspect-class review.
lb_rule_reference: When government terminates subsistence welfare benefits, due process generally requires meaningful pre-termination procedures because the private interest and risk of hardship are high.
