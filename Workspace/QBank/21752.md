Question: A store has a statutory duty to keep its public entrance reasonably safe. It hires an independent contractor to remove ice from the entrance before opening. The contractor negligently leaves a sheet of ice on the only entrance path, and a customer falls. What is the customer's best argument against the store?
Answer A: The store is liable because every independent contractor is treated as an employee on business premises
Answer B: The store may be liable because the entrance-safety duty is nondelegable
Answer C: The store is liable only if it personally placed the ice on the entrance path
Answer D: The store has no possible liability because it hired someone to perform the work
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A nondelegable duty can keep responsibility with the hirer despite independent-contractor status.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: That is too broad. C: Nondelegable duty doctrine may apply even when the contractor created the hazard. D: Hiring a contractor does not defeat a nondelegable duty.
lb_rule_reference: A hirer may remain liable when the law imposes a nondelegable duty, even if the negligent actor is an independent contractor.
