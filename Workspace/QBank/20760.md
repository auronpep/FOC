Question: In a federal murder trial, the prosecutor offers a knife found in a public park three miles from the crime scene. No witness connects the knife to David, the victim, or the killing. David objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the knife because all weapons are relevant in a murder trial.
Answer B: Admit the knife only as demonstrative evidence of what knives generally look like.
Answer C: Exclude the knife because without a connection to the charged event or a relevant issue, the object lacks probative value.
Answer D: Exclude the knife only because it is unfairly prejudicial as a matter of law.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Real evidence must be relevant to a fact of consequence. A weapon-like object with no connection to the case is not admissible merely because the charge involves a weapon.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A treats the category as enough. B invents a demonstrative use not offered. D overuses prejudice; the primary problem is lack of relevance/connection.
