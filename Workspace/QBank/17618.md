Question: Jacob agreed to build two storage sheds for Leah for $30,000 each, with each shed separately priced in the contract. Jacob completed the first shed properly and then wrongfully refused to build the second. Leah hired another builder to build the second shed for a reasonable price of $34,000. Leah has not paid Jacob anything. If Jacob sues for the completed first shed and Leah asserts her damages for the second, what is the net result?
Answer A: Jacob recovers nothing because breach of any part of a contract bars payment for completed work.
Answer B: Jacob recovers $30,000 and Leah has no claim because the second shed was separately priced.
Answer C: Jacob recovers $26,000, representing $30,000 for the completed shed minus Leah's $4,000 substitute-cost damages.
Answer D: Leah recovers $34,000 and owes nothing for the completed shed.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Because the sheds were separately priced and the first was properly completed, Jacob may recover the $30,000 price for that unit. Leah may offset the $4,000 extra cost of obtaining the second shed. The net recovery is $26,000 for Jacob.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a divisible contract can permit recovery for completed units subject to damages on the breached unit. B: B is wrong because Jacob's breach of the second shed caused Leah a $4,000 expectation loss. D: D is wrong because Leah received the first completed shed and cannot keep it without accounting for its contract price.
lb_rule_reference: Common law divisible contract; expectation setoff
