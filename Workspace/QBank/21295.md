Question: After a preservation duty arose, a defendant negligently failed to suspend deletion of relevant text messages. The messages cannot be restored, and the plaintiff is prejudiced. The court finds no intent to deprive the plaintiff of the information's use in the litigation. What sanction is most consistent with Rule 37(e)?
Answer A: An adverse-inference instruction is mandatory because the loss prejudiced the plaintiff
Answer B: Measures no greater than necessary to cure the prejudice, but not an adverse inference absent intent to deprive
Answer C: No remedy is available because the failure was negligent rather than intentional
Answer D: Default judgment is required whenever ESI cannot be restored or replaced
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Without intent to deprive, the court is limited to curative measures proportional to the prejudice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Adverse-inference sanctions require intent to deprive. C: Curative measures may be available for prejudicial negligent loss. D: Default is a severe sanction requiring more, including intent to deprive.
lb_rule_reference: If ESI that should have been preserved is lost and cannot be restored or replaced, the court may order measures no greater than necessary to cure prejudice; adverse-inference, dismissal, or default sanctions require intent to deprive.
