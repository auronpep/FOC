Question: Esther hired Jacob to paint her portrait for a fixed price. The contract stated, “Final payment is due only if Esther is personally satisfied with the finished portrait.” Jacob completed a technically competent portrait. Esther honestly disliked the way the portrait captured her expression and refused to pay. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Esther is not obligated to make final payment if her dissatisfaction is honest and in good faith.
Answer B: Esther must pay because a technically competent portrait always satisfies a satisfaction condition.
Answer C: Jacob can recover the price because substantial performance satisfies all express conditions.
Answer D: Esther may refuse payment only if a reasonable person would dislike the portrait.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When a satisfaction condition concerns personal taste, judgment, or aesthetics, honest good-faith dissatisfaction generally prevents the payment duty from maturing. The portrait context points to subjective satisfaction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because technical competence does not necessarily satisfy a personal taste condition. C: C is wrong because substantial performance does not automatically satisfy an express satisfaction condition. D: D is wrong because the objective reasonable-person standard is more appropriate for commercial utility, not personal aesthetic satisfaction.
lb_rule_reference: Satisfaction condition / subjective good faith
