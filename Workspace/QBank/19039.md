Question: Boaz sold Naomi a parcel that bordered a public road. Building a driveway from the parcel to the public road would require grading a steep slope and would cost far more than using an existing lane across Boaz's retained land. The deed did not grant Naomi a right to use Boaz's lane. Naomi claims an easement by necessity over Boaz's lane. What is the best result?
Answer A: Naomi has an easement by necessity because Boaz's lane is the cheapest route.
Answer B: Naomi does not have an easement by necessity because the parcel has legal road access and expense or convenience alone is not strict necessity.
Answer C: Naomi has an implied easement by necessity because all land-sale deeds include the most convenient access route.
Answer D: Naomi owns Boaz's lane by adverse possession because she would otherwise have to spend money on a driveway.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement by necessity requires strict necessity, usually the absence of legal access to the land. Naomi's parcel borders a public road. A costly or inconvenient driveway does not by itself create strict necessity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because cheaper access is not the same as necessity. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because deeds do not imply the most convenient route as a matter of course. D: D is wrong because cost does not create adverse possession or title.
lb_rule_reference: Strict necessity versus convenience
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is converting economic inconvenience into strict legal necessity.
