Question: After a jury verdict for plaintiff, the judge concludes the evidence was legally sufficient for a reasonable jury to find liability, but the damages award is so excessive that it shocks the conscience. Defendant asks for JMOL solely because the damages are excessive. What is the better procedural framing?
Answer A: JMOL is the correct remedy for any excessive verdict
Answer B: New trial or remittitur is the better route if the evidence is legally sufficient but damages are excessive
Answer C: The court must leave every damages verdict untouched
Answer D: The court must enter default judgment for defendant
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The judge is not saying no reasonable jury could find liability; the problem is the verdict amount.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Excessive damages usually point to new trial or remittitur, not judgment as a matter of law on liability. C: Rule 59 permits new trial for excessive damages in proper cases. D: Default has no role after a contested jury verdict.
lb_rule_reference: JMOL enters judgment based on legal insufficiency; a new trial may be ordered for verdict against the weight of evidence, trial error, excessive damages, or unfairness.
