Outline: Refreshing recollection
Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Mary testifies that she once knew the serial number stamped on a machine but cannot now recall it well enough to testify fully. She identifies a report she made the same day, says the matter was fresh in her memory then, and says the report was accurate when she made it. The plaintiff offers the report under the recorded-recollection exception and asks that it be received as an exhibit. The defendant objects to exhibit admission but not to reading the relevant entry to the jury. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the report entirely because Mary cannot now remember the serial number.
Answer B: Allow the relevant entry to be read into evidence, but do not receive the report as an exhibit unless offered by the adverse party.
Answer C: Receive the report as an exhibit because every recorded recollection is admitted like a business record.
Answer D: Allow the jury to inspect the entire report because Rule 612 gives the jury access to refreshing writings.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Rule 803(5), a qualifying recorded recollection may be read into evidence. It may be received as an exhibit only if offered by an adverse party. Mary's foundation supports reading the relevant entry, but the proponent may not have the report received as an exhibit over objection.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because lack of current memory is part of the recorded-recollection route. C: C is wrong because recorded recollection is not admitted as an exhibit when offered by the proponent. D: D is wrong because Rule 612 concerns adverse-party inspection and cross-examination, not automatic jury inspection.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 803(5) / recorded recollection read but not exhibit unless adverse party offers
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the presentation consequence of recorded recollection: read to the jury, not admitted as the proponent's exhibit.
