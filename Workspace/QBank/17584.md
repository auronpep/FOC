Outline: Presumptions and inferences
Question: Levi is charged in federal court with knowingly possessing stolen tablets. The government proves that Levi was found hiding the tablets under his cloak two hours after they were stolen. Levi offers no evidence. The prosecutor requests an instruction that the jury may, but need not, infer from Levi's unexplained recent possession that he knew the tablets were stolen. Levi objects that the judge is deciding guilt for the jury. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because only the jury may decide whether Levi's possession was recent.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because the prosecutor must use direct evidence of knowledge.
Answer C: Overrule the objection, because the instruction permits but does not require a rational inference and leaves the burden on the government.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because the judge has found that Levi knowingly possessed stolen tablets.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The instruction is permissive, not mandatory. It allows the jury to draw a rational inference from unexplained recent possession while leaving the ultimate finding and the government's burden of proof intact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the instruction does not make the judge decide the fact; the jury remains free to accept or reject the inference. B: B is wrong because knowledge may be proved by circumstantial evidence. D: D is wrong because the judge is not finding the element for the jury.
