Question: Sarah orally asked Peter to find a buyer for Sarah's house. Peter negotiated the sale to Lydia but had no written authority from Sarah. After reading the deed, Sarah personally signed it as grantor and delivered it to Lydia. Lydia accepted. Sarah later argued that Peter's lack of written authority made the deed invalid. Who owns the house?
Answer A: Sarah, because Peter had no written authority to negotiate the sale.
Answer B: Lydia, because Sarah herself signed and delivered the deed.
Answer C: Sarah, because a real estate agent may never help arrange a deed unless licensed to practice law.
Answer D: Lydia only if Peter also signs the deed as Sarah's agent.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is the grantor's execution. Written authority is required when an agent signs the deed for the owner. Here Sarah personally signed and delivered the deed, so Peter's lack of written authority to negotiate did not defeat the deed-execution lock, and title passed to Lydia.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the invalid-authority rule targets an agent executing the deed, not an owner who personally signs. C: C is wrong because the title question does not turn on unauthorized practice of law. D: D is wrong because Peter's signature is unnecessary when Sarah herself executed the deed.
lb_rule_reference: Written authority required only when agent executes deed
