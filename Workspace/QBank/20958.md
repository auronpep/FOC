Question: A warehouse negligently left a loading ramp wet. A delivery worker ignored a warning cone and walked quickly across the ramp while carrying boxes that blocked his view. He fell and suffered $80,000 in damages. The jurisdiction uses a 51 percent modified comparative fault rule: a plaintiff is barred only if his fault is greater than 50 percent. The jury assigns 50 percent fault to the worker and 50 percent fault to the warehouse. How much may the worker recover in his negligence claim against the warehouse?
Answer A: Nothing, because the worker was equally at fault
Answer B: $40,000, because the worker's recovery is reduced by his 50 percent fault but not barred
Answer C: $80,000, because the warehouse's negligence created the wet ramp
Answer D: $20,000, because modified comparative fault cuts the recovery in half twice
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the stated 51 percent rule, exactly 50 percent plaintiff fault does not bar recovery. The worker recovers half of $80,000, or $40,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: This jurisdiction bars recovery only when plaintiff fault is greater than 50 percent. At exactly 50 percent, the worker may recover the remaining 50 percent. C: The jurisdiction reduces damages by the plaintiff's assigned fault. The worker's own 50 percent fault cannot be ignored. D: Modified comparative fault first asks whether the threshold bars recovery. If not barred, damages are reduced once by the plaintiff's percentage of fault.
lb_rule_reference: Under a 51 percent modified comparative fault rule, a plaintiff is barred only if the plaintiff's fault is greater than 50 percent; a plaintiff at exactly 50 percent may recover the other 50 percent.
