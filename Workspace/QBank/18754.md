Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Joseph received a valid deed conveying Millacre “to Joseph and his heirs, but if Joseph operates a tavern on the land during Joseph's life, then to Benjamin and his heirs.” Joseph later conveyed Millacre to Andrew by a warranty deed stating that Andrew received “fee simple absolute.” At that time, Joseph had not operated a tavern on the land. What did Andrew receive?
Answer A: Fee simple absolute, because Joseph's deed used the words “fee simple absolute.”
Answer B: Joseph's fee simple subject to executory limitation, subject to Benjamin's executory interest.
Answer C: No interest, because Joseph's defeasible estate was not transferable.
Answer D: A tenancy in common with Benjamin.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Joseph owned a fee simple subject to executory limitation. He could transfer that estate to Andrew, but he could not eliminate Benjamin's executory interest by labeling the conveyance “fee simple absolute.” Andrew takes the same defeasible estate Joseph owned.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because deed wording cannot give the grantee more than the grantor owns. C: C is wrong because defeasible fees are generally transferable. D: D is wrong because Benjamin has a future interest, not a present co-ownership interest.
lb_rule_reference: Deed scope / transferee takes transferor's estate
