Question: A corporation negotiated with a federal environmental agency over a civil regulatory penalty. During those negotiations, the corporation's compliance officer admitted that discharge reports had been altered. Later, in a criminal prosecution for false environmental reporting, the government offers the compliance officer's negotiation statement. The corporation objects under Rule 408. Which ruling is most accurate?
Answer A: Do not exclude the statement under Rule 408 because it is offered in a criminal case and the negotiations concerned a claim by a public office exercising regulatory, investigative, or enforcement authority.
Answer B: Exclude the statement because Rule 408 bars all statements made during civil compromise negotiations.
Answer C: Exclude the statement because factual admissions in settlement negotiations are always protected.
Answer D: Admit the statement only to show witness bias, not as evidence of false reporting.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 408 does not require exclusion of conduct or statements made during compromise negotiations when offered in a criminal case and the negotiations related to a claim by a public office exercising regulatory, investigative, or enforcement authority. The decisive axis is the public-office criminal exception.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A is wrong because Correct answer B is wrong because This omits the public-office criminal exception. D is wrong because Bias is a separate permitted purpose under Rule 408(b), but the stem activates the Rule 408(a)(2) criminal/public-office exception.
