Question: Noah tells Esther, “I will pay you $800 if you paint my fence this weekend.” The offer does not invite a promise. Esther buys paint and brushes on Friday but does not go to Noah's property or begin painting. On Saturday morning, before Esther begins painting, Noah revokes the offer. Can Esther enforce the offer based only on buying supplies?
Answer A: No. Buying supplies was preparation, not the requested performance, so it did not accept the unilateral offer or make it irrevocable.
Answer B: Yes. Any reliance on a unilateral offer makes it irrevocable.
Answer C: Yes. Noah's statement was enforceable as a gift promise once Esther bought supplies.
Answer D: No, but only because Esther failed to send Noah a written promise to paint.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The offer invited acceptance by painting the fence. Buying supplies was preparation, not beginning the invited performance. Without completed performance or protected part performance, Noah revoked before acceptance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because any reliance is not automatically enough; the classic unilateral-contract protection arises from beginning the invited performance, and promissory estoppel would require a stronger justice showing. C: C is wrong because Noah requested performance in exchange, not a gratuitous transfer. D: D is wrong because a promise was not invited by the offer.
lb_rule_reference: common_law
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treated preparations as performance or as automatic promissory estoppel.
