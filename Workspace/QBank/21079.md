Question: A plaintiff from State A sues a defendant from State B in federal court based only on diversity. A nonparty from State B seeks to intervene as a plaintiff under Rule 24 and assert a state-law claim against the defendant arising from the same accident. What is the best jurisdictional analysis?
Answer A: The intervenor's claim may proceed because Rule 24 intervention automatically supplies supplemental jurisdiction
Answer B: The intervenor's claim is likely barred by Section 1367(b) because the intervenor seeks to join as a plaintiff and diversity would be undermined
Answer C: The intervenor's claim may proceed because Section 1367(b) applies only to defendants
Answer D: The claim is barred only if the intervenor's damages are below the amount threshold
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1367(b) targets claims by persons seeking to intervene as plaintiffs under Rule 24 when supplemental jurisdiction would be inconsistent with Section 1332.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intervention permission does not create subject-matter jurisdiction, and Section 1367(b) limits plaintiff-side claims in diversity-only cases. C: Section 1367(b) is especially concerned with specified plaintiff-side claims in diversity cases. D: The problem is nondiverse plaintiff-side intervention, not just amount.
lb_rule_reference: In a diversity-only case, Section 1367(b) can bar supplemental jurisdiction over claims by persons seeking to intervene as plaintiffs under Rule 24 when exercising jurisdiction would be inconsistent with diversity requirements.
