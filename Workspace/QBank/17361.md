Outline: Conspiracy
Question: A jurisdiction follows the MPC unilateral approach to conspiracy. An undercover officer tells Ben that she wants to help him sell stolen laptops. Ben agrees, sets a meeting time, and provides a list of buyers. The officer never intends to sell stolen goods. Ben is charged with conspiracy. Under this jurisdiction's unilateral conspiracy rule, is Ben guilty?
Answer A: No, because an undercover officer can never be part of a conspiracy
Answer B: Yes, because the unilateral rule focuses on Ben's agreement and intent, not the officer's true intent
Answer C: No, because conspiracy requires completion of the planned laptop sales
Answer D: Yes, but only if the officer can also be convicted
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: In a unilateral conspiracy jurisdiction, a defendant may conspire with a person who only pretends to agree. Ben agreed to pursue the criminal objective and took planning steps.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The facts specify a unilateral jurisdiction. The defendant's own agreement to commit the crime is enough even though the officer only feigned agreement. C: Conspiracy is complete upon the required agreement, and the target crime need not be completed. D: That is the bilateral intuition. The unilateral model allows liability based on the defendant's culpable agreement even if the other party lacks criminal intent.
lb_rule_reference: In a unilateral conspiracy jurisdiction, a defendant may be guilty of conspiracy even if the other apparent participant is an undercover officer who never truly intended to commit the crime.
