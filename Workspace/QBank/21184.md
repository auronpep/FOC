Outline: Larceny
Question: In a crowded train, Lee quietly slips a wallet from a passenger's coat pocket. The passenger does not feel the taking and is not threatened or touched beyond the slight movement of the coat. What is Lee's best common-law offense?
Answer A: Robbery, because the wallet was taken from the passenger's person
Answer B: Extortion, because the passenger lost property through fear of public transit
Answer C: Larceny, because Lee trespassorily took and carried away the wallet without force or intimidation
Answer D: No offense, because the passenger did not notice the taking when it occurred
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The wallet was personal property taken with intent to steal, but no force or intimidation elevated the taking to robbery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Robbery also requires force or intimidation. A stealthy pickpocket taking without force or fear is not robbery. B: No wrongful threat caused a future transfer. This is a direct stealth taking. D: Lack of awareness prevents robbery on these facts but does not defeat larceny.
lb_rule_reference: Robbery requires larceny from the person or immediate presence by force or intimidation; stealth pickpocketing without force or fear is larceny, not robbery.
