Question: Joshua agreed to buy Rachel's warehouse if he obtained zoning approval for storage use. The contract required Joshua to apply for approval. Joshua changed his mind about the purchase and intentionally never filed the zoning application. He then refused to buy, arguing that the zoning condition did not occur. Which result is most likely?
Answer A: Joshua wins because an express condition that did not occur always excuses performance.
Answer B: Joshua wins because zoning approval was controlled by the city, not by Joshua.
Answer C: Rachel loses unless she proves the city would certainly have granted approval.
Answer D: Joshua cannot rely on nonoccurrence of the condition if his failure to make the required good-faith effort caused it.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A party may not rely on the failure of a condition when that party's own wrongful failure or lack of good-faith efforts caused the nonoccurrence. Joshua was required to apply and intentionally did not. The condition may be excused and his duty to buy may mature.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because literal nonoccurrence can be excused by prevention. B: B is wrong because Joshua controlled the required application step even if the city controlled final approval. C: C is wrong because Rachel need not prove certainty; the key is Joshua's failure to make the required effort.
lb_rule_reference: Prevention doctrine / good-faith efforts
