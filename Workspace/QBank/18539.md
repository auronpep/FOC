Question: In a negligence action arising from a car crash, the plaintiff offers evidence that the defendant carried a large liability insurance policy. The plaintiff offers the evidence only to show that the defendant was probably careless because he expected the insurer to pay any judgment. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because insurance makes negligence more likely.
Answer B: Admit the evidence because Rule 411 applies only in criminal cases.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence only if the policy limits are high enough to create unfair prejudice.
Answer D: Exclude the evidence because liability insurance is not admissible to prove negligence or other wrongful conduct.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 411 bars evidence that a person was or was not insured against liability when offered to prove negligence or wrongful conduct. The offered purpose is the prohibited one. The decisive axis is insurance evidence offered for fault.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This uses exactly the improper propensity-to-carelessness inference that Rule 411 blocks. B: B is wrong because Rule 411 applies in civil negligence actions. C: C is wrong because The exclusion is not limited to high policy limits; the problem is the prohibited purpose.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 411 / liability-insurance barred purpose
lb_student_diagnostic: Student analyzes probative force without applying the insurance purpose screen.
