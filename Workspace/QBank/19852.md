MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Delegation Clause
Question: A workplace-safety agency has statutory authority to require employers to maintain safe equipment and report workplace hazards. Relying on that authority, the agency orders every large employer in the country to provide a national paid-leave benefit unrelated to any specific workplace hazard. The agency says the benefit will improve worker well-being and therefore workplace safety. What is the strongest argument against the agency rule?
Answer A: The rule is vulnerable because the agency is asserting major economic and political power without clear congressional authorization
Answer B: The rule is valid because any policy that improves worker well-being falls within workplace safety
Answer C: The rule is invalid only if Congress expressly prohibited paid-leave rules
Answer D: The rule is valid because paid leave is administered by private employers, not the agency
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A nationwide paid-leave mandate is a major policy choice. The agency is relying on general workplace-safety language rather than a clear congressional authorization for that kind of program.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Major questions doctrine resists using broad or ancillary language to find vast new regulatory power. C: Major questions doctrine can require clear authorization even without an express prohibition. D: The constitutional and statutory question is whether the agency has authority to impose the mandate.
lb_rule_reference: An agency claiming authority over a matter of vast economic or political significance generally needs clear congressional authorization, not a strained reading of broad or ancillary statutory language.
