internal_id: TR-031
Outline_code: 61020102
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Foreseeable plaintiffs
Question: A driver negligently typed a text message while turning into a residential driveway and clipped a curbside mailbox. The impact dented the mailbox but did not endanger anyone on the sidewalk or street. A freelance reporter several blocks away heard the crash described on a police scanner, ran toward the address to film it, and tripped over his own folded tripod while crossing an unrelated alley. The reporter sues the driver for negligence. Is the reporter likely to prevail in a negligence claim against the driver?
Answer A: Yes, because the driver's careless texting was negligent and the reporter was actually injured
Answer B: Yes, because a negligent driver owes a duty to everyone who is later affected by the accident
Answer C: No, because the reporter was outside the foreseeable zone of danger created by the driver's turn
Answer D: No, because the reporter's injury is better analyzed only as a proximate-cause problem, so duty is automatically satisfied
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The driver's texting made nearby road users, pedestrians, and property foreseeably vulnerable. It did not foreseeably endanger a reporter blocks away who later ran toward the scene and tripped over his own equipment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Duty is assessed before the accident by asking whether this plaintiff was foreseeably within the risk field. A remote reporter who later chose to run to the scene was not among the persons endangered by clipping the mailbox. B: The exam-default duty rule does not impose a duty to the whole world. It limits negligence duty to foreseeable plaintiffs within the zone of risk created by the conduct. D: The question asks whether this plaintiff can claim a negligence duty at all. Under the foreseeable-plaintiff tension, the reporter fails at duty before reaching proximate cause.
lb_rule_reference: A negligence duty is owed only to plaintiffs foreseeably within the zone of risk before the accident; hindsight injury does not create duty to a remote plaintiff.
