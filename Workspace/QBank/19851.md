Outline: Privileges or Immunities Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment
Question: A state allows resident citizens to apply for commercial fishing licenses but bars citizens of other states from applying. A nonresident citizen challenges the rule as discrimination in a common calling. An answer choice says the Fourteenth Amendment Privileges or Immunities Clause is the direct and ordinary basis for the claim. What is the problem with that answer choice?
Answer A: It uses the wrong clause; Article IV is the direct lane for discrimination against citizens of other states in fundamental privileges
Answer B: It is correct because the Fourteenth Amendment Privileges or Immunities Clause controls every interstate discrimination case
Answer C: It is wrong because no constitutional provision protects nonresident citizens
Answer D: It is correct only if the plaintiff is a corporation
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff is a nonresident citizen challenging discrimination in a common calling.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Article IV is the standard clause for this nonresident-citizen pattern. C: Article IV protects nonresident citizens regarding fundamental privileges. D: Corporations are not Article IV citizens, and the Fourteenth Amendment P/I clause is not the ordinary route here.
lb_rule_reference: Discrimination by a state against citizens of other states regarding fundamental privileges is analyzed under Article IV Privileges and Immunities, not by default under the Fourteenth Amendment Privileges or Immunities Clause.
