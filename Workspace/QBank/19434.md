Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A prescription medication carried a rare risk of tendon injury. Before a doctor prescribed it, the manufacturer sent the doctor an updated professional warning that clearly described the risk, symptoms, and alternative drugs. The doctor read the warning, discussed the risk with the patient, and prescribed the drug because alternatives were unsuitable. The patient later suffered the tendon injury and sued the manufacturer, arguing that the manufacturer did not send the same warning directly to the patient. The jurisdiction follows the learned-intermediary rule. Is the patient likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim for failure to warn against the manufacturer?
Answer A: Yes, because prescription manufacturers must always warn patients directly
Answer B: No, because the manufacturer adequately warned the prescribing doctor, who served as the learned intermediary
Answer C: Yes, because the patient was injured by the exact risk described in the warning
Answer D: No, because prescription drugs are exempt from all products liability claims
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The manufacturer gave the doctor a clear professional warning before prescription, and the doctor considered it. In a learned-intermediary jurisdiction, that can satisfy the manufacturer's warning duty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under the learned-intermediary rule, an adequate warning to the prescribing physician can satisfy the manufacturer's duty for prescription products. C: A warned-of risk can still occur without a warning defect. The issue is whether the manufacturer gave an adequate warning to the proper audience before the injury. D: Prescription products are not categorically exempt. This failure-to-warn claim fails because the learned-intermediary warning was adequate.
lb_rule_reference: For prescription products, a manufacturer may satisfy its warning duty by giving adequate warnings to the prescribing physician as learned intermediary.
