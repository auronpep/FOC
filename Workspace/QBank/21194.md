Question: An adult under a guardianship order sues in federal court under diversity jurisdiction. The parties dispute whether she has capacity to sue on her own. She is domiciled in State A, while the federal court sits in State B. Which source should the federal court use to determine her capacity?
Answer A: The federal judge's discretionary view of capacity
Answer B: State A's law, because Rule 17(b) points to the individual's domicile
Answer C: State B's law automatically because the federal court sits there
Answer D: Federal common law because capacity is procedural
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal rule supplies the choice mechanism, and that mechanism points to domicile law for an individual.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 17(b) gives a source rule; the judge does not invent one. C: The specific federal rule on capacity points to the individual's domicile, not automatically the forum state. D: The direct federal rule incorporates state law for this capacity question.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 17(b) generally determines an individual's capacity to sue or be sued by the law of the individual's domicile.
