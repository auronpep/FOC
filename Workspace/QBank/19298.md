internal_id: TR-179

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Abuse of process
Question: A supplier filed a valid lawsuit against a retailer for unpaid invoices and obtained a prejudgment writ of attachment for inventory tied to that invoice dispute. After the sheriff levied on that inventory, the supplier told the retailer it would release the levy only if the retailer transferred an unrelated delivery truck that was not part of the invoice dispute. The invoice claim had probable cause and was still pending. The retailer sued the supplier for abuse of process. Is the retailer likely to prevail on the abuse of process claim?
Answer A: No, because the supplier had probable cause to file the invoice lawsuit
Answer B: Yes, because the writ and levy were allegedly used to coerce transfer of unrelated property after process issued
Answer C: No, because abuse of process requires the entire lawsuit to terminate in the retailer's favor first
Answer D: Yes, but only because the invoice lawsuit itself was baseless
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The writ of attachment was process that issued for a legitimate invoice dispute, but the supplier allegedly used the levy as leverage to obtain unrelated property. That is a misuse of process after issuance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Abuse of process can arise from misuse of process after it issues even if the underlying lawsuit had probable cause. C: Favorable termination is an element of malicious prosecution, not abuse of process. Abuse focuses on misuse of process after issuance. D: The facts state the invoice lawsuit had probable cause. Liability, if any, comes from coercive use of the writ and levy, not baseless filing.
lb_rule_reference: Abuse of process targets use of legal process after it issues for an ulterior purpose, even when the underlying claim may have probable cause.
