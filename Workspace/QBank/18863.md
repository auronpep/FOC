Question: John signed and delivered a deed conveying Greenacre to Elizabeth. The deed identified John, Elizabeth, and Greenacre, used present words of transfer, and was signed by John. The printed line labeled “purchase price” was left blank. Elizabeth accepted the deed. John later claimed that no title passed because the price blank was not filled in. Who owns Greenacre?
Answer A: John, because a deed must state the purchase price to be valid.
Answer B: John, because leaving a blank on a printed deed form makes the entire deed void.
Answer C: Elizabeth, because the deed contained the required title-transfer terms and consideration was not required.
Answer D: Elizabeth only if she proves the agreed price by oral testimony.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is the required conveyance substance: parties, present transfer words, sufficient description, grantor signature, delivery, and acceptance. The blank price line does not defeat the deed because consideration is not required for title to pass by deed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the deed need not state a purchase price. B: B is wrong because an unused price blank does not void a deed that otherwise contains the necessary conveyance terms. D: D is wrong because outside proof of price is unnecessary to satisfy the title-transfer lock.
lb_rule_reference: Purchase-price blank does not defeat deed if transfer terms are complete
