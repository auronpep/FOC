MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Diversity Jurisdiction
Question: Three plaintiffs sue two defendants in federal court asserting only state-law negligence claims. Plaintiff 1 is a citizen of Ohio. Plaintiff 2 is a citizen of Texas. Plaintiff 3 is a citizen of Florida. Defendant 1 is a citizen of Georgia. Defendant 2 is a citizen of Florida. The amount in controversy exceeds $75,000 for each plaintiff's claim. The defendants move to dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Should the court dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction?
Answer A: No, because most of the parties are diverse — four of the five parties are from different states, satisfying the spirit of the diversity requirement
Answer B: No, because Plaintiff 3 and Defendant 2 are both Florida citizens, destroying complete diversity
Answer C: Yes, because the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000 for each plaintiff's individual claim, satisfying the statutory minimum
Answer D: Yes, because any plaintiff can cure the defect by dismissing Plaintiff 3 from the action before judgment
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1332 requires complete diversity: every plaintiff must be a citizen of a different state from every defendant. Plaintiff 3 (Florida) and Defendant 2 (Florida) share citizenship. Even though all other parties are diverse from one another, this single overlap eliminates the complete diversity required for § 1332 jurisdiction. The court must dismiss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Diversity jurisdiction requires complete diversity — no plaintiff may share state citizenship with any defendant. Plaintiff 3 (Florida) and Defendant 2 (Florida) share citizenship. That single overlap destroys diversity jurisdiction entirely, regardless of how many other parties are completely diverse. C: Meeting the amount-in-controversy threshold is a necessary but not sufficient condition for § 1332 jurisdiction. Complete diversity is an independent and equally necessary requirement. The amount-in-controversy is satisfied here, but complete diversity is not, so jurisdiction is absent. D: While a plaintiff could potentially dismiss the non-diverse party to cure the defect, the question asks whether the court should dismiss — not whether the defect could theoretically be cured later. At the time the motion is before the court, complete diversity is lacking and the court lacks jurisdiction. The motion to dismiss should be granted unless and until the defect is actually cured.
lb_rule_reference: 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a) — diversity jurisdiction requires complete diversity; no plaintiff may share citizenship with any defendant.
