Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Battery
Question: At a reception, a guest became angry that another guest was eating the last dessert. To humiliate her, he deliberately jerked the plate from her hands. The plate never touched her skin, but it was being held against her body and the sudden snatch startled and embarrassed her. The guest sues for battery. Is the defendant likely liable for battery?
Answer A: No, because the defendant did not touch the plaintiff's skin
Answer B: No, because the plaintiff suffered no physical injury
Answer C: Yes, because the defendant intentionally caused offensive contact with an object closely connected to the plaintiff
Answer D: Yes, but only if the plaintiff saw the defendant reach for the plate before it moved
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Deliberately snatching the plate from the plaintiff's hands is offensive contact through a connected object.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Battery can involve contact with an object closely connected to the body. B: Offensive contact is enough; bodily injury is not required. D: Awareness before contact is not required for battery; that concept belongs to assault.
lb_rule_reference: Battery can be committed by intentionally causing offensive contact with an object closely connected to the plaintiff's person.
