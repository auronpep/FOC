Question: A former federal contractor is convicted in federal court for bribing a federal procurement official. The same conduct also violates a state bribery statute, and state prosecutors later bring charges. The President issues a pardon for "all offenses arising from the federal procurement scheme." The contractor argues that the pardon bars the state prosecution. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The pardon bars the state prosecution because the same conduct produced the federal conviction
Answer B: The pardon does not bar the state prosecution because presidential pardons reach federal offenses, not state crimes
Answer C: The pardon bars the state prosecution only if the President consulted the governor before issuing it
Answer D: The pardon is invalid because the President may issue pardons only after impeachment proceedings
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The state prosecution enforces state criminal law. A presidential pardon can eliminate federal criminal liability but cannot pardon a state offense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The President's pardon power applies to offenses against the United States, not state offenses prosecuted by a state sovereign. C: Consultation with a governor would not convert presidential pardon power into state pardon authority. D: The President may pardon federal offenses outside impeachment. The problem is state prosecution, not timing.
lb_rule_reference: The President's pardon power reaches federal offenses, not state criminal liability or civil consequences imposed by state law.
