Question: Naomi sold a used printing press to Joseph under a written contract. The press was delivered and worked as promised. A separate paragraph stated, “Seller will send Buyer the maintenance booklet within seven days after delivery.” Naomi sent the booklet ten days late. Joseph suffered no loss but refused to make the first installment payment for the press. What is the best result?
Answer A: Joseph may refuse all payment because any broken promise is a condition failure.
Answer B: Joseph may cancel the entire sale because post-delivery booklet promises are always material.
Answer C: Naomi may recover the installment payment because the late booklet was at most a nonmaterial covenant breach.
Answer D: Naomi may recover only if Joseph expressly waived timely booklet delivery before the due date.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The booklet paragraph was a promise, not a condition to Joseph's payment duty. Naomi's late booklet delivery caused no material failure of the bargain. Joseph's payment duty for the delivered and working press had matured; his remedy, if any, is damages for the minor covenant breach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because covenant breach and condition failure are different consequences. B: B is wrong because the breach was minor and did not defeat the transaction. D: D is wrong because waiver is unnecessary when the breach is nonmaterial and payment has matured.
