Question: Congress offers a new small transportation grant to states that adopt a uniform bridge-inspection schedule. The condition is clear, related to the grant, and does not threaten existing funds. A state argues the offer commandeers its legislature because it encourages adopting the schedule. What is the best response?
Answer A: Any financial encouragement is commandeering
Answer B: A clear, related, noncoercive grant condition is generally an inducement, not a command
Answer C: The condition is valid only if Congress also orders state officials to enforce it
Answer D: The condition is invalid because transportation can never receive federal funds
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The state can decline the new grant without losing existing funds.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Noncoercive conditional spending is a permissible inducement. C: Ordering state officials would create a commandeering problem. D: Congress can fund transportation programs subject to constitutional limits.
lb_rule_reference: A valid conditional spending offer is not commandeering merely because it encourages states to adopt a policy; the line is crossed when the offer becomes coercive or otherwise violates Spending Clause limits.
