Question: Peter signed and notarized a deed conveying Oliveyard to Andrew. Peter then placed the deed in his desk and told no one about it. Peter died, and Andrew found the deed while helping clean the house. Andrew recorded it and claimed title. What is the best result?
Answer A: Andrew owns Oliveyard because notarization substitutes for delivery.
Answer B: Andrew owns Oliveyard because finding and recording a signed deed after death creates delivery.
Answer C: Andrew owns Oliveyard because a deed kept in a desk is presumed accepted by the named grantee.
Answer D: Peter's estate owns Oliveyard because Peter never delivered the deed with present intent to transfer title.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is present intent manifested by delivery. Peter signed and notarized the deed, but retained it in his own desk and told no one. Andrew's later discovery and recording occurred after Peter's death and could not create lifetime delivery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because notarization is not delivery. B: B is wrong because post-death discovery and recording cannot cure lack of delivery. C: C is wrong because acceptance cannot be presumed where the delivery lock never occurred.
lb_rule_reference: Signing, notarizing, and retaining a deed in the grantor's desk is not delivery
