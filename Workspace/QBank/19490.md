Question: A recorded easement allowed Joseph, as owner of Lot 1, to cross Rachel's Lot 2 to reach the public road. Years later, Joseph bought adjacent Lot 3, which had no easement over Lot 2. Joseph began using the Lot 2 road to reach both Lot 1 and Lot 3. What is Rachel's best claim?
Answer A: Rachel has no claim because the road still begins and ends in the same physical locations.
Answer B: Rachel may enjoin use of the easement to benefit Lot 3, while the easement may continue for Lot 1.
Answer C: Rachel may terminate the entire easement automatically because any misuse destroys all easement rights.
Answer D: Rachel has no claim unless Joseph's use of the road physically damages Lot 2.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An appurtenant easement may be used only to benefit the dominant estate. Using the easement to serve an additional parcel that was not part of the dominant estate overburdens the servient estate. The usual remedy is to stop the overburdening use, not necessarily to destroy the valid easement for Lot 1.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the benefited land matters, not merely the road's physical route. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because misuse does not automatically terminate the entire easement. D: D is wrong because overburden can exist without physical damage.
lb_rule_reference: Overburden / extra parcel use
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is checking only the route and missing that the easement is now serving extra land.
