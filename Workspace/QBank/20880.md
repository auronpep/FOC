Question: In a federal civil trial, Hannah offers a contract signed by Isaac. Isaac objects that his signature was forged. Hannah offers testimony from a banker who has seen Isaac sign checks for years and recognizes the signature. Isaac offers testimony from a friend who says the signature looks wrong. What should the judge do?
Answer A: Exclude the contract because the judge must resolve the forgery dispute before the jury sees it.
Answer B: Admit the contract if Hannah has offered enough evidence for a reasonable juror to find the signature genuine, leaving the conflicting evidence for the jury.
Answer C: Admit the contract only if Hannah proves genuineness beyond a reasonable doubt.
Answer D: Exclude the contract because only a handwriting expert can authenticate a signature.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Signature authentication is a conditional fact. The judge screens for sufficient evidence to support a finding that the signature is genuine; conflicting evidence is for the jury's weight determination.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the judge does not decide the ultimate authenticity dispute conclusively at this gate. C states the wrong standard. C: A is wrong because the judge does not decide the ultimate authenticity dispute conclusively at this gate. C states the wrong standard. D is wrong because lay familiarity may authenticate handwriting if the familiarity was not acquired for litigation.
