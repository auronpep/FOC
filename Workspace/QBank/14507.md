Question: A seller entered into an agreement to sell a machine to a buyer for $5,000. At the time of the order, the buyer gave the seller a down payment of $1,000. The buyer then built a foundation for the machine at a cost of $250. The seller failed to deliver the machine. The buyer made reasonable efforts to find a similar machine and bought one for $5,500 that did not fit on the foundation. The buyer sued the seller for breach of contract. Which of these amounts claimed by the buyer, if any, could best be described as restitution?
Answer A: A: The $250 cost of the foundation.
Answer B: B: The $500 difference in price.
Answer C: C: The $1,000 down payment.
Answer D: D: None of the claimed amounts.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. The protection of the restitutionary interest restores to a party any benefit conferred to the other party. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 344(c). A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The $250 cost of the foundation conferred no benefit on the seller and would constitute incidental damages rather than restitution (see UCC §2-711-12). In this case, however, the buyer would be entitled to return of the down payment as restitution. B is incorrect. The $500 difference conferred no benefit to the seller and would constitute "cover" (see UCC §2-711- 12). D is incorrect. Under UCC § 2-711(3), on a rightful rejection, a buyer is entitled to a return of any payments made on the goods.
