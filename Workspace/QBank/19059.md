Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: An agency releases a discussion draft saying it may consider future rules for small delivery drones. The draft requests comments and states that it has no legal effect. A drone company sues immediately, arguing that any future rule might be unconstitutional. The company identifies no current compliance duty, penalty threat, or final agency decision. What is the best ripeness analysis?
Answer A: The case is unripe because the agency position is tentative and imposes no immediate legal consequence or hardship
Answer B: The case is ripe because every agency discussion draft can be reviewed immediately
Answer C: The case is ripe because future rules might affect interstate commerce
Answer D: The case is moot because the agency has not yet adopted a rule
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The dispute is abstract and premature. There is no final agency action, credible enforcement threat, or present compliance cost.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Ripeness requires fitness and hardship. A nonbinding draft generally is not fit for review. C: Possible future merits do not create a ripe case now. D: Mootness concerns a once-live dispute that later ended. This dispute has not yet matured.
lb_rule_reference: A challenge can be unripe when the agency position is tentative, lacks legal consequences, and no immediate hardship follows from waiting for final action.
