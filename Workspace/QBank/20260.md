Question: In a federal civil trial, Miriam testifies for the defendant. The plaintiff asks whether Miriam was convicted last year of making a false statement on a federal form, an offense punishable by only six months in jail. Miriam objects that the conviction was not a felony. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the conviction was not punishable by more than one year.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless the plaintiff separately proves that Miriam is generally untruthful.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the conviction required proof of a false statement, so it is admissible regardless of punishment.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Miriam is a criminal defendant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 609(a)(2) requires admission of a conviction involving a dishonest act or false statement regardless of the punishment. The offense required proof of a false statement, so the misdemeanor label does not bar impeachment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the false-statement route is not limited to felonies. B: B is wrong because the conviction itself is the impeachment route. D: D is wrong because Rule 609 applies to witnesses generally, not only criminal defendants.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(a)(2) / misdemeanor false-statement conviction
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests misdemeanor false-statement convictions and repairs the felony-only misconception.
