Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Intentional infliction of emotional distress
Question: A bus driver learned that a passenger had a visible anxiety disorder. In front of a full bus, the driver repeatedly mocked the passenger's condition over the speaker, refused to let her off at three safe stops, and said he wanted to see her "break down." The passenger suffered severe emotional distress requiring treatment. The passenger sues for intentional infliction of emotional distress. Is the passenger likely to prevail?
Answer A: Yes, because the facts show abuse of a carrier position, targeted humiliation, intent or recklessness, and severe distress
Answer B: No, because words can never be extreme and outrageous
Answer C: No, because IIED always requires physical impact
Answer D: Yes, but only because the passenger was physically trapped on the bus
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts cross the ordinary-insult threshold through repeated targeted abuse by a carrier and severe distress.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Words plus special facts, such as abuse of authority and known vulnerability, can support IIED. C: Severe emotional distress can be enough without physical impact. D: Confinement may be relevant context, but the IIED claim turns on outrageous conduct and severe distress.
lb_rule_reference: Extreme and outrageous conduct can be found where a common carrier abuses a position of authority and intentionally or recklessly causes severe emotional distress.
