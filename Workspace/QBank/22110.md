Stem / Fact Pattern: On March 1, a landowner conveyed a tract of realty to her son and daughter as joint tenants with right of survivorship. On April 1, the landowner purported to sell that same tract to a buyer by general warranty deed. The buyer paid cash for the property and was unaware of the prior conveyance to the landowner’s children. The son and daughter recorded their deed on April 3. The buyer recorded his deed on April 5. The son died on April 7. The jurisdiction has a statute that provides as follows: “In determining the priority of conflicting interests in land, the first such interest to have been recorded shall have priority.” Who has priority on April 8?
Answer A: A: The daughter, because the conveyance to the son and daughter was recorded before the conveyance to the buyer was recorded.
Answer B: B: The daughter, because the realty was conveyed to the son and daughter before the conveyance to the buyer was recorded.
Answer C: C: The buyer, because the daughter’s interest did not ripen until after the buyer’s interest was recorded.
Answer D: D: The buyer, because the landowner conveyed to him by general warranty deed.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under this recording statute (a pure race- type statute), the first interest recorded is superior. The daughter’s interest derives from the deed that was recorded on April 3. Since the buyer’s deed was not recorded until April 5, the daughter’s interest had priority. B is incorrect because under a race- type statute, all that matters is the order in which the interests were recorded. C is incorrect because the daughter’s interest derives from the deed that was recorded on April 3. The general warranty deed by which the landowner conveyed to the buyer will determine the buyer’s rights against the landowner. But D is incorrect because the recording statute determines the rights of the buyer and the daughter as against each other.
