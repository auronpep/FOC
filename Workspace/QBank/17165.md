MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Larceny by trick
Question: Donna tells a jewelry store owner that she wants to "borrow" a diamond necklace for a single evening for a charity gala, promising to return it the next morning. The owner, believing her, hands her the necklace without any sale transaction or transfer of title documents. Donna has no intention of returning it and pawns it that night. The owner never agreed to sell the necklace and did not intend to pass title to Donna. Of which crime is Donna most likely guilty?
Answer A: False pretenses, because Donna made a false representation that caused the owner to part with property
Answer B: Larceny by trick, because Donna used fraud to induce the owner to transfer possession of the necklace for a limited purpose, without transferring title
Answer C: Embezzlement, because the owner entrusted the necklace to Donna for a specific purpose
Answer D: Theft by deception, which encompasses both false pretenses and larceny by trick
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Larceny by trick is committed when a defendant uses fraudulent misrepresentation to induce the owner to voluntarily hand over possession of property for a limited purpose, and the defendant then converts it. The owner transferred the necklace as a loan — temporary possession only — based on Donna's false story. Title never transferred; the owner had no intent to sell. Donna received possession through fraud and then converted the property permanently by pawning it. All elements of larceny by trick are satisfied: fraud induced the owner to transfer possession (not title), and Donna converted it with intent to permanently deprive.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: False pretenses requires that the victim, as a result of the fraud, transfer title — ownership — to the defendant. The owner here transferred only possession for a limited purpose (one evening loan); he retained title throughout and never intended to sell. Because only possession transferred, the fraud falls under larceny by trick, not false pretenses. C: Embezzlement requires that the initial possession be genuinely lawful — typically arising from an employment or fiduciary relationship entered into without fraud. Here, the owner's consent was induced by Donna's fraudulent misrepresentation. Fraud-induced consent is vitiated at common law, making Donna's possession trespassory from the start. Trespassory possession obtained through fraud is larceny by trick, not embezzlement. D: The MBE tests common-law distinctions. On common-law questions, the examiner expects the precise category (larceny by trick vs. false pretenses), not a modern statutory merger. "Theft by deception" is a modern statutory concept and is not the correct answer when the question calls for common-law analysis.
lb_rule_reference: Larceny by trick applies when a defendant uses fraud to induce the owner to hand over possession for a limited purpose; the defendant then exceeds that purpose. The entire transaction is larceny by trick because only possession (not title) transferred.
