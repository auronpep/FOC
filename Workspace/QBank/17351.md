Question: During trial preparation, lawyer Rachel wrote a memo ranking three witnesses, noting which testimony she thought was false, and outlining her legal theory for cross-examination. The opposing party seeks the memo, arguing that witness credibility is central to the case. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Compel the memo because credibility is always discoverable.
Answer B: Protect the memo because it reveals counsel's mental impressions, opinions, and legal theories concerning the litigation.
Answer C: Compel the memo if the opposing party promises not to show it to the jury.
Answer D: Protect the memo only if it was also sent to the client as legal advice.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Opinion work product receives heightened protection. A memo ranking witnesses and outlining cross-examination theory reveals mental impressions and legal theories. Need or relevance does not make that material ordinary factual work product. The decisive axis is opinion content.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is opinion-work-product underprotection. Importance does not erase mental-impression protection. C: A is wrong because This is opinion-work-product underprotection. Importance does not erase mental-impression protection. B is wrong because Correct answer C is wrong because A limited-use promise does not convert protected attorney strategy into discoverable material. D is wrong because Work-product protection does not require attorney-client communication to the client.
