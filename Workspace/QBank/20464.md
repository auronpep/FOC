Question: In a federal hit-and-run trial, Thomas testifies that he clearly read the license plate from 100 feet away at night. The defense calls Dr. Luke, an optometrist who examined Thomas the week before the accident, to testify that without corrective lenses Thomas could not read large letters from that distance. The prosecution objects that the testimony is extrinsic impeachment evidence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because extrinsic evidence may never be used to impeach a witness.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because it does not prove Thomas is generally untruthful.
Answer C: Admit the testimony because it attacks Thomas's ability to perceive the license plate, not merely his character for truthfulness.
Answer D: Admit the testimony only if Thomas was first convicted of perjury.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Impeachment is not limited to truthfulness character. Evidence may attack a witness's perception, memory, or capacity when that defect affects the reliability of the testimony. The truthfulness-specific extrinsic-evidence bar does not exclude otherwise admissible extrinsic evidence offered to show impaired perception.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because extrinsic impeachment is not categorically barred. B: B is wrong because impeachment can target perception rather than general truthfulness. D: D is wrong because perception impeachment does not require a conviction.
lb_rule_reference: Perception/capacity impeachment / extrinsic evidence not governed by truthfulness-only bar
