MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A demolition company received a city permit to implode a small industrial building. The implosion was carried out exactly as the permit required, but the shock wave damaged a neighboring structure. The neighbor sues for strict liability based on an abnormally dangerous activity. What effect does the permit have on the strict-liability claim?
Answer A: The permit is a complete defense to strict liability
Answer B: The permit does not automatically bar strict liability if the harm came from the abnormal implosion risk
Answer C: The permit makes the city strictly liable instead of the company
Answer D: The permit matters only if the neighbor proves the company violated it
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim turns on whether the activity was abnormally dangerous and whether the structural damage resulted from that risk. The permit alone does not end the inquiry.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Permission and compliance may bear on negligence or legality, but they do not automatically defeat strict liability for abnormal-risk harm. C: Authorization does not automatically transfer strict liability from the actor to the city. D: The strict-liability question can exist even without a permit violation.
lb_rule_reference: Government permission or regulatory compliance does not automatically bar strict liability for harm within the abnormal risk of an abnormally dangerous activity.
