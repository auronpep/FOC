Outline: Duty to mitigate
Question: Moses contracted to build Hannah a greenhouse for $100,000. Moses expected total performance costs of $82,000, so the expected profit was $18,000. Before Hannah repudiated, Moses had incurred $4,000 in nonrefundable permit and design costs. Hannah then repudiated before materials were purchased. After receiving the repudiation, Moses bought $20,000 of custom glass even though he could have canceled that purchase entirely. The glass has no resale value. How much may Moses recover from Hannah?
Answer A: $42,000, consisting of expected profit, pre-repudiation costs, and post-repudiation glass costs.
Answer B: $22,000, consisting of pre-repudiation costs plus expected profit.
Answer C: $18,000, expected profit only.
Answer D: Nothing, because Moses failed to mitigate after repudiation.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under common law. The breach posture is owner repudiation of a construction contract. The construction formula after repudiation protects the contractor's performance position by awarding costs reasonably incurred before breach plus expected profit, while excluding costs saved or avoidable after repudiation. The formula is $4,000 pre-repudiation cost + $18,000 expected profit = $22,000. The $20,000 glass purchase is excluded by the mitigation gate because it was avoidable after repudiation. The result scope is limited recovery; the answer does not award the contract price or restitution.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it awards avoidable post-repudiation costs, triggering WAL-REM-010, and overcompensates under WAL-REM-008. C: C is wrong because it omits the reasonable pre-repudiation costs already incurred. D: D is wrong because post-repudiation waste does not forfeit the whole claim; it excludes the avoidable cost under WAL-REM-009.
lb_rule_reference: Common law construction expectation — recover pre-repudiation costs plus profit, excluding avoidable post-repudiation expenditures
