Question: A district court denies a manufacturer's motion for summary judgment, finding that a genuine dispute of material fact remains. The manufacturer immediately files a notice of appeal, arguing the court should have granted the motion. Is the denial generally immediately appealable?
Answer A: Yes, because any summary-judgment ruling is final
Answer B: Yes, because the losing movant strongly disagrees with the ruling
Answer C: No, because denial of summary judgment generally is interlocutory and not final
Answer D: No, because summary judgment is unavailable in federal court
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The case continues toward trial, so the final judgment rule is not satisfied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Denial of summary judgment usually keeps the case alive. B: Disagreement does not create appellate jurisdiction. D: Summary judgment is available; this order is generally not immediately appealable.
lb_rule_reference: A denial of summary judgment is generally not a final appealable order because it does not end the litigation.
