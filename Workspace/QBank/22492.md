MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Contracts
Subtopic: Formation of Contracts
Outline: Formation of contracts
Question: A restaurant chain was interested in having a local bakery bake cakes for its restaurants. The bakery also had a small counter and coffee shop for customers and it was afraid to run out of cakes for the coffee shop. The restaurant told the bakery that it recognized the bakery's potential problem, so the restaurant sent the bakery a signed written agreement stating that the restaurant would buy all of the cakes it needed from the bakery and the bakery would sell the restaurant all of the cakes it wanted to sell. The bakery agreed, signed the contract, and sent it back to the restaurant. A week later, the restaurant sent an order for 100 cakes. The bakery said it couldn't fill the order because there was a home football game and it wanted to have a lot of cakes on hand to sell to tailgating parties. The restaurant sued the bakery to enforce the contract. Should the court rule in the restaurant's favor?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the agreement was a valid requirements contract.
Answer B: B: Yes, because both parties signed the written agreement.
Answer C: C: No, because there was no quantity term in the written agreement.
Answer D: D: No, because the contract was illusory.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A contract is illusory and unenforceable when one of the parties doesn't actually agree to do anything. Here, the bakery agreed to sell “all of the cakes it wanted to sell.” The contract is illusory because the bakery could decide it didn't want to sell any, which it did here.A, B, and C are incorrect because this was an unenforceable illusory contract.
