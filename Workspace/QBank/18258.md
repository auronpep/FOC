Question: In a federal robbery prosecution, Matthew testifies for the government that Peter drove the getaway car. Matthew has pleaded guilty to a related charge, and the prosecutor has agreed to recommend a lower sentence if Matthew gives truthful testimony. Peter's lawyer asks Matthew about that agreement. The prosecutor objects that the agreement is not a conviction and therefore cannot be used to impeach. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because only convictions may be used to impeach a witness in a criminal case.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because the agreement shows Matthew's possible bias and motive to please the government.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Matthew has first denied that he hopes for a lower sentence.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the agreement independently satisfies the hearsay exception for statements against interest.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A cooperation or plea agreement may be explored to show a witness's bias, interest, or motive to testify favorably for the government. The issue is not conviction impeachment; it is Matthew's incentive to help the prosecution obtain the promised recommendation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because bias impeachment is separate from conviction impeachment. C: C is wrong because the defense may expose the source of bias without waiting for a denial. D: D is wrong because the agreement is not being used as hearsay for the truth of a factual assertion; it is used to show motive.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 607 / plea agreement as bias
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the distinction between conviction impeachment and plea-deal bias.
