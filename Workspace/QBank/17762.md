Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Moses signed a deed conveying Cedaracre to Miriam and gave it to Aaron, Moses's lawyer. Moses told Aaron, “Hold this in your files while I think about the gift. Do not give it to Miriam unless I later tell you to, and return it if I ask.” Aaron later mailed the deed to Miriam without further instruction from Moses. Miriam accepted and recorded it. Who owns Cedaracre?
Answer A: Miriam, because Aaron physically delivered the deed to her and she recorded it.
Answer B: Miriam, because a lawyer is always treated as an escrow holder rather than the grantor's agent.
Answer C: Moses only if Miriam had actual notice that Aaron lacked authority.
Answer D: Moses, because Moses retained control and never made a present delivery beyond his power to recall the deed.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is whether Moses surrendered control with present transfer intent. Aaron held the deed as Moses's lawyer and subject to Moses's later instruction and right of return. That retained control defeated delivery, so Aaron's unauthorized mailing and Miriam's recording did not pass title.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because later physical transfer and recording cannot cure the grantor's retained control. B: B is wrong because the holder's role depends on the instructions; Aaron was acting for Moses, not as an independent escrow holder. C: C is wrong because notice status does not validate an undelivered deed.
lb_rule_reference: Delivery fails when the deed is left with the grantor's agent subject to the grantor's continuing control
