Outline: Truthfulness
Question: In a federal civil trial, Daniel testifies for the defendant. On cross-examination, Daniel denies falsifying an expense voucher in an unrelated job five years earlier. The question was allowed because the alleged falsification was probative of truthfulness. The plaintiff then offers the old voucher itself solely to prove that Daniel falsified it and is therefore an untruthful person. Daniel objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the voucher because Daniel denied the falsification on cross-examination.
Answer B: Admit the voucher because falsifying an expense voucher is probative of truthfulness.
Answer C: Exclude the voucher because extrinsic evidence is not admissible to prove a specific act solely to attack Daniel's character for truthfulness.
Answer D: Exclude the voucher only if the plaintiff failed to show it to Daniel before cross-examining him.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 608(b) allows discretionary cross-examination about specific acts probative of truthfulness, but it bars extrinsic evidence offered solely to prove that act for a truthfulness-character attack. Daniel's denial does not open the door to admitting the voucher for that sole purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because denial does not permit extrinsic proof for a truthfulness-only act. B: B is wrong because probative value supports possible inquiry, not extrinsic proof. D: D is wrong because the issue is the extrinsic-proof bar, not whether the document was shown before questioning.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(b) / document offered solely to prove truthfulness-specific act
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the difference between asking about impeachment material and offering the material itself as extrinsic proof.
