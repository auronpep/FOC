Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Esther calls Nathan and immediately asks, “In your expert opinion, did the ladder design cause the collapse?” No testimony has yet been offered about Nathan's education, training, experience, or basis for an expert opinion. The manufacturer objects before Nathan answers. What is the best objection?
Answer A: The question lacks foundation because the court has not yet determined that Nathan is qualified to give the expert opinion.
Answer B: The question is improper because only the jury may give opinions about causation.
Answer C: The question is improper only if Nathan's opinion would be written rather than oral.
Answer D: The question is proper because any witness may give expert opinions if the jury can decide the weight.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Before a witness gives expert opinion testimony, the court must decide the preliminary question whether the witness is qualified and whether the opinion is admissible. Without foundation for Nathan's expert qualification, the objection is proper.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because qualified experts may testify to causation when specialized knowledge will help. C: C is wrong because the issue is not whether the opinion is written or oral. D: D is wrong because jury weight does not eliminate the judge's gatekeeping role for expert qualification.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a) / expert qualification before opinion
