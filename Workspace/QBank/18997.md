Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: Two plaintiffs were injured in the same car accident caused by a defendant's negligence. Plaintiff 1 suffered injuries worth $60,000. Plaintiff 2 suffered injuries worth $50,000. The plaintiffs are both citizens of Kentucky; the defendant is a citizen of Tennessee. They join their claims in a single federal complaint and allege that together their claims total $110,000, satisfying the amount-in-controversy requirement. The defendant moves to dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Should the court dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction?
Answer A: No, because the two plaintiffs may aggregate their claims since they arise from the same accident, and the combined $110,000 exceeds $75,000
Answer B: No, because neither plaintiff's individual claim exceeds $75,000, and multiple plaintiffs with separate claims cannot aggregate to meet the threshold
Answer C: Yes, because diversity of citizenship is satisfied — both plaintiffs are Kentucky citizens and the defendant is Tennessee — so the court has jurisdiction regardless of the amount in controversy
Answer D: No, because the two plaintiffs' claims arose from the same transaction, making aggregation impermissible only for unrelated claims
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The general rule is that multiple plaintiffs with separate and distinct claims cannot aggregate those claims to satisfy § 1332's amount-in-controversy requirement. Each plaintiff must independently satisfy the $75,000 threshold. Plaintiff 1's $60,000 claim and Plaintiff 2's $50,000 claim each fall below the threshold. That neither plaintiff independently clears $75,000 means the court lacks diversity jurisdiction over either claim, and the action must be dismissed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Each plaintiff has a separate and distinct personal injury claim. Multiple plaintiffs cannot aggregate their independent claims merely because the claims arise from a common event. Each plaintiff must independently satisfy the AIC threshold. Plaintiff 1's $60,000 and Plaintiff 2's $50,000 each fall below $75,000, so neither independently qualifies. C: Section 1332 has two independent requirements: complete diversity of citizenship AND amount in controversy exceeding $75,000 for each qualifying plaintiff. Complete diversity of citizenship is necessary but not sufficient. Neither plaintiff meets the AIC threshold, so jurisdiction is absent regardless of the citizenship alignment. D: Multiple plaintiffs with separate and distinct claims cannot aggregate regardless of whether those claims arise from the same transaction. The bar on aggregation for separate plaintiffs does not contain a same-transaction exception. The reason each plaintiff must meet the threshold independently is that each holds a separate legal right to recovery, not that their claims are factually unrelated.
lb_rule_reference: Under § 1332, multiple plaintiffs with legally separate and distinct claims cannot aggregate those claims to meet the amount-in-controversy threshold; each plaintiff must independently satisfy $75,000.
