internal_id: TR-229
Outline_code: 61020401
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Cause in fact (actual cause)
Question: A pharmacist negligently failed to call a patient about a delayed refill at noon. Unknown to the pharmacist, the patient had already missed the needed medication dose at 8 a.m. and suffered the relevant medical setback at 10 a.m. Expert testimony shows a noon call could not have changed the setback. Is the noon failure to call an actual cause of the medical setback?
Answer A: Yes, because the pharmacist was negligent in failing to call
Answer B: Yes, because medication cases always use substantial-factor causation
Answer C: No, because the setback occurred before the noon call could have prevented it
Answer D: No, because pharmacists never owe duties to patients
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts show the damage was complete before the negligent omission could affect the outcome. Actual cause is absent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The injury had already occurred before the breach could matter. Breach after the loss cannot cause that loss. B: Substantial-factor analysis does not rescue a breach that occurred after the harm. D: The issue is not categorical duty. It is the timing failure in actual cause.
lb_rule_reference: A breach cannot be an actual cause of damage that had already occurred before the breach mattered.
