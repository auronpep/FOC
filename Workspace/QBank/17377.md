Outline: Clergy and penitent
Question: Martha privately confessed to Pastor Ezra during spiritual counseling that she had forged a receipt. Pastor Ezra later tells prosecutors he is willing to testify because he believes disclosure is morally required. Martha asserts clergy-penitent privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection, because the clergy member's willingness always defeats the privilege.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because privilege belongs only to the clergy member.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if Pastor Ezra's denomination requires secrecy.
Answer D: Sustain the objection, because Martha may prevent disclosure of her confidential spiritual communication even if Pastor Ezra is willing to testify.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The privilege protects the penitent's confidential spiritual communication. A clergy member's willingness to disclose does not automatically defeat the privilege when the penitent properly asserts it. The decisive axis is holder/claimant control.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is holder inversion. Clergy willingness is not automatically dispositive. B: B is wrong because This puts the privilege entirely in the wrong person's hands. C: C is wrong because Denominational secrecy rules may explain expectations, but the evidence privilege is not limited to churches with formal secrecy rules.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / clergy-penitent holder and claimant
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is giving the clergy witness sole control instead of identifying the protected penitent communication.
