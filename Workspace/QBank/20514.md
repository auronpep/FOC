Subtopic: Defenses to Enforceability
Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: Jacob orally promised Leah a permanent right to use a road across Jacob's farm to reach Leah's land. Leah used the road twice but made no improvements, paid no consideration, and did not otherwise change her position. Jacob later blocked the road and asserted the Statute of Frauds. Leah sues to enforce the permanent road right. What is the best answer?
Answer A: Leah can enforce the promise because an easement is merely a service contract.
Answer B: Leah can enforce the promise because she used the road twice.
Answer C: Leah can enforce the promise because all licenses to use land are permanent.
Answer D: Leah generally cannot enforce the oral permanent road right because a permanent easement is an interest in land requiring a writing absent an applicable equitable exception.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A permanent easement is an interest in land and generally falls within the Statute of Frauds. Leah's minimal use, without payment, improvements, or reliance clearly referable to the easement, does not create a strong equitable exception.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an easement is a land interest, not merely a service contract. B: B is wrong because casual use alone is not sufficient part performance. C: C is wrong because a license is generally revocable and does not automatically create a permanent interest.
