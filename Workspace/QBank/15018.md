Question: The homeowners' association of a small real estate development recently issued, through its board, a new landscaping rule for every individually owned lot within the community. The new rule stated that, from now on, owners and occupants may only plant oak trees in their front yards. The intended purpose was to prevent the planting of any more maple trees, which had most commonly been planted around the community in the past. The board believes that oak trees will make the entire real estate development more aesthetically pleasing. After the new rule was passed, one resident bought several maple trees with the intent to plant them in his front yard. The community's governing documents are silent as to the board's ability to impose rules on structures or landscaping that owners may place on individually owned property. Is the new rule enforceable against the owner?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the regulation is reasonably related to furthering a legitimate purpose of the association.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the regulation is not illegal, unconstitutional, or against public policy.
Answer C: C: No, because rules not contained in the governing documents are unenforceable.
Answer D: D: No, because the rule is unreasonable, and the association's governing documents do not authorize its enforcement.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. The association's attempt to restrict owners' landscaping only for aesthetic purposes will not be enforceable against the owner because it will not pass the reasonableness standard.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. On the contrary, the only interest put forth here is aesthetic, with no evidence that the rule is to protect community-owned structures. As an unwritten and unreasonable rule, it will be unenforceable against the owner. B is incorrect. Even if a rule of this kind is not illegal, unconstitutional, or against public policy, it may still be unenforceable if it is unreasonable, which is the applicable standard here. C is incorrect. The rule is unenforceable not simply because it is not in the governing documents, but because it is also unreasonable, as stated above.
