Question: A state runs a specialty license plate program in which every plate design is formally approved by the state, carries the state name, and is understood by observers as a state-issued message. The state rejects a proposed plate design praising a private political movement. What doctrine is most important?
Answer A: Government speech, because the state controls and communicates through the plate designs
Answer B: Traditional public forum, because license plates travel on public roads
Answer C: Viewpoint discrimination, because government may never choose among messages it speaks
Answer D: No doctrine applies because license plates contain no speech
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: If the plate design is the government's own speech, the state may choose its message without opening a private-speech forum.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Public roads do not turn state-issued license plate designs into a traditional public forum. C: Viewpoint limits restrict government regulation of private speech, not the government's own message selection. D: Specialty plate designs can communicate messages; the question is whose speech it is.
lb_rule_reference: When the government itself is speaking, it may choose the content of its own message without creating a forum for private speakers.
