Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Diversity Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff, a State A citizen, sues Defendant, a State B citizen, alleging $120,000 in damages in good faith. After discovery, Plaintiff's proof weakens and Plaintiff recovers only $40,000 at trial. Defendant argues after judgment that the federal court lacked diversity jurisdiction because the final award was below the jurisdictional amount. Is Defendant correct?
Answer A: Yes, because the final recovery controls the amount in controversy
Answer B: Yes, because damages must be proven before the complaint is filed
Answer C: No, because the good-faith amount at filing exceeded the threshold and later recovery below it does not usually destroy jurisdiction
Answer D: No, but only because federal-question jurisdiction exists
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Jurisdiction is measured at filing unless it was legally certain from the start that the claim could not exceed the threshold.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The amount is assessed at filing, not by hindsight from the final award. B: The plaintiff needs a good-faith amount that is not legally certain to fall below the threshold. D: The facts describe diversity jurisdiction, not a federal claim.
lb_rule_reference: The amount in controversy is measured in good faith at the time of filing; later recovery below the threshold usually does not defeat diversity jurisdiction.
