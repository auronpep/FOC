Question: Paul agreed to buy a parcel from Benjamin. The contract stated, “Buyer's duty to purchase is subject to the parcel appraising at no less than $200,000.” The appraisal came in at $190,000. Paul told Benjamin, “I still want to close.” Benjamin refused, arguing that the appraisal condition failed. What is the best result?
Answer A: Paul may waive the appraisal condition because it was inserted for his protection.
Answer B: Benjamin may refuse to sell because failure of any condition excuses both parties.
Answer C: Paul may waive the condition only if he pays more than the contract price.
Answer D: Benjamin may refuse because substantial performance cannot satisfy an appraisal condition.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The appraisal condition protected Paul as buyer by guarding against overpaying. The protected party may waive that condition and proceed. Benjamin cannot invoke Paul's protection to avoid the sale.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because not every condition benefits both parties. C: C is wrong because waiver does not require paying more than the agreed price. D: D is wrong because the issue is beneficiary waiver, not substantial performance.
