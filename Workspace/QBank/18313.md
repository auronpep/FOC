Question: Naomi told Peter he could build a driveway across Naomi's land to reach Peter's cottage. Peter spent substantial money building the driveway, and Naomi watched the work. Naomi later sold her land to Paul. Before closing, Paul walked the property, saw the paved driveway crossing it, and was told by Naomi that Peter had built it after Naomi gave permission. Paul then tried to block Peter's use. What is Peter's best argument against Paul?
Answer A: Paul is never bound because easement by estoppel can bind only the original owner.
Answer B: Paul is bound only if Peter acquired fee title by adverse possession.
Answer C: Paul may be bound because he took with actual or inquiry notice of the estoppel-based access right.
Answer D: Paul is automatically free of the right because the original permission was oral.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An estoppel-based easement or irrevocable license may bind a successor who takes with notice of the circumstances creating the right. Paul saw the driveway and was told of Naomi's permission and Peter's reliance before closing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because successors with notice may be bound by equitable land-use rights. B: B is wrong because Peter claims a use right, not fee title. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because reliance and notice can give effect to an oral permission route in equity.
lb_rule_reference: Estoppel and successor with notice
lb_student_diagnostic: The repair signal is treating the oral-permission defect as dispositive even after reliance and successor notice are supplied.
