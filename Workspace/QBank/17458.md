Outline: Rule of completeness
Question: At trial, Abigail testifies that Noah told her, “I kept the missing tools.” Noah's lawyer asks the court to require Abigail to add that Noah also said, “for safekeeping until Abigail returned.” Abigail denies that Noah said those words, and Noah offers no witness, recording, or other admissible evidence that the additional words were spoken. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Require Abigail to add the claimed words because any defendant may complete an accusation against him.
Answer B: Deny the request unless Noah provides admissible evidence from which the court can find that the completing words were actually said.
Answer C: Require Abigail to add the claimed words because Rule 106 eliminates all foundation requirements for completing statements.
Answer D: Exclude Abigail's original testimony because Noah disputes the rest of the conversation.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 106 can apply to oral statements, but the party seeking completion must provide a basis showing that the completing statement was made and that fairness requires it. If Abigail denies the added words and Noah offers no admissible proof that they were spoken, the court need not require completion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because completeness is not automatic whenever an accusation is offered. C: C is wrong because Rule 106 does not eliminate the need to show that the completing statement exists. D: D is wrong because a dispute over an alleged remainder does not automatically exclude the portion Abigail actually heard.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 106 / proof that completing statement exists
