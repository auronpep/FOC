Question: In a federal negligence action, Paul asks Esther, “Where was the truck when you first saw it?” Esther answers, “It was at the corner, and the driver told me he had plenty of liability insurance.” The driver immediately moves to strike the reference to liability insurance. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant the motion because the insurance reference was volunteered beyond the question and is not admissible to prove negligent driving.
Answer B: Deny the motion because every statement by a driver is automatically admissible for any purpose.
Answer C: Grant the motion and strike Esther's entire answer, including the truck's location.
Answer D: Deny the motion because liability insurance is always admissible in negligence cases.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The question asked for the truck's location. The insurance reference went beyond the question and risks improper use to prove negligence. The court should strike that volunteered portion while leaving the responsive location testimony if otherwise admissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because statements by a party or driver are not admissible for every purpose despite other limits. C: C is wrong because the responsive location testimony need not be stricken. D: D is wrong because liability insurance is generally barred when offered to prove negligence or wrongful conduct.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(1); FRE 411 decoy / volunteered insurance statement
