Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers walk a trained drug dog to the front porch of a house and have the dog sniff around the door for several minutes. The dog alerts. The officers use the alert to obtain a search warrant for the home. Was the dog sniff on the porch a Fourth Amendment search?
Answer A: No, because dog sniffs are never searches
Answer B: Yes, because the officers used a trained dog to explore the home's curtilage for evidence
Answer C: No, because the public may approach a front door to knock
Answer D: Yes, but only if the officers opened the door
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The front porch is part of the home's curtilage, and the investigative dog sniff exceeded the implied license for ordinary visitors.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A home and its curtilage receive special protection; bringing a dog to the porch to investigate is different. C: The implied license allows approaching to knock, not bringing a trained dog to conduct an evidence search. D: The curtilage search occurs even without entering the home.
lb_rule_reference: Bringing a drug-sniffing dog onto the home's curtilage to investigate can be a Fourth Amendment search.
