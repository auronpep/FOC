Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: After Ray shot and killed a man during a robbery, Ray fled to the home of his sister, Connie. Ray told Connie he had just committed murder and robbery and needed to hide. Connie, knowing Ray was telling the truth, hid Ray in her basement for three days, gave him food, and drove him to a bus station so he could flee the state. Connie is charged as an accomplice to murder and robbery. What is the most accurate characterization of Connie's criminal liability?
Answer A: Connie is guilty of murder and robbery as a principal because she substantially assisted Ray
Answer B: Connie is guilty only as an accessory after the fact, which is a separate, lesser offense
Answer C: Connie is not guilty of any crime because she was protecting her brother
Answer D: Connie is guilty of conspiracy to conceal the crime because she agreed to help Ray hide
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An accessory after the fact is a person who, knowing that a felony has been committed, renders assistance to the felon to help the felon escape arrest or punishment. The elements are met: (1) a completed felony (murder and robbery); (2) Connie's knowledge that the felony was committed; (3) personal assistance to the felon (hiding him, feeding him, driving him to the bus station); (4) intent to hinder apprehension. Critically, accessory-after-the-fact is a distinct, generally lesser offense at common law — not the same as liability for the underlying murder or robbery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Accomplice liability requires that the defendant's assistance precede or accompany the commission of the crime. Connie helped Ray only after the murder and robbery were complete. Post-crime assistance, however extensive, does not make one a principal or accomplice to the completed felony. C: Common law does not recognize a family-relation exception to accessory-after-the-fact liability. Some states have enacted limited spousal exceptions by statute, but a sibling relationship does not qualify at common law. Connie's motive, however sympathetic, is not a defense. D: The correct charge is accessory after the fact, not conspiracy. The conspiracy to commit the murder and robbery was already over. Connie and Ray did not conspire to commit a crime; they arranged concealment after the fact. While some obstruction charges might also apply, accessory after the fact is the traditional common-law charge that fits these facts.
lb_rule_reference: An accessory after the fact is one who, knowing that a felony has been committed, receives, relieves, comforts, or assists the felon with the purpose of hindering apprehension or punishment; the accessory is guilty of a separate offense, not the underlying felony
