Outline: Mistake / Lack of Mutual Assent
Question: Mary buys 1,000 clay tiles from Joshua for use in a courtyard. Both parties believe the tiles were made in 2019. After the contract is signed, Mary learns they were made in 2018. The year makes no difference to quality, appearance, durability, or price. May Mary avoid the contract for mutual mistake?
Answer A: No, because the mistake did not materially affect the exchange.
Answer B: Yes, because any wrong fact stated during negotiations makes the contract void.
Answer C: Yes, because the seller is strictly liable for every mistaken belief.
Answer D: No, but only because the contract was for goods rather than services.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A mutual mistake must materially affect the agreed exchange. A harmless mistake about manufacturing year that does not affect quality, use, or value does not justify avoidance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because mistake does not make a contract automatically void. C: C is wrong because mistake doctrine is not strict liability for every erroneous belief. D: D is wrong because the lack of material effect, not the goods label, controls.
