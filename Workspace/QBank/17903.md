Outline: Damages
Question: At a neighborhood meeting, a homeowner falsely said that another resident "is rude, unreliable, and impossible to work with on committees." The statement was spoken, concerned a private dispute, and did not accuse the resident of a crime, disease, professional incompetence, or sexual misconduct. The resident was embarrassed but lost no money. Is the resident likely to recover damages in an ordinary slander claim against the homeowner?
Answer A: Yes, because embarrassment is presumed in every defamation case
Answer B: No, because the spoken statement is not slander per se and no special damages are shown
Answer C: Yes, because any false statement made to a third party creates nominal damages
Answer D: No, because defamation requires written publication
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The statement is oral, falls outside the per se categories, and the plaintiff has not shown specific pecuniary loss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ordinary slander outside a per se category requires special damages, not merely embarrassment. C: Ordinary slander requires special damages unless it fits a per se category. D: Spoken defamation can be actionable as slander, but this claim lacks the necessary damages.
lb_rule_reference: Ordinary spoken defamation outside a per se category requires special damages.
