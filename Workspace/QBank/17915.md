Question: A seller contracted to sell 100 standard chairs to Gideon for $100 each. Gideon wrongfully rejected the conforming chairs before accepting them. The seller still has the chairs, and they are ordinary inventory that can be resold at a reasonable price with reasonable effort. What remedy is the seller most likely barred from using on these facts?
Answer A: Market damages for nonacceptance if proved.
Answer B: A proper resale-damages measure if the resale complies with Article 2.
Answer C: Incidental damages reasonably caused by Gideon's breach.
Answer D: An action for the full $10,000 price.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A seller's action for the price is limited to accepted goods, conforming goods lost or damaged after risk passed, or identified goods that cannot reasonably be resold. Because the chairs were wrongfully rejected before acceptance and are resellable standard inventory, the seller should use damages remedies rather than the full price action.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because market damages may be available. B: B is wrong because a compliant resale can support resale damages. C: C is wrong because proper incidental damages are available with seller remedies.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-709; UCC §2-708
