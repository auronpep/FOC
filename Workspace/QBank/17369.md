Question: In a federal negligence trial, five-year-old Miriam is called to testify about a fall she allegedly saw. On questioning by the judge, Miriam says she does not know what it means to tell the truth, says a lie is “whatever makes my mother happy,” and cannot promise to tell only what actually happened. The proponent argues that Miriam's young age alone cannot make her incompetent. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony because every person is competent to be a witness under the Federal Rules.
Answer B: Admit the testimony and let the jury decide whether Miriam understands the truth.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony because Miriam cannot give an oath or affirmation that impresses on her a duty to testify truthfully.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony only if Miriam also lacks personal knowledge of the accident.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Although competency is broad, a witness must give an oath or affirmation in a form designed to impress a duty to testify truthfully. If the court determines that the child cannot understand or undertake that duty, the testimony may be excluded on that competency ground.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because general competency does not eliminate the oath or affirmation requirement. B: B is wrong because the court must decide whether the witness can undertake the truth-duty before testimony. D: D is wrong because failure to understand the duty to tell the truth is independently sufficient; personal knowledge is a separate foundation issue.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 601 and 603 / inability to understand truth duty
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the difference between broad competency and the separate oath/affirmation truth-duty requirement.
