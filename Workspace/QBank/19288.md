Outline: Nuisance
Question: A warehouse unlawfully stored pallets across a public alley for two weeks. The blockage inconvenienced the public. It also trapped one restaurant's delivery truck inside its loading bay, causing the restaurant to lose several contracted catering jobs. Can the restaurant likely sue for public nuisance?
Answer A: Yes, because the restaurant suffered a special economic injury different in kind from the public's inconvenience
Answer B: Yes, because every member of the public may recover for the same inconvenience
Answer C: No, because economic harm can never be special injury
Answer D: No, because a public alley can never be the subject of nuisance
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The trapped truck and lost contracts distinguish the restaurant's harm from ordinary public delay.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A private public-nuisance suit usually requires special injury. C: Specific economic loss tied to a distinct interference can qualify. D: Obstruction of a public way is a classic public-nuisance setting.
lb_rule_reference: Public nuisance affects a public right; a private plaintiff generally needs special harm different in kind, not merely degree, to recover.
