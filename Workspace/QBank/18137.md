Outline_code: 43020201
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Judicial Review
Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: A federal act prohibited federal executive-branch employees from getting involved in “political management or . . . political campaigns.” The plaintiffs, a group of federal civil servants, attacked the constitutionality of the act. In their complaint, the plaintiffs stated that they wanted to engage in prohibited political activities, although they were not specific as to what they were planning to do. However, they conceded that they had not yet engaged in such activities. The court should find
Answer A: A: the act is constitutional because the plaintiffs are federal employees.
Answer B: B: the act is unconstitutional because it violates the plaintiffs' freedom of expression.
Answer C: C: the act is unconstitutional because it violates the plaintiffs' freedom of association.
Answer D: D: the plaintiff's action should be dismissed.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A case is not yet ripe and is therefore not yet decidable by a federal court if it has not yet become sufficiently concrete to be easily adjudicated. Here, since the plaintiffs have not been specific about the precise acts that they wish to carry out, a court will likely find the case is not yet ripe. When a federal court determines an action is unripe, it must dismiss the action entirely.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A, B, and C are therefore incorrect.
