Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: ClearPath Distribution Co. is a wholesale goods company incorporated in Ohio with its principal place of business in Columbus. ClearPath employs Marguerite, a commissioned sales representative, who lives and operates exclusively in the State of Caldova. Marguerite's job is to solicit orders from Caldova retailers, demonstrate ClearPath products, negotiate contract terms, and transmit signed orders to ClearPath's Columbus office. Marguerite is authorized to make commitments on behalf of ClearPath up to $50,000 per order. She works exclusively in Caldova and has called on 75 Caldova retailers in the past year, generating $1.2 million in Caldova sales for ClearPath. A Caldova retailer alleges that ClearPath breached a contract Marguerite negotiated in Caldova. The retailer sues in Caldova federal court. ClearPath moves to dismiss, arguing that it has no direct contacts with Caldova because Marguerite is an independent contractor and all final contract processing occurs in Ohio. Does the Caldova court have personal jurisdiction over ClearPath?
Answer A: No, because Marguerite is an independent contractor, and the acts of an independent contractor cannot be attributed to ClearPath for personal jurisdiction purposes
Answer B: No, because ClearPath's final contract processing and decision-making occur in Ohio, so any purposeful availment occurred in Ohio, not Caldova
Answer C: Yes, because ClearPath's authorized agent Marguerite regularly conducts business in Caldova on ClearPath's behalf, and her Caldova activities are attributed to ClearPath as minimum contacts with the forum
Answer D: Yes, but only because Caldova has a long-arm statute — without the statute, ClearPath would be immune from jurisdiction based solely on its agent's activities
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Marguerite is ClearPath's authorized sales agent in Caldova — she solicits orders, demonstrates products, negotiates contracts, and has authority to commit ClearPath up to $50,000. Her Caldova activities are ClearPath's purposeful availment of Caldova. *International Shoe* established that a company whose salespeople operate in a state is subject to personal jurisdiction there for claims arising from that business. The breach-of-contract claim arises directly from Marguerite's Caldova-negotiated contract. Both the long-arm step (transacting business in state through agent) and due process (purposeful availment, relatedness) are satisfied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The independent-contractor label does not control the PJ analysis. What matters is whether Marguerite acted as an authorized agent of ClearPath in the forum. Marguerite is authorized to make commitments up to $50,000, solicits orders on ClearPath's behalf, and operates within the scope of her authorized duties. For PJ purposes, the question is whether she was acting as ClearPath's agent — and on these facts she clearly was. The IRS or tort law distinction between employee and independent contractor is not the same as the PJ agency analysis. B: International Shoe* directly addressed this scenario: the company had no offices or stock in Washington, but its salespeople solicited orders there. The Court held that the solicitation of business in the state by agents operating there constitutes the minimum contacts. The relevant contact is not where the final paperwork is processed but whether the company, through its agent, deliberately directed commercial activity into the forum state. ClearPath's choice to have Marguerite operate in Caldova is itself ClearPath's purposeful availment of Caldova. D: The long-arm statute is necessary (it is step one of the analysis) but not sufficient, and it does not create jurisdiction — it only authorizes the court to exercise jurisdiction up to the constitutional limit. More importantly, the framing "without the statute ClearPath would be immune" is incorrect: it implies the statute is the source of the substantive right to jurisdiction. Due process is the constitutional floor; statutes can authorize jurisdiction up to that floor. The correct analysis gives the statute its proper role as the threshold step, not the source of immunity or permission for jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: A corporation is subject to personal jurisdiction in a state where its authorized agent or employee regularly conducts business on its behalf, because the agent's in-state activity is attributed to the corporation. *International Shoe Co. v. Washington* (1945) directly involved salespeople operating in-state. The company's deliberate choice to have agents operate in the forum constitutes purposeful availment.
