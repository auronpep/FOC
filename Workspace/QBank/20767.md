Question: Joseph owned an easement across Mary's land to reach a public road. After a new public road was built next to Joseph's parcel, Joseph used the new road and did not use the easement for 25 years. Joseph never released the easement, never said he was giving it up, and never acted inconsistently with owning it. Mary then blocked the easement path. May Joseph enforce the easement?
Answer A: No, because nonuse for 25 years automatically extinguishes any easement.
Answer B: No, because Joseph's use of a different road conclusively proves abandonment.
Answer C: Yes, but only if the easement was mentioned in Joseph's most recent deed.
Answer D: Yes, because mere nonuse without intent to abandon does not terminate an easement.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Joseph may enforce the easement. Mere nonuse, even for a long period, generally does not abandon an easement unless accompanied by intent to relinquish the right or conduct clearly inconsistent with continued ownership.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because nonuse alone is insufficient. B: B is wrong because using an alternate route does not conclusively prove intent to abandon. C: C is wrong because an existing appurtenant easement does not have to be repeated in every deed to remain enforceable.
lb_rule_reference: Easement nonuse / no abandonment without intent
