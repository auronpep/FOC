Question: A twelve-year-old takes his own skateboard to a public park without his parent's knowledge. While showing off, he crashes into a jogger. The jogger sues the parent, alleging only that the parent is liable because the rider is the parent's child. Is that allegation enough under the general rule?
Answer A: Yes, because parents are always vicariously liable for their minor children's torts
Answer B: Yes, because children cannot be personally liable for negligent acts
Answer C: No, because the parent-child relationship alone is insufficient without direct fault, agency, or a statute
Answer D: No, because tort law never imposes liability for injuries caused by minors
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim pleads only status, not a basis for parent liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Parent status alone generally does not create automatic vicarious liability. B: A child may be liable under the applicable child standard of care. D: Minors can cause actionable tort injuries.
lb_rule_reference: Parents are not automatically vicariously liable for a child's tort merely because of the parent-child relationship.
