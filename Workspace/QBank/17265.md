Question: In a federal assault trial, Mary testifies that she saw Simon strike Thomas. Simon calls John, who says he saw the same encounter. Simon's lawyer asks John, “Was Mary lying when she said Simon struck Thomas?” Mary objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because John saw the encounter.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because one witness may always state whether another witness lied.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if John is related to Simon.
Answer D: Sustain the objection because the question asks John to decide another witness's credibility rather than testify to his own observations.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: John may testify to what he saw, even if that contradicts Mary. But asking whether Mary was lying asks John to opine directly on another witness's credibility, a role for the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because personal observation supports John's own facts, not his judgment that Mary lied. B: B is wrong because witnesses do not generally decide other witnesses' credibility. C: C is wrong because relationship is not the basis for the objection.
