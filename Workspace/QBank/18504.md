Question: A state temporarily bars importation of trees from a region experiencing a plant disease outbreak. The disease is not detectable by ordinary inspection during the relevant period, and infected trees would likely destroy local orchards. The ban ends when reliable testing becomes available. What is the best DCC analysis?
Answer A: The ban is automatically invalid because it distinguishes out-of-state trees
Answer B: The ban is automatically valid because safety is mentioned
Answer C: The ban may survive because the state has a real health purpose and no reasonable nondiscriminatory alternative on these facts
Answer D: Pike balancing applies because all quarantine rules are evenhanded
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts support both the legitimate local purpose and the absence of a workable nondiscriminatory inspection alternative.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A discriminatory health measure may survive if the state proves no reasonable nondiscriminatory alternative. B: The state still must justify discrimination and show that alternatives are unavailable. D: The rule distinguishes imports from a region and must be analyzed as discrimination before any evenhanded-burden balance.
lb_rule_reference: A discriminatory quarantine or exclusion can survive only when the state proves a legitimate local purpose and no reasonable nondiscriminatory alternative.
