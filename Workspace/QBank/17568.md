Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A manufacturer sells a ladder with all rivets properly installed. Months later, a store employee drops the ladder from a loading dock, cracking several rivets, and then sells it without noticing the damage. The ladder collapses because of those cracks and injures a customer. The customer sues the manufacturer in strict products liability. What is the manufacturer's strongest defense?
Answer A: The defect did not exist when the ladder left the manufacturer's control
Answer B: The customer cannot recover because no one can be liable for a defective product after resale
Answer C: The manufacturer is liable because the product was defective when the customer bought it
Answer D: The manufacturer is liable only if the customer had a contract with it
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Strict products liability against this manufacturer requires a defect at the time the product left its control.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Another commercial seller may have liability; the issue is this manufacturer's timing defense. C: That fact does not show the defect existed when the product left the manufacturer. D: Privity is not the key issue in strict products liability.
lb_rule_reference: The product must be defective when it leaves the defendant's control; a later defect caused by someone else can defeat strict products liability against that defendant.
