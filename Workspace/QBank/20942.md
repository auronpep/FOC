Outline: Lay opinion testimony
Question: In a federal slip-and-fall case, Naomi testifies that she saw Solomon leave a mop bucket in a hallway. Naomi is then asked, “Was Solomon negligent?” Solomon objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because lay opinions may embrace ultimate issues.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because Naomi personally saw the bucket.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because the question asks for a legal conclusion rather than a helpful lay description.
Answer D: Sustain the objection because lay witnesses may not testify about anything they personally observed.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A lay opinion must be helpful and rationally based on perception. Naomi may describe what she saw and may give ordinary helpful impressions if appropriate, but “negligent” is a legal conclusion that tells the jury how to apply the law. The court should sustain the objection to that form of question.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because ultimate-issue doctrine does not permit a witness to give an unhelpful legal conclusion. B: B is wrong because personal observation supplies foundation but does not make the legal conclusion helpful. D: D is wrong because lay witnesses routinely testify about facts they personally observed.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 701 / legal conclusion not helpful
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests legal-conclusion overreach and prevents students from treating ultimate-issue language as a free pass.
