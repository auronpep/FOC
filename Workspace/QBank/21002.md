Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Aaron is charged in federal court with possessing a firearm after having been convicted of a qualifying felony. Aaron offers to stipulate that, before the firearm possession, he had been convicted of a qualifying felony. The government instead offers the full judgment showing that the prior felony was for a violent assault. Aaron objects to the name and nature of the prior crime. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the name and nature of the prior crime if Aaron's stipulation fully proves the status element and the details create unfair prejudice substantially outweighing added probative value.
Answer B: Admit the full judgment, because the government may always prove status with the most dramatic available evidence.
Answer C: Admit the full judgment only if Aaron denies that he possessed the firearm.
Answer D: Exclude all evidence of the prior felony, because status crimes are unconstitutional burden-shifting devices.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The prior conviction is relevant to the status element, but Aaron's stipulation may satisfy that element while reducing the probative need for inflammatory details. The court may exclude the name and nature if their unfair prejudice substantially outweighs their added value.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because Rule 403 can limit the form of proof when less prejudicial proof satisfies the point. C: C is wrong because the possession issue does not control whether the prior felony details are unfairly prejudicial. D: D is wrong because the government may prove the status element; the dispute is the form and probative need of the proof.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403 / reduced probative need
