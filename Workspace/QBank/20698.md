Question: Olive intends to burn down a warehouse. She pours gasoline along the loading dock and takes out a lighter. Before lighting it, she sees a police cruiser slowly turn into the alley. Olive runs away only because she fears being caught. She is charged with attempted arson and claims abandonment. Is abandonment likely a defense?
Answer A: Yes, because Olive left before lighting the fire
Answer B: No, because her withdrawal was motivated by fear of arrest after a substantial step
Answer C: Yes, because attempted arson requires actual burning
Answer D: No, but only because arson is a property crime
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Pouring gasoline and preparing to ignite it is a substantial step. Olive stopped because circumstances increased the risk of capture, so the abandonment was not voluntary.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Abandonment must be voluntary and complete. Stopping because police appear is not voluntary renunciation. C: Attempted arson does not require burning. Intent plus a substantial step is enough. D: The analysis would be the same for other target crimes: abandonment fails when it is driven by fear of arrest after a substantial step.
lb_rule_reference: Even where abandonment can be a defense to attempt, it must be voluntary and complete; stopping because of increased risk of arrest is not voluntary abandonment.
