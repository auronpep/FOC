MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Larceny by trick
Question: Bart approaches Rachel and tells her he needs to borrow her bicycle for one afternoon because his own has a flat tire. Rachel agrees and lets Bart take the bike. In fact, Bart has no bicycle of his own, has no flat tire, and has no intention of returning Rachel's bicycle. He rides it to a pawnshop and sells it. Of which crime is Bart most likely guilty?
Answer A: False pretenses, because Bart used a false representation to obtain Rachel's property
Answer B: Embezzlement, because Bart was entrusted with the bicycle by Rachel
Answer C: Larceny by trick, because Bart used fraud to induce Rachel to transfer only possession of the bicycle, retaining title in herself
Answer D: Larceny, because Bart permanently deprived Rachel of her bicycle
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Larceny by trick is committed when a defendant uses a fraudulent misrepresentation to induce the owner to transfer possession of property, where the owner retains title. Rachel agreed only to lend the bicycle — a transfer of temporary possession — based on Bart's false story about a flat tire. Rachel retained ownership throughout; she never intended to sell or permanently transfer the bicycle. Bart obtained possession (not title) through fraud, satisfying larceny by trick. His subsequent sale of the bicycle to the pawnshop does not retroactively convert the initial taking into false pretenses, because the fraudulently induced transfer was of possession only.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: False pretenses requires that title — legal ownership — pass to the defendant as a result of the fraud. Rachel agreed only to lend the bicycle temporarily; she retained title throughout. She transferred only possession, not ownership. When fraud induces the victim to transfer possession only, the crime is larceny by trick, not false pretenses. B: Embezzlement requires that the initial possession be lawful. Here, Rachel's consent to transfer possession was induced by fraud. A consent procured by fraud is vitiated — it is treated as if no consent was given — making the initial possession trespassory. Trespassory initial possession (even if obtained through fraud) is the province of larceny by trick, not embezzlement. D: The taking element of ordinary larceny (trespassory taking) is typically satisfied by stealth or force, not fraud. When fraud is the mechanism by which possession is obtained, the specific subcategory is larceny by trick. The answer here is technically correct that Bart committed a form of larceny, but "larceny by trick" is the most accurate and precise answer the MBE requires when the mechanism of taking was fraudulent inducement of the owner to hand over possession.
lb_rule_reference: If fraud induces the victim to transfer only possession of the property (retaining title), the crime is larceny by trick. If fraud induces the victim to transfer title itself, the crime is false pretenses.
