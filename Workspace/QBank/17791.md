Question: Sarah contracted with Jacob for Jacob to pay Sarah's creditor Moses $9,000 if Sarah delivered all inventory by May 1. Sarah sent Moses a copy of the contract, and Moses immediately extended Sarah's separate loan in reliance on Jacob's payment promise. Sarah later delivered only half the inventory. After that, Sarah and Jacob signed a release of the payment-to-Moses clause. If Moses sues Jacob, which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Moses automatically wins because his rights vested before the release.
Answer B: The release alone cannot defeat Moses's vested rights, but Jacob may assert Sarah's failure to satisfy the inventory condition if it excuses his payment duty.
Answer C: Moses loses because original parties may always release a third-party beneficiary promise.
Answer D: Moses loses because beneficiary reliance is consideration only between Moses and Sarah.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Moses's reliance vested his rights, so Sarah and Jacob could not eliminate his right by a later release. But vesting does not make the beneficiary better off than the contract allows. If Sarah's failure to deliver all inventory was a condition or material breach that excuses Jacob's payment duty, Jacob may assert that defense against Moses.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because vesting bars later release but does not erase contract defenses. C: C is wrong because post-vesting release does not cut off the beneficiary without consent. D: D is wrong because reliance here matters as vesting, not as consideration for the underlying contract.
