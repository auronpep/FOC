Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Settlement negotiations and offers of compromise
Question: Leah sends Boaz a demand letter claiming that Boaz injured her in a bicycle collision and demanding compensation. Boaz replies, “I will pay your hospital bills if you sign a full release of all claims.” Leah refuses, later sues Boaz for negligence, and offers the statement to prove Boaz's liability and the amount of her claim. Which policy-exclusion rule best fits Boaz's statement?
Answer A: Rule 409 only, because the statement mentions hospital bills.
Answer B: Neither Rule 408 nor Rule 409, because Boaz did not expressly admit fault.
Answer C: Rule 408, because the offer to pay hospital bills was conditioned on a full release of a disputed claim.
Answer D: Both rules always apply equally to any statement involving medical expenses after an injury.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 409 covers a naked offer or payment of medical expenses when offered to prove liability. Boaz did more than make a humanitarian medical-payment offer: he conditioned payment on Leah signing a full release after she asserted a disputed claim. That transaction structure is a compromise offer governed by Rule 408, and it is barred when offered to prove liability or amount.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it classifies by subject matter alone. The release condition and disputed claim make the statement a compromise offer, not merely a naked medical-payment offer. B: B is wrong because Rule 408 does not require an express admission of fault. It bars compromise offers and negotiation statements when offered for prohibited purposes. D: D is wrong because Rules 408 and 409 have different triggers and scopes. They are not interchangeable.

lb_student_diagnostic: Student routes by the phrase “hospital bills” instead of asking whether the offer was a compromise exchange for release of a disputed claim.
