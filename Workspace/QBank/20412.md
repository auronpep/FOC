Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Without a warrant, police stand on a public sidewalk and use a thermal-imaging device not commonly available to the public to detect heat patterns inside a suspect's home. The scan reveals unusually high heat in a room, which police use to obtain a warrant for a later search. Was the thermal scan a Fourth Amendment search?
Answer A: No, because officers stayed on a public sidewalk and did not physically enter the home
Answer B: Yes, because the device obtained information about the interior of the home using technology not in general public use
Answer C: No, because heat leaving a home is always abandoned property
Answer D: Yes, but only because the scan later produced contraband
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The home receives the highest Fourth Amendment protection, and the device revealed interior information without a warrant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A search can occur when technology obtains interior home information that could not otherwise be obtained without entry. C: The doctrine protects information about the interior of the home from advanced sense-enhancing technology. D: Whether the scan is a search does not depend on what later evidence is found.
lb_rule_reference: Use of sense-enhancing technology not in general public use to obtain information about the interior of a home can be a Fourth Amendment search.
