Question: A state allows residents to obtain commercial electrician licenses after passing an exam, but it refuses to issue the same licenses to citizens of other states even if they pass the exam. A nonresident citizen challenges the rule. Which constitutional provision is the strongest fit?
Answer A: Article IV privileges and immunities, because the rule burdens a nonresident citizen's pursuit of a common calling
Answer B: Dormant Commerce Clause only, because all job licensing is commerce
Answer C: No constitutional issue exists because states control all occupational licensing
Answer D: The Fourteenth Amendment privileges or immunities clause automatically controls every nonresident case
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff is an out-of-state citizen and the law burdens livelihood, a fundamental privilege under Article IV.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The fact pattern directly discriminates against nonresident citizens in a common calling, which is the Article IV lane. C: State licensing power is limited by Article IV when it discriminates against nonresident citizens regarding fundamental privileges. D: Article IV is the direct clause for discrimination against citizens of other states in fundamental privileges.
lb_rule_reference: Article IV protects out-of-state citizens against discrimination regarding fundamental privileges, including pursuit of a common calling, unless the state has a substantial reason and a close fit.
