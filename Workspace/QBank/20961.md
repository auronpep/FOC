Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: Detective Huang received a tip that a man matching Victor's description was selling drugs near a subway entrance. Huang approached Victor, displayed her badge, and said, "Stop, I need to talk to you — you're not under arrest but I have some questions." Huang patted Victor down for weapons and found none. Without Miranda warnings, Huang asked Victor what he was doing in the area. Victor responded, "I'm waiting for a friend who owes me money." Huang then asked if Victor had any drugs on him and Victor said, "Just a little weed for myself." Victor was subsequently arrested. He moves to suppress his statements. Should the court suppress Victor's statements made during the Terry stop?
Answer A: Yes, because Huang told Victor to stop, which meant Victor was not free to leave and was therefore in custody
Answer B: Yes, because the pat-down transformed the encounter into a custodial situation
Answer C: No, because a Terry investigatory stop does not constitute Miranda custody even though Victor was not free to leave momentarily
Answer D: No, because Huang told Victor he was not under arrest, which negates custody as a matter of law
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Miranda warnings are required only when a person is in custody and subject to interrogation. Miranda custody means formal arrest or restraint on freedom of movement of the degree associated with formal arrest. A Terry stop, though a Fourth Amendment seizure, does not meet this standard. The stop here was brief, conducted in a public place, and Huang explicitly told Victor he was not under arrest. Under *Berkemer v. McCarty* and the Terry line of cases, investigatory detentions do not trigger Miranda. Victor's statements are admissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The "not free to leave" formulation applies to determining whether a Fourth Amendment seizure occurred, not whether Miranda custody exists. Miranda custody requires a higher threshold — the equivalent of formal arrest. Terry stops are Fourth Amendment seizures but not Miranda custody. *Berkemer v. McCarty* confirmed that brief investigatory detentions do not trigger Miranda. B: A pat-down for weapons is authorized by *Terry v. Ohio* as a protective measure and does not convert an investigatory stop into Miranda custody. The pat-down is an additional officer safety measure within the Terry framework, not an arrest or its functional equivalent. D: Telling a person they are not under arrest is relevant evidence bearing on custody but is not dispositive as a matter of law. The test remains objective. A person can be in Miranda custody even if told they are not arrested if the objective circumstances demonstrate arrest-equivalent restraint. The answer overstates the legal effect of Huang's statement.
lb_rule_reference: A Terry investigatory stop does not constitute Miranda custody; the brief, limited detention based on reasonable suspicion is not the functional equivalent of a formal arrest.
