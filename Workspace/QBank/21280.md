Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Consent
Question: Plaintiff and Defendant are both citizens of State A. Plaintiff sues Defendant in federal court on a state-law contract claim for $90,000. The contract says, "The parties consent to federal jurisdiction for any dispute." Defendant answers and says it prefers federal court. Does the federal court have subject-matter jurisdiction because both parties consented?
Answer A: Yes, because parties may consent to any jurisdictional requirement
Answer B: Yes, because the amount exceeds $75,000
Answer C: No, because consent cannot create federal subject-matter jurisdiction
Answer D: No, but only if Defendant raises the objection in the first Rule 12 motion
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The parties are nondiverse and the claim arises under state law. Agreement cannot supply federal SMJ.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Subject-matter jurisdiction cannot be created by consent. B: Amount does not cure lack of complete diversity, and no federal claim appears. D: SMJ can be raised at any time and must be addressed by the court.
lb_rule_reference: Parties cannot create federal subject-matter jurisdiction by consent, waiver, contract, or failure to object; the court must dismiss if jurisdiction is lacking.
