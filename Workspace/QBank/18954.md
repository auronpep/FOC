Question: Martha hired Peter to maintain her orchard for one year, with weekly visits. Peter missed one weekly visit because a bridge was closed, but he returned the next week and the orchard suffered no harm. Martha immediately cancelled the one-year contract and refused to pay for the prior months of work. What is the best result?
Answer A: Martha may cancel because any missed weekly visit is a total breach.
Answer B: Martha may cancel only if Peter admits he had no reason for missing the visit.
Answer C: Peter's missed visit is at most a minor breach on these facts; Martha cannot treat the whole contract as discharged and must pay for matured performance less any proven damages.
Answer D: Martha owes nothing because ongoing service contracts are enforceable only if performance is perfect.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A minor breach does not discharge the other party's remaining duties. Peter missed one visit, promptly resumed performance, and caused no harm. Martha may claim proven damages, but she cannot treat the entire one-year contract as discharged on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every missed installment or visit is a total breach. B: B is wrong because cancellation depends on materiality, not Peter's admission. D: D is wrong because service contracts do not require perfect performance to preserve all payment rights.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law partial breach / materiality
