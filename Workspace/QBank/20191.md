Question: In a federal drug trial, the government offers a packet of powder allegedly seized from Thomas. The seizing officer testifies that he placed the powder in a sealed evidence bag with Thomas's case number. The evidence custodian testifies that the bag stayed overnight in a locked evidence room accessible only to evidence staff. The lab analyst testifies that the seal was intact when she received the bag. Thomas objects that the government did not call every staff member with access to the evidence room. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the packet because every person with access to the evidence room must testify.
Answer B: Admit the packet because the sealed bag, case number, locked-room testimony, and intact seal provide sufficient chain evidence; possible access goes to weight.
Answer C: Exclude the packet because fungible evidence can never be authenticated by chain of custody.
Answer D: Admit the packet only if Thomas stipulates that no evidence-room employee touched it.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Fungible evidence often requires chain-of-custody proof, but the proponent need not call every person with possible access. The sealed bag, case number, controlled storage, and intact seal provide sufficient evidence for admission.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because possible access by staff does not require testimony from every person. C: C is wrong because fungible evidence can be authenticated through chain-of-custody proof. D: D is wrong because a defendant's stipulation is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(a)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student overdemands every possible handler despite a sufficient sealed-chain foundation.
