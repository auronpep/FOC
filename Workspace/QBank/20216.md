Question: Elizabeth held an easement to cross Daniel's land. Daniel asked Elizabeth for permission to place a locked seasonal gate across the route during lambing season each year. Elizabeth agreed and kept a key. Daniel used the gate arrangement openly for 15 years. The jurisdiction recognizes extinguishment of easements by prescription after 10 years of adverse obstruction. Can Daniel extinguish Elizabeth's easement by prescription based on the gate?
Answer A: No, because the obstruction was permissive and did not satisfy the adversity requirement for prescription against the easement.
Answer B: Yes, because any gate across an easement route extinguishes the easement after one year.
Answer C: Yes, because prescription against easements never requires adversity.
Answer D: No, but only because recorded easements are categorically immune from prescription in every jurisdiction.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Prescription against an easement requires an open and adverse obstruction for the prescriptive period. Daniel's gate was installed with Elizabeth's permission, and she retained a key, so the gate did not operate adversely to her easement rights.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a gate does not automatically extinguish an easement. C: C is wrong because adversity is central to prescription. D: D is wrong because the question states the jurisdiction recognizes extinguishment by prescription.
lb_rule_reference: Prescription against easement / adversity requirement
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is counting years and visibility while ignoring permission, the same adversity failure that defeats ordinary prescriptive easement claims.
