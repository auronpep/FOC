Question: A tenant wins a diversity action under a state consumer lease statute. The statute says a prevailing tenant "shall recover reasonable attorney fees." The landlord argues that the federal court should deny fees because federal law has no comparable fee-shifting rule. What should the federal court do about entitlement to fees?
Answer A: Deny fees because attorney fees are always collateral federal procedure
Answer B: Award fees automatically in every diversity case
Answer C: Apply the state fee-entitlement rule because it is part of the state remedy
Answer D: Ask the jury to decide whether fees are fair
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal court should honor the state-created fee entitlement while using federal procedure for the fee request if applicable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: State-law fee entitlement can be part of the substantive remedial scheme in diversity. B: Fee entitlement depends on the governing statute or contract; there is no universal diversity fee rule. D: The legal entitlement is determined by the statute and court; reasonableness may be calculated later.
lb_rule_reference: In diversity, state law generally governs whether a party is entitled to attorney fees under a state statute or contract, while federal procedure may govern how the request is presented.
