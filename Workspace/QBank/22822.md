


BARMATRIX Q#: 22822
Outline_code: 92080105
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: A man sued a company for negligence in federal court. The company moved for summary judgment on the ground that the man had lost an identical claim against it the year before. The court denied the motion. May the company appeal the court's decision?
Answer A: A: No, because the company may not appeal until after a trial on the merits or other deposition resulting in a final judgment.
Answer B: B: No, because the court's decision is an interlocutory order.
Answer C: C: Yes, because a trial will result in undue delay.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the company's original motion was based solely on a legal question.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A denial of summary judgment is not appealable as it does not constitute a final decision within the meaning of 28 U.S.C. §1291. Consequently, the company would need to wait until a trial on the merits or other final judgment before it could appeal. B is incorrect because interlocutory orders refer to temporary or provisional decisions on an issue, and a denial of summary judgment is not such an order since it merely allows the case to proceed. C is incorrect because holding a trial would not result in any sort of undue delay. D is incorrect because the relevant issue did not involve whether the company was arguing a legal or factual question.
