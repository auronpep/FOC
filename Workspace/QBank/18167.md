Question: In a federal slip-and-fall trial, an expert for Esther wants to pour water on a new tile in court to show generally how water can reduce friction. The trial tile is not the same tile from the store, and Esther does not offer the demonstration as a recreation of the accident. The store objects that the demonstration is not identical to the accident. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Exclude the demonstration because any in-court experiment must exactly duplicate the accident.
Answer B: Allow the demonstration as a limited demonstrative aid if the court finds it helpful and not misleading, because it is not offered as a recreation.
Answer C: Admit the demonstration as real evidence of the store's floor condition.
Answer D: Exclude the demonstration unless the jury can take the tile into deliberations.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When a demonstration is offered only to illustrate a general principle, exact duplication of the event is not always required. The court controls the use to avoid misleading the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A overdemands similarity for a limited illustrative use. C misclassifies the demonstration as real evidence. D invents a deliberation requirement.
