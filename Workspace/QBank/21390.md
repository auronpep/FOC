Question: Advocates for housing reform set up tents and sleep overnight in a national park to dramatize homelessness. A park rule bans overnight camping in that area to preserve park grounds and public access. The rule applies to all overnight campers regardless of message. What standard best fits the First Amendment challenge?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny, because the campers intend to communicate a political message
Answer B: O'Brien-style intermediate scrutiny for content-neutral regulation of expressive conduct
Answer C: No First Amendment review, because sleeping can never be expressive
Answer D: Automatic invalidation, because political protests cannot be subject to park rules
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule targets camping impacts, not the message, while incidentally burdening expressive conduct.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Expressive conduct can be regulated by a content-neutral rule aimed at noncommunicative effects. C: Conduct may be expressive depending on intent and audience understanding. D: Political expression is protected but still subject to valid content-neutral time, place, manner, or conduct rules.
lb_rule_reference: Content-neutral regulations of expressive conduct are reviewed under O'Brien-style intermediate scrutiny when the government's interest is unrelated to suppressing expression.
