Question: Miriam and Noah orally agreed that Noah would sell Miriam 400 clay lamps. Noah signed a note stating, “I will sell Miriam 400 clay lamps for delivery on May 1.” The note did not state a price. Noah later refused to deliver and argued that the Statute of Frauds barred enforcement because the writing omitted price. Is Noah correct?
Answer A: No, because a goods writing is not insufficient merely because it omits price if it shows a quantity and otherwise indicates a contract.
Answer B: Yes, because price is the most important term in every goods contract.
Answer C: Yes, because UCC gap-fillers apply only when the contract is oral and outside the Statute of Frauds.
Answer D: No, but only because the lamps were specially manufactured.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: UCC §2-201 does not require every agreed term in the writing. A goods memorandum may omit or incorrectly state terms, but enforcement is capped by the quantity shown. Because Noah's signed note shows 400 lamps and indicates a contract, the missing price does not by itself make the writing insufficient.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because price can be supplied by UCC gap rules when the parties intended a contract. C: C is wrong because gap-fillers and SOF writing sufficiency are separate issues. D: D is wrong because no specially manufactured goods exception is needed if the writing is sufficient.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 / open price separate from SOF
