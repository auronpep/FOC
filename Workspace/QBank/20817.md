Outline: Hearsay Exceptions
Question: In a federal assault trial, the court admits Naomi's 911 call as an excited utterance. In the call, Naomi said, “Jonah hit me with a bottle.” Naomi is unavailable at trial. Jonah offers testimony from Ruth that, two days after the 911 call, Naomi said, “I did not see who hit me.” The prosecution objects that Naomi never had an opportunity to explain or deny the later statement. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude Ruth's testimony because Naomi was not confronted with the later statement.
Answer B: Exclude Ruth's testimony because hearsay declarants cannot be impeached.
Answer C: Admit Ruth's testimony to attack Naomi's credibility as a hearsay declarant, but not as substantive proof unless another rule permits that use.
Answer D: Admit Ruth's testimony as substantive proof that Jonah did not hit Naomi.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When a hearsay statement has been admitted, FRE 806 permits the declarant's credibility to be attacked as if the declarant had testified. The court may admit evidence of the declarant's inconsistent statement regardless of whether the declarant had an opportunity to explain or deny it. The use is impeachment unless a separate substantive route exists.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 806 removes the ordinary opportunity-to-explain requirement for hearsay declarant impeachment. B: B is wrong because hearsay declarants can be impeached. D: D is wrong because impeachment of the declarant does not automatically prove the truth of the later statement.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 806 / impeachment of hearsay declarant
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests hearsay-declarant impeachment as a use-of-material problem and separates credibility use from substantive proof.
