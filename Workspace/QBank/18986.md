Question: Rachel hired Isaac to install custom shelves in a library for $20,000. Isaac installed all the shelves in the correct places, using the correct wood. One shelf was stained slightly lighter than the others, and the difference could be fixed for $400. Rachel refused to pay any part of the price. Which result is most likely?
Answer A: Isaac may recover the contract price reduced by the cost of correcting the minor staining defect.
Answer B: Isaac may recover nothing because the shelves were not exactly as specified.
Answer C: Isaac may recover only in restitution because substantial performance does not apply to construction or installation contracts.
Answer D: Rachel may cancel the entire contract because any visible defect is a total breach.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Isaac substantially performed the installation. A minor and easily correctable defect does not prevent the owner's payment duty from maturing under a constructive-condition analysis. Rachel must pay the contract price less damages for the defect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because strict compliance is not generally required for constructive conditions. C: C is wrong because substantial performance can support recovery on the contract. D: D is wrong because a minor visible defect is not automatically a total breach.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law substantial performance / minor defect
