


BARMATRIX Q#: 22264
Outline_code: 31010400
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Witnesses
Question: A woman sued her psychiatrist for malpractice after she was injured by an adverse drug interaction. The woman's attorney called an expert witness to testify that the psychiatrist was negligent in prescribing a certain drug for anxiety when she knew the woman was already on a certain drug for thyroid problems. According to the expert, it was widely known that the two drugs could not be taken by a patient at the same time without severe adverse effects. The psychiatrist's attorney then cross- examined the expert. During cross, the expert stated that the Adverse Drug Interaction Manual was the most highly regarded book on adverse drug reactions and that most physicians used it as a primary resource. However, the expert herself did not rely on it in making her opinion regarding the psychiatrist's actions. The psychiatrist's attorney then asked the expert to read from a chapter in the book stating that there was no definitive proof that the anxiety and thyroid drugs at issue created an adverse drug interaction in patients. It the expert's reading from the book admissible?
Answer A: A: Yes, for the purpose of impeachment.
Answer B: B: Yes, for the purpose of impeachment and as substantive evidence.
Answer C: C: No, because it is inadmissible hearsay.
Answer D: D: No. because the expert did not rely upon the book.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: In some jurisdictions, a learned treatise can be used on the cross- examination of an expert witness only if the witness relied upon that treatise in forming his or her opinion. FRE 803(18) dispenses with that requirement, however, making the contents of a learned treatise an exception to the hearsay rule to the extent that they are called to the attention of a witness during cross- examination. This provision makes portions of learned treatises both for impeachment and as substantive evidence. A is incorrect because it is under- inclusive. C is incorrect because the above section makes the contents of a learned treatise an exception to the hearsay rule. D is incorrect because the expert did not have to rely upon the learned treatise for it to be admissible.
