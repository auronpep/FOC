Question: Two corporations from different states litigate a state-law warranty claim in federal court. The federal judge believes a single national warranty rule would be more efficient for interstate commerce. No federal statute governs the warranty issue. May the court create a federal common-law warranty rule?
Answer A: Yes, because diversity exists to create national commercial law
Answer B: Yes, whenever the parties are from different states
Answer C: No, the court must apply the governing state substantive law
Answer D: No, because federal courts may not hear warranty cases at all
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: In ordinary diversity litigation, state substantive law governs the state-law claim absent a controlling federal source.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Erie rejected general federal common law for ordinary state-law claims. B: Diversity supplies forum jurisdiction, not federal substantive law. D: A federal court can hear state-law claims under diversity if jurisdictional requirements are met.
lb_rule_reference: Federal diversity courts apply state substantive law to state-created claims and do not create general federal common law merely because a uniform federal rule would be convenient.
