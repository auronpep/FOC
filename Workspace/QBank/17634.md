Question: A newspaper negligently but not recklessly published a false article saying a private homeowner contaminated a creek with illegal dumping. The creek dispute was a matter of public concern. The homeowner proved actual reputational harm and lost consulting work but did not prove actual malice. The homeowner sues for defamation and seeks actual damages only. What fault showing is required for the homeowner's actual damages claim?
Answer A: Actual malice, because the article involved public concern
Answer B: Strict liability, because the statement was false
Answer C: Negligence, because the plaintiff is private and seeks actual damages for a public-concern statement
Answer D: No fault, because actual damages were proven
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The homeowner is private. For actual damages in a public-concern defamation case, negligence is constitutionally sufficient.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A private plaintiff seeking actual damages in a public-concern case needs at least negligence, not actual malice. B: Constitutional defamation doctrine requires fault, at least negligence for this private plaintiff. D: Actual damages do not replace the required fault showing.
lb_rule_reference: A private plaintiff in a public-concern defamation case may recover actual damages on proof of at least negligence, while presumed or punitive damages require actual malice.
