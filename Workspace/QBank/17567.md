Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: In a federal civil trial, Daniel proves that a demand letter to Elijah was properly addressed, stamped, and mailed. Elijah testifies that he never received it. Daniel moves for judgment as a matter of law, arguing that the presumption of receipt conclusively proves delivery. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant the motion because a presumption conclusively proves the presumed fact.
Answer B: Deny the motion because Elijah's denial permits the jury to decide whether the letter was received.
Answer C: Grant the motion unless Elijah produces a postal worker to corroborate nonreceipt.
Answer D: Deny the motion only if Daniel concedes that the letter might have been lost.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The presumption of receipt shifts the burden of production, not the ultimate burden of persuasion. Elijah's testimony is rebuttal evidence. The jury may decide which evidence to believe.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the presumption is not conclusive. C: C is wrong because Elijah need not produce a postal worker to create a jury question. D: D is wrong because Daniel's concession is not required once contrary evidence is produced.
