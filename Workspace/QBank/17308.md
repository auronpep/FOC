MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Foreseeable and unreasonable risk
Question: A company sells a line of portable space heaters with a narrow base. Every heater made to specifications tips over easily when bumped lightly, and a wider base would reduce the tip risk at low cost without impairing the heater's function. A heater tips, ignites nearby paper, and injures a user. What is the best classification of the claimed defect?
Answer A: Manufacturing defect, because the injured user's heater caused the fire
Answer B: Design defect, because the intended design may be unreasonably unsafe compared with a feasible safer design
Answer C: No products claim because the heater worked exactly as designed
Answer D: No products claim because the user was injured by fire rather than by direct contact with the heater
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A linewide tip hazard and feasible alternative point to design defect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The facts attack a hazard shared by all properly made heaters. C: A product can be defectively designed even if manufactured according to its design. D: Fire injury can be caused by a defective product design.
lb_rule_reference: A design-defect claim challenges an intended product design shared by the product line, often by showing an unreasonable risk and a feasible safer alternative.
