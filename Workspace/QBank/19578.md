


BARMATRIX Q#: 19578
Outline_code: 95070600
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Pretrial Conferences and Orders
Question: A man sued a woman in federal court for injuries he sustained in an auto accident. The court ordered the attorneys involved in the action to attend a pretrial conference. Sometime later, the court ordered a second pretrial conference. This time, the court required the man and woman to be present. The man did not want to attend the second conference because he did not want to see the woman any more than he had to.&#10;&#10;Does the man have to attend the second conference?
Answer A: A: No, because a party cannot be ordered to attend a pretrial conference.
Answer B: B: No, because a court cannot order more than one pretrial conference.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the court may require him to be available to consider possible settlement.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the man is the plaintiff in his suit.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 16, a court may order the attorneys to appear for one or more pretrial conferences for several reasons, such as expediting the action, discouraging wasteful pretrial activities, and facilitating settlement. Importantly, under Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(c)(1), “if appropriate, the court may require that a party or its representative be present or reasonably available by other means to consider possible settlement.” In fact, if the man fails to attend, he could be subject to sanctions. Therefore, A and B are incorrect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: D is incorrect because the fact the man is the plaintiff does not change the analysis.
