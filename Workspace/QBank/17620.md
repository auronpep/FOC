Outline: Privileges and Immunities Clause of Article IV, Section 2
Question: A state charges citizens of other states a higher fee for recreational elk-hunting permits. A nonresident hunter who does not hunt for a living challenges the rule under Article IV Privileges and Immunities. What is the state's strongest response?
Answer A: Recreational hunting is generally not a fundamental privilege protected by Article IV
Answer B: Every recreational difference between residents and nonresidents violates Article IV
Answer C: The hunter wins automatically because hunting affects commerce
Answer D: The hunter wins because Article IV protects corporations and hunters equally
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The privilege is recreational, not a common calling or similarly fundamental right.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Article IV protects only fundamental privileges. C: The claim is Article IV, and the asserted privilege is recreational. D: Corporations are not protected, and recreational hunting is not fundamental.
lb_rule_reference: Article IV protects fundamental privileges such as common callings and access to courts; purely recreational benefits, such as recreational hunting, generally are not fundamental privileges.
