Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Arson
Question: After Felix is indicted for arson, police place a cooperating inmate in the same holding cell and instruct the inmate not to ask Felix questions. Felix, unprompted, says, "I should have used less gasoline." The inmate says nothing about the case and later reports the statement. Does admitting Felix's statement violate Massiah?
Answer A: No, because the informant merely listened and did not deliberately elicit the statement
Answer B: Yes, because any informant in the cell after indictment automatically violates the Sixth Amendment
Answer C: Yes, because Felix was in custody and no Miranda warnings were given
Answer D: No, because indicted defendants have no privacy with cellmates
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Sixth Amendment was attached, but Massiah requires deliberate elicitation. Passive listening to an unsolicited statement is not enough.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The doctrine targets deliberate elicitation. Presence alone, with passive listening, is not a violation. C: The question asks Massiah. For Sixth Amendment purposes, the missing element is deliberate elicitation. D: The Sixth Amendment can regulate government agents even in jail. The statement is admissible because it was volunteered, not because the right never applies in cells.
lb_rule_reference: After attachment, a government agent violates the Sixth Amendment by deliberately eliciting statements about the charged offense; merely listening to volunteered remarks is not deliberate elicitation.
