Question: A city negligently failed to repair a broken sidewalk slab near a recreation center. A skater saw the raised slab but tried to jump over it while looking at his phone. He fell and suffered $100,000 in damages. The jurisdiction follows pure comparative fault. The jury assigns 60 percent fault to the skater and 40 percent fault to the city. How much may the skater recover in his negligence claim against the city?
Answer A: Nothing, because the skater was more at fault than the city
Answer B: $40,000, because pure comparative fault reduces damages by the skater's 60 percent fault
Answer C: $60,000, because the skater was 60 percent responsible
Answer D: $100,000, because the city was negligent and joint liability applies
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Pure comparative fault allows recovery reduced by the plaintiff's percentage of fault. Forty percent of $100,000 is $40,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The jurisdiction uses pure comparative fault. A plaintiff who is 60 percent at fault can still recover the defendant's 40 percent share. C: A plaintiff recovers the portion not attributable to the plaintiff. The skater's 60 percent fault is subtracted, not awarded. D: Plaintiff fault reduces recovery under pure comparative fault. Joint liability rules do not erase the plaintiff's own percentage reduction.
lb_rule_reference: Under pure comparative fault, a negligent plaintiff may recover damages reduced by the plaintiff's percentage of fault, even if the plaintiff is more at fault than the defendant.
