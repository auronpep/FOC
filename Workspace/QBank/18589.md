Question: A homeowner's indoor cat had always been calm around guests. During a dinner party, a guest reached down to move the cat away from a chair, and the cat unexpectedly scratched the guest's hand. The guest sues the homeowner on a strict-liability theory. Who should prevail?
Answer A: The guest, because a pet owner is strictly liable for any physical harm caused by the pet
Answer B: The homeowner, because there is no evidence the homeowner knew or should have known of a dangerous propensity
Answer C: The guest, because cats naturally have claws that can scratch people
Answer D: The homeowner, but only if the homeowner proves the guest was negligent
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: For domestic animals, strict liability depends on knowledge of a dangerous tendency abnormal to the animal's class.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Strict liability does not apply to every ordinary domestic-animal injury. C: The ordinary ability to scratch is not enough without a known dangerous propensity. D: The plaintiff lacks the required strict-liability predicate even without shifting the case to the guest's negligence.
lb_rule_reference: An owner of an ordinary domestic animal is not strictly liable for an unexpected injury unless the owner knew or had reason to know the animal had a dangerous propensity.
