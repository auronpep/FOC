Question: Miriam owned land on the shore of a lake. Over 15 years, the lake gradually receded and exposed a band of dry land between the old shoreline and the new shoreline. The recession was natural and gradual. Who owns the newly exposed band of land under the usual rule?
Answer A: The state, because all land once covered by a lake remains public forever.
Answer B: The owner of the opposite shore, because the lake receded in that direction.
Answer C: Miriam, because gradual reliction generally shifts the shoreline boundary and adds the exposed land to the littoral parcel.
Answer D: No private owner, because exposed land created by reliction must be auctioned before anyone owns it.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Miriam owns the exposed band. Reliction occurs when water gradually recedes, exposing new dry land. Under the usual rule, the shoreline boundary moves with gradual reliction, and the owner of the adjoining littoral parcel gains the exposed land.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because gradual reliction can add land to the shore owner's parcel. B: B is wrong because ownership follows the parcel adjoining the exposed band. D: D is wrong because no auction is required under the usual reliction rule.
lb_rule_reference: Reliction / gradual recession of water
