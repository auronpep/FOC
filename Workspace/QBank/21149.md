Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Commerce power
Question: Congress makes it a federal crime to possess any firearm within a certain distance of a school. The statute contains no requirement that the firearm moved in commerce and is justified only by findings that guns near schools reduce learning and long-term productivity. What is the strongest constitutional problem?
Answer A: The statute regulates noneconomic local possession without a jurisdictional commerce hook
Answer B: The statute is valid because education always substantially affects the national economy
Answer C: The statute is valid because all firearms are instrumentalities of commerce
Answer D: The statute is invalid because Congress may never regulate firearms
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The statute reaches local possession and relies on attenuated economic effects rather than a commerce category.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: That reasoning would convert commerce power into a general police power. C: The statute as stated lacks a movement or commerce connection. D: Congress may regulate firearms when a valid federal power hook exists.
lb_rule_reference: A federal statute regulating local gun possession near schools exceeds commerce power when it lacks an economic activity link, jurisdictional element, or other commerce category support.
