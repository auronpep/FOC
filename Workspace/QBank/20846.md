Question: Mary retained Isaac to create a “board-approved investor report.” The signed services agreement did not define board-approved. The court admitted evidence that both parties used that phrase to mean a report containing revenue, churn, and cash-runway tables in the format the board had adopted. Isaac delivered a report without cash-runway tables, and the board refused approval. If the court adopts that meaning of “board-approved,” what is the likely legal effect?
Answer A: Mary cannot rely on the adopted meaning because explanatory evidence affects admissibility only, not duties.
Answer B: Isaac's report may breach the agreement if it lacked a table required by the adopted meaning.
Answer C: Mary must prove that Isaac fraudulently hid the cash-runway requirement from the writing.
Answer D: Isaac wins because any board-approval clause gives the contractor absolute discretion to decide what the board should approve.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that once an ambiguous term is interpreted, the adopted meaning supplies the content of the contractual duty. The trigger facts are the undefined “board-approved” phrase, the admitted shared meaning, and the missing cash-runway table. The decisive lock is call type. This is not asking whether the evidence comes in; it asks the legal effect after meaning is adopted. Isaac may have breached if his report omitted a required component.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because meaning evidence is not isolated from the duties it explains. C: C is wrong because fraud is not required for ambiguity interpretation. D: D is wrong because the board-approval phrase is not a license for Isaac to rewrite the board's requirements.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law approval clause / same facts legal effect
