MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: False imprisonment
Question: A seller told a buyer that a used food truck was "the best little truck in the county" and "a money machine for anyone with hustle." The buyer inspected the truck, saw its repair records, bought it, and later earned less than expected. The seller did not hide defects or state any specific false fact. Is the buyer likely to prevail on a fraud claim against the seller?
Answer A: Yes, because the truck did not earn as much as the seller predicted
Answer B: No, because the seller made puffing statements rather than actionable factual misrepresentations
Answer C: No, because fraud claims never apply to sales of used property
Answer D: Yes, because reliance is presumed whenever a buyer hears a sales pitch
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The seller gave general praise and predictions without concealing defects or making a specific false factual assertion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Vague optimism and sales puffery are not specific false statements of material fact. C: Fraud can apply to used-property sales if there is an actionable misrepresentation; the issue here is puffery. D: Fraud requires justifiable reliance on an actionable fact; reliance on vague puffery is not enough.
lb_rule_reference: Vague opinion or puffery usually is not an actionable false statement of material fact for fraud.
