Question: Parents refuse a lifesaving blood transfusion for their minor child. A state court authorizes treatment after doctors testify that the child will likely die without it. The parents argue substantive due process gives them an absolute veto. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The parents have an absolute constitutional right to block all treatment
Answer B: The state may protect the child's life despite parental objection
Answer C: No due process issue exists because parents have no liberty interest in childrearing
Answer D: The case is governed only by the Dormant Commerce Clause because blood products move in commerce
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The state's compelling child-protection interest can justify intervention in lifesaving medical care.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Parental liberty does not include an absolute veto over lifesaving care for a child. C: Parents do have protected liberty interests; the issue is limits and state justification. D: The dispute concerns parental liberty and child safety, not interstate commerce regulation.
lb_rule_reference: Parents have a liberty interest in directing the care and upbringing of children, but that interest does not give an absolute veto over child-safety or lifesaving medical regulation.
