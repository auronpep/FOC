Question: In a signed writing, a retired general contracted to purchase a 25-foot travel trailer from a trailer company for $15,000, cash on delivery no later than June 1. The general arrived at the trailer company's sales lot on Sunday, May 31, to pay for and take delivery of the trailer, but refused to do so when he discovered that the spare tire was missing. The trailer company offered to install a spare tire on Monday when its service department would open, but the general replied that he did not want the trailer and would purchase another one elsewhere. Which of the following is accurate?
Answer A: A: The general had a right to reject the trailer, but the trailer company was entitled to a reasonable opportunity to cure the defect.
Answer B: B: The general had a right to reject the trailer and terminate the contract under the perfect tender rule.
Answer C: C: The general was required to accept the trailer, because the defect could be readily cured.
Answer D: D: The general was required to accept the trailer, because the defect did not substantially impair its value.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. The UCC perfect tender rule allows for the general to have rejected the non-conforming trailer that did not have a spare tire, but the company had the right to cure the defect because the time for delivery had not yet expired. B
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. Although the general's initial rejection was proper under the perfect tender rule, the company's reasonable notice of its intention to cure the defect by the next day precluded the general from terminating the contract. C is incorrect. When the delivery of goods is somehow defective, the buyer may reject all, accept all, or accept any of the commercial units and reject the rest. The seller does have a right to cure, but the buyer can still reject if the seller cannot cure within the original delivery time frame. D is incorrect. The general was permitted to reject the trailer for failing to conform to the contract; there is no requirement that the defect substantially impair the value in order for the buyer to be able to reject.
