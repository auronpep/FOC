Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Roles of judge and jury
Question: In a federal drug prosecution, the government offers a key found in Joseph's backpack, asserting that it opens the locker where narcotics were found. The government says a later witness will testify that the key opens the locker. Joseph objects that the key is irrelevant until that connection is proved. What is the court's best response?
Answer A: Exclude the key permanently because relevance cannot depend on later testimony.
Answer B: Admit the key subject to later proof connecting it to the locker, and allow Joseph to move to strike if the connection is not made.
Answer C: Admit the key only if the judge first personally tests it in the locker.
Answer D: Submit the key to the jury only after instructing that the key conclusively opened the locker.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When relevance depends on a later fact, the court may admit the evidence conditionally, subject to later proof sufficient for a reasonable juror to find the connecting fact. If the proof never arrives, the opponent may seek to strike it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because conditional relevance may depend on later connection. C: C is wrong because the judge need not personally test the key. D: D is wrong because the jury is not required to accept the connecting fact as conclusive.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b)
