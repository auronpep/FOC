MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers illegally entered Kai's garage and saw stolen bicycles. They then applied for a warrant. The affidavit relied only on what they saw during the illegal entry and contained no other information linking the bicycles to the garage. The warrant issued, officers returned, and seized the bicycles. Kai moves to suppress. Does independent source save the bicycles?
Answer A: Yes, because officers eventually obtained a warrant
Answer B: Yes, because the second entry was lawful on its face
Answer C: No, because the later warrant was based solely on information from the illegal entry
Answer D: No, because independent source never applies to physical evidence
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Independent source requires genuinely independent lawful information. Here, the affidavit had no facts apart from what officers illegally observed. The doctrine does not save the evidence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The warrant was based only on tainted observations from the illegal entry. It was not independent. B: The warrant itself was a fruit of the illegal entry. A later step is not independent if its factual basis is tainted. D: Independent source can apply to physical evidence. It fails here because the source was not independent.
lb_rule_reference: The independent-source doctrine admits evidence only when the later lawful source is genuinely independent of the illegality.
