Question: The jurisdiction applies the same implied-easement-by-prior-use test whether the easement is claimed by the grantee or by the grantor for retained land. Hannah owned Field 1 and Field 2. For years, she used a visible irrigation ditch across Field 2 to water crops on Field 1. The ditch was permanent and reasonably necessary for Field 1. Hannah sold Field 2 to Daniel by a deed that did not mention the ditch and retained Field 1. May Hannah continue using the ditch across Field 2?
Answer A: No, because an implied easement can never be reserved for a grantor's retained land.
Answer B: No, because the ditch was used by Hannah before severance and therefore was only a personal habit.
Answer C: Yes, because under the stated rule the apparent, continuous, reasonably necessary ditch use may be implied for Hannah's retained Field 1.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Field 1 is completely landlocked.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The question states that the jurisdiction applies the same prior-use test for implied grants and implied reservations. The irrigation ditch was visible, continuous, and reasonably necessary to Field 1 when Hannah severed Field 2, so the prior-use route supports an implied easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the stated jurisdictional rule allows implied reservation under the same test. B: B is wrong because pre-severance use by a common owner is the source of the quasi-easement. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because complete landlock is not required for prior-use implication.
lb_rule_reference: Prior-use implication in favor of grantor under stated rule
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is applying a categorical anti-grantor rule despite the stated jurisdictional rule.
