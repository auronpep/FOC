Question: Mary crossed an unused strip of city-owned parkland every day for 20 years to reach a bus stop. The jurisdiction's prescriptive period for private land is 10 years, but no statute authorizes acquiring prescriptive rights against government land. The city later fenced the strip. What is the best result?
Answer A: Mary has no prescriptive easement because prescriptive rights generally cannot be acquired against government land absent statutory authorization.
Answer B: Mary has a prescriptive easement because 20 years is twice the private prescriptive period.
Answer C: Mary owns the strip by adverse possession because the city did not object for 20 years.
Answer D: Mary has an easement by necessity because the bus stop is useful.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Public land is generally protected against acquisition by prescription unless a statute allows it. The facts state that no statute authorizes prescriptive rights against the city-owned parkland.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the private prescriptive period does not control government-owned land when prescription is barred. C: C is wrong because title by adverse possession is even less available against public land on these facts. D: D is wrong because convenience to a bus stop is not necessity and there was no severance of a landlocked parcel.
lb_rule_reference: No prescription against public land absent authorization
lb_student_diagnostic: The repair signal is applying private-land prescription mechanically to government property.
