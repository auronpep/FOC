Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Joshua sues a bus company after a bus mirror allegedly struck his shoulder. At trial, Joshua offers a mirror from an unrelated bus in another city to show what “bus mirrors look like.” He offers no testimony that the mirror is the same type, size, mounting style, or height as the mirror on the bus that allegedly struck him. The bus company objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the mirror because any bus mirror helps the jury imagine the accident.
Answer B: Admit the mirror only if Joshua first proves the mirror came from the same manufacturer as the bus involved.
Answer C: Exclude the mirror only if the bus company proves the mirror is misleading beyond a reasonable doubt.
Answer D: Exclude the mirror because Joshua has not shown enough similarity or connection to make this mirror probative of the accident.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A physical exhibit offered as a comparator must have a sufficient connection or similarity to the disputed object. Without evidence that this mirror resembles the accident mirror in relevant respects, it lacks probative value.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an exhibit is not admissible merely because it helps the jury visualize a general category. B: B is wrong because same manufacturer might help but is not the only possible foundation; the problem is the complete lack of relevant similarity. C: C is wrong because the opponent does not bear a beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 104(b)
lb_student_diagnostic: Treating illustrative usefulness as a substitute for a relevant foundation.
