Question: Lydia sues Aaron in federal court for failing to exercise an option before the deadline. Lydia proves that she mailed Aaron a deadline reminder two weeks before the option expired. Aaron testifies that he never received it. Lydia asks the judge to instruct the jury that the reminder was received because the mailing was proven. Which instruction is proper?
Answer A: The jury must find the reminder was received because mailing was proven.
Answer B: The jury may infer receipt from the mailing evidence but must decide receipt after considering Aaron's denial.
Answer C: The jury may consider the mailing evidence only if Lydia introduces every other letter sent to Aaron that month.
Answer D: The jury may consider the reminder only to impeach Aaron's denial, not as evidence of receipt.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Proof of mailing may support an inference or presumption of receipt, but the jury is not required to find receipt after contrary evidence. The jury weighs the mailing proof and Aaron's denial.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the presumption is not conclusive. C: C is wrong because the rule of completeness does not require every other letter. D: D is wrong because the mailing evidence is substantive evidence relevant to receipt.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 301 / mailing presumption
