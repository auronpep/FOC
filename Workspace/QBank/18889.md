Question: Jacob owned land bordering a river. Over many years, the river gradually and naturally wore away 30 feet of Jacob's bank. The change was slow and imperceptible from day to day. Jacob claims that he still owns the 30-foot strip as if the river had not moved. Is Jacob's claim correct?
Answer A: Yes, because water movement never changes a legal boundary.
Answer B: Yes, because gradual erosion affects possession but not title.
Answer C: No, but only if the river movement was caused by a sudden flood.
Answer D: No, because gradual erosion generally moves the riparian boundary and the owner loses the eroded land.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Jacob's claim is not correct. Gradual natural erosion generally shifts the riparian boundary as the water slowly wears away the land. The doctrine is the counterpart to accretion: gradual gains and gradual losses usually move the boundary.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because gradual water movement can change riparian boundaries. B: B is wrong because erosion affects the boundary, not merely physical possession. C: C is wrong because sudden movement is avulsion; this question states gradual erosion.
lb_rule_reference: Erosion / gradual loss of riparian land
