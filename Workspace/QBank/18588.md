Subtopic: Defenses to Enforceability
Outline: Mistake / Lack of Mutual Assent
Question: Noah buys an antique wagon from Rachel. Both believed at signing that the wagon had its original wheels, a fact that materially affected the price. A month later, Noah learns the wheels are modern replacements. After learning this, he keeps using the wagon for his business for another six months and makes three scheduled payments before seeking rescission. What is Rachel's best argument against rescission?
Answer A: Mutual mistake never makes a contract voidable.
Answer B: Noah ratified the contract by continuing to use the wagon and make payments after learning the truth.
Answer C: Rachel must prove that she intentionally deceived Noah.
Answer D: Noah's right to rescind expired the moment the contract was signed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A mutual mistake may make a contract voidable, but the power of avoidance can be lost by ratification after discovery. Noah continued using the wagon and making payments after learning the wheels were not original.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because mutual mistake can make a contract voidable. C: C is wrong because fraud is not required for mutual mistake; Rachel's argument is ratification. D: D is wrong because the right to avoid does not expire at signing; post-discovery conduct is the relevant timing.
lb_rule_reference: Ratification after discovery
