Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Mary was buying Lakeacre from Joseph. Mary authorized Peter, her closing agent, to receive the deed for her at closing. Joseph signed the deed and handed it to Peter, saying, “This deed is delivered for Mary.” Peter accepted it on Mary's behalf and later gave it to Mary. When did delivery occur?
Answer A: Only when Mary personally touched the deed after Peter gave it to her.
Answer B: Only when the deed was recorded after closing.
Answer C: When Joseph delivered the deed to Peter as Mary's authorized agent, because delivery to the grantee's agent can satisfy the delivery lock.
Answer D: Never, because an agent can negotiate a contract but cannot receive a deed for a buyer.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is delivery to the grantee or the grantee's authorized agent. Mary authorized Peter to receive the deed, and Joseph delivered it to Peter for Mary. Physical handoff to Mary herself and recording were not required for delivery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because personal physical receipt by the grantee is not required when an authorized agent receives the deed. B: B is wrong because recording is not the delivery event. D: D is wrong because an agent may receive a deed for the grantee.
lb_rule_reference: Delivery to the grantee's authorized agent can satisfy deed delivery
