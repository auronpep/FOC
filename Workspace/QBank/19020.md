Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Paul agreed to provide music and equipment for Lydia's festival for $20,000. Paul expected total costs of $14,000 and an expected profit of $6,000. Before Lydia wrongfully canceled, Paul had spent $5,000 on nonreturnable equipment rentals. Paul avoided the remaining $9,000 in costs. What is Paul's proper expectation recovery?
Answer A: $5,000, because Paul can recover only nonreturnable expenses.
Answer B: $6,000, because Paul can recover only profit.
Answer C: $11,000, because Paul recovers incurred costs plus expected profit.
Answer D: $20,000, because Lydia canceled the event.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The proper expectation formula awards Paul the costs caused by the breach that he already incurred plus his expected profit, while excluding avoided costs. Paul recovers $5,000 in nonreturnable costs plus $6,000 expected profit, for $11,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it omits expected profit. B: B is wrong because it omits the nonreturnable cost caused by the contract. D: D is wrong because full price ignores the $9,000 in costs Paul avoided.
lb_rule_reference: Common law expectation damages; incurred cost plus profit minus saved cost
