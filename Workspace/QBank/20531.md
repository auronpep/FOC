Outline: Duty to mitigate
Question: Ruth had six months remaining on an employment contract with a publisher, with $48,000 in salary still unpaid. The publisher wrongfully discharged Ruth. Ruth spent $900 on reasonable travel, résumé, and interview expenses while looking for comparable work. She then obtained substantially similar editing work for the same six-month period that paid $36,000. How much should Ruth recover for the discharge?
Answer A: $48,000, the unpaid salary under the original contract.
Answer B: $12,000, the salary differential only.
Answer C: $36,900, the replacement earnings plus the search expenses.
Answer D: $12,900, the salary differential plus reasonable mitigation expenses.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under employment/common-law contract rules. The breach posture is wrongful discharge, and the loss category is unpaid wages subject to mitigation offset plus reasonable mitigation expense. The formula is $48,000 unpaid contract salary − $36,000 comparable replacement earnings + $900 reasonable search expenses = $12,900. The limiting gate is mitigation/offset, and the result scope is partial recovery after crediting comparable earnings. The answer does not award restitution or emotional-distress damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores the offset for comparable replacement earnings. B: B is wrong because it omits the reasonable expense Ruth incurred trying to mitigate. C: C is wrong because it uses the replacement earnings as the recovery baseline rather than as an offset against the unpaid contract salary.
lb_rule_reference: Common law employment damages — unpaid wages minus comparable earnings plus reasonable mitigation expenses
