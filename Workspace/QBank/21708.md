Outline: Scope
Question: Abraham owned Lot 1 and Lot 2. He conveyed Lot 1 to Moses and granted “to Moses, as owner of Lot 1, a right to cross Lot 2 to reach the public road.” Moses later sold Lot 1 to Sarah. Sarah's deed did not mention the crossing right. The owner of Lot 2 refuses to let Sarah use the crossing. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Sarah cannot use the crossing because the easement was personal to Moses.
Answer B: Sarah cannot use the crossing because appurtenant easements pass only if repeated in every later deed.
Answer C: Sarah can use the crossing only by proving strict necessity at the time of Abraham's original conveyance.
Answer D: Sarah can use the crossing because the easement was appurtenant to Lot 1 and passed with the dominant estate.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement appurtenant benefits land, not merely a person. It generally passes automatically with the dominant estate even if the later deed does not mention it. The grant was to Moses “as owner of Lot 1,” and the crossing benefited Lot 1.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the wording ties the right to land ownership. B: B is wrong because appurtenant easements generally transfer with the land. C: C is wrong because strict necessity is not required where an express appurtenant easement already exists.
lb_rule_reference: Easement appurtenant / dominant estate transfer
