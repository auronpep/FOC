Question: Samuel conveyed Oliveacre “to Moses, on condition that no gambling occur on the land; if gambling occurs, Samuel has the right to reenter and retake the premises.” Moses allowed gambling on the land. Before Samuel took any action, Moses conveyed Oliveacre to Aaron. What did Aaron receive?
Answer A: Nothing, because Moses's estate automatically ended when gambling occurred.
Answer B: Fee simple absolute, because Moses's deed to Aaron did not repeat the condition.
Answer C: Fee simple subject to condition subsequent, subject to Samuel's right of entry.
Answer D: A life estate measured by Moses's life.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Moses held a fee simple subject to condition subsequent. Breach did not automatically end Moses's title. Because Samuel had not exercised the right of entry, Moses could transfer the defeasible estate to Aaron. Aaron takes subject to Samuel's outstanding right of entry.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because automatic termination is the fee simple determinable rule. B: B is wrong because a later deed cannot eliminate an outstanding condition by omitting it. D: D is wrong because the deed does not measure the estate by any life.
lb_rule_reference: Fee simple subject to condition subsequent / transfer after breach
