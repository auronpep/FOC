Question: In a federal robbery trial, a witness identifies Gideon as the robber. The defense offers testimony from another witness that the alley where the robbery occurred had only one dim lamp. The prosecutor objects that the lighting evidence does not prove the identifying witness was wrong. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the lighting evidence because it does not prove the identification was mistaken.
Answer B: Exclude the lighting evidence unless the defense proves the lamp was not working.
Answer C: Admit the lighting evidence because it has some tendency to affect the reliability of the identification; its strength is for the jury.
Answer D: Admit the lighting evidence only if Gideon testifies that the alley was dark.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Lighting conditions are probative of a witness's ability to perceive and identify a person. The defense need not prove the identification was actually wrong before offering the lighting evidence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because evidence affecting perception can be relevant without conclusively disproving the identification. B: B is wrong because a dim lamp can be probative even if not completely out. D: D is wrong because Gideon's testimony is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401; FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Student demands that perception evidence disprove the identification before admission.
