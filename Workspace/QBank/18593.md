Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal civil case, Jacob offers testimony that he received a call from a number assigned to Elijah and that the caller identified himself as Elijah while discussing details only Elijah would likely know. The court finds enough evidence for the jury to find Elijah was the caller. Jacob then argues that because the call is authenticated, every statement in the call is automatically admissible for its truth. Which statement is correct?
Answer A: Jacob is correct because authentication proves both identity and truth of all statements in the call.
Answer B: Jacob is correct only if the call was recorded.
Answer C: Jacob is incorrect because authentication is a threshold showing that the call is what he claims; hearsay and other rules may still limit the statements.
Answer D: Jacob is incorrect because authentication of telephone calls is never allowed by circumstantial evidence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Authentication establishes that the evidence is what the proponent claims. It does not by itself make every statement in the call admissible for its truth. Hearsay, relevance, privilege, and other limits may still apply.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because authentication is not a truth guarantee. B: B is wrong because recording does not automatically make all statements admissible for truth. D: D is wrong because circumstantial evidence can authenticate telephone calls.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901; independent admissibility limits
lb_student_diagnostic: Authentication-as-truth misconception.
