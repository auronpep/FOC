Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: In a federal contract trial, Sarah offers an email allegedly sent by Isaac accepting a delivery change. Sarah produces testimony that the email came from Isaac's usual address and discussed details only Isaac and Sarah knew. Isaac objects that Sarah has not proved by a preponderance of the evidence that he actually wrote it. Which ruling is most proper?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the proponent must prove authorship by a preponderance before the email may be admitted.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because email authorship can be proved only by the sender's testimony.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the email is automatically authenticated if it came from Isaac's usual address.
Answer D: Overrule the objection if Sarah has produced evidence sufficient to support a finding that Isaac sent the email; the jury may decide actual authorship.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The proponent's threshold burden for authentication is to produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims. The address and distinctive contents may satisfy that production burden. The jury decides whether Isaac actually wrote it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it converts the threshold production burden into a preponderance persuasion burden. B: B is wrong because authorship may be shown circumstantially. C: C is wrong because an email address alone does not automatically authenticate the message; the question turns on sufficient supporting facts.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901 / production burden versus persuasion burden
