Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: In a federal diversity action governed by State X contract law, Sarah claims that Isaac received a written demand for payment. State X law provides that proof of proper mailing creates a presumption of receipt and places the burden of persuasion on the alleged recipient to prove nonreceipt. Sarah proves proper mailing, and Isaac denies receipt. How should the federal court determine the effect of the presumption?
Answer A: By federal common law, because presumptions are always procedural in federal court.
Answer B: By the Federal Rules of Evidence alone, because the case is being tried in federal court.
Answer C: By State X law, because state law supplies the rule of decision for the claim.
Answer D: By asking the jury whether applying the presumption would be fair.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: In a civil case where state law supplies the rule of decision, state law governs the effect of a presumption regarding a claim or defense. Because State X contract law governs the claim, State X's burden effect controls.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the federal forum alone does not override the state-law presumption rule in this setting. B: B is wrong because the federal rule sends the court to state law when state law supplies the rule of decision. D: D is wrong because the effect of the presumption is a legal rule for the court, not a fairness choice for the jury.
