


BARMATRIX Q#: 22825
Outline_code: 94010300
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: A man who is a citizen of State A sued a retailer located in State B. The man claimed $100,000 in damages, claiming that he was injured by a coffee pot sold by the retailer. The retailer brought in as a third- party defendant a supplier that had provided the coffee pot, alleging that the supplier had a duty to indemnify the retailer for any damages recovered by the man. The supplier was incorporated in State B, with its principal place of business in State C. The supplier asserted a $75,000 counterclaim against the retailer for breach of the contract covering the coffee pots. The retailer moved to dismiss the counterclaim for lack of subject matter jurisdiction. Should the court grant the motion to dismiss?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the counterclaim is based on a separate contract between the retailer and supplier.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the retailer and supplier are citizens of State B.
Answer C: C: No, because there is supplemental jurisdiction over the supplier's counterclaim.
Answer D: D: No, because the supplier's claim meets the required amount in controversy.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Here, the plaintiff's claim is in federal court based on diversity jurisdiction. Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 14, if a defendant in such an action brings an impleader claim against another party by claiming that the other party must indemnify the defendant for any of the plaintiff's damages, the federal court has supplemental jurisdiction over the claim and the lack of diversity between the defendant and the third party is irrelevant. At the same time, the other party may bring any counterclaims the party has against the defendant. Consequently, the federal court would have supplemental jurisdiction over the supplier's counterclaim. Therefore, A and B are incorrect. D is incorrect because the amount in controversy would not be an issue since the court would have supplemental jurisdiction under Fed. R. Civ. P. 14.
