Question: A state allows public employees to swap shifts for religious observance if another qualified employee voluntarily agrees and public services remain covered. The rule is available for religious observance and comparable secular conflicts. A taxpayer sues, arguing that any religious accommodation establishes religion. What is the best Establishment Clause response?
Answer A: The accommodation is unconstitutional because any rule that helps religious practice establishes religion
Answer B: The accommodation is not automatically invalid because it is neutral, voluntary, and does not coerce participation or leave services uncovered
Answer C: The accommodation is valid only if religious employees receive priority over all secular scheduling needs
Answer D: The accommodation is invalid only if a court finds the religion untrue
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule accommodates religious practice without compelling others to participate or privileging religion in a way that imposes an undue burden.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Establishment Clause does not forbid all religious accommodations. C: Religious accommodation is not an automatic trump over all other interests or third-party burdens. D: Courts do not decide whether a religion is true; they evaluate constitutional effects such as neutrality, coercion, and burdens.
lb_rule_reference: Government may sometimes accommodate religion without violating the Establishment Clause, but accommodations cannot impose undue burdens or coerce religious practice.
