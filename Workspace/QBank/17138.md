MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Esther is charged with stealing a gold cup from a market in Jerusalem. The thief wore a blue cloak. To prove Esther was the thief, the prosecutor offers testimony that two days later a person wearing a blue cloak was seen in a different town, with no evidence connecting that person to Esther or to the cup. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony because the thief also wore a blue cloak.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because, without a link to Esther or the stolen cup, it does not make a consequential fact more probable.
Answer C: Admit the testimony unless Esther proves she did not own a blue cloak.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony only if blue cloaks are common.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The testimony lacks a logical connection to Esther or the theft. It does not merely have weak probative value; it fails to connect the observed person to a fact of consequence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a shared color, without a connection to Esther or the item, is not enough. C: C is wrong because Esther need not disprove ownership of a blue cloak before the prosecutor supplies a probative link. D: D is wrong because even rarity would not cure the absence of a connection to Esther unless the facts supplied that link.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401
lb_student_diagnostic: Student over-admits evidence based on surface similarity without a case-specific link.
