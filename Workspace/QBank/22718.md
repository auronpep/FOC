Question: Tattoo parlors were illegal in State A and had been since the early 1950s. A local bar owner started a grassroots organization to legalize tattoo parlors in the state and lobbied the State A legislature to pass regulations so that tattoo parlors could operate and compete with State B tattoo parlors. During legislative hearings on the issue, the owner presented evidence regarding the economic impact of tattoo parlors, the loss of business to State B, and the change in public attitudes toward tattoos. At the same time, the owner admitted that he had been giving illegal tattoos in the back of his bar for several years. Ultimately, the state legislature passed a law allowing and regulating tattoo parlors in State A. The law included a provision taking away the owner's liquor license for two years because he had broken the tattoo law in his bar earlier. The man challenged the new law in federal court as unconstitutional. Should the court find the new law unconstitutional?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the new law violates the Privileges and Immunities Clause.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the new law is a bill of attainder.
Answer C: C: No, because a liquor license is a privilege granted by the state.
Answer D: D: No, because the loss of the owner's liquor license is rationally related to the owner's earlier violation of the law.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A bill of attainder is a law that punishes a person or group without a judicial trial. Bills of attainder are barred for both federal and state legislatures in Sections 9 and 10 of Article I. Here, the law specifically takes away the owner's liquor license without a trial, so it is unlawful as a bill of attainder. A is incorrect because Privileges and Immunities concerns discrimination against nonresidents. Here, the liquor license revocation had nothing to do with state residency. C and D are incorrect because the law is an unconstitutional bill of attainder.
