Outline: Lay opinion testimony
Question: In a federal extortion trial, John testifies that he worked beside Paul for three years and spoke with him daily. The prosecution plays a short recorded call and asks John whether the caller's voice sounds like Paul's. Paul objects that John is not a voice-identification expert and is not completely certain. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because voice identification always requires an expert.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because John is not completely certain.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if John heard Paul make the recorded call in person.
Answer D: Overrule the objection if John's opinion is based on his prior familiarity with Paul's voice; uncertainty goes to weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A lay witness familiar with a person's voice may identify it based on that familiarity. John's daily work conversations with Paul supply a basis. Lack of complete certainty affects the weight of the testimony, not its admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because lay voice identification may rest on familiarity. B: B is wrong because uncertainty affects weight. C: C is wrong because John need not have heard the call live if he has enough prior familiarity with Paul's voice.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 701 and 901 / lay voice identification based on familiarity
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests voice identification as lay opinion and distinguishes certainty from admissibility.
