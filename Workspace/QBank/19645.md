Question: In a federal employment-discrimination trial, Jeremiah, a current manager for the defendant company, testifies that the plaintiff's work was poor. The plaintiff asks Jeremiah whether he still works for the defendant and hopes for a promotion. The defendant objects that employment status is not probative of truthfulness. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because employment and promotion interest may show Jeremiah's bias or motive to favor the defendant.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because bias must be proved by a conviction or reputation evidence.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because an employee is presumed to tell the truth about the employer.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Jeremiah has already made a prior inconsistent statement.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness's employment relationship and hope for promotion may show bias or interest. The evidence need not prove untruthfulness character; it helps the jury evaluate the witness's possible motive to favor the employer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because bias is not limited to conviction or reputation routes. C: C is wrong because no such presumption insulates an employee witness. D: D is wrong because bias may be explored even without a prior inconsistent statement.
lb_rule_reference: Bias impeachment / employment relationship
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests employment-based bias and the trap of treating bias as truthfulness character.
