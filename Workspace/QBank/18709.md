Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal negligence trial, Sarah calls Levi, expecting him to testify that a delivery truck entered the intersection after the light turned red. Levi instead testifies that the truck entered on a green light. While Levi is still on the stand, Sarah shows him a signed but unsworn statement he gave to an investigator two days after the accident, in which he said the truck entered on red. Levi denies making the statement. Sarah offers the statement only to impeach Levi. The defendant objects that Sarah called Levi as her own witness. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the statement because a party may not impeach a witness the party called.
Answer B: Admit the statement for impeachment only, subject to a limiting instruction on request.
Answer C: Admit the statement as substantive proof that the truck entered on red because it is inconsistent with Levi's testimony.
Answer D: Exclude the statement because prior inconsistent statements are never usable unless given at trial.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 607 allows any party, including the party that called the witness, to attack the witness's credibility. The signed statement was not given under penalty of perjury at a trial, hearing, or deposition, so it is not substantively admissible under the federal prior-statement route. It may be used to impeach Levi, with a limiting instruction if timely requested.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the old voucher rule is gone; a party may impeach its own witness. C: C is wrong because an ordinary unsworn prior inconsistent statement is not automatically substantive evidence under the federal rule. D: D is wrong because prior inconsistent statements may be used for impeachment even if not given at trial.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 607 and 613 / impeachment of own witness with prior inconsistent statement
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests use of impeachment material by the calling party and the impeachment-only limit for an unsworn prior inconsistent statement.
