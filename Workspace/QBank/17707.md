Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: Mary sued Thomas for negligence after a truck collision and lost after a full trial. Judgment was entered for Thomas. Mary then sues Thomas again based on the same collision, this time alleging strict liability for a defective truck part and seeking the same injury damages. Is Mary's second suit barred?
Answer A: Yes, because claim preclusion bars later theories from the same transaction that could have been raised earlier
Answer B: No, because strict liability is a different legal theory
Answer C: No, because issue preclusion requires actual litigation of strict liability
Answer D: Yes, but only if Thomas won on appeal
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The parties, judgment, and transaction are the same; changing legal theory does not avoid claim preclusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Claim preclusion uses the claim or transaction, not only the legal label. C: The question is claim preclusion, which can bar claims that could have been raised. D: Appellate success is not a universal prerequisite to preclusion.
lb_rule_reference: A valid final judgment on the merits between the same parties bars claims that were or could have been raised from the same transaction.
