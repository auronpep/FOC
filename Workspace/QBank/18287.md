Outline: Res ipsa loquitur
Question: A hardware store rented a folding ladder to a homeowner. The homeowner carried it home, altered the lock pins to make it fit in a closet, and later fell when the ladder collapsed. The homeowner cannot show whether the collapse came from a store defect or the altered pins. What is the best res ipsa analysis?
Answer A: Res ipsa clearly applies because ladders never collapse unless the seller was negligent
Answer B: Res ipsa is likely unavailable because the accident may have resulted from the plaintiff's own handling after rental
Answer C: Res ipsa makes the store strictly liable for all rented equipment
Answer D: Res ipsa applies only if a statute required the store to inspect every ladder
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The circumstances do not strongly point to negligence within the store's responsibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The plaintiff's alteration is an obvious competing cause. C: The doctrine is an evidentiary inference, not strict liability. D: The doctrine is not limited to statutory violations.
lb_rule_reference: Res ipsa is weak or unavailable when the plaintiff's own handling is a plausible cause of the accident.
