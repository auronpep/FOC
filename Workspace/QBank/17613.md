MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: At a crowded station, Ira sees a wallet-shaped outline in a commuter's jacket pocket. Ira reaches into the pocket intending to steal the wallet. The pocket is empty because the outline was a folded pamphlet sewn into the lining. Ira is arrested before withdrawing his hand and is charged with attempted larceny. Is factual impossibility a defense?
Answer A: Yes, because no property was actually present to steal
Answer B: No, because Ira intended larceny and took a direct step toward taking the supposed wallet
Answer C: Yes, because attempt liability requires completion of every element except escape
Answer D: No, but only because the station was crowded
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Ira tried to take property he believed was in the pocket. The absence of the wallet is a factual circumstance preventing completion, not a legal defense to attempt.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Factual impossibility is not a defense to attempt. If the facts had been as Ira believed, taking the wallet would have been larceny. C: Attempt exists precisely to punish conduct short of completion when intent and a sufficient act are present. D: The crowd is not the doctrinal reason. The reason is that factual impossibility is no defense.
lb_rule_reference: Factual impossibility is not a defense to attempt when the defendant intended to commit a crime and would have committed it if the facts were as the defendant believed.
