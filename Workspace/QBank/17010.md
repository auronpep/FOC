Question: Paul owned Lot 1, which benefited from a recorded appurtenant easement across Lot 2. Paul mortgaged Lot 1. The mortgage was later foreclosed, and John purchased Lot 1 at the foreclosure sale. The deed from the foreclosure sale did not mention the easement. The owner of Lot 2 argues that John did not acquire the easement. Who is correct?
Answer A: The owner of Lot 2, because foreclosure sales never transfer appurtenant easements.
Answer B: John, because an appurtenant easement generally passes with the dominant estate even if the foreclosure deed does not separately mention it.
Answer C: The owner of Lot 2, because appurtenant easements pass only by voluntary warranty deed.
Answer D: John, but only if he proves strict necessity for access to Lot 1.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement appurtenant benefits the dominant land and generally transfers with that land. A foreclosure sale of the dominant estate normally carries appurtenant benefits unless the governing documents provide otherwise. John acquired Lot 1 and its appurtenant easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because foreclosure of the dominant estate can transfer appurtenant benefits. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because the transfer need not be a voluntary warranty deed. D: D is wrong because the easement is express and appurtenant; John need not prove necessity.
lb_rule_reference: Appurtenant easement passes with dominant estate through foreclosure
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating foreclosure as an event that strips all easement rights, rather than asking whether the easement burdens or benefits the foreclosed parcel.
