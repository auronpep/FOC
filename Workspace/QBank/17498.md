Outline: Psychotherapist-patient
Question: Rachel files a federal civil action seeking damages for severe emotional distress. In her complaint and expert disclosure, Rachel relies on her therapy diagnosis with Dr. Mark to prove the extent of her injury. The defendant seeks communications with Dr. Mark directly bearing on that claimed condition. Rachel asserts psychotherapist-patient privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection categorically, because psychotherapist-patient privilege can never be waived.
Answer B: Overrule the objection to the extent Rachel has put the same mental condition and therapy diagnosis at issue.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Rachel is a criminal defendant.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because all therapy records are automatically public once a lawsuit is filed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A privilege holder can waive psychotherapist privilege by affirmatively placing the protected mental condition or therapy diagnosis at issue. The waiver is not unlimited; it reaches the condition and communications fairly implicated by the claim. The decisive axis is issue tender, not mere relevance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This ignores waiver. Recognized privileges can be waived by the holder's litigation conduct. C: C is wrong because The issue-tender principle is not limited to criminal defendants. D: D is wrong because This overstates waiver. Filing a lawsuit does not expose every therapy record regardless of scope.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / psychotherapist privilege waiver by putting condition at issue
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is either treating privilege as absolute or converting limited issue-tender waiver into a global record waiver.
