Subtopic: Remedies
Outline: Duty to mitigate
Question: Esther contracted to cater Paul's conference for $24,000. Esther had not yet bought food or hired extra staff. Her expected cost of performance was $16,000, leaving expected profit of $8,000. Paul repudiated and canceled the conference. After receiving the repudiation, Esther bought $10,000 of perishable food and paid $1,000 to store it, even though she knew the conference was canceled and could have avoided both expenses. The food spoiled. How much may Esther recover from Paul?
Answer A: $19,000, consisting of expected profit plus the food and storage costs.
Answer B: $8,000, her expected profit.
Answer C: Nothing, because Esther failed to mitigate after repudiation.
Answer D: $24,000, the contract price.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under common law. The breach posture is anticipatory repudiation of a services contract. Because Esther had not yet incurred performance costs when Paul repudiated, the expectation formula is the contract price minus costs saved: $24,000 price − $16,000 saved performance costs = $8,000 expected profit. The $10,000 food purchase and $1,000 storage expense are excluded by the mitigation gate because they were avoidable post-repudiation costs. The result scope is limited recovery of expected profit only.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it awards avoidable post-repudiation costs, triggering WAL-REM-010. C: C is wrong because waste after repudiation does not forfeit all damages under WAL-REM-009. D: D is wrong because the contract price would ignore the $16,000 in costs Esther saved by not performing.
lb_rule_reference: Common law anticipatory repudiation — injured service provider may not recover avoidable costs incurred after repudiation
