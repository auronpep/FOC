Question: Two negligent drivers collided at an intersection and caused one indivisible injury to a pedestrian. The pedestrian's total damages are $200,000. A jury assigns 20 percent fault to the pedestrian, 50 percent to Driver A, and 30 percent to Driver B. The jurisdiction uses comparative fault and joint and several liability for defendants after reducing for plaintiff fault. In the pedestrian's negligence action, what is the maximum amount the pedestrian may collect from Driver A?
Answer A: $100,000, because Driver A was assigned 50 percent fault
Answer B: $160,000, because the total damages are reduced by the pedestrian's 20 percent fault and Driver A may be liable for the full recoverable judgment
Answer C: $60,000, because Driver B's 30 percent share must be subtracted from Driver A's obligation
Answer D: $200,000, because joint and several liability ignores plaintiff fault
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Plaintiff fault reduces $200,000 to $160,000. Because the defendants are jointly and severally liable for the indivisible harm, the pedestrian may collect that full recoverable amount from Driver A, subject to contribution between defendants.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under joint and several liability, any defendant may be required to pay the full plaintiff-recoverable amount, not merely that defendant's allocated share. C: Contribution between defendants is separate from the plaintiff's collection right under joint and several liability. D: The full recoverable judgment is reduced by the plaintiff's own fault before collection from any defendant.
lb_rule_reference: For indivisible harm under joint and several liability, a plaintiff may collect the full recoverable judgment from one defendant after reducing for the plaintiff's own fault, with contribution handled among defendants.
