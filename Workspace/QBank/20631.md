Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Mary wrote and signed a letter to Martha stating, “This writing conveys my store at 4 Bethany Street to you now.” Mary handed the letter to Martha intending an immediate transfer, and Martha accepted. The letter was not captioned “deed,” was not on a deed form, and was not notarized. Who owns the store?
Answer A: Mary, because the writing was only a letter.
Answer B: Mary, because the writing was not notarized.
Answer C: Martha, because the writing used present words of conveyance and satisfied the deed-formality gates.
Answer D: Martha only if she first pays consideration.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed even though the document is labeled as a letter. The decisive validity gate is whether the writing contains the required conveyance elements, including present words of transfer, property identification, grantee identity, and grantor signature. Mary's signed writing conveyed the store to Martha now, and the informal label and lack of notarization do not defeat validity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because legal effect turns on the instrument's operative language, not its caption. B: B is wrong because notarization is not required for baseline deed validity between the parties. D: D is wrong because consideration is not required for a delivered deed.
lb_rule_reference: Operative words / deed label not required
