Question: Hannah sues Ezra in federal court under diversity jurisdiction, alleging a State A negligence claim and seeking damages for a back injury. State A recognizes a physician-patient privilege for confidential treatment communications, but its evidence code provides that the privilege does not apply to communications relevant to a medical condition the patient has placed in issue. Ezra subpoenas Dr. Luke, Hannah's treating physician, for records and testimony about the same back condition for which Hannah seeks damages. Hannah objects on physician-patient privilege grounds. Which statement best states the correct privilege route?
Answer A: The objection should be sustained because State A recognizes a physician-patient privilege and the records involve confidential medical treatment.
Answer B: The objection should be overruled because federal common law always governs privilege in any case filed in federal court, and federal law has no general physician-patient privilege.
Answer C: The objection should be overruled as to communications relevant to the claimed back injury because state privilege law governs the state-law claim, but Hannah tendered that medical condition by seeking damages for it.
Answer D: The objection should be sustained unless Ezra can show that Dr. Luke's records are nonhearsay or satisfy a hearsay exception.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: In a civil case, state privilege law governs a claim or defense for which state law supplies the rule of decision. Hannah's negligence claim is a state-law claim, so the state physician-patient privilege is the starting point. But Hannah placed the same back condition at issue by seeking damages for it, and the stated issue-tender exception removes the privilege for communications relevant to that condition. The decisive axis is source law plus exception; relevance alone does not decide the privilege issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it stops after finding a state privilege. The stated issue-tender exception defeats the privilege for communications relevant to the condition Hannah put in issue. B: B is wrong because it uses a federal-forum shortcut. In a diversity case involving a state-law claim, state privilege law can govern under Rule 501. D: D is wrong because hearsay status does not answer the privilege objection. Evidence may be relevant and nonhearsay yet still be blocked, or not blocked, by privilege rules.
