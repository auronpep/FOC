Question: A commercial printer bought a binding machine. Because of an internal defect, the machine stopped working after two months and damaged only itself. No person was injured, and no other property was harmed. The printer seeks strict products tort damages for the lost value of the machine. What is the seller's strongest response?
Answer A: Strict products tort liability is weak because the loss is only to the product itself
Answer B: Strict products liability always covers any product that fails
Answer C: The printer wins because privity is not required
Answer D: The seller wins only if it proves careful inspection
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Pure disappointed-bargain or product-only economic loss is usually handled by contract or warranty, not strict products tort.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Products tort liability requires tort-type harm, such as personal injury or damage to other property. C: The problem is damages type, not privity. D: The economic-loss limit does not depend on careful inspection.
lb_rule_reference: Strict products liability generally covers physical harm to person or other property, not disappointed-bargain loss to the product itself alone.
