MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Self-defense
Question: Grace attends a social gathering and drinks what she believes are non-alcoholic fruit punch beverages. Unknown to her, the host spiked the punch with high-proof alcohol. Severely intoxicated, Grace becomes delusional and attacks a guest, believing he is a threatening demon. Grace is charged with assault. She had no reason to suspect the punch was alcoholic. The jurisdiction applies M'Naghten for insanity. Which of the following best describes Grace's defense?
Answer A: Grace has no defense because she voluntarily attended the party and consumed the drinks
Answer B: Grace has a partial defense that reduces the charge but does not fully excuse her conduct
Answer C: Grace may have a complete defense if her involuntary intoxication caused a state equivalent to M'Naghten insanity — she did not know she was attacking a person
Answer D: Grace can only use this as a defense if she can prove the host intended to harm her
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Involuntary intoxication (unknown or coerced ingestion) is treated far more favorably than voluntary intoxication. A defendant involuntarily intoxicated may assert a complete defense — either because the intoxication negated the required mens rea, or because it caused a state meeting the applicable insanity standard. Grace's psychotic episode caused her to believe she was attacking a demon, not a human — she did not know the nature and quality of her act. This satisfies the first prong of M'Naghten. Combined with the fact that her intoxication was involuntary (she did not know the punch contained alcohol), she has a viable complete defense to assault.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Voluntary intoxication requires that the defendant knew she was consuming an intoxicant. Grace consumed beverages she reasonably believed were non-alcoholic. That is the paradigm case of involuntary intoxication — unknown consumption. Her voluntary presence at the party is irrelevant to whether her intoxication was voluntary in the legally relevant sense. B: Involuntary intoxication, unlike voluntary intoxication, is treated as a full defense if it negates the required mens rea or produces a state equivalent to legal insanity. The partial-defense limitation applies to voluntary, not involuntary, intoxication. If Grace's involuntary intoxication caused a state satisfying M'Naghten (she did not know the nature of her act or that it was wrong), she has a complete excuse. D: The host's intent is relevant to the host's criminal liability, not to Grace's defense. Involuntary intoxication for Grace's purposes is established by her own unknowing ingestion — regardless of why the host spiked the punch. The analysis focuses on Grace's state of mind and knowledge, not the host's motives.
lb_rule_reference: Involuntary intoxication — caused by ingesting an intoxicant unknowingly or under coercion — is a complete defense if it negated the required mental state or caused a condition meeting the applicable insanity test; unlike voluntary intoxication, it is not limited to specific intent crimes
