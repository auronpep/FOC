Question: In a federal dispute over whether Adam paid a filing fee, Clerk Ezra testifies that he personally watched Adam hand the fee to another clerk at the counter. A receipt was also printed, but it has been lost. The opposing party objects that the receipt is the best evidence of payment. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because payment can be proved only by the receipt's contents.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Ezra can remember the exact receipt number.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Ezra is testifying from personal knowledge of the payment event, not to prove the contents of the receipt.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Adam first proves that the receipt was lost without bad faith.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The original-writing rule applies when the proponent seeks to prove the contents of a writing, recording, or photograph. Ezra is proving an event he personally observed—the payment—not the contents of the receipt. His personal knowledge supplies the foundation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an event can be proved by witness testimony even if a writing recorded it. B: B is wrong because the exact receipt number is not required to prove the observed payment. D: D is wrong because lost-original rules matter when the proponent seeks to prove the receipt's contents, which is not the offered proof channel.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b), 901, and 1002 / content versus event foundation
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests content-versus-event routing and the original-writing false positive.
