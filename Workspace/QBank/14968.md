Question: A landowner died, validly devising his land to his wife “for life or until remarriage, then to” their daughter. Shortly after the landowner's death, his daughter executed an instrument in the proper form of a deed, purporting to convey the land to her friend. A year later, the daughter died intestate, with her mother, the original landowner's wife, as her sole heir. The following month, the wife remarried. The wife then executed an instrument in the proper form of a deed, purporting to convey the land to her new husband as a wedding gift. Who now owns what interest in the land?
Answer A: A: The daughter's friend owns the fee simple.
Answer B: B: The wife owns the fee simple.
Answer C: C: The wife's new husband has a life estate in the land for the wife's life, with the remainder in the daughter's friend.
Answer D: D: The wife's new husband owns the fee simple.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. The wife had a determinable life estate and the daughter validly assigned her own vested remainder and executory interest to the friend, and when the wife remarried, the wife's determinable life estate went to the friend in fee simple as the holder of the future interest.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. The wife did not have fee simple, but rather, a determinable life estate based on the language "for life" and "until remarriage. When she did, in fact, remarry, the land automatically went to the friend. C is incorrect. If the wife had not remarried, she would have been able to transfer her determinable life estate, but when she chose to remarry, her interest terminated and now belongs to the friend in fee simple. D is incorrect. The wife may not convey any estate greater than the life estate she holds. Any attempt to do so would have been effective only to the extent of her life estate, nothing more. The wife's interest was nevertheless terminated when she remarried.
