Question: Miriam owned Blueacre. Blueacre was benefited by an appurtenant easement over Caleb's private lane for access to a public road. Miriam then conveyed Blueacre to Ruth by a deed that did not mention the easement. Nothing in the deed reserved or excluded the easement. May Ruth use the lane?
Answer A: No, because an easement must be listed in the deed every time the dominant estate is transferred.
Answer B: No, because Miriam's silence in the deed abandoned the easement.
Answer C: Yes, because an appurtenant easement generally passes with the dominant estate even if the deed does not mention it.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Caleb signs a new easement agreement after Ruth receives Blueacre.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Ruth may use the lane. An appurtenant easement benefits the dominant estate and generally transfers automatically with that estate, unless the deed validly reserves, excludes, or terminates it. The deed's silence does not by itself destroy the easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because express repetition in each deed is not required for an appurtenant easement to pass. B: B is wrong because silence in the conveyance of the dominant estate is not abandonment. D: D is wrong because Caleb need not sign a new grant for an existing appurtenant easement to pass with Blueacre.
lb_rule_reference: Appurtenant easement / conveyance of dominant estate
