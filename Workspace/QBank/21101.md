Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Sarah signed and delivered a deed stating, “For ten dollars, I convey my orchard to Rachel.” The deed identified Sarah, Rachel, and the orchard, used present words of transfer, and Sarah signed it. Rachel accepted the deed, but she never paid the ten dollars. Sarah later demanded the orchard back solely because the money was unpaid. Who owns the orchard?
Answer A: Rachel, because consideration is not required for a valid delivered deed.
Answer B: Sarah, because the stated consideration was not actually paid.
Answer C: Sarah, because a gift deed must expressly say it is a gift.
Answer D: Rachel only if she records the deed before Sarah demands the orchard back.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is execution and delivery, not consideration. The deed satisfied the formal requirements and was delivered and accepted. Failure to pay the recited ten dollars may raise a separate contract or restitution issue in some settings, but it does not invalidate the deed as a conveyance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because nonpayment of recited consideration does not make a delivered deed invalid. C: C is wrong because a deed need not label itself as a gift deed. D: D is wrong because recording is not required to make the deed valid between Sarah and Rachel.
lb_rule_reference: Consideration not required for deed validity
