Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: In a federal fraud trial, Esther testifies that she spoke with Nathan by telephone many times before the lawsuit. The plaintiff offers a voicemail, and Esther testifies that the voice on it is Nathan's. On cross-examination, she admits she is “not one hundred percent certain.” Nathan objects for lack of foundation. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the voicemail because only an expert may identify a voice on a recording.
Answer B: Admit the voicemail because Esther's prior familiarity with Nathan's voice supplies sufficient foundation; her uncertainty affects weight.
Answer C: Exclude the voicemail unless Nathan admits that the voice is his.
Answer D: Exclude the voicemail because a witness may identify a voice only if she heard it face-to-face.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A voice may be identified by an opinion based on hearing the voice at any time under circumstances connecting it with the alleged speaker. Esther's prior phone conversations give a foundation. Her lack of absolute certainty goes to weight, not admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because expert testimony is not required for ordinary voice identification. C: C is wrong because admission by the speaker is not required. D: D is wrong because voice familiarity may come through mechanical or electronic transmission.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(5) / voice identification
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests voice identification foundation and the weight/admissibility split.
