Question: In a federal burglary trial, Aaron denies being near the house at the time of the break-in. The prosecutor offers cell-site data placing Aaron's phone within a half-mile of the house at about that time. Aaron objects that the data cannot pinpoint his exact location. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because approximate location data has some tendency to make Aaron's presence near the house more probable.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because location evidence must place the person at the exact address.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence because phone data is relevant only if Aaron admits carrying the phone.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if the prosecutor proves no one else could have used the phone.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Approximate location data tends to make presence near the scene more probable. Precision, phone possession, and alternate users affect weight or foundation, but they do not make the evidence irrelevant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B imposes an exact-location requirement. C and D demand admissions or exclusion of alternatives not required for threshold relevance.
