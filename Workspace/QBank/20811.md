Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: A Utah patient traveled to Idaho for surgery by an Idaho surgeon. All treatment occurred in Idaho. After returning home, the patient suffered complications in Utah and sued the surgeon in Utah. The patient argues that Utah is the fairest forum because she is ill, her family is in Utah, and travel would be difficult. The surgeon has never practiced, advertised, or treated patients in Utah. Can fairness considerations alone create specific jurisdiction in Utah?
Answer A: Yes, because the plaintiff's hardship is enough to create minimum contacts
Answer B: Yes, because the complications were felt in Utah
Answer C: No, because fairness cannot create minimum contacts absent purposeful Utah-directed conduct by the surgeon
Answer D: No, because medical-malpractice claims are excluded from federal courts
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The surgeon's relevant conduct occurred in Idaho. Without purposeful Utah contacts, the case fails before fairness can supply jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Fairness factors do not replace defendant-created contacts. B: The treatment and defendant conduct occurred in Idaho, and the surgeon did not target Utah. D: State-law claims can be heard in federal court with diversity and PJ. The defect here is PJ.
lb_rule_reference: Courts consider fairness after minimum contacts; forum convenience and plaintiff hardship do not themselves create purposeful availment.
