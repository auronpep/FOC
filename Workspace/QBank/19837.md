Question: A federal taxpayer sues to challenge a congressional appropriation that gives money directly to churches for religious instruction. She alleges that the spending violates the Establishment Clause. The government moves to dismiss, arguing that taxpayer status never supports standing. Which statement best resolves standing?
Answer A: The taxpayer lacks standing because taxpayer suits are always barred
Answer B: The taxpayer has standing because all federal spending affects all taxpayers
Answer C: The taxpayer may fit the narrow exception because she challenges congressional spending under the Establishment Clause
Answer D: The taxpayer has standing only if she paid more taxes than other citizens
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts align with the narrow taxpayer-standing exception: congressional taxing and spending plus an Establishment Clause claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A narrow exception exists for federal taxpayer challenges to congressional taxing and spending alleged to violate the Establishment Clause. B: Taxpayer status alone does not create standing; the exception is narrow and doctrine-specific. D: The recognized exception does not turn on paying a higher tax amount.
lb_rule_reference: Federal taxpayer standing is generally barred, with a narrow exception for challenges to exercises of congressional taxing and spending power alleged to violate the Establishment Clause.
