Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Domicile
Question: Vivienne owns a parcel of land in the State of Talend. She lives and is domiciled in the State of Bryce, where she conducts all of her business. Vivienne has no other contacts with Talend except owning this land. Two disputes arise simultaneously: (1) A Talend adjacent-property owner, Orin, files a quiet-title action in Talend court seeking to establish that the boundary line between his property and Vivienne's land runs six feet to the east of where Vivienne claims it runs. (2) A Bryce creditor who holds an unsatisfied money judgment against Vivienne seeks to satisfy the judgment by attaching Vivienne's Talend property in Talend court and having the attached property sold to pay the debt — a claim unrelated to the land itself. Which of the following best describes the jurisdiction of Talend courts in each dispute?
Answer A: Talend has valid jurisdiction in both proceedings because property located in the forum is a sufficient basis for jurisdiction under traditional in rem doctrine
Answer B: Talend has valid jurisdiction in neither proceeding because Vivienne's only contact with Talend is property ownership, which is insufficient for any jurisdiction after Shaffer
Answer C: Talend has valid in rem jurisdiction over the quiet-title action (1), but the quasi-in-rem proceeding (2) requires minimum contacts beyond property ownership, which Vivienne lacks
Answer D: Talend has valid jurisdiction in proceeding (2) because attaching property to satisfy a judgment is a special statutory remedy that falls outside the minimum-contacts requirement
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The quiet-title action (1) is a true in rem proceeding — it adjudicates the boundary of Talend land. The property's presence in Talend supplies the necessary nexus between the claim and the forum, so jurisdiction is valid. The debt-satisfaction proceeding (2) is quasi-in-rem — it uses Vivienne's Talend property as a jurisdictional hook to collect a money judgment from Bryce that has nothing to do with the land. Under *Shaffer v. Heitner*, quasi-in-rem jurisdiction must satisfy minimum contacts. Vivienne's only contact with Talend is ownership of the land being attached, which is insufficient on its own for the unrelated personal debt claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Shaffer v. Heitner* held that the same minimum-contacts analysis required for in personam jurisdiction applies when property is used as a jurisdictional hook for claims that are not about the property itself. Proceeding (2) — a money-debt claim being satisfied through property attachment — is a quasi-in-rem proceeding using property as a jurisdictional device to litigate a personal money claim. After *Shaffer*, this requires minimum contacts beyond property ownership alone. Vivienne's only contact with Talend is the land; that is insufficient for the quasi-in-rem proceeding. B: Shaffer* did not eliminate in rem jurisdiction for actions adjudicating title and interests in property located in the forum. When the claim is directly about the property — its ownership, boundaries, encumbrances — the property's presence supplies the requisite nexus between the claim and the forum. Orin's quiet-title action (proceeding 1) is a classic in rem action: it adjudicates the boundary of Talend land. Talend courts have valid jurisdiction for that proceeding. D: Statutory authorization for a procedure does not override constitutional due-process requirements. *Shaffer* squarely held that attaching a defendant's property in the forum to satisfy an unrelated personal judgment is quasi-in-rem jurisdiction that must satisfy minimum contacts. The fact that the procedure is authorized by statute does not insulate it from due-process scrutiny.
lb_rule_reference: After *Shaffer v. Heitner* (1977), the presence of property in the forum is not, by itself, a sufficient basis for adjudicating in personam claims against the property owner. However, true in rem actions to adjudicate title to or interests in the property itself remain valid because the property's presence in the forum provides the nexus between the claim and the forum. The defendant's minimum contacts must be considered for quasi-in-rem claims that use property as a jurisdictional hook to litigate unrelated personal claims.
