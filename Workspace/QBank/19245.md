Question: In a federal civil trial, Mark testifies that he has never been convicted of any crime involving dishonesty. On cross-examination, the opposing party asks about a two-year-old conviction for filing a false tax return, and Mark denies it. The opposing party then offers a certified copy of the judgment of conviction. Mark objects that the judgment is extrinsic evidence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the judgment because extrinsic evidence may never be used to attack credibility.
Answer B: Exclude the judgment because Mark denied the conviction on cross-examination.
Answer C: Admit the judgment as conviction impeachment because the conviction involved a dishonest act or false statement.
Answer D: Admit the judgment only if Mark is a criminal defendant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Conviction impeachment is governed by FRE 609, not the general no-extrinsic-proof rule for specific acts under Rule 608(b). A conviction involving a dishonest act or false statement is admissible to attack truthfulness, and a certified judgment is a proper way to prove the conviction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because convictions are a recognized impeachment route. B: B is wrong because the witness's denial does not bar proof of the conviction. D: D is wrong because conviction impeachment applies to witnesses generally, not only criminal defendants.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609 / certified conviction after witness denial
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests that conviction records are a distinct impeachment-material channel, not barred as ordinary extrinsic specific-act evidence.
