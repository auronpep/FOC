Question: In a federal trial, counsel for Eve asks a witness, “Did Abel tell you that Cain had confessed?” Before the witness answers, Cain objects on hearsay grounds. Eve argues that the witness should answer and the jury can decide whether the statement is reliable. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Allow the answer because reliability of hearsay is always for the jury.
Answer B: Require the witness to answer outside the jury's hearing if the court needs the answer to evaluate admissibility.
Answer C: Allow the answer before the jury and then strike it if the judge later disagrees.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because all questions about confessions are party-opponent statements.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court should prevent inadmissible evidence from being suggested to the jury when practicable. If the court needs more information to rule, it can take the proffer outside the jury's hearing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because admissibility is for the court. C risks exposing the jury to inadmissible evidence before the ruling. C: A is wrong because admissibility is for the court. C risks exposing the jury to inadmissible evidence before the ruling. D is wrong because the proposed testimony contains multiple statement layers and is not automatically a party-opponent statement. D: D is wrong because the proposed testimony contains multiple statement layers and is not automatically a party-opponent statement.
