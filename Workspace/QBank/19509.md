Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Ruth testifies that she never told anyone the ladder shook before it collapsed. Without asking Ruth anything more about the statement, the defendant seeks to call Samuel to testify that Ruth told him the day after the accident, “That ladder was shaking before I stepped on it.” The plaintiff objects that Ruth has not had a chance to explain or deny the statement. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit Samuel's testimony immediately because any prior inconsistent statement may be proved extrinsically.
Answer B: Exclude Samuel's testimony permanently because extrinsic evidence of prior inconsistent statements is never permitted.
Answer C: Do not admit Samuel's testimony unless Ruth is given an opportunity to explain or deny the statement and the plaintiff has an opportunity to examine her about it, unless the court orders otherwise.
Answer D: Admit Samuel's testimony only if Ruth's statement was under oath at a prior proceeding.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 613(b), extrinsic evidence of a witness's prior inconsistent statement generally may not be admitted until the witness is given an opportunity to explain or deny the statement and the adverse party is given an opportunity to examine the witness about it, unless the court orders otherwise.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 613(b) imposes an opportunity requirement before extrinsic proof is admitted. B: B is wrong because extrinsic evidence of a prior inconsistent statement can be used when the rule's procedure is satisfied. D: D is wrong because oath status affects substantive nonhearsay treatment under Rule 801(d)(1)(A), not the basic impeachment-use procedure.
