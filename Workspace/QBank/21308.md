Question: In a federal diversity action governed by State X probate law, Leah claims that Benjamin is presumed dead because he has been missing for eight years. State X law recognizes the presumption but allows evidence that the missing person was seen alive during the period. Benjamin's cousin testifies that he spoke with Benjamin six months ago. Leah asks the judge to instruct the jury that it must find Benjamin dead anyway. Benjamin's estate objects. What should the court do?
Answer A: Give the instruction unless the judge finds conclusively that the cousin is telling the truth.
Answer B: Give the instruction because the death presumption is stronger than witness testimony.
Answer C: Refuse the instruction because the contrary testimony creates a fact issue under the governing presumption rule.
Answer D: Refuse the instruction only because Leah did not offer a death certificate.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A rebuttable death presumption does not require the jury to find death after contrary evidence is introduced. The jury must resolve the factual conflict under the governing state-law presumption effect in this diversity setting.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the judge need not decide conclusively whether the cousin is truthful before the jury may consider rebuttal evidence. B: B is wrong because a rebuttable presumption does not defeat contrary evidence as a matter of law. D: D is wrong because the issue is the presumption's rebuttal effect, not a required proof channel.
lb_rule_reference: Death/absence presumption / preservation
