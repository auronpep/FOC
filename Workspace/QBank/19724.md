Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Domicile
Question: A plaintiff is a lawful permanent resident of the United States who has been domiciled in Texas for the past eight years. The plaintiff sues a defendant corporation. The corporation is incorporated in Texas and has its principal place of business (nerve center) in Texas. The plaintiff contends that because she is a foreign national, the suit is between a "citizen of a foreign state" and a Texas corporation — satisfying diversity under § 1332(a)(2). The amount in controversy exceeds $75,000. The defendant moves to dismiss for lack of diversity jurisdiction. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Should the court dismiss for lack of diversity jurisdiction?
Answer A: No, because the plaintiff is a foreign national and the defendant is a U.S. corporation, satisfying the alien-vs.-domestic-citizen diversity basis under § 1332(a)(2)
Answer B: Yes, because as a lawful permanent resident domiciled in Texas, the plaintiff is deemed a Texas citizen under § 1332(a)(7), and both parties are Texas citizens — destroying complete diversity
Answer C: No, because permanent residents are treated as stateless persons — they are neither citizens of a U.S. state nor citizens of a foreign state — and stateless persons always satisfy diversity
Answer D: No, because the plaintiff is still a citizen of her country of origin and the defendant is a U.S. citizen — both share global citizen status as persons rather than states — so diversity is not met on a state-vs.-state basis
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1332(a)(7) provides that an alien admitted to permanent residence in the United States is deemed a citizen of the state in which the alien is domiciled. The plaintiff is an LPR domiciled in Texas, making her a Texas citizen for diversity purposes. The defendant corporation is also a Texas citizen — incorporated in Texas with its nerve center in Texas. Because both parties are Texas citizens, there is no complete diversity and the court must dismiss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under § 1332(a)(7), an alien admitted to permanent residence in the United States is deemed a citizen of the state in which domiciled. The plaintiff is an LPR domiciled in Texas, so she is treated as a Texas citizen for diversity purposes. The defendant corporation is also a Texas citizen (both incorporation and nerve center). Complete diversity is absent. C: Congress resolved this gap in § 1332(a)(7): LPRs are expressly assigned the citizenship of their state of domicile. They are not treated as stateless; they are treated as state citizens. There is no "automatic diversity" rule for LPRs. D: Section 1332 determines citizenship by the statutory assignment, not by some abstract comparison of global personhood. The statute's answer for an LPR is clear: domicile state. The plaintiff's country-of-origin citizenship is legally irrelevant once she is classified as an LPR under § 1332(a)(7).
lb_rule_reference: 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a)(2) — diversity jurisdiction extends to suits between citizens of a state and citizens of a foreign state; but under § 1332(a)(7), an alien admitted to permanent residence in the United States is deemed a citizen of the state in which the alien is domiciled.
