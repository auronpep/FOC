Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: Tessa agreed to drive a getaway car for an armed robbery. An hour before the robbery, she texted the others that she was out, drove the car away from the meeting place, and called the store to warn that a robbery was planned. The others found another car, robbed the store anyway, and one robber killed a clerk during the robbery. Is Tessa liable for felony murder?
Answer A: Yes, because she originally agreed to help commit the robbery
Answer B: Yes, because withdrawal is never a defense to felony murder
Answer C: No, because she effectively withdrew before the robbery and neutralized her assistance
Answer D: No, but only because the clerk, not a co-felon, was killed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Tessa withdrew before the robbery, communicated withdrawal to the group, removed the promised getaway car, and warned the target. The later killing occurred after her effective withdrawal, so felony murder liability should not attach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Liability for later consequences can be cut off by effective withdrawal before the crime, communication to the other participants, and neutralization of prior assistance. Tessa did all three. B: Felony murder remains tied to participation in the felony. A timely, communicated withdrawal that neutralizes aid can prevent liability for later acts. D: The victim being a clerk would not itself defeat felony murder. The decisive fact is Tessa's withdrawal before the robbery and killing.
lb_rule_reference: A participant who effectively withdraws from the felony before the fatal act, communicates withdrawal, and attempts to neutralize assistance is generally not liable for later felony-murder consequences.
