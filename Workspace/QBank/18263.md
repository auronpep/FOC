Question: Joseph sold a field to Benjamin. The written contract required Benjamin to pay Miriam $15,000, but only if Joseph delivered a valid survey by June 1. Miriam was Joseph's creditor and was named in the contract. Joseph never delivered the survey. Miriam sues Benjamin for the $15,000. Is Benjamin liable?
Answer A: Yes, because Miriam was named in the contract.
Answer B: Yes, because Miriam was a creditor beneficiary and creditor beneficiaries are not subject to conditions.
Answer C: No, because the condition to Benjamin's duty to pay Miriam did not occur.
Answer D: No, but only because Miriam failed to sign the land-sale contract.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Miriam may be an intended creditor beneficiary, but the contract made Benjamin's duty to pay her conditional on Joseph's delivery of a valid survey by June 1. Because the condition did not occur, Benjamin's duty to Miriam did not mature.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because naming supports intended status but does not eliminate contract conditions. B: B is wrong because creditor beneficiaries take subject to conditions in the contract. D: D is wrong because the beneficiary need not sign the contract to enforce when the route and conditions are satisfied.
