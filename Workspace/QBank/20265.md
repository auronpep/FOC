MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Burglary
Question: Three separate scenarios: **Scenario 1:** Ian pushes open a closed but unlocked back door of a house and enters at night, intending to steal a laptop. **Scenario 2:** Jana walks through the front door of a house that the owner left standing wide open (propped open with a doorstop), intending to steal jewelry. **Scenario 3:** Kyle reaches through an open window — one that was already open — and unhooks a latch inside to open a second locked inner door, then enters. In which scenario(s) is the "breaking" element of common-law burglary satisfied?
Answer A: Scenarios 1 and 3 only
Answer B: Scenario 1 only, because only physical force was used to open a door
Answer C: All three scenarios, because in each case the defendant entered without permission
Answer D: Scenarios 2 and 3 only, because Scenario 1 involved an unlocked door and no real barrier was overcome
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Common-law "breaking" requires that the defendant create or enlarge an opening, or remove a barrier. In Scenario 1, Ian opened a closed door — even though it was unlocked, the act of pushing it open constitutes a constructive breaking (creating an opening). In Scenario 2, Jana entered through a door the owner had already propped fully open; no barrier was removed, no opening created — this is not a breaking. In Scenario 3, Kyle used an already-open window to reach inside and unlatch a locked interior door — removing the barrier of the locked inner door satisfies the breaking element. Scenarios 1 and 3 involve breaking; Scenario 2 does not.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Breaking does not require forceful destruction; it requires only the creation or removal of a barrier. Kyle in Scenario 3 removed the barrier of a locked interior door by reaching through an open window to unlatch it — this is a constructive breaking. Force is not required; the act of removing a physical barrier is sufficient. C: Lack of permission satisfies the "entering" element but not the "breaking" element. Breaking requires a physical act by the defendant to create or remove a barrier. Jana entered through an already fully-open door — she removed no barrier. No breaking occurred even though her entry was unauthorized. "Breaking" and "trespassory entry" are distinct elements. D: Common-law breaking does not require a locked door or any significant resistance. Even a closed but unlocked door represents a barrier that must be opened. The act of opening it — however easy — constitutes a constructive breaking. Scenario 1 satisfies the breaking element. Scenario 2 does not, because there was no door to open — it was already standing open.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law burglary requires a "breaking," which means the creation or enlargement of an opening by the defendant. Entering through a door that the owner left open (fully) is not a breaking. Opening a closed but unlocked door, or opening a window, constitutes a breaking even without force.
