


BARMATRIX Q#: 22331
Outline_code: 72060200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Justification Defenses
Question: A man was stopped by a police officer because the officer thought the man was driving too slowly in a residential neighborhood. The officer told the man to get out of the car, then searched the man and his car. The officer told the man to open the car's trunk. The man refused, and the officer told him that if he did not open the trunk, the officer would arrest him. The man then opened the trunk. Inside a fishing tacklebox, the officer found a small amount of cocaine. The man told him that a woman had sold it to him. Based on the man's statement, the woman was arrested and charged with selling drugs. At her trial, the woman moved to prevent the introduction of the cocaine into evidence. Should the court grant the woman's motion?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the traffic stop was unlawful.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the search was unlawful.
Answer C: C: No, because the man consented to the search.
Answer D: D: No, because the woman has no standing to object.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the Fourth Amendment, a person has standing to challenge a search if the person owns or has a right to possession of the thing searched. Here, the woman is challenging the search of the man's car, not her own, so she has no standing to object. A and B are therefore incorrect. C is incorrect because whether or not the man lawfully consented to the search does not change the analysis.
