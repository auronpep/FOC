Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Leah signed a deed conveying her orchard but left the grantee line blank. Before delivery, Leah instructed Ruth, her clerk, to write “Rachel” as the grantee. Ruth did so, and Leah then delivered the completed deed to Rachel intending an immediate transfer. Rachel accepted. Who owns the orchard?
Answer A: Leah, because a deed with a grantee line that was once blank can never be valid.
Answer B: Ruth, because Ruth physically wrote the grantee's name.
Answer C: Rachel, because the grantee was inserted with Leah's authority before delivery.
Answer D: Leah, unless Rachel separately paid consideration.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is authorized grantee identity at delivery. Leah authorized Ruth to insert Rachel's name before the deed was delivered, and the completed deed identified Rachel as grantee when delivered. The deed can pass title to Rachel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an authorized completion before delivery can supply the grantee name. B: B is wrong because the person who writes the name is not the grantee unless the deed says so. D: D is wrong because consideration is not required for deed validity.
lb_rule_reference: Authorized completion / grantee identity
