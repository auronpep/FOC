Question: A public library has a meeting room policy allowing any local group to reserve rooms for lectures, debates, workshops, or political meetings. After a controversial immigration lecture is scheduled, the library cancels it because the topic is "too divisive." The room remains available for other public lectures. What forum classification best fits the library room?
Answer A: Nonpublic forum, because libraries are government property
Answer B: Designated public forum, because the library opened the room broadly for public expressive events
Answer C: Government speech, because the lecture occurs in a government building
Answer D: Private forum, because local groups choose the topics
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The policy invites broad expressive use, making content-based cancellation of one topic highly suspect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Public ownership alone does not decide the forum. The government intentionally opened the room broadly for expressive use. C: Private speakers using a public meeting room are not automatically government speakers. D: The property remains government-controlled; private speakers do not make it private property.
lb_rule_reference: In a designated public forum, content-based exclusions receive strict scrutiny and content-neutral time, place, and manner rules receive intermediate review.
