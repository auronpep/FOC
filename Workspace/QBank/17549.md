Question: David told Abigail, “You may build and use a driveway across my land to reach your house.” Relying on that statement, Abigail spent $25,000 grading and paving the driveway. David watched the work and did not object. After the driveway was complete, David tried to revoke permission. What is Abigail's strongest argument?
Answer A: She owns David's land in fee because she spent money on it.
Answer B: She has no right because the access promise was oral and oral land-use permission can never have legal effect.
Answer C: David may be estopped from revoking because Abigail reasonably relied on his permission by making substantial expenditures.
Answer D: She has a prescriptive easement because construction work alone satisfies the statutory period.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement by estoppel or irrevocable license may arise when a landowner permits use and the user reasonably relies on the permission through substantial expenditures or improvements. David's statement, Abigail's $25,000 reliance, and David's acquiescence support estoppel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reliance may create a use right, not fee title. B: B is wrong because reliance can make oral permission irrevocable in equity. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because prescription requires adverse use for the statutory period, not merely construction.
lb_rule_reference: Easement by estoppel / reliance on oral access promise
lb_student_diagnostic: The recurring trap is treating all oral land-use permission as freely revocable despite reliance.
