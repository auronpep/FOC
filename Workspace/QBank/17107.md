Outline: Attorney-client
Question: Joseph asked his lawyer, Ruth, to draft a “consulting agreement” that would transfer most of his assets to a friend. Joseph's undisclosed purpose was to conceal assets from a judgment creditor. Ruth believed the transaction was legitimate. If the creditor later seeks Joseph's communications about drafting the agreement, what is the best privilege analysis?
Answer A: The communications are privileged because Ruth was unaware of Joseph's fraudulent purpose.
Answer B: The communications are privileged because fraudulent transfers are civil, not criminal.
Answer C: The communications are unprivileged only if Ruth personally committed fraud.
Answer D: The crime-fraud exception may apply because Joseph sought legal assistance to further a fraud, even if Ruth was unaware.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The crime-fraud exception focuses on the client's use of the legal consultation to further crime or fraud. The lawyer need not be culpable or aware of the improper purpose. The decisive axis is client purpose, not attorney knowledge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is the attorney-knowledge condition trap. Ruth's innocence does not preserve privilege if Joseph used the consultation to further fraud. B: B is wrong because Fraud can trigger the exception even when the scheme is civil rather than criminal. C: C is wrong because The exception does not require the lawyer to commit fraud.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / crime-fraud client purpose
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is adding lawyer culpability as a false element and missing the client-purpose route.
