Question: In a federal civil fraud trial, Ruth testifies for the plaintiff. On cross-examination, the defendant asks Ruth whether she was convicted two years ago of perjury. Ruth objects that the conviction is embarrassing and that the case is civil, not criminal. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because conviction impeachment is available only in criminal cases.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because only felony convictions may be used to impeach a witness.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because a perjury conviction involves a dishonest act or false statement and must be admitted to attack Ruth's character for truthfulness.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Ruth first testifies that she is a truthful person.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 609(a)(2) requires admission of a conviction, regardless of punishment, if the court can readily determine that establishing the elements required proving, or the witness admitted, a dishonest act or false statement. Perjury is a false-statement crime and is used to attack truthfulness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 609 applies in civil and criminal cases. B: B is wrong because Rule 609(a)(2) is not limited to felonies. D: D is wrong because conviction impeachment attacks the witness's credibility without requiring prior self-bolstering.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(a)(2) / dishonest-act or false-statement conviction
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the mandatory dishonest-act conviction route and rejects the felony-only and criminal-case-only traps.
