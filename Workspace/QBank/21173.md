Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: Mary is charged with stealing from a locked storage room. In Mary's desk, police find a hand-drawn map of the building showing the storage room and a back staircase. Mary objects that the map does not show she actually entered the room. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the map, because planning evidence is irrelevant unless it proves entry.
Answer B: Admit the map, because it has some tendency to show knowledge of the layout and possible planning.
Answer C: Exclude the map unless the prosecution proves Mary drew it on the day of the theft.
Answer D: Admit the map only if it accurately depicts every room in the building.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The map tends to show Mary's knowledge of the building and possible planning. Its age, accuracy, and connection to the theft affect weight, but they do not eliminate relevance if the basic inferential link exists.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because planning evidence need not prove the completed act by itself. C: C is wrong because same-day creation is not required for relevance. D: D is wrong because the map can be probative even if not perfectly complete.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating circumstantial planning evidence as irrelevant unless it proves the charged act.
