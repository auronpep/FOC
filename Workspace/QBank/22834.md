


BARMATRIX Q#: 22834
Outline_code: 91090200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jury Trials
Outline: Demand for Jury Trial
Question: A man was injured at a baseball game. The man sued the baseball team in federal court, alleging that the team was negligent when it failed to put up netting to protect spectators while they were in their seats. The man properly filed a written demand for a jury and served it on the team. After several weeks of discovery, the man decided he did not want a jury trial because he was afraid the jury would be biased in the team's favor. The team told the man it wanted to keep the jury. Will the court allow the man to withdraw his request for a jury?
Answer A: A: No, because the man's request for a jury cannot be withdrawn once discovery begins.
Answer B: B: No, because the team wanted to keep the jury.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the man was concerned about jury bias.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the man wanted to withdraw his own jury request.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A proper jury demand may only be withdrawn if the parties consent to the withdrawal. Fed. R. Civ. P. 38(d). Here, since the team wants to keep the jury, the man won't be able to withdraw his request for a jury. A is incorrect because a request for a jury could be withdrawn after discovery if the parties consent to the withdrawal. C and D are incorrect because the man cannot withdraw his jury request without the team's consent.
