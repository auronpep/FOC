Question: Esther hired Levi to design a restaurant's dining room layout, select colors, and supervise the work. Levi would also purchase several tables and lamps, but the contract stated that Levi was being retained for professional design services and supervision. A dispute arises about the meaning of “complete design package.” Which source-law route should govern the meaning dispute?
Answer A: UCC Article 2, because tables and lamps are movable goods.
Answer B: Common law, because the predominant purpose is professional services.
Answer C: UCC Article 2, because all mixed contracts are governed by Article 2.
Answer D: The law of negotiable instruments, because payment will be made by check.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The predominant purpose is design and supervisory services. The tables and lamps are incidental. The content/meaning dispute should be routed through common-law contract interpretation rather than Article 2 goods rules.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because movable items are incidental to the service package. C: C is wrong because mixed contracts are not automatically Article 2. D: D is wrong because the method of payment does not determine source law for interpreting the service contract.
lb_student_diagnostic: Goods-involved equals UCC overread.
