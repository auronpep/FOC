Question: Mary orally agrees to buy 300 wool blankets from Paul. Paul signs and sends Mary a letter stating, “I confirm that I will sell you 300 wool blankets for $1,800.” Mary never signs the letter. Paul later refuses to deliver, and Mary sues Paul. How should Paul's Statute of Frauds defense be resolved?
Answer A: Paul should prevail because Mary did not sign the letter.
Answer B: Paul should prevail because the buyer must sign every UCC goods memorandum.
Answer C: Mary should prevail only if she paid part of the price.
Answer D: Mary may enforce the contract against Paul because Paul, the party to be charged, signed a writing showing the quantity.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The relevant signature is the signature of the party against whom enforcement is sought. Mary sues Paul, and Paul signed a writing indicating a contract for 300 blankets. The buyer's signature is unnecessary for enforcement against Paul.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B are wrong because they require the wrong party's signature. C is wrong because part payment is an exception route, but Mary already has a signed writing by Paul.
