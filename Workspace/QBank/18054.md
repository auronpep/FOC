internal_id: TR-255
Outline_code: 61020104
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Affirmative duties to act
Question: A camper accidentally knocked a heavy branch loose while setting up a hammock. The branch fell and pinned a nearby hiker's leg. The camper saw the hiker trapped but walked away without calling for help or warning anyone, even though a ranger station was nearby. The delay worsened the hiker's injury. Did the camper have a duty to take reasonable steps to aid or warn?
Answer A: Yes, because the camper created the peril and knew of the danger
Answer B: Yes, because every bystander must rescue any person in danger
Answer C: No, because the branch fell accidentally
Answer D: No, because omissions are never actionable
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The camper's conduct created the risk, and he knew the hiker was trapped. That triggers a duty to use reasonable care to prevent further harm.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The duty arises from the camper's creation of the peril, not from a general bystander rescue rule. C: Even if the peril was created accidentally, knowledge of the resulting danger can require reasonable aid or warning. D: Omissions can be actionable when the defendant created the peril.
lb_rule_reference: One who creates a risk of harm, even innocently at first, has a duty to use reasonable care to prevent further harm once aware of the danger.
