MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A blind pedestrian used a cane at a downtown crosswalk. The audible crossing signal was broken, so the pedestrian listened for traffic, waited for a lull, and began crossing. A cyclist riding against traffic in the bike lane struck the pedestrian. The cyclist counterclaims that the pedestrian was negligent for crossing without seeing the cyclist. What standard applies to the pedestrian's alleged negligence in the cyclist's negligence counterclaim?
Answer A: The pedestrian is held to the standard of a reasonably prudent blind person under similar circumstances
Answer B: The pedestrian is held to the same visual-observation standard as a sighted person
Answer C: The pedestrian cannot be negligent because physical disability eliminates any duty of care
Answer D: The pedestrian is negligent per se because crossing without visual confirmation is unsafe
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Physical disability is a recognized adjustment to the objective standard. The pedestrian is not judged as sighted but as a reasonably prudent person with blindness under similar conditions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The objective standard is adjusted for physical disability. The law asks what reasonable care looks like for a person with that disability. C: Physical disability adjusts the standard; it does not create immunity from negligence. D: No statute is supplied, and negligence per se is not triggered. The issue is the common-law reasonable-person standard adjusted for physical disability.
lb_rule_reference: Physical disability is considered in applying the reasonable-person standard; the question is how a reasonably prudent person with that disability would act.
