


BARMATRIX Q#: 22756
Outline_code: 97100000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Judgments and Verdicts
Question: After a jury trial in federal district court based on diversity jurisdiction, the court believed the jury's compensatory damage award met the state standard for excessiveness. Consequently, the court offered the plaintiff the choice of either accepting a lower award than that given by the jury or submitting to a new trial. May the court do so?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the court believed the award was excessive under the state standard.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the court could simply lower the jury's award if it chose to do so.
Answer C: C: No, because there was no indication the jury's award was so excessive as to “shock the conscience.”
Answer D: D: No, because forcing the plaintiff to accept a potentially lower award violates the Seventh Amendment.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When a court believes a jury award is excessive, remittitur allows a court to offer a plaintiff the choice of a reduced award or a new trial. If the suit is in federal court based on diversity jurisdiction, the court can look at whether the award meets the state standard for excessiveness. B is incorrect because a court cannot simply reduce a jury award without giving a plaintiff the option of a new trial. Hetzel v. Prince William County, 523 U.S. 208 (1998). C is incorrect because the award does not necessarily have to “shock the conscience.” If the federal court is sitting in diversity, the court can look to the state standard. D is incorrect because remittitur does not violate the Seventh Amendment (however, additur, or offering a defendant the choice between a higher award or a new trial when damages are inadequate, does).
