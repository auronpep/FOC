Question: In a federal battery trial, Hannah testifies that she saw Reuben throw the first punch. On cross-examination, Reuben's lawyer asks Hannah whether the alleged victim, Leah, is Hannah's sister. Hannah objects that family relationships are not character evidence and are therefore irrelevant. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because the family relationship may show Hannah's possible bias in favor of Leah.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because only character evidence can be used to impeach credibility.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Hannah has already given opinion testimony about Leah's truthfulness.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Reuben offers the relationship to prove that Leah acted violently.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness's close relationship with a party or victim can show bias, sympathy, or motive to favor that person. The question does not use the relationship as character evidence; it uses it to assess Hannah's credibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because bias is an independent credibility theory. C: C is wrong because the relationship is relevant to bias even without truthfulness-character evidence. D: D is wrong because the relationship is not offered to prove Leah's conduct.
lb_rule_reference: Bias impeachment / relationship to party or victim
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests relationship bias and the distinction between bias and character.
