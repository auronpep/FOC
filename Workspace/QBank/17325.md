Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Caleb contracted to provide lighting for Deborah's festival. Before Deborah repudiated, Caleb reasonably bought $15,000 of specialized lighting gear for the job. After the breach, Caleb sold the gear for $9,000. Caleb cannot prove lost profits with reasonable certainty. What is Caleb's best reliance recovery for the gear purchase?
Answer A: $6,000, the $15,000 expenditure minus the $9,000 salvage proceeds.
Answer B: $15,000, because the full purchase price was spent in reliance on the contract.
Answer C: $9,000, because reliance damages equal the resale value of retained property.
Answer D: $0, because Caleb sold the gear and therefore suffered no legal injury.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages must be adjusted for value retained or salvaged. Caleb spent $15,000 but recovered $9,000 by selling the gear, leaving a $6,000 reliance loss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it ignores the salvage offset. C: C is wrong because the resale value is a credit, not the loss. D: D is wrong because the resale reduced damages but did not eliminate the loss.
