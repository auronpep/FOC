Question: A state gives scholarships directly to low-income families, who may use them at any accredited public or private school. Some families choose religious schools. A taxpayer argues the program violates the Establishment Clause solely because religious schools receive scholarship money. What is the best response?
Answer A: The program is automatically unconstitutional because public funds may never reach religious schools
Answer B: The program does not automatically violate Establishment because aid is neutral and reaches schools through private family choice
Answer C: The program is constitutional only if no family chooses a religious school
Answer D: The program is invalid because religious schools receive no Free Exercise protection
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The state supports families through a neutral program rather than directly sponsoring religion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Neutral aid reaching religious schools through private choice is not automatically establishment. C: Private choice can break the link between government and religious use. D: Religious organizations can assert constitutional protection.
lb_rule_reference: A neutral public-benefit program that reaches religious schools through private choice does not automatically violate the Establishment Clause.
