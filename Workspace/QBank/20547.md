MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A contractor used explosives to remove rock near a residential street. The contractor followed all safety protocols. The blast's concussion knocked a pedestrian standing outside the marked perimeter to the ground, injuring her shoulder. The pedestrian sues the contractor for strict liability. What is the best result?
Answer A: The contractor is strictly liable because the injury resulted from the abnormal blast-force risk
Answer B: The contractor is not liable because it followed all safety protocols
Answer C: The contractor is not liable because the pedestrian was outside the marked perimeter
Answer D: The contractor is strictly liable only if debris physically struck the pedestrian
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Blasting creates a high risk of serious harm that cannot be eliminated by due care, and the injury came from that risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Due care is not a defense when strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity applies. C: The key question is whether the harm resulted from the activity's abnormal risk, not whether the perimeter was marked. D: Blast force can be a characteristic abnormal risk even without contact from debris.
lb_rule_reference: Blasting is a classic abnormally dangerous activity, and strict liability applies to physical harm caused by the abnormal risks that make blasting dangerous.
