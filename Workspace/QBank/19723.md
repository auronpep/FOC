Question: Rachel orally agreed to buy grain from Isaac. Isaac claims the oral contract was for 1,200 bushels at $2 per bushel. No writing was signed. In deposition testimony, Rachel admits, “I agreed to buy 700 bushels from Isaac, but not 1,200.” Rachel raises the Statute of Frauds. To what extent is the contract enforceable under the admission exception?
Answer A: It is enforceable for 1,200 bushels because Rachel admitted a contract existed.
Answer B: It is enforceable for 700 bushels, the quantity Rachel admitted.
Answer C: It is unenforceable for any quantity because deposition testimony cannot satisfy the UCC.
Answer D: It is enforceable only if Isaac accepted payment for 700 bushels.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC §2-201, an oral goods contract may be enforced if the party against whom enforcement is sought admits in pleading, testimony, or otherwise in court that a contract was made, but enforcement is limited to the quantity admitted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the admission exception does not enforce beyond the admitted quantity. C: C is wrong because testimony can qualify as an admission under the UCC. D: D is wrong because payment and acceptance are separate exceptions; Rachel's admission is the operative route.
