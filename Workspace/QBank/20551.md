Outline: Rule of completeness
Question: In a federal civil trial, the plaintiff introduces the first sentence of Daniel's recorded statement: “I was driving 60 miles per hour.” The same recording continues: “because a truck suddenly swerved into my lane and I had to avoid it.” Daniel asks to introduce the second sentence immediately. The plaintiff objects that the second sentence is hearsay. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the second sentence because completeness never overcomes a hearsay objection.
Answer B: Exclude the second sentence because Daniel may offer it only during his own case-in-chief.
Answer C: Admit the second sentence at that time if fairness requires it to be considered with the first sentence.
Answer D: Admit the entire recording, including unrelated statements about Daniel's employment history.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When a party introduces part of a statement, the adverse party may require introduction at that time of another part or related statement that fairness requires to be considered with it. Current Rule 106 can permit that completion over a hearsay objection.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 106 can overcome a hearsay objection for fairness-required completion. B: B is wrong because the rule concerns same-time context, not delayed repair. D: D is wrong because completeness reaches related portions fairness requires, not the entire recording automatically.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 106 / fairness-required completion
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests completeness overreach versus fairness-required same-time context.
