Question: Naomi sues Rachel for breach of a contract to deliver grain. Rachel admits breach but disputes only the amount of damages. Rachel offers testimony that she is known in her town for giving grain to widows. Naomi objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony, because Rachel's charitable reputation does not make the amount of Naomi's contract damages more or less probable.
Answer B: Admit the testimony, because Rachel's generosity makes it less likely that she caused serious loss.
Answer C: Admit the testimony only if Rachel testifies and is subject to cross-examination.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony only because it is cumulative character evidence.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The only disputed issue is damages under the contract. Rachel's general generosity does not bear on that amount. The evidence is excluded for lack of materiality and probative connection.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because generosity does not logically reduce contract damages. C: C is wrong because cross-examination does not cure lack of relevance. D: D is wrong because the threshold problem is no consequential fact connection, not cumulative proof.
