internal_id: TR-050
Outline_code: 61020301
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Falling below the standard of care
Question: A surgeon performed a routine procedure using a technique that experts testified was accepted by comparable surgeons. A rare complication occurred despite proper technique, leaving the patient with additional pain. The patient offers no evidence that the surgeon deviated from accepted professional practice. The patient sues the surgeon for negligence. Is the patient likely to prevail in a professional negligence claim against the surgeon?
Answer A: Yes, because the patient suffered a worse medical condition after the procedure
Answer B: No, because without evidence of breach of the professional standard, a bad outcome alone is insufficient
Answer C: Yes, because surgeons are strictly liable for complications from routine procedures
Answer D: No, because doctors can never be liable when a technique is common
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The expert testimony shows accepted professional practice, and the patient has no contrary breach evidence. A rare complication alone does not establish malpractice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Professional negligence requires breach of the applicable professional standard, not merely an unfortunate result. C: Medical malpractice is a negligence theory based on professional-standard breach, not strict liability for complications. D: Custom or accepted practice is powerful evidence, but the categorical statement is too broad. Here the patient loses because there is no breach evidence, not because common techniques are always immune.
lb_rule_reference: A bad professional outcome does not itself prove malpractice; the plaintiff must show breach of the applicable professional standard.
