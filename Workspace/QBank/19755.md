MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A private collector kept a small monkey in a locked indoor enclosure. The collector had obtained all required permits and used a double-latch system. During a home inspection, the monkey escaped when an inspector accidentally failed to close the outer latch. The monkey climbed onto the inspector and bit her hand while she tried to pull away. The inspector sues the collector for strict liability for animal harm. Is the inspector likely to prevail on a strict liability claim against the collector for harm caused by the monkey?
Answer A: Yes, because a monkey is a wild animal and the bite was a harm within the animal's characteristic risk
Answer B: No, because the collector used reasonable care by obtaining permits and installing a double latch
Answer C: No, because the monkey had never bitten anyone before
Answer D: Yes, because animal owners are strictly liable for every injury connected to any animal they possess
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Wild-animal strict liability does not depend on the collector's negligence. A bite during escape is the kind of characteristic animal risk that supports strict liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Reasonable care is not a defense to strict liability for harm caused by a wild animal's characteristic danger. The precautions might defeat negligence, but the call asks strict liability. C: Prior viciousness is not required for wild-animal strict liability. That knowledge requirement applies to domestic animals, not to a monkey kept as an exotic pet. D: Strict liability is not automatic for every animal. It depends on wild status, known dangerous propensity for domestic animals, or specific livestock rules, and the harm must be within the relevant risk.
lb_rule_reference: A possessor of a wild animal is strictly liable for harm resulting from the animal's characteristic dangerous propensities, even if the possessor used reasonable care.
