Outline: Preliminary questions
Question: In a federal embezzlement trial, the prosecutor offers a text message allegedly sent by Miriam to a banker. The message refers to Miriam's private account number and asks that funds be moved “before the auditors arrive.” Miriam objects that someone else could have used her phone. The judge admits the message after finding enough evidence for a reasonable jury to find that Miriam sent it. On appeal, Miriam argues that the judge erred by failing to find by a preponderance of the evidence that she personally sent the message. Is Miriam correct?
Answer A: Yes, because the judge must finally decide the authorship of every writing before it can reach the jury.
Answer B: Yes, because text messages cannot be authenticated circumstantially.
Answer C: No, because the judge only had to find evidence sufficient to support a jury finding of authorship; the jury could then decide whether Miriam sent it.
Answer D: No, but only because the original phone was produced at trial.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Authentication can involve a conditional relevance determination. The judge screens for evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims. The jury may then decide whether the condition is actually satisfied and what weight to give the message.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the judge need not make the ultimate authorship finding conclusively. B: B is wrong because circumstantial evidence can authenticate a message. D: D is wrong because the original phone is not the decisive issue; the issue is threshold authentication sufficiency.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / authenticity and jury weight
