MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Greg, Henry, and Iris agreed in January to rob a payroll truck in March. In February, Greg told Henry and Iris that he was out and wanted nothing more to do with the plan. Henry and Iris proceeded with the robbery in March without Greg. Greg did nothing to inform police or otherwise thwart the robbery. Greg is charged with (1) conspiracy to rob the payroll truck and (2) the substantive robbery itself. What is the most accurate statement of Greg's criminal liability after his February withdrawal?
Answer A: Greg is not guilty of either conspiracy or robbery because he withdrew before the robbery occurred
Answer B: Greg is guilty of conspiracy but not the robbery, because his withdrawal cuts off Pinkerton liability for subsequent acts
Answer C: Greg is guilty of both conspiracy and robbery because withdrawal requires him to affirmatively prevent the crime
Answer D: Greg is not guilty of conspiracy because he withdrew before the overt act was committed
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The conspiracy was formed in January and was then a completed offense. Greg's February withdrawal does not retroactively negate the conspiracy conviction. However, effective withdrawal does terminate Pinkerton (co-conspirator) liability for substantive crimes committed by Henry and Iris after the withdrawal. Because Greg communicated his withdrawal to his co-conspirators in February — before the robbery — and did nothing affirmative to facilitate the robbery, he is not liable for the March robbery under Pinkerton or as an accomplice. He remains guilty of the original conspiracy.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The conspiracy was complete the moment Greg, Henry, and Iris reached their agreement in January. Withdrawal in February comes after the crime of conspiracy was already committed. Withdrawal cannot undo a completed offense. Greg is guilty of conspiracy. He is also not guilty of the robbery itself (he did not participate), but not because of withdrawal — simply because he wasn't there and there is no independent basis for accomplice liability. C: To cut off Pinkerton liability for future crimes, the majority rule requires only timely communication of withdrawal to co-conspirators (or, under some formulations, an affirmative act to defeat the conspiracy). Greg communicated his exit in February. Under the majority approach, that communication is sufficient to terminate forward-looking liability for the robbery. The robbery conviction is not supported on the facts as given. D: The conspiracy was formed by the January agreement itself. Even if no overt act occurred until March, the conspiracy was a continuing offense from January forward. Moreover, Greg participated in the agreement, which satisfies any overt-act requirement attributable to him. And withdrawal in February does not undo the January agreement.
lb_rule_reference: A conspirator who effectively withdraws does not thereby withdraw from the conspiracy itself (which was already completed at the moment of agreement); withdrawal stops the running of the statute of limitations and ends Pinkerton liability for future acts, but does not retroactively defeat the conspiracy conviction
