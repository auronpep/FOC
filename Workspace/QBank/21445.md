Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: SummitFoods Parent Inc. is incorporated and headquartered in Delaware. Its wholly owned subsidiary, SummitFoods Retail LLC, operates grocery stores in Nevada. The parent observes corporate formalities, keeps separate bank accounts, and does not control day-to-day store operations. A plaintiff injured in a Nevada store sued the parent in Nevada, arguing that the subsidiary's store contacts automatically count as the parent's contacts. Are the subsidiary's contacts automatically enough for personal jurisdiction over the parent?
Answer A: Yes, because a parent always has the contacts of every wholly owned subsidiary
Answer B: Yes, because the plaintiff was injured in Nevada
Answer C: No, not automatically; the plaintiff needs facts supporting attribution such as alter ego, agency, or parental control beyond ownership
Answer D: No, because parent corporations can never be sued outside Delaware
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts show separateness. The subsidiary's forum operations do not automatically establish PJ over the parent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ownership alone does not collapse separate corporate identities for PJ. B: The question is whether the parent, not the subsidiary, has attributable Nevada contacts. D: A parent can be subject to specific jurisdiction elsewhere based on its own contacts or proper attribution facts.
lb_rule_reference: Corporate separateness is respected for PJ unless facts support agency, alter ego, or another recognized attribution theory.
