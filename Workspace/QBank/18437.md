Question: Caleb is charged with setting fire to a shed. An hour after the fire, an officer stopped Caleb two blocks away and noticed that his coat smelled strongly of smoke. Caleb objects that the smell is irrelevant because he had attended a cooking fire earlier that evening. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because the smoke odor has some tendency to connect Caleb to the shed fire; the cooking-fire explanation affects weight.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because the prosecutor cannot prove which fire caused the smell.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence unless the officer can identify the chemical source of the smoke.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if the cooking-fire evidence is excluded.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The odor of smoke near in time and place to the fire has some tendency to connect Caleb to the charged event. The alternative cooking-fire explanation may persuade the jury to discount the evidence but does not make it irrelevant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the proponent need not prove the exact source of the odor for relevance. C: C is wrong because chemical-source certainty is not required. D: D is wrong because competing explanations may both be presented for the jury to weigh.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401; FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats an alternative innocent explanation as a bar to admissibility.
