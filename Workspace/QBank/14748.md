Question: A defendant entered the county museum at a time when it was open to the public, intending to steal a Picasso etching. Once inside, he took what he thought was the etching from an unlocked display case and concealed it under his coat. However, the etching was a photocopy of an original that had been loaned to another museum. A sign over the display case containing the photocopy said that similar photocopies were available free at the entrance. The defendant did not see the sign. Burglary in the jurisdiction is defined as "entering a building unlawfully with the intent to commit a crime." The defendant is guilty of
Answer A: A: burglary and larceny.
Answer B: B: burglary and attempted larceny.
Answer C: C: larceny.
Answer D: D: attempted larceny.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. The defendant, with the intent to steal, took the photocopy of the etching from its display case and concealed it. Therefore, the defendant is guilty of larceny. It does not matter that the etching was only a copy and not the original. Factual impossibility, which is when the substantive crime is incapable of completion because of a fact unknown to the defendant, is not a valid defense. In this case, it does not negate the defendant's intent to commit the crime.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Because the defendant's entry was lawful, he did not commit a burglary. B is incorrect. As stated above, because the defendant's entry was lawful, he did not commit a burglary. The defendant successfully committed larceny when he left with the painting. D is incorrect. The attempted larceny charge merged into the larceny charge when the defendant actually left with the painting. Despite the fact that the painting was a photocopy, the defendant would not be guilty of attempted larceny because the crimes merged.
