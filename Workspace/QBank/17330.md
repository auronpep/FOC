Question: In a federal negligence trial, Martha claims she left a building because she believed it was unsafe. Martha's lawyer asks her, “What warning did Mary give you just before you left?” The question is offered only to show why Martha left the building, not to prove the warning was accurate. The defendant objects that the question calls for hearsay. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because every question asking what someone said calls for hearsay.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because the statement is offered to show its effect on Martha, not to prove the warning's truth.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Mary is unavailable.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because all safety warnings are automatically admissible for their truth.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A statement offered to show its effect on the listener or to explain the listener's conduct is not hearsay when the truth of the statement is not the point. The court may still limit the use if needed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every out-of-court statement is offered for truth. C: C is wrong because unavailability is not needed for this nontruth use. D: D is wrong because the statement is not automatically admitted for its truth.
