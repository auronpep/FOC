


BARMATRIX Q#: 14065
Outline_code: 91090200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jury Trials
Outline: Demand for Jury Trial
Question: A homeowner from State A sued a man from State B in a federal district court in State B. The single-count complaint alleged breach of contract, asked for the remedy of specific performance, and demanded a jury under the Seventh Amendment. The man argued to the court that the homeowner is not entitled to a jury. Is the homeowner entitled to a jury trial?
Answer A: A: Yes, because this is a civil case, and the plaintiff is seeking both legal and equitable remedies.
Answer B: B: Yes, because this is a civil case, and the plaintiff is seeking purely legal remedies.
Answer C: C: No, because this is a civil case, and the plaintiff is seeking purely equitable remedies.
Answer D: D: No, because the Seventh Amendment applies only to federal question lawsuits, and this is a diversity case.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. The Seventh Amendment guarantees a jury trial in suits at common law. Here, specific performance is the remedy requested, which is a purely equitable remedy. Therefore, the homeowner is not entitled to a trial by jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. While it is true that the Seventh Amendment's jury guarantee generally is available where a plaintiff seeks both legal and equitable remedies, the plaintiff here seeks an exclusively equitable remedy, which is not a suit entitled to a jury under federal law. B is incorrect. The Seventh Amendment guarantees a jury for suits at common law, and specific performance is a remedy in equity. D is incorrect. The Seventh Amendment's application does not depend on the basis of the court's subject-matter jurisdiction.
