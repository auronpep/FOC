Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Joshua signed a deed conveying Springacre to Caleb and gave it to Ezra, an independent escrow holder. Joshua instructed Ezra, “Deliver this deed to Caleb if Caleb wins the boundary lawsuit. I will not ask for it back.” Caleb did not know about the deed. Caleb later won the lawsuit, Ezra delivered the deed to Caleb, and Caleb accepted it. What is the best title result?
Answer A: Joshua kept title because Caleb did not know about the deed when Joshua first gave it to Ezra.
Answer B: Joshua kept title because an escrow condition can be used only for a sale, not a gift.
Answer C: Caleb took title when Joshua first signed the deed, because all escrow conditions are ignored.
Answer D: Caleb took title after the condition occurred and the deed was delivered, because Joshua had placed the deed beyond his control and Caleb accepted the beneficial conveyance.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is conditional escrow beyond the grantor's control. Joshua gave the deed to an independent holder, disclaimed any right to retrieve it, and specified an objective condition. Once Caleb won and accepted delivery, the deed operated to pass title.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because grantee awareness at the initial escrow deposit is not essential when acceptance later occurs. B: B is wrong because a conditional escrow delivery is not limited to purchase transactions. C: C is wrong because conditions in a valid third-party escrow are not ignored.
lb_rule_reference: Third-party escrow delivery can be effective even before the grantee knows, if the condition later occurs and acceptance is presumed
