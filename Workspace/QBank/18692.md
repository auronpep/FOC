Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff files a diversity action in federal court in State A against Defendant, a State B citizen. Defendant lives 60 miles from the federal courthouse but outside State A. State A's long-arm statute does not reach Defendant, and Defendant has no contacts with State A. Plaintiff serves Defendant at home in State B and argues that service within 100 miles is enough. Is Rule 4(k)(1)(B) a valid basis for personal jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because any defendant served within 100 miles of the federal courthouse is subject to personal jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because diversity jurisdiction substitutes for state long-arm authorization
Answer C: No, because Rule 4(k)(1)(B) is limited to certain joined parties and Defendant is an original defendant
Answer D: No, but only because Defendant is a citizen of a different state from Plaintiff
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Without state long-arm reach, consent, a federal service statute, or another Rule 4(k) basis, service within 100 miles does not cover this original defendant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The 100-mile bulge applies to parties joined under Rule 14 or Rule 19, not ordinary original defendants. B: Subject-matter jurisdiction does not establish personal jurisdiction over the defendant. D: The defect is the lack of a personal-jurisdiction hook, not the parties' diverse citizenship.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 4(k)(1)(B) does not create personal jurisdiction over an original defendant merely because the defendant was served within 100 miles of the courthouse.
