Question: A bus driver negligently stopped on an icy bridge without activating hazard lights, causing a small car to skid and hang partly over the guardrail. A passerby saw the trapped driver calling for help and carefully approached to pull him away from the car. While doing so, the passerby slipped on the same icy bridge and injured her knee. She sues the bus company for negligence. Is the passerby likely to be treated as a foreseeable plaintiff in a negligence claim against the bus company?
Answer A: Yes, because a reasonable rescue attempt is a foreseeable response to peril created by negligence
Answer B: Yes, because all bystanders are automatically owed strict liability protection during emergencies
Answer C: No, because rescuers voluntarily choose to encounter danger
Answer D: No, because the bus company did not owe a duty to anyone outside the bus
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The bus driver's negligence created an apparent peril. A reasonable rescue attempt was a foreseeable response, so the rescuer can be within the scope of liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The claim is negligence, not strict liability. The correct basis is foreseeability of reasonable rescue, not automatic strict liability to all bystanders. C: The rescue doctrine treats reasonable rescue as foreseeable; voluntary action does not automatically bar the rescuer's negligence claim. D: A negligent driver can owe reasonable care to foreseeable persons endangered by the driving, including foreseeable rescuers.
lb_rule_reference: A rescuer injured in a reasonable rescue attempt is generally a foreseeable plaintiff when the defendant's negligence created peril to another.
