Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Trial judge’s discretion to control order and presentation of evidence
Question: In a federal insurance trial, counsel asks Moses, “Yes or no, did you sign the form after the fire?” Moses answers, “I need to explain because there were two forms.” Counsel demands that Moses be required to answer only yes or no. The judge allows Moses to explain briefly which form he signed before answering. Was the judge's ruling proper?
Answer A: No, because a witness must always answer yes-or-no questions with only yes or no.
Answer B: Yes, because the court may control examination to make the testimony effective for determining the truth.
Answer C: No, because allowing explanation turns the witness into an expert.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Moses is a party rather than a nonparty witness.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may control the mode of interrogation. When a yes-or-no answer would be misleading, the judge may permit a brief explanation so the testimony is effective for determining the truth.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because yes-or-no form is not absolute. C: C is wrong because explaining ambiguity in a question is not expert testimony. D: D is wrong because the court's control applies to witness examination generally, not only party witnesses.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / misleading yes-or-no control
