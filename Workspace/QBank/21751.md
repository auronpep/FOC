Question: Daniel is charged in federal court with robbing a federally insured bank. The prosecutor supplies an official federal banking record showing that the bank was federally insured on the day of the robbery. The court properly takes judicial notice of that fact. What instruction should the court give the jury about the noticed fact?
Answer A: The jury must accept the noticed fact as conclusive because the court properly noticed it.
Answer B: The jury must accept the noticed fact unless Daniel proves the bank was not insured.
Answer C: The jury may, but is not required to, accept the noticed fact as conclusive.
Answer D: The court must refuse to instruct the jury because judicial notice is never allowed for facts connected to an element of a criminal charge.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: In a criminal case, even when judicial notice is properly taken, the court must instruct the jury that it may or may not accept the noticed fact as conclusive. The notice does not create a mandatory finding against the accused.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because civil conclusive language does not apply in criminal cases. B: B is wrong because the instruction cannot shift the burden to Daniel. D: D is wrong because judicial notice is not categorically forbidden in criminal cases; the jury-use instruction is limited.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 201(f) / criminal jury instruction
