Question: A state inheritance statute allows marital children to inherit from a father automatically but allows nonmarital children to inherit only if paternity was judicially established during the father's lifetime. A nonmarital child challenges the rule. What level of scrutiny generally applies?
Answer A: Rational basis, because inheritance is economic
Answer B: Intermediate scrutiny, requiring an important objective and substantial relation
Answer C: Strict scrutiny, because all family-law classifications are fundamental
Answer D: No scrutiny applies because inheritance is created by statute
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Legitimacy classifications are quasi-suspect for Equal Protection tier purposes.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Nonmarital-birth classifications generally receive intermediate scrutiny. C: Nonmarital-birth classifications receive intermediate scrutiny, not strict scrutiny by default. D: State-created benefits and rules remain subject to Equal Protection.
lb_rule_reference: Classifications based on nonmarital birth generally receive intermediate scrutiny and must be substantially related to an important government interest.
