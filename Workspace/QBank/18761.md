Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: A professional licensing board suspends an engineer's license for 30 days after a disciplinary hearing. The engineer sues, alleging due process violations. By the time the case reaches judgment, the 30-day suspension has ended, but the disciplinary finding remains on the engineer's record and automatically increases penalties for any future violation. Is the case moot?
Answer A: Yes, because the 30-day suspension has ended and the court cannot restore those days
Answer B: No, because the disciplinary finding still carries collateral legal consequences that effective relief could address
Answer C: Yes, because collateral consequences matter only in criminal cases
Answer D: No, but only because professional licensing is always a political question
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The finding remains on the record and affects future penalties. Relief could remove or reduce those legal consequences.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ongoing collateral legal consequences can keep a case live. C: The relevant question is whether continuing legal consequences remain, not whether the case is criminal. D: The live controversy comes from collateral consequences, not from political-question doctrine.
lb_rule_reference: A completed sanction may not moot a case if continuing collateral legal consequences remain.
