Question: In a federal civil case, the judge admits a signed receipt after finding that the foundation for authentication has been satisfied. At closing, Rachel wants to argue to the jury that the signature is forged and that the receipt should receive no weight. The opposing party objects that the judge already found the receipt authentic. May Rachel make the argument?
Answer A: No, because the judge's authentication ruling conclusively establishes that the signature is genuine.
Answer B: No, because authentication rulings are never subject to jury disagreement.
Answer C: Yes, because the judge's ruling allowed the evidence to be considered, but the jury may still decide what weight it deserves.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Rachel first moves to strike the receipt.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The judge's authentication ruling is an admissibility threshold. Once admitted, the jury may still decide whether the evidence is persuasive and what weight to give it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B are wrong because admissibility does not make the disputed fact conclusive.
