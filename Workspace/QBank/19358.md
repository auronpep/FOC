Question: Miriam hired Peter to restore three separate rooms in an inn. The written contract set a separate price of $7,000 per room, payable when each room was finished. Peter completed the first room and began the second. Miriam then barred Peter from the property and refused to pay anything. What is Peter's strongest contract claim?
Answer A: He cannot recover until all three rooms are restored because the work was part of one inn.
Answer B: He may recover only the reasonable value of labor because divided prices are irrelevant.
Answer C: He may recover the full price for all three rooms because Miriam prevented completion.
Answer D: He may recover the $7,000 contract price for the completed room and seek appropriate damages for being prevented from finishing the rest.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The contract is divisible because it assigns a separate price and payment time to each room. Peter completed the first unit, so Miriam's payment duty for that unit matured. Miriam's prevention of further performance may support additional damages, but it does not automatically make the full contract price due.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because separately priced units can make the contract divisible. B: B is wrong because completed divisible units can be compensated at contract price. C: C is wrong because prevention may support damages but does not automatically award the price for unfinished units.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law divisible contracts / unit completion
