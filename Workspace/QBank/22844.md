


BARMATRIX Q#: 22844
Outline_code: 94050100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Venue
Question: A man was injured when a train hit his truck at a railroad crossing in State A. Under the applicable federal statute, venue for the claim was proper in the judicial district where the railroad company's board of directors was located. In this case, the board of directors was located in State B. Consequently, the man filed his suit in federal court in State B. During discovery, the man discovered that the accident was likely caused by a faulty track switch. The company that built the track switch was located and incorporated in State C. The man added the track switch company as a defendant. The track switch company moved to dismiss the man's claim against it, correctly noting that it did no business and had no contacts at all with State B. Should the court grant the track switch company's motion?
Answer A: A: No, because the claim against the track switch company arose from the same accident.
Answer B: B: No, because the track switch company consented to State B jurisdiction when it provided track switches to the railroad company.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the track switch company had no contacts with State B.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the track switch company did not consent to State B jurisdiction.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under 28 U.S.C. §1367 and the doctrine of pendent venue, a claim arising out of a common nucleus of operative fact can be heard in the same venue as another claim if that other claim is properly venued. Here, since the federal statute states that the man's claim against the railroad company is properly venued in State B, the man's claim against the track switch company can also be heard in State B because the claim arose out of the same accident. B is incorrect because the track switch company would not have consented to jurisdiction simply by providing track switches to the railroad company. C and D are incorrect because of the doctrine of pendent jurisdiction.
