Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Diversity Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff, a citizen of California, sues a limited liability company for breach of contract in federal court, asserting diversity jurisdiction. The LLC was organized under Texas law. Its principal office is in Nevada. The LLC has four members: one citizen of Texas, one citizen of Nevada, one citizen of Oregon, and one citizen of California. The amount in controversy exceeds $75,000. The LLC moves to dismiss for lack of diversity jurisdiction. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Should the court dismiss for lack of diversity jurisdiction?
Answer A: No, because the LLC is a citizen only of Texas (state of organization) and Nevada (principal place of business), neither of which is California
Answer B: Yes, because the LLC is organized in Texas and its nerve center is in Nevada — both states are diverse from the California plaintiff under the Hertz test
Answer C: Yes, because the LLC is a citizen of every state in which any of its members is a citizen, including California, and the plaintiff is a California citizen — diversity is destroyed
Answer D: No, because only the LLC's managing member's citizenship counts for diversity purposes, and the managing member's state is not specified in the facts
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An unincorporated association, including an LLC, takes the citizenship of each of its members for § 1332 purposes. The LLC here has a California member, making the LLC a citizen of California. The plaintiff is also a citizen of California. Complete diversity is therefore absent, regardless of the LLC's organizational state or principal office. The court must dismiss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The corporate citizenship rule under § 1332(c)(1) applies only to corporations. An LLC is an unincorporated association and takes the citizenship of each of its members. Because one member is a California citizen, the LLC itself is a California citizen. The plaintiff is also a California citizen, so diversity is destroyed. B: The *Hertz* nerve-center test applies only to corporations. For unincorporated associations, citizenship follows member citizenship, not organizational state or office location. *Hertz* is irrelevant to LLC citizenship analysis. D: Federal courts look to the citizenship of all members of an LLC, not just the managing member or principal decision-maker. Every member's citizenship must be tested. If any member shares citizenship with an adverse party, diversity is absent.
lb_rule_reference: Unincorporated associations take the citizenship of every member for § 1332 diversity purposes; the corporate dual-citizenship rule does not apply.
