


BARMATRIX Q#: 22196
Outline_code: 31010407
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Refreshing recollection
Question: At the defendant's trial for murder, the arresting officer was called to the stand by the prosecution. The prosecution asked the officer to describe the unique murder weapon he had discovered at the scene. The officer said he thought that the weapon was a club of some sort, but he couldn't remember exactly what it was at that moment. The prosecutor showed the officer his police report from the scene. The officer read through the report and said that he could remember what the weapon looked like after looking at the report. The defense then objected to the officer's testimony. Should the court sustain the objection?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the officer stated that he couldn't remember the murder weapon.
Answer B: B: Yes, because only the report can be admitted into evidence since the officer stated that he couldn't remember.
Answer C: C: No, because the report itself was admissible as a past recollection recorded.
Answer D: D: No, because the officer remembered what the weapon was after reading the report.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The memory of a forgetful witness can be refreshed with anything, because that thing is not being admitted as an exhibit or used substantially. Here, the prosecutor is simply using the report to refresh the officer's memory, so the officer's testimony will be admissible. A is incorrect because a forgetful witness's memory can be refreshed with anything. B is incorrect because the fact the officer stated that he couldn't remember at first does not mean only the report itself is admissible. As stated above, the prosecutor can use the report to refresh the officer's memory without the report being admitted into evidence. C is incorrect because a past recollection recorded is only needed when an attempt to refresh a witness's memory fails.
