Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A mining supplier stored blasting caps in a secure shed. A lightning strike ignited the caps, causing an explosion and fire that damaged a neighboring barn. The supplier had followed ordinary storage precautions. The neighbor sues for strict liability. Which statement best explains the likely result?
Answer A: The supplier may be strictly liable because explosion and fire are within the abnormal risk of storing blasting caps
Answer B: The supplier cannot be strictly liable because lightning was the immediate trigger
Answer C: The supplier is liable for every injury on the neighbor's farm that day
Answer D: The supplier avoids strict liability because it followed ordinary storage precautions
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The harm flowed from the explosive quality of the stored blasting caps, the risk that may make the activity abnormally dangerous.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The injury still resulted from the abnormal explosive risk of storing blasting caps. C: Strict liability would not cover unrelated injuries disconnected from explosion or fire risk. D: Due care does not defeat strict liability for abnormal-risk harm.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity reaches the specific abnormal risk, such as explosion or fire from stored explosives, but not unrelated harms.
