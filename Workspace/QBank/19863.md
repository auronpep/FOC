Question: Police lawfully arrest Sara in her kitchen on a valid arrest warrant. At the moment of arrest, Sara's zipped backpack is on the chair next to her hand. Before moving Sara away, an officer opens the backpack and finds a loaded pistol. Was the backpack search valid as incident to arrest?
Answer A: Yes, because the backpack was within Sara's immediate control at the time of the lawful arrest
Answer B: No, because all closed containers require a warrant after arrest
Answer C: Yes, because arrest allows officers to search the entire house
Answer D: No, because an arrest warrant never permits any search
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The search was limited to a container in the grabbing area during a lawful custodial arrest, fitting the safety and evidence-preservation rationale.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Search incident to arrest can cover containers within the arrestee's immediate control, subject to limits. C: Search incident does not authorize a full house search. It reaches the person and grabbing area. D: The search is justified by the arrest-incident exception, not by the arrest warrant itself as a search warrant.
lb_rule_reference: Following a lawful custodial arrest, officers may search the arrestee and the area within immediate control to remove weapons and prevent destruction of evidence.
