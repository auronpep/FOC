Question: Congress authorizes an agency to issue grants for emergency bridge repairs. The statute says no grant above $10 million may take effect unless approved by the transportation committees of both houses. The agency awards a $15 million grant to a state after the committees approve it by letter. The President never receives any bill concerning the grant. A taxpayer with standing challenges the committee-approval condition. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The committee approval is valid because committees may exercise Congress's delegated oversight power
Answer B: The committee approval is constitutionally suspect because it gives binding legal effect to committee action without bicameralism and presentment
Answer C: The committee approval is valid because spending decisions never require presidential involvement
Answer D: The committee approval is invalid only if the agency disagrees with the committees' decision
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The grant's legal effect depends on committee approval. That is legislative action with legal consequences, so it must proceed through both houses and presentment rather than committee letters.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Oversight is not the same as legally binding action. Committee approval that controls legal effect bypasses bicameralism and presentment. C: Congress's spending power still operates through constitutionally valid legislation. Binding post-enactment committee control does not escape presentment. D: An agency cannot waive bicameralism and presentment requirements for Congress.
lb_rule_reference: A congressional committee cannot bind an agency or alter legal rights through a committee approval device that bypasses bicameralism and presentment.
