Question: Rebekah and Leah owned adjoining lots. A recent survey clearly marked the true boundary, and both women knew where it was. Without signing a deed, Rebekah orally agreed to let Leah treat an extra ten-foot strip of Rebekah's land as Leah's property because Leah wanted a larger garden. Leah immediately began planting the strip. Did the oral agreement transfer title to the ten-foot strip to Leah?
Answer A: Yes, because any agreement between neighbors fixes a new boundary.
Answer B: No, because a boundary-settlement doctrine does not transfer known land merely because one owner orally agrees to give it away.
Answer C: Yes, because Leah took possession immediately after the oral agreement.
Answer D: No, but only because garden land cannot be conveyed separately from a house lot.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The oral agreement did not transfer title. Boundary-settlement doctrine applies to uncertain or disputed boundaries. Where the true boundary is known and one owner is simply giving additional land to the other, a deed or other valid conveyance method is required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because boundary agreements do not operate as informal conveyances of known land. C: C is wrong because immediate possession under permission does not itself transfer title. D: D is wrong because the issue is lack of a valid conveyance, not whether garden land can be separately conveyed.
lb_rule_reference: Boundary agreement limits / no transfer where boundary known
