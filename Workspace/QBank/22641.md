


BARMATRIX Q#: 22641
Outline_code: 43020201
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Judicial Review
Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: A prisoner was convicted of capital murder and sentenced to confinement and death in a state prison. He filed a habeas corpus action that included several state and federal claims in state court. The state court ruled against him. Thirty- three days later, the prisoner filed an appeal to the state's supreme court. Under state rules, appeals had to be filed within 30 days. Based solely on the prisoner's failure to file in time, the state supreme court dismissed his appeal, ruling that he had defaulted on his state and federal claims. The prisoner then appealed the state supreme court's decision to the United States Supreme Court. Will the United States Supreme Court hear the prisoner's appeal?
Answer A: A: No, because the decision was based solely on the prisoner's failure to file in time.
Answer B: B: No, because the prisoner was sentenced to confinement and death in a state prison.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the appeal came from the state's supreme court.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the prisoner's claim contained federal issues.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: If there are adequate and independent nonfederal grounds to support a state decision, the United States Supreme Court will not hear the case. Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722 (1991). “Adequate” means that the nonfederal grounds must fully dispose of the case. “Independent” means that the decision regarding state law was decided using only state law. Here, the state supreme court made its decision solely on the fact that the prisoner missed the appeals deadline. The fact that the prisoner missed the deadline got rid of all of his claims, and there would be no reason to consult federal law to determine how a state court would apply the state's own filing deadlines. B is incorrect because the United States Supreme Court would hear the appeal no matter where the prisoner was confined if the state decision was based on federal law. C and D are incorrect because there were adequate and independent nonfederal grounds to support the state's decision.
