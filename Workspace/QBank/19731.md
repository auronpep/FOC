Question: Officers have probable cause to believe a motor home contains stolen electronics. The motor home is parked in a public lot, licensed, connected to no utilities, and ready to drive away. Officers search compartments inside without a warrant and find the electronics. Does the automobile exception support the search?
Answer A: Yes, because the motor home was readily mobile and being used like a vehicle in a public lot
Answer B: No, because any vehicle with sleeping space is always treated as a home
Answer C: No, because probable cause for a vehicle never excuses the warrant requirement
Answer D: Yes, because officers may search any vehicle during any traffic encounter
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The vehicle was mobile, licensed, and not fixed as a residence. With probable cause, the automobile exception allowed the search.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The setting and ready mobility matter. This motor home was objectively being used as a vehicle, not a fixed home. C: The automobile exception is a recognized warrant exception when probable cause exists. D: The exception requires probable cause. A mere traffic encounter would not authorize a full search.
lb_rule_reference: Probable cause permits a warrantless search of a readily mobile vehicle in a setting that objectively indicates vehicle use rather than fixed residential use.
