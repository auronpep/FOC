Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers illegally searched Arman's apartment and found a safe containing stolen passports. At the suppression hearing, the prosecutor argued that officers "probably would have asked Arman for consent later" and "might have tried to get a warrant," but no warrant application, active investigation step, inventory policy, or independent source existed before the search. Does inevitable discovery admit the passports?
Answer A: Yes, because officers could have sought consent or a warrant
Answer B: Yes, because physical evidence is always admissible despite an illegal search
Answer C: No, because the government offered only speculation and no concrete lawful discovery path
Answer D: No, because inevitable discovery applies only to live witnesses
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Nothing shows lawful discovery was inevitable. Officers were not already pursuing a warrant, inventory, or independent source. Speculative consent or warrant possibilities do not satisfy the doctrine.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Inevitable discovery requires proof of a concrete lawful path, not speculation about what officers could have done. B: Fourth Amendment physical evidence can be suppressed unless an exception applies. D: Inevitable discovery can apply to physical evidence. It fails here because proof is speculative.
lb_rule_reference: The prosecution must show a concrete lawful discovery path; speculation that officers could have obtained consent or a warrant is not enough.
