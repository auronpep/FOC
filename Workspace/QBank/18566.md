Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff, a State A citizen, sues Defendant 1, a State B citizen, in federal court for $100,000 from a crash. Plaintiff then seeks to add Defendant 2, a State A citizen, under Rule 20 for a related claim from the same crash. Plaintiff argues that supplemental jurisdiction covers the related claim. Can Plaintiff use supplemental jurisdiction for the claim against Defendant 2?
Answer A: Yes, because Rule 20 permits related defendants to be joined
Answer B: Yes, because the claim arises from the same crash
Answer C: No, because Section 1367(b) bars the plaintiff's claim against the nondiverse Rule 20 defendant in this diversity-only case
Answer D: No, but only because Defendant 2 was not part of the original complaint
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff cannot use supplemental jurisdiction to defeat complete diversity through a claim against a Rule 20 joined party.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 20 permits joinder procedurally but does not supply subject-matter jurisdiction. B: Same case or controversy is not enough when Section 1367(b) blocks plaintiff claims that evade diversity. D: Later-added parties can sometimes be joined; the problem here is the diversity plaintiff-side bar.
lb_rule_reference: In a case founded only on diversity, Section 1367(b) bars certain plaintiff claims against persons made parties under Rules 14, 19, 20, or 24 when exercising jurisdiction would be inconsistent with Section 1332.
