


BARMATRIX Q#: 14000
Outline_code: 93110300
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Appealability and Review
Outline: Scope of Review
Question: After a federal trial in which the jury awarded the plaintiff $100,000 in compensatory damages and $7 million in punitive damages, the defendant moved for a new trial on the ground that the verdict was not supported by the evidence and also that the punitive damages award was unconstitutionally excessive. The trial court, after reviewing the evidence, denied the motion on the condition that the plaintiff accept a reduced punitive damages award of $1 million, which the plaintiff did. The defendant wants to appeal. Which of the following principles governs the appellate court's review?
Answer A: A: The trial court cannot weigh the evidence in ruling on a motion for a new trial.
Answer B: B: The trial court must give the plaintiff the minimum amount that the jury could have awarded under the evidence.
Answer C: C: The trial court's decision to reduce the punitive damages award is reviewed de novo.
Answer D: D: The trial court's reduction of the punitive damages award improperly invaded the province of the jury.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. An award of punitive damages is subject to the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments to the Constitution. The state is prohibited from imposing grossly excessive punishments. When a trial court reduces a punitive damages award on the ground that it is unconstitutionally excessive, the appellate court reviews that determination de novo. See Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Leatherman Tool Group, Inc., 532 U.S. 424 (2001).
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure (FRCP) 59 allows a party to move for a new trial on the ground that the verdict is against the weight of the evidence. Accordingly, the trial court may weigh the evidence and the credibility of witnesses when considering a motion for a new trial. B is incorrect. When a new trial motion is denied on the condition that the plaintiff accepts a lower amount of damages than the jury awarded, the trial court has discretion to award any amount supported by the evidence. D is incorrect. Remittitur is constitutional and does not interfere with the province of the jury, so long as the trial court gives the plaintiff the option of proceeding with a new trial. See Dimick v. Schiedt, 293 U.S. 474, 486–487 (1935). Here, the trial court conditioned the denial of the defendant's motion for a new trial on the plaintiff's acceptance of a lower damages award, thereby giving the plaintiff the option of proceeding with a new trial.
