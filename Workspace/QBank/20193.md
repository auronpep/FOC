Question: Congress offers states public-safety funds on the condition that state agencies deny permits to speakers who criticize federal officials. The condition is written clearly and is related to public-safety administration. A state challenges the condition. What is the strongest constitutional objection?
Answer A: The condition is valid because clarity and relatedness are the only Spending Clause limits
Answer B: The condition is invalid because Congress may not buy state violations of independent constitutional rights
Answer C: The condition is invalid only if the state refuses the money first
Answer D: The condition is valid because states have no First Amendment obligations
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Conditioning funds on viewpoint discrimination collides with an independent First Amendment bar.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A condition also cannot require unconstitutional conduct. C: The constitutional defect is the condition's content. D: State agencies remain bound by incorporated constitutional rights.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may not use spending conditions to induce states to engage in conduct that would itself violate an independent constitutional bar.
