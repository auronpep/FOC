Question: In a federal drug-possession trial, an officer testifies that he seized a sealed bag of tablets from Jonah, marked it with his initials, and placed it in the evidence locker. The lab analyst testifies that she received a sealed bag with the same initials and tested the tablets. Jonah objects that the custodian who transported the bag did not testify. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the tablets because every person who touched the bag must testify.
Answer B: Admit the tablets if the chain evidence is sufficient to support authenticity; the missing custodian affects weight unless the gap suggests tampering.
Answer C: Exclude the tablets because fungible evidence can never be authenticated by markings.
Answer D: Admit the tablets only for impeachment, not as real evidence.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Chain of custody need not be perfect. The proponent must provide enough evidence for a reasonable finding that the item is what it purports to be; gaps ordinarily affect weight unless they create a serious tampering concern.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A demands an impossible chain. C is too broad; markings and sealing can help authenticate fungible evidence. D misroutes real evidence as impeachment-only.
