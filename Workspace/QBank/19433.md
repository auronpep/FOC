Question: A safari park kept zebras in a paddock. One zebra had never injured anyone. While a guide was moving guests along a marked path, the zebra suddenly kicked through a low rail and broke a guest's arm. If the guest asserts strict liability, which result is most likely?
Answer A: The park is strictly liable because the injury resulted from a dangerous tendency characteristic of zebras as wild animals
Answer B: The park is not strictly liable because the zebra had been trained and displayed in a park setting
Answer C: The park is strictly liable only if the rail violated a safety regulation
Answer D: The park is not liable unless the guest touched or provoked the zebra
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Strict liability covers characteristic wild-animal harms even when the particular animal has no known prior history.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Captive care and training do not convert a wild animal into an ordinary domestic animal for this rule. C: That would matter to negligence, but strict liability does not require proof of a breached safety rule. D: Provocation can matter when present, but the facts state only that the guest used the marked path.
lb_rule_reference: A possessor of a wild animal is strictly liable when the animal causes harm through a dangerous tendency characteristic of the species, such as kicking, biting, or trampling.
