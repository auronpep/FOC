Question: Abraham orally agreed to sell Sarah a small field for $80,000 and, in the same conversation, a tractor kept on the field for $12,000. No writing was signed by either party. Sarah paid nothing, took no possession of the field, and received no part of the tractor. When Abraham refused to perform, Sarah sued to compel both transfers. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The oral agreement is enforceable as to both the field and the tractor because both were discussed in one conversation.
Answer B: The oral agreement is enforceable as to the tractor but not the field because goods contracts are never within the Statute of Frauds.
Answer C: The oral agreement is generally unenforceable as to both transfers because the land sale and the goods sale each trigger a writing requirement and no exception applies.
Answer D: The oral agreement is void, so Sarah may not seek any restitutionary relief if she later paid part of the price.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The field sale is within the common-law land Statute of Frauds, and the tractor sale is a sale of goods for more than $500 under UCC §2-201. Because there is no signed writing and no part-performance, payment, receipt-and-acceptance, admission, or specially manufactured goods exception, the contract is generally unenforceable as to both transfers.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because combining land and goods in one conversation does not remove either writing requirement. B: B is wrong because goods contracts of $500 or more are within UCC §2-201. D: D is wrong because the usual consequence is unenforceability of the contract action, not automatic voidness for all purposes.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law land SOF + UCC §2-201
