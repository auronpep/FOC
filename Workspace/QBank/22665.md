


BARMATRIX Q#: 22665
Outline_code: 43020201
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Judicial Review
Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: State A passed a law that affirmed a legal understanding that a marriage could only be between a man and a woman. Several years later, the State A supreme court held that the term “marriage” included same- sex marriages. Later that year, State A citizens passed a proposition that amended the State A constitution to define “marriage” as a union between a man and a woman. A gay couple sued the state officials responsible for enforcing State A's marriage laws in federal court, arguing that the new proposition violated their Equal Protection rights. The federal court ruled that the new proposition was unconstitutional, and the state officials named in the lawsuit chose not to appeal. However, an association of churches that had been proponents of the new proposition moved to intervene and appeal the federal court's decision. May the association intervene and appeal the action?
Answer A: A: No, because the association lacked standing to bring the appeal.
Answer B: B: No, because the association did not intervene in the earlier action.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the association can invoke the standing of the state to appeal.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the association had been proponents of the new proposition that was ruled unconstitutional.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Because the determination of any constitutional issue is likely to become an important part of United States law, the requirement of standing is designed to assure that persons litigating constitutional issues have incentive to litigate them vigorously and effectively. For this reason, standing requires that a party seeking to assert a constitutional issue must have a personal stake in the outcome. Usually, this takes the form of an actual or immediately threatened concrete injury that would be prevented by a favorable determination of the claim. Importantly, if a party can show nothing more than a generalized grievance in vindicating a particular state law, the party does not have standing to appeal. Hollingsworth v. Perry, 570 U.S. 693 (2013). Here, the association can't show anything more than a generalized grievance in upholding the law. B is incorrect because the association had no standing to bring the appeal. C is incorrect because an appealing party must assert his or her own rights. D is incorrect because the association only has a generalized grievance.
