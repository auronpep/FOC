Question: A church school fires a teacher who leads daily prayer, teaches religious doctrine, and is held out by the school as responsible for faith formation. The teacher sues under a generally applicable employment-discrimination statute. What defense is the church school most likely to raise?
Answer A: The ministerial exception may bar the claim because the teacher performed important religious functions
Answer B: The school has no defense because neutral employment laws always override church autonomy
Answer C: The exception applies only to employees with the title "minister."
Answer D: The exception applies only when the teacher signed a written waiver of civil rights
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The exception protects religious organizations' control over employees who perform ministerial functions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The ministerial exception is a distinct constitutional protection. C: Function matters; title alone is not dispositive. D: The ministerial exception arises from constitutional church autonomy, not a waiver requirement.
lb_rule_reference: The religion clauses protect a religious organization's authority to select, supervise, and remove ministers from certain employment-discrimination claims.
