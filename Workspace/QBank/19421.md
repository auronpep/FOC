Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Appointment and removal
Question: Congress creates a consumer-finance bureau led by one director. The director may issue binding rules, bring enforcement actions, and impose civil penalties. The statute provides that the President may remove the director only for inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance. The President seeks to remove the director for policy disagreement. What is the President's strongest constitutional argument?
Answer A: The removal restriction is suspect because it protects a single director exercising substantial executive power from presidential control
Answer B: The removal restriction is automatically valid because Congress used traditional for-cause language
Answer C: The removal restriction is valid because civil penalties are not criminal punishment
Answer D: The removal restriction is invalid only if the director was also improperly appointed
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A single head with major rulemaking and enforcement authority exercises executive power. Strong for-cause protection for that official can unduly restrict the President's ability to supervise executive administration.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The validity of removal protection depends on structure and function. A single director exercising substantial executive power raises different concerns than a multimember expert commission. C: Enforcement of federal law through civil penalties is still executive power for removal analysis. D: A removal restriction can be unconstitutional even if the officer was validly appointed.
lb_rule_reference: Restricting the President's removal of a single director who exercises substantial executive power can violate separation of powers by unduly limiting presidential control over executive administration.
