Question: A producer contracted to pay an inexperienced performer a specified salary to act in a small role in a play the producer was taking on a six-week road tour. The contract was for the duration of the tour. On the third day of the tour, the performer was hospitalized with a stomach disorder. The producer replaced her in the cast with an experienced actor. One week later, the performer recovered, but the producer refused to allow her to resume her original role for the remainder of the tour. In an action by the performer against the producer for breach of contract, which of the following, if proved, would be the producer's best defense?
Answer A: A: The actor, by general acclaim, was much better in the role than the performer had been.
Answer B: B: The actor was the only replacement the producer could find, and the actor would accept nothing less than a contract for the remainder of the six-week tour.
Answer C: C: The producer offered to employ the performer as the actor's understudy for the remainder of the six-week tour at the performer's original salary, but the performer declined.
Answer D: D: Both the producer and the performer knew that a year earlier the performer had been incapacitated for a short period of time by the same kind of stomach disorder.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. If these facts were true, the producer would have had no choice but to hire the actor. To simply wait until the performer recovered, without a replacement or any idea of how long until she could resume working, would have greatly burdened the producer. Thus, by hiring the only available substitute, the producer's obligations under the contract with the performer became impracticable once she was able to resume working. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The relative quality of the actor's performance is not a circumstance that would give the producer the right to cancel the performer's contract because a change in quality is not sufficient to make it impracticable or impossible to carry out his performance duties. C is incorrect. These facts would not be the best basis for the producer's defense. As stated above, if the actor was the only substitute option and the producer had to hire the actor for the remainder of the tour, the producer could cancel the contract. A declined offer to hire the performer as an understudy would not provide an effective defense. D is incorrect. A history of the performer having been ill would not justify the producer's cancellation of the contract. In fact, the knowledge that the performer suffered from the same illness would weaken the producer's defense.
