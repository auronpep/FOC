Question: An environmental statute requires an agency to prepare a public impact analysis before approving a dam. The agency skips the analysis and approves construction near a river used regularly by a local hiker for recreation. The hiker sues to require the analysis, alleging that the missing procedure increased the risk of harm to the river area she uses. Which statement best describes standing?
Answer A: The hiker has no standing because procedural violations can never support Article III injury
Answer B: The hiker likely has standing because the skipped procedure affects her concrete recreational interest and a proper analysis could protect that interest
Answer C: The hiker has standing even if she has never visited the river because everyone has an interest in lawful procedures
Answer D: The hiker lacks standing unless she proves the agency would certainly reject the dam after the analysis
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The hiker uses the affected area, so she alleges a concrete interest. Requiring the agency to perform the analysis could influence the decision and protect that interest, which is enough for procedural standing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A procedural violation can support standing when tied to a concrete interest, such as use of the affected river area. C: A plaintiff still needs a concrete personal interest affected by the procedure. D: In procedural-rights cases, the plaintiff need not prove the agency would reach a different outcome, only that the procedure could protect her concrete interest.
lb_rule_reference: A procedural-rights plaintiff still needs a concrete interest affected by the challenged procedure, though redressability can be somewhat relaxed if correcting the procedure could protect that interest.
