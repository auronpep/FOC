


BARMATRIX Q#: 22148
Outline_code: 33040302
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Hearsay and Circumstances of Its Admissibility
Outline: Not requiring unavailability of declarant
Question: The mayor of a small city was walking with a group of local politicians around a newly refurbished city park. As they were walking, a dead branch fell from a tree and knocked the mayor to the ground. A politician asked the mayor if he was hurt, and the mayor said, “I think I have a concussion!” In response, the politician exclaimed, “I can't believe the tree trimmer we hired missed that dead branch!” The mayor was taken to the hospital, where he had to undergo emergency surgery to repair a life- threatening brain bleed caused by being hit with the branch. The mayor filed suit against the tree trimmer for his injuries. At trial, the mayor wanted to introduce the politician's statement. Is the statement admissible?
Answer A: A: No, because the statement is inadmissible hearsay.
Answer B: B: No, because the statement is inadmissible under the Confrontation Clause.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the statement is an excited utterance.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the statement qualifies as the statement of an opposing party through respondeat superior.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An excited utterance is admissible as an exception to the hearsay rule if the declarant makes a statement relating to a startling event while the declarant is still under the excitement caused by the event. Here, the politician saw the branch hit the mayor and expressed concern, so she was probably still under the excitement of the event when she made her statement. Thus, the statement is admissible. A is incorrect because the politician's statement qualifies as an excited utterance. B is incorrect because the Confrontation Clause only applies to criminal cases. D is incorrect because the politician is not a party to the mayor's suit against the tree trimmer.
