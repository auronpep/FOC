Question: During a federal civil trial, the judge mistakenly excludes a duplicate copy of an email. The same email's contents are admitted through the original email and through undisputed testimony. The losing party moves for a new trial, arguing that any evidentiary mistake automatically requires a new trial. How should the court analyze the request?
Answer A: Deny relief if the error was harmless and did not affect substantial rights
Answer B: Grant a new trial automatically because any evidentiary error is reversible
Answer C: Enter JMOL for the losing party because an evidentiary ruling occurred during trial
Answer D: Dismiss the case without prejudice because harmless error is jurisdictional
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Civil procedure does not grant new trials for every technical mistake; harmless errors are disregarded.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The harmless-error principle rejects automatic relief for every evidentiary mistake. C: JMOL turns on legal sufficiency of evidence, not the mere existence of an evidentiary ruling. D: Harmless error is not a subject matter jurisdiction problem.
lb_rule_reference: Courts disregard harmless errors; trial error warrants relief only when it affects substantial rights or otherwise meets the governing standard for relief.
