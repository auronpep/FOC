MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Solicitation
Question: During a school safety drill, an instructor tells a trainee, "Pretend I am a criminal client. I want you to break into the supply room tonight." The instructor immediately explains that the exercise tests how trainees respond to illegal requests. The instructor does not want the trainee to commit any crime. The instructor is charged with solicitation to commit burglary. What is the instructor's strongest defense?
Answer A: The instructor lacked the specific intent that the trainee commit burglary
Answer B: The instructor is guilty because the words requested a burglary
Answer C: The instructor is guilty unless the trainee refused out loud
Answer D: The instructor is not guilty only because supply rooms contain low-value property
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Solicitation requires a genuine intent that another person commit the target crime. The instructor's words were part of a drill, not an effort to bring about a burglary.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The offense requires both communication and specific intent that the crime occur. C: The recipient's response is not the missing element. The missing element is the instructor's intent. D: The property value is irrelevant to the specific intent requirement for solicitation.
lb_rule_reference: Solicitation requires specific intent that the solicited crime be committed; a request made without that intent does not satisfy the offense even if the words describe a crime.
