Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Moses owned a small parcel. A deed stated, “I convey the parcel to Aaron.” Because Moses could not write, he placed an X on the signature line intending the mark to be his signature, and a neighbor wrote “Moses” beside the mark at Moses's request. Moses then handed the deed to Aaron intending an immediate transfer. Aaron did not sign the deed. Who owns the parcel?
Answer A: Moses, because an X mark can never satisfy the signature requirement for a deed.
Answer B: Aaron, because a mark made with intent to authenticate the deed can satisfy the grantor-signature requirement.
Answer C: Aaron only if the deed was sealed or witnessed by two people.
Answer D: Moses, because Aaron did not sign the deed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is the grantor signature. A grantor may sign by mark if the mark is made with intent to authenticate the instrument. The grantee's signature, a seal, and two witnesses are not ordinary deed-validity requirements on these facts, so title passes to Aaron.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a mark can operate as a signature when intended as one. C: C is wrong because the question gives no seal-or-two-witness requirement. D: D is wrong because the grantee generally need not sign the deed to receive title.
