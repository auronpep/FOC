Question: Leah owned Lot 1 and Lot 2. Before selling Lot 1 to Joseph, Leah used a paved road across Lot 2 every day to reach the only loading dock on Lot 1. The road was obvious from inspection. Leah sold Lot 1 to Joseph by a deed that did not mention the road. Leah later sold Lot 2 to Daniel. Daniel inspected Lot 2 before buying and saw the paved road and tire tracks leading to Lot 1. Daniel argues that Joseph cannot claim any implied easement because Daniel had no actual conversation about the road before closing. What is the best response?
Answer A: Joseph may enforce the implied prior-use easement because the visible road gave Daniel inquiry notice of the use.
Answer B: Joseph cannot enforce the easement because only actual spoken notice binds a successor.
Answer C: Joseph cannot enforce the easement because implied easements are automatically destroyed by any later sale of the servient parcel.
Answer D: Joseph can enforce the easement only by proving title to the road strip.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The prior-use facts support implication at severance, and a later purchaser of the servient land may be charged with inquiry notice from visible conditions. Daniel saw the road and tire tracks leading to Lot 1, so lack of an actual conversation does not defeat the claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because actual spoken notice is not the only notice route. C: C is wrong because a later sale of the servient parcel does not automatically extinguish an existing easement. D: D is wrong because Joseph claims a use right, not fee title.
lb_rule_reference: Visible implied easement and successor notice
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is converting notice into actual conversation and ignoring visible-use inquiry notice.
