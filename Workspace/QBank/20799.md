MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Cause in fact (actual cause)
Question: A city negligently failed to salt a bridge before dawn. A driver skidded on invisible black ice and was injured. Evidence shows that the bridge was already covered by a thick sheet of oil left by an unknown truck and that the driver would have skidded in the same way even if the city had salted the bridge. Is the city's failure to salt an actual cause of the driver's injury?
Answer A: Yes, because any negligent omission near the accident is an actual cause
Answer B: Yes, because the city owed drivers a duty to maintain bridges
Answer C: No, because the injury would have occurred in the same way even if the city had salted
Answer D: No, because omissions can never be actual causes
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The but-for test is not satisfied on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Actual cause still requires a causal connection. B: Duty and breach do not themselves establish cause. D: Omissions can be actual causes when the harm would have been avoided by reasonable action.
lb_rule_reference: A negligent act is not an actual cause under the but-for test if the same injury would have occurred at the same time without it.
