Question: A state requires food trucks to pass a low-cost annual sanitation inspection before selling food in public parks. A vendor argues the law violates substantive due process because it interferes with his economic liberty to operate without inspection. What standard most likely applies?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny, because every occupational rule burdens a fundamental right to work
Answer B: Intermediate scrutiny, because business licensing is always quasi-suspect
Answer C: Rational basis, because the rule is ordinary economic and health regulation that does not burden a fundamental right
Answer D: No review at all, because substantive due process never applies to economic regulation
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Sanitation inspections for food vendors are classic health and economic regulations reviewed deferentially.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ordinary economic regulation usually receives rational basis review. B: Business licensing is not a quasi-suspect classification. D: Deferential review is still review.
lb_rule_reference: Ordinary economic and social welfare regulation that does not burden a fundamental right generally receives rational basis review under substantive due process.
