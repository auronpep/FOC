Outline: Res ipsa loquitur
Question: An apartment elevator suddenly dropped several feet while a tenant was inside. The landlord hired an elevator company for monthly service, but the landlord kept responsibility for daily operation and safety complaints. The tenant cannot identify which component failed. Which statement best describes the tenant's res ipsa theory against the landlord?
Answer A: It fails automatically because an outside service company also touched the elevator
Answer B: It may proceed if the jury can find the likely negligence was within the landlord's operational responsibility
Answer C: It succeeds automatically because elevators always fall only when landlords are negligent
Answer D: It fails because res ipsa is limited to injuries from medical instruments
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The question is whether the circumstances reasonably support an inference against the landlord.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Literal exclusive control is not always required when the evidence points to the defendant's responsibility. C: Res ipsa permits, but does not require, the negligence inference. D: The doctrine applies across negligence settings, including common carrier and premises equipment cases.
lb_rule_reference: Modern res ipsa focuses on whether the negligence was probably within the defendant's responsibility, not on rigid literal exclusive control.
