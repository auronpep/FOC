Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: In an agricultural county where ordinary pesticide spraying is routine, a farm hired a service to apply a common low-toxicity spray under calm weather conditions. A neighbor alleged nausea after smelling the spray and sued the service under strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity. Which fact most strongly supports denying strict liability?
Answer A: The service was paid to perform the spraying
Answer B: The activity was common in the area and did not present a high serious risk that remained despite reasonable care
Answer C: The neighbor smelled the spray before becoming nauseated
Answer D: The farm rather than the service owned the land being sprayed
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Strict liability requires more than ordinary danger; commonness, context, and unavoidable serious risk matter.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Payment does not decide whether the activity is abnormally dangerous. C: That supports causation in a broad sense but does not establish abnormal danger. D: Land ownership does not supply the required abnormal-danger elements.
lb_rule_reference: A dangerous activity is not abnormally dangerous merely because harm is possible; courts consider whether the serious risk remains despite due care and whether the activity is uncommon or inappropriate in the area.
