Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal civil case, Deborah offers testimony from a phone-company employee that company logs show a call was placed from Nathan's phone to Deborah's phone at 9:12 p.m. Deborah offers the testimony only to show that a call occurred, not to prove what was said during the call. Nathan objects that Deborah must produce the original recording of the call. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because any proof about a phone call requires the original recording.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because the evidence is offered to show the occurrence of the call, not the contents of a recording.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because phone-company logs are never relevant unless the conversation is recorded.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Deborah proves what was said during the call.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The original-writing rule is triggered when a party seeks to prove the contents of a writing, recording, or photograph. Deborah is offering the log evidence to show the occurrence and timing of a call, not the contents of a recording.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a call's occurrence may be proved without the original recording. C: C is wrong because call logs may be relevant to timing and occurrence. D: D is wrong because Deborah's purpose is not to prove the conversation's words.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 1002; content-versus-event
lb_student_diagnostic: Content-versus-event confusion: student treats occurrence proof as content proof.
