Question: David signed and delivered to Jonathan a deed stating, “I convey my north field to Jonathan as a gift.” The deed accurately described the field and was accepted by Jonathan. Jonathan paid nothing. No statute in the jurisdiction requires consideration for a deed. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The deed is invalid because every conveyance of land requires consideration.
Answer B: The deed creates only an executory contract because Jonathan did not pay for the field.
Answer C: The deed is invalid unless Jonathan later gives David nominal consideration.
Answer D: The deed can transfer title to Jonathan even though Jonathan paid nothing.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is deed formalities, not bargain consideration. A delivered deed may operate as a gift conveyance when the required deed elements are present. Because David signed and delivered a deed with operative words and a sufficient description, title can pass to Jonathan without payment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because consideration is not required for deed validity. B: B is wrong because a delivered deed transfers title rather than merely creating a contract promise. C: C is wrong because later nominal consideration is unnecessary.
lb_rule_reference: Consideration not required
