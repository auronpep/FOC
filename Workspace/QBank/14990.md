Question: A land owner owned in fee simple Lots 1 and 2 in an urban subdivision. The lots were vacant and unproductive. They were held as a speculation that their value would increase. The land owner died and, by his duly probated will, devised the residue of his estate (of which Lots 1 and 2 were part) to his sister for life with remainder in fee simple to his niece. The land owner's executor distributed the estate under appropriate court order, and notified the sister that future real estate taxes on Lots 1 and 2 were her responsibility to pay. Except for the statutes relating to probate and those relating to real estate taxes, there is no applicable statute. The sister failed to pay the real estate taxes due for Lots 1 and 2. To prevent a tax sale of the fee simple, the niece paid the taxes and demanded that the sister reimburse her for same. When the sister refused, the niece brought an appropriate action against the sister to recover the amount paid. In such action, the niece should recover
Answer A: A: the amount paid, because a life tenant has the duty to pay current charges.
Answer B: B: the present value of the interest that the amount paid would earn during the sister's lifetime.
Answer C: C: nothing, because the sister's sole possession gave the right to decide whether or not taxes should be paid.
Answer D: D: nothing, because the sister never received any income from the lots.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. Since the land is vacant and unproductive, it is not generating any income to the sister; as a result, the sister qualifies for an exception to the general rule that the life tenant pays the taxes due on real property she possesses.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. This answer would be correct if the lots were generating income to the life tenant sufficient to pay the taxes due on the lots. However, since the land is unproductive, the sister, in this case, may not be compelled to reimburse the niece. B is incorrect. Because the property is not producing income, the life tenant is not obligated to pay property taxes out of her pocket. C is incorrect. The life tenant's obligations are not determined by whether she possesses the land and are not subjectively determined by the preferences of the life tenant (sister in this case).
