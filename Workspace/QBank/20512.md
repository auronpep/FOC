Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: Samuel sues Deborah for negligence after a bicycle collision. Samuel offers evidence that Deborah has a large savings account, arguing that wealthy people should be more careful in public. Deborah objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence, because a party's wealth is always relevant in civil cases.
Answer B: Admit the evidence only if Samuel seeks punitive damages.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence only if Deborah agrees to pay any judgment.
Answer D: Exclude the evidence, because Deborah's savings account does not make negligence in the bicycle collision more or less probable on these facts.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Deborah's wealth has no logical connection to whether she acted negligently in the bicycle collision. It does not make any fact of consequence more or less probable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because wealth is not automatically relevant in civil litigation. B: B is wrong because the problem is lack of logical connection to the negligence issue as framed. C: C is wrong because relevance does not depend on Deborah agreeing to pay a judgment.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating wealth as a background fact with automatic legal relevance.
