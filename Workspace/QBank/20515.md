Question: A pedestrian sues a delivery company after being struck by a van. The company denies that it owned or controlled the van. The pedestrian offers an insurance policy listing the company as the insured owner of that specific van, and requests a limiting instruction that the policy not be used to prove negligence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the policy for the limited purpose of proving ownership or control, if otherwise admissible.
Answer B: Exclude the policy because liability insurance is never admissible in negligence cases.
Answer C: Admit the policy for all purposes, including proving negligence.
Answer D: Exclude the policy unless the company first argues that it lacked funds to pay a judgment.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 411 bars liability insurance to prove negligence or wrongdoing, but permits it for other purposes such as ownership, control, agency, or bias. Because control/ownership is disputed and a limiting instruction is requested, limited admission is proper if other rules are satisfied.
