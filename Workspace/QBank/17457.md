Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A manufacturer filed a federal declaratory-judgment action asking for a declaration that it did not infringe a competitor's federal patent. The competitor had threatened to sue for patent infringement under federal law. The manufacturer seeks no damages, only a declaration of noninfringement. Does federal-question jurisdiction exist?
Answer A: No, because declaratory judgments never create federal jurisdiction
Answer B: No, because the manufacturer is asserting only a defense to infringement
Answer C: Yes, because the threatened coercive action is a federal patent-infringement claim
Answer D: Yes, but only if complete diversity also exists
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The competitor's coercive claim would arise under federal patent law, so the declaratory action may rest on federal-question jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Declaratory judgment is procedural, but jurisdiction can exist if the underlying coercive claim would arise under federal law. B: The court realigns the dispute by looking to the threatened coercive patent claim. D: Federal-question jurisdiction does not require diversity.
lb_rule_reference: In declaratory-judgment posture, federal-question jurisdiction depends on whether the coercive action that would have been brought by the declaratory defendant would arise under federal law.
