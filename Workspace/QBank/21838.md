internal_id: TR-019
Outline_code: 61020500
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Damages
Question: A delivery rider let a coworker use her electric scooter to cross the street and return within ten minutes. The coworker instead rode it to another town, kept it overnight, and crashed it into a curb. The scooter's frame was bent, and repairs would cost nearly its full value. The rider sues the coworker for conversion. Is the rider likely to prevail in a conversion claim against the coworker?
Answer A: No, because the coworker initially had permission to use the scooter
Answer B: No, because temporary use can never be conversion
Answer C: Yes, because the coworker seriously exceeded the permitted use and caused near-total damage
Answer D: Yes, but only for the cost of charging the scooter battery
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The coworker transformed a ten-minute local use into an overnight out-of-town use and caused serious damage. That degree of dominion supports conversion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Substantially exceeding the permitted use can become serious dominion inconsistent with the owner's rights. B: Temporary use can be serious enough for conversion when the deprivation, misuse, and damage are substantial. D: The interference was far more serious than battery depletion. Conversion generally permits recovery of full market value, not a token use cost.
lb_rule_reference: A substantial unauthorized use that seriously damages or deprives the owner of a chattel can amount to conversion rather than mere trespass to chattels.
