Question: Esther contracted to sell a delivery route to Daniel. Daniel promised to pay Esther's creditor Benjamin $10,000 after Esther transferred the route. Esther transferred the route and customer list, but one customer file was missing. The missing file caused Daniel $500 in repairable loss. Daniel refused to pay Benjamin anything. What is the likely result?
Answer A: Daniel owes Benjamin nothing because any defect in Esther's performance bars the beneficiary claim.
Answer B: Daniel cannot use the minor defect to avoid the entire beneficiary payment, though he may have an appropriate offset or damages claim against Esther.
Answer C: Daniel owes Benjamin nothing unless Benjamin replaces the missing customer file.
Answer D: Benjamin may recover only if Esther formally assigned the delivery-route contract to him.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An intended beneficiary's rights are subject to the promisor's valid defenses, but a minor defect after substantial performance does not automatically excuse the entire payment duty. Daniel may have damages or an offset for the missing file, but he cannot use it to avoid the entire beneficiary payment if Esther substantially performed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because any defect is not automatically total breach. C: C is wrong because Benjamin is not required to cure Esther's minor defect before enforcing the beneficiary promise. D: D is wrong because Benjamin's route is creditor-beneficiary status, not assignment.
