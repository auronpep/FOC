Question: Aaron owned two adjoining lots. Sometimes, when walking for exercise, Aaron crossed Lot B on the way from his house on Lot A to a scenic hill. The route was not marked, improved, or used for access to any building or utility on Lot A. Aaron later sold Lot A to Miriam. The deed was silent about Lot B. Miriam claims an implied easement by prior use to cross Lot B for walks. What is the best result?
Answer A: Miriam has the easement because Aaron crossed Lot B before severance.
Answer B: Miriam has an easement by necessity because exercise routes are necessary for residential enjoyment.
Answer C: Miriam has title to the path because walking is open use.
Answer D: Miriam likely has no implied easement because Aaron's occasional personal walking route was not an apparent, continuous use reasonably necessary to Lot A.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A prior-use easement requires more than a personal habit of the common owner. The use must be apparent, continuous, and reasonably necessary to the enjoyment of the parcel. Aaron's occasional unmarked walking route does not satisfy those requirements.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because any prior crossing is not enough. B: B is wrong because recreational convenience is not strict necessity. C: C is wrong because walking does not establish title on these facts. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Prior-use easement requires use tied to land enjoyment
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating the owner's personal habit as a land-based quasi-easement.
