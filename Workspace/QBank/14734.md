Question: A state statute provides as follows: "The maintenance of any ongoing enterprise in the nature of a betting parlor or bookmaking organization is a felony." A prosecutor has evidence that a woman has been renting an office to a man, that the man has been using the office as a betting parlor within the meaning of the statute, and that the woman is aware of this use. Which of the following additional pieces of evidence would be most useful to the prosecutor's effort to convict the woman as an accomplice to the man's violation of the statute?
Answer A: A: The woman was previously convicted of running a betting parlor herself on the same premises.
Answer B: B: The woman charges the man considerably more in rent than she charged the preceding tenant, who used the office for legitimate activities.
Answer C: C: The woman has personally placed bets with the man at the office location.
Answer D: D: The man has paid the woman the rent in bills that are traceable as the proceeds of gambling activity.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. Showing that the woman benefits from the gambling by charging higher rent for the office would indicate her personal stake in the continuing success of the man's criminal venture (and thus her intent to aid in that venture).
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The woman's prior conviction would not necessarily show that she was aiding the man in his criminal venture. C is incorrect. Showing that the woman has placed bets would confirm that she knows that the premises are being used for gambling. However, it would not necessarily show that she was aiding the man in his criminal venture. The woman could have lost money by placing bets with the man, and thus not benefitted at all from the gambling. D is incorrect. The source of the rent payments, assuming that the rent is not above the market price for the premises, would not necessarily show that the woman was aiding the man in his criminal gambling venture.
