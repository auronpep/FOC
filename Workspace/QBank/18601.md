Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: Police arrested Dana in a crowded subway station moments after witnesses reported that Dana threw a backpack containing a homemade explosive into the station. Before giving Miranda warnings, an officer asked, "Where is the backpack?" Dana pointed to a trash can, and officers safely removed the device. Is Dana's answer admissible despite the lack of Miranda warnings?
Answer A: No, because custodial interrogation always requires warnings before any question
Answer B: No, because public safety applies only to firearms, not explosives
Answer C: Yes, because the question was limited to locating an immediate threat in a crowded public place
Answer D: Yes, because emergency questioning waives all later Miranda rights
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The officer asked a narrow safety question to locate a dangerous device before it harmed the public. Miranda warnings were not required before that limited question.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The public-safety exception permits limited unwarned questioning aimed at neutralizing an immediate threat. B: The doctrine turns on immediate danger, not a firearm-only category. D: Public safety is limited. Later investigative questioning generally requires warnings and waiver.
lb_rule_reference: Officers may ask limited questions reasonably prompted by an immediate public-safety concern before giving Miranda warnings.
