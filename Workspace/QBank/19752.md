Question: John agreed to remodel Peter's kitchen for $75,000. Peter paid $55,000 during the job, leaving $20,000 unpaid. John substantially completed the work, but the cabinets were installed improperly. The reasonable cost to repair the cabinets is $6,000. John sues Peter for the unpaid balance. What is the proper result?
Answer A: John recovers the full $20,000 because he substantially performed.
Answer B: John recovers $14,000, the unpaid balance minus Peter's repair-cost damages.
Answer C: John recovers nothing because any defect bars recovery under a construction contract.
Answer D: Peter recovers $6,000 and also keeps the entire $20,000 unpaid balance.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Substantial performance keeps the contract price claim alive, but the owner receives damages for defects. The usual result is contract price less the reasonable cost to repair. John's unpaid balance is $20,000, reduced by $6,000, leaving $14,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because substantial performance does not excuse compensating Peter for defects. C: C is wrong because minor or repairable defects do not automatically forfeit the contract price. D: D is wrong because that would double-count the same $6,000 defect and overcompensate Peter.
lb_rule_reference: Common law construction damages; repair cost setoff
