Outline: Contribution and indemnity
Question: A delivery employee negligently struck a pedestrian while making an assigned delivery. The pedestrian sued both the employee and the employer. The employer had not been negligent in hiring, training, or supervising the employee, but was held vicariously liable under respondeat superior and paid the full judgment. The employer now seeks reimbursement from the employee. What claim is the employer most likely to bring against the employee after paying the vicarious liability judgment?
Answer A: Contribution only, because both employer and employee were named defendants in the pedestrian's suit
Answer B: Indemnity from the employee, because the employer's liability was purely vicarious and the employee was the active tortfeasor
Answer C: No claim, because respondeat superior makes the employer primarily liable for the employee's tort
Answer D: A second action against the pedestrian to recover any amount above the employee's personal assets
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A vicariously liable employer that is without personal fault may seek indemnity from the employee whose negligence caused the judgment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The employer's liability was purely vicarious and not based on its own fault. Indemnity can shift the entire loss to the active tortfeasor. C: Respondeat superior governs plaintiff recovery. It does not prevent a fault-free employer from seeking indemnity from the negligent employee. D: The pedestrian received proper satisfaction. The reimbursement target is the active tortfeasor, not the injured plaintiff.
lb_rule_reference: A party held vicariously or derivatively liable without personal fault may seek indemnity from the active tortfeasor whose conduct caused the loss.
