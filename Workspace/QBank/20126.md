Question: Moses orally agrees to buy a set of hand tools from Aaron for $480. Aaron immediately sets aside the tools but does not deliver them. Moses refuses to pay, and Aaron sues for breach. Moses raises the Statute of Frauds as his only defense. How should the court rule on that defense?
Answer A: Reject it, because the UCC goods Statute of Frauds applies only to contracts for the sale of goods priced at $500 or more.
Answer B: Sustain it, because every contract for goods must be in a signed writing.
Answer C: Sustain it, because tools are movable goods and therefore always within the Statute of Frauds.
Answer D: Reject it only if Moses is a merchant.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The UCC goods Statute of Frauds applies to contracts for the sale of goods for a price of $500 or more. A $480 tools contract is not within that writing requirement. Merchant status is not needed to defeat this defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the UCC threshold is $500 or more. C: C is wrong because the fact that tools are goods identifies the source law but does not itself trigger the writing requirement. D: D is wrong because merchant status is irrelevant when the contract price is below the threshold.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(1)
