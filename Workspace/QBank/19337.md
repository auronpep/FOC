Question: A district judge denies a motion and says in a footnote, "This issue is important." The judge does not certify the order for interlocutory appeal. The losing party files a notice of appeal claiming Section 1292(b) appeal as of right. Is the party correct?
Answer A: Yes, because important questions are automatically appealable
Answer B: Yes, because district-court certification is unnecessary
Answer C: No, because Section 1292(b) requires proper certification and appellate permission
Answer D: No, because interlocutory appeal is impossible in federal court
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The statute creates a discretionary, two-step interlocutory route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Importance alone is not enough. B: Certification is required before the court of appeals may permit the appeal. D: It is possible when the statutory requirements are satisfied.
lb_rule_reference: A controlling legal question with substantial ground for difference of opinion and material advancement is not enough unless the required certification and permission steps occur.
