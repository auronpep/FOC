Question: Miriam contracted with Isaac to create a customized training program for Isaac's company. Isaac paid Miriam $20,000 at signing and later repudiated. Before the repudiation, Miriam reasonably spent $70,000 developing the program. After the repudiation, Miriam spent another $12,000 finishing optional materials even though they would not be useful without Isaac's cooperation. Miriam salvaged reusable training templates worth $5,000. Miriam cannot prove expected profit with reasonable certainty. What is Miriam's reliance recovery?
Answer A: $57,000, because all development spending should be counted, less payment and salvage.
Answer B: $45,000, because recoverable reliance is $70,000 minus the $20,000 payment and $5,000 salvage.
Answer C: $65,000, because salvage should be credited but Isaac's payment should not reduce reliance damages.
Answer D: $70,000, because that is the amount spent before repudiation.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is reliance under common law after repudiation. The formula is reasonable pre-repudiation expenditures minus benefits already received and retained salvage: $70,000 − $20,000 payment − $5,000 salvage = $45,000. The $12,000 spent after repudiation on optional unusable materials is excluded by the mitigation gate. The result scope is limited net reliance. The answer does not decide expectation profits because those profits are not reasonably certain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it includes avoidable post-repudiation spending, a WAL-REM-010 error. C: C is wrong because it ignores the payment offset and overcompensates Miriam. D: D is wrong because it ignores both payment and salvage offsets.
lb_rule_reference: Common law reliance damages — pre-repudiation expenditures minus payments, salvage, and avoidable post-repudiation costs
