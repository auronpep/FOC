Outline: Common objections to questions
Question: In a federal assault trial, Deborah asks Samuel what time he arrived at the market. Samuel answers, “About 8 p.m.” Deborah asks the same question two more times, and Samuel gives the same answer each time. Deborah then asks it a fourth time. The opposing party objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because a party may repeat any relevant question.
Answer B: Overrule the objection unless Samuel changes his answer.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because the question has been asked and answered.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if the repeated question is also hearsay.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may control the examination to avoid wasting time. A repeated question that has already been answered may be barred as asked and answered.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevance does not allow unlimited repetition. B: B is wrong because the rule does not require waiting until the witness changes answers. D: D is wrong because asked-and-answered is a separate courtroom-control objection.
