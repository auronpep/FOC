Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A homeowner sues a solar-panel company in federal court for state-law fraud, alleging that the company lied about installation costs. The complaint pleads only state fraud elements. The company says the case belongs in federal court because it will defend by arguing that federal energy regulations authorized its disclosures. Does federal-question jurisdiction exist?
Answer A: Yes, because the defense will require interpretation of federal regulations
Answer B: Yes, because any mention of federal regulations in the dispute creates jurisdiction
Answer C: No, because the complaint itself pleads only state-law fraud and the federal issue appears only as a defense
Answer D: No, because federal-question cases require complete diversity
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1331 depends on the plaintiff's properly pleaded claim. The defendant's federal defense is ignored for this purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A federal defense does not satisfy the well-pleaded complaint rule. B: Federal background facts are not enough unless the plaintiff's claim arises under federal law. D: Complete diversity is a Section 1332 requirement, not a Section 1331 requirement.
lb_rule_reference: Federal-question jurisdiction must appear on the face of the plaintiff's well-pleaded complaint, not in a defense or anticipated defense.
