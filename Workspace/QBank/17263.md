Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: A defendant answers a breach-of-contract complaint and denies liability. The answer does not mention the statute of limitations, even though the defendant knew all facts supporting that defense. Months later, on the eve of trial, the defendant seeks to rely on limitations for the first time. The plaintiff objects. What is the plaintiff's strongest argument?
Answer A: The limitations defense should have been affirmatively pleaded in the answer
Answer B: The defense is preserved because a general denial preserves every defense
Answer C: The defense is preserved because limitations can be raised only at trial
Answer D: The defense is unavailable only if the plaintiff proves bad faith
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Statute of limitations is an affirmative defense under Rule 8(c). A defendant who omits a known affirmative defense risks forfeiture or exclusion absent proper amendment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Affirmative defenses are avoidance matters that must be affirmatively stated. A general denial is not enough. C: Rule 8(c) requires pleading the defense in the responsive pleading. Trial is too late without amendment or lack of prejudice circumstances. D: The problem is failure to plead a required affirmative defense, not only bad faith. Prejudice and amendment may matter, but bad faith is not the threshold requirement.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 8(c) requires a party responding to a pleading to affirmatively state affirmative defenses such as statute of limitations, res judicata, release, and similar avoidance defenses.
