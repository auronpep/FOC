Question: In a federal civil trial, Samuel testifies for David. The opposing party calls Eli, who testifies that Samuel has a reputation for untruthfulness. David then calls Deborah to testify that Samuel's reputation in the same community is for truthfulness. The opposing party objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because truthful-character evidence is always inadmissible bolstering.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because Samuel's character for truthfulness has been attacked, and reputation support is now permitted.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if Samuel first denies Eli's testimony.
Answer D: Sustain the objection unless Deborah can identify a specific truthful act by Samuel.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness's truthful character may be supported by reputation or opinion evidence only after the witness's character for truthfulness has been attacked. Eli attacked Samuel's character for truthfulness by reputation testimony for untruthfulness, so Deborah's reputation support is permitted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because support is permitted after an attack on character for truthfulness. C: C is wrong because the rule does not require Samuel personally to deny the attack. D: D is wrong because specific truthful acts are not the proper direct form for Rule 608(a) support.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(a) / truthful-character support after attack
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the rehabilitation sequence after a Rule 608(a) untruthfulness attack.
