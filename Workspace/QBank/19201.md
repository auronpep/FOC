Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A landowner hired an independent blasting company to remove rock for a driveway on the landowner's residential lot. The contractor controlled the blasting details. A blast cracked the foundation of the neighbor's house. The neighbor sues the landowner for strict liability. What is the landowner's best defense based only on independent-contractor status?
Answer A: The landowner automatically avoids strict liability because the contractor controlled the blasting details
Answer B: Independent-contractor status alone does not defeat strict liability for abnormal-risk blasting harm
Answer C: The landowner is liable only if the contractor was an employee under agency law
Answer D: The neighbor loses because foundation cracks are economic loss only
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The harm came from the very blasting risk. The landowner cannot rely solely on contractor status to avoid strict liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Abnormally dangerous activity liability generally cannot be avoided merely by delegating the work. C: The claim is strict liability for abnormally dangerous activity, not ordinary respondeat superior. D: Property damage from the abnormal risk can support strict liability.
lb_rule_reference: A party responsible for an abnormally dangerous activity generally cannot avoid strict liability for abnormal-risk harm merely by hiring an independent contractor to perform it.
