Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Lydia contracted to buy 10 copying machines from a seller for $4,000 each, with Lydia responsible for $2,000 total installation costs under the original deal. The seller breached. Lydia reasonably covered by buying equivalent machines for $4,300 each, and the cover seller included installation at no additional charge. Lydia had no other losses. What is Lydia's proper recovery?
Answer A: $1,000, the cover differential less the installation expense saved.
Answer B: $3,000, the cover differential without considering saved installation expenses.
Answer C: $5,000, the cover differential plus the saved installation expenses.
Answer D: $43,000, the total price Lydia paid for the substitute machines.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Cover damages are the cover price minus the contract price, plus proper incidental or consequential damages, less expenses saved. Lydia's cover differential is $3,000 (($4,300 - $4,000) × 10). She saved $2,000 in installation costs, so her recovery is $1,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it ignores expenses saved because of the breach. C: C is wrong because saved expenses are subtracted, not added. D: D is wrong because the buyer does not recover the full substitute purchase price.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-712; UCC §1-305
