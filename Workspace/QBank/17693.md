Question: Abraham and Sarah signed a contract under which Abraham would pay Mary $12,000. The contract stated, “Abraham and Sarah may change or eliminate the payment-to-Mary term until Mary sends written acceptance of this benefit.” Mary received a copy of the contract but did not respond. Before Mary accepted or relied, Abraham and Sarah reduced the payment to $4,000. Mary then sent written acceptance and sued for $12,000. What is the likely result?
Answer A: Mary may enforce $12,000 because she was the named beneficiary in the first contract.
Answer B: Mary may enforce $4,000 because the parties reserved a power to change the beneficiary term and exercised it before Mary accepted or relied.
Answer C: Mary may enforce nothing because a reservation of power makes every beneficiary incidental.
Answer D: Mary may enforce $12,000 because receipt of a copy automatically vested her rights.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The contract expressly reserved a power to modify the beneficiary term before Mary's written acceptance. Mary did not accept or rely before the reduction, so her later acceptance vested only the $4,000 benefit that remained.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because naming Mary did not override the reserved pre-acceptance modification power. C: C is wrong because a reserved power to modify does not make the beneficiary incidental. D: D is wrong because receipt of a copy alone did not vest her rights.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — reserved modification power
