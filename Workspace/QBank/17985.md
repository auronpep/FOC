Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: A motorist was pulled over for speeding. Under state law, the offense was a non-arrestable infraction for which officers were required to issue a citation. The officer issued the citation, but before letting the driver go, conducted a full search of the passenger compartment "incident to the traffic stop." The officer found a bag of heroin under the seat. The driver moves to suppress. Should the court suppress the heroin found under the seat?
Answer A: No, because the officer had lawful authority to stop the driver, and SITA applies to any lawful police encounter
Answer B: No, because traffic stops create a per se exception to the warrant requirement that allows a full vehicle search
Answer C: Yes, because SITA applies only to custodial arrests; issuing a citation does not constitute a custodial arrest and does not authorize a full vehicle search
Answer D: Yes, because the officer searched the vehicle after the traffic stop was effectively completed, making the search untimely
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Knowles v. Iowa held that SITA's two rationales — officer safety and evidence preservation — are diminished to nonexistence when police issue a citation rather than making a custodial arrest. The officer here issued a citation as required by state law — no custodial arrest occurred. Without a custodial arrest, SITA provides no authority to search the vehicle. No other exception (automobile exception requires probable cause; consent was not given) applies. Suppression is required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: SITA's two rationales — officer safety and preservation of evidence — apply to custodial arrests, not to traffic citations. Knowles v. Iowa expressly held that when an officer issues a citation rather than making a custodial arrest, the arrest justifications are absent and SITA does not authorize a full search. B: No such per se exception exists. During a traffic stop, officers may conduct a Terry frisk for weapons if they have reasonable suspicion the driver is armed and dangerous, and they may use the automobile exception if they have probable cause to believe the vehicle contains contraband. A full search requires more than the stop itself. D: The timing problem is secondary to the fundamental issue: no custodial arrest ever occurred. Even if the officer had searched before issuing the citation, SITA would still not apply because there was no custodial arrest. Knowles is categorical: the exception requires arrest, not merely a lawful stop.
lb_rule_reference: SITA is valid only incident to a lawful custodial arrest; where state law requires issuance of a citation rather than a custodial arrest, SITA does not apply (Knowles v. Iowa)
