Question: A client who speaks no English meets with an English-speaking lawyer. A professional interpreter translates the client's statements so the lawyer can give legal advice. The interpreter agrees to keep the consultation confidential. The opposing party later argues that the interpreter's presence destroyed privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: The privilege was preserved because the interpreter was reasonably necessary for the legal consultation.
Answer B: The privilege was destroyed because any third person outside the lawyer-client relationship waives confidentiality.
Answer C: The privilege applies only if the interpreter is a licensed lawyer.
Answer D: The privilege applies only to the lawyer's statements, not to the client's translated statements.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The presence of a third party reasonably necessary to obtain or provide legal advice, such as an interpreter, does not destroy attorney-client confidentiality. The protected object remains the legal-advice communication, routed through a necessary agent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A is wrong because Correct answer B is wrong because This is third-party overwaiver. Not all third-party presence destroys privilege. Necessity matters. C is wrong because A necessary interpreter need not be a lawyer to preserve confidentiality.
