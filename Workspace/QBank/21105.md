Question: Nora gives a sealed envelope containing rare coins to a courier and asks him to deliver the sealed envelope to a buyer. During delivery, the courier opens the envelope, removes two coins, reseals the envelope, and keeps the coins. What is the best common-law theft classification for the coins?
Answer A: Embezzlement only, because the courier had lawful possession of the envelope
Answer B: Larceny, because breaking the sealed envelope and taking the coins was a trespassory taking of the contents
Answer C: False pretenses, because Nora trusted the courier
Answer D: No theft offense, because Nora voluntarily handed over the envelope
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The breaking-bulk doctrine treats the courier's opening of the sealed package and removal of contents as larceny of the contents.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The courier's lawful possession of the sealed container does not necessarily give lawful possession of the contents after breaking bulk. C: False pretenses requires obtaining title by false representation. No title passed to the courier. D: Consent to transport a sealed envelope did not authorize opening it and converting its contents.
lb_rule_reference: Under the breaking-bulk doctrine, a bailee entrusted with a closed container who opens it and converts part of the contents can commit larceny of the contents.
