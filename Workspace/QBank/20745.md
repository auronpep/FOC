Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Aaron contracted to paint Moses's house for $12,000. Before Aaron began work, Moses wrongfully cancelled. Aaron would have spent $7,000 on labor and materials and had incurred no costs yet. Aaron could not find substitute work for the same period. What is Aaron's expectation recovery?
Answer A: $5,000.
Answer B: $7,000.
Answer C: $12,000.
Answer D: Nothing, because Aaron had not begun performance.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When the owner breaches before the service contractor begins work, the contractor's expectation is the profit that would have been earned: contract price minus costs saved by not performing. $12,000 minus $7,000 equals $5,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because saved costs are not damages. C: C is wrong because the full contract price overcompensates Aaron by ignoring costs he did not incur. D: D is wrong because the absence of begun performance does not eliminate expectation damages.
lb_rule_reference: Expectation damages / service contractor lost profit
