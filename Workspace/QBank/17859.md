Outline: Larceny
Question: In a jewelry store, Mara picks up a ring from an open display tray, slips it into her sleeve, and turns toward the exit intending to keep it. A guard stops her after she has moved only two steps from the tray. Has the carrying-away element of larceny been satisfied?
Answer A: Yes, because even slight movement of the property is enough
Answer B: No, because the property must be removed from the building
Answer C: No, because the guard stopped Mara before she sold the ring
Answer D: No, because touching store goods is always permitted until the customer exits
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Mara moved the ring from the owner's possession with intent to steal; asportation need not be far.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Larceny does not require leaving the premises. C: Sale is not an element of larceny. D: Consent to inspect does not include hiding the item with intent to steal.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law larceny requires trespassory taking and carrying away, but the asportation element is satisfied by even minimal movement.
