Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A ladder manufacturer knew homeowners often stood on the top step to reach gutters, even though a small label said not to. The top step looked like a standing platform, and adding a molded cap would have made standing there difficult at low cost. A homeowner stood on the top step, the ladder tipped, and the homeowner was injured. How should the misuse fact affect the design-defect claim?
Answer A: It automatically bars recovery because the homeowner ignored the label
Answer B: It can support liability if the misuse was foreseeable and a reasonable design could reduce it
Answer C: It proves manufacturing defect because this ladder tipped
Answer D: It eliminates all comparative-fault issues because strict liability ignores user conduct
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The manufacturer knew the top-step behavior was common, and the product's appearance encouraged it. Foreseeable misuse is part of design analysis, not an automatic bar.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Known, common misuse can be foreseeable and relevant to whether the design should reduce the risk. C: The facts attack the intended design and foreseeable use pattern across the product line. D: Strict liability does not erase all plaintiff-conduct analysis; foreseeable misuse and comparative responsibility may matter.
lb_rule_reference: Foreseeable misuse may be folded into defect analysis and does not automatically bar recovery.
