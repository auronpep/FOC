Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: A federal court sitting in diversity in State C enters judgment on a state-law contract claim. A later court must determine the judgment's claim-preclusive effect. A party argues that every federal judgment has one uniform federal preclusion rule regardless of the basis of jurisdiction. Is that argument correct?
Answer A: Yes, because all federal judgments use the same federal preclusion rule
Answer B: No, because a federal diversity judgment commonly borrows the preclusion law that would apply in the state where the federal court sat
Answer C: Yes, because diversity cases are really federal claims
Answer D: No, because federal diversity judgments are never preclusive
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Diversity judgments on state-law claims often track state preclusion law absent a conflicting federal interest.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The basis and source of the prior judgment matter. C: Diversity supplies a federal forum, not a federal substantive claim. D: Federal diversity judgments can be preclusive.
lb_rule_reference: Do not apply one generic federal preclusion answer without asking the source and basis of the prior judgment.
