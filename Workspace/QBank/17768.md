Question: A careless cyclist struck a pedestrian on a walking path. The pedestrian suffered a bruised shoulder that healed without medical treatment and missed no work, but the bruise caused several days of pain and limited sleep. The cyclist argues that the pedestrian cannot recover in negligence because there were no medical bills or lost wages. May the pedestrian recover pain-and-suffering damages in a negligence claim against the cyclist?
Answer A: Yes, because pain from a physical injury can be compensable even without medical bills or lost wages
Answer B: Yes, because negligence always permits punitive damages when conduct is careless
Answer C: No, because negligence requires economic loss
Answer D: No, because bruises are too minor to count as actual harm as a matter of law
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The pedestrian suffered an actual physical injury. Pain and suffering caused by that injury are compensable even if economic losses are small or absent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Ordinary negligence does not automatically support punitive damages. The proper recovery is compensatory pain and suffering for actual physical injury. C: Economic loss is not required when there is physical injury. Noneconomic pain and suffering can be compensable. D: A minor physical injury can still be actual harm. The amount may be modest, but the damages element is satisfied.
lb_rule_reference: Pain and suffering from a proven physical injury can be compensable negligence damages even when the plaintiff has little or no economic loss.
