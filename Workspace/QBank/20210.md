Outline: Personal knowledge
Question: In a federal contract case, Naomi is asked, “Did Solomon sign the delivery agreement?” Naomi answers that she did not see the signing, but Deborah told her that Solomon signed it. Solomon objects. The testimony is offered to prove that Solomon signed the agreement. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony because Naomi has personal knowledge that Deborah made the statement.
Answer B: Admit the testimony if Deborah is generally reliable.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony only if the original delivery agreement is unavailable.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony because Naomi lacks personal knowledge of the signing and is repeating Deborah's out-of-court assertion for its truth.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Naomi may have personal knowledge that Deborah spoke, but she has no personal knowledge that Solomon signed. Because the testimony is offered to prove the signing through Deborah's assertion, the foundation fails and the hearsay problem is not cured by Naomi's hearing the statement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because personal knowledge of the statement's making is not personal knowledge of the signing. B: B is wrong because reliability alone does not supply personal knowledge or a hearsay route. C: C is wrong because the problem is not the original-writing rule; it is personal knowledge and hearsay.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 / no personal knowledge of underlying fact
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the statement-versus-fact distinction in personal-knowledge foundation.
