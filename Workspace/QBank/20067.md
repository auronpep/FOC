Question: Plaintiff seeks damages for breach of contract and an injunction enforcing the same contract. Both claims turn on whether defendant breached. The judge proposes to hold a bench trial first on the injunction and decide breach before the jury hears the damages claim. Is that sequencing proper over plaintiff's objection?
Answer A: Yes, because equitable claims are always tried first
Answer B: Yes, because the judge's equity findings can bind the later jury
Answer C: No, the jury should decide common breach facts before the judge resolves equitable relief
Answer D: No, because injunctions are legal remedies
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Legal damages preserve the jury right on shared factual issues.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common facts tied to legal claims must be preserved for the jury. B: That would undermine the jury right on common legal facts. D: Injunctions are equitable; the point is shared facts with a legal damages claim.
lb_rule_reference: When legal and equitable claims share factual issues, the jury must decide the common facts before the court resolves equitable relief.
