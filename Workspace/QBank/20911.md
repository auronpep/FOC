Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Impeachment and other Congressional limits on executive
Question: After a cyberattack disables a port's scheduling software, the President directs federal transportation officials to coordinate temporary vessel-routing priorities at the port for ten days. Congress has not enacted a statute approving or rejecting temporary federal routing during cyber emergencies. A shipping company argues that the order is invalid because Congress never gave the President express authority to control port routing. Which statement best describes the separation-of-powers analysis?
Answer A: The order is automatically invalid because the President needs express statutory authority for every emergency action
Answer B: The order is automatically valid because Congress has not forbidden it
Answer C: The order falls in Youngstown's middle category, so its validity depends on the practical balance between executive authority, congressional silence, and historical practice
Answer D: The order is valid only if the shipping company is foreign-owned
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Congress has neither authorized nor prohibited the temporary routing order. That places the President in the zone of twilight, where courts assess the action in light of independent presidential power, congressional inertia or acquiescence, and practical history.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Youngstown does not make every action without express statutory authority invalid. Congressional silence places the action in a middle category requiring a more contextual assessment. B: Lack of prohibition does not give the President unlimited domestic authority. The question still asks whether the action is supported by independent executive power and historical practice. D: The problem turns on congressional silence and domestic emergency administration, not on the ownership of one regulated company.
lb_rule_reference: When the President acts without either congressional authorization or congressional prohibition, the validity of executive action depends on the balance of congressional silence, historical practice, and the President's independent constitutional powers.
