Question: Adam privately told his lawyer, Ruth, that he had seen loose bolts on a ladder before he fell. In Adam's later negligence suit, the defendant asks Adam at a deposition whether he personally saw loose bolts before the fall. The defendant does not ask what Adam told Ruth. How should the court rule on Adam's privilege objection?
Answer A: Sustain it, because Adam first disclosed the fact during a privileged consultation.
Answer B: Overrule it, because the privilege protects the communication with Ruth, not Adam's underlying personal knowledge of facts.
Answer C: Sustain it unless Adam expressly waives all attorney-client privilege in the lawsuit.
Answer D: Overrule it because filing a lawsuit automatically waives every attorney-client communication related to the accident.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications made for legal advice; it does not make the underlying facts immune from discovery. Adam may refuse to repeat what he told Ruth, but he must answer from personal knowledge whether he saw the loose bolts. The decisive axis is protected communication versus independently known fact.
