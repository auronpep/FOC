Outline: Double Jeopardy
Question: The state charged Iris with aggravated assault. On the first day of trial, twelve jurors were selected through voir dire. Before the selected jurors were administered the oath, the prosecutor discovered a critical witness had become unavailable. The judge granted the prosecutor's motion to continue, dismissed the jurors without swearing them in, and rescheduled the trial. Iris moves to bar retrial on double jeopardy grounds. Is Iris's double jeopardy claim valid?
Answer A: Yes, because the selection of the twelve jurors through voir dire initiated the trial proceedings, attaching jeopardy
Answer B: Yes, because the prosecution's motion to continue constituted a mistrial that bars retrial once a jury has been seated
Answer C: No, because jeopardy had not yet attached — the jurors were selected but not yet sworn before the continuance was granted
Answer D: No, because the judge — not the prosecutor — granted the continuance, and judicial action cannot trigger double jeopardy
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The Double Jeopardy Clause bars retrial after jeopardy has attached. In a jury trial, jeopardy attaches when the jury is sworn — the administration of the oath is the constitutional threshold. Here, voir dire was completed and jurors were seated, but the oath had not been administered before the continuance was granted. Because jeopardy had not attached, the subsequent prosecution does not violate the Double Jeopardy Clause. Iris's motion should be denied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Double jeopardy attaches in a jury trial when the jury is sworn — not when the jury is selected. Voir dire completion and seating jurors is a necessary prerequisite, but the constitutional moment of attachment is the administration of the oath. Jurors who are selected but not yet sworn have not triggered jeopardy. Iris's motion fails because jeopardy had not yet attached. B: A mistrial only implicates double jeopardy if jeopardy has already attached. Since the jurors were never sworn, jeopardy had not attached when the continuance was granted. There was no mistrial in the double jeopardy sense — only a continuance before jeopardy began. The prosecution's role in seeking the continuance is irrelevant to the attachment analysis. D: Whether a judge or prosecutor initiates a continuance or mistrial is relevant to whether retrial is barred after jeopardy has attached (under the manifest necessity or prosecutorial misconduct analysis), not to the threshold question of whether jeopardy attached in the first place. The correct reason there is no double jeopardy bar here is that jeopardy had not yet attached, not that judicial action is categorically exempt.
lb_rule_reference: In a jury trial, double jeopardy attaches when the jury is sworn; a dismissal or mistrial before the jury is sworn does not bar retrial.
