Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Gideon, a neighbor with no engineering training, testifies that he saw a boiler explode. Gideon's lawyer asks, “Was the boiler's pressure valve defectively designed?” The manufacturer objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the question calls for specialized technical opinion not shown to be within Gideon's lay knowledge.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because Gideon may not describe the explosion he saw.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because anyone who sees an accident may testify to any cause.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because product design is always within common experience.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Gideon may describe what he observed, but whether a pressure valve was defectively designed requires specialized knowledge absent from the facts. The objection to improper lay technical opinion should be sustained.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because Gideon may testify to what he saw. C: C is wrong because observation of an accident does not qualify the witness to give technical design opinions. D: D is wrong because product design is not automatically common lay knowledge.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 701 / technical opinion overreach
