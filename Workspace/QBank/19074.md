Question: Jonah has a diagnosed compulsion disorder. He knows that setting fires is illegal and morally wrong, and he understands that lighting a curtain will burn a building. During an episode, he feels an uncontrollable urge and sets fire to an empty warehouse. The jurisdiction applies only the M'Naghten test, with no irresistible-impulse supplement. Does Jonah have a successful insanity defense?
Answer A: Yes, because he could not control his conduct
Answer B: Yes, because a diagnosed compulsion disorder automatically satisfies insanity
Answer C: No, because under pure M'Naghten he knew the nature of the act and knew it was wrong
Answer D: No, because insanity is never available for property crimes
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Pure M'Naghten is cognitive. Jonah understood that he was burning a building and understood that doing so was illegal and morally wrong. His inability to resist might matter under a jurisdiction that uses irresistible impulse or MPC, but those tests are not supplied here.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The jurisdiction uses only M'Naghten. M'Naghten does not include an inability-to-control prong. B: A diagnosis is only a threshold. The defendant must meet the legal test. Jonah knew the nature and wrongfulness of his act. D: Insanity can apply to property crimes if the test is satisfied. Jonah loses because the jurisdictional test is not met, not because arson is categorically excluded.
lb_rule_reference: Pure M'Naghten asks cognitive knowledge of nature and wrongfulness; it does not excuse a defendant solely because he could not resist an impulse
