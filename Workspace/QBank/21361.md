Question: A drain cleaner emitted dangerous fumes if mixed with bleach, a foreseeable household combination. The only warning was a tiny phrase on the back label saying "avoid incompatible household substances," with no mention of bleach or fumes. A consumer mixed the products and inhaled fumes. What is the best warning-defect analysis?
Answer A: The manufacturer wins because any warning language is enough
Answer B: The consumer has a plausible warning claim because the warning was vague and buried
Answer C: The consumer automatically wins because any injury involving chemicals is strict liability
Answer D: The claim must be manufacturing defect because the bottle contained chemicals
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The risk was serious, foreseeable, and nonobvious in its chemical consequences. A warning that fails to identify bleach or fumes may not be adequate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Warning adequacy depends on clarity, specificity, and prominence. A vague buried phrase may be inadequate. C: The plaintiff still must prove warning defect and causation. D: The product contained the intended chemicals; the problem is the warning about foreseeable mixing risk.
lb_rule_reference: A warning must be adequate in content and presentation; a vague or buried warning may not satisfy the duty.
