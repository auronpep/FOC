Question: A state requires all witnesses in court to swear "so help me God" and refuses to allow a secular affirmation. A witness with sincere religious objections to oaths is barred from testifying. What is the best constitutional objection?
Answer A: The state may not force a religious oath when a secular affirmation can serve the same truth-telling function
Answer B: The oath is valid because courtroom tradition always defeats religious objections
Answer C: The witness loses because only majority religions receive oath accommodations
Answer D: The oath is valid because testimony is a privilege, not a right
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Government cannot condition legal participation on affirming religious language when an affirmation alternative is available.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Tradition does not authorize compelled religious affirmation. C: Constitutional protection does not depend on majority status. D: Government may not impose unconstitutional religious conditions on participation.
lb_rule_reference: Government generally may not condition public office or legal participation on affirming a religious belief; secular affirmations can serve the same legal function as religious oaths.
