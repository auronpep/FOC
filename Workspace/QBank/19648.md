Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Ruth, a retailer, orally agrees to buy 700 clay tablets from Samuel, a seller, at $2 per tablet. Neither party signs any writing, no goods are shipped, no payment is made, and neither party later admits the contract in court. Ruth refuses to buy the tablets, and Samuel sues for the price. Which Statute of Frauds result is most accurate?
Answer A: Samuel may enforce the oral contract because both parties clearly agreed to the price and quantity.
Answer B: Ruth's defense should succeed because the contract is for the sale of goods priced at $500 or more and no UCC writing or exception is present.
Answer C: Ruth's defense should fail because the Statute of Frauds affects only land and suretyship contracts.
Answer D: Samuel may enforce the contract if he proves the oral agreement by a preponderance of the evidence.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC §2-201, a contract for the sale of goods for $500 or more is not enforceable unless there is a sufficient signed writing or an applicable exception. The tablets are goods, the total price is $1,400, and no writing, payment, acceptance, special manufacture, or admission appears.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because agreement alone does not satisfy the UCC goods Statute of Frauds. C: C is wrong because goods contracts of $500 or more are a separate SOF category. D: D is wrong because proof of the oral agreement does not substitute for the required writing or exception.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(1)
