Question: A pedestrian obtained a $300,000 judgment against two negligent delivery drivers for one indivisible leg injury. Driver A paid the entire $300,000 judgment. The pedestrian then tried to collect another $120,000 from Driver B, arguing that Driver B was also negligent and had not personally paid anything. The jurisdiction permits joint and several liability and contribution among joint tortfeasors. May the pedestrian collect additional damages from Driver B for the same negligence injury?
Answer A: Yes, because each negligent defendant is separately liable for the full amount of the judgment
Answer B: Yes, but only up to Driver B's percentage of fault
Answer C: No, because the pedestrian has already received full satisfaction of the indivisible injury judgment
Answer D: No, because Driver A's payment retroactively proves Driver B was not a tortfeasor
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The pedestrian cannot collect more than the judgment for the same indivisible injury. Driver A may seek contribution from Driver B, but the plaintiff cannot obtain a second recovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Joint and several liability allows one full satisfaction, not multiple full or partial satisfactions beyond the judgment. Once the judgment is paid, plaintiff recovery is complete. B: Driver B's share is a contribution issue between defendants, not an additional plaintiff recovery after full satisfaction. D: Driver B may still be a tortfeasor. The bar comes from full satisfaction, not from erasing Driver B's negligence.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff may obtain only one full satisfaction for an indivisible injury; after full payment, further recovery from another tortfeasor is barred, though contribution may remain available among defendants.
