MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Freedom of religion
Question: Two factions of a church dispute ownership of a building. The deed and corporate charter are ambiguous. One faction asks the state court to decide which group is "the true continuation of the faith" based on competing interpretations of scripture and church discipline. The other faction asks the court to avoid theology and apply only neutral property documents if possible. What limitation applies to the court?
Answer A: The court may decide which faction has the better theology because property ownership depends on religious truth
Answer B: The court may use neutral principles of property law if it can avoid deciding doctrine or ecclesiastical authority
Answer C: The court must dismiss every church property dispute without looking at any document
Answer D: The court may decide doctrine if both factions ask for judicial help
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The First Amendment permits neutral civil rules but bars courts from resolving theological or church-governance questions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Civil courts may not resolve ecclesiastical questions or decide religious truth. C: Neutral-principles analysis can sometimes resolve property disputes without theological judgment. D: Party consent does not authorize civil courts to decide ecclesiastical truth.
lb_rule_reference: Civil courts may apply neutral principles to church property disputes, but they may not resolve questions of doctrine, polity, or ecclesiastical authority.
