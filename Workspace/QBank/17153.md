Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: After indictment, police place an informant in a holding cell with Omar and instruct the informant not to ask questions. The informant remains silent. Omar spontaneously says, "I should never have robbed that store." The prosecution offers the statement. What is Omar's best Sixth Amendment objection?
Answer A: The objection likely fails because the informant did not deliberately elicit the statement
Answer B: The statement is automatically suppressed because a government agent heard it
Answer C: The statement is suppressed because Omar was not given counsel before speaking spontaneously
Answer D: The statement is admissible only if Omar signed a written waiver first
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Sixth Amendment bars government agents from deliberately eliciting statements after attachment. A volunteered statement to a silent listener is different.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Agent presence alone is not enough. Deliberate elicitation is the key. C: The government did not question or deliberately elicit; Omar volunteered the statement. D: A waiver is not required for a volunteered statement that was not deliberately elicited.
lb_rule_reference: Massiah is not violated merely because a government agent hears a charged defendant's volunteered statement after attachment; deliberate elicitation is required.
