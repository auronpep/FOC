Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: In a federal medical-malpractice trial, Elizabeth claims that a surgery by Dr. Luke caused her severe shoulder pain. Dr. Luke offers evidence that two days after the surgery, Elizabeth fell down stairs and landed on the same shoulder. Elizabeth objects that the fall happened after the surgery and may not have caused the pain. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence because later events are never relevant to what caused an earlier injury.
Answer B: Admit the evidence because the later fall tends to make an alternative or contributing cause of the shoulder pain more probable.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence unless Dr. Luke proves the fall was the sole cause of Elizabeth's pain.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if Elizabeth admits the fall hurt her shoulder.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A later fall affecting the same body part can make an alternative or contributing cause more probable. It need not be proved to be the sole cause to be relevant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because later events can bear on causation of continuing symptoms. C demands conclusive proof rather than relevance. C: A is wrong because later events can bear on causation of continuing symptoms. C demands conclusive proof rather than relevance. D is wrong because the evidence may be relevant without Elizabeth's admission. D: D is wrong because the evidence may be relevant without Elizabeth's admission.

lb_student_diagnostic: Tests alternative-cause relevance and rejects sole-cause inflation.
