Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Defense of property
Question: Garrett owns a small electronics store. He observes a shoplifter, Mia, conceal a smartphone worth $800 in her bag and walk toward the exit. Garrett is a large man and could physically restrain Mia without risk to himself. Instead, he pulls out a firearm and shoots Mia in the leg to stop her from leaving with the phone. Mia survives. Garrett claims he was protecting his property. Is Garrett's use of his firearm justified as defense of property?
Answer A: Yes, because the stolen property had significant monetary value
Answer B: Yes, because the shoplifter was in the act of committing a crime
Answer C: No, because deadly force may never be used to protect property alone, regardless of its value
Answer D: No, because Garrett should have called police instead of acting himself
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The common law categorically prohibits deadly force to protect property. A person may use reasonable, proportionate, non-deadly force to prevent theft or trespass — e.g., physically restraining a shoplifter, blocking exit, or using non-harmful means. But the moment force rises to deadly force (a firearm, regardless of where it is aimed), the justification requires a threat to persons, not just property. Mia posed no threat to Garrett or others — she was attempting to leave with merchandise. Garrett had non-deadly options available. The shooting was unjustified, and Garrett is liable for the resulting injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The value of the property is irrelevant to the deadly-force analysis. No amount of property value justifies deadly force to protect property alone. The rule is categorical: property, no matter how valuable, cannot justify lethal or grievous force. B: The fact that Mia was committing a crime does not authorize Garrett to use deadly force. Defense of property allows only non-deadly force proportionate to the threat to property. A person's commission of a non-violent theft does not create the threat to persons that would justify deadly force. D: The reason Garrett's conduct is unlawful is not the failure to involve police — citizens may use non-deadly force to protect property and may detain shoplifters under shopkeeper's privilege. The issue is the use of deadly force. Calling police is prudent but its absence is not the legal basis for liability.
lb_rule_reference: A person may use reasonable non-deadly force to protect property from theft or trespass, but may never use deadly force to protect property alone; deadly force requires an independent threat to persons
