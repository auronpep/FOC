Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Trial judge’s discretion to control order and presentation of evidence
Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Paul calls six customers who each give the same testimony that a ladder collapsed in the same way. Paul seeks to call eight more customers to repeat the same point. The judge limits Paul to one additional customer witness, finding the testimony cumulative and time-consuming. Paul objects that he has an absolute right to call every witness. How should an appellate court view the ruling?
Answer A: As error, because a party has an absolute right to present every nonprivileged witness.
Answer B: As a permissible exercise of reasonable control over cumulative proof, so long as Paul has a fair chance to present his point.
Answer C: As error unless the opposing party agrees to the limitation.
Answer D: As proper only if all additional witnesses are legally incompetent.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 611(a) gives the court reasonable control over the mode and order of presenting evidence to avoid wasting time and needless cumulative proof. The court may limit repetitive witnesses while allowing a fair opportunity to present the evidence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the right to present evidence is not unlimited. C: C is wrong because the judge does not need party consent to control cumulative proof. D: D is wrong because limiting cumulative testimony does not require witness incompetence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / reasonable control over presentation
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests discretion-versus-absolute-right errors in trial-control rulings.
