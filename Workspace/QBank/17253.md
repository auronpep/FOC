Question: A riding stable owned a horse that had twice kicked stable hands who approached from behind. The stable manager warned employees but did not warn a customer who was allowed into the grooming area. The horse kicked the customer in the leg. Is the stable strictly liable?
Answer A: No, because horses are domestic animals and are never subject to strict liability
Answer B: No, because the stable warned its own employees about the horse
Answer C: Yes, because the stable knew the horse had a dangerous kicking propensity and the injury resulted from that risk
Answer D: Yes, but only because the customer paid for riding services
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The known propensity was kicking when approached, and that is exactly what injured the customer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Domestic-animal owners can face strict liability when they know of the animal's dangerous propensity. B: The warning to employees does not erase known-propensity liability to the injured customer. D: The payment relationship is not the source of strict liability; the known dangerous propensity is.
lb_rule_reference: The owner of a domestic animal is strictly liable for harm caused by a dangerous propensity if the owner knew or had reason to know of that propensity.
