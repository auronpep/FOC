Question: A funeral home negligently misplaced a family's decedent and delivered the wrong body for a private viewing. The decedent's daughter suffered severe emotional distress but was not personally endangered and did not witness a physical injury occur. The jurisdiction recognizes mishandling of a corpse as a special NIED category. What is the daughter's strongest argument for recovery?
Answer A: She must lose because she was not in a zone of physical danger
Answer B: She must lose because she did not see a close relative get injured
Answer C: She may recover under the recognized special category for mishandling a corpse
Answer D: She may recover because every breach of contract with a funeral home creates tort damages
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The stated jurisdiction treats negligent corpse mishandling as a recognized basis for emotional-distress recovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The jurisdiction recognizes a separate mishandled-corpse category. B: Mishandling remains a distinct special category from bystander recovery. D: The better argument is the special NIED category, not a universal rule for all funeral contracts.
lb_rule_reference: Some jurisdictions allow emotional-distress recovery for mishandling a corpse without requiring ordinary zone-of-danger or bystander elements.
