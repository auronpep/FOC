Outline: Receipt of stolen property
Question: Marta did not create a forged payroll check, but she knows her cousin forged the employer's signature. Marta presents the check to a check-cashing store and says it is genuine, hoping to split the proceeds. What offense best fits Marta's conduct?
Answer A: No forgery-related offense, because Marta did not make the false check
Answer B: Uttering, because Marta knowingly offered a forged check as genuine with intent to defraud
Answer C: No offense, because the store can inspect the check before paying
Answer D: Embezzlement, because Marta hoped to split the proceeds
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Marta knew the check was forged and presented it as valid to obtain money. That is uttering.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Passing a forged instrument as genuine can be uttering even if another person made it. C: Uttering does not require successful deception or payment. Offering the forged instrument with intent to defraud is enough. D: She was not entrusted with property and did not convert it after lawful possession. The conduct is passing a forged instrument.
lb_rule_reference: Uttering occurs when a person offers a forged instrument as genuine with knowledge of its falsity and intent to defraud.
