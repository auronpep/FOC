Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Cara and Eli agree to rob a cash courier at night. Cara serves as lookout while Eli confronts the courier. During the robbery, Eli brandishes a gun and strikes the courier when the courier resists. Cara did not know Eli brought a gun. The jurisdiction follows Pinkerton, and Cara is charged with assault with a deadly weapon. Is Cara likely liable under Pinkerton?
Answer A: No, because Cara did not personally carry or use the gun
Answer B: Yes, because force with a weapon during an agreed nighttime robbery can be foreseeable and in furtherance of the plan
Answer C: No, because Pinkerton applies only to fraud conspiracies
Answer D: Yes, and Cara is liable for every crime Eli ever commits after the agreement
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Violence used to complete or escape a planned robbery is a foreseeable consequence of that conspiracy. The assault advanced the robbery when the courier resisted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Pinkerton can impose liability without personal participation if the crime was in furtherance of the conspiracy and reasonably foreseeable. C: The doctrine is not limited to fraud. The elements are furtherance, foreseeability, and timing. D: Liability is limited to crimes in furtherance of the conspiracy, foreseeable, and committed during it.
lb_rule_reference: A conspirator may be liable for a co-conspirator's substantive crime when it is committed in furtherance of the conspiracy and is a reasonably foreseeable consequence of the agreed plan.
