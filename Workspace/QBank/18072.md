Question: A store hired Lydia to install refrigeration units, with payment due when the units were “satisfactory to the store.” Lydia installed units that met the specifications, passed inspection, and kept food at the required temperature. The store refused to approve them because its manager disliked the brand name, though the brand was not specified in the contract. Which result is most likely?
Answer A: The store may refuse payment for any reason because satisfaction language gives absolute discretion.
Answer B: The store may refuse payment unless Lydia proves the manager subjectively liked the units.
Answer C: Lydia may recover only in restitution because satisfaction clauses are unenforceable.
Answer D: Lydia's performance likely satisfies the commercial satisfaction condition because the units objectively met the contract's functional purpose.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Where satisfaction concerns commercial utility or operative fitness rather than personal taste, the condition is commonly measured by objective reasonableness. The refrigeration units met specifications and passed inspection. The manager's brand preference does not defeat the condition.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because satisfaction language does not always create unlimited discretion. B: B is wrong because subjective taste is not the standard for commercial utility. C: C is wrong because satisfaction clauses can be enforceable when routed to the correct standard.
lb_rule_reference: Satisfaction condition / commercial utility
