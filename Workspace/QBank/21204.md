Question: Leah signed and recorded a deed granting “to Jacob personally, for Jacob's lifetime and not to Jacob's assigns, the right to park one wagon in the marked space on Leah's lot during market days.” Jacob owns no parcel that benefits from the parking space. How should Jacob's right be classified?
Answer A: An easement appurtenant because parking near the market benefits Jacob economically.
Answer B: A profit because Jacob may use the parking space while selling goods.
Answer C: A revocable license because Jacob owns no dominant parcel.
Answer D: An easement in gross because the deed gives Jacob a nonpossessory right that benefits him personally rather than a dominant parcel.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement in gross benefits a person rather than a parcel. The deed grants a nonpossessory parking right to Jacob personally, for his lifetime, and identifies no dominant estate. That makes it in gross rather than appurtenant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because economic benefit to a person is not the same as benefit to a dominant parcel. B: B is wrong because Jacob is not removing resources from Leah's land. C: C is wrong because the absence of a dominant parcel does not automatically make a deeded easement a revocable license.
lb_rule_reference: Personal easement in gross
