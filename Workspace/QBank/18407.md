Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police knock at an apartment shared by two residents. Both are standing at the door. One resident says, "You can search the apartment." The other immediately says, "No, I do not consent." Police enter anyway and find evidence in the living room. Does the consenting resident's permission validate the warrantless entry?
Answer A: Yes, because consent from any occupant with common authority is always enough
Answer B: No, because a present co-occupant with common authority expressly objected
Answer C: Yes, because the objecting resident did not own the evidence
Answer D: No, but only because police failed to read Miranda warnings before asking
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The objecting resident was physically present and refused consent, so the other occupant's consent did not authorize the entry.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A physically present co-occupant's express refusal can defeat consent by another occupant. C: Consent authority turns on occupancy and privacy, not evidence ownership. D: Miranda warnings are not required to request consent to search.
lb_rule_reference: Consent from one occupant generally cannot override the express refusal of a physically present co-occupant with common authority.
