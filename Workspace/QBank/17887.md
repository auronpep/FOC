MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: A store security guard, acting under store policy and without police direction, opens a shopper's backpack in a private office and finds stolen watches. The guard then calls police. When officers arrive, the guard hands them the watches and describes the search. The officers do not repeat the backpack search. If the shopper moves to suppress the watches under the Fourth Amendment, what is the strongest response?
Answer A: Suppress the watches because any intrusive search of personal property violates the Fourth Amendment
Answer B: Admit the watches because the guard acted privately and not as an instrument or agent of police
Answer C: Suppress the watches because police later received the evidence from the guard
Answer D: Admit the watches only if police had probable cause before the guard opened the backpack
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The guard searched on private initiative before contacting police. The officers did not direct, encourage, or repeat the search, so the Fourth Amendment is not triggered by the private search.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Fourth Amendment applies to government action, not purely private searches. C: Later receipt of evidence does not convert the earlier private search into state action. D: Probable cause is not required for a private guard's independent search.
lb_rule_reference: The Fourth Amendment limits government action; a private search becomes governmental only when the private actor acts as an instrument or agent of police.
