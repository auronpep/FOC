Outline: Expectation damages
Question: A manufacturer contracted to sell 2,000 custom-labeled bottles to Aaron for $30,000. The bottles were identified to the contract and printed with Aaron's trade name. Aaron wrongfully repudiated after production was complete. The manufacturer made reasonable efforts to resell the bottles but could not find any buyer at a reasonable price because of the labels. Which remedy is most appropriate?
Answer A: An action for the price, because the identified goods could not be resold at a reasonable price after reasonable effort.
Answer B: No recovery, because the manufacturer still possesses the bottles.
Answer C: Only nominal damages, because Aaron never accepted the bottles.
Answer D: Only the resale-damages measure, even though no reasonable resale is possible.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A seller may recover the price of goods identified to the contract if the seller is unable after reasonable effort to resell them at a reasonable price or the circumstances show that resale efforts would be unavailing. The custom-labeled bottles meet that price-action route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because possession by the seller does not bar an action for price when the statutory conditions are satisfied. C: C is wrong because buyer acceptance is not the only route to price recovery. D: D is wrong because a resale measure assumes a resale route; here the goods are not reasonably resellable.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-709(1)(b)
