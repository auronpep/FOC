Question: Rachel contracted with Samuel to sell Samuel a field. Samuel promised Rachel that he would pay $8,000 to Rachel's brother Benjamin. Rachel told Samuel, “I will tell Benjamin about the payment after closing.” Before Rachel told Benjamin, Rachel and Samuel deleted the payment-to-Benjamin clause. Benjamin later learned of the original clause and sued Samuel. What result?
Answer A: Benjamin may recover because Rachel intended to tell him.
Answer B: Benjamin may recover because Rachel and Samuel originally intended to benefit him.
Answer C: Benjamin may not recover because the clause was deleted before he assented, relied, sued, or otherwise vested his rights.
Answer D: Benjamin may not recover because family members cannot be third-party beneficiaries.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Benjamin may have been an intended beneficiary of the original promise, but his rights had not vested before the deletion. Rachel's plan to tell him later was not assent, reliance, suit, or acceptance of performance by Benjamin.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the promisee's future plan to notify the beneficiary does not vest beneficiary rights. B: B is wrong because intended status alone does not prevent pre-vesting modification. D: D is wrong because family members can be intended beneficiaries when the contract shows direct intent.
