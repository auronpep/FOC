Outline: Offers of proof
Question: Titus sues Aquila on a written guaranty. Titus offers a photocopy of a letter in which Aquila allegedly admitted the guaranty. The court excludes the photocopy on authentication grounds. Outside the jury's hearing, Titus's lawyer states on the record the date of the letter, the author, the addressee, and the exact admission the letter contains. Aquila argues that Titus failed to preserve error because the original letter was not produced during the offer of proof. Is Aquila correct?
Answer A: Yes, because an offer of proof involving a writing always requires the original writing.
Answer B: Yes, because counsel may never summarize an excluded document for the record.
Answer C: No, because Titus made the substance of the excluded evidence known to the court; the offer of proof itself did not have to satisfy the original-writing rule.
Answer D: No, but only because photocopies are always admissible as duplicates.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An offer of proof preserves what the excluded evidence would have shown. Titus's lawyer placed the substance of the excluded letter on the record. The offer of proof is not itself admission of the document, and it need not independently satisfy the original-writing rule to preserve the claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the offer of proof is a record-preservation device, not proof admitted to the jury. B: B is wrong because counsel may state the substance of excluded evidence unless the court directs a different form. D: D is wrong because the question is preservation, not whether the photocopy was ultimately admissible.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(2) / writing excluded / substance of document proffered
