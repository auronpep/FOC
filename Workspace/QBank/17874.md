Question: A buyer ordered 500 blue lamps from a seller. The seller tendered 500 red lamps on the delivery date and did not offer to cure. The buyer rejected the shipment and refused to pay. The seller sued, arguing that the buyer must pay because 500 lamps were delivered and the color difference was minor. Which result is most likely?
Answer A: The seller wins because substantial performance applies to all sales of goods.
Answer B: The seller wins because the buyer must accept commercial goods and sue only for damages.
Answer C: The buyer loses unless the red lamps are worthless.
Answer D: The buyer may reject the nonconforming tender and need not pay for goods it properly rejected.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC Article 2, a buyer generally may reject goods if the tender fails in any respect to conform, subject to cure and other limits. The seller tendered red lamps instead of the ordered blue lamps and did not offer to cure. The buyer may reject and need not pay for rejected goods.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it imports common-law substantial performance into a UCC perfect-tender setting. B: B is wrong because the buyer need not accept a nonconforming tender in the first instance. C: C is wrong because perfect tender does not require the goods to be worthless before rejection is allowed.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-601 / perfect tender as goods condition
