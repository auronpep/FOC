Outline: Nuisance
Question: A feedlot operated on rural land for years. A developer later built homes next door. After the area became mostly residential, the feedlot expanded its operation and odors became intense enough to keep residents indoors. The feedlot argues that the residents cannot sue because they moved next to an existing agricultural use. How should the court treat that argument?
Answer A: It is a complete defense because the residents knowingly came to the nuisance
Answer B: It is relevant to reasonableness and remedy but does not itself defeat the claim
Answer C: It matters only if the residents bought their homes before the feedlot existed
Answer D: It converts the claim into trespass because odors are intangible
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Coming to the nuisance may affect balancing, but substantial unreasonable interference can still be actionable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Moving near an existing use does not automatically bar a claim. C: Timing matters in both directions, but it is not the sole issue. D: Odor is commonly analyzed as nuisance, not trespass.
lb_rule_reference: Coming to the nuisance is relevant to the reasonableness and remedy analysis, but it is not a complete defense to a private nuisance claim.
