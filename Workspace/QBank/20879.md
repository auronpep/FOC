Outline: Scope
Question: Joshua held an easement to use a gravel road across Deborah's land to reach Joshua's parcel. The deed creating the easement did not require Deborah to maintain the road. After heavy rain washed gravel from the road, Joshua demanded that Deborah pay to restore it, even though Joshua was the only person who used the road. Who generally bears the maintenance burden absent contrary agreement?
Answer A: Deborah, because the servient owner always must maintain improvements used by an easement holder.
Answer B: Deborah, because allowing an easement automatically creates an affirmative covenant to repair.
Answer C: Joshua and Deborah equally, regardless of use or agreement.
Answer D: Joshua, because the easement holder generally must bear reasonable maintenance costs necessary for the easement's use absent contrary agreement.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Absent an agreement assigning maintenance duties, the easement holder generally bears the cost of ordinary maintenance reasonably necessary to enjoy the easement, particularly where the holder is the only user. The servient owner's land is burdened by the use right but is not automatically burdened by an affirmative repair duty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because servient ownership does not automatically create a duty to maintain the easement for the holder. B: B is wrong because an easement is not automatically an affirmative covenant to repair. C: C is wrong because equal sharing is not automatic regardless of use or agreement. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Easement maintenance burden
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is converting a right to use another's land into a duty by that owner to pay for the easement holder's use.
