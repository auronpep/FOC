Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A paint remover emitted irritating fumes if used in a closed room. The label warned in small print to open windows. A homeowner bought the product, immediately poured it into an unlabeled spray bottle, threw away the original container without reading it, and later used the remover in a closed closet. The homeowner testified that she never reads product labels and would not have read a larger warning on the container before discarding it. The homeowner sues the manufacturer under strict products liability for failure to warn, arguing that the original warning was too small. Is the homeowner likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim for failure to warn?
Answer A: Yes, because the original warning was small and therefore inadequate
Answer B: No, because the plaintiff cannot show that a larger adequate warning would have been read or followed
Answer C: Yes, because manufacturers are liable whenever a product has a hidden risk
Answer D: No, because a label warning is never required for chemical products sold to adults
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Failure-to-warn liability requires causation. The homeowner discarded the container without reading it and testified she would not have read a larger warning, so the alleged warning defect did not cause the injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Even if the warning format was inadequate, the plaintiff must show warning causation. Her testimony that she would not have read any container warning before discarding it breaks that causal link. C: A hidden risk may create a duty to warn, but the plaintiff still must prove that the warning failure caused the harm. D: Chemical products can require warnings for nonobvious risks. This claim fails because warning causation is missing, not because adult buyers are categorically outside warning protection.
lb_rule_reference: A warning defect must cause the injury; if an adequate warning would not have been read or followed, failure to warn may not be a legal cause.
