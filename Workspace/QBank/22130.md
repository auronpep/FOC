


BARMATRIX Q#: 22130
Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: A landlord leased a warehouse to a salesman for a term of six years. Under the lease agreement, the salesman covenanted to pay $1,000 per month in rent. Two years later, the salesman assigned the balance of the lease period to a brewer. The brewer took immediate possession. Two years after that, the brewer assigned his interest to a contractor. The contractor took possession, but didn't pay any rent for three months. The landlord sued the salesman for the unpaid rent because he knew the contractor was bankrupt. Should the court rule in the landlord's favor?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the salesman still had a contractual obligation to pay rent.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the landlord did not object to the salesman's assignment.
Answer C: C: No, because the salesman is no longer in possession of the property.
Answer D: D: No, because the landlord knew the contractor was bankrupt.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Even if a tenant assigns his or her leasehold interest to another party, the tenant is not released from his or her original contractual obligations under the lease. Consequently, the salesman is still liable for rent under the lease terms. B is incorrect because the landlord's lack of objection would not change the analysis. C and D are incorrect because the salesman is still liable under the terms of the lease.
