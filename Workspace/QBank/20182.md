Outline: Reliance damages
Question: David promised Ruth that he would donate $100,000 to help Ruth open a school. David reasonably expected Ruth to begin planning. Ruth signed no contract with David, but she reasonably spent $12,000 on architectural plans before David withdrew the promise. The court finds the promise enforceable only by promissory estoppel. Which remedy is most consistent with the reliance basis of enforcement?
Answer A: A remedy limited to the $12,000 Ruth reasonably spent, if that is what justice requires.
Answer B: Automatic recovery of the full $100,000 promised amount in every promissory-estoppel case.
Answer C: No recovery, because charitable or gift-like promises can never be enforced after reliance.
Answer D: Restitution measured by David's benefit from Ruth's architectural plans.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When enforcement rests on reliance, the remedy may be limited as justice requires. The $12,000 Ruth reasonably spent in reliance is the core reliance measure. Full expectation may be inappropriate when the enforcement theory is reliance rather than bargain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because promissory-estoppel remedies may be limited. C: C is wrong because reliance can support enforcement in appropriate cases. D: D is wrong because David received no benefit; the claim is reliance, not restitution.
lb_rule_reference: Promissory estoppel / reliance remedy limited as justice requires
