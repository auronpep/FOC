MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Without reasonable suspicion, officers stop Carla on a sidewalk and tell her she cannot leave. Two minutes later, while still detaining her, they ask to search her backpack. Carla says yes. No intervening event occurs, and officers find stolen credit cards. Is the backpack search valid based on consent?
Answer A: No, because the consent was obtained during an illegal detention and was not attenuated from the seizure
Answer B: Yes, because saying "yes" always cures any prior Fourth Amendment violation
Answer C: Yes, because backpacks receive no Fourth Amendment protection in public
Answer D: No, because consent searches always require written consent
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Consent must be voluntary and untainted. The illegal stop was ongoing, close in time, and no intervening event broke the causal chain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Consent during an illegal detention can be tainted unless sufficiently attenuated. C: A person can have a privacy interest in a backpack even in public. D: Written consent is not constitutionally required. The problem is taint from the illegal seizure.
lb_rule_reference: Consent obtained during an illegal seizure is invalid unless the government shows the consent was sufficiently voluntary and attenuated from the unlawful detention.
