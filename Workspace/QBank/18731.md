Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Caleb testifies that he never saw sparks coming from the saw before it stopped. At his deposition in the same case, while under oath, Caleb testified that sparks came from the saw twice before it stopped. Caleb is subject to cross-examination at trial. The defendant offers the deposition excerpt. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the excerpt only to impeach Caleb, because prior inconsistent statements are never substantive evidence.
Answer B: Admit the excerpt to impeach Caleb and as substantive proof that sparks came from the saw.
Answer C: Exclude the excerpt because Caleb is available and has testified at trial.
Answer D: Exclude the excerpt unless Caleb first denies making the deposition statement.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A declarant-witness's prior inconsistent statement is not hearsay if the declarant testifies and is subject to cross-examination about the statement, and the prior statement was given under penalty of perjury at a trial, hearing, other proceeding, or deposition. Caleb's deposition testimony satisfies that route, so it is admissible both to impeach him and for the truth.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because sworn deposition inconsistency can be substantive under the federal rule. C: C is wrong because Caleb's trial testimony and availability for cross-examination are requirements, not bars. D: D is wrong because the statement's substantive route does not require a denial; the inconsistency and the rule conditions control.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 801(d)(1)(A) / sworn deposition prior inconsistent statement
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the federal substantive-use exception for sworn prior inconsistent statements.
