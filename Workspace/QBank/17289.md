Question: A home seller knew an engineer had found serious foundation cracks. Before showing the home, the seller covered the cracks with temporary paneling and told the buyer the foundation had "no known structural problems." The buyer relied on the statement and paid full price. Is the buyer likely to establish fraud?
Answer A: Yes, because the seller concealed and misstated a known material fact to induce reliance
Answer B: Yes, because every defect in sold property is fraud
Answer C: No, because fraud can never be based on concealment
Answer D: No, because the buyer's damages are economic
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts support falsity, scienter, reliance, causation, and damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Fraud requires the specific mental-state and reliance elements. C: Active concealment of a known material defect can be actionable. D: Fraud is a recognized business tort and is not barred merely because damages are economic.
lb_rule_reference: Concealing a known material defect to induce a transaction can satisfy fraud when the buyer justifiably relies and suffers damages.
