Outline: Strict scrutiny (suspect) classifications
Question: A city requires all restaurants to close outdoor patios at midnight to reduce neighborhood noise. A restaurant owned by a religious community uses late-night outdoor meals as part of a weekly observance and seeks an exemption. The ordinance applies to every restaurant, secular and religious, and contains no discretionary exemptions. What standard most likely applies to the Free Exercise claim?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny, because the restaurant has a sincere religious reason for violating the closing rule
Answer B: Rational basis, because the rule is neutral, generally applicable, and lacks individualized exemptions
Answer C: The rule is invalid per se because religious conduct can never be regulated
Answer D: The rule is valid only if the city proves the restaurant would cause actual noise every week
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The closing rule applies across the board to all restaurants. Its incidental burden on religious practice ordinarily does not require a religious exemption.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A sincere religious motive does not alone trigger strict scrutiny when the rule is neutral and generally applicable. C: The Constitution protects belief absolutely, but religiously motivated conduct can be regulated by neutral generally applicable laws. D: The city does not bear a strict-scrutiny evidentiary burden for a neutral generally applicable ordinance.
lb_rule_reference: A neutral and generally applicable law usually does not trigger strict scrutiny merely because a person violates it for religious reasons.
