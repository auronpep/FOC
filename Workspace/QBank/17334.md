Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: In a federal civil trial, David claims that a delivery truck owned by Saul blocked his shop entrance. Five neighbors have already testified that they saw Saul's truck parked there for two hours. David seeks to call eight more neighbors who would give the same testimony from the same vantage point. Saul objects that the additional testimony is cumulative. Which ruling is proper?
Answer A: The court must admit all eight witnesses because David has the burden of persuasion.
Answer B: The court must exclude all eight witnesses because a fact proved by five witnesses is no longer material.
Answer C: The court may limit the additional witnesses if their marginal probative value is substantially outweighed by cumulative presentation and waste of time.
Answer D: The court may exclude the testimony only if Saul concedes that the truck blocked the entrance.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may control cumulative proof under Rule 403. The fact remains material, but repeated testimony from the same vantage point may add little while wasting time.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a party's burden does not create a right to unlimited cumulative proof. B: B is wrong because the fact remains material; the problem is cumulative presentation. D: D is wrong because exclusion for cumulative proof does not require a formal concession.
