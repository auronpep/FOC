Question: Abel granted Eve a recorded easement over Abel's Lot 2. One year later, Abel mortgaged Lot 2 to a lender. The lender had notice of the recorded easement. The lender later foreclosed the mortgage and sold Lot 2 to Peter, who refused to allow Eve to use the easement. What is the best result?
Answer A: Eve's easement generally survives the foreclosure because it was created and recorded before the mortgage.
Answer B: Eve's easement is extinguished because any mortgage foreclosure destroys all easements on the land.
Answer C: Eve's easement is extinguished because Peter personally did not sign the easement grant.
Answer D: Eve now owns Lot 2 because a senior easement becomes fee title after foreclosure.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Foreclosure of a later mortgage generally does not extinguish interests that are senior to the mortgage. Eve's easement was created and recorded before the mortgage, so the foreclosure buyer generally takes Lot 2 subject to that senior easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because foreclosure generally cuts off junior interests, not senior interests. C: C is wrong because a servient-land successor can take subject to a prior recorded easement without personally signing the grant. D: D is wrong because easement priority does not become fee ownership.
lb_rule_reference: Mortgage priority / senior easement survival
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is using “foreclosure” as a shortcut without asking whether the easement is senior or junior to the mortgage.
