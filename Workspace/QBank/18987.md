Outline: Use of impeachment material
Question: In a federal premises case, John testifies that the hallway light was on. After John is given an opportunity to explain, the defendant offers a letter John wrote the next day stating, “The hallway light was out.” The same letter also says, “The plaintiff has stolen from three neighbors,” a subject unrelated to the case. The defendant offers the letter to impeach John. The plaintiff objects to the unrelated theft accusations. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the entire letter because any document containing impeachment material is admissible as a whole.
Answer B: Exclude the entire letter because part of it is unfairly prejudicial.
Answer C: Admit the inconsistent portion for impeachment, but exclude or redact the unrelated theft accusations.
Answer D: Admit the theft accusations as substantive proof that the plaintiff is dishonest.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The inconsistent statement about the hallway light may be used for impeachment if the Rule 613 procedure is satisfied. But unrelated accusations that add unfair prejudice and do not serve the impeachment purpose may be excluded or redacted. The court should control the admissible portion of the impeachment material.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because admissible impeachment material does not make an entire document admissible. B: B is wrong because the proper response is targeted exclusion or redaction, not necessarily exclusion of the useful impeachment portion. D: D is wrong because the unrelated theft accusations are not admissible as substantive character proof.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403, 105, and 613 / redaction of impeachment material
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests admissible-portion control when impeachment material contains unrelated prejudicial matter.
