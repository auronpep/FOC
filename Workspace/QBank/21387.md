Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: Ortega sued a contractor for defective patio work. The parties filed a stipulated dismissal with prejudice, and the court entered it. Six months later, Ortega sues the same contractor again for the same patio defects, this time labeling the claim as negligent repair rather than breach of contract. What is the best preclusion argument for the contractor?
Answer A: Claim preclusion, because the dismissal with prejudice is treated as a final merits judgment on claims from the same transaction
Answer B: Issue preclusion, because every issue in the patio dispute was actually litigated
Answer C: No preclusion, because no witness testified
Answer D: No preclusion, because negligent repair is a different label
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The same parties and same patio transaction are involved, and the dismissal with prejudice generally bars relitigation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A stipulated dismissal usually does not mean specific issues were actually litigated. C: Claim preclusion can arise from a merits judgment without testimony. D: Claim preclusion looks to the transaction, not just the legal label.
lb_rule_reference: A dismissal with prejudice is generally treated as a final merits judgment for claim-preclusion purposes.
