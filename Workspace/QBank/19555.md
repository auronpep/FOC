Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Sarah and Isaac orally agree that Isaac will buy 200 wool cloaks from Sarah. They agree that the price will be the market price on the delivery date, and both know that the market price is expected to be about $20 per cloak. No writing is signed. Isaac later refuses to buy the cloaks, and Sarah sues. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The oral contract is enforceable because the UCC can supply an open price term.
Answer B: The oral contract is unenforceable only because the quantity was not stated.
Answer C: The UCC may supply an open price term if a contract is otherwise enforceable, but the goods Statute of Frauds still requires a sufficient writing or exception for this contract.
Answer D: The Statute of Frauds is irrelevant because price terms are never material under the UCC.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An open price term does not itself defeat a goods contract if the parties intended to contract, but the Statute of Frauds remains a separate enforceability gate. With 200 cloaks at an expected price far above $500 and no writing or exception, the open-price rule does not avoid §2-201.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it collapses the UCC gap-filler rule into the SOF writing requirement. B: B is wrong because the quantity was stated; the defect is the lack of a sufficient writing. D: D is wrong because price may be supplied in some cases, but the SOF threshold and writing requirement still matter.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §§2-201(1), 2-305
