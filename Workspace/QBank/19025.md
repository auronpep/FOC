Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Freedom of the press
Question: A state agency posts a press release falsely stating that a contractor is "dishonest and unreliable." The agency does not cancel any license, remove the contractor from an approved vendor list, or change any legal right. The contractor sues for procedural due process, claiming reputation harm alone required a hearing. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The contractor has a due process liberty claim whenever government speech damages reputation
Answer B: The contractor likely lacks a procedural due process liberty claim absent stigma plus alteration of legal status or right
Answer C: The contractor wins because due process applies to all false statements by private and public actors
Answer D: The contractor loses only if the agency proves the statement was true beyond a reasonable doubt
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts give stigma, but not the additional government action that changes legal status or entitlement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Reputation harm alone generally is not enough for procedural due process. C: Due process is not a general defamation code, and private false statements are not state action. D: The procedural due process threshold is not a criminal burden of proof.
lb_rule_reference: Government defamation alone generally does not deprive liberty for procedural due process; a liberty interest may arise when stigma is coupled with a tangible alteration of legal status or right.
