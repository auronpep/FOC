Question: Joseph agreed to build one stone wall around Benjamin's garden for a lump sum of $18,000, payable “when the wall is finished.” Joseph built half the wall, then quit to take a more profitable job. Benjamin hired someone else to finish it. Joseph sues Benjamin for the $18,000 contract price. What is the likely result?
Answer A: Joseph wins the full contract price because he completed a substantial part of the wall.
Answer B: Joseph wins half the contract price because every construction contract is divisible by percentage completed.
Answer C: Joseph wins the full contract price unless Benjamin proves bad faith.
Answer D: Joseph cannot recover the contract price because completion of the wall was a constructive condition to that payment duty.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: This is an entire contract for one wall at a lump sum payable on completion. Joseph's unjustified quitting before completion means the constructive condition to the contract-price payment did not occur. Restitution may be a separate possibility, but the contract price is not due.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a half-built wall is not substantial performance of the whole wall. B: B is wrong because the contract was not priced by separable units or progress payments. C: C is wrong because Benjamin need not prove bad faith to defeat an unmatured payment duty.
lb_rule_reference: Constructive condition of complete performance
