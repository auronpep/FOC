Question: In a federal civil trial, Andrew testifies for the defendant. The plaintiff seeks to impeach Andrew with a qualifying felony conviction entered last year. Andrew objects that his appeal of the conviction is pending. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the conviction because a conviction on appeal is not final for impeachment.
Answer B: Admit the conviction if it otherwise qualifies; the pendency of the appeal may also be shown.
Answer C: Admit the conviction only if Andrew has exhausted every appeal.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction unless the conviction involved a false statement.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 609(e), a conviction that satisfies Rule 609 is admissible even if an appeal is pending. Evidence of the pendency of the appeal is also admissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and C are wrong because a pending appeal does not bar use of an otherwise admissible conviction. D is wrong because a qualifying felony may be admitted under the felony route even if it is not a false-statement conviction. C: A and C are wrong because a pending appeal does not bar use of an otherwise admissible conviction. D is wrong because a qualifying felony may be admitted under the felony route even if it is not a false-statement conviction. D: D is wrong because a qualifying felony may be admitted under the felony route even if it is not a false-statement conviction.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(e) / pending appeal
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the pending-appeal rule and the distinction between admissibility of conviction and admissibility of appeal status.
