internal_id: TR-227
Outline_code: 61020401
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Cause in fact (actual cause)
Question: A building owner negligently failed to install a handrail on a stairway. A visitor walking down the stairs suffered a sudden fainting episode from an unrelated medical condition and fell straight backward. Video shows that a handrail would not have been within reach and would not have changed the fall. Is the missing handrail an actual cause of the visitor's injury?
Answer A: Yes, because missing handrails increase the risk of stairway falls
Answer B: Yes, because breach always satisfies actual cause
Answer C: No, because the fall would have occurred the same way even with a handrail
Answer D: No, because landowners owe no duty for stairway safety
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: But-for causation is absent. The medical fainting episode caused the fall, and the missing handrail made no difference on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The visitor must show this missing handrail caused this injury. The evidence says the fall would have happened the same way with a handrail. B: Breach and causation are separate elements. D: The likely missing element is actual cause, not duty. Landowners generally owe reasonable care for stairs.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant's breach must cause the plaintiff's damages; increasing risk alone is not enough when the harm would have occurred anyway.
