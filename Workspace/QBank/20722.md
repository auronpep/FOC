Question: Abraham owned Lot 1. Sarah owned neighboring Lot 2. Abraham signed and delivered a deed granting Sarah, as owner of Lot 2, a permanent right to use the driveway on Lot 1 to reach the public road. Sarah did not record the deed and paid Abraham no money for the grant. Abraham later blocked the driveway and argued that Sarah had no easement because the grant was unrecorded and unsupported by consideration. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Sarah has no easement because an easement is invalid between the original parties unless it is recorded.
Answer B: Sarah has no easement because consideration is required to create any interest in land.
Answer C: Sarah has only a revocable license because Abraham did not convey fee title to the driveway strip.
Answer D: Sarah has an express easement because Abraham signed and delivered a writing granting a permanent use right; recording affects notice or priority, not creation between the original parties.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An express easement is created by a sufficient written grant satisfying the statute of frauds. Recording is not required to make the easement valid between the original grantor and grantee. The deed was signed and delivered, identified the benefited land, and granted a permanent driveway use right.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because recording protects against later purchasers; it is not a creation requirement between the original parties. B: B is wrong because a deed may convey an interest in land without consideration. C: C is wrong because an easement is a use right in land; it does not require conveyance of fee title to the strip.
lb_rule_reference: Express easement / recording not required between parties
