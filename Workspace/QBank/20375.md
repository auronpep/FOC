Question: Solomon and Nathan owned adjoining rural parcels. The original deed descriptions were ambiguous, and neither owner could determine the exact boundary from the records. Solomon and Nathan agreed that an existing stone wall would be treated as the boundary, and both occupied their parcels according to that line for many years. Is the agreed boundary likely enforceable?
Answer A: Yes, because owners may settle an uncertain boundary by agreement followed by recognition of the agreed line.
Answer B: Yes, but only if Solomon proves every element of adverse possession.
Answer C: No, because boundary agreements are never enforceable without a new deed.
Answer D: No, because using a wall as a boundary is merely a license revocable at will.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The agreed boundary is likely enforceable. When an uncertain or disputed boundary exists, adjoining owners may settle the boundary by agreement, and long mutual recognition or reliance on the agreed line can make that boundary binding.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because boundary by agreement is distinct from adverse possession. C: C is wrong because equity may enforce an agreed boundary when the true line was uncertain and the parties recognized the agreed line. D: D is wrong because the agreement concerns boundary location, not a revocable permission to use land.
lb_rule_reference: Boundary by agreement / uncertainty and acceptance
