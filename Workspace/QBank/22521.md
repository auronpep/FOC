


BARMATRIX Q#: 22521
Outline_code: 55020100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Contracts
Subtopic: Formation of Contracts
Outline: Offer and acceptance
Question: A sports retailer called a baseball manufacturer and ordered 600 baseballs at a price of $1 apiece, requesting that they be delivered in 30 days. The manufacturer agreed to sell the retailer the baseballs. After the manufacturer got off the phone, he filled out a written contract with the agreed terms and signed it. He then took a picture of the contract and emailed it to the retailer. The retailer received the email and responded that he had read it. However, when the baseballs arrived 30 days later, the retailer rejected the baseballs even though they conformed to the contract. Under the circumstances, does the manufacturer have a claim against the retailer for breach of contract?
Answer A: A: No, because the email did not satisfy the Statute of Frauds.
Answer B: B: No, because the retailer never signed any agreement.
Answer C: C: Yes, because there was an enforceable contract.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the manufacturer delivered conforming goods.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC §2- 201, a contract for the sale of goods over $500 has to have a writing that meets the requirements of the Statute of Frauds. If both parties to a contract are merchants, a memorandum of the agreement sent by one party to the other meets these requirements unless the receiving party objects within 10 days. Here, the retailer received the manufacturer's memorandum and did not object within 10 days. Consequently, the parties had an enforceable contract. A is incorrect because, in many circumstances, an email will satisfy the Statute of Frauds. Additionally, the photo is not really an email in that it is a picture of a signed memorandum. B is incorrect because even though the retailer didn't sign anything, the contract is still enforceable because he did not object to the memorandum. D is incorrect because a contract's enforceability is not dependent on the delivery of the goods covered by the contract.
