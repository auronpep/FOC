Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Miriam is charged with breaking into a shed. Investigators found a muddy shoeprint near the broken door. The prosecutor offers a cast of the print and a pair of Miriam's boots. An analyst testifies that the tread pattern and size are consistent, but that thousands of boots have the same tread pattern. Miriam objects that the evidence is irrelevant unless the analyst can identify the boots as the exact source of the print. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence because a common tread pattern cannot conclusively identify the source.
Answer B: Admit the evidence because consistency between the print and Miriam's boots has some tendency to connect her to the shed; the commonness of the pattern affects weight.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence unless the analyst testifies that no other boot could have made the print.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if Miriam admits owning the boots.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The evidence has a logical tendency to connect Miriam to the scene even if it is not unique. The prosecution does not need a conclusive source identification at the relevance-foundation stage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because nonunique match evidence can still be probative. C: C is wrong because uniqueness is not required. D: D is wrong because ownership may be proved circumstantially.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / FRE 401
lb_student_diagnostic: Converting nonuniqueness into irrelevance.
