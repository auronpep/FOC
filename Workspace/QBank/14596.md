Question: The legislature of a state is debating reforms in the law governing insanity. Two reforms have been proposed. Proposal A would eliminate the insanity defense altogether. Proposal B would retain the defense but place on the defendant the burden of proving insanity by a preponderance of the evidence. Opponents of the reforms argue that the proposals would be unconstitutional under the due process clause of the United States Constitution. Which of the following proposed reforms would be unconstitutional?
Answer A: A: Both proposals.
Answer B: B: Neither proposal.
Answer C: C: Proposal A only.
Answer D: D: Proposal B only.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. Neither proposal is unconstitutional. Proposal A is constitutional because no state is constitutionally required to recognize any form of an insanity defense. Proposal B is also constitutional because, If a state does recognize an insanity defense, it is permissible to shift the burden to the defendant to prove it as an affirmative defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. As stated above, states can regulate who bears the burden to prove insanity, and even deny the use of the insanity defense altogether. As a result, neither proposal would be unconstitutional under due process. C is incorrect. Although Proposal B is, in fact, constitutional, so is Proposal A; defendants do not have a constitutional right to present an insanity defense. D is incorrect. Although Proposal A is, in fact, constitutional, so is Proposal B; if a state allows for an insanity defense, it can properly shift the burden to the defendant to prove it because it is an affirmative defense.
