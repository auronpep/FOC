Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Commerce power
Question: A state statute allows grocery stores to sell milk only if the milk was produced in the state. The state says the law protects consumers from unsafe milk, but neighboring states use the same federal inspection standards and the state could inspect out-of-state milk at the border. How should a court analyze the statute under the Dormant Commerce Clause?
Answer A: Uphold it because health and safety are legitimate state interests
Answer B: Apply Pike balancing because all commerce burdens use Pike
Answer C: Treat the law as almost per se invalid unless the state proves no reasonable nondiscriminatory alternative
Answer D: Uphold it because states may always prefer local products
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The law expressly reserves the market for in-state milk and the facts identify nondiscriminatory safety alternatives.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A safety label does not save facial discrimination when reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives exist. B: Pike applies to evenhanded incidental burdens, not facial discrimination against out-of-state goods. D: Protectionist local preference is exactly what DCC scrutiny targets when the state acts as regulator.
lb_rule_reference: A state law that facially discriminates against out-of-state commerce is almost per se invalid unless the state proves a legitimate local purpose that cannot be served by reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives.
