Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Trespass to chattel
Question: At an art fair, a vendor intentionally flicked washable paint at a rival vendor's display sign to make it unreadable. The paint missed the sign and landed on a nearby sculptor's small clay sculpture, staining it and requiring repair. The sculptor sues the vendor for trespass to chattels. Is the sculptor likely to prevail in a trespass to chattels claim against the vendor?
Answer A: No, because the vendor intended to affect the rival's sign, not the sculptor's sculpture
Answer B: Yes, because the vendor's intent to interfere with a chattel transfers to the damaged sculpture
Answer C: No, because transferred intent applies only to personal torts and never to property torts
Answer D: Yes, but only if the vendor intended permanent damage
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The vendor intended a trespass to chattels against the sign and instead impaired the sculptor's chattel. Trespass to chattels is within the transferred-intent group.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Transferred intent can apply within the traditional intentional tort group, including trespass to chattels, when the defendant's intended invasion lands on another protected interest. C: The traditional group includes trespass to land and trespass to chattels, not only personal torts. D: Trespass to chattels does not require intent to cause permanent damage. Intentional interference causing impairment is enough.
lb_rule_reference: Intent to commit trespass to chattels can transfer when the defendant intends to interfere with one chattel but instead interferes with another.
