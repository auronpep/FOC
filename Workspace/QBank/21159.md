Outline: Reliance damages
Question: After contracting to perform at a festival, Lydia spent $3,000 on nonrefundable travel deposits. The festival organizer, Samuel, then repudiated and immediately told Lydia not to incur any more expenses. Lydia nevertheless spent another $2,000 printing special programs for the canceled event. What amount of these expenses is most likely recoverable as reliance damages?
Answer A: $3,000, because the later $2,000 was avoidable after clear repudiation.
Answer B: $5,000, because all expenses were related to the contract.
Answer C: $2,000, because only the last expense was closest in time to the breach.
Answer D: Nothing, because Samuel repudiated before Lydia performed.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages may include reasonable expenditures incurred before breach or before notice of repudiation. The $3,000 deposits were incurred before Samuel's repudiation, but the $2,000 printing cost was incurred after clear notice and was avoidable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it includes avoidable post-repudiation spending. C: C is wrong because timing closer to breach does not make an expense recoverable. D: D is wrong because repudiation does not eliminate recovery for reasonable pre-repudiation reliance.
lb_rule_reference: Avoidable reliance after notice of breach
