Question: At a company lunch, employee Mary casually told the company's in-house lawyer that another employee, James, “always cuts corners.” Mary was not asked to provide information for legal advice, no investigation was underway, and the conversation was ordinary workplace gossip. Is Mary's statement privileged merely because she spoke to the company's lawyer?
Answer A: Yes, because every employee statement to in-house counsel is privileged.
Answer B: No, because the statement was not made confidentially for the purpose of obtaining or providing legal advice to the corporate client.
Answer C: Yes, because the statement might later become relevant to litigation.
Answer D: No, because in-house lawyers can never receive privileged communications.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Corporate privilege requires a legal-advice purpose and a confidential communication in the lawyer's legal capacity. A casual lunch conversation with in-house counsel is not privileged merely because the listener is a lawyer. The decisive axis is legal capacity and purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is the all-employee overclaim. Job status and lawyer status do not automatically create privilege.
