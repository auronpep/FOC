Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A bicycle model is designed with a welded front fork that passes strength testing when made to specifications. One bicycle leaves the factory with a missing weld on the fork. The fork snaps during ordinary riding, injuring the rider. Other bicycles of the same model do not have the missing weld. Which products theory best fits?
Answer A: Manufacturing defect, because this bicycle departed from the intended design
Answer B: Design defect, because any bicycle can injure a rider in a fall
Answer C: Failure to warn, because the rider was not warned that bicycles can fall
Answer D: No strict products claim unless the rider proves exactly which employee missed the weld
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The missing weld makes this unit a rogue unit that differs from properly made bicycles.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The claim attacks one unit's departure from design, not the whole product line. C: The harm came from a defective weld, not an omitted warning about an obvious general risk. D: Strict manufacturing defect does not require identifying the negligent employee.
lb_rule_reference: A manufacturing defect exists when a particular unit departs from the intended design or specifications and is more dangerous than properly made units.
