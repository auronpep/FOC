Question: A competent adult refuses an unwanted medical procedure. A state hospital seeks to perform the procedure anyway because doctors think it is in the patient's best medical interest. The patient invokes liberty and bodily integrity. What is the best due process frame?
Answer A: The state always wins because doctors know best
Answer B: The patient has a protected liberty interest, though the right must be carefully framed and balanced against state interests
Answer C: The patient automatically wins because every medical preference is fundamental
Answer D: The issue is only procedural due process because a hospital is involved
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Bodily integrity and refusal of unwanted treatment are recognized liberty interests, not merely policy preferences.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Competent adults have important liberty interests in refusing unwanted medical treatment. C: Substantive due process requires careful description of the right and attention to state interests. D: The claim is about whether the state may force treatment, a substantive liberty question.
lb_rule_reference: Substantive due process protects certain bodily integrity and medical-decision liberty interests, but courts carefully identify the right and consider the state's interests.
