Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A gym employee drilled a small peephole into a private locker room wall and used it to watch members changing clothes. The employee never recorded or shared what he saw. A member discovered the hole and sued for intrusion upon seclusion. Is publication required for the member to prevail?
Answer A: No, because the claim is complete upon the highly offensive intrusion into a private space
Answer B: Yes, because every privacy tort requires public disclosure
Answer C: No, but only if the employee made a false statement about the member
Answer D: Yes, unless the member can prove reputational harm
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Intrusion protects seclusion itself; disclosure is not an element.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Only some privacy torts require publicity; intrusion does not. C: Intrusion does not require falsity. D: Intrusion protects private space, not reputation alone.
lb_rule_reference: A highly offensive intentional invasion of a private place can establish intrusion upon seclusion without any publication of what was seen.
