Question: A plaintiff sues for permanent back injuries after a collision. The defendant wants the plaintiff examined by an orthopedic specialist. The plaintiff refuses to appear voluntarily. The defendant moves for a court order and identifies the specialist, time, place, manner, conditions, and scope of the exam. What must the defendant show?
Answer A: Only that the plaintiff's back condition is relevant to damages
Answer B: That the plaintiff's condition is in controversy and there is good cause for the examination
Answer C: That the plaintiff has already designated a medical expert to testify
Answer D: Nothing, because a defendant may schedule a physical examination by notice alone
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A claimed permanent back injury places the condition in controversy, but the moving party still must show good cause and obtain a court order.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A compelled examination requires both the condition in controversy and good cause. C: Rule 35 does not require the examined party to designate an expert first. D: A compulsory Rule 35 exam requires a court order.
lb_rule_reference: A court may order a party or person in a party's custody or legal control to submit to a physical or mental examination only when the condition is in controversy and good cause is shown.
