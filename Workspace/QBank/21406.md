Question: In a federal civil trial, Peter testifies for Paul. On cross-examination, Peter denies that he forged a receipt in an unrelated matter. The question was allowed because forgery is probative of truthfulness. Paul's opponent then calls Andrew to testify that he saw Peter forge the receipt. Peter objects. The testimony is offered only to show Peter's character for untruthfulness. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit Andrew's testimony because Peter denied the act on cross-examination.
Answer B: Admit Andrew's testimony because forgery is probative of truthfulness.
Answer C: Exclude Andrew's testimony because extrinsic evidence is not admissible to prove the specific act solely to attack Peter's character for truthfulness.
Answer D: Exclude Andrew's testimony only if Peter is a criminal defendant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 608(b) permits inquiry on cross-examination in the court's discretion, but it forbids extrinsic evidence offered solely to prove a specific instance of conduct to attack or support a witness's character for truthfulness. Peter's denial does not open the door to Andrew's extrinsic testimony for that sole purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because denial does not make extrinsic proof admissible for truthfulness-only conduct. B: B is wrong because probative value for truthfulness permits possible cross-examination, not extrinsic proof. D: D is wrong because the extrinsic-evidence bar applies to witnesses generally, not only criminal defendants.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(b) / extrinsic specific-act proof barred
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the denial-on-cross trap and the extrinsic-proof wall under Rule 608(b).
