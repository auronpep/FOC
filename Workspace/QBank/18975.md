Question: A religious group solicits donations for spiritual counseling. Prosecutors do not dispute the group's religious beliefs. They charge the group's treasurer under a neutral fraud statute for knowingly sending donors fake receipts claiming that money was spent on counseling when it was used for personal vacations. What is the strongest Free Exercise response to the prosecution?
Answer A: The prosecution is invalid because courts may never examine conduct connected to religious fundraising
Answer B: The prosecution is permissible if it targets knowing deception rather than the truth of the group's religious beliefs
Answer C: The prosecution is invalid unless prosecutors prove the group's theology is insincere
Answer D: The prosecution triggers strict scrutiny because any criminal law burdening a religious group is suspect
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The state may not declare religious doctrine false, but it can punish knowingly false factual representations in fundraising under a neutral law.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Neutral fraud laws may apply to deceptive conduct even when religious groups are involved. C: Fraud liability here turns on knowingly false receipts, not whether the religious beliefs are sincere or true. D: A neutral fraud law aimed at deceptive conduct ordinarily does not become suspect merely because the defendant is religious.
lb_rule_reference: Government may not punish religious belief as false, but it may enforce neutral fraud laws against deceptive conduct.
