Outline: Liquidated damages
Question: Mary agreed to buy a custom restaurant oven from Paul, a merchant seller, for $40,000 and paid a $6,000 deposit. The contract provided that if Mary repudiated after production was scheduled, Paul could retain $2,500 as liquidated damages. At contracting, the parties reasonably expected hard-to-measure losses from idle production time, component commitments, and resale delay, and $2,500 was a reasonable forecast. Mary repudiated after production was scheduled. Paul later minimized his loss and proved only $1,200 in actual damages. What amount of Mary's deposit must Paul return?
Answer A: $6,000, because Paul ultimately proved only a small actual loss.
Answer B: $4,800, because Paul may retain only the $1,200 actual damages he proved.
Answer C: $3,500, because Paul may retain the valid $2,500 liquidated-damages amount from the $6,000 deposit.
Answer D: $5,500, because the UCC allows Paul to retain only $500 when a buyer defaults.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is restitution of the buyer's deposit under UCC goods law, offset by a valid liquidated-damages term. Mary is the buyer, Paul is the merchant seller, and the custom oven is goods. The penalty gate is satisfied because the clause was reasonable in light of anticipated hard-to-measure production and resale-delay losses. The statutory no-clause retention amount does not displace a valid liquidated-damages clause. The formula is $6,000 deposit − $2,500 valid liquidated damages = $3,500 returned. The result scope is offset restitution.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores the valid stipulated remedy. B: B is wrong because it substitutes later actual damages for an enforceable liquidated amount, triggering WAL-REM-015. D: D is wrong because it applies the UCC statutory retention shortcut despite the presence of a valid liquidated-damages clause, a WAL-REM-016 error.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-718 liquidated damages — valid stipulated damages control deposit restitution for buyer breach
