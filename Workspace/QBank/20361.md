Question: A federal statute gives a government agency an unconditional right to intervene in a specific type of enforcement action. The agency files a timely motion to intervene in such an action. A party objects that intervention is always discretionary. How should the court rule on that objection?
Answer A: Sustain it, because intervention is always permissive and discretionary
Answer B: Overrule it, because a timely applicant with an unconditional federal statutory right must be allowed to intervene
Answer C: Sustain it unless the agency also proves inadequate representation under Rule 24(a)(2)
Answer D: Overrule it only if all existing parties consent
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: This is intervention of right under Rule 24(a)(1), not permissive intervention.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 24(a)(1) makes intervention mandatory when a federal statute gives an unconditional right and the motion is timely. C: A federal statutory unconditional right is a separate basis for intervention of right. D: Intervention of right does not depend on consent of existing parties.
lb_rule_reference: On timely motion, the court must permit intervention by anyone given an unconditional right to intervene by federal statute.
