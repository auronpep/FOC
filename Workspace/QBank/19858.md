Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Esther owned three separate lots in Galilee County. She signed and delivered to Daniel a deed stating, “I convey one of my three Galilee County lots to Daniel.” The deed did not say which lot was conveyed and gave no method for choosing among them. Esther privately intended to convey Lot 2, but that intent was not stated in the deed or otherwise communicated. Who owns Lot 2?
Answer A: Daniel, because he may choose which of the three lots the deed conveyed.
Answer B: Daniel, because Esther privately intended Lot 2.
Answer C: Esther, because the deed does not identify the property conveyed with reasonable certainty.
Answer D: Daniel, but only if he records the deed before Esther conveys Lot 2 to someone else.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is property description. The instrument must identify the land conveyed with reasonable certainty or provide a way to do so. A private, unstated intention to convey Lot 2 cannot cure an indefinite description, so title to Lot 2 remains with Esther.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the grantee does not get unilateral power to select the land absent an authorized selection method. B: B is wrong because private intent cannot cure the deed's uncertainty. D: D is wrong because recording cannot validate a deed that fails to identify the property conveyed.
