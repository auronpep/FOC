Question: A city is sued over a permit policy that excludes certain speakers from a public event. Two weeks after suit is filed, the city suspends the policy but states that it may reinstate the policy for future events if needed. Is the case necessarily moot?
Answer A: Yes, because the challenged policy is not being enforced today
Answer B: Yes, because mootness has no exceptions
Answer C: No, not if the city remains free to reinstate the challenged policy
Answer D: No, because every speech case remains live forever
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The city's voluntary suspension does not make recurrence impossible, so the controversy may remain live.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Voluntary cessation does not moot a case if the defendant remains free to resume the conduct. B: Recognized exceptions and limitations can preserve a live controversy. D: The case remains live only if effective relief or a recognized mootness principle applies.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant's voluntary cessation does not moot a case unless it is absolutely clear the challenged conduct cannot reasonably be expected to recur.
