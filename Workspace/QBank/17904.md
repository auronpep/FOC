Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A courier company required its drivers to remain on assigned routes and gave them a 20-minute paid break during each shift. While delivering packages downtown, a driver turned two blocks off the route to buy a sandwich and immediately headed back toward the next delivery address. On the way back to the route, the driver negligently ran a red light and struck a pedestrian. The pedestrian sues the courier company for vicarious liability. The driver was using a company van, was still on the clock, and was carrying undelivered packages. Is the pedestrian likely to prevail on a vicarious liability claim against the courier company under respondeat superior?
Answer A: No, because any personal errand by an employee is a frolic outside the scope of employment
Answer B: Yes, because the driver was still acting substantially within authorized time, place, and purpose when the negligent driving occurred
Answer C: Yes, because employers are strictly liable for every tort committed by an employee during a paid shift
Answer D: No, because the courier company did not personally drive negligently or instruct the driver to run the red light
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The driver was on the clock, in the company van, carrying packages, and returning to the assigned delivery route after a minor break-related detour. That places the negligent driving within the scope of employment for respondeat superior.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A brief meal detour during authorized work time, while still driving the employer's vehicle and returning to deliveries, is usually a detour rather than a frolic. Scope does not vanish merely because the employee had a mixed personal motive. C: Respondeat superior is not liability for every paid-shift tort. The employee still must be acting within the scope of employment, and a major personal frolic would defeat vicarious liability. D: The claim is vicarious liability, not direct negligence. If the employee was acting within scope, the employer may be liable even though the employer itself used reasonable care.
lb_rule_reference: An employer is vicariously liable for an employee's tort committed within authorized time, place, and purpose; a minor detour to serve a personal need usually remains within scope.
