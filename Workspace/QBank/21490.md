Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: A contractor orally agreed to repair Miriam's porch for $900, including $250 worth of lumber and nails. The repair could be fully completed in two weeks. After the contractor bought materials but before work began, Miriam canceled and asserted the UCC Statute of Frauds because the total price exceeded $500. What is the best response?
Answer A: The UCC goods Statute of Frauds does not control if the predominant purpose of the contract is repair services rather than the sale of goods.
Answer B: The contract is unenforceable because any contract involving more than $500 in total value must be written.
Answer C: The contract is unenforceable because any contract involving lumber is a sale of goods.
Answer D: The contract is within the one-year provision because the price exceeds $500.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The source-law gate matters. If the predominant purpose is repair services with incidental materials, Article 2's goods Statute of Frauds does not apply. The one-year provision also is not triggered because the work could be completed within two weeks.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the $500 threshold belongs to UCC goods contracts, not all contracts. C: C is wrong because incidental goods do not automatically make the transaction goods-dominant. D: D is wrong because price does not trigger the one-year provision.
lb_rule_reference: Mixed transaction / predominant services
