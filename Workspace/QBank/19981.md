Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police ask a tenant for permission to search an apartment she shares with her roommate. The tenant says officers may search the living room and kitchen. Officers then enter the roommate's locked bedroom, which the tenant says she never uses, and find contraband in a desk drawer. Was the bedroom search justified by consent?
Answer A: Yes, because one tenant's consent always authorizes a search of the entire apartment
Answer B: No, because the consenting tenant lacked actual or apparent authority over the locked bedroom
Answer C: Yes, because police had consent to enter the apartment doorway
Answer D: No, but only because the roommate was absent
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The tenant limited consent to common areas and told officers she did not use the locked bedroom. Searching the bedroom exceeded consent and authority.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Consent authority depends on common access or apparent authority over the specific place searched. C: Consent to enter or search common areas does not authorize locked private spaces. D: Absence is not the key; lack of authority and scope is.
lb_rule_reference: Third-party consent is valid if the person has actual or apparent authority over the area searched, but authority does not automatically extend to exclusive private spaces.
