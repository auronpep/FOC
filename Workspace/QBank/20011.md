Question: Elijah conveyed Blackacre “to Leah for life, then to Daniel and his heirs.” Daniel later validly conveyed all of his remainder interest in Blackacre to Leah. There were no intervening interests. What estate does Leah have after Daniel's conveyance?
Answer A: Only a life estate, because a life tenant can never acquire a remainder.
Answer B: A life estate plus no enforceable future interest, because Daniel's conveyance destroyed the remainder.
Answer C: A fee simple subject to condition subsequent, because Leah's original estate was limited by her life.
Answer D: Fee simple absolute, because the life estate and the vested remainder merged in Leah.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Leah has fee simple absolute. When a life estate and the next vested estate in fee simple come into the hands of the same person and no intervening estate exists, the interests merge, leaving the holder with fee simple ownership.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a life tenant may acquire a future interest from its holder. B: B is wrong because Daniel's remainder was transferred, not destroyed. C: C is wrong because no condition subsequent or right of entry exists.
lb_rule_reference: Estate merger / life estate plus vested remainder
