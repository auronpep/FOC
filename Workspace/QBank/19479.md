Question: A state owns a cement plant and sells cement for public construction projects. During a shortage, the plant sells first to in-state buyers before taking orders from out-of-state buyers. The state does not regulate private cement sellers. How does the market participant doctrine apply?
Answer A: The state may favor local buyers when selling its own cement as a participant in that market
Answer B: The preference is invalid because states can never prefer residents in commerce
Answer C: The preference is valid only if Congress expressly authorized it
Answer D: Article IV privileges and immunities automatically protects corporations buying cement
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The state is acting as a seller in its own market, not regulating private commerce generally.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The market participant exception allows local preference inside the state's own transaction. C: Market participant status is a separate exception. D: Article IV protects citizens, not corporations, and this fact pattern asks the DCC market participant rule.
lb_rule_reference: When a state acts as a market participant rather than a regulator, it may favor its own citizens in that market.
