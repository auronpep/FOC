Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Ruth contracted with Boaz to rent a booth at a new crafts market for one month. Boaz wrongfully refused to provide the booth. Ruth cannot prove any lost profits because the market had never opened and she had no prior sales history. Before Boaz breached, Ruth reasonably spent $6,000 on booth fixtures and $3,000 on required permits for that market. What is Ruth's best damages measure?
Answer A: No damages, because lost profits are too speculative.
Answer B: $9,000 in reliance damages, assuming the expenses were reasonably incurred in reliance on the contract.
Answer C: The reasonable rental value of the booth, because reliance damages are measured by Boaz's benefit.
Answer D: The profits Ruth hoped to earn, because a breach always entitles the injured party to expected profits.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When expectation damages are too speculative, the injured party may recover reasonable expenditures made in reliance on the contract, subject to limitations such as avoidability and loss the breaching party proves would have occurred. Ruth's recoverable reliance expenditures are the $6,000 fixtures plus $3,000 permits.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the failure to prove profits does not bar all recovery if reliance expenses are proven. C: C is wrong because benefit to Boaz is restitution, not reliance. D: D is wrong because hoped-for profits must still be proven with reasonable certainty.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance damages / speculative expectation fallback
