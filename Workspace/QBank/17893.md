Outline: Receipt of stolen property
Question: Police recover a stolen watch and return it to secure police custody. The next day, an undercover officer offers the recovered watch to Quinn, saying, "This is stolen." Quinn buys it, believing it is stolen. No statute treats recovered property used in a sting as still stolen. Is Quinn guilty of common-law receiving stolen property?
Answer A: Yes, because Quinn believed the watch was stolen when he received it
Answer B: Yes, because police can always preserve stolen status by saying the item is stolen
Answer C: No, because the watch was no longer legally stolen after police recovered it
Answer D: No, because Quinn did not personally steal the watch
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: At common law, receiving stolen property requires stolen property at receipt. Because police had recovered the watch and no statute extends liability, the completed offense fails.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The offense also requires property that is stolen when received. Police recovery ended the stolen status under the common-law formulation supplied. B: The facts state no statute preserving stolen status. Police words do not make recovered property legally stolen. D: Receiving stolen property is a separate offense from theft. The problem is stolen status, not Quinn's role.
lb_rule_reference: At common law, property recovered by police and then used in a sting may no longer be stolen, so receiving-stolen-property liability may fail absent a statute.
