Question: After a jury convicts Ruben of burglary, his attorney withdraws before sentencing. At sentencing, Ruben appears alone, does not waive counsel, and receives the statutory maximum sentence after the prosecutor argues aggravating facts. Ruben challenges the sentence. What is the best Sixth Amendment answer?
Answer A: Ruben had a right to counsel at sentencing because sentencing is a critical stage
Answer B: Counsel is required only until the jury returns a guilty verdict
Answer C: Counsel was unnecessary because the sentence was within the statutory range
Answer D: Counsel was unnecessary because Ruben already had counsel at trial
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Sentencing can substantially affect liberty and legal rights. Counsel is required unless validly waived.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The right to counsel extends to critical post-verdict stages such as sentencing. C: The counsel right is about representation at a critical stage, not only whether the final sentence was authorized. D: Representation at one critical stage does not waive or satisfy the right at a later critical stage.
lb_rule_reference: Sentencing is a critical stage of a criminal prosecution, so the defendant is entitled to counsel unless the right is validly waived.
