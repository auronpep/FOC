Question: On March 1, Ruth orally agrees to work as Daniel's bookkeeper for exactly 15 months beginning on April 1. Ruth begins work, but Daniel fires her after two months. Ruth sues for the balance of the 15-month term. Daniel raises the Statute of Frauds. What is the best result?
Answer A: Ruth can recover because employment contracts are never within the Statute of Frauds.
Answer B: Ruth can recover because she began performance.
Answer C: Daniel has a Statute of Frauds defense because the agreement, by its terms, could not be fully performed within one year.
Answer D: Daniel has no defense unless Ruth also agreed not to work for anyone else during the 15 months.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An oral agreement that cannot be fully performed within one year from its making falls within the Statute of Frauds. A 15-month employment term beginning a month after the oral agreement cannot be completed within one year of the agreement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because employment contracts can fall within the one-year provision when the stated term makes performance within one year impossible. B: B is wrong because beginning performance does not generally remove an executory services contract from the one-year SOF. D: D is wrong because exclusivity is not the controlling SOF trigger.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law Statute of Frauds — one-year category
