Outline: Attorney-client
Question: A defendant in a fraud case testifies, “I acted in good faith because my lawyer told me the transaction was legal.” The defendant refuses to disclose the communications with that lawyer about the transaction, asserting attorney-client privilege. The plaintiff seeks those communications only on the advice the defendant claims to have relied on. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Protect all communications because attorney-client privilege is never waived without an express written waiver.
Answer B: Protect all communications because relevance and fairness cannot affect privilege.
Answer C: Find a waiver as to every communication the defendant ever had with any lawyer.
Answer D: Find a waiver as to communications concerning the advice the defendant placed at issue by asserting reliance on counsel.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A party who affirmatively relies on advice of counsel may waive attorney-client privilege as to communications concerning that advice. The party cannot use privileged advice as a sword while withholding the communications needed to test the claim. The decisive axis is at-issue waiver and subject scope.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Waiver can arise from litigation conduct, not only an express written waiver. B: B is wrong because Relevance alone does not defeat privilege, but fairness can matter when the holder affirmatively puts the advice at issue. C: C is wrong because The waiver is limited to the subject matter of the advice placed at issue, not all lawyer communications.
lb_rule_reference: Attorney-client waiver / advice-of-counsel defense
lb_student_diagnostic: Student misses implied waiver or fails to limit the waiver to the advice actually placed in issue.
