Question: A statute authorizes the Department of Transportation to approve new drone routes. The statute also provides that either house of Congress may cancel any approved route by passing a resolution within 30 days. The Department approves a route. The House alone passes a resolution canceling it, and the agency treats the route as void. A drone operator challenges the cancellation. What is the best constitutional argument?
Answer A: The cancellation is valid because Congress reserved oversight authority when it delegated route approval to the agency
Answer B: The cancellation is invalid because a one-house resolution altering legal rights bypasses bicameralism and presentment
Answer C: The cancellation is valid because the House is closer to the people than the agency
Answer D: The cancellation is invalid only if the Senate objects to the House resolution
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The House resolution cancels an approved route and changes private legal rights. That legislative effect requires passage by both houses and presentment to the President.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: When Congress changes legal rights or cancels executive action with legal effect, it must comply with bicameralism and presentment. Labeling it oversight does not cure the defect. C: Constitutional law requires bicameralism and presentment for legislative action. Policy convenience does not replace the required process. D: Bicameralism requires affirmative passage by both houses, not silence by one house.
lb_rule_reference: Congressional action that alters legal rights, duties, or executive action generally must satisfy bicameralism and presentment; a one-house legislative veto is invalid.
