Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Paul signed a deed conveying Vineyardacre to John. Paul handed the deed to John and said, “This deed transfers title to you today, but if you do not pay the remaining price by Friday, title will automatically come back to me.” John accepted the deed but did not pay by Friday. The deed itself contained no condition or right of reentry, and John signed no reconveyance. Who owns Vineyardacre after Friday?
Answer A: Paul, because John's nonpayment automatically voided the delivered deed.
Answer B: Paul, because consideration is required for delivery of a deed.
Answer C: John only if he recorded the deed before Friday.
Answer D: John, because title passed by present delivery and the oral forfeiture condition did not automatically reconvey the land to Paul.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery/result locks are present delivery and no effective reconveyance. Paul delivered the deed with words that title transferred today, and John accepted. John's nonpayment may create a contract or payment remedy, but the oral forfeiture statement did not automatically revest title in Paul absent an effective written condition or reconveyance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because nonpayment does not automatically void a delivered deed. B: B is wrong because consideration is not required for deed delivery. C: C is wrong because recording is not the condition for title passage between Paul and John.
lb_rule_reference: A post-delivery oral forfeiture condition does not automatically revest title in the grantor
