Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Leah paid Jacob $8,000 in advance to install a well. Jacob repudiated before doing any work. Leah spent no additional money preparing for the installation and hired another installer at the same price. Leah sues Jacob to recover the $8,000 she paid. What is the best characterization of Leah's $8,000 recovery?
Answer A: Expectation damages, because Leah expected a well.
Answer B: Restitution, because Leah seeks return of a benefit she conferred on Jacob.
Answer C: Reliance damages, because every payment made under a contract is reliance.
Answer D: Consequential damages, because Jacob's breach forced Leah to sue.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Leah seeks return of money paid to Jacob. That is restitution—the recovery of a benefit conferred on the breaching party—not reliance expenditures made in preparation for performance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Leah has no price differential; she hired a substitute at the same price. C: C is wrong because a prepaid benefit transferred to the defendant is restitution, not automatically reliance. D: D is wrong because the payment is not a downstream consequential loss.
