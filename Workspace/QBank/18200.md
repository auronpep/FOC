Question: A city prohibits people from racing motorized scooters on crowded sidewalks. A rider challenges the ordinance under substantive due process, defining the right as "the fundamental right to move freely and enjoy personal autonomy outdoors." The city responds that the asserted right should be defined more specifically. What is the best analysis?
Answer A: The rider can define the right at any level of generality and thereby trigger strict scrutiny
Answer B: The court should carefully describe the asserted right and ask whether that right is deeply rooted before applying fundamental-right scrutiny
Answer C: The ordinance receives strict scrutiny because sidewalks are public property
Answer D: The ordinance is invalid unless the city gives each rider an individual pre-race hearing
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The doctrine does not let a litigant convert ordinary activity into a fundamental right through broad phrasing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Substantive due process requires careful description before deciding whether a right is fundamental. C: The claim is substantive due process about scooter racing, not speech in a forum. D: The challenge is to the substance of the ordinance, not a deprivation hearing.
lb_rule_reference: Substantive due process asks whether the claimed right, carefully described, is deeply rooted in history and tradition before treating it as fundamental.
