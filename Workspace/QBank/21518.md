Question: After a serious truck collision, Adam's lawyer hired an investigator to interview eyewitness Mary and prepare a signed statement for anticipated litigation. The opposing party knows Mary's address and can interview or depose her, but seeks the statement because it would be convenient. Should the court compel production of the statement?
Answer A: No, because the statement was prepared in anticipation of litigation and the requesting party has not shown substantial need or undue hardship.
Answer B: Yes, because all witness statements are freely discoverable once the witness is identified.
Answer C: Yes, because work product protects only materials personally written by lawyers.
Answer D: No, because ordinary work product is absolutely immune from discovery.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Documents and tangible things prepared in anticipation of litigation by or for a party or representative are work product. Ordinary work product may be discovered only with substantial need and inability to obtain the substantial equivalent without undue hardship. Convenience is not enough. The decisive axis is ordinary work product plus special showing.
