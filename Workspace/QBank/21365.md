Question: In a federal contract trial, Rachel claims David breached a delivery agreement. To prove liability, Rachel's lawyer asks David, “During settlement talks, didn't you say, ‘I missed the deadline, but I will pay only half'?” David objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because David is a party and all party statements are admissible.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the question seeks a statement from compromise negotiations to prove the validity or amount of the claim.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because settlement statements are admissible whenever they contain facts.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if David's lawyer was present during the settlement conversation.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Statements made during compromise negotiations are not admissible when offered to prove or disprove the validity or amount of the disputed claim. The party-opponent label does not override the policy exclusion for this purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because another rule can exclude a party's statement. C: C is wrong because factual statements made during compromise negotiations are also protected for the prohibited purpose. D: D is wrong because the exclusion does not depend on counsel's presence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 408/FRE 611 / settlement statement objection
