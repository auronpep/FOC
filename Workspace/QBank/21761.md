Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Miriam contracted to organize a public lecture for Nathan. Nathan breached. Miriam proves with reasonable certainty that, had Nathan performed, she would have earned $20,000 in net profit after all expected costs. Miriam also spent $5,000 on advertising that would have been necessary to earn that profit. Miriam seeks $25,000: the $20,000 net profit plus the $5,000 advertising expense. How should damages be measured?
Answer A: Miriam may recover $25,000 because reliance expenses are always added to expected profits.
Answer B: Miriam may recover $5,000 only, because reliance damages always replace expectation damages.
Answer C: Miriam may recover nothing because advertising expenses are never compensable.
Answer D: Miriam may not stack the $5,000 expense on top of the $20,000 net-profit expectation recovery because that would overcompensate her.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Expectation damages measured by net profit already put Miriam in the position she would have occupied after paying the expenses necessary to earn that profit. Adding the advertising cost again would overcompensate her. Reliance can be an alternative measure, but it is not stacked onto a full net expectation award.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it double counts the advertising expense. B: B is wrong because expectation damages are available when proven with reasonable certainty. C: C is wrong because advertising can be a reliance expense in the right measure, but not as an add-on to net profits already awarded.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance damages as alternative to expectation
