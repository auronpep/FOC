Question: David owned Cedaracre. David signed and delivered to Jonathan a gift deed conveying Cedaracre, and Jonathan accepted. Jonathan paid no value. Later, Solomon claimed that Jonathan could not even hold title because a recording statute protects only purchasers for value without notice. What is the best analysis?
Answer A: Jonathan received no title because only purchasers for value can take by deed.
Answer B: Jonathan received title and is automatically protected against all prior claims because every valid deed makes the grantee a protected purchaser.
Answer C: Jonathan received title only if the gift deed contained warranty language.
Answer D: Jonathan may receive title under the gift deed, but his donee status is a separate priority lock if a recording-act dispute arises.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a gift deed, and the decisive route separates validity from priority. A delivered gift deed can convey title without consideration. But if a later priority question arises under a statute protecting purchasers for value, Jonathan's lack of value matters to protected-party status; it does not retroactively invalidate the deed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because consideration is not required for deed validity. B: B is wrong because a valid deed does not automatically create protected purchaser status. C: C is wrong because warranty language is not required for a gift deed to transfer title.
lb_rule_reference: Gift deed can convey title but does not make the donee a purchaser for value under a recording act
