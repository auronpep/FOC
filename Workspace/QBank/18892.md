Question: Police arrest Malik for robbery. The next morning, a magistrate tells Malik the robbery charge, sets bail, and commits him to jail when he cannot post bond. Later that day, detectives question Malik about the robbery without counsel. Malik argues that his Sixth Amendment right had attached. Is Malik likely correct that the Sixth Amendment had attached before the questioning?
Answer A: Yes, because formal judicial proceedings began at the first appearance where the charge was stated and bail was set
Answer B: No, because the Sixth Amendment attaches only after a jury is sworn
Answer C: Yes, because arrest alone always attaches the Sixth Amendment
Answer D: No, because bail hearings are never part of criminal proceedings
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Attachment does not wait for trial. A first appearance that begins adversary judicial proceedings and restricts liberty is enough to attach the Sixth Amendment right for that charge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The right attaches much earlier, at the start of formal adversary proceedings. C: Arrest alone is not the attachment event. The key added fact is the judicial first appearance on the charge. D: A judicial proceeding stating the charge and restricting liberty is part of adversary criminal process for attachment purposes.
lb_rule_reference: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches when formal adversary judicial proceedings begin, including an initial appearance before a judicial officer where the defendant is told of the charge and liberty is restricted.
