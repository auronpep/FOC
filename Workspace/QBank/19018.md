Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Jacob contracted to buy 500 barrels of grain from Leah for $100 per barrel. Leah repudiated before delivery. Jacob did not cover. When Jacob learned of the breach, the market price at the place for tender was $114 per barrel. Jacob reasonably spent $900 inspecting available substitutes but decided not to buy because none met his delivery schedule. What is Jacob's proper damages measure?
Answer A: Nothing, because Jacob failed to cover.
Answer B: $7,000, the market-price difference only.
Answer C: $7,900, the market-price difference plus reasonable incidental expenses.
Answer D: $57,000, the market value of the grain at the time Jacob learned of the breach.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A buyer who does not cover may recover market damages: market price when the buyer learned of the breach minus contract price, plus proper incidental and consequential damages, less expenses saved. The market differential is $7,000 (($114 - $100) × 500), plus $900 reasonable incidental expenses, for $7,900.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because failure to cover does not bar the buyer from another remedy. B: B is wrong because it omits reasonable incidental expenses. D: D is wrong because the buyer does not recover the full market value; expectation damages measure the differential caused by the breach.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-713; UCC §2-715
