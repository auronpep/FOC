MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff, a citizen of Nevada, files a breach-of-contract suit in California state court against two defendants. Defendant A is a citizen of Texas. Defendant B is a citizen of California. The amount in controversy is $200,000. Defendant A and Defendant B jointly file a notice of removal, arguing that because diversity of citizenship exists between the plaintiff (Nevada) and all defendants (Texas and California), removal is proper. The plaintiff moves to remand. The governing forum is initially California state court; the issue is whether removal to federal court is proper under the MBE baseline. Should the court remand the case?
Answer A: No, because complete diversity exists between the Nevada plaintiff and the two defendants, satisfying § 1332, and both defendants joined in the removal
Answer B: Yes, because the forum-defendant rule applies only when the forum defendant is the sole defendant; here, Defendant A is also a party and may remove independently
Answer C: Yes, the court should remand, because Defendant B is a California citizen and California is the forum state, triggering the § 1441(b)(2) forum-defendant bar on removal
Answer D: No, because California is a neutral forum — neither the plaintiff's nor both defendants' home state — and the forum-defendant rule protects only plaintiffs filing in their own home state
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1441(b)(2) provides that a civil action otherwise removable solely on diversity grounds may not be removed if any properly joined and served defendant is a citizen of the state in which the action is brought. Here, Defendant B is a citizen of California, the state in which the plaintiff brought the action. Even though diversity of citizenship exists between the plaintiff and all defendants (which would support original federal jurisdiction), removal is barred by the forum-defendant rule. The district court should grant the plaintiff's motion to remand.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1441(b)(2) imposes an additional requirement beyond § 1332 diversity: in a diversity removal, no properly joined and served defendant may be a citizen of the state in which the action is brought. Defendant B is a citizen of California — the forum state. This is the forum-defendant rule, and it bars removal even when § 1332 diversity would exist. B: The forum-defendant rule bars removal if ANY properly joined and served defendant is a citizen of the forum state. There is no exception for cases with multiple defendants where at least one is not a forum defendant. The presence of Defendant A (Texas) does not cure Defendant B's (California) bar on removal. D: The forum-defendant rule does not depend on whether the plaintiff chose a home forum. It categorically bars removal in diversity cases where any served defendant is a citizen of the forum state, regardless of where the plaintiff lives or why the plaintiff chose that state court.
lb_rule_reference: 28 U.S.C. § 1441(b)(2) — a diversity case may not be removed if any properly joined and served defendant is a citizen of the state in which the action is brought.
