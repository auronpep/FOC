Question: Daniel agrees to buy a store from Abigail because both believe a new road will be built nearby within a year and increase customer traffic. After the contract is signed, the city cancels the road project. Daniel seeks rescission for mutual mistake. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant rescission because both parties believed the road would be built.
Answer B: Grant rescission because every failed assumption about profitability is a mutual mistake.
Answer C: Deny rescission because the parties were mistaken about a future event, not an existing fact at the time of contracting.
Answer D: Deny rescission only if Abigail proves that Daniel knew the road would be cancelled.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Mistake doctrine generally concerns erroneous beliefs about facts existing at the time of contracting. A later cancellation of a road project is a failed future expectation. It may raise other doctrines in some cases, but it is not mutual mistake about an existing fact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a shared expectation about a future event is not enough. B: B is wrong because profitability assumptions are ordinary business risks. D: D is wrong because Daniel's knowledge of cancellation is not necessary to reject the mutual mistake theory.
lb_rule_reference: Future event / no mistake at assent
