Question: The declaration for a condominium community prohibited owners from leaving trash bins in common walkways after collection day. The declaration authorized the association, after notice and a hearing, to impose reasonable fines and suspend access to recreational common areas for repeated violations. Aaron repeatedly left his bins in the common walkway. After written notice and a hearing, the association imposed a modest fine and suspended Aaron's use of the community pool for 30 days. The association did not enter Aaron's unit or remove any property from it. Aaron challenges the enforcement action. Which is the best result?
Answer A: The association's only valid remedy was to enter Aaron's unit and remove his bins.
Answer B: The association had no remedy because no other owner proved money damages.
Answer C: The association's rule is invalid because local zoning does not regulate trash-bin placement.
Answer D: The association's action is likely valid because it used remedies authorized by the declaration after notice and a hearing.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An HOA may enforce valid rules through remedies authorized by its governing documents, especially after notice and an opportunity to be heard. The declaration authorized reasonable fines and suspension of recreational common-area privileges, and the association followed that process.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because self-help entry is not the only remedy and may be unauthorized. B: B is wrong because association rules may be enforced without ordinary damages proof when the documents authorize enforcement. C: C is wrong because private land-use rules may regulate matters that zoning does not.
