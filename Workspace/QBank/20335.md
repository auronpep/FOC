Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Solicitation
Question: Pia asks Rowan to steal a set of lab keys from a university office. Rowan agrees and steals the keys that night. Pia is charged with solicitation to commit larceny and with the completed larceny as an accomplice. Both charges arise from the same request and theft. Under the usual merger rule, may Pia be convicted of both solicitation and the completed larceny?
Answer A: Yes, because solicitation never merges with any later offense
Answer B: No, because the solicitation merges into the completed larceny
Answer C: Yes, because Rowan, not Pia, physically took the keys
Answer D: No, but only because keys are not valuable property
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Once the solicited theft is completed, the solicitation is absorbed into the completed target offense for merger purposes.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Solicitation generally merges into the completed target offense when the solicited crime occurs. C: Pia can be liable for the completed offense as an accomplice. The merger question remains whether solicitation can be separately punished. D: The question assumes the completed larceny charge and asks about merger.
lb_rule_reference: Solicitation generally merges into the completed target offense when the solicited crime is actually committed by the solicited person.
