Outline: Lay opinion testimony
Question: In a federal contract case, Priscilla owns a small bakery. She handled the bakery's sales, inventory, and payroll for ten years. Priscilla is asked to estimate the value of the bakery's lost inventory after a flood. The defendant objects that Priscilla has not been qualified as an accountant or appraiser. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection if Priscilla's estimate is based on her particularized knowledge from owning and operating the bakery.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because valuation always requires expert testimony.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because lost inventory is an ultimate damages issue.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Priscilla uses generally accepted accounting principles.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A lay opinion can be based on particularized knowledge from the witness's own position or experience, so long as it is not specialized expert testimony disguised as lay testimony. An owner's estimate of business inventory value based on personal business knowledge can be admissible lay opinion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because owner or officer valuation can sometimes be lay opinion based on particularized knowledge. C: C is wrong because relation to damages does not automatically bar otherwise proper opinion. D: D is wrong because lay owner valuation does not require expert accounting methodology.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 701 / owner or officer value opinion from particularized knowledge
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests particularized-knowledge lay opinion and rejects expert-only valuation traps.
