Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Trial judge’s discretion to control order and presentation of evidence
Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Paul cross-examines Mary for three hours about the same five inspection entries. The judge tells Paul that he may spend fifteen more minutes on any remaining questions about those entries and may then move to other topics. Paul objects that cross-examination cannot be time-limited. Was the judge's ruling proper?
Answer A: No, because a party has an absolute right to cross-examine for as long as counsel wants.
Answer B: Yes, if the limit is reasonable and still permits meaningful cross-examination.
Answer C: No, unless Mary is a hostile witness.
Answer D: Yes, because the judge may prevent any cross-examination that seems repetitive.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may impose reasonable control to avoid wasting time, including reasonable limits on repetitive cross-examination, so long as the party retains a fair opportunity to examine the witness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because cross-examination is not unlimited. C: C is wrong because the court's authority to manage time is not limited to hostile witnesses. D: D is wrong because the judge may not eliminate meaningful cross-examination merely because some questions repeat.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / reasonable time limits
