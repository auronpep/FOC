Outline_code: 95070600
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Pretrial Conferences and Orders
Question: A woman sued a university in federal court alleging violations of her civil rights. During the final pretrial conference, the parties and the judge agreed to allow the testimony of two expert witnesses. The judge then named the two expert witnesses in the pretrial order. Three days before the trial was set to commence, the woman asked the court to modify the pretrial order to allow an additional expert witness. What does the woman need to show for the court to allow the additional witness?
Answer A: A: That the additional expert witness is necessary to prevent manifest injustice.
Answer B: B: That the university will not be prejudiced by the additional expert witness.
Answer C: C: That the woman's claim will be prejudiced by not having the additional expert witness.
Answer D: D: That the additional expert witness is necessary for the woman's prima facie case.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: “The court may hold a final pretrial conference to formulate a trial plan, including a plan to facilitate the admission of evidence. The court may modify the order issued after a final pretrial conference only to prevent manifest injustice.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(e). Consequently, unless the woman can show manifest injustice will result without the additional expert witness, the court will not modify the pretrial order. Thus, B, C, and D are incorrect.
