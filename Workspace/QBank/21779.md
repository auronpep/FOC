Question: In a federal civil battery trial, Ruth testifies that she saw David throw the first punch. On cross-examination, David's lawyer asks, “You expect this jury to believe that ridiculous story?” Ruth's lawyer objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the question is argumentative rather than a request for admissible testimony.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because no cross-examination may challenge a witness's credibility.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because all questions on cross-examination may be sarcastic or accusatory.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because the jury alone decides whether Ruth is telling the truth.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The question asks the witness to argue with counsel about the believability of her testimony instead of seeking a factual answer. The court may control argumentative questioning.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because credibility may be tested on cross-examination. C: C is wrong because cross is not unlimited in tone or form. D: D is wrong because the jury decides credibility, but the judge still controls improper question form.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / argumentative question
