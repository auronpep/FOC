Question: In a federal product-liability suit, the plaintiff requests customer complaint summaries about the same model of appliance. The manufacturer objects that the summaries contain hearsay and therefore could not be admitted at trial for their truth. The summaries are limited to the same model and the same alleged defect. What is the best response to the manufacturer's objection?
Answer A: The summaries are not discoverable because inadmissible evidence can never be discovered
Answer B: The summaries are discoverable if they are nonprivileged, relevant to a claim or defense, and proportional
Answer C: The summaries are discoverable only if the manufacturer admits the appliance was defective
Answer D: The summaries are privileged because customers sent them to the manufacturer
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Hearsay concerns may affect admissibility, but they do not automatically defeat discovery of relevant, proportional, nonprivileged information.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Discovery is not limited to evidence that will itself be admissible at trial. C: A party need not concede the merits before the opponent can obtain proper discovery. D: Customer complaints are not privileged merely because they were sent to a party.
lb_rule_reference: Information need not be admissible in evidence to be discoverable if it is nonprivileged, relevant to a claim or defense, and proportional to the needs of the case.
