Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers have probable cause to believe a motorcycle contains stolen parts. The motorcycle is parked under a tarp beside the suspect's house, inside the driveway area immediately next to the home. Without a warrant, officers walk up the driveway, lift the tarp, and search the motorcycle. Does the automobile exception alone justify the search?
Answer A: Yes, because any vehicle with probable cause may be searched wherever police find it
Answer B: No, because the automobile exception does not itself authorize entry into the home's curtilage to search the motorcycle
Answer C: Yes, because lifting a tarp is not a search
Answer D: No, because the automobile exception never applies to motorcycles
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Probable cause to search a vehicle does not supply authority to invade protected curtilage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The exception does not give police a right to intrude into the home's curtilage to reach the vehicle. C: The problem is the warrantless entry and search in the home's curtilage. D: A motorcycle can be a vehicle; the limiting fact is curtilage.
lb_rule_reference: The automobile exception does not by itself authorize officers to enter the home's curtilage to search a vehicle parked there.
