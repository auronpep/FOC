Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers unlawfully entered Sam's garage and saw crates of stolen laptops. They then applied for a warrant. The affidavit relied heavily on what the officers saw during the unlawful entry; without those observations, it contained only a vague anonymous tip. A magistrate issued the warrant, and officers seized the laptops. Sam moves to suppress. How should the court rule on the independent-source argument?
Answer A: Deny suppression, because a warrant was obtained before the laptops were seized
Answer B: Deny suppression, because independent source always applies after officers leave and return
Answer C: Grant suppression, because the warrant affidavit depended on the unlawful entry and lacked an independent lawful basis
Answer D: Grant suppression only if the officers acted in bad faith when entering the garage
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The government cannot rely on independent source when the warrant was built from the illegal search. Without the tainted observations, probable cause was absent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The warrant itself was tainted because the affidavit depended on illegal observations. A later warrant is not independent merely because it exists. B: Physical separation does not create independence. The legal source must be untainted. D: The independent-source issue is whether the later warrant was untainted. Bad faith is not the only way taint defeats admission.
lb_rule_reference: Independent source requires a genuinely independent lawful basis; a warrant built from tainted observations does not break the causal chain.
