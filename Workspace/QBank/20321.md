


BARMATRIX Q#: 20321
Outline_code: 62060100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Vicarious Liability for Acts of Others
Question: One of the businesses in the building inspector's territory was a nightclub. On one of her inspections of the nightclub, the building inspector asked the manager of the business whether the window draperies were fire-retardant as required by the city's building code. The manager responded that they were. Although the building inspector was familiar with the instructions in the manual, she was in a hurry. Taking a cigarette lighter from her pocket, she held its flame under one of the draperies where it was hanging. The drapery caught fire, which spread, completely destroying the building. A passerby was injured in the fire. If the passerby brings an action for damages against the city on a theory of respondeat superior, the passerby will
Answer A: A: lose, since the building inspector was acting in violation of specific instructions from her employer.
Answer B: B: lose, since the building inspector's duties involve the exercise of unsupervised discretion.
Answer C: C: win, because the building inspector was negligent.
Answer D: D: win, whether or not the building inspector was negligent.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the doctrine of respondeat superior, an employer is vicariously liable for the negligence of an employee committed within the scope of employment. An employee is acting within the scope of employment if his or her conduct is intended to further the interests of his or her employer and if his or her employer has a right to control his or her conduct. Since the building inspector was attempting to find out whether the drapes were fire-retardant, which was part of what the city hired her to do, and since the city had the right to tell her how to test drapes, she was acting within the scope of her employment even if she was violating specific instructions that she received from the city.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is therefore incorrect. B is a fabrication with no basis in law, since even when an employee's conduct is unsupervised, respondeat superior may result in the imposition of vicarious liability on his or her employer. D is incorrect because under respondeat superior, an employer is vicariously liable only for that for which the employee is liable.
