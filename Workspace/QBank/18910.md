Question: Elijah sues a bus company after a crash. Elijah offers Deborah, a licensed accident reconstruction engineer, to testify about the likely speed of the bus. The bus company objects that Deborah is not qualified because she has never inspected this particular bus model. The court hears Deborah's qualifications and the basis of her proposed testimony. How should the court proceed on the qualification issue?
Answer A: The judge should decide whether Deborah is qualified to offer the expert opinion.
Answer B: The jury must decide whether Deborah is qualified before the judge rules on admissibility.
Answer C: The court must exclude Deborah because only eyewitnesses may estimate vehicle speed.
Answer D: The court must admit Deborah automatically because all licensed engineers are qualified for all accident cases.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Expert qualification is a preliminary question for the court. The judge decides whether the expert is qualified and whether the opinion may be admitted; if admitted, the jury may evaluate weight and credibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because qualification is a threshold admissibility question for the judge. C: C is wrong because expert testimony can be used when specialized knowledge will help, even if eyewitnesses also testified. D: D is wrong because licensure may support qualification but does not automatically admit every opinion in every case.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a) / expert qualification gate
