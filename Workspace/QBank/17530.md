Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Isaiah signed a deed conveying Orchardacre to Jeremiah and had the deed notarized. Isaiah then placed the deed in his own filing cabinet and told Jeremiah, “I may deliver this after we finish negotiating, but I am keeping it for now.” Jeremiah argued that the notarization completed the conveyance. Who owns Orchardacre?
Answer A: Jeremiah, because acknowledgment before a notary is the legal equivalent of delivery.
Answer B: Isaiah, because notarization may support recordability but does not replace delivery with present intent.
Answer C: Jeremiah, because the deed was signed and contained all required formalities.
Answer D: Isaiah only if Jeremiah had not paid the agreed purchase price.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is present intent, not acknowledgment. Isaiah kept the deed and expressly said he might deliver it later. Notarization may assist recording, but it did not deliver the deed or pass title.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because acknowledgment is not delivery. C: C is wrong because deed formalities are separate from the delivery requirement. D: D is wrong because nonpayment is not the decisive lock when no delivery occurred.
lb_rule_reference: Acknowledgment or notarization does not itself constitute delivery
