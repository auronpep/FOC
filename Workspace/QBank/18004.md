Question: Samuel sold Rachel a parcel that, at the time of sale, had legal access to a public road through a recorded public access route. Five years later, the county closed that public access route because a bridge failed. Rachel then demanded an easement by necessity over Samuel's retained land. Is Rachel likely entitled to an easement by necessity over Samuel's retained land?
Answer A: No, because the parcel had legal access when Samuel and Rachel severed the tract.
Answer B: Yes, because the parcel lacks access now and current landlock controls.
Answer C: Yes, because a county road closure automatically burdens the original seller's retained land.
Answer D: No, but only because recorded public access routes can never be lost.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Necessity is measured at severance. Rachel's parcel had legal access when Samuel sold it, so a later county closure does not retroactively create an easement by necessity over Samuel's retained land.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because current landlock alone is insufficient. C: C is wrong because public road closure does not automatically create a private burden on the original seller. D: D is wrong because the point is not that public access can never be lost; it is that necessity did not exist at severance.
lb_rule_reference: No necessity when legal access existed at severance
lb_student_diagnostic: The repair signal is to timestamp the claimed necessity before analyzing the current hardship.
