Outline: Affirmative duties to act
Question: A store manager saw a customer collapse. The manager announced that he was trained in first aid and told bystanders not to call emergency services because he would handle it. The manager then left the customer unattended for ten minutes while he finished a phone call. The delay worsened the customer's injury. What duty did the manager have after undertaking aid?
Answer A: No duty, because he had no duty to start the rescue
Answer B: Strict liability for any bad outcome
Answer C: A duty to use reasonable care in the undertaken aid
Answer D: A duty only if the customer paid a separate rescue fee
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The manager voluntarily undertook aid and discouraged other help. His negligent abandonment after reliance can create liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Once he undertook aid and induced reliance, he had to use reasonable care. B: The duty is reasonable care, not a guarantee of success. D: Voluntary undertaking and reliance can create a duty without a separate fee.
lb_rule_reference: One who voluntarily undertakes a rescue or service must act with reasonable care and may be liable if negligent performance or abandonment increases risk or induces reliance.
