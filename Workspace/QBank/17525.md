Question: A delivery van parked in a loading zone for longer than a city safety ordinance allowed. A pedestrian noticed the violation and walked around the van without incident. The pedestrian was not touched, did not fall, lost no property, and incurred no expense. The pedestrian sues the driver for negligence based only on the ordinance violation. Assuming the ordinance applies, what result is most likely?
Answer A: The pedestrian wins automatically because a statutory violation proves all negligence elements
Answer B: The pedestrian may recover only nominal damages
Answer C: The claim fails because no injury or compensable loss was caused by the violation
Answer D: The claim fails because statutes can never affect civil negligence claims
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The ordinance may establish breach, but the facts supply no actual harm caused by that breach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A statutory violation does not prove causation and damages. B: Negligence normally requires actual harm, even when breach is supplied by statute. D: Proper safety statutes can affect breach; the missing element here is damages, not the statute's relevance.
lb_rule_reference: Even if a statutory violation supplies breach through negligence per se, the plaintiff still must prove causation and actual damages.
