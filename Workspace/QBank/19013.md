Question: In a federal premises-liability trial, Hannah testifies that she saw water on the floor before Miriam fell. On cross-examination, the defendant shows Hannah a security photo suggesting she was facing away from the aisle at the relevant moment. The defendant does not call Hannah a liar and offers the photo only to show mistaken perception. The plaintiff then calls Elizabeth to testify that Hannah is an honest person. The defendant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit Elizabeth's testimony because any contradiction of a witness permits truthful-character evidence.
Answer B: Admit Elizabeth's testimony because the photo attacks Hannah's character for truthfulness.
Answer C: Exclude Elizabeth's testimony because the photo attacks perception or accuracy, not Hannah's character for truthfulness.
Answer D: Exclude Elizabeth's testimony only if the photo is admitted as substantive evidence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A contradiction or perception attack does not automatically attack the witness's character for truthfulness. Here the photo is used to show Hannah may have been mistaken about what she saw, not that she is generally untruthful. Truthful-character support is therefore not opened under Rule 608(a).
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because contradiction is not always a truthfulness-character attack. B: B is wrong because the photo targets perception and accuracy, not general veracity. D: D is wrong because the form of the photo's admission does not transform the attack into a character-for-truthfulness attack.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(a) / contradiction not automatically truthfulness attack
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the difference between factual contradiction/perception impeachment and truthfulness-character impeachment.
