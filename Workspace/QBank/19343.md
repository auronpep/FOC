Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A manufacturer sold a metal press with an interlocked guard that stopped the press when the guard was open. The guard and interlock worked properly when sold. A factory later disabled the interlock and welded the guard open to increase production speed. An operator's hand was crushed when the press cycled while the guard was open. The operator sues the manufacturer under strict products liability, alleging that the press was defective because it operated without an active guard at the time of injury. Is the operator likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim against the manufacturer despite the post-sale modification?
Answer A: Yes, because the press was dangerous when it injured the operator
Answer B: No, because the factory's substantial post-sale modification created the guard danger that caused the injury
Answer C: No, because any post-sale change to a product automatically bars all products claims
Answer D: Yes, because manufacturers must design products that cannot be modified by later users
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The press left the manufacturer with a working interlocked guard. The later disabling and welding open of that guard was a substantial causal modification that defeats this strict products claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Product-defect analysis must track the sale-to-injury timeline. The factory's substantial post-sale disabling of a working guard created the danger. C: Not every change is a complete defense. The change must be substantial and causally tied to the injury, and foreseeable changes may be folded into design analysis. D: Manufacturers are not insurers against every later bypass or alteration. The facts show an extreme disabling of a functioning safety system after sale.
lb_rule_reference: A substantial post-sale modification that creates the injury-causing danger can defeat products liability when the product was not defective in that respect when sold.
