Question: Peter granted a utility easement “over the route shown on the attached survey.” The attached survey marked a six-foot corridor along the east edge of Peter's field. Years later, the utility company installed new lines through the center of the field because that route was cheaper. Peter objected. Which is the best result?
Answer A: The company may use the center route because a utility easement gives the holder discretion to choose the cheapest route.
Answer B: The company may use the center route because the survey is relevant only to notice, not scope.
Answer C: Peter must accept the center route unless he proves that the east-edge route is strictly necessary.
Answer D: Peter should prevail because the attached survey fixed the easement route along the east edge.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: When the grant incorporates a survey or map, that document can define the easement's location and scope. The easement was over the surveyed six-foot east-edge corridor, so installation through the center of the field exceeds the grant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because utility convenience does not override the surveyed route. B: B is wrong because the survey defines scope, not merely notice. C: C is wrong because strict necessity is not the test for an express surveyed route. D is correct because the incorporated survey fixes location.
