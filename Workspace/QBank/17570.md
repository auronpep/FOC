Question: A coffee shop in a quiet area had never had violent incidents. One afternoon, a stranger suddenly ran in from the street and punched a customer without warning. The shop had no security guard. The injured customer sues the shop for negligent failure to protect against criminal acts. Is the shop likely liable for failing to prevent the attack?
Answer A: Yes, because businesses are insurers of customer safety
Answer B: Yes, because any criminal act on business premises is foreseeable
Answer C: No, because the facts show no prior similar incidents or other reason to foresee the sudden attack
Answer D: No, because businesses owe no duty of reasonable care to invitees
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The attack was sudden and random, with no supplied history or warning facts. The shop is not an insurer against every third-party crime.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Businesses owe reasonable care, not insurance against unforeseeable random crimes. B: Foreseeability usually depends on specific warning facts, prior similar incidents, location risk, or other notice. D: Businesses do owe invitees reasonable care. The claim fails because the crime was not reasonably foreseeable on these facts.
lb_rule_reference: A duty to protect against third-party crime generally requires special relationship plus foreseeable risk, such as prior similar incidents or specific warning facts; random unforeseeable crime may fall outside the duty.
