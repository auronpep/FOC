Outline: Nuisance
Question: Two small factories discharged smoke that combined over a residential block. Experts could not divide which factory caused which portion of the residents' lost use of their yards, but the jury found Factory A 60 percent responsible and Factory B 40 percent responsible. The jurisdiction applies joint and several liability for indivisible tort harms. The residents collected the full judgment from Factory A. Factory A then sought payment from Factory B. What claim, if any, may Factory A bring against Factory B after paying the nuisance judgment?
Answer A: Contribution for Factory B's equitable share, because Factory A paid more than its share of an indivisible harm
Answer B: Indemnity for the entire amount, because Factory B also contributed to the nuisance
Answer C: No claim, because joint and several liability makes Factory A solely responsible once plaintiffs choose to collect from it
Answer D: A claim to make the residents return 40 percent of the judgment before Factory B pays anything
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Joint and several liability allows the residents to collect the full judgment once from Factory A. Because Factory A paid more than its 60 percent share, it may seek contribution from Factory B for B's equitable responsibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Indemnity shifts the entire loss only in special relationships or active/passive fault settings. Joint wrongdoers generally use contribution for equitable shares. C: Joint and several liability governs plaintiff recovery; contribution governs loss sharing among defendants after payment. D: The residents received one full satisfaction, not a double recovery. The adjustment is between defendants through contribution, not by refund from plaintiffs.
lb_rule_reference: When multiple tortfeasors contribute to one indivisible harm, a plaintiff may recover the full allowable judgment once, and a defendant who pays more than its equitable share may seek contribution.
