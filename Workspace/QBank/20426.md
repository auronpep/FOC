Question: A warrant authorized police to search "the single-family residence at 14 Oak Street" for stolen laptops. The warrant did not mention any outbuilding. Officers searched a locked detached shed behind the house and found the laptops there. The shed was separate from the residence and not listed in the warrant. What is the best suppression argument?
Answer A: The laptops should be suppressed because officers searched a place outside the warrant's particularized scope
Answer B: The laptops are admissible because any valid warrant for a home includes all nearby structures
Answer C: The laptops are admissible because officers subjectively believed the shed was included
Answer D: The laptops should be suppressed only if the shed belonged to a different owner
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The warrant described the residence, not the detached shed. Searching a separate locked structure outside the warrant's scope exceeds the authorization.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Warrant scope depends on the description and authorized places. A separate detached shed is not automatically included. C: Scope is judged objectively by the warrant and facts. Subjective belief cannot expand a facially limited warrant. D: The defect is warrant scope, not separate ownership. The warrant did not authorize the shed search.
lb_rule_reference: Officers executing a warrant must stay within its particularized scope; evidence found in areas not authorized by the warrant may be suppressed.
