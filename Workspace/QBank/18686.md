Question: A state benefit statute gives automatic caregiver stipends to mothers of disabled children. Fathers may receive the same stipend only if they prove that the mother is unavailable. The state defends the law by saying mothers are usually the primary caregivers. What level of scrutiny applies, and what is the likely problem?
Answer A: Rational basis applies because family benefits are social welfare
Answer B: Intermediate scrutiny applies, and reliance on a caregiving stereotype is not an exceedingly persuasive justification
Answer C: Strict scrutiny applies because sex is a suspect classification equal to race
Answer D: The statute is valid automatically because most caregivers are mothers
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Sex classifications require more than broad stereotypes about typical family roles.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The statute expressly classifies by sex, triggering intermediate scrutiny. C: The standard is intermediate scrutiny, not strict scrutiny. D: Intermediate scrutiny rejects overbroad sex stereotypes as a substitute for a tailored justification.
lb_rule_reference: Sex classifications receive intermediate scrutiny and must be supported by an important governmental objective and an exceedingly persuasive, substantially related justification; stereotypes are not enough.
