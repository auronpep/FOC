Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: In a federal civil action, Rachel must prove that David received a notice cancelling a delivery contract. Rachel introduces evidence that the notice was placed in an envelope addressed to David, properly stamped, and deposited in the mail. David then testifies that he never received the notice. What effect should David's testimony have?
Answer A: The jury must find that David received the notice because Rachel established the mailing presumption.
Answer B: The jury may find that David received the notice, but David's testimony satisfies his burden of production and the burden of persuasion remains with Rachel.
Answer C: David now has the burden of persuading the jury that he did not receive the notice.
Answer D: The court must find that David did not receive the notice because his denial is direct evidence and Rachel's mailing proof is only circumstantial.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A federal civil presumption generally shifts only the burden of production. Once David produces evidence of nonreceipt, the presumption no longer requires a finding for Rachel, but Rachel still bears the burden of persuasion. The jury may credit either side's proof.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the presumption does not make receipt conclusive after rebuttal. C: C is wrong because the burden of persuasion does not shift to David under the ordinary federal civil rule. D: D is wrong because direct denial does not automatically defeat circumstantial proof of mailing; the jury decides weight.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 301
