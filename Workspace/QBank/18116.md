Question: In a federal assault trial, a witness named Peter testifies that he saw the attacker from across a dim parking lot. The defendant offers evidence that Peter failed an eyesight test one month later. The prosecutor objects that the test occurred after the assault and does not prove Peter's eyesight on the night in question. How should the court rule on relevance?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence because only an eyesight test from the same night can be relevant.
Answer B: Admit the evidence only if Peter admits his eyesight was unchanged.
Answer C: Admit the evidence because a failed eyesight test one month later has some tendency to make Peter's ability to perceive less reliable, subject to weight and other limits.
Answer D: Exclude the evidence because credibility evidence is never relevant to identity.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A later eyesight test can have some tendency to bear on the witness's ability to perceive, especially when the observation conditions were difficult. Differences in time affect weight and possibly balancing, not threshold relevance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A demands same-night proof. B wrongly requires the witness's admission. D is wrong because perception and credibility can affect identity proof. B: A demands same-night proof. B wrongly requires the witness's admission. D is wrong because perception and credibility can affect identity proof. D: D is wrong because perception and credibility can affect identity proof.
