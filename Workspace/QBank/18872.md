Question: A state statute lets a private creditor freeze a debtor's bank account before judgment by filing a short affidavit with a clerk. No judge reviews the affidavit, no bond is required, and the debtor receives a hearing only months later. What is the strongest due process objection?
Answer A: A creditor's allegation is enough because property disputes are private
Answer B: The procedure lacks safeguards before a significant property deprivation
Answer C: No process is required because money is fungible
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies because creditors are a suspect class
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Freezing funds before judgment with no judge, bond, prompt hearing, or meaningful showing creates a high risk of erroneous deprivation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: State attachment machinery seizes property, triggering due-process safeguards. C: Freezing a bank account deprives use of property and can require process. D: The issue is procedural safeguards for property seizure, not suspect-class review.
lb_rule_reference: Seizing property before final judgment usually requires safeguards such as judicial involvement, notice or prompt hearing, and a showing of need, unless exigent circumstances justify delay.
