Question: Joseph granted Mary a written easement over his driveway “for ingress and egress to one single-family residence on Mary's lot.” At the time of the grant, Mary's lot contained one house. Mary later leased the lot to a delivery business that used the driveway all day for commercial trucks serving a warehouse on the lot. Joseph sues to stop the commercial truck use. What is Mary's best answer?
Answer A: Mary may use the driveway for any purpose because every express easement gives unlimited access over the servient land.
Answer B: Mary's commercial truck use may be stopped because the grant limited the easement to ingress and egress for one single-family residence.
Answer C: Mary owns the driveway in fee because the easement was written.
Answer D: Joseph's only remedy is to terminate the easement completely, even if some residential access remains proper.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The scope of an express easement is controlled by the grant language and the reasonable expectations created by that language. This grant is limited to access for one single-family residence. Heavy commercial truck use serving a warehouse exceeds that purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an easement does not give unlimited use of the servient land. C: C is wrong because a written easement remains a use right, not fee title. D: D is wrong because misuse may be enjoined or limited without automatically terminating the entire easement.
lb_rule_reference: Express scope / purpose-limited grant
