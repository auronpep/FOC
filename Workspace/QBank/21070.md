Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Bathsheba sues a chair manufacturer after a folding chair collapsed during a banquet. To prove the chair model was defective, Bathsheba offers three complaints about wooden rocking chairs made by the same company ten years earlier. The folding chair in the lawsuit is metal and uses a different hinge design. The manufacturer objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the complaints because any prior complaint against the same manufacturer is relevant.
Answer B: Admit the complaints only for impeachment of the manufacturer's engineer.
Answer C: Exclude the complaints because the different product and design give them little or no probative connection to the disputed defect.
Answer D: Exclude the complaints only if the manufacturer proves they are false.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Prior incidents can be probative when sufficiently similar to the disputed product, condition, and theory. Complaints about a different product, material, design, and time period do not meaningfully bear on whether this metal folding chair was defective.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a same-manufacturer link alone is not enough. B: B is wrong because the complaints are offered substantively on defect, not as impeachment of a particular witness. D: D is wrong because the proponent must establish probative connection; the opponent need not prove the prior complaints false.
