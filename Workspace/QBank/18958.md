Question: A plaintiff sues a defendant in federal court for a federal wage claim. In the same complaint, the plaintiff also asserts an unrelated state-law defamation claim against the same defendant based on a separate neighborhood dispute. Under the MBE baseline, what is the best Rule 18 analysis?
Answer A: The plaintiff may join the unrelated defamation claim under Rule 18, but the court must still have subject matter jurisdiction over it
Answer B: The plaintiff may not join the defamation claim because Rule 18 requires all joined claims to arise from the same transaction
Answer C: The plaintiff may join the claim, and Rule 18 automatically creates federal jurisdiction over it
Answer D: The plaintiff may join the claim only if the defendant consents in the answer
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 18 is broad as to claims against an opposing party. It does not require transactional relatedness. But a joinder rule is not a jurisdictional grant, so the defamation claim must rest on independent jurisdiction or supplemental jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Rule 18 does not require same transaction or occurrence for claims against an opposing party. C: Joinder rules do not create subject matter jurisdiction. D: Rule 18 does not require defendant consent for claim joinder in the complaint.
lb_rule_reference: A party asserting a claim may join as many claims as it has against an opposing party under Rule 18(a), related or unrelated, but each claim still needs an independent jurisdictional basis or supplemental jurisdiction.
