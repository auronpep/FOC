Question: During federal discovery, a company accidentally produces one privileged email from its general counsel. Before production, the company used a reasonable privilege-review protocol. Two days after discovering the mistake, it notifies the opponent and requests return of the email under the applicable discovery procedure. The opponent argues that any production waives privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Find waiver because any disclosure to an opponent destroys privilege.
Answer B: Find no waiver if the disclosure was inadvertent, reasonable steps were taken to prevent disclosure, and prompt reasonable steps were taken to rectify the error.
Answer C: Find waiver unless the opponent actually read the email aloud in court.
Answer D: Find no waiver only if the email contained work product rather than attorney-client advice.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 502(b) provides that inadvertent disclosure in a federal proceeding does not operate as a waiver if the holder took reasonable steps to prevent disclosure and promptly took reasonable steps to rectify the error. The decisive axis is inadvertence plus prevention and rectification steps.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is automatic-waiver overbreadth. Rule 502(b) rejects automatic waiver for qualifying inadvertent disclosures. C: A is wrong because This is automatic-waiver overbreadth. Rule 502(b) rejects automatic waiver for qualifying inadvertent disclosures. B is wrong because Correct answer C is wrong because Waiver analysis does not depend on whether the email has been read aloud in court. D is wrong because Rule 502 covers both attorney-client privilege and work-product protection. D: D is wrong because Rule 502 covers both attorney-client privilege and work-product protection.
