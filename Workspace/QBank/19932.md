Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff sues Defendant in federal court in State A. Defendant timely impleads Indemnitor under Rule 14, alleging that Indemnitor must reimburse Defendant if Defendant is liable. Indemnitor lives in State B, 70 miles from the federal courthouse in State A, and is personally served at home. State A's long-arm statute would not otherwise reach Indemnitor. What is the best personal-jurisdiction analysis for Indemnitor?
Answer A: Jurisdiction may be supported by Rule 4(k)(1)(B) because Indemnitor was joined under Rule 14 and served within 100 miles
Answer B: Jurisdiction fails automatically because State A's long-arm statute does not reach Indemnitor
Answer C: Jurisdiction exists because all third-party defendants are subject to nationwide personal jurisdiction
Answer D: Jurisdiction exists only if Plaintiff has a direct claim against Indemnitor
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The 100-mile bulge rule applies to parties joined under Rule 14 or Rule 19 when served within the required distance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Rule 4(k)(1)(B) is an independent rule-based hook for certain joined parties. C: Rule 4(k)(1)(B) is limited to service within 100 miles; it is not nationwide jurisdiction. D: Rule 4(k)(1)(B) turns on Rule 14 or Rule 19 joinder and service distance, not on whether Plaintiff asserts a direct claim.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 4(k)(1)(B) can establish personal jurisdiction over a party joined under Rule 14 or Rule 19 and served within 100 miles of where the summons issued.
