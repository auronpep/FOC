Question: Rebecca signed a deed granting Isaac a right to cross her land. She then placed the signed deed in her desk and told a friend, “I may give this to Isaac next month if I decide to.” Isaac never received the deed, Rebecca never recorded it, and Rebecca never otherwise communicated a present transfer of the easement to Isaac. Isaac later learned of the deed and claimed an express easement. What is the best result?
Answer A: Isaac has an express easement because Rebecca signed a deed that described the right.
Answer B: Isaac has no express easement because the deed was not delivered with present intent to transfer the easement.
Answer C: Isaac has an easement by necessity because Rebecca considered giving him access.
Answer D: Isaac has fee title to the crossing route because the signed deed was in existence.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A deed or easement grant generally must be delivered with present intent to transfer the interest. Rebecca retained the deed, expressed only possible future intent, and never delivered or otherwise made a present transfer to Isaac.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because signing alone is not enough without delivery or present transfer intent. C: C is wrong because potential future generosity does not create strict necessity. D: D is wrong because even a delivered easement deed would create a use right, not fee title to the route.
lb_rule_reference: Delivery requirement / undelivered easement deed
