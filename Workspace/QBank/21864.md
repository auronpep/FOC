Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: A plaintiff files a federal complaint alleging that a city denied her permit in January. In March, while the case is pending, the city imposes a new fine on the plaintiff based on the same permit dispute. The plaintiff wants to add the March fine to the action. What is the proper pleading route?
Answer A: No pleading route exists because post-filing events can never be added
Answer B: Move for leave to file a supplemental pleading under Rule 15(d)
Answer C: Amend as of course at any time because the fine relates to the same dispute
Answer D: Wait until trial and mention the fine without changing the pleadings
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The March fine happened after the original complaint. Rule 15(d), not ordinary relation-back amendment analysis, is the direct route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 15(d) exists specifically to add post-pleading events with court permission. C: Amendment as of course is time-limited, and Rule 15(d) requires a motion for supplemental post-pleading events. D: If the plaintiff wants the new event as part of the claim, the pleading should be supplemented with court permission.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 15(d) permits the court, on motion and reasonable notice, to allow a supplemental pleading setting out events that happened after the date of the pleading to be supplemented.
