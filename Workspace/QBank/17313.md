Question: Moses is charged with killing Aaron. Moses concedes before trial that Aaron died from a gunshot wound and that Aaron is the person named in the indictment. The prosecutor offers a large, graphic autopsy photograph solely to prove Aaron's identity and cause of death. Moses objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Admit the photograph, because the prosecution may always prove every element with any evidence it chooses.
Answer B: Admit the photograph, because a stipulation can never reduce the probative value of evidence.
Answer C: Exclude the photograph if its limited probative need is substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice.
Answer D: Exclude the photograph because all photographs of victims are inadmissible character evidence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The photograph has some relevance, but Moses's concessions sharply reduce the prosecutor's need for this graphic proof on identity and cause of death. The court may exclude it if unfair prejudice substantially outweighs the remaining probative value.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevant evidence is still subject to Rule 403. B: B is wrong because a stipulation can reduce the need for otherwise relevant proof. D: D is wrong because victim photographs are not categorically character evidence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403 / stipulated fact reduces probative need
