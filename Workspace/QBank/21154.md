Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Investigatory power
Question: A House committee investigating whether to revise federal ethics laws subpoenas limited financial records from the President's private accounting firm. The committee explains that the records will help it assess disclosure requirements and conflict-of-interest rules. The President argues that Congress has no power to subpoena private records connected to a sitting President. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The subpoena is automatically invalid because Congress can never seek records connected to a sitting President
Answer B: The subpoena is evaluated by asking whether it serves a valid legislative purpose and whether separation-of-powers concerns are properly balanced
Answer C: The subpoena is valid only if the committee is conducting a criminal prosecution
Answer D: The subpoena is invalid because private accounting firms are outside the federal government
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The committee has identified possible legislation, but the target involves presidential materials. Courts assess legislative purpose, need, scope, and burdens in light of separation-of-powers concerns.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Congress can investigate in aid of legislation, including by subpoena, subject to limits and balancing when presidential interests are involved. C: Congress's investigative power supports legislative purposes, not prosecution as such. Criminal prosecution is an executive function. D: A subpoena may seek information from private third parties if it is otherwise within Congress's investigative authority and properly tailored.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may issue subpoenas in aid of a valid legislative purpose, but subpoenas directed at the President or presidential materials require careful separation-of-powers balancing rather than ordinary law-enforcement reasoning.
