Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: BikeSpan, incorporated and headquartered in Oregon, regularly advertised its mountain bikes in New Mexico, used New Mexico dealers, and serviced warranty claims there. A New Mexico resident bought a BikeSpan mountain bike from a New Mexico dealer. The brakes allegedly failed during a ride in New Mexico. BikeSpan argues that specific jurisdiction is absent because the exact ad the plaintiff saw cannot be identified. How should the court analyze relatedness?
Answer A: Jurisdiction is absent unless the plaintiff proves the exact advertisement that caused the purchase
Answer B: Jurisdiction is absent because BikeSpan is not at home in New Mexico
Answer C: Jurisdiction is likely proper because the claim relates to BikeSpan's deliberate New Mexico market for the same product line
Answer D: Jurisdiction is proper solely because the plaintiff is a New Mexico resident
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: BikeSpan served the New Mexico market through ads, dealers, and warranty service, and the claim concerns a New Mexico purchase and injury involving that product line.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Specific jurisdiction can also cover claims that relate to deliberate forum market activity for the same product line. B: The issue is specific jurisdiction, not general jurisdiction. D: The decisive facts are BikeSpan's own New Mexico market contacts tied to the claim.
lb_rule_reference: Forum advertising and service of a product market can support relatedness when the plaintiff's claim concerns that product line and forum market activity.
