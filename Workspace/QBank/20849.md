Question: In a federal negligence trial, John testifies on direct that he saw Mary's truck enter the intersection. On cross-examination, Mary's lawyer asks John whether he has a pending lawsuit against Mary arising from the same accident. John objects that the question is beyond the scope of direct examination. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because cross-examination is limited to the exact facts described on direct.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because cross-examination may cover matters affecting the witness's credibility, including bias.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless John first testifies about the lawsuit on direct.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Mary offers a certified copy of the lawsuit before asking the question.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Cross-examination may cover the subject matter of direct examination and matters affecting credibility. A pending lawsuit against a party may show bias or interest, so it is proper credibility cross-examination.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A ignores the credibility component of cross-examination. C would improperly require the direct examiner to open the bias topic first.
