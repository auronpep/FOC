Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Isaac contracted to sell 100 generators to Miriam for $900 each. Miriam wrongfully refused to accept them. Isaac resold the generators in good faith and in a commercially reasonable private resale for $840 each after giving Miriam reasonable notice. Isaac incurred $1,500 in reasonable resale expenses and saved $500 in delivery costs because Miriam refused the goods. What may Isaac recover under the resale-damages measure?
Answer A: $7,000, the contract-resale differential plus incidental expenses less expenses saved.
Answer B: $6,000, the difference between the contract price and resale price only.
Answer C: $1,500, the resale expenses only.
Answer D: $90,000, the full contract price.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A seller who makes a good-faith, commercially reasonable resale may recover the contract price minus resale price, plus incidental damages, less expenses saved. The differential is $6,000 (($900 - $840) × 100), plus $1,500 incidental resale expenses, less $500 saved delivery costs, for $7,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it omits incidental damages and the saved-expense offset. C: C is wrong because the seller also recovers the contract-resale differential. D: D is wrong because the action for the price is limited; a proper resale calls for the resale-damages formula, not the full price.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-706; UCC §2-710
