Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A manufacturer sold a household adhesive in 2019. At that time, reliable scientific testing had not identified any risk of a rare nerve condition. In 2025, new research first revealed the risk. A user injured in 2019 claims the 2019 label was defective for not warning of the condition. What is the manufacturer's strongest warning defense?
Answer A: The risk was not known or reasonably knowable when the product was sold
Answer B: The manufacturer is liable because every later-discovered risk must have been warned about earlier
Answer C: The manufacturer is liable because adhesives are never useful enough to justify risk
Answer D: The manufacturer wins only if the user was negligent
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Warning duties generally concern risks known or reasonably knowable at the relevant time. Hindsight discovery alone does not prove the earlier warning was defective.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Later discovery does not show the risk was reasonably knowable at sale. C: The facts test warning knowability, not a supplied design-defect alternative. D: The defense here is risk unknowability, independent of user negligence.
lb_rule_reference: Warning liability generally depends on risks known or reasonably knowable at the relevant time, not risks undiscoverable by then-existing science.
