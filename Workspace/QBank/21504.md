Outline: Larceny
Question: A landowner cut several walnut logs and stacked them beside a barn to sell the next day. That night, Blake loaded the already-cut logs onto his truck and drove away intending to sell them as his own. Can Blake be guilty of larceny of the logs?
Answer A: Yes, because the logs had already been severed from the land and were personal property when Blake took them
Answer B: No, because timber can never be personal property for larceny purposes
Answer C: No, because Blake drove only a short distance before police stopped him
Answer D: Yes, but only if Blake personally cut the logs from the land
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Once the owner cut and stacked the logs, they were personal property. Blake trespassorily took and carried them away with intent to steal.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The logs were no longer standing timber; they had been severed and stacked before Blake took them. C: Any movement of the property can satisfy asportation. D: The important point is that the logs were personal property when he took them. He need not have severed them himself.
lb_rule_reference: Timber, crops, or fixtures already severed from realty can be the subject of larceny when another takes and carries them away with intent to steal.
