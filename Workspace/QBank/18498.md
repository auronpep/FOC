Question: Peter is charged with knowingly transporting stolen grain. The evidence shows that Peter drove the wagon containing the grain, gave a false name to the guard, and fled when officers approached. The prosecutor asks the court to instruct: “You may, but are not required to, consider Peter's false name and flight as evidence of consciousness of guilt.” Peter objects that the instruction comments on the evidence. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Refuse the instruction because flight evidence is collateral to guilt.
Answer B: Refuse the instruction because any inference from conduct shifts the burden to Peter.
Answer C: Give the instruction if the evidence supports it, because it is permissive and leaves guilt for the jury.
Answer D: Give the instruction because Peter's conduct conclusively proves knowledge.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A court may give a permissive inference instruction when the evidence supports the inference and the jury remains free to reject it. The instruction does not shift the burden or decide the element.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because conduct showing consciousness of guilt can be relevant. B: B is wrong because a permissive inference is not a burden shift. D: D is wrong because the jury, not the judge, decides whether the conduct proves knowledge.
