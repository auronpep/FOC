Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Isaac owned an established inn. Rebekah breached a contract to repair the inn's water system, causing six guest rooms to close for one week. Isaac proves average occupancy, room rates, variable cleaning costs, and cancellations during the closure. Rebekah argues that Isaac cannot recover lost profits because no witness can calculate the exact profit from each lost guest. Which statement is correct?
Answer A: Isaac may recover nothing because exact mathematical precision is required.
Answer B: Isaac may recover lost profits if the evidence permits a reasonably certain net-profit calculation.
Answer C: Isaac may recover gross room revenue without subtracting saved costs.
Answer D: Isaac may recover only the contract price Rebekah would have been paid for repairs.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Lost profits must be proven with reasonable certainty, not exact mathematical precision. Occupancy, rates, variable costs, and cancellations may provide a sufficient basis for net-profit calculation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because exact precision is not required. C: C is wrong because lost profits are net of costs saved. D: D is wrong because the repair price is not Isaac's lost-profit measure.
lb_rule_reference: Lost profits / reasonable certainty not mathematical precision
