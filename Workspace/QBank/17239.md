Question: Sarah is charged in federal court with knowingly possessing stolen spices. The prosecutor asks the judge to instruct the jury: “If you find that Sarah had the spices within one hour after the theft, you must presume she knew they were stolen unless she proves she bought them honestly.” Sarah objects. Which ruling is proper?
Answer A: Give the instruction because the best evidence of Sarah's knowledge is her possession of the spices.
Answer B: Refuse the instruction because it converts a possible inference into a mandatory presumption on an element.
Answer C: Give the instruction because any objection to the inference is an overbroad objection slogan.
Answer D: Refuse the instruction only because federal and California evidence rules differ on possession inferences.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The instruction would require the jury to presume knowledge unless Sarah proves an innocent explanation. That improperly shifts the burden on an element of the crime. A permissive inference may be possible, but this instruction is mandatory.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because possession may be probative but does not compel knowledge. C: C is wrong because the objection targets a real burden-shift problem. D: D is wrong because the federal criminal burden issue controls; a California comparison is not the operative basis.
lb_rule_reference: Mandatory presumption / criminal element
