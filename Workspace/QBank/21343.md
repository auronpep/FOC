Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Jonah spent $2,500 traveling to meet with a landowner about a possible lease. After that trip, the landowner, Rachel, promised in a signed writing to lease the land to Jonah. In reliance on the signed promise, Jonah then spent $7,000 preparing the land for planting. Rachel repudiated before the lease term began, and Jonah cannot prove expected crop profits with reasonable certainty. What reliance amount is most appropriate?
Answer A: $9,500, because every expense related to the transaction is reliance.
Answer B: $2,500, because only negotiation expenses are recoverable.
Answer C: Nothing, because expected crop profits are speculative.
Answer D: $7,000, because the recoverable reliance was incurred after and because of Rachel's promise.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages require causation and timing. Expenses incurred before the promise normally were not made in reliance on that promise. Jonah's $7,000 preparation cost was incurred after and because of the lease promise, while the earlier $2,500 meeting cost was not.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it includes expenses incurred before the promise. B: B is wrong because negotiation expenses are not the relevant reliance here. C: C is wrong because speculative expectation does not defeat provable reliance expenditures.
