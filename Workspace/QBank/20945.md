Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: In a jurisdiction that requires an overt act for conspiracy, Asha and Nolan agree to steal medical equipment from a clinic. The next day, Nolan buys a pair of bolt cutters to use on the clinic's storage cage. Police arrest both before they go to the clinic. Has the overt-act requirement likely been satisfied?
Answer A: No, because buying bolt cutters is lawful by itself
Answer B: Yes, because buying the tool for the planned theft is an act in furtherance of the agreement
Answer C: No, because the overt act must itself be a substantial step for attempt
Answer D: Yes, but only if Asha personally bought the bolt cutters
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Nolan's purchase was done to advance the agreed theft. In a jurisdiction requiring an overt act, that slight step is enough.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: An overt act in furtherance of a conspiracy need not be independently criminal. A slight act can satisfy the requirement. C: Conspiracy's overt-act requirement is less demanding than attempt's act requirement. D: An overt act by any conspirator in furtherance of the agreement can satisfy the requirement for the conspiracy.
lb_rule_reference: If a jurisdiction requires an overt act for conspiracy, the act may be slight and need not itself be criminal, as long as it is done in furtherance of the agreement.
