Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A hospital advertised its emergency department as staffed by "our emergency physicians." A patient entered through the emergency room, had no reason to select a particular doctor, and relied on the hospital to provide treatment. The treating physician was technically an independent contractor and negligently treated the patient. May the hospital face vicarious liability despite the contractor label?
Answer A: Yes, under apparent agency if the hospital held the physician out and the patient reasonably relied
Answer B: Yes, because hospitals are strictly liable for every medical injury
Answer C: No, because an independent contractor label always defeats vicarious liability
Answer D: No, because apparent agency applies only to written contracts
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The contractor label does not necessarily control the patient's apparent-agency claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The theory depends on agency facts, not strict liability. C: The source rule recognizes exceptions. D: Apparent agency can arise from representations and reasonable reliance.
lb_rule_reference: A principal may be liable for an independent contractor's tort under apparent agency when the principal holds the actor out as its agent and the plaintiff reasonably relies.
