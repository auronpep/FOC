Outline: Strict scrutiny (suspect) classifications
Question: A school district adopts a voluntary integration plan to reduce racial segregation in its elementary schools. Under the plan, when a school is oversubscribed, the district uses race as a tiebreaker in assignment decisions — giving priority to applicants whose enrollment would help the school achieve the district's overall racial composition goal of 30-45% minority students per school. Students whose enrollment would move the school's racial composition outside that range are assigned to another school. A parent of a student denied enrollment at her first-choice school based solely on the race tiebreaker challenges the plan under the Equal Protection Clause. Under Equal Protection strict scrutiny, which of the following correctly evaluates the school district's race-based assignment tiebreaker?
Answer A: The plan is constitutional because school diversity is a compelling interest and the tiebreaker uses race only as one factor, which satisfies narrow tailoring under the higher education diversity model
Answer B: The plan is subject to rational basis because the school district is pursuing remediation of segregated conditions, which is a traditional government function
Answer C: The plan fails strict scrutiny because using race as an individual assignment tiebreaker to achieve general racial balance is not a compelling interest in the K-12 context, and the district did not establish that race-neutral alternatives were considered and found inadequate
Answer D: The plan is constitutional because the district's remedial goal of correcting historical segregation constitutes a compelling interest, and the modest race-based tiebreaker — used only as a secondary factor — is the least restrictive means available
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Parents Involved held that individual race-based student assignment for the purpose of achieving racial balance violates the Equal Protection Clause. General racial balance is not a compelling interest in K-12 schools. Even assuming the interest were compelling, the individual race-based tiebreaker is not narrowly tailored when the district has not exhausted race-neutral alternatives (controlled choice, magnet programs, attendance zone redesign). The plan fails strict scrutiny.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Supreme Court rejected this reasoning in Parents Involved in Community Schools v. Seattle. The Court held that using race as a tiebreaker in individual K-12 student assignments does not satisfy strict scrutiny. The interest in racial balance or diversity in K-12 schools is not the same compelling interest recognized in higher education, and individual race-based assignment does not satisfy narrow tailoring. Grutter's higher education framework does not transfer to K-12 assignment. B: Any use of race in government action is a suspect classification triggering strict scrutiny, regardless of whether the purpose is remediation, desegregation, or diversity. Rational basis does not apply when race is the operative classification. The school district's benign purpose does not lower the standard of review. D: The school district in this fact pattern did not identify remediation of its own specific prior segregation — it adopted a voluntary plan for racial balance. Voluntary balance is not the same as court-ordered remediation of identified prior segregation. And a tiebreaker that mechanically assigns students based on race is not narrowly tailored when alternatives were not considered.
lb_rule_reference: A school district's voluntary race-conscious student assignment plan that uses race as a factor in individual assignment decisions is subject to strict scrutiny; general racial balance or diversity does not constitute a compelling interest sufficient to justify individual race-based assignments.
