Question: Jacob writes to a bank, “If you lend Benjamin up to $20,000 this month, I guarantee repayment.” The bank receives the letter and lends Benjamin $20,000 the next day. Jacob dies two days later. Benjamin later defaults. Assuming no writing-defense issue, what is the best formation analysis?
Answer A: No guaranty formed because Jacob died before the bank notified him of the loan.
Answer B: A unilateral guaranty contract formed when the bank extended the requested credit before Jacob's death.
Answer C: No guaranty formed because unilateral guaranties always require a return promise by the lender.
Answer D: No guaranty formed because all guaranties are gratuitous promises.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Jacob invited acceptance by performance—lending money to Benjamin. The bank performed before Jacob died, so the unilateral guaranty contract was formed when the loan was made.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because death after performance acceptance does not undo the formed contract. C: C is wrong because the letter invited performance, not a return promise. D: D is wrong because guaranties can be supported by the requested extension of credit.
lb_rule_reference: surety/common_law
lb_student_diagnostic: Student missed that performance can accept a unilateral guaranty before death.
