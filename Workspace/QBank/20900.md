MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff, a citizen of Indiana, files a state-court action seeking $200,000 in damages arising from a defective product. The plaintiff names as defendants: (1) the product manufacturer, a citizen of Delaware (incorporated) and Illinois (nerve center); and (2) a local retail store in Indiana, a citizen of Indiana. The local retailer has no involvement in the product's design or manufacture. Unrebutted evidence shows that the plaintiff has no colorable product liability claim against the retailer under the applicable state law — the retailer stocked and sold the product in sealed packaging with no opportunity to inspect it, and state law expressly excludes retailers from liability in such circumstances. The manufacturer seeks to remove the action, arguing that the retailer is fraudulently joined and its Indiana citizenship should be disregarded. The governing forum is initially Indiana state court; the issue is whether removal is proper under the MBE baseline. Should the court permit removal?
Answer A: No, because the retailer is a named defendant and its Indiana citizenship defeats both complete diversity and the forum-defendant rule, barring removal regardless of the merits of the claims against it
Answer B: Yes, because the retailer has no colorable claim against it under state law, making it a fraudulently joined party whose Indiana citizenship is disregarded, leaving complete diversity between the Indiana plaintiff and the Delaware/Illinois manufacturer
Answer C: No, because removal based on fraudulent joinder requires the defendant to prove actual fraud — intentional deception — by the plaintiff in naming the retailer, and that is not established here
Answer D: Yes, but only because Indiana courts would also dismiss the claim against the retailer at the demurrer stage, meaning the retailer is a nominal party by operation of law in any forum
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The fraudulent joinder doctrine allows courts to disregard the citizenship of a defendant against whom the plaintiff has no reasonable basis for a claim under applicable state law. Here, the unrebutted evidence shows that the retailer is statutorily immunized under state law from liability in this scenario. The retailer's joinder is improper — it has no real stake in the litigation. Its Indiana citizenship is disregarded. With only the manufacturer as an actual defendant (Delaware and Illinois), and the plaintiff as a citizen of Indiana, complete diversity exists. The forum-defendant rule does not apply because the actual defendant is not an Indiana citizen. Removal is proper.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Courts disregard the citizenship of a fraudulently or improperly joined party — one against whom the plaintiff has no colorable cause of action under the applicable law. Here, state law expressly immunizes the retailer in this scenario, and there is no colorable claim against it. The retailer's joinder is improper; its citizenship is disregarded for diversity and removal purposes, leaving only the manufacturer as an actual defendant. C: The "fraudulent joinder" doctrine does not require proof of actual intentional fraud. The standard is whether the plaintiff has any reasonable possibility of recovery against the joined defendant under applicable state law. If no colorable claim exists under state law — here, because of statutory immunity — the joinder is improper (sometimes called "fraudulent") regardless of the plaintiff's subjective intent. D: The correct analysis is the fraudulent joinder doctrine applied at the time of removal — asking whether the plaintiff has any colorable claim under applicable state law. The fact that an Indiana state court might also dismiss the claim is consistent with, but not the source of, the federal fraudulent-joinder conclusion. The analysis looks at the applicable substantive law, not at what another court would do procedurally.
lb_rule_reference: For removal and diversity purposes, a court disregards the citizenship of a party who is merely nominal — joined only because of a formal legal requirement without a real stake in the litigation — or fraudulently joined to prevent removal.
