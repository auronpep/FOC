Question: Ruth sues Solomon after a crane collapse. Ruth calls Lydia to testify as an engineering expert. Solomon objects that Lydia is not qualified. Ruth states Lydia's education, license, and ten years of crane-inspection experience. The judge excludes Lydia without deciding whether she is qualified, saying, “The jury can decide whether her engineering background is good enough.” Was the court's approach proper?
Answer A: Yes, because expert qualification is always only a matter of jury weight.
Answer B: Yes, because Ruth was required to prove Lydia's qualifications with the original license certificate before the court could rule.
Answer C: No, because the judge must decide the preliminary question whether Lydia is qualified to testify as an expert.
Answer D: No, but only because crane-collapse cases require expert testimony as a matter of law.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Whether a witness is qualified to testify as an expert is a preliminary question for the court. If the witness is admitted, the jury may weigh the expert's credibility, but the judge cannot abdicate the threshold qualification ruling to the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the jury's weight function begins only after the court resolves threshold admissibility. B: B is wrong because the objection was not an original-writing issue; the court can decide qualification from testimony and other appropriate information. D: D is wrong because the point is the judge's qualification gate, not whether the subject always requires expert testimony.
