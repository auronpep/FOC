


BARMATRIX Q#: 22269
Outline_code: 32020301
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Probative value
Question: A woman crashed her motorcycle into a delivery truck. Just after the accident, the woman told a police officer at the scene that the truck had been speeding and changed lanes without looking to see whether anyone was driving next to it. The officer wrote the woman's statement into his official report, which she was required to complete for all accidents. The woman sued the truck driver for her injuries. At trial, the woman wanted to testify as to her statement to the officer. Is the statement admissible?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the statement is a prior consistent statement.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the statement is part of a public record.
Answer C: C: No, because the statement is inadmissible hearsay.
Answer D: D: No, because the statement can only be testified to by the officer.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Hearsay is an out- of- court statement being offered for the truth of the matter asserted. Here, the woman's statement is that the truck was speeding and turned into her lane without looking. Thus, the statement is hearsay. A is incorrect because a prior consistent statement can only be used if that statement falls under a hearsay exception. Unless the woman was being impeached by a showing of recent fabrication or improper influence, her statement could not be admissible as a prior consistent statement. Here, there is no indication that the woman is being impeached. B is incorrect because the statement is still hearsay even if the officer wrote it down in his official report. Writing it into the report would not transform the statement into non- hearsay. D is incorrect because the statement is inadmissible hearsay.
