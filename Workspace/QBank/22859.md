


BARMATRIX Q#: 22859
Outline_code: 95070303
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Class actions
Question: A federal prisoner brought a lawsuit against the United States Parole Commission in federal court challenging the validity of the United States Parole Commission's Parole Release Guidelines. In his suit, the prisoner asked the federal court to certify a class of all federal prisoners who were eligible or would be eligible for parole. The federal court denied class certification as neither necessary nor appropriate, and the prisoner appealed the denial of certification. While his appeal was pending, the prisoner was released. The United States Parole Commission moved to dismiss the prisoner's appeal, arguing that his appeal was now moot. Is the prisoner's appeal moot?
Answer A: A: No, because the prisoner's release did not moot the entire class action.
Answer B: B: No, because the appeal was filed before the prisoner was released.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the prisoner's release mooted the prisoner's substantive claim.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the prisoner is no longer an appropriate class representative.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Even if a class representative's substantial claim is mooted, the class action itself is not rendered moot. United States Parole Commission v. Geraghty, 445 U.S. 388 (1980). Consequently, even if the prisoner has been released, it does not mean the court must dismiss the prisoner's appeal regarding the denial of class certification. B is incorrect because the timing of the appeal would not change the analysis. C is incorrect because the class action suit can continue even if the prisoner's substantive claim is now moot. D is incorrect because the prisoner may still be an appropriate class representative even if his substantive claim is now moot.
