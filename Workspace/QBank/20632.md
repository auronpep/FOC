Outline: Offers of proof
Question: Before trial, Ruth moves to admit testimony from Luke that he saw Naomi sign a release. At the hearing, Ruth gives the court Luke's expected testimony in detail. The court states on the record, “My ruling is final. Luke's testimony is excluded.” At trial, Ruth does not call Luke or repeat the offer of proof. After judgment against Ruth, is the exclusion claim preserved?
Answer A: No, because Ruth had to call Luke at trial and ask the excluded questions again.
Answer B: No, because an offer of proof must always be repeated in front of the jury.
Answer C: Yes, because Ruth made the substance known and the court made a definitive ruling on the record, so renewal was unnecessary.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Naomi first introduced part of the same release under the rule of completeness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Once the court rules definitively on the record, a party need not renew an objection or offer of proof to preserve the claim. Ruth made the substance of Luke's testimony known and received a definitive exclusion ruling.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a definitive ruling eliminates the need for the formalism of repeating the offer at trial. B: B is wrong because offers of proof are not made in front of the jury; the court should avoid exposing the jury to excluded matter. D: D is wrong because completeness is not the preservation rule here.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(b) / definitive motion in limine ruling
