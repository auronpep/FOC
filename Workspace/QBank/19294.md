Question: In a federal criminal arson trial, Jonah testifies in his own defense. The court admits Jonah's prior felony conviction for drug trafficking under Rule 609(a)(1)(B) to impeach his credibility. Jonah timely requests an instruction limiting the jury's use of the conviction. What should the court do?
Answer A: Instruct the jury that it may use the conviction only to evaluate Jonah's credibility, not as evidence that he committed the charged arson.
Answer B: Instruct the jury that once the conviction is admitted, it may use the conviction for any purpose.
Answer C: Exclude the conviction because limiting instructions are never adequate when a criminal defendant testifies.
Answer D: Allow the conviction only if it proves Jonah's motive for the arson.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A conviction admitted under Rule 609 is admitted to attack credibility. When evidence is admitted for a limited purpose and a timely limiting instruction is requested, the court should restrict the evidence to its proper scope under Rule 105.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because impeachment evidence does not become propensity or merits evidence merely because it is admitted. C: C is wrong because Rule 609 contemplates admission of some defendant convictions with appropriate limits. D: D is wrong because the conviction was admitted for impeachment, not motive.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(a)(1)(B) and Rule 105 / limited use of defendant conviction
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests limited-use control and rejects substantive/propensity overread.
