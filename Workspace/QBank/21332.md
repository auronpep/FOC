Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A dog owner knew her dog became aggressive when strangers pulled its ears and warned a visitor not to tease the dog. The visitor laughed, grabbed both ears, and pulled hard. The dog immediately bit the visitor's hand. The visitor sues the owner under strict liability for a domestic animal with known dangerous propensity. Which issue most directly limits the visitor's strict-liability claim?
Answer A: The owner used reasonable care by warning the visitor, so strict liability automatically disappears
Answer B: The visitor's provocation or assumption of the known risk may limit or defeat recovery
Answer C: The dog becomes a wild animal because it reacted aggressively
Answer D: The owner is liable for every injury because strict liability has no defenses
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The visitor knowingly did the act that triggered the dog's known aggressive behavior after being warned. Plaintiff conduct is the key limiting issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Reasonable care alone is not the strict-liability defense. The stronger limiting issue is the visitor's provocation or knowing encounter with the risk. C: Aggressive behavior by a dog does not reclassify it as a wild animal. It remains a domestic-animal known-propensity problem. D: Strict liability still has scope limits and may account for plaintiff conduct such as provocation or assumption of risk.
lb_rule_reference: Plaintiff provocation or knowing encounter with a known animal danger can limit or defeat strict animal liability depending on the jurisdiction and facts.
