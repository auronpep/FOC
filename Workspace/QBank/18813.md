MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Battery
Question: In a dim parking garage, a pedestrian shouted and quickly raised a metal pipe while stepping toward a driver. The driver reasonably believed the pedestrian was about to strike him and shoved the pedestrian away. The pipe was actually part of a broken sign the pedestrian was moving out of the lane. The pedestrian sues the driver for battery. Can the driver defeat the pedestrian's battery claim by relying on self-defense?
Answer A: No, because the pedestrian did not actually intend to strike the driver
Answer B: Yes, because the driver reasonably believed imminent contact was threatened and used proportionate force
Answer C: No, because self-defense requires the driver to retreat before using any force
Answer D: Yes, because any insult permits a physical response
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The raised pipe and movement toward the driver supported a reasonable belief in imminent contact, and a shove was proportionate nondeadly force.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Self-defense can rest on a reasonable apparent threat. Actual danger is not required. C: The basic self-defense privilege does not impose an automatic retreat requirement before proportionate nondeadly force. D: Insults alone do not justify force. The privilege depends on reasonable belief of imminent contact and proportionate force.
lb_rule_reference: Self-defense permits reasonable proportionate force based on a reasonable belief in imminent harmful or offensive contact, even if the belief is mistaken.
