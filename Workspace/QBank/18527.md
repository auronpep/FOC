Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff sues a city official in federal court under a federal civil-rights statute, alleging a violation of the First Amendment. Plaintiff seeks only nominal damages and an injunction. The official moves to dismiss for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction because Plaintiff has not alleged more than $75,000 in damages. Should the court dismiss on that ground?
Answer A: Yes, because every federal civil case requires more than $75,000
Answer B: Yes, unless Plaintiff and the official are citizens of different states
Answer C: No, because the claim arises under federal law and Section 1331 has no amount-in-controversy requirement
Answer D: No, but only because Plaintiff seeks an injunction rather than damages
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal cause of action creates federal-question jurisdiction without a diversity amount threshold.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The amount threshold belongs to diversity jurisdiction under Section 1332, not federal-question jurisdiction under Section 1331. B: A federal civil-rights claim supplies federal-question jurisdiction regardless of diversity. D: The key point is federal-question jurisdiction. The absence of a diversity amount requirement does not depend on the remedy label.
lb_rule_reference: A federal constitutional or federal statutory cause of action can support Section 1331 jurisdiction without satisfying the diversity amount-in-controversy threshold.
