MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Trial judge’s discretion to control order and presentation of evidence
Question: In a federal fraud trial, Joseph calls Miriam to identify a photograph and describe what it shows. Before Miriam says when or where the photograph was taken, Joseph asks, “What does this photograph prove about the defendant's intent?” David objects and asks the judge to require foundation first. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Require Joseph to lay a foundation for the photograph before asking Miriam to draw conclusions from it.
Answer B: Allow the question because the order of foundation is always for the party calling the witness.
Answer C: Exclude the photograph permanently because Joseph asked one question before laying the foundation.
Answer D: Allow the jury to decide whether the foundation is sufficient without any ruling by the judge.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A trial judge may reasonably control the mode and order of examination. Requiring foundation before a witness describes the effect or meaning of a photograph is a proper courtroom-control ruling.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the calling party does not have absolute control over the order of foundation. C: C is wrong because asking prematurely does not require permanent exclusion if foundation can still be supplied. D: D is wrong because the judge controls the orderly presentation and foundation sequence before the jury uses the proof.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / foundation before details
