Question: Jacob agreed to buy Sarah's warehouse if he obtained a bank loan by August 1. The contract gave Jacob the right to cancel if financing was not obtained. Jacob never applied for a loan because he found another warehouse he liked better. On August 2, he told Sarah that the financing condition had failed and he would not buy. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Jacob may cancel because a financing condition gives the buyer an unrestricted right to avoid the contract.
Answer B: Jacob may cancel unless Sarah proves that some bank would have approved him.
Answer C: Jacob may cancel because condition nonoccurrence always excuses the buyer without inquiry into conduct.
Answer D: Jacob may not invoke the financing condition if his own failure to make reasonable good-faith efforts caused its nonoccurrence.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A party protected by a financing condition generally must make reasonable good-faith efforts to satisfy it. Jacob made no application and instead used the condition as an escape because he preferred another property. He cannot rely on nonoccurrence caused by his own failure of good-faith efforts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a financing condition is not an unrestricted cancellation right. B: B is wrong because Sarah need not prove approval with certainty to show Jacob failed to try. C: C is wrong because nonoccurrence caused by the protected party's bad-faith inaction may be excused.
lb_rule_reference: Condition precedent / implied good-faith efforts
