Question: Congress authorizes an environmental agency to set emission limits for a class of industrial plants at levels "reasonably necessary to protect public health while considering technical feasibility and compliance costs." A plant owner argues that the statute unconstitutionally delegates legislative power because the agency must choose the exact numerical limits. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The delegation is likely valid because Congress supplied standards to guide the agency's discretion
Answer B: The delegation is invalid because agencies may never set binding numerical standards
Answer C: The delegation is invalid unless Congress votes again on each emission limit
Answer D: The delegation is valid only because the agency regulates private industry rather than states
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The statute directs the agency to protect public health while considering feasibility and costs. Those standards provide an intelligible principle for agency implementation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Agencies often fill in technical details under statutory standards. Binding rules are not unconstitutional merely because they include numbers. C: Congress need not reenact every agency rule when it has validly delegated implementation authority. D: The nondelegation question turns on statutory guidance for agency discretion, not on whether the regulated party is private.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may delegate authority to agencies if it supplies an intelligible principle to guide the exercise of delegated power; broad delegations are not automatically unconstitutional.
