Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A student bought a laptop from an electronics retailer. A defect in the laptop's internal fan caused the laptop to overheat and stop working after two weeks. The overheating did not injure anyone and did not damage any other property. The student seeks the laptop's replacement cost and lost time spent recovering files. The student sues the retailer under strict products liability. Is the student likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim against the retailer?
Answer A: Yes, because the retailer is a commercial seller and the laptop was defective
Answer B: No, because the claimed loss is only the failed product and related economic loss, not personal injury or other property damage
Answer C: No, because a retailer can never be liable when the defect originated with the manufacturer
Answer D: Yes, because privity exists between the student and the retailer
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The defect caused the laptop itself to fail and caused economic losses, but no personal injury or other property damage. Strict products liability is clipped at the tort-damages element.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Strict products liability also requires tort-type harm, usually personal injury or damage to other property. Loss of the product itself and disappointed bargain are generally contract or warranty matters. C: Retailers can be strict products defendants when the other elements are met. This claim fails because the harm is economic loss only, not because the defendant is a retailer. D: Privity does not establish strict products liability. It may support warranty theories, but the strict tort claim still lacks personal injury or other property damage.
lb_rule_reference: Strict products liability generally requires personal injury or property damage; disappointed bargain or damage only to the defective product usually belongs in warranty or contract.
