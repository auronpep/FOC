Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Jacob owned a two-acre parcel. He signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey the parcel to Leah and Rachel as tenants in common, one-half each.” Leah was alive and accepted the deed. Rachel had died before the deed was delivered. The deed did not say that Leah should take Rachel's share if Rachel could not take. What is the result?
Answer A: Leah takes an undivided one-half interest, and Jacob retains the one-half interest that was attempted for Rachel.
Answer B: The entire deed is void because one named grantee was dead.
Answer C: Leah takes the entire parcel because she was the only living grantee.
Answer D: Rachel's devisee takes Rachel's one-half interest through Rachel's estate.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantee capacity for each stated share. Leah was a living, ascertainable grantee and can take the one-half interest conveyed to her. Rachel was dead at delivery and took nothing, so Jacob retains the attempted one-half share for Rachel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the invalid share does not necessarily void the conveyance to a valid living co-grantee. C: C is wrong because the deed fixed equal shares and did not give Leah Rachel's failed share. D: D is wrong because Rachel could not pass by devise an interest that never vested in her.
