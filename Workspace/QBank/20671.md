Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: False imprisonment
Question: A private security guard in a uniform stopped a shopper at a mall exit and falsely stated, "I am placing you under arrest. If you leave, I will have you jailed." The guard stood aside and did not lock any doors, but the shopper reasonably believed the guard had legal authority and remained in a security office for 30 minutes. The shopper sues for false imprisonment. Is the guard likely liable?
Answer A: Yes, because confinement may result from asserted legal authority reasonably believed by the plaintiff
Answer B: No, because the guard never physically touched the shopper
Answer C: No, because the office door was unlocked
Answer D: Yes, but only if the guard was actually a police officer
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: False imprisonment does not require a locked door; asserted authority can confine when reasonably believed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Physical force is not required if threat or asserted authority confines the plaintiff. C: An unlocked door does not defeat confinement if the plaintiff reasonably believes leaving will trigger legal restraint. D: The false assertion of authority can be enough when it reasonably causes confinement.
lb_rule_reference: False imprisonment can be based on confinement by asserted legal authority or threat when the plaintiff reasonably believes leaving is not permitted.
