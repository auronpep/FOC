Question: A plaintiff sues a delivery company in federal court after one of its trucks struck her car. She requests "all accident files for every company vehicle in the United States for the last 15 years." The company objects that the request is overbroad. The plaintiff says the files may reveal a company-wide safety pattern. How should the court most likely rule?
Answer A: Compel the entire request because any document that could lead to useful evidence is discoverable
Answer B: Deny all discovery because files from other accidents are never relevant in a negligence case
Answer C: Limit the request to nonprivileged accident files sufficiently similar in time, vehicle type, and safety issue to make the discovery proportional
Answer D: Compel production only if the plaintiff first proves the company was negligent
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court should tailor discovery to relevant, nonprivileged, proportional material instead of granting or denying the request wholesale.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The current rule also requires proportionality and limits discovery to nonprivileged matter relevant to a claim or defense. B: Similar accidents may be relevant, but the request must still be proportional. D: A party need not prove the merits before obtaining relevant, proportional discovery.
lb_rule_reference: Discovery reaches nonprivileged matter relevant to a claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case.
