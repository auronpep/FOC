Question: Naomi owned a parcel along a river. Over ten years, the river gradually deposited soil along Naomi's bank, creating a new strip of dry land attached to her parcel. The change was slow and natural. Who owns the new strip of land?
Answer A: Naomi, because gradual natural accretion belongs to the owner of the riparian parcel.
Answer B: The owner across the river, because the river once covered the strip.
Answer C: The public, because all land formed by water movement is public land.
Answer D: No one, because newly formed land cannot be privately owned until surveyed.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Naomi owns the new strip. Accretion is the gradual and imperceptible buildup of soil along a water boundary. The boundary generally moves with the accretion, and the riparian owner gains the newly attached land.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the new land attached to Naomi's bank through gradual deposit. C: C is wrong because not all water-formed land is public. D: D is wrong because ownership can arise through accretion without a new survey.
lb_rule_reference: Accretion / gradual natural deposit
