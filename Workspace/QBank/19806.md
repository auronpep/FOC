Question: In a federal civil trial, Moses testifies for one party. On cross-examination, the opposing party asks whether Moses was convicted two years ago of perjury. Moses objects that the conviction is unfair because the case is civil and the conviction is embarrassing. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because convictions are never admissible to attack a witness in a civil case.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because only reputation or opinion evidence may attack truthfulness.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because a perjury conviction involves a dishonest act or false statement and is admissible to attack truthfulness.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Moses first testifies that he is truthful.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 609(a)(2) requires admission of a conviction, regardless of punishment, if the court can readily determine that the elements required proving—or the witness admitted—a dishonest act or false statement. Perjury is the classic truthfulness conviction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 609 applies in civil cases. B: B is wrong because conviction impeachment is a separate truthfulness route. D: D is wrong because the conviction may attack Moses's truthfulness without prior self-bolstering.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(a)(2) / dishonesty conviction as truthfulness attack
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests recognition of conviction impeachment as a separate truthfulness channel from Rule 608 reputation/opinion evidence.
