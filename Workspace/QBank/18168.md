MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Affirmative duties to act
Question: A restaurant negligently allowed smoke to fill its kitchen, causing a cook to step outside coughing. The cook was standing safely on the sidewalk when a bystander arrived. Instead of calling emergency services or checking on the cook, the bystander climbed onto the restaurant's roof to "look for trapped people" despite being told that everyone was outside. The bystander jumped from the roof to a neighboring awning, fell, and was injured. The bystander sues the restaurant for negligence. Is the bystander likely to recover as a rescuer in a negligence claim against the restaurant?
Answer A: Yes, because every rescue attempt is foreseeable once negligence creates any danger
Answer B: Yes, because the bystander suffered a physical injury, not just emotional distress
Answer C: No, because the bystander's roof jump was a reckless response outside the foreseeable rescue risk
Answer D: No, because rescuers can never sue the person who created the original danger
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Reasonable rescue is foreseeable, but the facts show no trapped person and warnings that everyone was outside. Climbing and jumping from the roof was a reckless, bizarre response outside the protected rescue chain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The rescue doctrine does not cover every self-created danger. A reckless or bizarre response can fall outside foreseeable rescue consequences. B: Actual injury does not answer whether the plaintiff is within the scope of liability. The issue is the reckless rescue attempt, not whether damages exist. D: Reasonable rescuers may sue when negligence creates peril. This rescuer loses because the attempted rescue was reckless, not because rescuers are categorically barred.
lb_rule_reference: The rescue doctrine protects reasonable rescue efforts, but a reckless or bizarre rescue attempt may fall outside the foreseeable scope of risk.
