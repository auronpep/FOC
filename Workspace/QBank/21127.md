Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff, a citizen of Arizona, sues two defendants — Defendant A (a citizen of California) and Defendant B (a citizen of California) — in federal court asserting a $90,000 diversity claim arising from an automobile accident. Defendant A files a crossclaim against Defendant B seeking $25,000 in contribution, alleging that Defendant B was primarily responsible for the accident and should indemnify Defendant A for any damages the plaintiff recovers against Defendant A. Defendant B moves to dismiss the crossclaim for lack of subject matter jurisdiction, arguing that $25,000 does not satisfy the $75,000 AIC threshold. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Should the court dismiss Defendant A's crossclaim?
Answer A: Yes, because $25,000 does not satisfy the $75,000 amount-in-controversy requirement, and supplemental jurisdiction does not apply to crossclaims between codefendants who are citizens of the same state
Answer B: Yes, because § 1367(b) bars supplemental jurisdiction over claims between parties who lack independent diversity
Answer C: No, because the crossclaim arises from the same automobile accident as the main claim, satisfying the § 1367(a) common-nucleus test, and § 1367(b) does not apply to claims by defendants
Answer D: No, because both defendants are California citizens, satisfying a special supplemental-jurisdiction rule for codefendant claims in diversity cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1367(a) grants supplemental jurisdiction over all claims that share a common nucleus of operative fact with the anchor claim — regardless of independent AIC or independent diversity. Defendant A's crossclaim for contribution arises directly from the same accident that is the subject of the plaintiff's main claim. The § 1367(b) limitation applies only to claims by plaintiffs; a crossclaim asserted by a defendant falls outside the § 1367(b) restriction. The court should deny the motion to dismiss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Supplemental jurisdiction under § 1367(a) does not require independent AIC or independent diversity. Because the crossclaim arises from the same automobile accident as the main claim, it shares a common nucleus of operative fact and qualifies for supplemental jurisdiction. The § 1367(b) bar applies only to claims by plaintiffs — not to crossclaims by defendants. B: Section 1367(b) limits claims asserted by plaintiffs against specified parties joined under Rules 14, 19, 20, or 24. A Rule 13(g) crossclaim is not within the list of claim types § 1367(b) restricts, and the crossclaim is asserted by a defendant, not a plaintiff. Section 1367(b) simply does not apply here. D: There is no special supplemental-jurisdiction rule based on codefendants sharing state citizenship. The correct basis is § 1367(a)'s common-nucleus test. The codefendants' shared citizenship is irrelevant to supplemental jurisdiction analysis; what matters is the relationship between the crossclaim and the anchor claim.
lb_rule_reference: A Rule 13(g) crossclaim between codefendants that shares a common nucleus of operative fact with the main claim falls within § 1367(a) supplemental jurisdiction; § 1367(b) does not bar crossclaims between defendants.
