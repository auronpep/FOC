MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A counselor was treating a patient who repeatedly threatened to stab a named coworker the next morning. The patient had recently brought knives to sessions. The counselor did nothing to warn the coworker or alert authorities, despite a statute and professional rule requiring reasonable steps for identifiable threats. The patient attacked the coworker. Is a duty to take reasonable protective steps likely recognized?
Answer A: Yes, because the counselor had a special relationship with the dangerous patient and knew of an identifiable threat
Answer B: Yes, because everyone has a duty to prevent all foreseeable crimes
Answer C: No, because criminal acts always supersede prior negligence
Answer D: No, because counselors can never owe duties to nonpatients
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts supply a special relationship, specific known threat, identifiable victim, and a legal rule requiring reasonable protective steps.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The duty is not universal. It arises from specific relationship and identifiable-threat facts. C: A foreseeable, specifically threatened criminal act may be within the duty and proximate-cause scope. D: Some rules recognize duties to identifiable victims when the professional relationship reveals a serious threat.
lb_rule_reference: A professional or custodian with a special relationship to a dangerous person may have a duty to use reasonable care to warn or protect an identifiable victim when a serious threat is known.
