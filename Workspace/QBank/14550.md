Question: A janitorial service contracted in writing with a hospital for a one-year term. Under the terms of the contract, the janitorial service agreed to clean the hospital daily in accordance with the hygiene standards of the city's health code. Because the janitorial service did not clean a patient's room in accordance with the required hygiene standards, the patient contracted an infection that required continued hospitalization. In addition to suing the hospital, the patient sued the janitorial service for breach of contract. Which of the following statements is most accurate with respect to the breach of contract claim against the janitorial service?
Answer A: A: The janitorial service is liable to the patient as a matter of public policy, because it violated the city's health code.
Answer B: B: The patient is an intended third-party beneficiary under the contract, because the janitorial service's promise was intended to benefit all hospital patients.
Answer C: C: The patient has no claim for breach of contract against the janitorial service, because she is an incidental beneficiary.
Answer D: D: The patient cannot sue on the contract, because she was not named in the contract.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. The patient has no ability to enforce the contract as an incidental beneficiary because the hospital did not intend to give the patient the benefit of the promised performance. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Because the patient is an incidental beneficiary, the janitorial service is not liable for breach of contract, even if there was a violation of public policy. B is incorrect. There are no facts indicating that the original contract intended the rights to be conferred directly to the patient. D is incorrect. An intended beneficiary does not need to be named for their enforcement rights to vest.
