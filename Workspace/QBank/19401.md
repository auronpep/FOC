Question: In a federal bench-and-jury trial, neither party calls Levi, the clerk who handled a disputed filing. The judge believes Levi's testimony may clarify the record and calls him as a witness. Both parties object that a judge may never call a witness. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The court may call Levi, and each party is entitled to cross-examine him.
Answer B: The court may call Levi, but neither party may cross-examine him because he is the court's witness.
Answer C: The court may call Levi only if both parties consent.
Answer D: The court may not call Levi because calling witnesses belongs exclusively to the parties.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may call a witness on its own or at a party's request, and each party is entitled to cross-examine the witness. Objections to the court's calling a witness may be made then or at the next opportunity when the jury is not present.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B denies the cross-examination right. C adds a consent requirement. D incorrectly treats witness-calling power as exclusive to the parties.
