Question: After final judgment, Paula files a notice of appeal. Two days later, the opposing party files a timely Rule 59 motion to alter or amend the judgment. Paula argues her notice instantly transfers full jurisdiction to the court of appeals and the district court cannot decide the Rule 59 motion. What is the best answer?
Answer A: Paula is correct because a notice of appeal always defeats a timely Rule 59 motion
Answer B: Paula is correct because Rule 59 motions never toll appeal timing
Answer C: Paula is incorrect because the notice generally becomes effective after the timely Rule 59 motion is resolved
Answer D: Paula is incorrect because notices of appeal are never valid in civil cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The district court can decide the timely motion, and the notice takes effect afterward.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A timely tolling motion affects when the notice becomes effective. B: Timely Rule 59 motions are classic tolling motions. D: Notices of appeal are required, but timing rules determine their effect.
lb_rule_reference: When a party files a notice of appeal after judgment but before disposition of a timely tolling motion, the notice generally becomes effective after the motion is resolved.
