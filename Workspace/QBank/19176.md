Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A commuter stumbled on a public sidewalk outside a train station. A freelance photographer standing on the sidewalk took a picture from several feet away and later sold it to a local newsletter. The photo was embarrassing but showed only what anyone on the sidewalk could see. Is the commuter likely to prevail on an intrusion upon seclusion claim against the photographer?
Answer A: Yes, because the photo embarrassed the commuter
Answer B: No, because photographing visible conduct in a public place is not an intrusion into seclusion
Answer C: Yes, because selling the photo satisfies the publication element
Answer D: No, because intrusion claims require the statement to be false
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The photographer captured what was openly visible on a public sidewalk, so no private seclusion was invaded.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intrusion requires invasion of a private place or matter; public sidewalk activity is not secluded. C: Publication is not the intrusion element, and the threshold private-invasion requirement is absent. D: Intrusion does not require falsity; this claim fails because the place and matter were public.
lb_rule_reference: Observation or photography in a public place generally is not intrusion because there is no private seclusion invaded.
