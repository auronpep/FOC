Outline: Attorney-client
Question: Solomon emails his lawyer Nathan and asks him to draft a “consulting agreement” for Leah. Solomon explains that Leah will perform no services and that the document will make a payment to Aaron, a public official, look legitimate so Aaron will award Solomon a city contract. A grand jury later subpoenas Solomon's email to Nathan. Solomon objects that the email is a confidential attorney-client communication seeking legal drafting services. Is the email protected by attorney-client privilege?
Answer A: Yes, because drafting agreements is legal work and the email was sent privately to Solomon's lawyer.
Answer B: No, because Solomon sought legal drafting to disguise a bribe or fraudulent payment, placing the communication within the crime-fraud exception.
Answer C: The answer depends only on whether the sham consulting agreement would be enforceable under contract law.
Answer D: Yes, unless Nathan personally agreed to participate in the bribe or shared Solomon's criminal intent.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The email sought a lawyer's help to create a false paper trail for a bribe or fraudulent payment. Attorney-client privilege does not extend to communications made for the purpose of obtaining advice or services to commit or conceal crime or fraud. The decisive axis is Solomon's illegal purpose in using legal services, not the formal label of contract drafting.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a confidential communication with a lawyer is not protected when the client seeks legal services to further crime or fraud. C: C is wrong because contract enforceability is not the admissibility route. The privilege issue turns on the purpose of the communication. D: D is wrong because the exception does not require the lawyer to become a participant or share the client's intent. The client's use of legal services to further the fraud is the key.
lb_rule_reference: Crime-fraud exception for legal drafting in furtherance of bribery/fraud
lb_student_diagnostic: Student answers from the form of the legal service rather than the function the client wants that service to perform.
