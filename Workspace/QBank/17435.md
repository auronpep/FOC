Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Assault
Question: In a parking lot, a driver angrily pointed what looked like a real pistol at another driver from ten feet away and said, "Move now or I will make you move." The other driver reasonably believed the pistol was real and froze. The pistol was an unloaded plastic replica. The other driver sues the first driver for assault. Is the other driver likely to prevail in an assault claim against the first driver?
Answer A: Yes, because the defendant intentionally caused reasonable apprehension of imminent contact with apparent ability
Answer B: No, because the replica pistol could not actually fire
Answer C: No, because the defendant never touched the other driver
Answer D: No, because the other driver froze instead of showing fear or panic
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Assault turns on reasonable apprehension of imminent harmful or offensive contact. Apparent ability is enough when the plaintiff reasonably believes the threat can be carried out immediately.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Assault does not require actual ability if the defendant creates reasonable apprehension through apparent ability. C: Assault is complete without contact if the plaintiff reasonably apprehends imminent harmful or offensive contact. D: Assault requires apprehension, not fear. A calm or frozen plaintiff can still apprehend imminent contact.
lb_rule_reference: Assault requires intentional creation of reasonable apprehension of imminent harmful or offensive contact based on apparent ability, even if actual ability is absent.
