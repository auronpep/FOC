Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Offers of proof
Question: In a jury trial, Noah offers testimony from Miriam. The court excludes the testimony. Outside the jury's hearing, Noah makes a detailed offer of proof describing Miriam's expected testimony so the record will show what was excluded. Later, Noah asks the judge to instruct the jury that Miriam would have testified in Noah's favor. Should the court give that instruction?
Answer A: Yes, because an offer of proof becomes evidence once it is placed on the record.
Answer B: Yes, because excluded evidence must be summarized to the jury for fairness.
Answer C: No, because the offer of proof preserves the record for review; it is not admitted evidence for the jury to use.
Answer D: No, but only because offers of proof can never be made outside the jury's presence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An offer of proof informs the court and preserves the record when evidence is excluded. It does not make the excluded testimony evidence for the jury. The court should not instruct the jury to treat the proffer as proof.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because making a record is not the same as admitting evidence. B: B is wrong because the jury generally should not hear excluded matter. D: D is wrong because offers of proof are often made outside the jury's hearing.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(2), (c) / offer of proof not evidence
