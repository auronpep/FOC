Question: A state scholarship program pays tuition for students pursuing degrees in fields with teacher shortages. The program is open to public and private colleges, but it excludes students who will use the scholarship for religious teacher training. A student admitted to an accredited religious college challenges the exclusion. What is the student's strongest constitutional argument?
Answer A: The exclusion is required because the Establishment Clause forbids any scholarship use at a religious college
Answer B: The exclusion burdens Free Exercise because the state denies an otherwise neutral public benefit due to religious use
Answer C: The exclusion is valid because government never has to fund private education
Answer D: The student loses because only religious institutions, not students, can bring Free Exercise claims
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The state opened a neutral scholarship program, then disqualified the student because the education is religious. That religious-use exclusion raises serious Free Exercise concerns.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Neutral benefits do not have to exclude religious uses automatically, and such exclusion can burden Free Exercise. C: Once the state creates a neutral benefit, it generally cannot disqualify recipients for religious status or use without constitutional scrutiny. D: The student is denied a public benefit because of religious education and can assert her own Free Exercise injury.
lb_rule_reference: Once government offers a neutral public benefit, excluding otherwise eligible recipients because they will use the benefit for religious education raises serious Free Exercise concerns.
