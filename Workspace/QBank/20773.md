Question: Joseph granted Mary “a right to cross the gravel lane along the north boundary of Joseph's land.” Years later, Joseph built a smoother lane along the south boundary and told Mary she must use that lane instead. The south lane was equally convenient, but Mary refused to consent to the change. Under the traditional common-law rule, which is the best result?
Answer A: Joseph may force Mary to use the south lane because the substituted route is equally convenient.
Answer B: Joseph may force Mary to use the south lane because the servient owner always controls the location.
Answer C: Mary must use the south lane unless she proves that the north lane is strictly necessary.
Answer D: Mary may continue using the north-boundary lane because the express easement fixed that location and Joseph cannot unilaterally relocate it.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the traditional rule, when an express easement fixes a route, the servient owner generally cannot unilaterally relocate it without the easement holder's consent or applicable legal authority. The grant identified the north-boundary lane, and Mary did not consent to substitution.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because equal convenience alone does not authorize unilateral relocation under the traditional rule. B: B is wrong because servient ownership does not include power to rewrite a fixed easement. C: C is wrong because strict necessity is not the test for an express fixed route. D is correct because location was part of the grant's scope.
lb_rule_reference: Fixed location / traditional relocation rule
