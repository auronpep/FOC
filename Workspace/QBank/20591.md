Question: Abraham owned two adjoining parcels, Lot 1 and Lot 2. For many years, Abraham used a visible gravel driveway across Lot 2 to reach a barn on Lot 1. The driveway was the only practical route for farm vehicles to reach the barn, although a narrow footpath from a public road also touched Lot 1. Abraham then sold Lot 1 to Sarah. The deed did not mention the driveway. Sarah continued using the driveway. Later, Abraham sold Lot 2 to Moses, who knew the driveway existed but objected to Sarah's use. Sarah sues to establish a right to continue using the driveway. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Sarah has an easement by necessity because any practical inconvenience is enough to create a necessary easement.
Answer B: Sarah has no easement because implied easements require the land to be completely landlocked.
Answer C: Sarah has an implied easement by prior use because the driveway was apparent, continuous, and reasonably necessary when the parcels were severed.
Answer D: Sarah has no easement because Abraham's later sale of Lot 2 to Moses merged and extinguished any access right.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An implied easement by prior use may arise when, before severance, one part of commonly owned land was used for the benefit of another part, the use was apparent and continuous, and the use was reasonably necessary to enjoyment of the conveyed parcel. The driveway existed before severance, was visible, and was reasonably necessary for farm-vehicle access to the barn.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it uses the stricter necessity route and overstates necessity; convenience alone is not enough. B: B is wrong because complete landlock is not required for an implied easement by prior use. D: D is wrong because Abraham's later sale to Moses did not merge the parcels; merger requires unity of ownership of the relevant estates.
lb_rule_reference: Implied easement by prior use
