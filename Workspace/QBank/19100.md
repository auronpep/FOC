Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Leah hired Daniel to provide lights for a concert. Before Daniel repudiated, Leah reasonably spent $4,000 on permits that would have been useful only for the concert. After Daniel unequivocally repudiated and no substitute lighting was available, Leah spent another $6,000 printing posters for the same concert. If Leah's expected profits are too speculative, what reliance amount is recoverable?
Answer A: $10,000, because all expenditures connected to the contract are reliance damages.
Answer B: $6,000, because only expenditures after repudiation show reliance.
Answer C: $4,000, because the later poster expense was avoidable after Daniel's repudiation.
Answer D: Nothing, because repudiation eliminates reliance recovery.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages protect reasonable expenditures made before the breach or repudiation, but they do not include avoidable expenses incurred after the injured party knows performance will not occur. Leah may recover the $4,000 permit expense but not the $6,000 post-repudiation poster expense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because avoidable post-repudiation expenditures are excluded. B: B is wrong because post-repudiation spending is the problem, not the measure. D: D is wrong because repudiation does not eliminate reasonable pre-repudiation reliance expenses.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance damages / post-repudiation expenditures
