


BARMATRIX Q#: 22824
Outline_code: 94050100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Venue
Question: A bus crashed in State A, killing all of the passengers, who were citizens of State B. The company that owned the bus was incorporated and had its principal place of business in State C. An attorney representing the passengers' estates filed a wrongful death action against the bus company in federal court in State A. If the company does not want to have the federal court in State A hear the action, what should it do?
Answer A: A: File a motion to dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction.
Answer B: B: File a motion to transfer the action to a State C federal court.
Answer C: C: File a motion to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction.
Answer D: D: File a motion to dismiss for improper venue.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under 28 U.S.C. §1391(b), venue is proper in any judicial district where any defendant resides, if all defendants are residents of the state in which the district is located; where a substantial part of the events giving rise to the claim occurred; or where any defendant is subject to the court's personal jurisdiction with respect to the action. Here, venue would be proper in State A because the bus crashed in State A. However, under 28 U.S.C. §1401(a), a federal district court may transfer an action to any other district where it might have been brought. Here, since the bus company resides in State C, it can ask the court to transfer the action there. This would be the best way for the bus company to avoid the federal court in State A. A is incorrect because the federal court in State A likely has subject matter jurisdiction. Specifically, the parties are citizens of different states and the amount in controversy is likely over $75,000 because more than one person died in the crash. C is incorrect because the State A court likely has personal jurisdiction over the bus company since it operated its buses in State A. D is incorrect because State A would not be an improper venue.
