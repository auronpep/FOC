Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal employment trial, Paul sues a warehouse company. Leah, the company's shift supervisor, testifies for the company that she never saw Paul report a broken loading dock. Paul offers Leah's written incident note, made during her employment and concerning the dock, stating, “Paul told me today that the dock plate is broken.” Paul did not ask Leah about the note while she was on the stand. The company objects under Rule 613(b). How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the note because Leah was not first given an opportunity to explain or deny it.
Answer B: Admit the note only to impeach Leah, because it is inconsistent with her testimony.
Answer C: Admit the note against the company as an employee statement on a matter within the scope of employment while the relationship existed.
Answer D: Exclude the note because employee statements are admissible only if the employee was authorized to speak publicly for the employer.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A statement by a party's agent or employee on a matter within the scope of the relationship and while the relationship existed is an opposing party's statement when offered against the party. Leah's incident note concerned the loading dock, was made during her supervisory employment, and is offered against the company. Rule 613(b)'s opportunity requirement does not bar that independent party-opponent route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 613(b) does not apply to opposing-party statements under Rule 801(d)(2). B: B is wrong because the note is not limited to impeachment if it qualifies as the employer's party-opponent statement. D: D is wrong because employee-scope statements do not require separate authority to speak publicly.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 801(d)(2)(D) / employee statement as party-opponent evidence
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests employee statement as a substantive route and the difference between prior inconsistent impeachment and opposing-party evidence.
