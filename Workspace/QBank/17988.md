Question: Rachel agreed to buy 600 lamps from Samuel in a single shipment by May 1. The signed goods contract stated that changes required a signed writing. After a fire damaged Rachel's warehouse, Rachel orally asked Samuel to hold the lamps and ship them in three lots over six weeks. Samuel paid for storage, repacked the lamps, and shipped the first two lots, which Rachel accepted and resold. Before the third lot arrived, Rachel rejected it, arguing that the written contract required a single May 1 shipment and that no written amendment existed. Can Rachel reject the third lot on that ground?
Answer A: Yes, because a signed no-oral-modification clause always defeats contrary conduct.
Answer B: Yes, because the parol evidence rule bars Samuel from proving Rachel's oral request.
Answer C: Probably no, because Rachel's request, acceptance of the first two lots, and Samuel's reliance can operate as waiver, and retraction would be unjust after the material change in position.
Answer D: Probably no, but only because the fire made Samuel's original May 1 performance impossible.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Article 2, an attempted modification that fails a signed-writing requirement can still operate as a waiver, and a waiver affecting executory performance may not be retracted if retraction would be unjust because of material reliance. The trigger facts are Rachel's post-signing request, Samuel's storage and repacking costs, and Rachel's acceptance of the first two split shipments. The decisive locks are timing and reliance. Samuel is not trying to prove a prior oral term; he is relying on later waiver conduct.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because NOM-clause absolutism ignores Article 2 waiver. B: B is wrong because the request occurred after signing. D: D is wrong because the warehouse fire affected Rachel's storage problem, not Samuel's ability to perform under the original contract.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-209(4), (5) / waiver and reliance after attempted modification
