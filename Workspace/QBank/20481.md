


BARMATRIX Q#: 20481
Outline_code: 62050401
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Defamation
Question: If the plaintiff asserts a defamation claim against the defendant, which of the following would be the defendant's most effective argument in defense?
Answer A: A: The plaintiff did not experience mental suffering.
Answer B: B: The plaintiff did not sustain damage to his reputation as a result of the statement.
Answer C: C: The defendant was only repeating what he had heard.
Answer D: D: The defendant did not publish any statement about the plaintiff.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: There is some question about whether it is defamatory to say that a person is impoverished or on the verge of bankruptcy. But even if the defendant's statement was defamatory, it was oral and, therefore, classified as slander. Ordinarily, there is no liability for slander unless the plaintiff establishes actual damage to his or her reputation. Since the company did not believe what the defendant told him, there was no damage to the plaintiff's reputation, and the defendant will not be held liable for saying it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect because mental suffering is not an essential element of defamation. Since liability for defamation is imposed on the publisher of a defamatory statement, the fact that the defendant was only repeating what he heard would not in itself protect him against liability. C is therefore incorrect. Since publication is defined as communication to any person other than the plaintiff, D is incorrect.
