Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: Antoine and Xavier planned and carried out an armed bank robbery. During the robbery, a customer attempted to trigger a silent alarm. Xavier, without any prior discussion or agreement with Antoine, shot and killed the customer to prevent the alarm. Antoine was at the teller station and did not shoot anyone. Both were apprehended as they fled. The jurisdiction uses the agency theory for felony murder. Is Antoine guilty of felony murder for the customer's death?
Answer A: No, because Antoine did not shoot anyone and Xavier acted without Antoine's prior agreement
Answer B: No, because under the agency theory, only the person who physically commits the killing can be charged with felony murder
Answer C: Yes, because Xavier's killing was committed in furtherance of the robbery, and all co-felons are vicariously liable for killings by co-felons in furtherance of the common enterprise
Answer D: Yes, and Antoine is also liable for first-degree premeditated murder because he planned the robbery with Xavier
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The agency theory holds that killings by a co-felon in furtherance of the inherently dangerous felony are attributed to all participants in the criminal enterprise. Robbery is a qualifying inherently dangerous felony. Xavier shot the customer to prevent an alarm during the robbery — clearly in furtherance of the enterprise. Antoine, as a co-felon, is vicariously liable for that killing under felony murder. The agency theory does not require prior agreement for the specific killing, only that the killing be in furtherance of the shared criminal plan.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Felony murder imputes liability for killings committed in furtherance of the qualifying felony regardless of whether the surviving co-felon specifically agreed to or authorized the killing. The act must be in furtherance of the common criminal enterprise — not a personal frolic. Stopping a customer from triggering an alarm is squarely in furtherance of the bank robbery. Antoine is liable under felony murder for Xavier's killing. B: The agency theory limits felony murder to killings committed by a co-felon (the enterprise's "agent"), not killings committed by third parties. It does not restrict liability to the particular co-felon who pulled the trigger. All co-felons are agents of the enterprise; when any one of them kills in furtherance of it, all are liable. D: Planning a robbery does not constitute premeditation of a specific killing that was not contemplated. First-degree murder requires premeditation and deliberation toward the specific killing. There is no evidence Antoine contemplated the killing of any customer — Xavier's decision was spontaneous. Antoine's liability is properly felony murder, not premeditated first-degree murder.
lb_rule_reference: Under felony murder, each participant in an inherently dangerous felony is liable for killings committed by any co-felon in furtherance of the criminal enterprise, even if the participating defendant did not personally commit the killing
