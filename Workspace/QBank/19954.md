Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Exclusionary Rule
Question: Police illegally arrested Marco, questioned him briefly, and released him without charges. Two days later, after speaking with his attorney and family, Marco voluntarily went to the station and confessed to the crime. The confession was not prompted by new police pressure. Marco moves to suppress the confession as fruit of the illegal arrest. What is the best argument against suppression?
Answer A: The confession is admissible because the release, time lapse, legal consultation, and voluntary return attenuated the taint
Answer B: The confession must be suppressed because any later confession after an illegal arrest is fruit forever
Answer C: The confession is admissible only if police had probable cause for the original arrest
Answer D: The confession must be suppressed unless Marco received Miranda warnings during the illegal arrest
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Significant intervening events broke the causal chain: Marco was released, had time, consulted counsel, and returned voluntarily. Those facts support attenuation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Fruit analysis includes attenuation. Later voluntary acts can break the chain. C: The question assumes the arrest was illegal. Admission turns on attenuation, not retroactive probable cause. D: The strongest attenuation facts are release, time, counsel, and voluntary return. Miranda during the earlier arrest is not the required path here.
lb_rule_reference: Attenuation can break the causal chain when intervening circumstances and time dissipate the taint of police illegality.
