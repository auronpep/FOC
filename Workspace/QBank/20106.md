MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Freedom of speech and expression
Question: To protest surveillance, activists project messages onto the walls of a federal courthouse at night. A content-neutral rule bars all unauthorized projections on courthouse walls to protect building surfaces and avoid confusion with official notices. The activists argue the rule fails because the government could use less restrictive case-by-case cleanup fees. Which tailoring standard applies?
Answer A: The government must prove the projection ban is the least restrictive means
Answer B: The rule is evaluated under O'Brien because it regulates the noncommunicative effects of expressive conduct
Answer C: No First Amendment analysis applies because projected words are conduct
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies automatically because the protest concerns politics
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule targets unauthorized projections on the building, not the activists' message.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Least restrictive means is strict-scrutiny language, not the O'Brien standard. C: Projected words and symbolic conduct can be expressive. D: Political context does not convert a content-neutral conduct rule into a content-based restriction.
lb_rule_reference: Under O'Brien, a content-neutral regulation of expressive conduct must further an important interest unrelated to suppression of expression and burden no more speech than essential to that interest; it need not be the least restrictive means.
