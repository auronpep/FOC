MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A warehouse in a dense commercial block hired a licensed company to fumigate stored imports with a toxic gas rarely used in the city. The company sealed the warehouse carefully, but an unexpected pressure change pushed gas through an old utility conduit into the neighboring florist's shop. The gas killed plants and forced employees to evacuate. The florist sued the fumigation company for strict liability. Is the florist likely to prevail on the strict liability claim for the fumigation gas escape?
Answer A: No, because the fumigation company was licensed and carefully sealed the warehouse
Answer B: Yes, because toxic gas escaped and caused plant loss and evacuation, the very risks that made the fumigation abnormal in that setting
Answer C: No, because only bodily injury can support strict liability for toxic activities
Answer D: Yes, because the fumigation company is liable for all losses suffered by any business on the block that day
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rare toxic fumigation in a dense commercial block creates an uncommon high risk not eliminated by care. Gas escape causing property damage and evacuation falls within that risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: If the activity is abnormally dangerous, due care and permits do not bar strict liability for harms within the abnormal risk. C: Strict liability for abnormally dangerous activities can cover property damage caused by the abnormal risk, including destroyed plants. D: Strict liability extends only to harms caused by the abnormal gas risk, not every business loss on the block.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability applies when an abnormally dangerous fumigation or toxic chemical activity causes the kind of escape and property or personal harm that makes the activity abnormal.
