Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A homeowner's dog had never bitten anyone, but it had repeatedly lunged at delivery workers, snapped at their hands, and once tore a sleeve while growling at the front gate. The homeowner warned friends not to reach over the gate because the dog "goes for hands." A delivery worker later reached toward the mailbox slot next to the gate, and the dog bit the worker's hand. The worker sues the homeowner for strict liability for animal harm. Is the worker likely to prevail on a strict liability claim against the homeowner for harm caused by the dog?
Answer A: No, because the dog had not previously bitten anyone
Answer B: Yes, because the dog showed a known dangerous propensity toward hands and the bite matched that risk
Answer C: Yes, because every dog owner is strictly liable for every dog bite
Answer D: No, because the delivery worker voluntarily reached toward the mailbox slot near the gate
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The dog was domestic, but the homeowner knew it snapped and lunged at hands. The worker's hand injury resulted from that known dangerous propensity, supporting strict liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A prior bite is not required if other behavior gives the owner reason to know of a dangerous propensity. Lunging, snapping, tearing a sleeve, and the owner's own warning show knowledge of hand-biting danger. C: Domestic pet strict liability depends on known dangerous propensity. This owner is liable because of specific knowledge, not because all dog bites automatically impose strict liability. D: Merely reaching toward a mailbox slot to make a delivery is not provocation that defeats strict liability on these facts. The dog bit in the exact way the owner knew was dangerous.
lb_rule_reference: A domestic animal owner is strictly liable when the owner knows or has reason to know of the animal's dangerous propensity and the harm results from that propensity.
