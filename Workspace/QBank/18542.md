MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: False imprisonment
Question: A museum guard mistakenly believed a visitor had entered a closed gallery. The guard stood at the main archway and told the visitor to wait until a manager arrived. A clearly marked side door, visible to the visitor and opening directly into a public hallway, remained unlocked. The visitor stayed for ten minutes because leaving through the side door would have been embarrassing. The visitor sues the guard for false imprisonment. Is the visitor likely to prevail in a false imprisonment claim against the guard?
Answer A: Yes, because the guard intentionally delayed the visitor
Answer B: No, because the visitor knew of a reasonable safe exit through the side door
Answer C: Yes, because embarrassment made the side door unreasonable
Answer D: No, because false imprisonment requires physical force
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: False imprisonment requires confinement within a bounded area. A known, safe, unlocked exit defeats confinement even if using it would be awkward.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Delay alone is not confinement. The visitor knew of a reasonable safe exit. C: Mere embarrassment does not make an otherwise safe and reasonable exit unavailable. D: False imprisonment can be based on threats or asserted authority. The correct reason is the known safe exit, not the absence of force.
lb_rule_reference: There is no false imprisonment when the plaintiff knows of a reasonable, safe means of escape.
