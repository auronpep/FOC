Subtopic: Contract Content and Meaning
Outline: Gap-Fillers
Question: Peter bought a set of brewing tanks from a manufacturer under a signed contract requiring “sanitary tri-clamp fittings.” The contract contained a merger clause but did not define the phrase. Peter rejected the tanks because the fittings had removable silicone gaskets rather than welded seals. The manufacturer offered evidence that, in the commercial brewing-equipment trade, “sanitary tri-clamp fittings” ordinarily include removable silicone gaskets. Should the court admit the trade-usage evidence?
Answer A: No, because the merger clause requires the court to apply a default gap-filler rather than trade usage.
Answer B: No, because the transaction includes equipment setup and therefore cannot use Article 2 meaning sources.
Answer C: No, because a technical term in a goods contract can never be explained by industry usage.
Answer D: Yes, if the usage is established and reasonably consistent with the written fitting requirement.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that UCC goods contracts may be explained or supplemented by established trade usage, including usage that defines a technical commercial term, so long as it can reasonably coexist with the express writing. The trigger facts are a goods sale, the undefined phrase “sanitary tri-clamp fittings,” and the offered brewing-equipment usage. The decisive locks are source law and purpose: the evidence explains a technical term rather than adding a separate obligation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because agreement sources such as usage come before default gap-fillers, and merger clauses do not automatically bar UCC usage evidence. B: B is wrong because ordinary setup incidental to equipment does not defeat the goods route. C: C is wrong because technical trade terms are classic candidates for usage explanation.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-202 / UCC 1-303 technical trade term
