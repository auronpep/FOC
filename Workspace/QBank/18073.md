Question: In a federal assault trial, the prosecutor offers a rock found in a public alley the day after Nathan was injured. No witness saw a rock used, no one connects this rock to Nathan or to the defendant, and the prosecutor offers no distinctive mark or chain of custody. The defendant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the rock because any object capable of causing the injury is relevant.
Answer B: Admit the rock as demonstrative evidence of what rocks look like.
Answer C: Exclude the rock because the proponent has not connected it to a fact of consequence in the case.
Answer D: Exclude the rock only if the defendant proves it was not the weapon.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An object must be connected to the case by relevance and authentication. A generic object found later with no link to the event is not admissible merely because it could have caused harm.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B treat a generic possibility as enough. D improperly shifts the burden to the opponent to disprove the connection.
