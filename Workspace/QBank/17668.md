Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Joshua contracted to build a warehouse for Isaac for $200,000 using plans supplied by Isaac. Joshua completed the work. During construction, Joshua reasonably incurred $18,000 in extra costs because the plans Isaac supplied contained a structural error that required correction. Joshua did not assume responsibility for design errors in the contract. What is Joshua's proper recovery for the extra costs?
Answer A: Nothing, because a fixed-price contractor bears every cost overrun.
Answer B: $18,000, because the extra cost was caused by Isaac's defective specifications rather than Joshua's assumed performance risk.
Answer C: $200,000 only, because the contract price is the exclusive remedy even for owner-caused extra work.
Answer D: The reasonable value of the entire warehouse, because the contract price no longer matters.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A fixed-price contractor generally bears ordinary performance-cost risk, but not extra costs caused by the owner's defective specifications unless that risk was assumed. The $18,000 owner-caused extra cost is recoverable as expectation damages in addition to the agreed price.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because fixed price does not automatically transfer design-error risk supplied by the owner. C: C is wrong because the contract price does not compensate Joshua for extra owner-caused work outside the assumed risk. D: D is wrong because the contract remains the measure; restitution for the whole warehouse would overcompensate.
lb_rule_reference: Common law expectation damages; owner-caused extra work
