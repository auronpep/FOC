Question: Mary orally agreed to buy 300 lamps from John for $4 each. No writing was signed. Mary paid John for 90 lamps, and John accepted the payment. John then refused to deliver any lamps, asserting the Statute of Frauds. To what extent is the oral contract enforceable under UCC §2-201?
Answer A: It is unenforceable for any lamps because there is no signed writing.
Answer B: It is enforceable for all 300 lamps because any payment validates the entire oral contract.
Answer C: It is enforceable only for the 90 lamps for which payment was made and accepted.
Answer D: It is enforceable only if Mary proves that the lamps were specially manufactured.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC §2-201, an otherwise valid goods contract that lacks a sufficient writing is enforceable with respect to goods for which payment has been made and accepted. Mary's payment validates enforcement only for the paid quantity, not the entire oral quantity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because payment made and accepted is a UCC exception. B: B is wrong because the exception is limited to the goods paid for. D: D is wrong because specially manufactured goods are a separate exception; the payment-accepted exception controls here.
