Question: Ruth hired Jacob to paint her house. The signed writing stated the color, price, and completion date. It contained no merger clause and did not mention cleanup. Before signing, Jacob orally promised to remove drop cloths and empty paint cans after finishing. The court finds the writing final as to the included terms but not complete and exclusive. May Ruth introduce the oral cleanup promise? May Ruth introduce the oral cleanup promise?
Answer A: Yes, because it is a consistent additional term that may supplement a partial integration.
Answer B: No, because any prior oral promise is barred after a written agreement is signed.
Answer C: No, because cleanup relates to the same project as the written painting contract.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Ruth proves the written agreement was invalid.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A final writing may be a partial integration. A consistent additional term may supplement a partial integration if it does not contradict the written terms. The cleanup promise does not negate the color, price, or completion date.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because final does not always mean complete and exclusive. C: C is wrong because relation to the same transaction does not automatically bar a consistent additional term. D: D is wrong because Ruth is supplementing the agreement, not avoiding it.
lb_rule_reference: Partial integration / consistent additional term
