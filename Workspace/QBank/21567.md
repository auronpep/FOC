Outline: Attorney-client
Question: David, a company president, emailed in-house lawyer Esther and two executives: “We need legal risk advice on whether this advertisement violates federal law, and we also need a stronger sales slogan.” Esther replied with a legal analysis and suggested three marketing slogans. If the email thread is requested in discovery, what is the best ruling?
Answer A: Produce the entire thread because it included business advice.
Answer B: Withhold the entire thread because a lawyer was copied.
Answer C: Produce the legal analysis because business executives were copied.
Answer D: Protect the portions primarily seeking or giving legal advice, but do not treat purely business marketing advice as privileged merely because counsel gave it.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Mixed communications must be routed by purpose. Legal-risk advice from counsel can be privileged even when executives are copied, but ordinary business or marketing advice is not automatically privileged. The decisive axis is legal-advice content versus business content.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This underprotects legal advice by treating any business component as fatal. B: B is wrong because This overprotects all content based on lawyer involvement. C: C is wrong because Copying executives with a need to know does not automatically waive or defeat privilege for legal advice.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / mixed-purpose corporate communication
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is using an all-or-nothing approach instead of splitting legal and business purposes.
