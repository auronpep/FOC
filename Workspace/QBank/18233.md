Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Naomi sues a bus company, claiming a crash left her unable to walk more than a block. The bus company offers a video from two weeks after the crash showing Naomi walking several blocks through a market. Naomi objects that the video does not prove she was pain-free and that she may have suffered later that day. How should the court rule, assuming the video is authenticated?
Answer A: Exclude the video because it does not prove Naomi was pain-free.
Answer B: Exclude the video unless a doctor testifies that Naomi was fully healed.
Answer C: Admit the video only if Naomi denied ever leaving her home.
Answer D: Admit the video because it has some tendency to bear on the extent of Naomi's claimed injury; her explanations go to weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The video tends to make Naomi's claimed limitation less probable or to affect the extent of damages. It need not prove that she was pain-free or fully healed. Her explanation of pain afterward is for the factfinder.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the evidence need not disprove all pain to be relevant to injury extent. B: B is wrong because medical testimony is not required for the video to have some probative value. C: C is wrong because the video can be relevant even without a total-homebound denial.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Treating partial contradiction as irrelevant because it is not a complete contradiction.
