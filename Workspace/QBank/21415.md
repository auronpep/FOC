Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Lydia is charged with stealing gems from a traveling merchant. The prosecutor offers evidence that a person named Lydia worked as a jewel cleaner in another city ten years earlier. The prosecutor offers no evidence that the jewel cleaner is the same Lydia or that the old job relates to the stolen gems. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because jewel cleaning and gem theft both involve gems.
Answer B: Admit the evidence unless Lydia proves she never worked as a jewel cleaner.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence because the prosecutor has not connected the old job to this defendant or to the charged theft.
Answer D: Exclude the evidence only under Rule 403 because all gem-related evidence is relevant first.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The evidence lacks a necessary case-specific connection. Without proof that the old job involved this defendant or the charged theft, the evidence does not make a consequential fact more probable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because topical similarity is not enough without a link to the defendant or event. B: B is wrong because Lydia need not disprove an unconnected assertion. D: D is wrong because the problem is threshold relevance, not merely discretionary exclusion.
