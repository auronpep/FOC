Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Esther orally agrees to sell Daniel 600 bricks for $900. Daniel argues that the deal is outside the UCC because the bricks will be used in a building project and because Esther offered to stack them near the site for free. No writing is signed, no bricks are delivered, and Daniel later refuses to buy. What is the best analysis?
Answer A: The contract is governed by common law because the bricks will be used in construction.
Answer B: The free stacking service removes the transaction from Article 2.
Answer C: The oral contract is enforceable because building materials are not goods once intended for a project.
Answer D: The transaction is still a sale of goods, and because the price is $500 or more, the UCC Statute of Frauds requires a sufficient writing or exception.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Bricks are movable goods, and the incidental free stacking service does not change the basic transaction into a service contract. Because the goods price is $900, §2-201 requires a sufficient writing or exception. None is present.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because construction use does not change bricks into services or land. B: B is wrong because an incidental service does not displace Article 2 in this fact pattern. C: C is wrong because building materials are goods before installation.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(1)
