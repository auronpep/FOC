


BARMATRIX Q#: 22066
Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: A professor was going on a year sabbatical, so he leased his cheap and dirty apartment to an extremely wealthy celebrity lecturer pursuant to a one- year lease. Shortly after moving in, the lecturer significantly improved the apartment by installing air conditioning, a new kitchen counter, a small wine cellar, and a rooftop balcony. The lease did not contain any provisions concerning improvements or renovations. When the professor returned, he filed suit against the lecturer, arguing that the lecturer's improvements were not permissible under the lease. The apartment was in a jurisdiction that followed the majority rule. Were the lecturer's improvements permissible?
Answer A: A: No, because the improvements were permissive waste.
Answer B: B: No, because the improvements were ameliorative waste.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the lease did not forbid improvements.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the improvements added value to the property.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Ameliorative waste occurs when a tenant acts in a way that increases the value of the property. All leases prohibit waste, so the lecturer was not allowed to add his improvements. A is incorrect because permissive waste occurs when a tenant simply allows the waste to happen. Here, the lecturer affirmatively improved the property, making his actions unlawful ameliorative waste. C and D are incorrect because all leases prohibit waste, even if it is ameliorative waste that improves the value of the property.
