


BARMATRIX Q#: 22847
Outline_code: 94040200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Service of Process
Question: A woman sued a company in federal district court for money owed under a contract. Under the state's civil procedure rules, service could be made on a company by sending an email to the company's primary email address if service could not be made by physical delivery. The woman's process server tried to physically deliver the notice, but when he could not do so, he sent an email to the company's primary email address. The company challenged the service by email. The woman correctly pointed out that both federal and state courts had found similar email service to be constitutional and reasonably calculated to give defendants notice. Was the company properly served?
Answer A: A: No, because such service is not authorized by the federal rules.
Answer B: B: No, because service was not made on the company's CEO or other designated person.
Answer C: C: Yes, because federal courts must follow the applicable state's service of process rules.
Answer D: D: Yes, because such service was constitutional and reasonably calculated to give defendants notice.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(h)(1)(A), a person can serve a corporation following state law rules for serving an individual. However, Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co., 339 U.S. 306 (1950), noted that such service has to be constitutional and reasonably calculated to apprise interested parties of the pendency of the action. Here, since the question states that this particular form of service has been found constitutional and reasonably calculated to give defendants notice, the company was properly served. A and B are incorrect because the federal rules allow service under state rules so long as that service is constitutional and reasonably calculated to give defendants notice. C is incorrect because there is no rule stating that a federal court must follow a state's applicable service of process rules.
