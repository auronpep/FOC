Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A ladder manufacturer designed each ladder rung to be welded at four points. One ladder left the factory with a rung welded at only two points, even though the manufacturer used a reasonable automated inspection system that failed to catch the error. The rung snapped during ordinary use, causing the buyer to fall. The buyer sues the manufacturer under strict products liability for a manufacturing defect. Is the buyer likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim for a manufacturing defect?
Answer A: No, because the manufacturer used a reasonable automated inspection system
Answer B: Yes, because this ladder departed from intended welding specifications and caused injury during ordinary use
Answer C: No, because only a design defect can support strict products liability
Answer D: Yes, because the manufacturer was negligent in programming the inspection system
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The ladder was intended to have four welds per rung but left the factory with only two on the injury-causing rung. That sale-time departure supports manufacturing-defect liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Strict manufacturing-defect liability turns on the defective condition of the product, not on whether the manufacturer's inspection process was reasonable. C: Manufacturing defects are an independent basis for strict products liability. This is a rogue-unit departure, not a challenge to the ladder design. D: The facts do not require or establish negligent programming. The proper strict-liability reason is the ladder's departure from specifications when sold.
lb_rule_reference: A manufacturer may be strictly liable for a manufacturing defect even if it used a reasonable quality-control process.
