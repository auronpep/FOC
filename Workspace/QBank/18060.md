Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue

Question: A defendant is sued in federal court. Before answering, she files a Rule 12 motion asserting improper venue and insufficient service of process. The court denies the motion. The defendant then files a second pre-answer motion asserting lack of personal jurisdiction. How should the court rule on the personal jurisdiction defense?
Answer A: Deny it as waived because it was omitted from the first Rule 12 motion
Answer B: Hear it because personal jurisdiction can be raised at any time
Answer C: Hear it because the defendant has not yet filed an answer
Answer D: Deny it only if the plaintiff proves minimum contacts
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Personal jurisdiction is one of the Rule 12(b)(2)-(5) defenses subject to the early consolidation and waiver rule. It had to be included in the first Rule 12 motion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Subject matter jurisdiction can be raised at any time. Personal jurisdiction is waivable and must be raised early. C: Filing a pre-answer Rule 12 motion triggered the consolidation rule. The defense was waived when omitted from that motion. D: The court need not reach minimum contacts if the defense was procedurally waived.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 12(h)(1) waives Rule 12(b)(2)-(5) defenses if they are omitted from a pre-answer Rule 12 motion or, if no motion is made, omitted from the responsive pleading.
