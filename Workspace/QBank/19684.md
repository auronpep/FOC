Outline: Commerce power
Question: A state bans imported nursery plants, citing pests, but allows in-state nurseries with the same pest risk to sell freely. The state could inspect all plants at distribution centers, and inspectors already do so for in-state plants. What is the best DCC analysis?
Answer A: The law is likely invalid because the local exemption and inspection alternative undercut the health justification
Answer B: The law is valid because a state may say "pests" and end the inquiry
Answer C: The law is valid because in-state businesses have a constitutional right to be preferred
Answer D: The law receives no review because plants are not commerce until sold by retailers
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule burdens only out-of-state commerce despite comparable local risks and available nondiscriminatory inspection.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The fit and alternatives matter when the law discriminates. C: Economic protectionism is a DCC danger sign. D: Nursery plants are articles of commerce.
lb_rule_reference: A state cannot save discriminatory commerce restrictions by invoking health when the law exempts comparable in-state risks or reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives exist.
