Question: A plaintiff sues two drivers in federal court for injuries from a three-car collision. One defendant wants to assert a claim against the other defendant for unpaid rent under an unrelated apartment lease. May the rent claim be asserted as a crossclaim in this action?
Answer A: Yes, because both defendants are co-parties
Answer B: Yes, because any claim between defendants is compulsory once they are joined
Answer C: No, because the rent claim is unrelated to the collision and does not involve the property at issue
Answer D: No, because defendants may never sue each other in the same federal action
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim is between co-parties, but it lacks the required connection to the collision or property in the action.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Co-party status is necessary but not sufficient; the crossclaim must arise from the same transaction or relate to the same property. B: Crossclaims are permissive and still require the Rule 13(g) relationship. D: Proper crossclaims between co-parties are allowed when the rule's relationship requirement is met.
lb_rule_reference: A crossclaim may be asserted against a co-party only if it arises out of the same transaction or occurrence as the original action or a counterclaim, or relates to property that is the subject of the action.
