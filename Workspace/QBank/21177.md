Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A consumer filed a state-court complaint asserting only state-law warranty claims against a manufacturer. The manufacturer removed to federal court, arguing that a federal safety statute preempts the warranty claims. The complaint itself does not assert a federal cause of action. Is removal proper on federal-question grounds?
Answer A: Yes, because a federal preemption defense always creates removal jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because defendants may remove whenever they plan to cite federal law
Answer C: No, because the complaint asserts only state-law warranty claims and federal law appears only as a defense
Answer D: No, because only plaintiffs can remove cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The well-pleaded complaint rule blocks removal based solely on the federal defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A federal defense ordinarily does not create federal-question removal jurisdiction. B: Removal requires original jurisdiction based on the plaintiff's claim, absent special complete-preemption facts not supplied. D: Defendants remove; the problem is lack of federal-question jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant cannot remove a state-law action solely because the defendant has a federal defense.
