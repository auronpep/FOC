Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A national appliance retailer sells a sealed-box toaster manufactured by another company. A wiring defect present when the toaster left the manufacturer causes a fire that injures the buyer. The retailer argues that it cannot face strict products liability because it never opened the box and did not know about the defect. Is that argument likely correct?
Answer A: Yes, because strict products liability applies only to manufacturers
Answer B: Yes, because a sealed box eliminates all strict-products liability for a retailer
Answer C: No, because the retailer commercially sold a defective product that caused physical injury
Answer D: No, because all sellers are liable for every accident involving a product
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A retailer in the business of selling the product can be in the strict-products chain even without creating or discovering the defect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Commercial sellers in the distribution chain can be covered. B: Lack of inspection may matter to indemnity or fault allocation, but it is not a categorical bar. D: The rule still requires commercial supplier status, defect, causation, and tort-type damages.
lb_rule_reference: A commercial retailer may be strictly liable for selling a defective product even if the product was sold in a sealed box and the retailer did not create the defect.
