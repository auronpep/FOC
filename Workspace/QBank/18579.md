Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: Employee sues Employer in federal court under a federal wage statute for unpaid overtime in 2026. In the same complaint, Employee adds a state-law claim that Employer negligently damaged Employee's parked car at a company picnic five years earlier. The two claims involve different events, witnesses, and proof. Does supplemental jurisdiction cover the car-damage claim merely because the same parties are involved?
Answer A: Yes, because the same plaintiff and defendant are on both claims
Answer B: Yes, because one federal claim in a complaint supports all state-law claims
Answer C: No, because the car-damage claim is factually unrelated to the federal overtime claim
Answer D: No, but only because the car-damage claim is based on state law
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Different events, proof, and witnesses indicate no common nucleus of operative fact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Same parties are not enough. The claims must share a common nucleus of operative fact. B: The state claim must be part of the same case or controversy. D: Related state-law claims can be supplemental; this one fails because it is unrelated.
lb_rule_reference: A state-law claim requires a common nucleus of operative fact with the original-jurisdiction claim; same parties alone do not satisfy Section 1367(a).
