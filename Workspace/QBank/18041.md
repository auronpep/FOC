Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Assault
Question: A prankster stepped from behind a parked car, held what appeared to be a real knife six inches from a pedestrian's chest, and said, "Do not move." The pedestrian reasonably believed the knife was real. It was actually a rubber prop. The pedestrian sues for assault. Is the prankster likely liable?
Answer A: Yes, because the prankster intentionally created reasonable apprehension of imminent offensive or harmful contact
Answer B: No, because the knife could not actually cut the pedestrian
Answer C: No, because assault requires actual contact
Answer D: Yes, but only if the prankster intended to stab the pedestrian
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Apparent ability and immediacy are enough even though the weapon was fake.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The test is reasonable apprehension based on apparent ability, not secret actual danger. C: Contact is the battery element. Assault protects against imminent apprehension. D: Intent to create apprehension is sufficient; intent to complete the contact is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Assault can exist when the defendant creates reasonable apprehension of imminent contact based on apparent ability, even if the weapon is fake.
