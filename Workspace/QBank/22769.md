


BARMATRIX Q#: 22769
subject_display: Civil Procedure
topic: Jurisdiction and Venue
subtopic: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
difficulty: 94010000
Stem / Fact Pattern: A poet sued a playwright in federal district court in State A, claiming the playwright copied a play written by the poet and produced it in a theater in State A. The poet claimed violations of both federal copyright law and State A unfair competition laws. The playwright moved to dismiss the unfair competition claim for lack of subject matter jurisdiction. Does the federal district court have jurisdiction over the state claim?
Answer A: A: Yes, because success on the federal claim is a prerequisite to success on the state claim.
Answer B: B: Yes, because both claims arose out of production of the play in State A.
Answer C: C: No, because the federal claim does not create pendent jurisdiction over the state claim.
Answer D: D: No, because the unfair competition claim is governed by State A substantive law.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: If jurisdiction is based on a question of federal law, the federal court will also have jurisdiction over state law claims arising from the same case or controversy. Hurn v. Oursler, 289 U.S. 238 (1933). Here, both the federal and state claims arise from the playwright putting on the play in State A. A is incorrect because it is unclear whether the federal claim is actually a prerequisite to the state claim. In any event, it would not matter since both claims arise out of the same case or controversy. C is incorrect because, as stated above, the federal court would have supplemental or pendent jurisdiction over the state law claim because it is part of the same controversy as the federal claim. D is incorrect because the mere fact a claim might be governed by state law does not remove that claim from potential federal jurisdiction.
