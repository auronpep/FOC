Question: An affidavit states that eight months ago an informant saw stolen phones in a suspect's apartment. The affidavit gives no facts suggesting the phones remained there, no ongoing theft operation, and no recent corroboration. A magistrate issues a warrant, and officers find phones in the apartment. What is the best challenge to the warrant?
Answer A: The probable cause was stale because the affidavit lacked current facts tying the phones to the apartment
Answer B: The warrant is valid because probable cause, once established, lasts forever
Answer C: The warrant is valid because a magistrate signed it
Answer D: The warrant is invalid only if the informant was lying
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Eight-month-old information about movable phones, without ongoing activity or recent corroboration, does not establish a current fair probability that evidence remains there.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Probable cause must support the search when the warrant issues. It can become stale. C: A signed warrant must still be supported by nonstale probable cause. A signature does not create missing facts. D: Even truthful information can be too stale to establish current probable cause.
lb_rule_reference: Information supporting a warrant can become stale when too much time passes without facts showing ongoing criminal activity or continued presence of evidence.
