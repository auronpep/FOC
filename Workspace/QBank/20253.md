MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A cafe negligently failed to salt an icy front step, creating a slipping risk. A customer saw the ice, decided not to enter, and walked to a different cafe two blocks away. At the other cafe, the customer ate spoiled soup and became ill. The customer sues the first cafe for the illness. Is the illness within the proximate-cause scope of the first cafe's breach?
Answer A: Yes, because the customer went to the second cafe only because of the icy step
Answer B: Yes, because all consequences of negligence are recoverable
Answer C: No, because food poisoning is outside the slipping risk created by the icy step
Answer D: No, because illness is never a compensable negligence injury
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The breach created a risk of falling on ice, not a risk of foodborne illness at a different cafe.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: But-for cause alone is not enough. Spoiled soup illness is outside the slip-and-fall risk that made the icy step negligent. B: Proximate cause limits liability to foreseeable risk types. D: Illness can be compensable when caused within the scope of liability. This illness is outside the risk.
lb_rule_reference: Proximate cause limits liability to the type of harm that made the defendant's conduct negligent.
