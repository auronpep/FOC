Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Hannah leased a small theater from Samuel to open a dinner show. Samuel breached by refusing access. Hannah had not previously operated a dinner show, but she had already sold 80% of the seats for the first month, had fixed menu costs from supplier contracts, and offered testimony from an experienced accountant using those contracts and comparable local venue data. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Hannah cannot recover lost profits because the dinner show was a new business.
Answer B: Hannah can recover the gross ticket receipts without subtracting meal costs.
Answer C: Hannah can recover only nominal damages because no business had opened yet.
Answer D: Hannah may recover lost net profits if the evidence proves them with reasonable certainty.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: New-business lost profits are not automatically barred. The question is whether the evidence provides a reasonably certain basis for net profit. Pre-sold seats, fixed cost contracts, and reliable comparable data may satisfy that standard.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it applies an outdated categorical bar. B: B is wrong because expectation damages use net profit, not gross receipts. C: C is wrong because pre-opening status does not automatically reduce recovery to nominal damages when profit proof is sufficiently reliable.
lb_rule_reference: Lost profits / new business with reliable proof
