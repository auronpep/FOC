Outline: Commerce power
Question: Congress regulates the production and sale of locally grown cannabis as part of a national scheme controlling the interstate drug market. A grower argues that her plants were grown, possessed, and consumed entirely within one state. What is Congress's strongest commerce-power argument?
Answer A: Local production and possession of a commodity can be regulated as part of a class of economic activity that substantially affects interstate commerce in the aggregate
Answer B: Congress may regulate because every local act, economic or not, can be aggregated
Answer C: Congress may regulate only if this grower's own plants crossed state lines
Answer D: Congress may regulate only by imposing a tax, never by prohibiting conduct
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Congress may regulate an economic class of activity as part of a comprehensive market-control scheme.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The aggregation route is strongest for economic activity; noneconomic local activity is harder. C: Substantial-effects aggregation can reach intrastate economic activity. D: Commerce power can support direct regulation of qualifying economic activity.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may regulate intrastate economic activity when, in the aggregate, the regulated class substantially affects interstate commerce.
