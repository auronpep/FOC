Question: Eve signed and recorded a grant giving a utility company “a perpetual easement to install, inspect, maintain, and replace electric lines across the north edge of Eve's farm.” The utility company owns no neighboring parcel that is benefited by the lines. What is the best classification of the utility company's interest?
Answer A: An easement appurtenant because the lines physically cross land.
Answer B: A revocable license because the utility company owns no dominant parcel.
Answer C: A real covenant because the utility company may maintain equipment.
Answer D: A commercial easement in gross because the right belongs to the utility company without a dominant parcel.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement in gross benefits a person or entity rather than a dominant parcel. A recorded utility easement that allows a utility company to install and maintain lines across land is a classic commercial easement in gross.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because physical crossing does not make an easement appurtenant; a dominant parcel must be benefited. B: B is wrong because the lack of a dominant parcel makes the right in gross, not automatically revocable. C: C is wrong because the grant creates a use right in land, not merely a promise about land.
lb_rule_reference: Commercial easement in gross
