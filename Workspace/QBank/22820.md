


BARMATRIX Q#: 22820
Outline_code: 95070302
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Joinder of parties
Question: A man wrecked his employer's truck in a parking garage. The man and his employer were citizens of State A. The parking garage was owned by a company in State B. The man sued the parking garage owner for negligence in federal court in State B, seeking $100,000 in personal injury damages. The employer filed a timely motion to intervene, asserting a $100,000 negligence claim against the parking garage owner for the damage to the truck. Is the court likely to grant the employer's motion?
Answer A: A: No, because the employer's claim will unduly delay the adjudication of the man's claim.
Answer B: B: No, because the man did not choose to include the employer in his claim.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the employer's claim shares common questions of law and facts with the man's claim.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the employer has an unconditional right to intervene by federal statute.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Fed. R. Civ. P. 24(b)(1)(b) states that the court may permit anyone to intervene who makes a timely motion and has a claim or defense that shares a common question of law or fact with the main action. Here, since both the man's claim and the employer's claim would share common questions of law and fact related to the truck accident, the court is likely to grant the employer's motion to intervene. A is incorrect because there is no indication that the employer's claim will unduly delay the adjudication of the man's claim. B is incorrect because the employer can still intervene even if the man did not choose to include the employer in his claim. D is incorrect because there is no indication the employer had an unconditional right to intervene.
