Question: A small shop contracted to sell its only antique lamp to Deborah for $5,000. Deborah wrongfully repudiated. The shop resold that same lamp to Nathan for $5,000 two days later. The shop had no other lamp of that kind and could not have made both sales. What is the best damages analysis?
Answer A: The shop is automatically a lost-volume seller because it resold at the same price.
Answer B: The resale appears to substitute for the lost sale, so the shop generally has no lost-profit damages from Deborah apart from any proper incidental damages.
Answer C: The shop may recover the full $5,000 price from Deborah and keep the resale proceeds.
Answer D: The shop must pay Deborah the resale proceeds because the resale was profitable.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Lost-volume damages require that the seller would have made the second sale in addition to the first. A seller with only one unique item and no ability to make both sales generally has the resale as a substitute, not an additional lost volume sale. The shop may still recover proper incidental damages, but not lost-volume profit on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because same-price resale does not itself establish lost-volume status. C: C is wrong because the action for price is limited and would overcompensate the seller. D: D is wrong because the defaulting buyer is not entitled to resale proceeds as a profit account in this ordinary seller-resale posture.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-708(2)
