Question: Abigail sues Eli for fraud. Eli objects to Abigail's testimony about a conversation between Eli and his lawyer, claiming privilege. Abigail responds that the conversation occurred in a crowded restaurant where several strangers could hear. After hearing the evidence outside the jury's presence, the judge finds no privilege and admits the testimony. Eli asks the judge to instruct the jury that it may disregard the testimony if it believes the conversation was privileged. How should the court rule on the requested instruction?
Answer A: Give the instruction, because the jury always decides privilege after hearing the disputed communication.
Answer B: Refuse the instruction, because privilege is a preliminary admissibility matter for the judge, not a jury issue after admission.
Answer C: Give the instruction only in a criminal case, because juries decide privilege in criminal trials but not civil trials.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony automatically because any communication with a lawyer is privileged.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Whether a privilege exists is a preliminary question for the court. Once the judge decides the privilege issue and admits the evidence, the jury does not decide whether the communication was privileged.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because privilege is not submitted to the jury as a factual issue after admission. C: C is wrong because this is not a civil/criminal instruction distinction. D: D is wrong because attorney involvement alone does not establish privilege; the judge decides the privilege foundation.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a) / privilege preliminary fact / no jury instruction
