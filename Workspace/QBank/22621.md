Outline_code: 42010202
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Chief executive
Question: The Postmaster General refused to pay several contractors who had built a new post office in State A. The contractors presented a request to Congress, and Congress passed an act and appropriated funds to pay the contractors. The Postmaster then went to the President and asked him to withhold the funds because the Postmaster was unsatisfied with the contractor's work. The President agreed to withhold the funds and directed the government not to pay the contractors until the Postmaster General was satisfied. May the President lawfully withhold the funds?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the Postmaster General is subject to the direction and control of the President.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the President has ultimate power over internal affairs.
Answer C: C: No, because Congress has the exclusive power to pay government debts.
Answer D: D: No, because the President is violating the Take Care Clause.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The Take Care Clause of the Constitution, Article II, Section 3, Clause 4, provides that the President “shall take care that the laws be faithfully executed.” Consequently, if Congress expressly mandates that funds be spent, the President has no power to refuse to do so. Kendall v. United States ex rel. Stokes, 37 U.S. 524 (1838). Here, the President is violating the Clause by withholding the appropriated funds. A is incorrect because the issue here is not the President's power over the Postmaster General. B is incorrect because the President does not have complete control over all internal affairs. C is incorrect because Congress does not have the exclusive power to pay government debts.
