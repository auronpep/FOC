MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A blind pedestrian used a cane and audible-crossing signal to cross a street. Halfway across, the signal changed unexpectedly because of a malfunction. The pedestrian paused, listened, and continued when traffic remained stopped. A driver then ran the red light and hit him. The driver argues the pedestrian should be judged as if he had ordinary sight. What standard applies to the pedestrian's conduct?
Answer A: The care of a reasonably prudent person with the same blindness under the circumstances
Answer B: The care of a person with ordinary sight
Answer C: The pedestrian's personal belief about what was safe
Answer D: No standard applies because physical disability is complete immunity
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The standard remains objective but accounts for relevant physical disability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Objective does not mean ignoring physical disability. C: The standard is not purely subjective; it asks what a reasonably prudent person with the same disability would do. D: Physical disability affects the standard but does not erase the negligence inquiry.
lb_rule_reference: Physical disability is considered in framing the objective reasonable-person standard.
