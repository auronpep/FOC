Question: Peter excavated on his own land next to Mary's improved lot. Mary's land in its natural condition would not have collapsed from the excavation, but the added weight of Mary's house contributed to subsidence and caused the house to crack. Mary sues Peter for damage to the house. No statute applies. What must Mary generally prove to recover for the damage to the house?
Answer A: Only that Peter excavated and the house was damaged, because support liability is always strict.
Answer B: Only that the excavation made Mary's house less valuable, because nuisance replaces support rules.
Answer C: That Mary had an express easement for support in Peter's land.
Answer D: That Peter was negligent in excavating.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A landowner has a right to lateral support for land in its natural condition. Strict liability generally applies when excavation causes land in its natural state to subside. But when the damage is attributable to the added weight of buildings or improvements, the plaintiff generally must prove negligence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it overextends strict support liability to improvements. B: B is wrong because nuisance does not replace the lateral-support analysis. C: C is wrong because the lateral-support right does not depend on an express easement.
lb_rule_reference: Lateral support / improved land / negligence proof
