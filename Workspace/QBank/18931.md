Question: David conveyed Millacre “to Mary and her heirs, but if John marries before John turns 30, then to John and his heirs.” John is 24 and unmarried. How should John's future interest be classified?
Answer A: A springing executory interest, because John must marry before taking.
Answer B: A contingent remainder, because John's right depends on a condition precedent.
Answer C: A shifting executory interest, because it would divest Mary's present fee simple estate.
Answer D: A possibility of reverter, because the property might leave Mary automatically.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: John has a shifting executory interest. If John marries before age 30, his interest will cut short Mary's present fee simple estate. A future interest in a third party that divests a prior grantee is shifting, not springing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a springing executory interest divests the grantor, not a prior grantee. B: B is wrong because no life estate or other supporting estate creates a remainder. D: D is wrong because a possibility of reverter is retained by a grantor after a fee simple determinable.
lb_rule_reference: Shifting executory interest / divests grantee
