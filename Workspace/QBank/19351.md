Question: Moses contracted to build a retaining wall for Rachel for $70,000. Rachel paid $50,000 and withheld the final $20,000. The wall was completed, but it departed from the contract specifications and was unsafe. The reasonable cost to correct the defect is $18,000. The defect reduces the market value of Rachel's land by only $5,000. Moses sues for the unpaid balance. What is the proper result?
Answer A: Moses recovers $20,000 because the wall increased the land's overall value.
Answer B: Moses recovers $15,000 because damages are limited to the $5,000 diminution in market value.
Answer C: Moses recovers nothing because any nonconforming construction bars contract recovery.
Answer D: Moses recovers $2,000 because Rachel may offset the $18,000 reasonable repair cost against the $20,000 unpaid balance.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under common law, with Rachel's repair damages used as an offset against the unpaid price. Because the defect makes the wall unsafe, the proper loss category is repair cost rather than mere diminution, and the economic-waste limit does not force the lower $5,000 figure. The formula is $20,000 unpaid balance − $18,000 repair-cost offset = $2,000. The result scope is offset, and the answer does not decide rescission or restitution.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores the repair-cost offset. B: B is wrong because it uses WAL-REM-024, an economic-waste overread; safety defects can justify repair cost. C: C is wrong because it uses an all-or-nothing breach punishment rather than expectation with setoff.
lb_rule_reference: Common law construction defects — repair-cost damages when repair is necessary and not economic waste
