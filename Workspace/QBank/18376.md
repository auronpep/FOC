Question: Detectives asked Lena to come to the police station to answer questions about a store robbery. Lena drove herself there. The detectives told her she was not under arrest and could leave whenever she wanted. The interview room door was unlocked. After forty minutes of questioning, Lena admitted that she drove the getaway car. She then left the station. No Miranda warnings were given. Should Lena's admission be suppressed under Miranda?
Answer A: Yes, because questioning at a police station is inherently custodial
Answer B: Yes, because detectives asked questions designed to elicit an incriminating response
Answer C: No, because Lena was not in custody during the voluntary interview
Answer D: No, because Miranda applies only after formal charges are filed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Miranda did not apply because Lena was not subjected to restraint comparable to formal arrest. The voluntary arrival, free-to-leave statement, unlocked room, and actual departure all point away from custody.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Custody is objective and depends on restraint comparable to formal arrest. Lena came voluntarily, was told she could leave, sat in an unlocked room, and did leave afterward. B: Interrogation matters only if custody is also present. Miranda requires both custody and interrogation. D: Miranda can apply before charges if there is custodial interrogation. The reason it does not apply here is lack of custody, not absence of charges.
lb_rule_reference: Miranda warnings are required only for custodial interrogation; a voluntary station interview with no restraint comparable to formal arrest is not custody.
