Outline: Tenth Amendment
Question: Congress orders county sheriffs to conduct background checks for a new federal licensing program and to report results to a federal agency. The statute does not regulate private applicants directly; it assigns the administrative work to state officers. What is the strongest Tenth Amendment objection?
Answer A: Congress may not commandeer state executive officers to administer a federal regulatory program
Answer B: The statute is valid because federal law is always allowed to give orders to state officials
Answer C: The statute is invalid only if sheriffs object for religious reasons
Answer D: The statute is valid only because counties are not states
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The statute commands state officers to carry out federal administration.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Supremacy does not permit commandeering state executives. C: Anti-commandeering protects state structure regardless of religious objection. D: State and local executive officers cannot be conscripted to administer federal programs in this way.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may not commandeer state executive officers by requiring them to administer or enforce a federal regulatory program.
