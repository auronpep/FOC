Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Zechariah promised in a signed agreement to finance Elizabeth's new orchard business. In reliance, Elizabeth demolished an old shed for $25,000 and graded the land for $10,000. The work increased the land's market value by $12,000. Zechariah then repudiated. Elizabeth cannot prove future orchard profits with reasonable certainty. What is the best reliance measure?
Answer A: $35,000, because all reliance expenditures are recoverable without offset.
Answer B: $23,000, the $35,000 expenditures minus the $12,000 retained land-value increase.
Answer C: The speculative future orchard profits, because profit was the purpose of the agreement.
Answer D: Nothing, because future profits are uncertain.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages compensate expenditures caused by the promise, but they are adjusted for value retained to avoid overcompensation. Elizabeth spent $35,000 and retained a $12,000 land-value increase, leaving $23,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores the retained benefit. C: C is wrong because the future profits cannot be recovered without reasonable certainty. D: D is wrong because uncertain profits do not defeat proven reliance damages.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance with retained property value offset
