Outline: Arson
Question: During a dispute with his upstairs neighbor, Grant pours gasoline in the common hallway of their apartment building and lights it to "scare everyone." He says he did not specifically want the building to burn. The flames char the wooden stair rail before firefighters arrive. Is Grant's lack of a purpose to burn the building a complete defense to common-law arson?
Answer A: Yes, because arson requires a conscious purpose to burn the structure
Answer B: Yes, because Grant only meant to scare people
Answer C: No, because reckless disregard of the risk that the dwelling would burn can satisfy malice
Answer D: No, but only because someone was inside the building
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Grant intentionally set gasoline on fire in an apartment hallway, creating an obvious risk to the structure. The stair rail actually charred. Purpose to burn the building is not required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Malice can include reckless disregard of an obvious risk that the structure will burn. B: A benign or different motive does not defeat reckless malice when he intentionally set a dangerous fire in a building. D: The arson issue turns on malicious burning of the protected structure. Occupants are not a separate required element in this question.
lb_rule_reference: Malicious burning for arson can include acting with reckless disregard of an obvious risk that the protected structure will burn.
