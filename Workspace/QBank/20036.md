MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Self-defense
Question: Eli starts a fistfight by punching Grant. After several punches, Eli silently steps back because he decides the fight is foolish. Grant, still unarmed, steps forward with fists raised. Eli immediately hits Grant in the face with a heavy bottle, breaking Grant's jaw. Eli is charged with aggravated battery. Can Eli rely on self-defense?
Answer A: Yes, because Eli subjectively decided he was done fighting
Answer B: Yes, because Grant stepped forward after Eli stepped back
Answer C: No, because Eli did not communicate withdrawal and Grant did not escalate to deadly force
Answer D: No, because an initial aggressor can never regain self-defense
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Eli began the fight. To regain self-defense, he needed to withdraw and communicate that withdrawal, or face a deadly escalation by Grant. Grant remained unarmed and used only fists. Eli's bottle strike was not justified.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: An initial aggressor must withdraw and communicate that withdrawal. A silent private decision does not give the other party notice that the aggression has ended. B: Grant did not escalate to deadly force, and Eli had not communicated withdrawal. Eli remained the initial aggressor for self-defense purposes. D: Initial aggressors can regain self-defense through communicated withdrawal or by facing deadly escalation. Those facts are simply missing here.
lb_rule_reference: An initial aggressor regains self-defense by completely withdrawing and communicating withdrawal, unless the other party escalates to deadly force
