Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Identifications
Question: A witness saw a masked robber for about two seconds at night from across a parking lot. Six weeks later, police showed the witness a single photograph and said, "This is the person we caught." The witness hesitated, then identified the person in the photograph. The defendant moves to suppress the identification. Should the identification be suppressed?
Answer A: Yes, because the police-arranged single-photo procedure was suggestive and the reliability factors are weak
Answer B: No, because witness identifications are always for the jury to weigh
Answer C: No, because the witness eventually identified the defendant
Answer D: Yes, because every single-photo display is automatically excluded
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The procedure was highly suggestive, and the witness had a brief distant view, poor conditions, a long delay, and hesitation. Those facts create a substantial likelihood of misidentification.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Due process can require suppression when police-arranged suggestiveness and unreliability create a substantial likelihood of misidentification. C: The question is whether that identification is reliable enough after a suggestive police procedure. Hesitation and weak observation undermine reliability. D: Suppression is not automatic. This identification is suppressed because suggestiveness combines with weak reliability.
lb_rule_reference: A police-arranged identification violates due process when it is unnecessarily suggestive and creates a substantial likelihood of irreparable misidentification.
