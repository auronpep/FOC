Outline: Liquidated damages
Question: Joseph agreed to buy standard refrigerators from Hannah, a merchant seller, for $30,000 and paid an $8,000 deposit. The contract stated that if Joseph canceled for any reason, Hannah could keep the entire deposit as liquidated damages. At contracting, cancellation damages for this standard inventory were readily measurable by resale or market price, and the $8,000 forfeiture was unreasonably large. Joseph repudiated. Hannah resold the refrigerators and established $1,600 in Article 2 damages. Under UCC §2-718, how much of the deposit should Joseph recover?
Answer A: $5,900, because the invalid forfeiture is replaced by the $500 statutory retention amount plus Hannah's $1,600 damages offset.
Answer B: Nothing, because the contract says the entire deposit is forfeited on cancellation.
Answer C: $6,400, because Hannah may retain only her $1,600 actual damages.
Answer D: $7,500, because Hannah may retain only the $500 statutory amount and no additional damages.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is restitution of a buyer deposit under UCC goods law, subject to seller offsets. Joseph is the buyer, Hannah is the merchant seller, and the refrigerators are standard goods. The penalty gate invalidates the full-deposit forfeiture because damages were readily measurable and the amount was unreasonably large. With no valid liquidated amount, UCC §2-718 allows statutory retention of the lesser of 20% of $30,000 or $500; the statutory amount is $500. Hannah also offsets $1,600 in established Article 2 damages. The formula is $8,000 − $500 − $1,600 = $5,900 returned. The result scope is offset restitution.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because an unreasonable forfeiture clause is a penalty and cannot be enforced merely because it appears in the contract, triggering WAL-REM-015. C: C is wrong because it omits the statutory retention amount. D: D is wrong because it applies the statutory cap while ignoring seller damages, the WAL-REM-016 shortcut.
