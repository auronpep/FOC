Outline: Procedural due process
Question: A city runs a discretionary grant program for artists. The ordinance says the arts director "may award grants to applicants whose projects advance the public interest." An artist applies and is denied without a hearing. The artist argues that due process required notice and a hearing because she expected to receive a grant based on prior awards. What is the city's strongest procedural due process response?
Answer A: The artist first must show a protected property or liberty interest, and a mere expectation of a discretionary grant is not enough
Answer B: The artist automatically gets a hearing because every denial of government money triggers strict scrutiny
Answer C: The artist wins because Mathews balancing always applies before any protected interest is identified
Answer D: The artist loses only if the city proves the grant denial was economically efficient
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Procedural due process analysis starts with a protected interest; discretionary hope is not a property entitlement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Procedural due process is not strict scrutiny, and no process is due without a protected interest. C: Mathews balances process only after a protected interest is found. D: Efficiency is not the threshold question; the protected-interest gate controls first.
lb_rule_reference: Procedural due process begins by asking whether the government deprived a person of life, liberty, or property; a unilateral hope for a government benefit is not enough unless law or rules create an entitlement.
