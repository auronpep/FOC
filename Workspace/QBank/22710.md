MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Fundamental rights
Question: A state legislature was concerned about voter fraud in its local elections. Consequently, the state legislature passed a law requiring that all voters in state and local elections live within the state for at least one year before voting. The law was aimed at specific allegations that citizens of a neighboring state had moved to the state for a short time for the sole purpose of voting in the last election. A woman moved into the state. Six months later, the woman went to vote in her local elections and was turned away at the door. The woman sued, arguing that the new law was unconstitutional. Should the court rule in the woman's favor?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the one- year residency requirement is not narrowly tailored to satisfy strict scrutiny.
Answer B: B: Yes, because preventing voter fraud is not a compelling state interest.
Answer C: C: No, because the one- year residency requirement is rationally related to preventing voter fraud.
Answer D: D: No, because the one- year residency requirement is necessary to achieve a compelling state interest.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Voting is a fundamental right, and durational residency requirements of over one year have been found unconstitutional because they are not narrowly tailored to satisfy strict scrutiny. B is incorrect because preventing voter fraud is a compelling state interest. The problem with the new law is that a one- year durational residency requirement is not narrowly tailored to prevent voter fraud and instead unnecessarily infringes on new state residents' right to vote for a significant amount of time. C and D are incorrect because the one- year residency voting would not pass under strict scrutiny analysis.
