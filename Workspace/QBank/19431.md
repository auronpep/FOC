Question: A district court enters a standard 14-day temporary restraining order to preserve the status quo until a preliminary-injunction hearing. The restrained party seeks immediate appeal solely because the order restricts its conduct. Is the TRO ordinarily immediately appealable?
Answer A: Yes, because every order restricting conduct is appealable
Answer B: Yes, because final judgment has been entered
Answer C: No, standard TROs are not ordinarily immediately appealable absent features making them function like preliminary injunctions
Answer D: No, because injunction-related orders are never appealable
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Ordinary short-term TROs usually await later review.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: TROs and preliminary injunctions are not identical for appeal purposes. B: A TRO does not end the litigation. D: Preliminary-injunction orders are immediately appealable.
lb_rule_reference: TROs are generally short-term emergency orders; appealability may change only when the order functions like a preliminary injunction.
