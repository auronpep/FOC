Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Ezra owned a cottage. He signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey the cottage to my family.” At the time, Ezra had a wife, Abigail, two children, James and John, a brother, Nehemiah, and a cousin, Esther. The deed gave no method for deciding which relatives were included. Ezra privately meant only Abigail and the children. Who owns the cottage?
Answer A: Ezra, because the deed does not identify the grantees with reasonable certainty.
Answer B: Abigail, James, and John, because Ezra privately intended only them.
Answer C: All of Ezra's relatives, because the word “family” automatically includes every blood relative.
Answer D: Abigail alone, because a spouse takes priority over other relatives.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is ascertainable grantee identity. A class description must identify the takers with reasonable certainty. “My family” is vague on these facts because several relatives could be included and the deed gives no selection rule. Ezra's private intent cannot cure the uncertainty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because private intent does not identify the deed's grantees. C: C is wrong because the deed does not define family to include every relative. D: D is wrong because spousal status does not supply a missing grantee selection rule.
lb_rule_reference: Vague class description / private purpose cannot cure
