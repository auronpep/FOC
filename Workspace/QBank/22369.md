


BARMATRIX Q#: 22369
Outline_code: 75070000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: While patrolling a dangerous neighborhood at night, a police officer decided to stop a car and check if the driver had proper license and registration. A woman was driving the car. As the officer approached the woman along the driver's side of the vehicle, the officer shined his flashlight into the car and saw a clear plastic bag full of marijuana sitting on the passenger seat. The officer arrested the woman, who was later charged with possessing drugs with the intent to sell. At trial, the woman moved to suppress the marijuana. Should the court suppress the marijuana?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the traffic stop was unlawful.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the officer used his flashlight to look into the car.
Answer C: C: No, because the marijuana was in plain view.
Answer D: D: No, because the officer was patrolling a dangerous neighborhood at night.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A random stop of a vehicle is unconstitutional. Here, the officer stopped the car without any suspicion of wrongdoing. Evidence discovered as a result of a constitutional violation cannot be admitted into evidence under the fruits of the poisonous tree doctrine. Consequently, because the stop was unlawful, the marijuana discovered as a result of that stop cannot be admitted. B is incorrect because the fact the officer used his flashlight to see into the car would not change the analysis if the stop had been lawful. C is incorrect because the plain view doctrine would only allow the admittance of the marijuana if the stop had been lawful. D is incorrect because the fact the officer was patrolling a dangerous neighborhood at night would not justify the random stop.
