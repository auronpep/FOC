Question: During a federal bribery trial, a seated juror named Nathan tells the judge that he saw Levi hand cash to a witness during a break. The prosecutor asks to call Nathan immediately as a witness in front of the other jurors and then return him to the jury box. Levi objects. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Allow Nathan to testify before the other jurors because he has personal knowledge.
Answer B: Allow Nathan to testify before the other jurors only if Levi cross-examines him.
Answer C: Not allow Nathan to testify as a witness before the other jurors at the trial; if the issue is pursued, the court must handle any objection outside the jury's presence.
Answer D: Exclude all evidence of the cash transfer because a juror can never report misconduct observed during trial.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A juror may not testify as a witness before the other jurors at the trial. If a juror is called to testify, the court must give a party an opportunity to object outside the jury's presence. The problem is not lack of perception; it is the juror's role in the trial.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because personal knowledge does not permit a seated juror to testify before the other jurors. B: B is wrong because cross-examination does not solve the role problem. D: D is wrong because the rule does not make all evidence of observed misconduct inadmissible; it controls the juror-as-witness channel.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 606(a) / juror may not testify before other jurors at trial
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the juror-witness role rule and separates role competency from personal knowledge.
