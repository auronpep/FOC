Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Assault
Question: A collector angrily pointed an antique pistol at a neighbor from across a small porch and said, "Step closer and I will shoot." The neighbor reasonably believed the pistol was loaded and that the collector could fire immediately. In fact, the pistol had no firing pin, but the neighbor did not know that. The neighbor sues for assault. Is the collector likely liable for assault?
Answer A: No, because the pistol could not actually fire
Answer B: No, because the neighbor was not physically touched
Answer C: Yes, because the collector intentionally caused reasonable apprehension of imminent harmful contact
Answer D: Yes, but only if the neighbor later learned the gun was defective
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The collector pointed what appeared to be a working gun and threatened immediate shooting. The neighbor's apprehension was reasonable despite the unknown defect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Assault can rest on apparent ability when the plaintiff reasonably apprehends imminent contact. B: Assault does not require actual contact. D: Assault is measured by the apprehension at the time of the act, not by later discovery.
lb_rule_reference: Assault can exist when the plaintiff reasonably apprehends imminent contact based on apparent ability, even if the defendant secretly lacks actual ability.
