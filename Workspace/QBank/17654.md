Question: Deborah's parcel was landlocked when it was severed from Jonah's parcel, so Deborah obtained an easement by necessity over Jonah's road. Years later, the county built a public road directly along Deborah's parcel, giving Deborah legal and practical access to the parcel. Deborah still prefers Jonah's road because it is shorter. Does Deborah's easement by necessity continue solely because Jonah's road is more convenient?
Answer A: Yes, because an easement by necessity is permanent once created.
Answer B: Yes, because the shortest route is always necessary.
Answer C: No, because an easement by necessity generally ends when the necessity ceases, and convenience alone is insufficient.
Answer D: No, because all implied easements terminate whenever a public road exists anywhere in the county.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement by necessity is based on strict necessity for access. It generally lasts only as long as the necessity exists. Once a public road gives Deborah legal and practical access, the necessity ceases; the shorter route is merely more convenient.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because necessity easements are not necessarily permanent. B: B is wrong because convenience is not strict necessity. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because the relevant question is whether this parcel now has adequate access, not whether a public road exists somewhere else.
lb_rule_reference: Easement by necessity / termination when necessity ceases
lb_student_diagnostic: The repair signal is treating necessity as a continuing condition, not a one-time label.
