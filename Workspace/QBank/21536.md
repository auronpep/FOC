Question: In a federal trespass trial, Sarah testifies that she heard a truck engine and saw headlights from her porch at night. Sarah is asked whether the truck sounded “very close” when the headlights came over the hill. The defendant objects that Sarah cannot state an exact distance. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection if Sarah's estimate is rationally based on her perception and helpful to the jury.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because a lay witness must state measurements precisely.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because sound impressions are never admissible.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Sarah measured the distance the next morning.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Lay opinion may include ordinary estimates of distance, sound, light, or appearance when based on perception and helpful. Sarah's inability to give an exact measurement affects weight, not admissibility.
