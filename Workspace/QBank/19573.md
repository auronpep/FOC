Question: Samuel agreed to buy Mary's warehouse if a structural engineer approved the roof by Friday. Mary promised to give the engineer access. The engineer arrived on Thursday, but Mary refused to unlock the warehouse because she had received a better offer. On Friday, Mary refused to sell, saying the roof-approval condition never occurred. What is the best result?
Answer A: Mary may avoid the contract because the condition literally did not occur.
Answer B: Mary may avoid the contract unless Samuel proves the roof actually would have passed inspection.
Answer C: Mary may avoid the contract because conditions are never excused.
Answer D: Mary may not rely on the nonoccurrence because she prevented the condition from occurring.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A party who wrongfully prevents or hinders the occurrence of a condition generally cannot rely on the condition's nonoccurrence. Mary promised access, refused access to pursue a better offer, and caused the approval condition not to occur. The condition is excused against her.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because literal nonoccurrence does not help the party who prevented occurrence. B: B is wrong because prevention is the decisive route; Samuel need not conclusively prove the engineer would have approved. C: C is wrong because conditions may be excused by prevention, waiver, or other doctrines.
lb_rule_reference: Prevention doctrine / condition excused
