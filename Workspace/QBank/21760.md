Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Bias
Question: A former co-defendant settles with the plaintiff and agrees to testify for the plaintiff at trial. The defendant seeks to cross-examine the witness about the settlement agreement, not to prove liability or the amount of the plaintiff's claim, but to show the witness's possible bias and motive to favor the plaintiff. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Bar all questions because Rule 408 excludes every reference to settlement.
Answer B: Allow the questions as substantive proof that the defendant is liable.
Answer C: Bar the questions unless the witness denies bias first.
Answer D: Allow the questions for the limited purpose of showing bias or prejudice.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 408 excludes compromise evidence for validity, amount, and certain impeachment-by-contradiction uses, but it permits settlement evidence for another purpose such as proving a witness's bias or prejudice. The evidence should be limited to that purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is settlement absolutism. Rule 408 has permitted purposes. B: B is wrong because The settlement cannot be used as substantive proof of liability. C: C is wrong because Bias cross-examination does not require a prior denial by the witness as a condition to asking.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 408(b) / bias permitted purpose
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats settlement as a categorical evidence ban rather than a purpose-specific exclusion.
