Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: A customer sues a bank in federal court under a federal credit-reporting statute based on a January credit denial. In the same complaint, she adds a state-law slip-and-fall claim from a July accident in the bank's parking lot. The two events involve different employees, different evidence, and different injuries. Does supplemental jurisdiction cover the slip-and-fall claim?
Answer A: Yes, because both claims are against the same bank
Answer B: No, because the slip-and-fall claim does not share a common nucleus of operative fact with the federal credit claim
Answer C: Yes, because Rule 18 permits a plaintiff to join unrelated claims
Answer D: No, because state tort claims can never be heard in federal court
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The claims arise from separate events and would not ordinarily be tried together.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Same parties do not replace the common-nucleus requirement. C: Joinder permission does not create subject matter jurisdiction. D: State claims can be heard under diversity or supplemental jurisdiction when requirements are met.
lb_rule_reference: Section 1367(a) requires claims to form part of the same Article III case or controversy, usually a common nucleus of operative fact.
