Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A museum charged admission to a special exhibit. For several hours, a floor tile at the exhibit entrance rocked loose whenever visitors stepped on it. The museum had no inspection schedule for the entrance. A visitor tripped on the tile. What duty did the museum owe the visitor?
Answer A: Reasonable care, including reasonable inspection for hazards it should discover
Answer B: Only a duty to warn about hazards it actually knew about
Answer C: No duty because the museum did not create the loose tile
Answer D: Strict liability for any injury on the premises
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A paying visitor is an invitee. The museum owed reasonable care, including inspection for discoverable entrance hazards.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Invitees receive the broader reasonable-inspection duty. C: An invitee duty can include discovering hazards even if the possessor did not create them. D: Invitee status creates a duty of reasonable care, not automatic liability.
lb_rule_reference: A possessor owes invitees reasonable care, including reasonable inspection for hazards the possessor should discover.
