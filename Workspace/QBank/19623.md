Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Martha sues a mill owner, Abigail, claiming a grain press malfunctioned because a gear was worn. Martha offers testimony that the same press jammed twice during the week after Martha's injury. Abigail objects that the later jams do not prove the gear was worn at the time of the accident. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence because later conditions can never show earlier conditions.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence unless Martha proves the press was unchanged after the accident.
Answer C: Admit the evidence because later similar malfunctions may have some tendency to show an ongoing condition; differences in timing and condition affect weight.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if Abigail admits the same gear caused the later jams.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Later similar malfunctions can have probative value if they tend to show a continuing condition. They do not conclusively prove the condition existed at the accident time, but that weakness affects weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because later condition evidence can sometimes support an inference about earlier condition. B: B is wrong because unchanged condition proof may be important, but threshold relevance does not always require conclusive proof before admission. D: D is wrong because Abigail's admission is not required for probative value.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401; FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats timing weakness as a categorical relevance bar.
