Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: An officer stopped a sedan for speeding. The driver, Malik, was alone in the car and had the keys. The car had been stolen the day before, and Malik knew it was stolen. Without a warrant exception, the officer searched the glove compartment and found stolen credit cards. Malik moves to suppress the cards as the product of the car search. Does Malik have standing to challenge the glove-compartment search?
Answer A: Yes, because Malik had physical possession of the car and the keys
Answer B: Yes, because every driver has standing to challenge any car search
Answer C: No, because Malik knowingly drove a stolen car and lacked a legitimate privacy interest in it
Answer D: No, because Fourth Amendment standing is available only to titled owners
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Fourth Amendment standing requires the defendant's own legitimate privacy interest. Knowing wrongful possession of a stolen car does not create that interest in the glove compartment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Possession must be lawful enough to create a reasonable expectation of privacy. Malik knowingly possessed a stolen car. B: A driver of a stolen car generally lacks a legitimate privacy expectation in the car. D: Titled ownership is not required in every case; lawful borrowers can have standing. Malik loses because his possession was wrongful.
lb_rule_reference: Lawful possession can support standing, but wrongful possession of stolen property generally does not create a protected privacy interest.
