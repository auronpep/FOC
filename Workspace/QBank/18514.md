Question: A state legislature redraws its congressional districts after a new census. One newly drawn district has an unusual, elongated shape that connects two geographically distant Black communities along a narrow corridor. The district is 72% Black in population. The legislature's stated purpose is to create a majority-minority district to provide Black voters an effective opportunity to elect a representative of their choice, in compliance with the Voting Rights Act. A white resident who is placed in the majority-minority district challenges it as a racial gerrymander under the Equal Protection Clause. Under Equal Protection analysis, which of the following correctly identifies the standard and key issue for the racial gerrymander claim?
Answer A: Rational basis, because creating majority-minority districts serves the legitimate purpose of compliance with the Voting Rights Act, and VRA compliance is a well-established legislative goal
Answer B: Strict scrutiny applies, and the district is automatically unconstitutional because it was drawn to create a majority-minority district, which is categorically prohibited racial gerrymandering
Answer C: Strict scrutiny applies because race was the predominant factor; the district survives only if VRA compliance is a compelling interest supported by a strong basis in evidence, and the specific district configuration is narrowly tailored to serve that interest
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies, but the district is constitutional because the legislature's explicit racial purpose was benign — designed to help Black voters, not to harm them — and benign racial classifications receive more permissive review under strict scrutiny
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Miller v. Johnson and Shaw v. Reno, racial gerrymandering — where race is the predominant factor in drawing district lines — triggers strict scrutiny. VRA compliance can constitute a compelling interest, but only if the state had a strong basis in evidence that the VRA actually required the racial classification. Narrow tailoring requires that the district be drawn no more broadly than necessary to comply with the VRA's requirements.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: When race is the predominant factor in drawing a district's boundaries, strict scrutiny applies regardless of the legislative purpose. VRA compliance is a potential compelling interest, but the mere invocation of the VRA does not lower the standard of review from strict scrutiny. The Court applies strict scrutiny to racial gerrymanders even when the stated purpose is VRA compliance. B: Majority-minority districts are not categorically prohibited. Creating a majority-minority district as a remedy for proven VRA violations can constitute a compelling interest, and some race-conscious districting can survive strict scrutiny if narrowly tailored. The Court applies the two-pronged strict scrutiny test; it does not categorically prohibit majority-minority districts. D: The Supreme Court has rejected the benign-classification distinction for race. All racial classifications, regardless of purpose or direction, receive strict scrutiny and must satisfy the same two-pronged test. There is no more lenient "benign strict scrutiny" for pro-minority racial classifications.
lb_rule_reference: When race is the predominant factor in drawing a legislative district's boundaries, the district is a racial gerrymander subject to strict scrutiny under the Equal Protection Clause; VRA compliance is a compelling interest only if the state has a strong basis in evidence that the VRA requires the race-based districting.
