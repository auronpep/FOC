Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Hannah sues Elijah after a collision on a rural road. Elijah claims fog made visibility poor. Elijah offers a weather-station report from an airport three miles from the collision site showing dense fog at the time of the collision. Hannah objects that the report is irrelevant because the fog at the airport might not have matched the road conditions exactly. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the report because it does not prove the exact visibility at the collision site.
Answer B: Admit the report because nearby fog data has some tendency to make poor visibility at the road more probable.
Answer C: Exclude the report unless Elijah also produces a witness who was on the road.
Answer D: Admit the report only if the airport and road have identical elevation.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Weather data from a nearby location can make the condition at the accident site more probable, even if differences in location or elevation affect weight. The proof is not conclusive, but it is probative.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevance does not require exact proof of the ultimate fact. C: C is wrong because a road eyewitness is not required before nearby weather evidence is relevant. D: D is wrong because identical elevation may strengthen the inference, but it is not a prerequisite to relevance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Demanding exact duplication rather than recognizing a reasonable probative link.
