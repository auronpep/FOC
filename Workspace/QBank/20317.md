Question: Congress creates a federal civil remedy for victims of a local violent assault, relying on findings that violence imposes nationwide economic costs. The statute contains no jurisdictional element and does not regulate commercial conduct. What is the strongest objection?
Answer A: Aggregate economic costs alone do not turn noneconomic local violent crime into commerce
Answer B: The statute is valid because any serious national problem substantially affects commerce
Answer C: The statute is valid because Congress may regulate all tort remedies
Answer D: The statute is invalid only if the assault happened on federal land
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Commerce Clause does not give Congress a general police power over noneconomic local violence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Findings cannot erase the noneconomic character and local-crime posture. C: Congress still needs an enumerated power. D: The question is Commerce Clause authority; federal land is not the only possible federal hook, and its absence is not the full test.
lb_rule_reference: Congress generally may not regulate noneconomic local violent crime under the Commerce Clause merely by finding aggregate effects on the national economy.
