Question: Joshua signed and delivered a deed conveying a warehouse to Caleb. The deed recited, “For fifty thousand dollars paid by Caleb.” In fact, the money was paid by Caleb's aunt, Mary, as a gift to Caleb. Caleb accepted the deed. Joshua later argued that the deed was invalid because Caleb personally paid nothing. Who owns the warehouse?
Answer A: Caleb, because grantee payment is not a deed-validity requirement and the delivered deed passed title.
Answer B: Joshua, because the recital was inaccurate about who supplied the money.
Answer C: Joshua, because Caleb cannot receive title unless he personally gives value.
Answer D: Mary, because she supplied the money used for the conveyance.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is not whether the named grantee personally supplied consideration. Joshua signed and delivered a deed conveying the warehouse to Caleb, and Caleb accepted. Even if the recital inaccurately described the source of payment, title passed to Caleb.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because an inaccurate consideration recital about the source of funds does not void the delivered deed. C: C is wrong because grantee value is not required for deed validity. D: D is wrong because payment by Mary did not make Mary the grantee of the deed.
lb_rule_reference: Consideration may come from third party; grantee value not required for validity
