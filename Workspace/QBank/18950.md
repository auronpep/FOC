MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Cause in fact (actual cause)
Question: A safety coating used by many small furniture makers contained the same chemical formula regardless of manufacturer. Years later, a worker developed a disease linked to long-term exposure to that formula. Old invoices were destroyed in a flood, and no witness can identify which manufacturer's coating was used at the workshop. The worker sues several manufacturers who together supplied most of the relevant market during the exposure years. In the worker's negligence claim against the coating manufacturers, which causation theory is most likely available if the jurisdiction recognizes market-share liability?
Answer A: Alternative liability, because all manufacturers made the same chemical formula
Answer B: Market-share liability, because the product was fungible and manufacturer identity cannot be determined
Answer C: Joint and several liability without proof of market participation, because the injury is indivisible
Answer D: No causation doctrine can ever help when the plaintiff lacks invoices
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: If recognized, market-share liability addresses exactly this narrow product-identification problem: a fungible product, inability to identify the manufacturer, and defendants tied to the relevant market.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Alternative liability is aimed at a small group of negligent actors before the court where one caused the injury. A broad fungible-product identification gap is the market-share setting, not classic alternative liability. C: Joint and several liability does not eliminate the need for a causation theory connecting defendants to the product market. Market-share liability depends on relevant market participation. D: Market-share liability is a narrow exception recognized in some jurisdictions for fungible product cases where exact identification is unavailable.
lb_rule_reference: Market-share liability is a narrow doctrine for fungible products where manufacturer identity cannot be determined; it does not require proof that each defendant individually supplied the plaintiff.
