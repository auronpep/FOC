Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Prescott Medical Devices Inc. is a manufacturer incorporated and headquartered in the State of Ferris, where it designs, manufactures, and sells its products. Prescott sells its surgical instruments exclusively to hospitals in Ferris and the adjacent State of Mallow, pursuant to long-term supply contracts with those hospitals. A surgeon employed by a Ferris hospital brought a Prescott surgical instrument on a personal trip to the State of Dunwick to perform a volunteer procedure at a Dunwick clinic. The instrument malfunctioned during the surgery, injuring the patient. The patient filed suit against Prescott in Dunwick federal court. Prescott has never sold a product in Dunwick, does not advertise there, and has no contacts with Dunwick aside from this incident. Dunwick's long-arm statute reaches any defendant "whose product causes injury within the State of Dunwick." Does the Dunwick court have personal jurisdiction over Prescott?
Answer A: Yes, because the long-arm statute expressly covers products that cause injury in Dunwick, and this product caused injury in Dunwick
Answer B: Yes, because Prescott should have foreseen that its instruments might be transported to any state by physicians who use them
Answer C: No, because the Prescott instrument reached Dunwick through the unilateral act of the surgeon, not through Prescott's deliberate commercial activity directed at Dunwick, so Prescott has not purposefully availed itself of Dunwick
Answer D: No, because the long-arm statute does not reach Prescott — the statute covers products that cause injury in Dunwick, but this product was not "of" Dunwick or designed to be sold there
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Although the Dunwick long-arm statute may cover the situation literally, the constitutional due-process analysis fails. Prescott sells only in Ferris and Mallow; it has never directed commercial activity at Dunwick. The instrument entered Dunwick because the surgeon — not Prescott — chose to bring it there. Under *World-Wide Volkswagen*, a product reaching the forum through a third party's unilateral, fortuitous act does not supply minimum contacts. Prescott could not reasonably anticipate being haled into a Dunwick court based solely on a physician's personal transport of its product. The motion to dismiss should be granted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Long-arm statutory reach is necessary but not sufficient. The court must also confirm that exercising jurisdiction complies with due process. Even if the statute's literal language applies, the constitutional analysis requires that Prescott purposefully availed itself of Dunwick. The instrument reached Dunwick only because a Ferris surgeon personally transported it there — a unilateral act of the plaintiff's agent, not of Prescott. Under *World-Wide Volkswagen*, a defendant must be able to reasonably anticipate being haled into court in the forum; a product that enters a state through a third party's unilateral act does not satisfy that requirement. B: World-Wide Volkswagen* rejected the notion that foreseeability of the product reaching the forum is sufficient for purposeful availment. The Court distinguished between the defendant "delivering its products into the stream of commerce with the expectation that they will be purchased by consumers in the forum State" — which satisfies purposeful availment — and a product reaching the forum through the fortuitous, unilateral act of a third party. Prescott sold only to Ferris and Mallow hospitals and never sent a product to Dunwick; a physician personally transporting an instrument to Dunwick is exactly the unilateral third-party act that *World-Wide Volkswagen* excluded from the minimum-contacts analysis. D: The long-arm statute says "whose product causes injury within Dunwick" — Prescott's product did cause injury in Dunwick. The statutory prong is likely met by the statute's literal language. The correct basis for dismissal is the constitutional due-process step — unilateral plaintiff act, no purposeful availment. An answer granting the motion on the wrong ground is procedurally incorrect even if its conclusion is right.
lb_rule_reference: The defendant's contacts with the forum must arise from its own deliberate acts, not from the unilateral acts of the plaintiff or a third party. A plaintiff who brings the defendant's product to the forum state does not thereby make the defendant subject to jurisdiction in that state. *World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson* (1980).
