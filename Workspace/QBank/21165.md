Question: Ivan owns a house that he has leased to a tenant for one year. Angry about a rent dispute, Ivan pours gasoline on the wooden porch at night and lights it. The fire chars the porch boards before firefighters arrive. Can Ivan be guilty of common-law arson?
Answer A: Yes, because the leased house is the dwelling of another for arson purposes and the structure was charred
Answer B: No, because a title owner can never commit arson of his own property
Answer C: No, because arson requires the entire house to burn down
Answer D: No, because arson requires a purpose to kill the tenant
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The tenant has the possessory dwelling interest, and the fire actually charred part of the structure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Common-law arson protects the possessory dwelling interest of another. C: Charring part of the structure can satisfy burning; total destruction is not required. D: Arson requires malicious burning, not intent to kill.
lb_rule_reference: An owner can commit common-law arson by maliciously burning a dwelling occupied by another, such as a house leased to a tenant.
