Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal robbery trial, the prosecutor knows that Eve will testify only that she never heard anyone identify the robber. The prosecutor also knows that Eve once told police, without oath or recording, “Moses told me Joseph was the robber.” The prosecutor calls Eve solely so she will deny hearing that statement and then offers the police officer's testimony about Eve's prior statement. The prosecutor argues the police officer's testimony is only for impeachment. Joseph objects that the prosecutor is using impeachment as a pretext to place otherwise inadmissible hearsay before the jury. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Admit the officer's testimony automatically because any party may impeach its own witness.
Answer B: Admit the officer's testimony as substantive proof because the statement identified Joseph.
Answer C: Exclude the officer's testimony or limit it in the court's discretion because impeachment may not be used as a mere pretext to put otherwise inadmissible hearsay before the jury.
Answer D: Exclude the officer's testimony only if Eve refuses to answer any questions.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Although a party may impeach its own witness and prior inconsistent statements may be used for impeachment, the court may control misuse of impeachment when the real purpose is to place otherwise inadmissible hearsay before the jury. Here the prosecutor called Eve knowing she would not provide useful admissible testimony and seeks to use the impeachment route as a conduit for the unsworn police account of Eve's report of Moses's accusation. The court should exclude or strictly limit the evidence to prevent backdoor substantive use.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the right to impeach one's own witness does not authorize pretextual introduction of inadmissible hearsay. B: B is wrong because neither Eve's unsworn statement nor Moses's accusation becomes substantive proof merely by being routed through impeachment. D: D is wrong because the danger is the pretextual impeachment use, not only complete refusal to testify.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 613 and Rule 403 / PIS as pretext for inadmissible substantive evidence
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests backdoor-hearsay control when a party uses prior inconsistent impeachment as a conduit for inadmissible substantive proof.
