Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police stop a driver for a broken taillight, discover an outstanding arrest warrant for unpaid parking tickets, handcuff the driver, and lock him in the patrol car. Without consent or probable cause, officers search the passenger compartment of his car and find drugs in the glove box. Was the passenger-compartment search valid as a search incident to arrest?
Answer A: Yes, because any lawful arrest of a recent vehicle occupant permits a passenger-compartment search
Answer B: No, because the driver was secured and there was no reason to believe evidence of unpaid parking tickets was in the car
Answer C: Yes, because the broken taillight made the car inherently searchable
Answer D: No, but only because police found drugs rather than parking records
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Gant limits vehicle search incident authority when the arrestee cannot access the vehicle and no offense-related evidence is reasonably expected inside.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The arrestee was secured and the offense of arrest was unrelated to evidence likely in the car. C: A valid traffic stop does not itself create authority to search the vehicle. D: The search was invalid before discovery because no access or offense-evidence rationale existed.
lb_rule_reference: After a vehicle occupant's arrest, police may search the passenger compartment incident to arrest only if the arrestee can access it or it is reasonable to believe evidence of the offense of arrest is inside.
