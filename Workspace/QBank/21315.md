Question: Deborah orally agrees to buy 300 custom bronze nameplates from Caleb, each bearing Deborah's family seal. The nameplates would be unsuitable for Caleb's ordinary resale. Before Caleb orders materials or begins work, Deborah sends Caleb a notice repudiating the deal. Caleb then buys bronze and starts engraving the nameplates. Deborah asserts the Statute of Frauds. Is Caleb likely to defeat the Statute of Frauds under the specially manufactured goods exception?
Answer A: Yes, because the nameplates would be unsuitable for sale to others.
Answer B: No, because Caleb did not make a substantial beginning or procurement commitment before receiving Deborah's repudiation notice.
Answer C: Yes, because Caleb began engraving before filing suit.
Answer D: No, because the specially manufactured goods exception applies only to merchants.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The specially manufactured goods exception requires the seller, before receiving notice of repudiation, to make a substantial beginning of manufacture or commitments for procurement under circumstances indicating the goods are for the buyer. Caleb did not act until after receiving Deborah's repudiation notice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because unsuitability is necessary but not sufficient; timing and substantial beginning or procurement also matter. C: C is wrong because beginning work before suit is too late if it occurs after repudiation notice. D: D is wrong because the exception is not limited to merchant sellers.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(3)(a)
