Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Uri joins a conspiracy to steal delivery trucks. Before any theft occurs, Uri tells the other conspirators, "I am out," retrieves the garage key he had provided, and reports the plan to police. Two weeks later, the remaining conspirators steal a truck. Prosecutors charge Uri with the later theft under Pinkerton. What is the best answer?
Answer A: Uri is liable for the later theft because withdrawal can never affect Pinkerton liability
Answer B: Uri is not liable for the later theft if his withdrawal was effective before that theft occurred
Answer C: Uri is not liable for the conspiracy itself because he withdrew before the theft
Answer D: Uri is liable for the later theft only if he personally drove the truck
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Uri communicated withdrawal, neutralized his prior assistance by retrieving the key, and alerted police before the later theft. Those facts support effective withdrawal, cutting off Pinkerton liability for later crimes.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Withdrawal does not erase the already-formed conspiracy, but effective withdrawal can cut off liability for later substantive crimes. C: The conspiracy offense was complete when Uri joined the agreement. Withdrawal generally does not undo that completed conspiracy. D: Pinkerton can impose liability without personal participation. The issue is whether withdrawal cut off that vicarious liability.
lb_rule_reference: Effective withdrawal from a conspiracy can cut off liability for later substantive crimes by co-conspirators, though it does not erase liability for the conspiracy already formed.
