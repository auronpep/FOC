Question: A caterer carelessly ordered too few desserts for a banquet. No one became ill, no property was damaged, and the customer alleges only that the event was less impressive than promised. The customer sues the caterer in negligence for disappointed expectations under the catering agreement. What is the caterer's strongest response?
Answer A: The claim likely belongs in contract, not ordinary negligence, because only disappointed expectations are alleged
Answer B: The customer wins because careless performance of any contract is automatically negligence
Answer C: The customer wins because emotional disappointment is always personal injury
Answer D: The customer loses because contracts can never support any lawsuit
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The customer alleges a failure to deliver the promised bargain, with no personal injury, property damage, or independent tort duty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A contract breach does not automatically create negligence liability for pure expectation loss. C: General disappointment with performance is not the same as recognized personal injury or NIED. D: Contract remedies may exist; the point is that the negligence claim is not the right vehicle for pure expectation loss.
lb_rule_reference: Disappointed contractual expectations, without personal injury, property damage, or an independent tort duty, generally sound in contract rather than negligence.
