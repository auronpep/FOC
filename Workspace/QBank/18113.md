MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Affirmative duties to act
Question: A driver negligently struck a cyclist, leaving the cyclist lying in a traffic lane. A pedestrian ran into the lane during a break in traffic to pull the cyclist to safety. While doing so, the pedestrian slipped on debris from the crash and broke an ankle. The pedestrian sues the driver for negligence. Is the driver likely to owe a duty to the pedestrian rescuer?
Answer A: Yes, because reasonable rescue efforts are a foreseeable response to danger created by negligence
Answer B: No, because the driver did not specifically foresee this pedestrian
Answer C: No, because rescuers always assume the risk as a matter of law
Answer D: No, because negligence duty extends only to the first person injured
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The driver created a peril that foreseeably invited rescue. A reasonable rescuer is within the risk field.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The duty question asks whether a rescuer was a foreseeable class of plaintiff, not whether this exact person was identified. C: Reasonable rescue is foreseeable and not automatically barred; only rash or unreasonable rescue may reduce or bar recovery. D: Rescue doctrine recognizes that negligence can foreseeably endanger rescuers too.
lb_rule_reference: A negligent actor who creates a risk of physical harm may be liable to a reasonable rescuer injured while attempting a foreseeable rescue.
