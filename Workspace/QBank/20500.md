Outline: Clergy and penitent
Question: Jonah privately met with Pastor Aaron in Aaron's office and said, “I lied to the investigators.” Jonah asked for forgiveness and spiritual guidance. Pastor Aaron understood the conversation to be confidential religious counseling. A federal grand jury later subpoenas Pastor Aaron to repeat Jonah's statement. How should the court rule on Jonah's privilege claim?
Answer A: Overrule it, because federal law recognizes no privilege for communications with clergy.
Answer B: Overrule it, because the communication contains an admission of wrongdoing.
Answer C: Sustain it, because the statement was a confidential communication to clergy in a spiritual counseling capacity.
Answer D: Sustain it only if Pastor Aaron is also a licensed psychotherapist.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The clergy-penitent privilege protects confidential communications made to clergy in a spiritual or religious counseling capacity. Jonah's private request for forgiveness and spiritual guidance fits that protected lane. The decisive axis is spiritual capacity plus confidentiality.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This under-recognizes the common-law clergy privilege under Rule 501. B: B is wrong because Wrongdoing is the ordinary reason penitential communications matter; it does not by itself defeat privilege. D: D is wrong because The privilege is clergy-based, not dependent on psychotherapist licensure.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / clergy-communicant privilege
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is failing to identify the spiritual-counseling protected relationship or is replacing it with another privilege.
