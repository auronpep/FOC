Question: A seller tells a buyer that a used piano is "the finest instrument in the county" and "sounds better than any new piano." The buyer inspects the piano and buys it. The piano is ordinary but playable. The buyer sues for fraud based only on those statements. Is the buyer likely to establish fraud?
Answer A: Yes, because any exaggerated sales statement is a false representation
Answer B: Yes, because the buyer later regretted the purchase
Answer C: No, because the statements are likely nonactionable puffery or opinion rather than factual misrepresentations
Answer D: No, because fraud can never arise during a sale of goods
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Vague subjective praise usually does not satisfy the false-fact element.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Fraud requires more than ordinary puffery or opinion. B: Regret does not establish a false material fact. D: Fraud can arise in a sale if the elements are met.
lb_rule_reference: Fraud generally requires a false statement of material fact; vague opinion or puffery is usually not enough unless special circumstances make it reasonably understood as fact.
