Question: Abraham wrote and signed a letter to Isaac stating, “I hereby convey my cabin at 10 Cedar Lane to you, effective immediately. We can have a formal deed prepared later if you want one.” Abraham handed the letter to Isaac, and Isaac accepted. No printed deed form was ever prepared. Who owns the cabin?
Answer A: Abraham, because the writing was called a letter rather than a deed.
Answer B: Abraham, because the reference to a later formal deed prevented any present conveyance.
Answer C: Isaac, because the signed writing used present transfer words, identified the property and grantee, and was delivered.
Answer D: Isaac only if the later formal deed is prepared and recorded.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is an informal written deed, and the decisive validity gate is whether the writing contains the required deed substance. Abraham's signed letter identified the grantor, Isaac as grantee, the cabin, and present words of transfer, then was delivered and accepted. The deed label and later formal-document reference do not defeat the present title-transfer lock.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a writing need not be labeled deed if it functions as one. B: B is wrong because the letter stated an immediate conveyance; the later formal deed was optional. D: D is wrong because recording or a later formal duplicate is not required for validity between Abraham and Isaac.
lb_rule_reference: Informal signed writing with present transfer words
