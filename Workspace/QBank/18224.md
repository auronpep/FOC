Question: A consultant had an at-will service contract with a retailer. A competitor knew of the contract and sent forged safety complaints to the retailer, intending to get the consultant fired. The retailer terminated the contract because of the forged complaints. Does the at-will nature of the contract automatically defeat the consultant's interference claim?
Answer A: Yes, because at-will contracts can never support interference liability
Answer B: Yes, because competitors are always privileged regardless of method
Answer C: No, because knowingly using improper means to cause termination of an at-will contract can support liability
Answer D: No, because the competitor becomes a party to the contract by interfering
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The forged complaints supply the improper interference.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: At-will status does not automatically defeat the tort. B: Competition privilege does not protect forged complaints. D: The competitor remains an outsider; that is why interference is possible.
lb_rule_reference: An at-will contract or relationship may support an interference claim when the defendant knowingly uses improper means to cause termination.
