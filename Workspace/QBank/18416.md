MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A contractor negligently left an unlit trench across half of a narrow road at night. A driver swerved to avoid the trench and negligently crossed the center line, striking an oncoming car. The contractor argues that the driver's negligent swerve superseded any liability. Is the driver's negligence likely superseding?
Answer A: Yes, because the driver was the last negligent actor before impact
Answer B: Yes, because any intervening negligence cuts off liability
Answer C: No, because swerving negligently to avoid an unlit trench is within the foreseeable risk chain
Answer D: No, because proximate cause is satisfied whenever actual cause is satisfied
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The trench created a risk of evasive driving and collisions. The driver's negligent response does not cut off the contractor's proximate cause.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Being the last actor is not the test. A negligent evasive maneuver is foreseeable when an unlit trench blocks a road. B: Foreseeable intervening negligence usually does not supersede. D: Proximate cause is a separate scope limit. It is satisfied here because the intervention was foreseeable, not automatically.
lb_rule_reference: A later negligent act does not supersede when it is a foreseeable response to the danger created by the first breach.
