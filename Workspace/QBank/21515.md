Question: People held in a county detention center for short pretrial hearings file a putative class action challenging a policy that denies access to counsel during the first 48 hours. The named plaintiff is released before the court rules on class certification. New detainees are subject to the same short policy window, making individual claims likely to expire before certification. What is the best mootness analysis?
Answer A: The case is automatically moot because the named plaintiff is no longer detained
Answer B: The case may remain live because the challenged detention period is inherently transitory and the class claim can relate back
Answer C: The case remains live solely because any person might be detained someday
Answer D: The case is unripe because the plaintiff is already released
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The short detention period predictably ends before certification. Relation-back doctrine can preserve review for the putative class claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Inherently transitory class claims can relate back to certification so the case is not automatically moot. C: The answer depends on class-action relation back and inherently transitory claims, not an unlimited future-plaintiff rule. D: Release after filing raises mootness, not ripeness.
lb_rule_reference: In some inherently transitory class-action settings, a named plaintiff's claim may relate back to the certification motion even if the individual claim ends quickly.
