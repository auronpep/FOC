Question: John orally agrees to buy 40 clay lamps from Mary for a total price of $480. Mary tenders the lamps on time, but John refuses to pay and asserts that the oral agreement is unenforceable because it was not in writing. How should the court rule on John's Statute of Frauds defense?
Answer A: John prevails because all goods contracts must be in writing.
Answer B: Mary prevails because the UCC goods Statute of Frauds applies only to sales of goods for $500 or more.
Answer C: John prevails unless Mary can show that the lamps were specially manufactured.
Answer D: Mary prevails only if John is a merchant.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: UCC §2-201 applies to contracts for the sale of goods for the price of $500 or more. Because the total price is $480, this goods contract is outside the UCC SOF threshold.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the UCC threshold is not all goods contracts. C: C is wrong because the specially manufactured goods exception matters only when the contract is within the UCC SOF. D: D is wrong because merchant status is not needed when the transaction falls below the threshold.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 — $500 threshold
