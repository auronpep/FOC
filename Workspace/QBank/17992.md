Question: Deborah orally ordered 1,000 clay tablets from a merchant seller, each engraved with Deborah's family mark and a date selected by Deborah. The tablets were not suitable for sale to others in the seller's ordinary course of business. Before Deborah repudiated, the seller bought special molds and engraved 300 tablets. Deborah asserts the Statute of Frauds. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The contract is unenforceable because specially manufactured goods always require a signed writing.
Answer B: The contract is enforceable only for the 300 tablets already engraved.
Answer C: The contract is enforceable only if Deborah accepted delivery of the engraved tablets.
Answer D: The contract may be enforceable despite no writing because the goods were specially manufactured for Deborah, unsuitable for others, and the seller made a substantial beginning before repudiation.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: UCC §2-201 allows enforcement without a writing when goods are specially manufactured for the buyer, unsuitable for sale to others in the ordinary course, and the seller has substantially begun manufacture or procurement before receiving repudiation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the UCC specifically recognizes a specially manufactured goods exception. B: B is wrong because this exception is not automatically limited to the number already completed if the statutory requirements are met. C: C is wrong because acceptance is a separate exception; the specially manufactured goods route controls.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 / specially manufactured goods
