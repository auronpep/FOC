Question: David executed and delivered a valid deed conveying Riveracre “to Samuel when Samuel marries.” At the time of the conveyance, Samuel was unmarried. The deed did not give possession to anyone else. Who has the present possessory estate immediately after delivery of the deed?
Answer A: Samuel, because the deed was delivered to him.
Answer B: Samuel and David as tenants in common until Samuel marries.
Answer C: David, subject to Samuel's springing executory interest.
Answer D: No one, because the deed creates a gap in possession.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Samuel does not receive present possession because the deed gives him the land only “when Samuel marries.” Until that condition occurs, David retains the present possessory estate. Samuel has a springing executory interest because his future interest will divest the grantor if the condition occurs.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because delivery of the deed does not erase the condition precedent to Samuel's possession. B: B is wrong because the deed does not create co-ownership. D: D is wrong because the law does not leave a gap in seisin; David retains the estate until Samuel's interest becomes possessory.
lb_rule_reference: Springing executory interest / retained estate during gap
