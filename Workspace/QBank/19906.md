Question: Rachel signed a written power of attorney authorizing Joseph to convey her orchard to Esther. Joseph then prepared a deed accurately describing the orchard, signed it “Rachel, by Joseph as agent,” and delivered it to Esther. Esther accepted the deed. Who owns the orchard?
Answer A: Rachel, because only the owner can personally sign a deed conveying the owner's land.
Answer B: Rachel, because Joseph's written authority had to be repeated word-for-word in the deed.
Answer C: Rachel, because Esther did not sign the power of attorney.
Answer D: Esther, because Joseph had written authority to execute the deed for Rachel.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed executed through an agent, and the decisive validity gate is written authority. Rachel signed a written power authorizing Joseph to convey the orchard, and Joseph signed and delivered the deed within that authority. The agency-authority lock is satisfied, so title passes to Esther.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a properly authorized agent may execute a deed for the owner. B: B is wrong because the authority need not be repeated in the deed itself so long as written authority exists. C: C is wrong because the grantee need not sign the grantor's power of attorney to receive title.
lb_rule_reference: Written agency authority / valid agent-signed deed
