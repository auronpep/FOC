Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: After arresting Paige for shoplifting, an officer placed her in the back of a patrol car and began filling out paperwork in silence. No Miranda warnings had been given. Paige suddenly said, "I only took the necklace because my rent is due." The officer had not asked any question or made any remark likely to prompt a response. Is Paige's statement suppressible under Miranda?
Answer A: Yes, because Paige was in custody when she spoke
Answer B: Yes, because Miranda warnings must precede every statement after arrest
Answer C: No, because the statement was volunteered and not the product of interrogation
Answer D: No, because shoplifting is not serious enough for Miranda to apply
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Paige was in custody, but the officer did not question her or use words likely to elicit a response. Miranda does not require suppression of a spontaneous statement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Miranda requires both custody and interrogation. Paige volunteered the statement without police prompting. B: Warnings are required before custodial interrogation, not before unprompted statements. D: Miranda is not limited by offense seriousness. It does not apply here because interrogation was absent.
lb_rule_reference: Miranda protects against custodial interrogation; a spontaneous volunteered statement is admissible when not prompted by express questioning or its functional equivalent.
