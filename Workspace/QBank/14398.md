Question: A landholder was land-rich by inheritance but money-poor, having suffered severe losses on bad investments, but still owned several thousand acres of unencumbered timberland. He had a large family, and his normal, fixed personal expenses were high. Pressed for cash, he advertised a proposed sale of standing timber on a choice 2,000-acre tract. The only response was an offer by a logger, the owner of a large, integrated construction enterprise, after inspection of the advertised tract. The logger offered to buy, sever, and remove the standing timber from the advertised tract at a cash price 70% lower than the regionally prevailing price for comparable timber rights. The landholder, by then in desperate financial straits and knowing little about timber values, signed and delivered to the logger a letter accepting the offer. If, before the logger commences performance, the landholder's investment fortunes suddenly improve and he wishes to get out of the timber deal with the logger, which of the following legal concepts affords his best prospect of effective cancellation?
Answer A: A: Bad faith.
Answer B: B: Equitable estoppel.
Answer C: C: Unconscionability.
Answer D: D: Duress.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. The landholder should invoke substantive and procedural unconscionability. At the time he agreed to sell, the landholder was financially desperate, with no cash and only land rights. When presented with a cash offer that was 70% lower than comparable contracts, which he was unaware of, the landholder effectively had no bargaining power to refuse this unconscionable price term. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Even though the logger won the contract with an extremely low bid, this alone does not establish bad faith. Without more evidence, such as unreasonable, deceitful behavior by the logger, this claim would not succeed. B is incorrect. The logger did not lie or fraudulently mislead the landholder, whose ignorance prevented him from knowing the price was unfair. D is incorrect. A duress claim requires a showing of some sort of threat, which the logger did not make here.
