Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: During a valid Terry stop, an officer reasonably suspects the suspect is armed and pats down the suspect's outer clothing. The officer feels a small soft pouch. It does not feel like a weapon. The officer squeezes and rolls it between his fingers until he concludes it contains drugs, then reaches in and removes it. Was the seizure justified under plain feel?
Answer A: Yes, because any lawful frisk permits seizure of evidence discovered by touch
Answer B: No, because the officer manipulated a nonweapon item before its incriminating character became apparent
Answer C: Yes, because drugs are dangerous and officer safety permits full pocket searches
Answer D: No, because Terry stops never permit any physical touching
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The pouch was not immediately identifiable as contraband during a lawful pat-down. Manipulating it exceeded Terry's safety scope.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A frisk is for weapons, and plain-feel seizure requires immediately apparent contraband without manipulation. C: Terry permits a weapons frisk, not a general evidence search for drugs. D: A limited weapons frisk is allowed with reasonable suspicion the person is armed and dangerous.
lb_rule_reference: During a lawful frisk, plain-feel seizure is allowed only when contraband's incriminating character is immediately apparent without further manipulation.
