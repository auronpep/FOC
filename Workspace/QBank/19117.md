Question: In a federal debt-collection trial, Joseph offers a stack of account printouts. He calls Naomi, who works for the company, and asks, “Do these printouts prove that Deborah owes the money?” Naomi has not yet testified how the records were made, kept, or retrieved. Deborah objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because business papers are always admissible.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because Naomi works for the company.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because Joseph has not laid a foundation for the records before asking Naomi to use them as proof.
Answer D: Sustain the objection because business records can never be admitted in debt cases.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Before business records are used as proof, the proponent must lay the required foundation or certification. Employment alone does not establish how the records were made and kept.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because business papers are not automatically admissible. B: B is wrong because the witness's employment is not enough by itself. D: D is wrong because properly supported business records can be admitted in debt cases.
