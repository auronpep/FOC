Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: Lena found Mateo vandalizing her car. She chased him into an alley, knocked him down, and repeatedly struck his head and ribs with a metal pipe while saying, "I'm not killing you; I'm making sure you never walk right again." Mateo died from internal bleeding caused by the blows. Which homicide offense is most strongly supported?
Answer A: Murder, because Lena intended to inflict serious bodily injury and Mateo died from that attack
Answer B: Voluntary manslaughter, because Mateo provoked Lena by vandalizing her property
Answer C: Involuntary manslaughter, because Lena did not specifically intend to kill Mateo
Answer D: No homicide liability, because Mateo's death was greater than the harm Lena intended
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Malice can be established by intent to kill, intent to inflict serious bodily injury, depraved-heart conduct, or felony murder. Lena's statement and repeated pipe blows show an intent to cause grievous bodily harm. When death results from that intended serious injury, murder is supported even if she did not purposefully desire death.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Property damage is not the kind of adequate provocation that ordinarily reduces a lethal beating to voluntary manslaughter. The facts show retaliatory serious-injury intent, not legally adequate heat of passion. C: A purpose to kill is not the only malice route. Intent to inflict serious bodily injury is enough when death results. D: The law does not require the exact harm intended. Lena caused death through the serious bodily harm she intended to inflict.
lb_rule_reference: Malice murder includes intent to inflict grievous bodily harm; the prosecution need not prove a purpose to kill if serious-injury intent caused death.
