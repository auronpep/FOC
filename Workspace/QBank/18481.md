


BARMATRIX Q#: 18481
Outline_code: 41030402
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Relations Between Federal and State Governments
Outline: Dormant Commerce Clause
Question: A coastal state has enacted the Ocean Fishing License Act, which regulates the right to fish in coastal waters. Section 1 of the Act provides that no person shall fish in the ocean from a vessel registered in the state who has not obtained an ocean-fishing license from the state Department of Fish and Game. Section 2 of the Act sets the fees for ocean-fishing licenses at $10 per year for residents of the state and $20 per year for nonresidents. An action is brought in a federal court by a resident of the state for an injunction to prohibit the state from enforcing Section 2 of the Ocean Fishing License Act. Which of the following arguments would be LEAST likely to lead to a finding that the provisions of that section are invalid?
Answer A: A: The Act violates the Commerce Clause.
Answer B: B: The Act violates the Equal Protection Clause.
Answer C: C: The Act violates the Necessary and Proper Clause.
Answer D: D: Section 1 of the Act is constitutionally invalid.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The Necessary and Proper Clause permits Congress to do what is necessary and proper in carrying out the powers delegated to it by the Constitution. It is therefore irrelevant to the constitutionality of this state statute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A might lead to the conclusion that the section is invalid since it obviously discriminates against interstate commerce. B might lead to the same conclusion since the higher license fee imposed on nonresidents could constitute invidious discrimination. If Section 1, which requires a license, is constitutionally invalid, Section 2, which sets the fees for such licenses, could not be validly enforced. For this reason, D might likewise justify the conclusion that Section 2 is invalid.
