Question: In a federal drug prosecution, Lydia testifies for the government. Lydia is not charged in the case, but she is facing possible removal from the United States, and the prosecutor wrote a letter to immigration officials asking that Lydia receive favorable treatment for cooperating. The defendant asks Lydia about the letter. The prosecutor objects that Lydia has not been convicted of any crime. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because only criminal convictions may be used to attack credibility.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless the letter is independently admissible as a public record.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the letter tends to show Lydia's possible bias or motive to favor the government.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Lydia first denies hoping for favorable treatment.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness's hope for government help or leniency can show bias or motive to testify favorably. The defendant is not using the letter as conviction impeachment; the issue is Lydia's potential incentive to please the prosecution.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because bias is broader than conviction impeachment. B: B is wrong because the question may explore bias; the admissibility of the letter as a public record is not the only route. D: D is wrong because the defense need not wait for a denial before asking about bias.
lb_rule_reference: Bias impeachment / immigration or charging leniency
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests bias from government leverage and rejects conviction-only impeachment thinking.
