Question: After arresting Henry for bank robbery, officers required him to stand in a lineup and repeat the words, "Put the money in the bag," so witnesses could compare his voice. Henry refused, claiming the Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination barred the compelled voice sample. Does compelling Henry to repeat the words for voice identification violate the Fifth Amendment privilege?
Answer A: Yes, because the words repeated by Henry are incriminating
Answer B: Yes, because the privilege bars any evidence taken from a suspect against his will
Answer C: No, because the voice sample is physical identifying evidence rather than testimonial communication
Answer D: No, because the Fifth Amendment applies only during custodial interrogation
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The government seeks Henry's voice characteristics for comparison, not the truth of any statement. Compelling physical identifying evidence does not violate the Fifth Amendment privilege.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Henry is not being compelled to admit the robbery or communicate a belief. The compelled evidence is the physical quality of his voice. B: The privilege bars compelled testimonial self-incrimination, not all compelled evidence. D: The privilege is broader than Miranda, but it protects only testimonial compulsion. The right applies outside interrogation when testimonial compulsion is at issue.
lb_rule_reference: Compelling a suspect to provide fingerprints, voice samples, handwriting exemplars, or appear in a lineup generally does not violate the Fifth Amendment privilege because the evidence is physical, not testimonial.
