Question: Devin and Sal agree to sell fake concert tickets online. While the ticket scheme is ongoing, Sal kills a former romantic partner during a private argument unrelated to the ticket sales. Devin had no knowledge of the dispute, and the killing did not help the fraud scheme. Prosecutors charge Devin with murder under Pinkerton. What is Devin's strongest argument?
Answer A: Devin is not liable because the murder was not in furtherance of the ticket conspiracy and was not reasonably foreseeable from it
Answer B: Devin is liable because the ticket conspiracy was still ongoing
Answer C: Devin is liable because murder is a foreseeable crime in every criminal conspiracy
Answer D: Devin is not liable only because the fraud involved concert tickets
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The killing was a personal act outside the fraud objective. Pinkerton does not make every conspirator liable for every crime committed by another conspirator.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Timing alone is not enough. The substantive offense must also be in furtherance of the conspiracy and reasonably foreseeable. C: Foreseeability is tied to the scope and nature of the specific conspiracy. Online fake-ticket sales do not make this personal murder foreseeable. D: The doctrinal reason is lack of furtherance and foreseeability, not the specific item sold.
lb_rule_reference: Pinkerton liability does not cover an unforeseeable personal crime by a co-conspirator that does not further the agreed criminal objective.
