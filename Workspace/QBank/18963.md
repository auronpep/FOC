Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Trespass to land
Question: A restaurant owner intentionally hung a large advertising sign from his building so that it extended three feet over a neighbor's second-floor balcony. The sign blocked the neighbor's access to part of the balcony and rattled against the railing in the wind. The owner believed the airspace was not protected by property law. The neighbor sues for trespass to land. What is the best result?
Answer A: The neighbor likely prevails because the intentional low airspace intrusion interfered with use of the balcony
Answer B: The neighbor loses because trespass requires a person to walk onto the land
Answer C: The neighbor loses because the restaurant owner misunderstood the property line
Answer D: The neighbor prevails only if the sign caused structural damage
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Airspace close to the land and connected to its use can be protected against physical intrusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Objects placed onto or into protected land space can be trespasses. C: Mistake about the right to intrude does not defeat intent to place the sign. D: Trespass to land does not require structural damage.
lb_rule_reference: Trespass to land can include intentional intrusion into airspace close enough to interfere with the possessor's use and enjoyment of the land.
