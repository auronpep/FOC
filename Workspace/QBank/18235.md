Outline: Mode and order of witnesses
Question: In a federal civil fraud trial, Ruth asks the court to exclude all witnesses from the courtroom before testimony begins. Abraham, the defendant, is also expected to testify. Ruth asks that Abraham be excluded along with the other witnesses so he cannot tailor his testimony. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude Abraham because any expected witness may be sequestered on request.
Answer B: Exclude all nonexempt witnesses, but not Abraham because a natural-person party may not be excluded under the witness-exclusion rule.
Answer C: Deny the request entirely because witness exclusion is always discretionary.
Answer D: Exclude Abraham only if Ruth first proves that Abraham previously lied under oath.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: At a party's request, the court must order witnesses excluded so they cannot hear other witnesses' testimony. But the rule does not authorize excluding a party who is a natural person. Abraham may remain even if he will testify; ordinary nonexempt witnesses must be excluded.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the rule expressly protects a natural-person party from exclusion. C: C is wrong because exclusion is mandatory on a party's request, subject to exceptions. D invents a misconduct prerequisite. D: A is wrong because the rule expressly protects a natural-person party from exclusion. C is wrong because exclusion is mandatory on a party's request, subject to exceptions. D invents a misconduct prerequisite.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 615(a) / witness exclusion; natural-person party exception
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests sequestration modal force plus the natural-person party exception.
