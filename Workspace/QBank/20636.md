Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Burglary
Question: Detectives read Malik his Miranda rights and began questioning him about a burglary. Malik said, "I want a lawyer before I answer anything else." A detective replied, "We can get one later, but why do you need a lawyer if you are innocent?" Malik then said, "Because I broke in, and I need help." Should Malik's statement "I broke in" be suppressed?
Answer A: No, because the detective asked only why Malik wanted a lawyer
Answer B: No, because Malik answered voluntarily after receiving warnings
Answer C: Yes, because police continued interrogation after Malik clearly invoked counsel
Answer D: Yes, because Malik's initial waiver was invalid as a matter of law
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: "I want a lawyer before I answer anything else" was an unambiguous invocation. The detective's follow-up was likely to elicit incrimination, so the resulting statement must be suppressed from the prosecution's case-in-chief.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Malik's invocation was clear. The detective's question was reasonably likely to elicit an incriminating response and was police-initiated interrogation after invocation. B: A prior waiver is cut off by a clear request for counsel. Police may not reinitiate interrogation after that request. D: The facts do not show the initial waiver was invalid. Suppression follows from the later clear counsel invocation and improper police follow-up.
lb_rule_reference: A clear Miranda counsel invocation bars further police-initiated interrogation; follow-up questions seeking incriminating explanation violate the rule.
