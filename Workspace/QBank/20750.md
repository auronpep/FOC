Question: Joseph sold equipment to Daniel. Daniel promised Joseph that he would pay Leah $3,000 from the purchase price. Leah did not know of the promise. Before Leah learned of it, Joseph and Daniel rescinded the contract. After learning of the original promise and rescission, Leah immediately sued Daniel. Can Leah enforce Daniel's promise?
Answer A: No, because Joseph and Daniel rescinded before Leah's rights vested.
Answer B: Yes, because filing suit always vests a beneficiary's rights even if the original parties already rescinded.
Answer C: Yes, because Joseph and Daniel named Leah as the payment recipient.
Answer D: No, because creditor beneficiaries are never enforceable unless they are parties.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Leah's suit came after rescission. A suit can vest beneficiary rights only if the beneficiary still has a right to vest. Because the original parties rescinded before Leah knew, assented, relied, or sued, Leah cannot revive the eliminated promise by filing later.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the suit was too late. C: C is wrong because naming Leah made her an intended beneficiary but did not automatically vest her rights. D: D is wrong because creditor beneficiaries can enforce when their rights are not cut off before vesting.
