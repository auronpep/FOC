Outline: Competency
Question: During a federal extortion trial, the presiding judge, Deborah, tells the parties that she overheard David make an incriminating statement in the courthouse hallway before trial. The prosecutor asks to call Judge Deborah as a witness before the same jury. David's lawyer, surprised, says nothing. Is the judge's testimony permitted?
Answer A: Yes, because David failed to object when the prosecutor asked to call the judge.
Answer B: Yes, because the judge has firsthand knowledge of the hallway statement.
Answer C: Yes, but only if the judge first rules that her testimony will be credible.
Answer D: No, because the presiding judge may not testify at the trial, and no objection is needed to preserve the issue.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The presiding judge may not testify as a witness at the trial. The rule makes the problem automatic; a party need not object to preserve the issue. Firsthand knowledge does not override the judge-witness disqualification.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because no objection is needed to preserve the issue under the federal rule. B: B is wrong because personal knowledge is not enough when the witness is the presiding judge. C: C is wrong because the judge cannot cure the problem by assessing her own credibility.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 605 / presiding judge may not testify
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests judge-witness competency and rejects personal-knowledge and waiver traps.
