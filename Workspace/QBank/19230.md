Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Sarah signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey my garden to Moses, as trustee for Aaron.” Moses was alive, identifiable, and accepted the deed in his trustee capacity. Aaron did not sign the deed. Sarah later argued that the deed failed because Aaron, the beneficiary, was not the named grantee. Who owns legal title to the garden?
Answer A: Sarah, because a deed cannot name a trustee as grantee.
Answer B: Aaron, because a beneficiary always takes legal title directly.
Answer C: Sarah, because Aaron did not sign the deed.
Answer D: Moses as trustee, because the deed identifies Moses as the grantee who takes legal title.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is the identification of a grantee capable of taking legal title. The deed names Moses as trustee, and Moses is living and ascertainable. Aaron's status as beneficiary and lack of signature do not prevent legal title from passing to Moses as trustee.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a trustee can be the legal-title grantee. B: B is wrong because the deed names Moses as trustee, not Aaron as direct legal-title holder. C: C is wrong because the beneficiary's signature is not a deed-validity requirement.
lb_rule_reference: Trustee as grantee / beneficiary not deed grantee
