Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: A student says she might someday apply for a state license that requires applicants to sign a pledge. She has not chosen a career path, has not applied, and faces no present penalty for waiting. She sues now for a declaration that the pledge is unconstitutional. What is the best justiciability answer?
Answer A: The case is ripe because declaratory relief is requested
Answer B: The case is not ripe because the alleged harm depends on speculative future choices
Answer C: The case is moot because the student has not yet applied
Answer D: The case must be decided now because licensing rules always chill speech
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff faces no present hardship or credible imminent enforcement; the dispute depends on uncertain future events.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Declaratory relief still requires a concrete, ripe dispute. C: Mootness concerns disputes that were live but later ended. This is an early-timing ripeness problem. D: A possible future speech burden does not eliminate the need for concrete timing and hardship facts.
lb_rule_reference: A dispute is not ripe when it depends on uncertain future choices and the plaintiff faces no present hardship or credible enforcement threat.
