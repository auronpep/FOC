Outline: Nuisance
Question: A cabinet shop operated for twenty years in a mixed-use area. A buyer later converted the building next door into apartments. The shop's saws and dust collectors ran late into the evening, shaking the apartments and sending fine dust through old vents. The buyer knew the shop existed before buying but did not know it had recently added a late-night production shift. The buyer sued for private nuisance. Does the buyer's knowledge of the cabinet shop completely bar the private nuisance claim?
Answer A: Yes, because a person who moves next to an existing business assumes all nuisance conditions created by that business
Answer B: No; the prior existence of the shop is relevant to reasonableness and remedy, but it does not automatically defeat the claim
Answer C: Yes, but only because the shop operated for more than the statute of limitations period
Answer D: No, because coming to the nuisance is never relevant in a private nuisance case
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The nuisance inquiry balances the severity and reasonableness of the interference. The buyer's knowledge matters, but the late-night expansion and substantial dust and vibration can still support relief.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Coming to the nuisance is not a complete bar. It may affect reasonableness, equities, and remedy, especially if the defendant later expands or changes the interference. C: The question asks about coming to the nuisance, and the facts include a recent production change. The duration alone does not create an automatic limitations bar on ongoing nuisance relief. D: Coming to the nuisance is relevant to reasonableness and equitable relief even though it is not a complete defense.
lb_rule_reference: Coming to the nuisance is generally a factor in assessing reasonableness and remedy, not a complete bar to a private nuisance claim.
