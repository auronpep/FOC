Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Identifications
Question: Before contacting police, a store owner shows a witness a single social-media photo and says, "This is probably the thief." The witness agrees. Police did not arrange, suggest, or know about the photo display beforehand. The defendant moves to suppress on due process grounds. What is the best due process response?
Answer A: Suppression is not required by the due process identification rule because the suggestive display was privately arranged, not police arranged
Answer B: Suppression is automatic because any suggestive identification violates due process
Answer C: Suppression is required because social-media photos are never admissible
Answer D: Suppression is required only because the store owner used the word "probably."
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The constitutional due process rule targets police-arranged suggestiveness. Private suggestiveness may affect weight and cross-examination, but it is not the same suppression trigger.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The constitutional rule generally requires state action through police-arranged suggestiveness. C: There is no categorical constitutional ban on identifications involving social-media photos. D: The constitutional threshold turns on police arrangement, not that specific word.
lb_rule_reference: Due process suppression for suggestive identification generally requires police-arranged suggestiveness; a private person's suggestive identification procedure ordinarily does not trigger the same constitutional rule.
