Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: Police questioned Nora in custody without Miranda warnings. Her statement was voluntary but inadmissible in the prosecution's case-in-chief. At trial, Nora testified, "I was never at the warehouse." The unwarned statement said, "I was at the warehouse, but only briefly." The prosecutor seeks to use the statement only to impeach Nora. May the statement be used for impeachment?
Answer A: Yes, because the voluntary statement may be used to impeach Nora's inconsistent testimony
Answer B: Yes, because Miranda violations have no remedy
Answer C: No, because evidence excluded from the case-in-chief can never be used for any purpose
Answer D: No, unless the statement was also recorded by a neutral magistrate
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A voluntary Miranda-defective statement cannot be used in the case-in-chief, but may be used to impeach the defendant if she testifies inconsistently.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The statement remains excluded from the case-in-chief. The rule permits a limited impeachment use. C: Some suppressed statements are available for impeachment if voluntary. D: The impeachment rule does not require a magistrate recording. Voluntariness and limited use are key.
lb_rule_reference: A voluntary statement taken in violation of Miranda may be used to impeach the defendant's inconsistent trial testimony, but not in the prosecution's case-in-chief.
