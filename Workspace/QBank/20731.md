Question: For eight years, Nathan allowed Jonah to cross Nathan's land to reach a fishing pond. Jonah then sent Nathan a letter stating, “I no longer use the path by permission; I claim a legal right to use it.” Nathan did not respond. Jonah continued using the path for five more years. The jurisdiction's prescriptive period is 10 years. Does Jonah have a prescriptive easement?
Answer A: Yes, because Jonah used the path for a total of 13 years.
Answer B: Yes, because Nathan's silence after the letter immediately created an easement.
Answer C: No, because only five years of use followed Jonah's clear repudiation of permission, less than the 10-year prescriptive period.
Answer D: No, because permission can never be repudiated under any circumstances.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Use that begins as permissive is not adverse until the user clearly repudiates the permission and the owner has notice of the adverse claim. Jonah's adverse period began with the letter, and only five years passed afterward.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the first eight years were permissive and do not count as adverse use. B: B is wrong because silence did not instantly create an easement; the statutory period still had to run. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because permissive use can become adverse after clear repudiation and sufficient time.
lb_rule_reference: Permissive use becomes adverse only after clear repudiation
lb_student_diagnostic: The repair signal is counting permissive years before the adverse claim actually begins.
