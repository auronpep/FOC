Question: A negligent driver caused a minor roadside crash. The occupants were shaken but standing safely on the shoulder. A passerby ignored firefighters' instructions, sprinted across active freeway lanes, and was hit while trying to retrieve a backpack from the damaged car. The passerby sues the driver. What is the best statement about the rescue doctrine?
Answer A: The passerby automatically recovers fully because every rescue is protected
Answer B: The passerby's unreasonable rescue may reduce recovery under plaintiff-fault principles
Answer C: The driver owes no duty to any rescuer as a matter of law
Answer D: The passerby recovers only nominal damages because no one in the car was hurt
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A rescuer is not automatically barred, but ignoring instructions and entering active freeway lanes for a backpack can be found unreasonable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The doctrine protects reasonable rescue efforts; rash conduct can reduce or bar recovery. C: The categorical no-duty statement is too broad. The better analysis is reasonableness and comparative fault. D: Actual injury to the rescuer can support compensatory damages if liability is established, subject to fault allocation.
lb_rule_reference: The rescue doctrine protects reasonable rescuers; a rash or reckless rescue may be treated as comparative fault or assumption of risk depending on the jurisdiction.
