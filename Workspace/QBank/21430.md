Outline: Offers of proof
Question: At trial, Rebecca offers a certified copy of a court record against Jacob. Jacob believes the record is being offered to prove the truth of factual allegations in a complaint, not merely that the complaint was filed. Jacob says only, “Objection, improper,” and the court admits the record. On appeal, Jacob argues that the record was admissible only for the fact of filing, not for the truth of the allegations. What is the best result?
Answer A: The argument is preserved because “improper” is specific enough for any evidentiary issue.
Answer B: The argument is preserved because a certified record can always be challenged on appeal without objection.
Answer C: The argument is not preserved unless the specific ground was apparent from context, because Jacob did not state the limited-use objection.
Answer D: The argument is not preserved only if Rebecca proves the record was actually true.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A party challenging admitted evidence must object or move to strike and state the specific ground unless it is apparent from context. Saying only “improper” did not alert the court to the limited-use problem unless the context made that ground clear.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a vague objection does not preserve all grounds. B: B is wrong because certified records are still subject to timely, specific objections. D: D is wrong because preservation does not depend on whether the factual allegations were true.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(1) / specific objection after admission
