Subtopic: Law Applied by Federal Courts (Erie)
Outline: Erie Doctrine
Question: A federal court hears a diversity action involving a novel state consumer-protection issue. The state's highest court has never decided the issue, but lower state decisions and state statutes give clues. One party asks the federal court to choose the rule it thinks is best as a matter of national policy. What should the federal court do?
Answer A: Create the best national common-law rule for the dispute
Answer B: Dismiss automatically because no state supreme court case exists
Answer C: Predict how the state's highest court would decide the issue
Answer D: Apply the law of the state where the judge previously practiced
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal court's task is to apply state substantive law, including a reasoned prediction when the state's highest court has not spoken.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Diversity jurisdiction does not authorize general federal common law for state-law claims. B: Federal courts routinely predict unsettled state law; absence of a high-court case is not automatic dismissal. D: Personal experience does not choose the governing state law.
lb_rule_reference: If the state's highest court has not decided an issue, the federal court must predict how that court would decide it using available state-law sources.
