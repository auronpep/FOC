Question: At an auction of farm equipment, the auctioneer says, “Tractor up for auction,” but does not say “without reserve.” Samuel bids $4,000. Before the hammer falls, the auctioneer withdraws the tractor from sale. Samuel argues that his bid created a completed contract. Is Samuel correct?
Answer A: Yes, because announcing goods for auction is always an offer that the first bid accepts.
Answer B: No, because the auction was with reserve, and the sale was not complete before the auctioneer's acceptance by the fall of the hammer or similar announcement.
Answer C: Yes, because Samuel's bid was definite enough to accept the auctioneer's offer.
Answer D: No, but only because tractors are goods and goods contracts can be accepted only by shipment.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An auction is with reserve unless the goods are explicitly put up without reserve. In an auction with reserve, bids are offers and the sale is complete when the auctioneer announces completion, such as by the fall of the hammer. The auctioneer withdrew before acceptance.
