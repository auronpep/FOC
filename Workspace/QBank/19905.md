Outline: Spousal Privileges
Question: The government serves Daniel with a subpoena requiring him personally to produce “the notebooks you kept in your home safe recording hidden cash payments.” No witness has yet established that such notebooks exist or that Daniel controls them. Daniel invokes the Fifth Amendment as to the act of producing the notebooks. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The Fifth Amendment can never apply to document production because documents are physical objects.
Answer B: Daniel automatically wins because all contents of private documents are privileged.
Answer C: The act of production may have testimonial significance if it would admit existence, possession, or authenticity of incriminating documents.
Answer D: The privilege applies only if the notebooks were written by Daniel's lawyer.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Although the contents of preexisting documents are not privileged merely because they are incriminating, the act of producing documents can be testimonial when it communicates existence, possession, control, or authenticity. The decisive axis is contents versus act of production.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This ignores the testimonial component that production itself may carry. B: B is wrong because This overclaims the privilege as to document contents. The Fifth Amendment is not a general privacy shield for papers. D: D is wrong because Attorney involvement is not the act-of-production test.
lb_rule_reference: Fifth Amendment / act-of-production testimony
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is either overblocking all documents or underrecognizing that producing them can itself communicate facts.
