Question: In a federal fraud trial, Solomon cross-examines Eve and asks, “Isn't it obvious that you invented this story because you hate my client?” Eve's lawyer objects that the question is argumentative. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the question argues a conclusion rather than seeking specific facts.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because cross-examination may always be argumentative.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because motive to lie is always inadmissible.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Eve refuses to answer.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may control the form of questioning to make the examination effective and fair. A question that asks the witness to accept counsel's argumentative conclusion, rather than seek specific facts about bias, may be excluded as argumentative.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B overstates cross-examination latitude. C is backwards; bias is often a proper topic, but the form of the question is defective.
