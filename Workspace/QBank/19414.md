Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal assault case, the prosecutor offers a bloodstained shirt. The officer who seized the shirt testifies that she sealed it in an evidence bag and signed across the seal. The lab technician testifies that the seal was intact when the bag arrived. One courier who transported the bag cannot be located. The defendant objects that the courier must testify. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the shirt because every custodian must testify.
Answer B: Exclude the shirt because sealed physical evidence is inadmissible without the courier.
Answer C: Admit the shirt because the sealed bag and testimony support a finding that it is the same shirt; the missing courier affects weight.
Answer D: Admit the shirt only if the defendant proves no tampering occurred.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The intact seal and testimony from the seizing officer and lab technician provide sufficient evidence that the shirt is what the prosecutor claims. A missing courier does not automatically defeat authentication.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because every custodian is not required if the foundation is otherwise sufficient. B: B is wrong because the seal helps authenticate rather than bar the evidence. D: D is wrong because the defendant does not bear the authentication burden.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(a)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student confuses ideal chain proof with legal sufficiency.
