Question: Elijah signed and recorded an instrument granting the city “a perpetual right for the public to walk on the marked trail across Elijah's land.” No particular neighboring parcel is identified as benefited by the trail. How should the city's interest be classified?
Answer A: A license because members of the public, not a neighboring owner, will use the trail.
Answer B: An easement in gross or public easement because the right burdens Elijah's land without benefiting a specific dominant parcel.
Answer C: A real covenant because the city will enforce the trail right.
Answer D: An easement appurtenant to every home owned by a city resident.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement can exist in gross when it benefits a person, entity, or public right rather than a specific dominant parcel. The recorded grant gives the city a perpetual public trail right and identifies no dominant parcel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because public use does not reduce a recorded perpetual property right to a license. C: C is wrong because the grant creates a use right over land, not merely a promise. D: D is wrong because no individual resident's parcel is identified as the dominant estate.
lb_rule_reference: Public easement / easement in gross
