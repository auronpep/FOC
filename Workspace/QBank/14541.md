Question: A computer retail outlet contracted to service a bank's computer equipment for one year at a fixed monthly fee under a contract that was silent as to assignment or delegation by either party. Three months later, the retail outlet sold the service portion of its business to an experienced and well-financed computer service company. The only provision in the agreement between the retail outlet and the computer service company relating to the outlet's contract with the bank stated that the outlet “hereby assigns all of its computer service contracts to [the computer service company].” The computer service company performed the monthly maintenance required under the service contract. Its performance was defective, however, and caused damage to the bank's operations. Whom can the bank sue for damages arising from the computer service company's defective performance?
Answer A: A: The retail outlet only, because the computer service company made no promises to the bank.
Answer B: B: Either the retail outlet or the computer service company, because the bank has not released the outlet and the bank is an intended beneficiary of the outlet's agreement with the computer service company.
Answer C: C: Either the retail outlet or the computer service company, because since each has the right to enforce the bank's performance of its contract with the retail outlet, mutuality of remedy renders either potentially liable for the defective performance.
Answer D: D: The computer service company only, because it is a qualified and a financially responsible supplier of computer services.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. The retail outlet did not execute a novation when delegating its service contract to the computer services company, so the retail outlet will remain liable to the bank, which can enforce the outlet's duty to perform as an intended beneficiary of the contract. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The retail outlet remains liable on the services contracts delegated to the computer services company, and the services contracts include performance to the bank, as an intended beneficiary. C is incorrect. The bank can sue either party because the retail outlet did not execute a novation releasing themselves from liability. D is incorrect. No matter what the qualifications are of the party delegated to, the original obligor will remain liable on a contract, even after delegating their duties, until a valid novation is executed.
