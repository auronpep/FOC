Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: Solomon signed a written contract to sell 1,000 shelves to Deborah for $3 each. The writing identifies the parties, goods, quantity, and price. Deborah later seeks to prove that before signing, Solomon orally promised that the shelves would be made of cedar. Solomon argues that the Statute of Frauds bars evidence of the oral cedar promise. What is the best response to Solomon's Statute of Frauds argument?
Answer A: The Statute of Frauds is not the right objection because the signed writing satisfies the goods writing requirement; any objection to the oral cedar promise would be a parol-evidence or contract-content issue.
Answer B: The Statute of Frauds bars all evidence of oral promises whenever a contract for goods exceeds $500.
Answer C: The oral cedar promise is automatically enforceable because the signed writing includes quantity.
Answer D: The oral cedar promise is enforceable only if Deborah paid for the shelves before trial.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Statute of Frauds asks whether there is a sufficient writing for enforceability. Here the signed writing includes the goods and quantity, so the SOF gate is satisfied. Whether a prior oral cedar term can be used is a separate parol-evidence/content issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the Statute of Frauds is not a blanket rule excluding all oral evidence once a writing exists. C: C is wrong because satisfying SOF does not automatically admit or enforce every prior oral term. D: D is wrong because payment is a UCC SOF exception, not the issue when a sufficient signed writing already exists.
