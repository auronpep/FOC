Question: An insurer holds a policy fund and faces competing claims from two claimants. The insurer files "rule interpleader" in federal court but pleads no federal question and cannot satisfy ordinary diversity jurisdiction. What is the problem with the filing?
Answer A: Rule interpleader itself creates federal subject-matter jurisdiction whenever competing claims exist
Answer B: Rule interpleader requires an independent basis for federal jurisdiction, which is missing
Answer C: Interpleader is unavailable unless the insurer claims it owns the fund
Answer D: Interpleader is unavailable because claimants are never joined as defendants
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule permits joinder of claimants but does not itself open the federal courthouse.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule interpleader is procedural and needs an independent jurisdictional basis. C: Interpleader often applies when the stakeholder claims no beneficial ownership and faces rival claims. D: Interpleader joins competing claimants so their entitlement can be resolved.
lb_rule_reference: Rule interpleader is a joinder device and requires an independent basis for federal subject-matter jurisdiction.
