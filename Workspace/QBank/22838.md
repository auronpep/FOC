


BARMATRIX Q#: 22838
Outline_code: 97100000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Judgments and Verdicts
Question: A woman sued a concert promoter in federal district court, claiming that she suffered a permanent back injury when a performer jumped off the stage and landed on her head. The woman presented evidence from several witnesses that demonstrated that she had broken her neck and could no longer do many of the activities she enjoyed doing, such as horseback riding, running, and gymnastics. The jury found that the woman was permanently prohibited from engaging in these activities and awarded her $1 million. Six months later, the promoter saw a video on the Internet showing the woman winning a horseback riding competition. The promoter believed that he was entitled to relief from the $1 million judgment. What should the promoter do?
Answer A: A: Move for relief from the judgment based on fraud.
Answer B: B: Move for relief from the judgment based on jury mistake.
Answer C: C: Move for remittitur to reduce the damage award.
Answer D: D: Move for a new trial.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The best option for the promoter is to move for relief from judgment on the ground that the woman committed fraud by claiming permanent injuries. Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(b)(3) specifically allows for a party to obtain a relief from judgment if the opposing party has committed fraud. Here, the fact that the woman is winning horseback riding competitions six months after the award for her “permanent” damages seems to imply that she committed a fraud. B is incorrect because there seem to be no grounds for claiming a jury mistake, although the jury may have been fooled by the woman. C is incorrect because the promoter should do more than simply ask for a reduction in the damage award, considering that the damages were apparently calculated in the belief that the woman's injuries were permanent. D is incorrect because the promoter does not necessarily have to go through a new trial, considering the woman's likely fraud.
