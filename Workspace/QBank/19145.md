Question: A cabinet officer is impeached by the House for accepting bribes in office. Before the Senate trial, the President issues a full pardon for all federal bribery offenses arising from the conduct and announces that the pardon "terminates all consequences of the alleged misconduct." The officer argues that the Senate trial must stop. What is the best constitutional answer?
Answer A: The Senate trial must stop because a full presidential pardon eliminates all consequences of the conduct
Answer B: The Senate trial may proceed because the pardon power does not apply to impeachment consequences
Answer C: The Senate trial must stop unless the House re-impeaches the officer after the pardon
Answer D: The Senate trial may proceed only if prosecutors first indict the officer
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The Constitution excludes impeachment from the pardon power. The Senate may continue the impeachment trial even if the President pardoned federal criminal offenses arising from the same conduct.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The pardon power does not extend to impeachment. A pardon may affect federal criminal liability but cannot halt impeachment proceedings or remove impeachment consequences. C: A presidential pardon does not erase the House's impeachment or require a new impeachment vote. D: Impeachment is a constitutional political process, not dependent on criminal indictment.
lb_rule_reference: The President may pardon federal criminal offenses but cannot use the pardon power to prevent or undo impeachment consequences.
