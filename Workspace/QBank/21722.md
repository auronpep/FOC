Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff sues a defendant in federal court on a diversity claim arising from a car accident in a state that does not allow punitive damages in negligence cases. The plaintiff's complaint claims $30,000 in compensatory damages and an additional $50,000 in punitive damages, for a stated total of $80,000. The plaintiff argues that the combined demand exceeds $75,000 and the AIC threshold is satisfied. The defendant moves to dismiss for failure to satisfy the AIC requirement. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Does the amount in controversy satisfy § 1332?
Answer A: Yes, because the plaintiff's good-faith demand of $80,000 in the complaint satisfies the AIC regardless of the merits of each component of damages
Answer B: No, because the state does not allow punitive damages for negligence, so the punitive demand must be excluded, leaving only $30,000 in compensable damages — below the threshold
Answer C: Yes, because punitive damages are always included in the AIC calculation when a plaintiff expressly demands them in the complaint
Answer D: No, because including punitive damages in the AIC calculation is never permitted in diversity cases since it would encourage plaintiffs to add punitive claims artificially to manufacture federal jurisdiction
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the legal certainty test, jurisdiction is defeated if it is legally certain that the plaintiff cannot recover the claimed amount. State law applicable to this claim prohibits punitive damages in negligence cases. The $50,000 punitive demand is therefore legally unavailable. Excluding it, the plaintiff's recoverable claim is $30,000 — well below the $75,000 threshold. The complaint's total demand does not satisfy the AIC, and the court must dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under the legal certainty test, jurisdiction fails if it is clear to a legal certainty that the plaintiff cannot recover the claimed amount. When state law clearly prohibits punitive damages for the type of claim asserted — here, negligence — the plaintiff legally cannot recover them. The $50,000 punitive demand must be excluded because it is legally unavailable. Excluding it, only $30,000 in compensable damages remains — well below $75,000. C: Punitive damages are included in the AIC only when they are recoverable under applicable law. If state law categorically prohibits punitive damages for the cause of action asserted, an express demand for them does not satisfy the legal certainty test — it is certain to a legal certainty that those damages cannot be recovered. D: Punitive damages are routinely included in the AIC when they are recoverable under state law and the claim is made in good faith. There is no blanket rule excluding punitives from diversity AIC calculations. The concern about manufactured jurisdiction is addressed by the legal certainty test, not by categorically excluding punitives.
lb_rule_reference: Punitive damages are included in the AIC for § 1332 purposes if they are recoverable under the applicable law and the claim is made in good faith; they may not be used to manufacture jurisdiction when state law forecloses punitive damages on the specific claim.
