Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A drain cleaner emits toxic fumes when mixed with bleach, a risk not obvious to ordinary consumers. The bottle has no warning about mixing. A consumer mixes the products while cleaning and is injured by fumes. She testifies credibly that she always reads chemical labels and would not have mixed the products if the warning had appeared. How does her testimony affect the warning claim?
Answer A: It supports warning causation because an adequate warning would likely have changed her conduct
Answer B: It is irrelevant because strict products liability never requires causation
Answer C: It defeats the claim because all cleaning chemicals have obvious risks
Answer D: It converts the case into manufacturing defect
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The missing warning can be causal when the user would have heeded it and avoided the injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Defect and causation remain required. C: The specific mixing danger may be nonobvious and warnable. D: The facts concern an omitted warning, not a rogue product unit.
lb_rule_reference: Failure to warn requires causation; causation is supported when an adequate warning would have changed the user's conduct and avoided the injury.
