Outline: Conviction of crime
Question: In a federal civil trial, Elijah testifies for the plaintiff. On cross-examination, the defendant asks whether Elijah was convicted last year of felony embezzlement. Elijah denies the conviction. The defendant then offers a certified court record of the conviction. Elijah objects that extrinsic evidence may not be used to attack truthfulness. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the record because extrinsic evidence is never permitted on credibility issues.
Answer B: Exclude the record because Elijah denied the conviction, making the issue collateral.
Answer C: Admit the record because a conviction that is admissible under Rule 609 may be proved if the witness denies or does not remember it.
Answer D: Admit the record only if Elijah first offers opinion testimony that he is truthful.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Unlike Rule 608(b) truthfulness-specific acts, a qualifying conviction under Rule 609 may be proved by a record if the witness denies or does not remember the conviction. The certified record is the proper proof channel for the conviction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because conviction impeachment has its own proof route. B: B is wrong because the conviction is not barred merely because the witness denies it. D: D is wrong because conviction impeachment does not depend on prior bolstering.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609 / proving conviction after denial
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the distinction between extrinsic proof of convictions and extrinsic proof of mere specific acts.
