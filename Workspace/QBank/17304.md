Question: In a federal contract action, Solomon offers a warehouse log to show that ten shipments were delivered to Noah. Solomon calls the warehouse supervisor, who testifies that employees are required to enter each delivery at or near the time it occurs and that the log was kept in the ordinary course of business. Noah objects that the jury, not the judge, must decide whether the log qualifies as a business record. Who decides whether the business-record foundation has been met?
Answer A: The judge decides whether the foundation for the exception is sufficient for admissibility.
Answer B: The jury decides whether the foundation is sufficient because the log is offered for its truth.
Answer C: The jury decides admissibility, while the judge decides only whether the document is relevant.
Answer D: The judge must exclude the log unless every employee who made an entry testifies.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The court decides preliminary questions of admissibility, including whether a hearsay exception's foundation has been satisfied. If admitted, the jury decides what weight to give the log.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B and C are wrong because the judge decides admissibility gates. D is wrong because business records can be admitted through a qualified witness or custodian when the rule's foundation is met; every entrant need not testify. C: B and C are wrong because the judge decides admissibility gates. D is wrong because business records can be admitted through a qualified witness or custodian when the rule's foundation is met; every entrant need not testify. D: D is wrong because business records can be admitted through a qualified witness or custodian when the rule's foundation is met; every entrant need not testify.
