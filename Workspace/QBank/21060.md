Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: Lena filed a federal negligence action against a local driver. The federal court dismissed for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction because both parties were citizens of the same state and no federal question existed. Lena then files the same negligence claim in state court. Does the federal dismissal bar Lena's state-court negligence claim on claim-preclusion grounds?
Answer A: Yes, because every federal dismissal is a merits judgment
Answer B: Yes, because lack of jurisdiction means the plaintiff loses forever
Answer C: No, because dismissal for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction is not a final judgment on the merits of the negligence claim
Answer D: No, because claim preclusion applies only after jury verdicts
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Claim preclusion requires a merits judgment on the claim; the state court may hear the claim if it has jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A jurisdictional dismissal does not decide the substantive negligence claim. B: Lack of jurisdiction means that court cannot decide the merits; it does not usually extinguish the claim. D: Claim preclusion can follow many merits judgments, not only jury verdicts.
lb_rule_reference: Claim preclusion generally requires a valid final judgment on the merits; dismissal for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction is not a merits judgment on the underlying claim.
