Question: During a family dinner, Ellis loudly accused Maren of stealing from their parents and called her a disgrace. Maren became enraged, grabbed a kitchen knife, and stabbed Ellis in the chest. Ellis died. Is Maren's offense reduced to voluntary manslaughter?
Answer A: Yes, because Ellis's accusation was humiliating and made Maren actually angry
Answer B: Yes, because the killing happened immediately after the insult
Answer C: No, because insulting words alone generally are not adequate provocation at common law
Answer D: No, because voluntary manslaughter requires premeditation
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Voluntary manslaughter requires legally adequate provocation. Ellis's accusation and insult were words only. However upsetting, they do not reduce Maren's intentional killing to voluntary manslaughter at common law.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Actual anger is not enough. Provocation must be legally adequate under an objective standard. B: No cooling matters only if the provocation is adequate. Mere words generally are not. D: Premeditation is a first-degree murder concept, not an element of voluntary manslaughter. The mitigation fails because the provocation is legally inadequate.
lb_rule_reference: At common law, mere words or insults, however offensive, generally are not adequate provocation to reduce murder to voluntary manslaughter.
