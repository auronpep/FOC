Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: John sues David for breach of a lease. The only disputed issue is whether John delivered a written renewal notice to David before March 1. David offers testimony from Mary that, two years earlier, John once paid rent late under a different lease. John objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony, because any evidence suggesting John was careless in lease matters has some probative value.
Answer B: Admit the testimony, but only to allow the jury to decide how much weight it deserves.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony, because John's late payment under a different lease does not make timely delivery of this renewal notice more or less probable.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony only if its probative value is substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Relevant evidence must have some tendency to make a fact of consequence more or less probable. The offered late-payment evidence does not logically bear on whether John delivered this renewal notice by March 1. The court should exclude it for lack of relevance, before any Rule 403 balancing is needed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a general suggestion of carelessness in lease matters is not enough; the evidence must connect to the fact of consequence. B: B is wrong because the jury weighs admitted relevant evidence, but the judge first screens out evidence with no probative link. D: D is wrong because Rule 403 balancing assumes relevance; this evidence fails the threshold relevance test.
