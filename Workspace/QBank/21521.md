Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Caleb had a deed prepared conveying his garden to Joshua. The deed named Caleb as grantor, Joshua as grantee, used present words of conveyance, and described the garden. Caleb never signed the deed and never authorized anyone to sign for him. A notary signed a certificate stating that Caleb had appeared before the notary. Joshua accepted the document. Who owns the garden?
Answer A: Joshua, because the notary's certificate supplies Caleb's missing signature.
Answer B: Caleb, because the deed was not signed by Caleb or by an authorized signer for Caleb.
Answer C: Joshua, because the grantee accepted the document.
Answer D: Joshua, because the deed identified the parties and the property.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is the grantor signature. A notary's certificate does not replace the grantor's signature or an authorized signature for the grantor. Because Caleb did not sign and no authorized signer signed for him, the deed does not pass title.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because acknowledgment is not a substitute for execution by the grantor. C: C is wrong because acceptance cannot cure a missing grantor signature. D: D is wrong because party and property identification do not eliminate the signature requirement.
