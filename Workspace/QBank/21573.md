Outline: Judicial notice
Question: In a federal criminal trial, the prosecutor asks the judge to take judicial notice that July 4, 2026, fell on a Saturday. The date is accurately and readily determined from a reliable calendar source. The date matters because the government alleges that Aaron entered a federal building on a Saturday. Aaron objects that the jury must be told it may reject the noticed fact. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Refuse to take judicial notice because criminal juries cannot consider noticed facts.
Answer B: Take judicial notice and instruct the jury that it must accept the noticed fact as conclusive.
Answer C: Take judicial notice but give no jury instruction because judicial notice is only for the judge.
Answer D: Take judicial notice and instruct the jury that it may or may not accept the noticed fact as conclusive.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A court may take judicial notice of an adjudicative fact that is accurately and readily determined from a reliable source. In a criminal case, the court must instruct the jury that it may or may not accept the noticed fact as conclusive.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because criminal cases do not bar judicial notice. B: B is wrong because the conclusive-instruction rule is for civil cases, not criminal cases. C: C is wrong because Rule 201 contains jury-instruction consequences.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 201 / criminal jury may accept noticed fact
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests civil/criminal judicial-notice instruction inversion.
