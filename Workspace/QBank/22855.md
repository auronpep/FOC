


BARMATRIX Q#: 22855
Outline_code: 95070400
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Discovery
Question: An autoworker sued his employer for negligence in federal district court, claiming that he was injured by one of his employer's robotic welding machines. Although the autoworker believed that no one had witnessed the accident, the employer discovered that a delivery person had seen the accident. The delivery person told the employer that he saw the autoworker goofing around with the robot before he was hurt. The employer's attorney had only discovered the delivery person after a lengthy and costly search of the employer's records and video surveillance tapes. Does the employer have to provide the autoworker with the name of the delivery person?
Answer A: A: No, because the name is protected as attorney work product.
Answer B: B: No, because the delivery person's testimony would not help the autoworker.
Answer C: C: Yes, if the autoworker serves an interrogatory specifically asking for the names of witnesses.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the delivery person is likely to have discoverable information that the employer may use in its defense.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(1)(a)(i) requires the parties to provide the names of each individual likely to have discoverable information that the disclosing party may use to support its claims or defenses. Here, the delivery person saw the autoworker goofing around with the robot, and the employer is likely to use his testimony in its defense. A is incorrect because the name of the delivery person would not be protected as attorney work product. B is incorrect because the employer does not only have to reveal the names of people who may be helpful to the autoworker. C is incorrect because Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(1)(A) requires a party to disclose names without waiting for a discovery request.
