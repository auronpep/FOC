Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Mode and order of witnesses
Question: In a federal crash case, Martha wants her accident reconstruction expert, Solomon, to testify before any eyewitness or investigating officer describes the scene. The defendant objects that the jury will not understand the opinion without basic scene facts. The judge orders Martha to call one foundation witness before Solomon. Is the judge's order proper?
Answer A: No, because expert testimony must always be presented before lay testimony.
Answer B: Yes, because the court may reasonably control the order of proof to make the presentation effective for determining the truth.
Answer C: No, because the party calling an expert has an absolute right to choose when the expert testifies.
Answer D: Yes, but only because expert testimony is inadmissible unless the expert personally observed the crash.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The judge may manage the order of proof to make the evidence understandable and effective. Requiring some factual foundation before an expert opinion is a proper trial-control ruling when reasonable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because there is no rule requiring experts to testify first. C: C is wrong because witness order is subject to the court's reasonable control. D: D is wrong because experts may rely on facts or data without personally observing the event; the point is orderly presentation.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / expert after factual basis
