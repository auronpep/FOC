Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Isaac is charged with vandalizing a courthouse door. The prosecutor offers a photograph taken by a bystander minutes before the vandalism showing a person in a red coat near the courthouse holding a can of paint. The person's face is partly turned away, but a distinctive star tattoo on the hand matches Isaac's tattoo. Isaac objects that the photograph does not conclusively show his face. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the photograph if the tattoo and surrounding circumstances are sufficient to support a finding that the person is Isaac.
Answer B: Exclude the photograph because a face must be fully visible before a person can be identified.
Answer C: Exclude the photograph unless the bystander personally knew Isaac before the event.
Answer D: Admit the photograph only if the prosecutor proves by a preponderance that the person is Isaac.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Identity may be supported by distinctive characteristics and surrounding circumstances. The proponent need not provide full facial identification or persuade the judge by a preponderance if the issue is conditional relevance. The jury weighs the inference.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because facial visibility is not the only possible identifying feature. C: C is wrong because a witness's prior familiarity is not the only route to identification. D: D is wrong because the court screens for sufficient evidence to support a finding.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901 / FRE 104(b)
lb_student_diagnostic: Over-demanding one specific identification method instead of accepting a threshold circumstantial link.
