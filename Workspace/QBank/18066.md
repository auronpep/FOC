Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police unlawfully searched Ava's apartment and found a notebook listing payments from Ben. Ben did not live there, had no key, stored nothing there, and had never visited. At Ben's trial for conspiracy, the prosecution seeks to use the notebook. Ben moves to suppress based on the unconstitutional search of Ava's apartment. Should Ben's motion be granted?
Answer A: Yes, because the notebook is evidence against Ben
Answer B: Yes, because a co-conspirator may assert the rights of every member of the conspiracy
Answer C: No, because Ben had no personal privacy or property interest in Ava's apartment
Answer D: No, because notebooks are documentary evidence and cannot be suppressed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The unconstitutional search violated Ava's rights, not Ben's. Ben lacked any connection to the apartment and cannot suppress the notebook merely because it hurts his defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Evidence use against a defendant does not establish that the defendant's Fourth Amendment rights were violated. B: Fourth Amendment rights remain personal. Conspiracy membership does not transfer standing. D: Documentary evidence can be suppressed when obtained through a violation of the moving defendant's rights and no exception applies.
lb_rule_reference: Fourth Amendment suppression is personal; a defendant must show that the challenged police conduct violated his own protected interest.
