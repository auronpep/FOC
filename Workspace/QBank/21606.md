MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Clara and Derek agreed to rob a bank on the following Friday. The next day, Derek drove past the bank twice to study the entry points. Neither Clara nor Derek did anything else before they were arrested on Thursday. The jurisdiction requires an overt act for conspiracy and applies the majority rule. Clara was in another city the entire week and had no contact with Derek after their agreement. Is Clara guilty of conspiracy to commit bank robbery?
Answer A: No, because Clara personally took no overt act in furtherance of the conspiracy
Answer B: No, because Derek's drive-by was only preparation and cannot satisfy the overt-act requirement
Answer C: Yes, because Derek's act of reconnaissance satisfied the overt-act requirement for the entire conspiracy
Answer D: Yes, but only if the prosecution can prove Clara knew Derek took the reconnaissance drive
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Once a conspiracy is formed, an overt act by any member satisfies the element for all members. Clara and Derek had a genuine bilateral agreement to rob the bank — satisfying the agreement element. Derek's drive-by surveillance was an act in furtherance of that plan — satisfying the overt-act element — even though it was purely preparatory. Clara is bound by Derek's act. She is guilty of conspiracy.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under the majority rule, only one co-conspirator needs to commit the overt act to satisfy the requirement for the entire conspiracy. Derek's reconnaissance drive satisfies the overt-act element for both conspirators. Clara need not personally take any act after the agreement. B: The overt-act requirement for conspiracy is far less demanding than the act requirement for attempt. Any act in furtherance — even a purely preparatory act — suffices. Driving past the bank to study entry points is clearly in furtherance of the planned robbery and qualifies. D: The overt-act requirement for conspiracy is not a notice or knowledge requirement. It exists to confirm that the conspiracy is not merely in words. Clara's knowledge of Derek's specific act is irrelevant; the conspiracy agreement is what binds her to the overt act.
lb_rule_reference: Under the majority common-law rule (and the MPC for non-felonies), conspiracy requires an overt act by any one of the conspirators in furtherance of the agreement; the act need not be criminal or even close to the completed crime
