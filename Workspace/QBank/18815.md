Question: Naomi sold land to Jacob. As part of the sale price, Jacob promised Naomi that he would pay Naomi's daughter Rachel $20,000 at closing “as a wedding gift from Naomi.” Rachel did not sign the land-sale agreement. Jacob later refused to pay Rachel, arguing that the promise was a gift promise and therefore unenforceable by Rachel. May Rachel enforce Jacob's promise?
Answer A: No, because a donee beneficiary can never enforce a contract.
Answer B: No, because Rachel was not a party to the land-sale agreement.
Answer C: Yes, because Rachel was an intended beneficiary of Jacob's promise to pay her directly.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Rachel paid consideration to Jacob.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rachel may enforce as an intended beneficiary. The promise was made as part of Jacob and Naomi's bargain and required direct payment to Rachel. A donee beneficiary can be an intended beneficiary; Rachel's lack of party status or consideration does not defeat enforcement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because donee status does not nullify intended-beneficiary rights. B: B is wrong because a third-party beneficiary is by definition not a party to the contract. D: D is wrong because consideration from the beneficiary is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — intended gift beneficiary
