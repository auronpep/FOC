Question: A private veterans association organizes an annual parade with a chosen theme honoring military service. A city orders the association to include a private group carrying a banner opposing all military service, even though the association says the banner changes the parade's message. What is the association's best First Amendment argument?
Answer A: A parade is never expressive because it involves walking
Answer B: The city can force inclusion because parade routes use public streets
Answer C: Forced inclusion may violate the organizer's right not to alter its expressive message
Answer D: The association loses unless it proves the opposing banner is obscene
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Compelling the parade to include a conflicting banner can intrude on private editorial discretion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Parades can be expressive conduct conveying a message. B: Use of public streets does not eliminate the private organizer's expressive discretion. D: The claim is compelled speech/editorial discretion, not obscenity.
lb_rule_reference: The government may not compel a private expressive association or parade organizer to include a message that alters the organizer's own expression.
