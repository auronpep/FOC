Question: Ruth sues Boaz after a warehouse accident. Boaz's lawyer, Samuel, interviews witness Caleb and writes a two-part memo. The first part records Caleb's factual account of where the crates were stacked. The second part states Samuel's views about Caleb's credibility, which facts hurt Boaz most, and how Samuel should cross-examine Ruth. Caleb later becomes unavailable, and Ruth shows substantial need for Caleb's factual account. How should the federal court handle Ruth's motion to compel Samuel's memo?
Answer A: Order production of the entire memo because Ruth has shown substantial need for the factual account.
Answer B: Deny production of the entire memo because anything written by a lawyer in anticipation of litigation is absolutely protected.
Answer C: Order production of the factual portions if the Rule 26 showing is satisfied, but protect or redact Samuel's mental impressions, legal theories, and strategy comments.
Answer D: Deny production because the memo is attorney-client privileged simply because Samuel, a lawyer, wrote it.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Trial-preparation materials are protected work product, but ordinary factual work product may be discovered if the requesting party shows substantial need and undue hardship in obtaining the equivalent. Even when the court orders discovery of such materials, it must protect attorney mental impressions, conclusions, opinions, and legal theories. The decisive axis is factual-versus-opinion work product, so the court should split the memo rather than produce all or none.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because substantial need does not open the door to attorney mental impressions or strategy. The court must protect opinion work product. B: B is wrong because it treats all work product as absolute. Ordinary factual work product has qualified protection. D: D is wrong because attorney-client privilege is not triggered merely because a lawyer wrote the memo. Work product, not attorney-client privilege, is the operative doctrine.
lb_rule_reference: FRCP 26(b)(3) ordinary work product plus opinion work-product redaction
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats work product as all-or-nothing and fails to split ordinary factual material from attorney mental impressions.
