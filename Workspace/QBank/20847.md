Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: An employee sues her employer in federal court under a federal wage statute that expressly creates a private civil action. She seeks $8,000 in unpaid wages. The employer moves to dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction, arguing that the amount in controversy is far below $75,000. Should the federal court dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because every civil action in federal court must exceed $75,000
Answer B: Yes, unless the employee also proves complete diversity of citizenship
Answer C: No, because federal law creates the employee's claim and Section 1331 has no amount-in-controversy requirement
Answer D: No, but only if the employer is incorporated in a different state
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The complaint asserts a claim created by federal law. That is enough for federal-question jurisdiction even though the damages are below $75,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The $75,000 threshold belongs to diversity jurisdiction, not federal-question jurisdiction. B: A federal claim supplies original jurisdiction under Section 1331 without diversity. D: Corporate citizenship matters for diversity, not for a federal-question claim.
lb_rule_reference: Federal-question jurisdiction exists when federal law creates the plaintiff's cause of action, and Section 1331 has no amount-in-controversy requirement.
