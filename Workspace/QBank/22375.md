Outline_code: 75120000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Other Considerations
Question: A man was in a single car accident and taken to a hospital. While he was being treated for his injuries, a police officer ordered a doctor to take a blood sample because the officer believed that the man had been driving while intoxicated. When the test came back with a blood alcohol level above the legal limit, the man was arrested. At trial, the blood test was introduced as evidence against the man and the man was convicted. The man appealed, arguing that the blood test violated his Fifth Amendment rights. Did the blood test violate the man's Fifth Amendment rights?
Answer A: A: No, because the blood test was done by the doctor.
Answer B: B: No, because the blood test was not testimony or communicative evidence.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the officer ordered the blood test while the man was being treated for his injuries.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the blood test violated his right against self- incrimination.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A person's Fifth Amendment right against self- incrimination only protects a person from giving testimonial or communicative evidence. Schmerber v. California, 384 U.S. 757 (1966). Physical evidence is not protected by the Fifth Amendment. Consequently, the man's blood test would not be protected under the Fifth Amendment. A is incorrect because the fact that the blood test was done by the doctor would not change the analysis. C and D are incorrect because the Fifth Amendment does not protect physical evidence.
