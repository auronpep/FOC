Question: Rebecca sues Nathan in federal court for failing to respond to a demand letter. Rebecca proves that the letter was properly addressed, stamped, and mailed. Nathan testifies that the letter never arrived. The judge proposes to instruct the jury that it “may consider the mailing evidence, along with Nathan's denial, in deciding whether the letter was received.” Rebecca objects that the judge must instruct the jury to find receipt. What should the court do?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a mailing presumption compels a finding of receipt.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because Nathan's denial was a nonresponsive answer and must be struck.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the jury may evaluate the competing evidence and is not required to find receipt.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only because Rebecca did not produce the original envelope.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: After contrary evidence is introduced, the jury may consider the mailing evidence and the denial. The federal presumption does not compel a finding of receipt or shift the burden of persuasion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the presumption is not conclusive. B: B is wrong because Nathan's denial was responsive evidence, not a nonresponsive answer. D: D is wrong because the ruling turns on the presumption's effect, not the original envelope.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 301 / mailing presumption
