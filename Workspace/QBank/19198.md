MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: David is charged with possessing illegal tablets. An officer testifies that, while chasing David on foot, she saw David throw a small blue bag over a fence. Two minutes later, another officer recovered a small blue bag from the same fenced yard. The bag contained the tablets. David objects that the officers cannot prove no one else placed the bag there during the two minutes. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the bag because a perfect chain of custody is required for physical evidence.
Answer B: Exclude the bag unless the second officer personally saw David throw it.
Answer C: Admit the bag because the testimony is sufficient to support a finding that it is the bag David threw; the brief gap goes to weight.
Answer D: Admit the bag only if the prosecutor proves beyond a reasonable doubt that no one else entered the yard.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Physical evidence may be admitted if there is enough evidence for a reasonable juror to find that it is connected to the case. The officers' testimony provides a sufficient link. The two-minute gap and alternative possibilities are weight arguments.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a perfect chain is not required. B: B is wrong because the foundation may be built through combined testimony. D: D is wrong because the standard for admission is not beyond a reasonable doubt.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / FRE 401
lb_student_diagnostic: Treating chain weakness as automatic exclusion instead of a jury-weight issue.
