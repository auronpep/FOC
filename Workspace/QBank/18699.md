Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal drug trial against Aaron, the government offers a clear vial of powder. Two identical unmarked vials were seized from two different tables during the search: one from Aaron's table and one from a table used by another person. The officer who seized them put both in one envelope without labels. At trial, no witness can say which vial came from Aaron's table. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the vial because the government has not produced evidence sufficient to support a finding that this vial came from Aaron's table.
Answer B: Admit the vial because any drug vial found during the search is automatically relevant against Aaron.
Answer C: Admit the vial because chain-of-custody problems always go only to weight.
Answer D: Exclude the vial only if Aaron proves that the vial came from the other person's table.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: For fungible or indistinguishable items, the proponent must provide enough evidence to connect the offered item to the relevant source. Here the vials were identical, unmarked, placed together, and no witness can connect this vial to Aaron. The foundation is insufficient.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because physical evidence must be connected to the party or event in a relevant way. C: C is wrong because some chain problems are so serious that no reasonable finding of identity is supported. D: D is wrong because the proponent bears the threshold burden of connecting the item.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b); FRE 901(a)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student repeats the chain-gap-goes-to-weight rule when the connection is actually missing.
