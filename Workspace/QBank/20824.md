Question: Noah conveyed Farmacre “to Abel so long as the land is used as a farm.” One year later, while Abel was still farming the land, Noah validly conveyed to Abel “all future interests and retained rights that Noah has in Farmacre.” No third party has any interest in Farmacre. What estate does Abel hold after the second conveyance?
Answer A: A fee simple determinable, because the original deed still contains durational language.
Answer B: A life estate measured by Abel's continued farming use.
Answer C: A fee simple absolute, because the present defeasible estate and the possibility of reverter are united in Abel.
Answer D: A fee simple subject to condition subsequent, because Abel must now reenter against himself if farming stops.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Abel holds a fee simple absolute. Abel already had the present fee simple determinable, and Noah then conveyed the retained possibility of reverter to Abel. With the present estate and the only future interest united in the same owner, the limitation is extinguished and Abel holds the full fee simple.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores the merger of the retained future interest into the present estate. B: B is wrong because the estate is not measured by Abel's life. D: D is wrong because a right of entry was never created and a person does not hold a power of termination against that same person's own fee.
lb_rule_reference: Merger / same owner holds present defeasible fee and future interest
