Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A snack manufacturer used a nut-derived oil in a product marketed as fruit crisps. The ingredient name on the package was technical and did not disclose that it came from nuts. Nut allergy reactions were a known risk, and a clear warning would have allowed allergic consumers to avoid the product. An allergic consumer ate the crisps and became ill. Which warning analysis is strongest?
Answer A: No warning was required because consumers know all snacks might contain allergens
Answer B: A failure-to-warn claim is plausible because the nonobvious allergen risk was known and warnable
Answer C: The only possible claim is manufacturing defect because the plaintiff got sick from one package
Answer D: The claim fails unless the consumer proves the manufacturer was negligent in testing the oil
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The risk was foreseeable, nonobvious, and material to safe use by allergic consumers. Adequate labeling could prevent the harm.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The specific nut-derived ingredient was not obvious from the product or technical label, and a clear warning could reduce the risk. C: The product contained the intended ingredient; the problem is failure to communicate a hidden risk. D: Strict failure-to-warn analysis focuses on warning duty, adequacy, and causation, not necessarily negligent testing.
lb_rule_reference: Failure to warn may apply when a product contains a foreseeable nonobvious risk that adequate labeling could reduce.
