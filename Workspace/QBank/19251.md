Outline: Physician-patient
Question: A plaintiff sues a city police officer in federal court under a federal civil-rights statute, alleging excessive force. The plaintiff's treating physician observed the plaintiff's injuries and recorded the plaintiff's statements during treatment. The state where the treatment occurred has a physician-patient privilege statute. The plaintiff moves to exclude the physician's testimony by invoking that state statute. Which ruling is most proper on the plaintiff's privilege objection?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because state privilege law governs any communication made to a physician inside that state.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because federal privilege law incorporates state medical privileges in every federal-question case.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because state law governs, but physician-patient privilege is treated only as a rule of professional confidentiality.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because federal privilege law governs the federal claim, and federal common law does not recognize a general physician-patient privilege on these facts.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The source-law gate decides the item before the relationship label does. In a federal-question civil-rights case, federal privilege law governs unless a specific authority provides otherwise. A state physician-patient statute does not itself control the federal claim, and the federal common law baseline does not include a general physician-patient privilege for ordinary medical-treatment testimony.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it applies state privilege law simply because the treatment occurred in the state. Location is not the source-law test for a federal-question claim. B: B is wrong because it uses the right source-law family but invents an automatic federal incorporation of every state medical privilege. C: C is wrong because it reaches the right result through the wrong source-law route. The problem is not that state privilege is merely professional ethics; the problem is that federal privilege law controls the federal claim.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 source-law gate; no general federal physician-patient privilege
