Question: In a federal extortion trial, Mary testifies that she received a call demanding money and that the caller had the same distinctive accent and speech pattern as Paul, whom she had known for years. Paul objects that Mary cannot be certain the caller was him and that the judge must decide the caller's identity before the jury hears the testimony. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because Mary is not certain.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony unless an expert voice analyst identifies Paul.
Answer C: Admit the testimony if there is enough evidence for a reasonable juror to find that Paul was the caller.
Answer D: Admit the testimony only if the judge finds by a preponderance of the evidence that Paul was the caller.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Voice identification is a conditional foundation issue. The judge screens for sufficient evidence supporting a finding of identity; the jury decides whether to accept the identification and what weight to give it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because uncertainty goes to weight. B: B is wrong because expert identification is not required.
