Outline: Incidental damages
Question: Esther contracted to sell Ruth 500 bolts of fabric for $20 per bolt. Esther failed to deliver. Ruth reasonably covered by buying the same quantity of substitute fabric for $23 per bolt and paid $300 in reasonable shipping charges for the substitute goods. Ruth also claims $4,000 in lost resale profits from a special customer contract that Esther had no reason to know about when the contract was made. What is Ruth's proper recovery?
Answer A: $1,500.
Answer B: $5,800.
Answer C: $300.
Answer D: $1,800.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under UCC Article 2 for buyer cover after seller nondelivery. The direct formula is cover price minus contract price, plus proper incidental damages: (500 × $23 − 500 × $20) + $300 = $1,800. The limiting gates exclude the $4,000 consequential lost profit because Esther had no reason to know of the special resale contract at formation. The result scope is limited to cover and incidental damages, and the answer does not decide a separate consequential-damages claim beyond those facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it omits the reasonable shipping incidental expense. B: B is wrong because it includes the undisclosed special lost profit, a WAL-REM-003 foreseeability-only overreach. C: C is wrong because it awards only incidentals and omits the cover differential.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §§2-712 and 2-715 — buyer cover damages plus incidental damages
