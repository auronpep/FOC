Question: Jeremiah agreed to sell Elizabeth “all pumpkins from the east field this season.” The writing did not state a pickup date. Elizabeth argues that no Article 2 rules can apply because the pumpkins were not yet harvested when the agreement was signed. Which answer is best?
Answer A: Elizabeth is correct because crops become goods only after harvest.
Answer B: Elizabeth is wrong because the contract can involve future goods or growing crops identified to the contract.
Answer C: Elizabeth is correct because any missing pickup date makes the agreement common law.
Answer D: Elizabeth is wrong only if the pumpkins had already been delivered.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Article 2 can govern contracts for future goods, including identified growing crops to be severed and sold. A missing pickup date does not remove the agreement from Article 2; it may call for interpretation, usage, or a gap-filler.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and D are wrong because harvest or delivery is not required before Article 2 can apply to identified future goods. C is wrong because a missing term does not itself change source law. C: C is wrong because a missing term does not itself change source law. D: A and D are wrong because harvest or delivery is not required before Article 2 can apply to identified future goods. C is wrong because a missing term does not itself change source law.
lb_student_diagnostic: Future goods and gap-filler sequencing error.
