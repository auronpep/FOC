Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Attorney-client
Question: A client meets with her lawyer to discuss whether to file for bankruptcy. She brings a longtime friend into the meeting for emotional support. The friend is not an interpreter, paralegal, accountant, or other person needed for legal advice. The opposing party later calls the friend to testify about the client's statements in the meeting. Should the attorney-client privilege bar the friend's testimony?
Answer A: Yes, because the statements were made in a lawyer's office.
Answer B: Yes, because emotional support friends are treated as necessary agents for privilege purposes.
Answer C: No, but only because bankruptcy communications are never privileged.
Answer D: No, because the client voluntarily disclosed the communications in the presence of an unnecessary third party.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege requires confidentiality. Voluntary disclosure in the presence of an unnecessary third party generally destroys confidentiality as to that communication. The decisive axis is third-party necessity, not the location of the meeting.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because The lawyer's office does not automatically make a communication confidential when unnecessary outsiders are present. B: B is wrong because Emotional support alone does not make the friend necessary to obtain legal advice. C: C is wrong because Bankruptcy legal advice can be privileged; the problem is waiver through unnecessary third-party presence.
lb_rule_reference: Attorney-client confidentiality and waiver
lb_student_diagnostic: Student uses relationship or location rather than confidentiality as the protected-object lock.
