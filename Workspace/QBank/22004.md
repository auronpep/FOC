


BARMATRIX Q#: 22004
Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: A tenant rented a house on a local beach under a one- year lease. Two months into the lease, a hurricane came through and completely destroyed the house. The lease contained no provision dealing with natural disasters. The landlord told the tenant that he was required to rebuild the house because he had primary responsibility for the property because of the lease agreement. The tenant refused, and the landlord sued to force the tenant to rebuild. Should the court rule in the landlord's favor?
Answer A: A: Yes, because a tenant has the duty to repair while the lease is in effect.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the lease did not address natural disasters.
Answer C: C: No, because a tenant only has the duty to make minor repairs.
Answer D: D: No, because a tenant has no duty to make any repairs.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the common law, a tenant has a duty not to commit waste, which means the tenant has the duty to make minor repairs to protect the premises (for example, fixing a broken window to keep water out of the property). Many modern lease agreements expressly relieve the tenant of the duty to make any repairs, mainly because most landlords would prefer to make their own repairs to their property. A and B are incorrect because the tenant would not have a duty to completely rebuild the premises. D is incorrect because the common law gives the tenant a duty to make minor repairs to avoid waste.
