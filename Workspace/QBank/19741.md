Question: A software consultant had an at-will maintenance arrangement with a client. A rival knew of the arrangement and sent the client fabricated screenshots purporting to show the consultant selling client data. The client immediately terminated the consultant and hired the rival. Is the consultant likely to prevail on an intentional interference claim against the rival?
Answer A: No, because at-will relationships can never support interference liability
Answer B: Yes, because the rival knowingly used fabricated evidence to disrupt the at-will business relationship
Answer C: Yes, but only because the rival committed fraud against the consultant
Answer D: No, because competition privilege protects any act by a rival
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The rival knew of the relationship and used independently wrongful falsehoods to cause termination and economic loss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: At-will relationships may be protected against knowing disruption by improper means such as fabricated evidence. C: The consultant's claim is for interference with a third-party relationship; fraud would require reliance by the plaintiff on a misrepresentation. D: Competition privilege does not protect fabricated accusations used to disrupt a known relationship.
lb_rule_reference: At-will relationships can support interference liability when the defendant knowingly uses improper means to disrupt them.
