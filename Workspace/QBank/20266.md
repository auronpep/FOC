Question: The city adopted an ordinance requiring every apartment owner to allow a communications company to permanently install a small cable box on the roof of each apartment building. Esther owned an apartment building and objected. The installation would occupy only a small area and would not reduce the building's market value. Esther seeks just compensation. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Esther receives no compensation because any ordinance labeled as public safety is immune from takings review.
Answer B: Esther receives no compensation because the occupied area is small.
Answer C: Esther is entitled to compensation because a permanent physical occupation authorized by the government is a per se taking.
Answer D: Esther receives compensation only if she proves that the ordinance destroyed all economic value of the building.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A permanent physical occupation authorized by the government is generally a per se taking, even if the occupied area is small and the economic impact is minor. The city required a permanent installation on Esther's building.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because public purposes may justify government action but do not eliminate compensation for a taking. B: B is wrong because small size does not defeat the per se rule. D: D is wrong because total economic wipeout is the Lucas regulatory-taking route, not the permanent-physical-occupation route.
lb_rule_reference: Permanent physical occupation / per se taking
