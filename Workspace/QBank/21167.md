MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A developer hired an independent blasting company to use explosives to clear rock for a building foundation. The blasting company used reasonable care, but the blast sent rock fragments through a neighbor's greenhouse. The neighbor sues the developer under strict liability. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The developer is not strictly liable because an independent contractor performed the blasting
Answer B: The developer is strictly liable because the greenhouse damage resulted from the abnormal blasting risk
Answer C: The developer is strictly liable only if it controlled the contractor's exact blasting method
Answer D: The developer is not liable because the blasting company used reasonable care
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rock fragments are within the risk that makes blasting abnormally dangerous, and hiring a contractor does not shift the strict-liability burden away.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The party who has an abnormally dangerous activity carried out cannot avoid strict liability by delegation. C: Control may matter in other contexts, but strict liability for abnormally dangerous activity is not defeated by lack of operational control. D: Due care is not a defense to strict liability for harm within the abnormal risk.
lb_rule_reference: A party that causes an abnormally dangerous activity to be carried out cannot avoid strict liability for harm within the abnormal risk merely by hiring an independent contractor.
