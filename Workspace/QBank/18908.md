Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Lydia sues Matthew after a delivery truck struck her car. Matthew denies that he owned or controlled the truck. Lydia calls an insurance broker to testify that, two months before the collision, Matthew bought a liability policy covering the truck. Lydia offers the testimony only to show ownership or control. Matthew objects that insurance evidence is inadmissible. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because liability insurance evidence is inadmissible for every purpose.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because ownership can be proved only by the vehicle title.
Answer C: Overrule the objection and allow the jury to use the policy as proof that Matthew drove negligently.
Answer D: Overrule the objection for the limited purpose of showing ownership or control, subject to an appropriate limiting instruction on request.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Insurance evidence is not admissible to prove negligence, but it can be relevant for another purpose such as ownership or control. Because Matthew disputes ownership or control, the broker's testimony is admissible for that limited purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because insurance evidence is not categorically barred for all purposes. B: B is wrong because ownership can be proved circumstantially, not only by title. C: C is wrong because the policy may not be used to prove negligence; the admissible route is limited to ownership or control.
lb_rule_reference: Limited purpose / insurance ownership-control
