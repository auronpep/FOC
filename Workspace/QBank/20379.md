Outline: Payment of medical expenses
Question: Peter is injured outside a warehouse. Joseph later pays Peter's emergency-room bill. In Peter's suit against Joseph, Peter offers evidence of Joseph's payment only to show that Joseph was the person who transported Peter to the hospital and knew of Peter's injury before Joseph later deleted warehouse video. Peter does not offer the payment to prove that Joseph caused the injury. Does Rule 409 require exclusion of the payment evidence?
Answer A: Yes. Rule 409 bars all evidence that a party paid or offered to pay an injured person's medical expenses.
Answer B: No. Rule 409 bars medical-payment evidence when offered to prove liability for the injury, but it does not bar a genuinely non-liability use, subject to ordinary relevance, balancing, and limiting-purpose controls.
Answer C: No. The payment is admissible to prove Joseph's liability because paying medical expenses is an admission of fault.
Answer D: Yes. Any payment of medical bills is automatically a settlement offer, even without a disputed claim or release demand.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 409 excludes evidence of furnishing, promising, or offering to pay medical or similar expenses only when offered to prove liability for the injury. Peter offers Joseph's payment to show identity and knowledge before video deletion, not to prove that Joseph caused the injury. The evidence may still be screened for relevance, unfair prejudice, and limiting-purpose problems, but Rule 409 does not require categorical exclusion for this non-liability use.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it treats Rule 409 as an absolute label. The rule targets liability use. C: C is wrong because it flips the policy rule. Rule 409 prevents medical-payment evidence from being used as a liability admission. D: D is wrong because a medical payment is not automatically a settlement offer. Rule 408 requires compromise negotiation about a disputed claim.
