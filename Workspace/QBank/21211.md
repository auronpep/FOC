Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Jacob is charged with robbing a pharmacy. The prosecutor offers a still image from the pharmacy's surveillance video. The store manager testifies that the camera was working, that the image fairly shows the front counter at the time of the robbery, and that the timestamp matches the store's system. Jacob objects that the robber's face is blurry and the image cannot conclusively identify him. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the image because a blurry image can never be relevant.
Answer B: Admit the image if the testimony sufficiently supports that it is a fair image from the pharmacy's camera; the jury decides its weight and any identity inference.
Answer C: Exclude the image unless the manager can identify Jacob as the robber.
Answer D: Admit the image only if a video engineer testifies that the robber is Jacob.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The foundation for a photograph or video image requires sufficient evidence that the image is what the proponent claims. The image need not conclusively identify the defendant. Poor quality affects weight and the strength of the inference.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because blurry evidence can still have probative value. C: C is wrong because the authenticating witness need not identify the defendant. D: D is wrong because expert identification is not automatically required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / FRE 901
lb_student_diagnostic: Demanding conclusive identity from an exhibit that only needs threshold authentication.
