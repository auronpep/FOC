MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Trespass to land
Question: A homeowner invited a contractor inside to inspect a kitchen leak. After the inspection ended, the homeowner told the contractor to leave. The contractor refused and stayed in the kitchen for another hour to pressure the homeowner into signing a repair contract. He caused no physical damage. The homeowner sues for trespass to land. Is the contractor liable?
Answer A: Yes, because he intentionally remained on the land after permission was revoked
Answer B: No, because he entered the home with permission
Answer C: No, because the homeowner suffered no property damage
Answer D: Yes, but only if the contractor intended to violate the homeowner's legal rights
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Initial consent ended when the homeowner told him to leave; intentionally staying after that point is trespass.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Consent can be limited or revoked; it does not authorize remaining indefinitely. C: Trespass to land does not require actual land damage. D: The intent required is intent to enter or remain, not intent to commit a legal wrong.
lb_rule_reference: A person who initially enters land with permission can become a trespasser by intentionally remaining after consent is revoked.
