MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: False imprisonment
Question: A warehouse supervisor accused an employee of taking tools and ordered him into a windowless stockroom. The supervisor stood in the only doorway and said, "If you try to leave before I finish searching your bag, I will knock you down." The employee remained inside for twenty minutes and knew no other exit existed. The employee sues the supervisor for false imprisonment. Is the employee likely to prevail in a false imprisonment claim against the supervisor?
Answer A: No, because the stockroom door was never locked
Answer B: Yes, because the supervisor intentionally confined the employee in a bounded area by threat of force
Answer C: No, because twenty minutes is too short to constitute confinement
Answer D: Yes, but only if the employee proves the accusation was false
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The employee was kept in a bounded area with no known safe exit, was aware of the confinement, and the supervisor intentionally used a threat to prevent leaving.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Confinement can be accomplished by threats of force. A locked door is not required. C: False imprisonment can occur during a short period if the plaintiff is intentionally confined in a bounded area. D: The claim turns on confinement, not whether the theft accusation was true. A truthful suspicion does not itself authorize unlawful confinement.
lb_rule_reference: False imprisonment requires intentional confinement within a bounded area with awareness or harm and no reasonable known safe exit.
