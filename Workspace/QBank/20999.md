


BARMATRIX Q#: 20999
internal_id: TR-111
Outline_code: 61020402
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A stroller manufacturer designed all models in a line with a rear brake lever that could be bumped upward by a caregiver's foot while walking downhill. The stroller met the company's specifications, and every unit had the same brake layout. Before sale, engineers proposed a recessed lever that would have cost less than one dollar per stroller and would not affect folding or braking. The manufacturer rejected the change. A caregiver walking downhill accidentally bumped the lever, the stroller rolled, and a child was injured. The child sues the manufacturer under strict products liability for a design defect. Is the child likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim for a design defect?
Answer A: No, because the stroller that injured the child conformed to the manufacturer's specifications
Answer B: Yes, because the brake layout was linewide and a feasible low-cost alternative would have reduced the foreseeable downhill risk
Answer C: No, because the caregiver accidentally bumped the lever and therefore caused the injury
Answer D: Yes, because any product that injures a child has a defective design
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The hazard existed in all units by design, and the facts identify a practical safer alternative that preserved utility. Those facts support a design-defect claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Design defect challenges the specifications themselves. A product can conform perfectly and still have an unreasonably unsafe intended design. C: Design analysis includes foreseeable use and misuse. A caregiver's foot near a rear brake lever while walking downhill is foreseeable and does not automatically defeat defect liability. D: Injury alone does not prove design defect. The reason liability is plausible is the linewide brake hazard plus feasible alternative, not the mere fact of injury.
lb_rule_reference: A design defect challenges the intended design of the product line when risks outweigh utility or a reasonable alternative design would reduce foreseeable harm.
