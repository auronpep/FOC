Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Joseph contracted with Esther to remodel “the upper rooms” of Esther's inn. The final writing did not define “upper rooms.” Before the final contract, Joseph had sent Esther a written bid describing the upper rooms as “the two guest rooms above the stable.” Esther offers the bid to explain what “upper rooms” means. Joseph objects that the bid predates the final writing. How should the court treat the bid? How should the court treat the bid?
Answer A: Exclude it because a prior writing can never be considered once a final contract is signed.
Answer B: Admit it because written negotiations are never parol evidence.
Answer C: Exclude it because the bid would automatically add a new room to the contract.
Answer D: Admit it if the phrase “upper rooms” is ambiguous and the bid helps explain its meaning.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A prior writing is still parol evidence if it is outside the final writing, but parol evidence may be admissible to explain an ambiguous term. The bid is offered to interpret “upper rooms,” not to add a separate obligation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because prior writings are not automatically excluded; purpose matters. B: B is wrong because prior writings can be parol evidence. C: C is wrong because the offered use is explanation of an existing ambiguous phrase, not necessarily adding a room.
