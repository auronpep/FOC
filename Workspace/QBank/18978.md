Question: A public middle school principal opens every assembly by leading students in a prayer over the loudspeaker. Students are not formally punished for staying silent, but attendance at assemblies is expected. What is the strongest Establishment Clause concern?
Answer A: The prayer is government-sponsored in a public-school setting where coercion concerns are heightened
Answer B: There is no Establishment Clause issue unless students are fined for refusing to pray
Answer C: The prayer is private speech because students hear it rather than write it
Answer D: The prayer is constitutional because all references to religion are historical
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Official school prayer can coerce students even without a formal penalty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: School coercion can be social and institutional, not only legal penalties. C: The principal is speaking in an official capacity at a school assembly. D: Official prayer directed to public-school students is different from many historical-practice settings.
lb_rule_reference: Government-sponsored prayer in public schools raises serious Establishment Clause problems, especially because school settings heighten coercion concerns.
