Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: In a federal breach-of-contract case, Abigail testifies that she called the number listed on a repair company's website. The person who answered said, “Repair office,” and discussed scheduling the repair at issue. The repair company objects that Abigail cannot identify the employee by name. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony because a call to the business's assigned number, concerning business reasonably conducted by phone, supplies sufficient foundation.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because Abigail cannot name the employee who answered.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony unless the business produces its phone logs.
Answer D: Admit the testimony only if the call was recorded.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A telephone conversation may be authenticated by evidence that the call was made to the number assigned to a business and that the call related to business reasonably transacted over the telephone. Abigail need not identify the employee by name.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the caller need not name the answering employee if the business-call circumstances authenticate the conversation. C: C is wrong because phone logs are not required. D: D is wrong because recording is not a prerequisite.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(6) / call to assigned business number
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests circumstantial authentication of a business telephone call.
