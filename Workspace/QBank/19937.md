Question: Officers see Grant commit an armed robbery and chase him on foot. Grant runs into a nearby house and slams the door. Seconds later, officers enter without a warrant, find Grant hiding in a hallway closet, and arrest him. They do not search drawers or unrelated rooms. Was the warrantless entry justified?
Answer A: Yes, because officers were in immediate hot pursuit of a fleeing armed-robbery suspect and limited the entry to capture
Answer B: No, because police may never enter a home without a warrant
Answer C: Yes, because hot pursuit also allowed officers to search every drawer for evidence
Answer D: No, because officers needed to pause outside and seek a warrant once Grant closed the door
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Hot pursuit of a serious fleeing suspect creates an exigency justifying prompt entry. The officers' scope was tied to locating and arresting Grant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Exigent circumstances, including hot pursuit, can justify warrantless home entry. C: The exigency justified entry and locating the fleeing suspect, not an unrelated evidence search. D: Immediate pursuit and risk of escape made waiting for a warrant impracticable on these facts.
lb_rule_reference: Police in immediate hot pursuit of a fleeing felony suspect may enter a home without a warrant to prevent escape, subject to scope limits.
