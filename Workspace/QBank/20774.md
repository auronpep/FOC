Outline: Opinion or reputation
Question: In a federal fraud trial, Thomas testifies for the defendant. The prosecutor attacks Thomas with reputation testimony that he is untruthful. The defendant then calls Philip and asks him to describe three times when Thomas returned money that others had lost. The defendant offers the stories to prove Thomas is truthful. The prosecutor objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because Thomas's character for truthfulness has been attacked.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because the examples involve honest conduct.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because specific truthful acts are not the proper direct form for supporting character for truthfulness.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Philip lacks personal knowledge of the incidents.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: After an attack on character for truthfulness, Rule 608(a) allows reputation or opinion evidence supporting truthfulness. It does not permit direct proof of specific truthful acts to prove the witness's truthful character.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the attack opens only proper support forms. B: B is wrong because relevance to honesty does not cure the wrong form. D: D is wrong because personal knowledge would not make specific truthful acts the proper Rule 608(a) support form.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(a) / specific truthful acts not direct support
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests form control: support after attack must use opinion or reputation, not specific truthful acts.
