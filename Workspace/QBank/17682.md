Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: Gavin plans to break into a closed pharmacy next month. He writes down the pharmacy's hours, buys gloves and a flashlight from a hardware store, and stores them in his apartment. He never goes to the pharmacy after hours and never tries to enter. Police discover the notebook and supplies and charge him with attempted burglary. What is the best defense to the attempt charge?
Answer A: Gavin lacked criminal intent because gloves and a flashlight are lawful items
Answer B: Gavin's conduct was likely mere preparation rather than a substantial step or dangerous proximity to burglary
Answer C: Gavin is guilty because any plan plus any purchased item completes attempt
Answer D: Gavin is guilty only if the pharmacy was open when he bought the supplies
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Gavin planned and bought supplies, but he did not approach the target, try to enter, or otherwise cross from preparation into an attempt. Intent alone is not enough.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The stronger defense is not lack of intent; the facts show planning to burglarize. The issue is whether Gavin took a sufficient act toward the crime. C: Attempt requires more than planning and preparation. The act must be sufficiently close to the completed crime or strongly corroborative of the criminal purpose. D: The store's hours when Gavin bought supplies do not decide whether his later conduct crossed the attempt line.
lb_rule_reference: Planning, researching, or buying ordinary supplies usually remains mere preparation unless the conduct comes dangerously close to the crime or strongly corroborates the criminal purpose.
