Question: An apartment owner installed balcony rails that met the minimum building code height. After several tenants reported that toddlers could climb the horizontal bars like a ladder, a contractor offered to add inexpensive vertical guards. The owner declined. A toddler climbed the rail and fell. The toddler's guardian sues the owner for negligence. Does code compliance conclusively defeat breach in the negligence claim against the owner?
Answer A: Yes, because meeting the building code always proves reasonable care
Answer B: No, compliance is relevant evidence, but the factfinder may consider the known risk and inexpensive guard
Answer C: Yes, because later tenant reports are inadmissible to show any safety issue
Answer D: No, code compliance automatically proves breach whenever an injury occurs
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The owner can use code compliance as due-care evidence, but it does not automatically defeat breach where specific known risks and easy precautions exist.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Compliance is relevant but not always conclusive. Known recurring risk and a cheap additional precaution may support a finding that reasonable care required more. C: The reports preceded the injury and go to notice of risk. The later-remedial-measure concern is not the controlling issue here. D: The plaintiff still must persuade the factfinder that reasonable care required more. Injury alone does not make code-compliant conduct negligent.
lb_rule_reference: Compliance with custom or a minimum safety code is relevant evidence of due care but does not always conclusively defeat breach.
