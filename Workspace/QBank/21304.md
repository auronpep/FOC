Outline: Strict scrutiny (suspect) classifications
Question: A state enacts a statute directing that in any public school district in which a single racial group constitutes more than 65% of enrolled students, the district must implement a mandatory transfer program to move students of that racial group to schools in neighboring districts to reduce racial concentration. The transfer program is triggered solely by the enrolled students' racial identification. The state justifies the program as necessary to produce racially integrated public schools and improve educational outcomes for minority students. A parent of a student assigned to a mandatory transfer sues, arguing that the race-based transfer program violates Equal Protection. Applying Equal Protection strict scrutiny to the mandatory racial transfer program, which of the following best describes the constitutional analysis?
Answer A: The program is constitutional because achieving racial integration in public schools is a compelling state interest, and the Court has recognized integration as sufficient to justify any race-conscious remedy
Answer B: The program receives intermediate scrutiny because the racial classification is designed to benefit minority students rather than to burden them
Answer C: The program is subject to strict scrutiny, and its constitutionality turns on whether racial integration of public schools is a compelling interest and whether the mandatory race-based transfer mechanism is narrowly tailored — including whether race-neutral alternatives were seriously considered
Answer D: The program is unconstitutional per se because any state-mandated racial sorting of public school students is categorically prohibited under the Equal Protection Clause regardless of the state's motivation
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Facially race-based classifications receive strict scrutiny. The program must survive both prongs. Racial integration may qualify as a compelling interest in the public school context, but narrow tailoring requires that the specific means be no broader than necessary and that race-neutral alternatives be genuinely considered and found inadequate. A categorical race-based transfer without individualized consideration or evidence that less restrictive alternatives fail will face significant constitutional difficulty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Strict scrutiny is two-pronged. Even if racial integration is a compelling interest, the program must also be narrowly tailored. A mandatory race-based transfer triggered solely by racial demographics — without individualized consideration or evidence that less-intrusive alternatives were considered — may fail narrow tailoring. The government must show the specific means are narrowly tailored, not merely that the ends are compelling. B: The Supreme Court has expressly held that all facial race-based classifications receive strict scrutiny regardless of the beneficent intent behind them. Adarand Constructors v. Peña confirmed that federal and state racial classifications are subject to strict scrutiny whether the goal is remediation or integration. There is no "benign" exception to strict scrutiny for race. D: The Equal Protection Clause does not create a per se bar on all race-conscious remediation in public schools. The analysis proceeds under strict scrutiny, where some narrow, carefully tailored race-conscious measures may survive. The program is not unconstitutional per se; it must be evaluated under strict scrutiny's two prongs.
lb_rule_reference: Race is a suspect classification. Any law that classifies individuals by race on its face triggers strict scrutiny: the government must demonstrate a compelling interest served by narrowly tailored means.
