Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: Officers arrested Vera outside a warehouse after finding and securing the only gun reported in the incident. Vera was handcuffed in a patrol car, the scene was cleared, and no other danger was suspected. Without Miranda warnings, a detective asked, "Who planned the robbery with you?" Vera named two people. Does the public-safety exception apply to Vera's answer?
Answer A: Yes, because the case originally involved a gun
Answer B: Yes, because questions about accomplices always protect officer safety
Answer C: No, because the scene was secure and the question was investigative custodial interrogation requiring Miranda warnings
Answer D: No, because public-safety answers are always inadmissible at trial
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Vera was in custody, no immediate danger remained, and the question sought evidence about the robbery. The public-safety exception does not apply.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The gun was already secured and the scene cleared. The question sought investigative information about accomplices, not immediate safety. B: The facts do not show an immediate threat from accomplices. The question asked who planned the robbery. D: Proper public-safety answers can be admitted. This question fails because the emergency had ended.
lb_rule_reference: Public-safety questioning must be limited to an immediate safety need; once the danger is secured, unwarned investigative questions remain subject to Miranda.
