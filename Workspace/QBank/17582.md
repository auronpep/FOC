Question: Naomi agreed to buy Aaron's building. The contract stated, “Buyer's duty to purchase is subject to Buyer completing an environmental inspection by April 1.” Naomi scheduled an inspector for March 25. Aaron refused to unlock the building and then claimed on April 2 that Naomi's inspection condition had failed. What is the best result?
Answer A: Aaron may cancel because the inspection was not completed by April 1.
Answer B: Naomi may cancel because every failed inspection condition benefits the buyer only.
Answer C: Aaron cannot rely on the failed inspection condition because he prevented the inspection from occurring.
Answer D: Naomi must buy because she did not complete the inspection by the deadline.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A party cannot prevent the occurrence of a condition and then rely on that nonoccurrence. Aaron's refusal to provide access excused the inspection condition as against him.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Aaron caused the condition failure. B: B is wrong because the issue is Aaron's prevention, not an automatic buyer cancellation right. D: D is wrong because Naomi's failure to inspect was caused by Aaron's refusal to provide access.
lb_rule_reference: Prevention doctrine / inspection condition
