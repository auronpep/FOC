Question: A store patron collapsed in a checkout lane. Another customer announced, "I am calling 911 and will stay with her," so the cashier stopped dialing and went to clear the aisle. The customer then forgot to call, left the store, and the patron's condition worsened before help arrived. The patron sues the customer for negligence. Is the patron likely to establish a duty in the negligence claim against the customer?
Answer A: No, because a customer has no general duty to rescue a stranger in a store
Answer B: Yes, because the customer undertook aid and induced reliance, requiring reasonable care in that undertaking
Answer C: Yes, because beginning a rescue makes the customer strictly liable for any bad outcome
Answer D: No, because only professionals can create a duty by promising to help
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A voluntary undertaking plus reliance is a recognized affirmative-duty exception. The duty is to act reasonably in the undertaking, not to guarantee a successful rescue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The customer's announcement induced reliance and displaced the cashier's call. That voluntary undertaking can create a duty to use reasonable care in carrying it out. C: Undertaking aid creates a duty of reasonable care. It does not make the rescuer strictly liable for every bad medical outcome. D: A voluntary undertaking can create a duty even for a nonprofessional when others reasonably rely on it.
lb_rule_reference: One who voluntarily undertakes aid and induces reliance must use reasonable care in the undertaking.
