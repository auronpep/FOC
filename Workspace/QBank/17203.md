Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: Ramon was serving a sentence in state prison. A corrections investigator asked Ramon if he would speak in a conference room about a fight in the yard. Ramon was told he could decline, was not restrained beyond normal prison rules, and was returned to his housing unit after fifteen minutes. Without Miranda warnings, Ramon admitted starting the fight. Should Ramon's statement be suppressed under Miranda?
Answer A: Yes, because any prisoner is always in custody for Miranda purposes
Answer B: Yes, because the investigator asked about a criminal assault
Answer C: No, because the prison interview did not add restraints comparable to Miranda custody
Answer D: No, because prisoners have no Fifth Amendment privilege while serving a sentence
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Although Ramon was incarcerated, the interview was brief, optional, and involved no added restraint beyond ordinary confinement. Miranda warnings were not required absent custodial interrogation in the relevant sense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Miranda custody for prisoners asks whether the interview imposed additional coercive restraint beyond ordinary incarceration. Ramon was told he could decline and faced no added restraint. B: Interrogation alone does not trigger Miranda. Custody must also be present in the Miranda sense. D: Prisoners retain Fifth Amendment protection. The statement is admissible because Miranda custody was absent, not because the privilege disappears.
lb_rule_reference: A prisoner is in Miranda custody only when the questioning imposes restraints beyond ordinary prison life that are comparable to formal arrest for the interview setting.
