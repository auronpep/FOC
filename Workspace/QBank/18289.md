MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A warehouse hired specialists to fumigate a sealed storage room with a highly toxic gas uncommon in the area. The fumigation was underway when a visitor arrived at the front office to pick up paperwork. The visitor never entered the fumigation zone and was not exposed to gas. While walking through the office lobby, the visitor slipped on rainwater tracked in by customers and broke an arm. The visitor sues the warehouse for strict liability based on the abnormally dangerous fumigation. Is the visitor likely to prevail on a strict liability claim for abnormally dangerous fumigation?
Answer A: Yes, because toxic-gas fumigation is an abnormally dangerous activity
Answer B: No, because the broken arm resulted from a lobby slip, not from the toxic-gas risk that made fumigation abnormal
Answer C: No, because the warehouse used specialists rather than doing the fumigation itself
Answer D: Yes, because strict liability applies to all injuries occurring while the dangerous activity is underway
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The injury did not result from gas exposure, escape, contamination, or another fumigation risk. Strict liability is clipped at harm-within-risk, leaving any claim to ordinary negligence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Even if the fumigation is abnormally dangerous, strict liability reaches only harm from the abnormal risk. A lobby slip on rainwater is unrelated to toxic-gas danger. C: The claim fails because the harm was outside the abnormal risk, not because specialists were used. Delegating dangerous work does not automatically eliminate liability for covered abnormal-risk harm. D: Temporal overlap is not enough. The injury must arise from the risk that makes the activity abnormally dangerous.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity is limited to the type of harm that makes the activity abnormal; unrelated premises accidents are not covered by that doctrine.
