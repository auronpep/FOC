Question: In a federal criminal case, the prosecutor seeks to offer David's confession. David asks for a hearing, arguing that the confession was involuntary. The prosecutor responds that the statement is admissible as David's own opposing-party statement and that the jury can hear the facts about voluntariness. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Hold the hearing outside the jury's presence to decide the preliminary admissibility issue.
Answer B: Admit the confession automatically because a party's own statement is never subject to preliminary review.
Answer C: Let the jury hear all voluntariness evidence first, because voluntariness is always a question of weight.
Answer D: Refuse the hearing unless David first promises to testify at trial on the merits.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When the issue is the admissibility of a confession in a criminal case, the court must conduct the hearing outside the jury's presence. The party-statement label does not bypass the preliminary admissibility issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because opposing-party statement treatment does not defeat voluntariness review. C: C is wrong because admissibility of the confession is for the court before the jury hears it. D: D is wrong because requesting a preliminary hearing does not require a merits-testimony promise.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(c) / confession hearing outside jury
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests preliminary-hearing routing and party-admission decoy control.
