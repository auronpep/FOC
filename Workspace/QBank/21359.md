Question: Deborah and Isaac are merchants who buy and sell oil. They orally agree that Isaac will buy 1,000 jars of oil from Deborah. Deborah sends Isaac a signed written confirmation. Six days after receiving it, Isaac sends Deborah a signed letter stating, “I object to your confirmation and deny that we made this contract.” Deborah sues Isaac. How should the court treat the merchant-confirmation issue?
Answer A: Deborah wins because Isaac received the confirmation.
Answer B: Deborah wins because Isaac's objection was not sent the same day.
Answer C: Deborah wins because only the sender may object to a confirmation.
Answer D: Isaac's timely written objection prevents the confirmation from satisfying the Statute of Frauds against him under the merchant-confirmation rule.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A recipient merchant avoids the confirmation rule by giving written notice of objection within 10 days after receiving the confirmation. Isaac objected in writing after six days, so the confirmation does not satisfy the SOF against him under §2-201(2).
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because receipt alone is not enough when timely written objection follows. B: B is wrong because the statute gives 10 days, not same-day notice. C: C is wrong because the recipient is the one who must object to avoid being bound by the confirmation.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(2)
