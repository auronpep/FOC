


BARMATRIX Q#: 22609
Outline_code: 44040300
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Equal Protection Clause
Question: A man applied for a job at a local police department and was not hired. The man sued the department for discrimination, claiming that he was not hired because he was Asian and a less- qualified white man was hired in his place. The court ruled in the man's favor, finding that the police department had a long history of discriminating against people with Asian heritage. Pursuant to a consent decree, the department agreed to hire five employees with Asian heritage over the next three years and hire a person of Asian heritage to fill a vacant leadership position at the department. A white woman applied for a job and was not hired. The woman sued, arguing that she was not hired because of the preference given to employees of Asian heritage. She claimed that the department's hiring policy was unconstitutional. Is a court likely to find that the department's hiring policy is unconstitutional?
Answer A: A: Yes, because race cannot be used in making hiring decisions.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the preference violates the Equal Protection Clause.
Answer C: C: No, because the preference is necessary for a compelling state interest.
Answer D: D: No, because the department discriminated against people of Asian heritage in the past.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: If an entity committed racial discrimination in the past, a racial quota to remedy that discrimination does not violate the Constitution. Here, the police department was found to commit racial discrimination against people of Asian heritage, so the hiring preference is constitutional. A and B are incorrect because the racial preference in this question is constitutional as a remedy to the department's past discrimination. C is incorrect because the policy is only constitutional because it is intended to remedy past racial discrimination done by the department itself. A general state hiring policy or a preference without a finding of past discrimination by the particular entity using the preference would not be constitutional.
