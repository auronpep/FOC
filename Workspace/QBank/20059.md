Question: Ruth sold Vineyard Lot to Boaz. In the same deed, Ruth stated, “Seller grants Buyer and Buyer's successors a permanent right to cross Seller's retained Orchard Lot over the marked lane for access to the public road.” The deed did not use the word “easement.” Ruth later argued that no express easement was created because the deed omitted that word. Is Ruth correct?
Answer A: Yes, because the word “easement” is required in every express easement grant.
Answer B: Yes, because a right to cross land is always a revocable license unless called an easement.
Answer C: No. The deed's operative grant of a permanent crossing right to the buyer and successors can create an express easement without using the word “easement.”
Answer D: No, but only because Boaz received a profit to remove crops from Orchard Lot.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: No particular magic word is required if the writing manifests an intent to create a land-use right and reasonably identifies the burden and benefit. The deed grants Boaz and successors a permanent right to cross the retained parcel over a marked lane for access.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because substance controls over labels. B: B is wrong because permanent grant language to successors is not merely revocable permission. D: D is wrong because the right is access, not removal of crops or other resources.
lb_rule_reference: Grant language / no magic word required
