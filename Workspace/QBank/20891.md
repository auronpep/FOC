MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A portable generator line placed the hot exhaust port directly under the carrying handle. All units used the same layout. The box warned, "Exhaust surfaces may be hot after operation." Before sale, engineers proposed moving the handle three inches away from the exhaust port at minimal cost without affecting performance. The company kept the original layout. A homeowner turned off the generator, waited one minute, grabbed the handle as intended, and suffered a severe burn. The homeowner sues the manufacturer under strict products liability for a design defect. Is the homeowner likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim for a design defect?
Answer A: No, because the box included a warning that exhaust surfaces may be hot
Answer B: Yes, because all units shared a handle-placement hazard and a feasible safer layout was available
Answer C: No, because the homeowner was not using the generator at the moment of the burn
Answer D: Yes, because the warning was written in language the homeowner did not remember reading
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim challenges the intended design of every unit. The handle's location near the hot exhaust created a foreseeable burn risk, and a practical redesign could reduce that risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A warning does not automatically cure an unreasonable design. The facts identify a linewide handle placement hazard and a feasible alternative that would reduce burns without impairing utility. C: Grabbing the carrying handle shortly after shutdown is a foreseeable use of a portable generator. The design risk persists during that ordinary handling. D: The design-defect claim does not turn mainly on the homeowner's memory of the warning. The stronger reason is the unreasonable linewide handle placement and feasible alternative.
lb_rule_reference: A warning does not automatically cure an unreasonable design when a feasible safer alternative would reduce a linewide hazard without undermining utility.
