Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: Dana became furious when she saw a stranger, Paul, kick her dog on a public sidewalk. Dana picked up a large rock and hurled it directly at Paul's head from six feet away. The rock missed Paul by inches and shattered a storefront window behind him. Paul was unharmed. The jurisdiction defines battery as intentional application of unlawful force causing harmful or offensive contact, and assault as intentional creation of reasonable apprehension of immediate harmful contact. The prosecutor charges Dana with attempted battery. Which mental state must the prosecution prove to establish attempted battery?
Answer A: That Dana acted recklessly with respect to the possibility of hitting Paul
Answer B: That Dana knew her conduct was substantially certain to cause contact
Answer C: That Dana specifically intended to make harmful or offensive contact with Paul
Answer D: That Dana was negligent in failing to foresee that throwing a rock at a person would cause contact
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Attempt is a specific-intent crime at common law. The prosecution must prove Dana's conscious object was to apply force to Paul. The fact that battery itself may be classified as a general-intent crime does not lower the mens rea requirement for the attempt. Because Dana aimed directly at Paul, this specific intent is easily inferred.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Attempt always requires specific intent — the purpose to bring about the forbidden result. Recklessness cannot satisfy the mens rea element of attempt even if it would satisfy the completed offense. B: Knowledge of substantial certainty is the civil Garratt v. Dailey standard, not the common-law criminal attempt standard. Attempt demands purpose — the goal of achieving the result. D: Negligence is categorically insufficient for attempt. Criminal attempt requires conscious purpose to achieve the prohibited result; it is the paradigmatic specific-intent offense.
lb_rule_reference: At common law, criminal attempt requires the specific intent to commit the target crime; the mental state of the underlying offense is irrelevant to the attempt charge
