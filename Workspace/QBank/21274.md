Question: A delivery van negligently struck a child in a crosswalk. The child's parent was standing on the corner, saw the impact occur, and immediately suffered severe shock with physical symptoms. The parent was not personally touched by the van and was not in danger of being hit. The parent sues the delivery company for negligent infliction of emotional distress. Is the parent likely to recover on a bystander negligent infliction of emotional distress claim?
Answer A: Yes, because the parent was a close relative who contemporaneously perceived the injury-causing event and suffered physical symptoms
Answer B: Yes, because anyone who sees a serious accident may recover for emotional distress
Answer C: No, because the parent was not personally in the zone of physical danger
Answer D: No, because the parent must prove the delivery company intended to cause distress
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Many jurisdictions allow bystander NIED for a close relative who witnesses the accident as it occurs and suffers serious distress or physical symptoms. The parent fits that recognized category.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Bystander NIED is limited. It generally requires a close relationship and contemporaneous perception, not mere presence as any witness. C: Zone of danger is not the only NIED category. Bystander recovery may apply to close relatives who contemporaneously perceive the injury. D: NIED is negligence-based. The parent need not prove intent if the bystander NIED requirements are met.
lb_rule_reference: Bystander NIED is commonly limited to plaintiffs who closely relate to the injured person and contemporaneously perceive the injury-causing event.
