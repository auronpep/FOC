Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Rachel agreed in a signed writing to buy a used wagon from Samuel for $900. The writing identified the wagon and price but said nothing about accessories and did not say it was complete. Rachel offers evidence that, before signing, Samuel orally promised to include the wagon's spare wheel and cover. Samuel objects under the parol evidence rule. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence if the writing was final but only partially integrated and the accessories promise is a consistent additional term.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because all accessories must be listed in the written sale contract.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence because oral evidence is never admissible in a sale of goods.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if Rachel proves Samuel had no title to the wagon.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A final writing that is not complete and exclusive may be supplemented by consistent additional terms. The spare wheel and cover can be a collateral, consistent addition if the parties did not intend the writing to be the exclusive statement of all terms.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because not every collateral accessory term must appear in the writing. C: C is wrong because Article 2 allows consistent additional terms in appropriate circumstances. D: D is wrong because title is not the issue; integration level and consistency control.
lb_rule_reference: Collateral consistent term / partial integration
