Question: David orally agreed to sell a cottage to Esther. Esther already lived in the cottage as David's month-to-month tenant. After the oral agreement, Esther continued living there, paid the same monthly rent, and made no improvements. David later refused to convey the cottage and asserted the Statute of Frauds. Esther argues that her continued possession is part performance. How should the court rule?
Answer A: The continued possession is insufficient because it is equally consistent with Esther's existing tenancy and does not clearly point to the alleged sale contract.
Answer B: The continued possession is sufficient because any possession by a buyer satisfies the Statute of Frauds.
Answer C: The oral sale is enforceable because rent payments are treated as purchase payments whenever a sale is alleged.
Answer D: The oral sale is automatically void because land contracts are never subject to equitable exceptions.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Part performance must be strongly referable to the alleged land-sale agreement. Esther's continued possession under an existing tenancy and continued rent payments are not unequivocal acts pointing to a sale.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because possession must be referable to the sale, not merely consistent with another legal relationship. C: C is wrong because rent does not automatically become purchase money. D: D is wrong because land SOF has equitable part-performance exceptions in appropriate cases.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law land contract SOF / unequivocal acts
