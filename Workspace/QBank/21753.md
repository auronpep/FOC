Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Appointment and removal
Question: Congress creates a new federal enforcement board to investigate companies and issue binding cease-and-desist orders for violations of federal law. The statute provides that two members of the House and two members of the Senate will jointly select the board's enforcement director. The director serves a fixed term and has final authority to bring enforcement actions. What is the strongest constitutional objection?
Answer A: Congress may appoint the director because Congress created the office
Answer B: The appointment is likely invalid because members of Congress are selecting an officer who exercises executive power
Answer C: The appointment is valid because the director serves for only a fixed term
Answer D: The appointment is invalid only if the director is removable for cause
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The director has continuing federal duties and significant enforcement authority. Congress cannot reserve appointment of that executive officer to members of Congress.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Article II controls appointment of federal officers. Congress may create offices, but it may not itself appoint an executive officer. C: A fixed term does not let Congress appoint an executive officer. The appointment route remains constitutionally defective. D: The defect here is appointment by Congress. Removal limits are analytically separate.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may create federal offices and define duties, but Congress itself may not appoint officers who exercise executive power.
