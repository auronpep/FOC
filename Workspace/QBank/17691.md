MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A pipeline company operates an interstate natural gas line under a federal certificate of public convenience and necessity issued by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission. A landowner whose property abuts the pipeline sues the pipeline company in federal court alleging only state-law trespass and nuisance arising from vibrations that crack her foundation. The pipeline company's answer asserts a federal defense: that federal regulations governing pipeline operation preempt the state-law claims and require dismissal. The pipeline company moves to retain the case in federal court, arguing that the preemption defense raises a substantial federal question. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Should the federal court retain jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because the pipeline company's preemption defense raises a substantial federal question that is central to the outcome
Answer B: Yes, because the landowner's claims arise out of the pipeline company's federally certified operations, embedding a federal element in the cause of action
Answer C: No, because the complaint alleges only state-law causes of action and the federal issue enters only through the defense
Answer D: No, because the amount in controversy does not exceed $75,000
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the well-pleaded complaint rule, federal-question jurisdiction under § 1331 exists only when federal law creates the plaintiff's cause of action or a necessary element of the state cause of action is a question of federal law. Here the complaint pleads only state-law trespass and nuisance; no element of those claims requires interpretation of federal law. The pipeline company's federal preemption defense is irrelevant to jurisdiction. The court lacks § 1331 jurisdiction and must dismiss or remand if removed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under the well-pleaded complaint rule, courts look only at the plaintiff's complaint as drafted — not at defenses, counterclaims, or anticipated federal issues. A federal defense, however substantial, does not create § 1331 jurisdiction. The pipeline company's preemption argument is irrelevant to the jurisdictional analysis. B: The plaintiff's claims are state-law trespass and nuisance. Those causes of action are created by state law, not federal law. The fact that the defendant holds a federal certificate does not transform a state tort into a federal cause of action. *Grable* applies only when the federal element is a necessary element of a state-created cause of action, and here the plaintiff's claims require no proof of any federal law. D: Federal-question jurisdiction under § 1331 has no amount-in-controversy requirement. The reason federal jurisdiction is lacking is the absence of a federal question in the plaintiff's complaint — not a failure to meet any monetary threshold.
lb_rule_reference: 28 U.S.C. § 1331 — federal question jurisdiction exists when a federal law creates the plaintiff's cause of action, judged by the well-pleaded complaint rule; anticipated defenses and counterclaims are ignored.
