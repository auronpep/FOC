Question: A nonprofit that helps eligible voters register sues a state official over a new rule that forces the nonprofit to spend staff time rebuilding its registration program and turning away clients. The nonprofit alleges those costs directly impair its mission. Does the nonprofit allege a sufficient injury for its own standing?
Answer A: No, because organizations can never suffer Article III injury
Answer B: No, because the nonprofit is asserting only the public's interest in lawful elections
Answer C: Yes, because diversion of resources and impairment of activities can be concrete organizational injury
Answer D: Yes, but only because at least one member must have standing
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The organization alleges that the rule directly changes its operations and imposes resource costs that a favorable order would redress.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Organizations can have standing when they suffer their own concrete injury. B: The nonprofit alleges direct resource and mission impairment, not only civic disagreement. D: This question asks about the organization's own direct injury, not member-based representational standing.
lb_rule_reference: An organization can sue on its own behalf if it suffers a concrete injury, such as a diversion of resources or impairment of activities, that is traceable and redressable.
