Question: In a federal arson trial, the prosecutor asks Aaron, “What did Samuel tell you about Moses?” Moses objects, and the judge sustains the objection before Aaron answers. Aaron nevertheless blurts out, “Samuel said Moses burned the house.” Moses immediately moves to strike and asks that the jury be told to disregard the answer. What should the court do?
Answer A: Deny the motion because Aaron's answer was already heard by the jury.
Answer B: Grant the motion and instruct the jury to disregard the volunteered answer.
Answer C: Deny the motion because the original objection, once sustained, exhausted Moses's remedy.
Answer D: Grant the motion only if Aaron acted intentionally.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The witness answered after the court sustained the objection. The court should strike the volunteered answer and, to the extent practicable, protect the jury from using inadmissible matter by instructing it to disregard the answer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because matter already heard can be stricken and limited by instruction. C: C is wrong because a motion to strike is the correct cleanup step after the witness blurts the answer. D: D is wrong because the remedy does not depend on the witness's intent.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(d); FRE 611(a) / answer after sustained objection
