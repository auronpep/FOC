Outline: Expectation damages
Question: A seller contracted to make a specially configured machine for Amos for $100,000. Amos repudiated after the seller had reasonably incurred $70,000 in manufacturing costs. If Amos had performed, the seller would have made a $20,000 profit. The completed machine had no ordinary market, but the seller salvaged parts for $15,000. Incidental damages are not claimed. If the ordinary market formula is inadequate, what is the seller's proper expectation measure?
Answer A: $20,000, the expected profit only.
Answer B: $70,000, the costs incurred only.
Answer C: $75,000, the expected profit plus costs incurred minus salvage proceeds.
Answer D: $100,000, the full contract price without credit for salvage.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When the ordinary seller market measure is inadequate, the UCC permits recovery of the profit the seller would have made from full performance, plus reasonable costs incurred, with due credit for payments or resale/salvage proceeds. The formula is $20,000 profit + $70,000 costs - $15,000 salvage = $75,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it omits reasonably incurred costs. B: B is wrong because it omits the expected profit needed to put the seller in the performance position. D: D is wrong because it ignores the required credit for salvage proceeds and may overcompensate the seller.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-708(2)
