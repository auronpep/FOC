Question: A passenger suffered one indivisible injury in a crash caused by two negligent drivers. Before trial, the passenger settled with Driver A for $30,000. The written settlement released "Driver A only" and expressly reserved claims against Driver B. The jurisdiction gives Driver B a dollar-for-dollar credit for the settlement but does not treat a release of one tortfeasor as a release of all unless the document says so. The passenger then obtains a $100,000 judgment against Driver B. In the passenger's negligence action against Driver B, what is the effect of the release and satisfaction rules?
Answer A: The release of Driver A automatically releases Driver B, so the passenger recovers nothing from Driver B
Answer B: Driver B owes $70,000, and once that amount plus the settlement fully satisfies the $100,000 judgment, the passenger cannot collect more
Answer C: Driver B owes the full $100,000 because the settlement with Driver A is irrelevant
Answer D: Driver B owes $30,000 because a nonsettling tortfeasor pays only the amount paid by the settling tortfeasor
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The $30,000 settlement is credited against the $100,000 judgment, leaving $70,000 from Driver B. Once the passenger has received full satisfaction of the judgment, double recovery is barred.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The jurisdiction and document preserve claims against Driver B. A release of one tortfeasor is not automatically a release of all here. C: Preserving the claim against Driver B does not erase the settlement credit. The passenger cannot collect more than full satisfaction. D: The settlement amount is credited against the judgment; it is not the cap on the nonsettling defendant's liability.
lb_rule_reference: A release of one tortfeasor does not necessarily release others unless its terms or jurisdictional rule say so, but full satisfaction of the judgment prevents double recovery.
