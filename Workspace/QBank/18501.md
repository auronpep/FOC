Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: A grand jury subpoena orders Dana, the sole owner of a small business, to produce "all ledgers showing cash payments from the unlicensed gambling room." The government does not know whether such ledgers exist or whether Dana has them. Dana argues that producing responsive documents would admit that the ledgers exist, are in her possession, and are authentic. What is Dana's strongest Fifth Amendment argument?
Answer A: The subpoena is invalid because the contents of all business records are automatically privileged
Answer B: The privilege cannot apply because producing documents is always physical conduct, never testimony
Answer C: Producing the ledgers may be testimonial because it would admit existence, possession, and authenticity of documents the government cannot otherwise identify
Answer D: The privilege applies only if Dana is in custody and has not received Miranda warnings
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Dana's strongest argument is not that the ledger contents are privileged, but that compelled production would itself communicate incriminating facts not already a foregone conclusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Voluntarily created document contents are not automatically privileged. The issue is the testimonial act of producing them. B: The act of production can communicate facts such as existence, possession, and authenticity. D: The act-of-production privilege can arise in response to subpoenas outside custody. Miranda is not the only Fifth Amendment doctrine.
lb_rule_reference: The contents of voluntarily created documents are not privileged merely because they are incriminating, but a compelled act of production may have testimonial significance unless immunity or another doctrine removes the risk.
