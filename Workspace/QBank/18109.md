Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers stopped a car solely because it was driving through a neighborhood known for narcotics arrests. They had no traffic violation, warrant, or reasonable suspicion tied to the driver or passenger. During the stop, they ordered the passenger out, patted him down without justification, and found heroin in his jacket. The passenger moves to suppress the heroin. What is the strongest basis for the passenger's suppression motion?
Answer A: The passenger may suppress the heroin because he was personally seized by the unlawful traffic stop, and the heroin was found as a direct result of that seizure
Answer B: The passenger may suppress the heroin only if he owned the car
Answer C: The passenger may not suppress the heroin because passengers never have standing in traffic-stop cases
Answer D: The passenger may suppress the heroin only if the driver also moves to suppress
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A traffic stop seizes both the driver and the passenger. The passenger need not claim privacy in the car when his own person was unlawfully detained and searched. If the heroin is the fruit of that seizure and no exception applies, suppression is available to him.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: This motion challenges the stop and search of the passenger's person, not a search of the car. The passenger's personal seizure supplies the Fourth Amendment interest. C: A passenger may lack privacy in a car compartment but still challenge his own unlawful detention. D: Fourth Amendment standing is personal. Each defendant asserts his own rights and need not depend on a co-defendant's motion.
lb_rule_reference: Fourth Amendment rights are personal, but every passenger is seized during a traffic stop and may seek suppression of evidence that is the fruit of that unlawful seizure.
