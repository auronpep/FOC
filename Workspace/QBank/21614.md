Question: In a federal civil battery trial, nine-year-old Samuel is called by Hannah to describe what he saw in a hallway. Samuel becomes frightened and answers only “yes” or “no.” Hannah's lawyer asks, “Were you standing by the elevator when Sarah pushed Eve?” Sarah objects that the question is leading. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a child witness may never be led on direct examination.
Answer B: Overrule the objection if the court finds leading questions are necessary to develop Samuel's testimony.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because only an adverse party may be led.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Samuel has no personal knowledge.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Leading questions may be allowed on direct when necessary to develop a witness's testimony. A frightened child who can respond only to narrow questions may justify controlled leading questions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the rule allows flexibility to develop testimony. C treats the adverse-party category as exclusive.
