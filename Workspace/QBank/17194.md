Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Settlement negotiations and offers of compromise
Question: After a written demand letter claimed $200,000 in damages from a delivery accident, the delivery company disputed both liability and amount. During a settlement meeting, the company's claims manager said, “Our driver was probably 80 percent at fault, so we will offer $90,000.” The plaintiff offers the statement at trial to prove the company's liability. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the fault statement because Rule 408 protects only the dollar offer, not factual statements.
Answer B: Admit the statement because it is an opposing-party statement and therefore cannot be excluded by a policy rule.
Answer C: Exclude only the dollar amount but admit the percentage-of-fault statement for liability.
Answer D: Exclude the statement because it was made during compromise negotiations about a disputed claim and is offered to prove liability.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 408 bars offers, conduct, and statements made during compromise negotiations about a disputed claim when offered to prove or disprove validity or amount. The explicit fault statement is within the protected negotiation, not a free-standing admission. The decisive axis is settlement trigger plus offered purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is the factual-statement myth. Rule 408 covers statements made during compromise negotiations about the claim. B: B is wrong because Opposing-party statement status does not override an independent public-policy exclusion. C: C is wrong because This incorrectly splits a protected negotiation statement from the protected compromise context.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 408 / statements during compromise negotiations
lb_student_diagnostic: Student uses hearsay admission logic to override a policy-purpose exclusion.
