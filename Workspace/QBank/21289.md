Question: Solomon granted Hannah a written easement over Solomon's road “for construction access until Hannah's house on Lot 1 is completed.” Hannah completed the house and obtained a certificate of occupancy. Six months later, Hannah continued using Solomon's road for ordinary daily access even though Lot 1 had its own separate driveway to the public road. May Solomon stop Hannah's continued use under the construction easement?
Answer A: No, because every construction easement automatically becomes a permanent residential access easement.
Answer B: Yes, because the easement ended by its express duration or condition when the house was completed.
Answer C: No, because an easement can terminate only by merger.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Hannah physically damaged Solomon's road.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement may terminate according to its express terms. This grant was limited to construction access until the house was completed. Once the stated event occurred, the construction easement ended and did not become a general permanent access easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the grant was purpose- and time-limited. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because merger is only one termination method. D: D is wrong because termination follows from the easement's terms, not from physical damage.
lb_rule_reference: Termination by express duration or condition
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is ignoring limiting language after recognizing that an easement once existed.
