Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A station employee negligently shoved a passenger who was boarding a train while carrying an unmarked heavy toolbox. The toolbox fell and struck another passenger standing one foot away on the platform, breaking her ankle. Under a Palsgraf-style duty and proximate-cause analysis, may the nearby passenger recover?
Answer A: Yes, because she was within the foreseeable zone of physical danger from mishandling the boarding passenger
Answer B: No, because Palsgraf bars every claim by a bystander
Answer C: No, because the employee did not know the toolbox was heavy
Answer D: Yes, because duty is owed to the whole world for every consequence
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A person standing one foot away is a foreseeable plaintiff for impact from dropped luggage or tools.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The problem is foreseeability of the plaintiff and risk, not bystander status alone. C: Physical impact to a nearby passenger from forced boarding remains foreseeable. D: That overstates the Palsgraf limitation.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff within the foreseeable zone of physical danger may recover if the defendant's negligence caused the type of harm risked.
