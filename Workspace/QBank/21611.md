Question: Martha signed and delivered a deed conveying her shop to Lazarus. The deed recited, “Receipt of eighty thousand dollars is acknowledged.” Lazarus had promised to wire the price that afternoon, but he never did. The deed did not make delivery conditional on the wire. Martha sues Lazarus for the unpaid price. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The deed is void, so Lazarus owns nothing and Martha has no need for a money claim.
Answer B: The recital conclusively proves payment, so Martha has neither title nor money remedies.
Answer C: The deed passed title, but the unpaid-price dispute is separate from the deed-validity lock.
Answer D: Lazarus owns the shop only if he records before Martha files suit.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive title lock is unconditional delivery, not whether the consideration recital was true. Martha delivered the deed without conditioning title on the wire, and Lazarus accepted. Title passed, while Martha's unpaid-price claim remains a separate contractual or restitutionary issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because nonpayment does not automatically void the deed after unconditional delivery. B: B is wrong because the question's title route does not convert the recital into a total bar to any separate payment dispute. D: D is wrong because recording affects notice and priority, not title passage between these parties.
lb_rule_reference: Consideration recital does not merge all separate payment remedies into title result
