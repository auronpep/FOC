Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: Priya is indicted for drug distribution and released pending trial. At police direction, Priya's cousin wears a recording device and visits Priya at a restaurant. The cousin repeatedly asks where Priya hid the drug proceeds, and Priya eventually answers. Priya did not know the cousin was cooperating with police. Should the recorded statements be suppressed under the Sixth Amendment?
Answer A: Yes, because a government agent deliberately elicited statements about the charged offense after indictment without counsel or waiver
Answer B: No, because Priya was not in custody at the restaurant
Answer C: No, because the cousin was a family member rather than a uniformed officer
Answer D: Yes, because every recorded conversation after indictment is inadmissible
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Indictment attached the right. The cousin acted as a government agent and actively steered the conversation to the charged offense, which is deliberate elicitation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Massiah is a Sixth Amendment doctrine and does not require custody after formal proceedings begin. C: A private person acting at police direction can be a government agent for Massiah purposes. D: The key is government-agent deliberate elicitation about the charged offense. Passive recordings or unrelated topics may present different issues.
lb_rule_reference: Once formal proceedings have begun, the government may not use an agent to deliberately elicit statements about the charged offense in counsel's absence, even outside custody.
