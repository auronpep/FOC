Question: A state makes it a civil offense for married adults to purchase contraceptives from a pharmacy. The state defends the law solely on the ground that a majority of voters morally disapprove of contraception. What is the strongest substantive due process challenge?
Answer A: The law burdens a protected privacy liberty interest in using contraception
Answer B: The law is valid because moral disagreement always satisfies strict scrutiny
Answer C: The law is valid because married people have fewer privacy rights than unmarried people
Answer D: The law raises only a procedural due process issue about whether a hearing was provided before purchase
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Contraception is within the protected privacy line, so the state cannot defend the burden as ordinary economic regulation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A protected privacy right cannot be defeated simply by moral disagreement. C: Marriage does not eliminate the contraception privacy right. D: The challenge is to the substance of the law burdening privacy, not the procedure before enforcement.
lb_rule_reference: The right to use contraception is a protected privacy liberty interest, and a law substantially burdening that right triggers heightened constitutional scrutiny.
