Question: A merchant agreed to sell 200 clay lamps to John. The contract said the lamps would be picked up at the seller's warehouse on June 1 and was silent about payment time. On June 1, the seller had the lamps ready at the warehouse and asked John to pay the price. John refused, saying he would pay 30 days after pickup. Who is most likely in breach?
Answer A: The seller, because a seller must deliver goods to the buyer's place unless the contract says otherwise.
Answer B: Neither party, because payment is never due until 30 days after goods are received.
Answer C: The seller, because goods contracts never require concurrent tender and payment.
Answer D: John, because the seller's tender of the goods made John's payment duty due absent a contrary agreement.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Article 2, tender of delivery generally entitles the seller to acceptance and payment according to the contract. When the contract calls for pickup at the seller's warehouse and is silent about delayed credit, the buyer's payment duty is concurrent with tender, not automatically deferred 30 days.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the contract specified pickup at the seller's warehouse. B: B is wrong because no 30-day credit term appears. C: C is wrong because tender and payment are often concurrent under Article 2.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-507 / tender and payment concurrent conditions
