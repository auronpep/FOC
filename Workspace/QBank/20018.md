Outline: Presumptions and inferences
Question: Mary sues Joseph in federal court for negligence after a ladder collapsed. Mary proves that Joseph's store had no inspection entries for the ladder during the month before the collapse, even though store policy required weekly entries. Mary asks the judge to instruct the jury that it may infer no inspection occurred. Joseph objects. Which is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because an inference can never be drawn from missing records.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because Mary is trying to shift the burden of persuasion to Joseph.
Answer C: Overrule the objection if the missing entries reasonably support the inference, because the jury may accept or reject it.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Joseph first admits that he failed to inspect the ladder.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A jury may be permitted to draw a reasonable inference from missing records when the inference is supported by the evidence. The instruction does not shift persuasion if it tells the jury it may, not must, draw the inference.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because circumstantial inferences from missing records can be permissible. B: B is wrong because may-language does not shift the burden of persuasion. D: D is wrong because an admission is not required before the jury may draw a rational inference.
lb_rule_reference: Permissive inference / civil evidence
