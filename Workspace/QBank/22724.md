


BARMATRIX Q#: 22724
Outline_code: 44040301
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Fundamental rights
Question: Due to staffing problems, a county changed its voting rules to shut down several voting locations in rural areas of the county. To balance the fewer voting locations, the rules also lengthened the time polls would be open from 6 p.m. to 8 p.m. Because of new rules, rural residents had to either drive 20 minutes into the city or vote by mail. The overwhelming majority of rural voters identified as Republican, while city voters overwhelmingly identified as Democrats. A rural voter sued the county, arguing the new voting rules were unconstitutional. Is the court likely to find the county's new voting rules unconstitutional?
Answer A: A: Yes, because voting is a fundamental right.
Answer B: B: Yes, because it discriminates against a political party.
Answer C: C: No, because shutting down voting locations is rationally related to staffing problems.
Answer D: D: No, because the voter's claim involves a political question.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: While voting itself is a fundamental right, rules concerning the time, place, and manner of casting votes are judged on a rational basis. If a time, place, or manner rule is rationally related to the government's objective or concern, the rule will be upheld. Here, the new rules were enacted to deal with staffing problems, and more time to vote was added so voters that have to drive could get to a polling place. The distance into the city is not very long, and rural voters also have a vote by mail option. Consequently, the court will likely find the new rules constitutional. A is incorrect because not all voting rules infringe on the fundamental right to vote (which would then require strict scrutiny analysis). Here, the county is trying to deal with a staffing issue and the problems the new rules cause for rural voters are minor. B is incorrect because the rule doesn't discriminate against a political party. The fact that more rural voters identify as Republican does not make the rule discriminatory. D is incorrect because a restriction on voting has justiciable standards that can be properly determined by a court.
