Outline: Intermediate scrutiny (quasi-suspect) classifications
Question: Congress enacts a statute requiring male citizens and lawful permanent residents between ages 18 and 25 to register with the Selective Service. Female citizens and lawful permanent residents of the same age are not required to register. A female citizen who argues that she should be equally obligated to serve challenges the male-only registration requirement as a sex-based classification that fails Equal Protection. The government defends the registration requirement on military preparedness grounds, arguing that differential treatment is justified by the needs of the armed forces. Under current Equal Protection doctrine, which of the following best describes the analytical framework for the male-only draft registration challenge?
Answer A: Rational basis applies because Congress has plenary authority over military affairs, and courts defer completely to congressional military judgments without applying heightened scrutiny
Answer B: The male-only registration requirement easily survives intermediate scrutiny under Rostker v. Goldberg because the purpose of registration is to prepare for the draft of combat soldiers, and women are excluded from combat
Answer C: Intermediate scrutiny applies, but whether male-only registration survives is now a genuinely open question because the combat-exclusion rationale that supported Rostker's holding has been legislatively overturned, potentially removing the government's exceedingly persuasive justification
Answer D: The male-only registration requirement is unconstitutional per se under United States v. Virginia because all sex-based government distinctions require exceedingly persuasive justification, and the military context cannot supply one
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Intermediate scrutiny applies to sex-based classifications. Rostker v. Goldberg upheld male-only Selective Service registration under intermediate scrutiny because the distinction was tied to women's statutory exclusion from combat. Since the combat exclusion was lifted in 2013, the factual predicate for Rostker's justification has changed. Whether the registration distinction remains substantially related to an important government interest without the combat-exclusion predicate is genuinely unsettled and a current constitutional question.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Sex-based classifications — even in the military context — are reviewed under intermediate scrutiny, not rational basis. Rostker v. Goldberg applied intermediate scrutiny to the male-only draft registration system. Congressional authority over the military is substantial and informs the application of intermediate scrutiny, but does not lower the standard to rational basis. B: The factual predicate for Rostker has changed. The Department of Defense lifted the combat exclusion in 2013, opening all combat roles to women. Whether male-only draft registration continues to survive intermediate scrutiny is now a live constitutional question because the combat-exclusion justification has been removed. The old Rostker analysis may not apply given changed facts. D: United States v. Virginia does not make all sex-based distinctions per se unconstitutional. It requires exceedingly persuasive justification, which is a demanding but satisfiable standard. Rostker had persuasive justification at the time; whether that justification remains after combat integration is an open question — not a per se rule.
lb_rule_reference: Under Rostker v. Goldberg, a male-only military draft registration system survived intermediate scrutiny as substantially related to the important interest in maintaining a combat-ready force, based on congressional authority over military matters; the current status of combat exclusion changes the underlying justification.
