Question: A privately owned dinner club with no government funding or public-function role admits only members from one ethnic group. A rejected applicant sues the club directly under the Equal Protection Clause. What is the club's strongest constitutional defense?
Answer A: The Equal Protection Clause does not reach purely private discrimination without state action
Answer B: Strict scrutiny applies because the club uses ethnicity
Answer C: The club loses because the Constitution bans all private discrimination
Answer D: Rational basis applies because private clubs receive deference
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Constitutional Equal Protection requires government action or a recognized state-action theory.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The threshold problem is the absence of state action. C: Other statutes may regulate private discrimination, but Equal Protection itself requires state action. D: The constitutional claim fails at state action before scrutiny tiers apply.
lb_rule_reference: Equal Protection constrains government action, not purely private discrimination unless state action is present.
