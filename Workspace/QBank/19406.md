Question: Congress validly enacts a nationwide workplace-safety law requiring all employers, including state-owned factories, to use protective equipment for employees handling dangerous chemicals. A state argues the law commandeers it because it must comply in its own factory. What is the best response?
Answer A: The law is not commandeering merely because it applies generally to state and private employers as regulated participants
Answer B: The law is invalid because the Tenth Amendment exempts states from every generally applicable federal law
Answer C: The law is invalid because Congress may never regulate workplace safety
Answer D: The law is valid only if the state legislature separately adopts the federal standard
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The statute regulates employers directly; it does not require the state to legislate or administer a federal program for private parties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Anti-commandeering is not a blanket exemption from valid generally applicable regulation. C: Congress may regulate under an enumerated power when the statute fits that power. D: Valid federal law can bind regulated parties directly without state reenactment.
lb_rule_reference: The anti-commandeering doctrine bars commands to states to regulate or administer federal programs, but it does not categorically exempt states from generally applicable federal laws validly regulating employers or market participants.
