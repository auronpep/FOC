Question: Dana is walking home at night when a large man bumps into her and says, "Watch where you're going — I should beat you senseless." The man then turns and walks in the opposite direction. Dana, convinced he is circling to attack her from behind, pulls a knife and stabs him in the back, killing him. Witnesses confirm the man was walking away and had made no move to return. Dana testifies she was genuinely terrified and certain he would attack. If Dana is charged with murder, what is the most likely outcome?
Answer A: Acquittal, because Dana genuinely believed she was in imminent danger
Answer B: Acquittal, because the man made a verbal threat
Answer C: Conviction of voluntary manslaughter, because Dana had an honest but unreasonable belief in the need for deadly force
Answer D: Conviction of murder, because Dana attacked from behind
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Imperfect self-defense applies when a defendant has a genuine (honest) belief in the necessity of deadly force, but that belief is objectively unreasonable. The doctrine reduces the charge from murder (which requires malice aforethought) to voluntary manslaughter because the honest belief negates the extreme recklessness or purpose-to-kill element of murder, but the objective unreasonableness prevents full acquittal. Here, Dana genuinely believed she was in danger, but a reasonable person would have recognized the threat had dissipated when the man walked away.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common-law self-defense requires the belief to be both honest AND objectively reasonable. A purely subjective test is the MPC approach, not the majority common-law rule. An honest but unreasonable belief does not support full acquittal. B: Self-defense requires imminence. The man had turned and walked away — the threat was not imminent at the moment Dana acted. A past verbal threat does not justify preemptive deadly force. D: The direction of the attack is relevant to the reasonableness analysis, but it does not determine the charge by itself. The controlling issue is whether imperfect self-defense applies. Because Dana had an honest (if unreasonable) belief, the charge is voluntary manslaughter, not murder.
lb_rule_reference: Self-defense requires both an honest and objectively reasonable belief that force was immediately necessary; an honest but unreasonable belief does not support acquittal but may reduce murder to voluntary manslaughter (imperfect self-defense)
