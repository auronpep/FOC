Outline: Clergy and penitent
Question: Lydia emailed Pastor Silas privately, writing, “I falsified the donation ledger and need spiritual counsel.” She asked Pastor Silas to keep the email confidential and advise her about repentance. In a federal proceeding, the government subpoenas Pastor Silas for the email. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because a confidential written request for spiritual guidance to clergy can fall within the clergy-penitent privilege.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because clergy privilege protects only spoken confessions.
Answer C: Overrule the objection, because written communications are governed only by the original-writing rule.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Lydia later attended an in-person service.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The protected object is a confidential spiritual communication to clergy; written form does not defeat the privilege. Lydia's email sought confidential spiritual counsel. The decisive axis is communication purpose and confidentiality, not medium.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because This creates a false oral-only limitation. C: C is wrong because The original-writing rule concerns proof of contents; it does not displace privilege. D: D is wrong because An in-person service is not required where the communication itself qualifies.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / written clergy communication
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is confusing evidence format with privilege scope.
