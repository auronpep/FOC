Question: In a federal drunk-driving civil trial, Elisha testifies that he watched Isaac step out of a car, stagger, smell strongly of wine, and slur his words. Isaac objects when Elisha is asked, “Did Isaac appear intoxicated?” How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because only an expert may testify about intoxication.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because lay witnesses may never give opinions.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if Elisha first states Isaac's blood-alcohol level.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because the opinion is rationally based on Elisha's perception and helpful to the jury.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A lay witness may give an opinion rationally based on perception and helpful to the jury. Appearance of intoxication is a common shorthand opinion based on observed behavior.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because expert testimony is not required for ordinary observations of intoxication. B: B is wrong because lay opinions are sometimes admissible. C: C is wrong because a blood-alcohol number is not required for this perception-based lay opinion.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 701 / permissible lay opinion
