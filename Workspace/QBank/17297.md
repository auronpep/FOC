Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Assumption of risk
Question: A worker knew a rented concrete saw's blade guard was jammed open and had watched it throw debris twice that morning. The rental company offered a replacement saw, but the worker refused because switching tools would take time. The worker then used the saw without eye protection and was injured by debris. Which defense is most directly raised?
Answer A: Assumption of risk or comparative responsibility based on knowing use of the defective saw
Answer B: No defense, because strict products liability ignores all plaintiff conduct
Answer C: No defense unless the worker signed a written release
Answer D: A complete defense because any careless product use bars recovery in every jurisdiction
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The worker knew the specific defect and voluntarily used the saw anyway, rejecting a safer replacement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Plaintiff conduct can matter through assumption of risk, comparative responsibility, misuse, or modification doctrines. C: Knowing voluntary encounter with a specific risk can raise implied assumption or comparative responsibility even without a written release. D: The effect varies by jurisdiction and fault system; it may reduce rather than bar recovery.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff who knowingly and voluntarily encounters a known product defect may face assumption-of-risk or comparative-responsibility defenses.
