Outline: Attorney-client
Question: A client who has received a subpoena for company ledgers privately asks his lawyer, “How can I move these ledgers so the investigators will not find them?” The lawyer refuses to help and reports nothing. In a later obstruction proceeding, the government seeks the client's question to the lawyer. Is the communication protected by attorney-client privilege?
Answer A: Yes, because the lawyer did not agree to help the client.
Answer B: Yes, because the lawyer was unaware of any completed obstruction when the question was asked.
Answer C: No, because the client sought advice to further concealment of subpoenaed evidence.
Answer D: No, because attorney-client privilege never applies in criminal cases.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The crime-fraud exception removes protection for communications made to obtain legal assistance for future or ongoing crime or fraud. The lawyer need not agree with or participate in the plan. The decisive facts are the subpoena and the client's request for advice on hiding the ledgers.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Lawyer refusal does not restore privilege for a client communication made to further crime or fraud. B: B is wrong because The lawyer's innocence is not the gate; the client's improper purpose is. D: A is wrong because Lawyer refusal does not restore privilege for a client communication made to further crime or fraud. B is wrong because The lawyer's innocence is not the gate; the client's improper purpose is. C is wrong because Correct answer D is wrong because Attorney-client privilege applies in criminal cases. This communication loses protection because of the exception, not because of case type.
