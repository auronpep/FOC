Outline: Privileges and Immunities Clause of Article IV, Section 2
Question: A state charges nonresidents more than residents for a recreational elk-hunting license. A nonresident hunter argues that Article IV privileges and immunities requires identical recreational license fees for residents and nonresidents. What is the best Article IV answer?
Answer A: The claim is strong because every state benefit is a fundamental privilege
Answer B: The claim is weak because recreational hunting is not the kind of fundamental privilege Article IV protects
Answer C: The hunter wins automatically because nonresidents can never be charged more
Answer D: The case belongs only under the Twenty-first Amendment
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Recreational benefits receive more state leeway than common callings or basic civil rights.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Article IV does not protect every recreational benefit as a fundamental privilege. C: Article IV does not require identical treatment for every resident/nonresident distinction. D: The Twenty-first Amendment concerns alcohol, not hunting licenses.
lb_rule_reference: Article IV protects fundamental privileges, not every recreational or nonessential benefit a state offers.
