Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Leah is charged with stabbing Hannah with a narrow folding knife outside a market. The prosecutor offers an ordinary kitchen knife found in a drawer in the home of Hannah's neighbor, Ezra. No witness connects Ezra's home, the knife, or Leah to the stabbing, and the medical examiner says the wound was not made by a kitchen knife. Leah objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the knife because any knife in the neighborhood has some tendency to show that a knife was used.
Answer B: Admit the knife because the prosecution need not prove the exact weapon at trial.
Answer C: Exclude the knife only if Leah proves that Ezra owned it.
Answer D: Exclude the knife because the record supplies no reasonable connection between this knife and a consequential fact in the case.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Although relevance has a low threshold, the offered item still needs a logical connection to a consequential fact. Here, the ordinary kitchen knife is not connected to Leah, the stabbing, the scene, or the wound. Without a link, the item is irrelevant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the fact that the case involves a knife does not make every knife in the area relevant. B: B is wrong because the prosecution need not prove the exact weapon, but it still needs a probative link. C: C is wrong because ownership by Ezra would not be the relevant connection needed to admit the knife against Leah.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402 / FRE 104(b)
lb_student_diagnostic: Confusing low-threshold relevance with no-threshold relevance.
