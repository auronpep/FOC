Outline_code: 44040305
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Rational basis review classifications
Question: A woman applied to be employed as a state park ranger. The woman was granted an interview, but during the interview, the state park director admitted that the woman had perfect qualifications for the job but that he would not hire her because she was deaf. The woman sued, arguing that the state park director violated her equal protection rights. During trial, the woman showed that she had better qualifications than the person that was ultimately hired for the position. The state park director replied that he regretted not hiring the woman, but he believed that a state park ranger needed to be able to hear due to wilderness rescue concerns and the fact that all communications with state rangers occurred over CB radios. Is the court likely to find that the state park director violated the woman's constitutional rights?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the director is unlikely to show his decision furthered a compelling state interest.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the director is unlikely to show his decision was rationally related to a legitimate state interest.
Answer C: C: No, because the woman is unlikely to show that the decision was not rationally related to a legitimate state interest.
Answer D: D: No, because the woman is unlikely to show that the decision did not further a compelling state interest.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: If the state discriminates against a person based on disability, that discrimination is analyzed under rational basis scrutiny because disability does not implicate a fundamental right or suspect class. Consequently, the plaintiff must show that the state's discriminatory decision is not rationally related to a legitimate state interest. This answer choice states the correct level of scrutiny and correctly places the burden upon the plaintiff. A and B are incorrect because the burden of persuasion would be on the woman, not the state park director. D is incorrect because the correct scrutiny level is rational basis, not strict scrutiny's requirement of a compelling state interest.
