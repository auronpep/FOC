MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Solicitation
Question: An undercover officer posing as a burglar meets with Keira. Keira asks the officer to break into a competitor's office and steal customer files. The officer says, "I will see what I can do," but never intends to commit the burglary. Keira is arrested and charged with solicitation to commit burglary. Is the officer's undercover status a defense to solicitation?
Answer A: Yes, because the officer never intended to commit burglary
Answer B: No, because Keira intentionally asked another person to commit burglary
Answer C: Yes, because solicitation requires two guilty parties
Answer D: No, but only if the officer had already obtained a warrant
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Keira communicated a request that the officer commit a burglary, with intent that it be done. Solicitation was complete regardless of the officer's feigned willingness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Solicitation focuses on Keira's intentional request. The officer's true intent is not an element. C: The solicited person need not be guilty or willing. The defendant's request and intent are enough. D: Warrant status is unrelated to whether Keira committed solicitation.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant may commit solicitation by asking an undercover officer to commit a crime, because solicitation focuses on the defendant's intentional request, not the solicited person's true willingness.
