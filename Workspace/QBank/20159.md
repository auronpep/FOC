Question: A buyer orally agrees to buy 100 shelves from a seller for $8,000. The seller delivers 30 shelves, which the buyer receives and keeps without objection. The buyer refuses to accept the remaining 70 shelves and asserts the Statute of Frauds. There is no signed writing and no other exception. How much of the oral goods contract is enforceable under the UCC exception for received and accepted goods?
Answer A: None, because there is no signed writing.
Answer B: All 100 shelves, because accepting part proves the entire oral contract.
Answer C: Only the shelves the buyer paid for in advance.
Answer D: The 30 shelves received and accepted, but not the remaining 70 on these facts.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A goods contract that fails the writing requirement is enforceable with respect to goods that have been received and accepted. Acceptance of 30 shelves supports enforcement for those 30, not the undelivered remainder absent another exception.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because received-and-accepted goods create a UCC exception. B: B is wrong because the exception is partial and does not prove the entire oral quantity. C: C is wrong because received and accepted goods can trigger enforceability even apart from advance payment.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(3)(c) — received and accepted goods
