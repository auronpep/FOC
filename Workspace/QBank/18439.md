Question: In a federal theft trial, the only evidence linking Titus to the missing tools is that he was found with the tools two hours after they disappeared. Titus asks the court to instruct the jury that circumstantial evidence cannot support a verdict unless supported by direct eyewitness testimony. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Give the instruction because circumstantial evidence cannot support a verdict by itself.
Answer B: Give the instruction only because the case is criminal.
Answer C: Refuse the instruction because circumstantial evidence can support a factual finding and is for the jury to weigh.
Answer D: Refuse the instruction because possession of recently stolen property is direct evidence that Titus stole it.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Circumstantial evidence is a recognized proof type. The jury may decide its weight and may draw reasonable inferences; direct eyewitness testimony is not required as a category.
