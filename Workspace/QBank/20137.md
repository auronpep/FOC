Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Rebekah contracted with a repair company owned by Eli to restore her shop's oven. Before Eli repudiated, Rebekah reasonably spent $1,500 removing the oven from the wall so Eli's workers could access it. Rebekah also lost $20,000 in profits when her shop later remained closed. The lost profits are not proven with reasonable certainty. Which item is the clearest reliance loss?
Answer A: The $20,000 lost profits, because they were the largest loss.
Answer B: The $1,500 removal expense, because it was an expenditure made in preparation for performance.
Answer C: Both amounts automatically, because any loss after a breach is reliance.
Answer D: Neither amount, because reliance damages are unavailable if any lost profits are claimed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages focus on expenditures made in preparation for or performance of the contract. The $1,500 removal expense is reliance. The $20,000 lost profits are a consequential or expectation-type loss and fail here for lack of reasonable certainty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because size of loss does not make it reliance. C: C is wrong because not every post-breach loss is reliance. D: D is wrong because a failed lost-profit claim does not bar recovery of proven reliance expenditures.
