


BARMATRIX Q#: 22807
Outline_code: 91090400
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jury Trials
Outline: Jury Instructions
Question: A man sued a hospital for negligence in federal court. At trial, the man's attorney submitted a proposed jury instruction on negligence. The court did not accept the attorney's proposed instruction. Instead, the court gave a jury instruction that the attorney believed was legally incorrect and hurt the man's claim. The attorney did not object to the jury instruction and the jury returned a verdict in favor of the hospital.The man moved for a new trial, claiming that the court's jury instruction was improper. What is the man's best argument in support of his motion?
Answer A: A: The court's jury instruction was plain error that affected the man's substantial rights.
Answer B: B: The court's jury instruction was an issue of law that could be raised at any time.
Answer C: C: The man's proposed jury instruction acted as a formal objection.
Answer D: D: The man's jury instruction was a correct statement of the applicable law.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Because the attorney did not raise an objection to the jury instruction before the jury reached a verdict, the best argument for a new trial would be made under Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(b). Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(b)(1), the court can grant a relief from a judgment or order if the moving party shows that there was a “mistake” during trial. Here, the man should claim the court's instruction was a mistake. B, C, and D are incorrect because they do not address the fact that the man's attorney did not object to the court's jury instruction.
