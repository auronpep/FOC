Subtopic: Contract Content and Meaning
Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Abraham hired Moses to build “a premium cedar fence” around Abraham's orchard. The signed contract contains a merger clause and states the price, length, height, and deadline. The parties later dispute whether “premium cedar” means knot-free cedar or ordinary cedar boards treated with oil. Abraham offers evidence that, during negotiations, Moses showed him a sample of knot-free cedar and said, “This is what premium cedar means in this contract.” Should the court admit the evidence?
Answer A: No, because a merger clause bars all evidence of precontract negotiations for every purpose.
Answer B: Yes, to explain the meaning of the ambiguous phrase “premium cedar,” not to add a new obligation.
Answer C: No, because the contract is detailed and therefore every word in it must be interpreted only by the four corners.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Abraham first proves that Moses breached the contract.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A merger clause and detailed writing are strong evidence of integration, but they do not bar evidence offered to explain an ambiguous term. The evidence is offered to define “premium cedar,” not to add a separate duty outside the writing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because merger clauses do not automatically bar interpretive evidence. C: C is wrong because integration does not eliminate the possibility of ambiguity. D: D is wrong because admissibility of meaning evidence is decided before the merits question whether Moses breached.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law PER / ambiguity explanation despite integration
