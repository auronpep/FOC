Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: A plaintiff timely sues "Northstar Delivery, Inc." for a truck collision. Within the Rule 4(m) service period, "Northstar Logistics, LLC" receives notice of the action and knows its truck and driver were involved. The plaintiff later learns she named the wrong related Northstar entity and moves to amend after limitations expires. Northstar Logistics knew it would have been sued but for the plaintiff's identity mistake. May the amendment relate back?
Answer A: Yes, if the Rule 15(c) notice and mistake requirements are satisfied
Answer B: No, because amendments adding defendants can never relate back
Answer C: No, because the old and new defendants are legally distinct entities
Answer D: Yes, but only if the plaintiff served the correct defendant before limitations expired
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim arises from the same collision, and the proposed defendant had timely notice plus knowledge that it was omitted because of an identity mistake. Those facts satisfy the change-of-party relation-back route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Rule 15(c)(1)(C) specifically allows relation back for party changes when its notice and mistake requirements are met. C: Distinctness explains why amendment is needed, but it does not defeat relation back if the new party had timely notice and knew of the mistake. D: Rule 15(c)(1)(C) uses the Rule 4(m) period for notice, not necessarily service before limitations expires.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 15(c)(1)(C) allows relation back when the claim arises from the same conduct, transaction, or occurrence, and within the Rule 4(m) period the new party received notice and knew or should have known it would have been sued but for a mistake concerning identity.
