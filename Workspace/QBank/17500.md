Question: Esther orally agreed to buy wool from Daniel for $8 per bundle. Daniel signed a memo stating, “I agree to sell Esther wool at $8 per bundle, delivery next month.” The memo did not state any quantity. Esther later claimed the oral agreement was for 700 bundles. Is the memo sufficient under UCC §2-201 to enforce the 700-bundle contract against Daniel?
Answer A: Yes, because the price and delivery date are enough to show a contract.
Answer B: Yes, because the court may always supply a reasonable quantity under the UCC.
Answer C: Yes, but only for a commercially reasonable quantity.
Answer D: No, because a UCC goods memorandum must show a quantity for the contract to be enforceable.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A UCC §2-201 writing must indicate a contract for sale and, for enforcement, must show the quantity. Other terms can be omitted or misstated, but quantity is the critical cap. A writing with no quantity does not enforce the alleged 700-bundle goods contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because price and delivery date do not substitute for quantity. B: B is wrong because UCC gap-fillers do not supply the Statute of Frauds quantity requirement. C: C is wrong because there is no enforceable reasonable quantity when the writing shows none.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 / missing quantity
