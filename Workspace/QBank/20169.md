MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: In general
Question: A homeowner invited a neighbor to dinner. The homeowner knew that the third basement stair looked normal but shifted sideways unless stepped on at the far left edge. The homeowner forgot to mention it. The neighbor used the basement stairs to get ice from a cooler and fell when the step shifted. The neighbor sues the homeowner for negligence. Is the neighbor likely to establish the homeowner's duty in the negligence claim?
Answer A: No, because a social guest receives no protection from premises conditions
Answer B: Yes, because the neighbor was a licensee and the homeowner knew of a hidden dangerous condition
Answer C: Yes, because the neighbor was an invitee and the homeowner had a duty to inspect the entire house
Answer D: No, because the loose step was part of the house rather than an active operation by the homeowner
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The neighbor was a social guest, generally a licensee. The homeowner knew the normal-looking stair shifted and had a duty to warn of that hidden danger.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A lower duty is not no duty. A possessor must warn a licensee of known hidden dangers the guest is unlikely to discover. C: Social guests are usually licensees, not invitees. The homeowner's duty turns on known hidden danger, not a business-invitee inspection duty. D: Known hidden conditions can trigger a duty to warn licensees. The danger need not be an ongoing activity.
lb_rule_reference: A social guest is generally a licensee, and the possessor must warn of known hidden dangers the guest is unlikely to discover.
