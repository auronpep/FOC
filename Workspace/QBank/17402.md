Question: David is charged with distributing illegal tablets from his apartment. During a search, officers find a digital scale in a locked closet in David's bedroom, next to empty plastic bags. David objects that the scale is inadmissible because the government cannot prove the scale was used in any charged sale. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the scale because the government must prove it was used in a charged sale before it is relevant.
Answer B: Exclude the scale because physical objects found in a home are never relevant unless they contain fingerprints.
Answer C: Admit the scale only if David admits that the closet was his.
Answer D: Admit the scale because its location and association with packaging supplies make it probative; the lack of proof of use in a particular sale goes to weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The scale need not be tied to a specific sale to be relevant. Its location in David's locked bedroom closet and proximity to packaging supplies make it probative of distribution activity. The weakness affects weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevance does not require proof that the object was used in the precise charged transaction. B: B is wrong because fingerprints are not required to connect a physical object circumstantially. C: C is wrong because other circumstances can connect David to the closet.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401; FRE 901(a)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student demands exact transaction linkage rather than recognizing weak-but-probative physical evidence.
