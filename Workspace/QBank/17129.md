Question: Noah orally agrees to buy 500 brass lamps from Miriam for $10,000. Miriam delivers the lamps to Noah's store. The next morning, before using or reselling any lamps and after a reasonable inspection, Noah sends Miriam a notice rejecting the lamps and holds them for pickup. No writing was signed. Miriam sues to enforce the oral contract. Can Miriam rely on the received-and-accepted goods exception?
Answer A: Yes, because the lamps physically reached Noah's store.
Answer B: Yes, because Noah had possession overnight.
Answer C: Yes, because goods delivered to a buyer are always accepted under the UCC.
Answer D: No, because Noah received the lamps but did not accept them; he effectively rejected after inspection.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The exception applies to goods received and accepted, not merely received. Acceptance occurs when the buyer indicates acceptance, fails to make an effective rejection after a reasonable opportunity to inspect, or acts inconsistently with the seller's ownership. Noah seasonably rejected after inspection and did not accept.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B are wrong because physical possession alone does not equal acceptance. C is wrong because delivery does not automatically create acceptance under §2-606.
