Question: Samuel sues Deborah after a warehouse accident, claiming the accident caused his back injury. Deborah offers evidence that Samuel fell from a ladder at home one week after the warehouse accident. Samuel objects that the home fall occurred after Deborah's alleged negligence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence because events after the defendant's conduct can never be relevant to causation.
Answer B: Admit the evidence because the later ladder fall has some tendency to provide an alternative explanation for Samuel's back condition.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence unless Deborah proves the ladder fall was the sole cause of Samuel's back injury.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if Samuel first denies falling from the ladder.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A later injury can be relevant to causation or damages when it provides an alternative explanation for the plaintiff's current condition. Deborah need not prove that the later fall was the sole cause before the evidence becomes relevant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because later events can be relevant to causation and damages. C: C is wrong because sole-cause proof is not required at the relevance stage. D: D is wrong because the evidence may be relevant regardless of whether Samuel has already denied the fall.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Treating alternative-cause evidence as inadmissible unless it fully disproves the plaintiff's theory.
