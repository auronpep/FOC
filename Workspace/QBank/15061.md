Question: A recent widow is trying to sell her home and downsize to a much smaller home in a retirement community. The widow has an attorney and a licensed real estate agent helping her sell the old home and purchase the new home. The widow and the attorney have disagreed throughout the process, while the widow and the agent get along very well. In fact, when the widow has a question, she always calls the agent first to avoid speaking with the attorney. The agent has been working in real estate for 20 years; prior to that time, he graduated from law school but was never licensed. The widow is closing on the new home next week. She does not wish the attorney to be present out of personal dislike for him, although she does have several legal questions about the process. May the agent conduct the closing?
Answer A: A: Yes, the agent may conduct the closing and answer the widow's legal questions.
Answer B: B: Yes, the agent may conduct the closing, but may not answer the widow's legal questions.
Answer C: C: No, the agent may not conduct the closing, but may answer the widow's legal questions.
Answer D: D: No, the agent may neither conduct the closing, nor answer the widow's legal questions.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. Most states do not require closings to be conducted by an attorney, although if a non-attorney does conduct it, he may not offer legal advice as if he were an attorney.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The agent is permitted to conduct the closing, but because he is not a licensed attorney, he may not answer the widow's legal questions because that would be practicing law. C is incorrect. On the contrary, the agent MAY conduct the closing and may NOT answer the legal questions, as stated above. D is incorrect. The only role the agent may play is to conduct the closing as a non-attorney.
