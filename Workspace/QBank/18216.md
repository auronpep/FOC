Outline: Clergy and penitent
Question: A business owner meets a pastor who also works as a tax preparer. Over lunch at a crowded restaurant, the owner asks the pastor how to classify revenue on the company's tax forms. The discussion is about business accounting, not confession, spiritual counseling, or religious advice. The owner later claims clergy-penitent privilege. Should the privilege apply?
Answer A: Yes, because the listener was clergy.
Answer B: Yes, because the statement involved possible wrongdoing.
Answer C: No, because clergy-penitent privilege applies only to statements made inside a church building.
Answer D: No, because the communication was not made confidentially to clergy acting in a spiritual or penitential capacity.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Clergy-penitent privilege depends on the role and purpose of the communication. A secular tax/accounting discussion with a pastor acting outside a spiritual advisory capacity is not protected, especially where confidentiality facts are absent. The decisive axis is spiritual capacity plus confidentiality.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is a relationship shortcut. Clergy status alone is not enough. B: B is wrong because Wrongdoing does not create the privilege; a confidential spiritual communication does. C: C is wrong because The location is not dispositive. A privileged communication can occur outside a church if the spiritual/confidential elements are met.

lb_student_diagnostic: Student routes by clergy label instead of capacity and communication purpose.
