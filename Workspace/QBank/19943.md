Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Payment of medical expenses
Question: Immediately after a bicyclist is injured in a collision, the driver says, “I'll pay your ambulance and emergency-room bills.” The driver does not ask for a release, and no claim has yet been discussed. At trial, the bicyclist offers the statement to prove that the driver was liable for the injury. Which ruling is most proper?
Answer A: Admit the statement as an opposing-party statement because the driver made it out of court.
Answer B: Exclude the statement under Rule 408 because every offer to pay after an accident is a compromise offer.
Answer C: Exclude the statement under Rule 409 because it is an offer to pay medical or similar expenses offered to prove liability.
Answer D: Admit the statement because humanitarian offers are relevant circumstantial evidence of fault.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 409 excludes evidence of furnishing, promising to pay, or offering to pay medical, hospital, or similar expenses when offered to prove liability for the injury. The facts show a naked medical-expense offer, not a release-conditioned settlement offer, and the proponent offers it for liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a hearsay classification does not defeat an independent public-policy exclusion. B: B is wrong because there is no compromise negotiation or release bargain. The better container is Rule 409, not Rule 408. D: D is wrong because Rule 409 reflects the policy that medical-payment offers should not be used as liability admissions.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 409 medical-expense offers/payments
lb_student_diagnostic: Student routes the statement by hearsay or settlement labels instead of identifying the medical-payment container and offered purpose.
