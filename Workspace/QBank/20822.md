Question: Daniel is charged with murdering Abigail, who disappeared eight years ago and has never been heard from. Before closing instructions, the judge says she will instruct the jury: “Because Abigail has not been heard from for more than seven years, you must find that she is dead.” Daniel's lawyer objects, stating that the proposed instruction would require the jury to find an element of the charged offense. The court gives the instruction anyway. On appeal, is Daniel's challenge to the instruction preserved?
Answer A: Yes, because Daniel's lawyer made a timely objection stating the burden-shifting problem before the instruction was given.
Answer B: Yes, but only if Daniel's lawyer also made an offer of proof outside the jury's presence.
Answer C: No, because objections to jury instructions are preserved only by a motion to strike after the jury retires.
Answer D: No, because the original death records, not witness testimony about disappearance, were the best evidence of Abigail's death.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A timely, specific objection to the proposed mandatory instruction preserved the issue. The problem is the instruction's compulsory effect on an element, not a missing offer of proof, motion to strike, or original-document issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because an offer of proof preserves excluded evidence, not an objection to a burden-shifting jury instruction. C: C is wrong because the defect was in the instruction, not a nonresponsive answer that needed to be struck. D: D is wrong because the objection concerns the jury instruction's force, not proof of document contents.
