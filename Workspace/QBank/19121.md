Outline: Presumptions and inferences
Question: In a federal civil action, Mary proves that she properly addressed, stamped, and mailed a notice to Daniel. Daniel testifies that he never received it. Mary asks the judge to instruct the jury that it must find Daniel received the notice. How should the court respond?
Answer A: Refuse the mandatory instruction; the jury may infer receipt from proper mailing but is not required to find receipt after contrary evidence.
Answer B: Give the instruction because proof of mailing conclusively establishes receipt in a civil case.
Answer C: Give the instruction because Daniel's denial is legally irrelevant once mailing is proved.
Answer D: Refuse the instruction and also tell the jury that it may not consider the mailing evidence at all.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Proper mailing can support a presumption or inference of receipt, but rebuttal evidence prevents a mandatory finding. The factfinder may draw the inference, but the burden of persuasion does not shift merely because mailing was proved.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the mailing proof is not conclusive in this posture. C: C is wrong because Daniel's denial is contrary evidence for the jury to weigh. D: D is wrong because the mailing evidence remains probative even if it does not compel a finding.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 301 / permissive inference after rebuttal
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests may-versus-must inference language and burden conversion.
