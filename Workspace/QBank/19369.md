Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: After receiving and waiving Miranda warnings, Jonah answered questions about a shooting. Halfway through, he said, "Maybe I should talk to a lawyer if this gets serious." The detectives continued questioning. Ten minutes later, Jonah admitted firing the gun. Should Jonah's admission be suppressed because of his statement about a lawyer?
Answer A: Yes, because any mention of a lawyer invokes the right to counsel
Answer B: Yes, because police were required to clarify whether Jonah wanted counsel
Answer C: No, because Jonah's lawyer reference was ambiguous and did not invoke Miranda counsel
Answer D: No, because Miranda counsel applies only after formal charges are filed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Jonah had waived his rights. His later statement was not an unambiguous request for counsel, so the detectives were not required to stop, and the admission is not suppressible on that ground.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Invocation must be clear and unambiguous. "Maybe I should talk to a lawyer if this gets serious" is conditional and equivocal. B: Police may clarify an ambiguous reference but are not constitutionally required to do so before continuing. D: Miranda counsel applies during custodial interrogation before charges. The problem is ambiguity, not timing of formal charges.
lb_rule_reference: A suspect must invoke Miranda counsel unambiguously; ambiguous or equivocal references do not require questioning to cease.
