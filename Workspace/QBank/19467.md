Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Daniel and Esther signed a written contract for Esther to buy 200 lamps from Daniel for $2 each. Later, they orally agreed to change the order to 400 lamps for $2 each. Daniel delivered no additional lamps, and there is no signed writing or UCC exception covering the oral increase. Esther seeks to enforce the oral modification for 400 lamps. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The oral modification is enforceable because UCC modifications never require a writing.
Answer B: The oral modification is enforceable because the original contract was signed.
Answer C: The oral modification is not enforceable for the increased quantity because the contract as modified is within UCC §2-201 and no writing or exception satisfies the goods Statute of Frauds.
Answer D: The oral modification is unenforceable only if Daniel is not a merchant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: UCC modifications need no consideration, but if the contract as modified falls within the UCC Statute of Frauds, the modified quantity must satisfy §2-201. The oral increase to 400 lamps at $2 each exceeds $500 and lacks a sufficient writing or exception.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because no-consideration modification is not the same as no writing requirement. B: B is wrong because the original signed writing does not necessarily satisfy the later increased quantity. D: D is wrong because merchant status is not the decisive issue for this modification-within-SOF problem.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-209 / modification within SOF
