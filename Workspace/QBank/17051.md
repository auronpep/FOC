Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A homeowner's dog had never bitten anyone, but it had repeatedly lunged at delivery drivers, snapped at their pant legs, and forced one driver to jump back from the porch. The homeowner knew about those incidents. A new delivery driver approached the porch, and the dog bit her ankle. The driver sues the homeowner for strict liability. Is the homeowner likely strictly liable for the dog bite?
Answer A: No, because the dog had never previously bitten anyone
Answer B: Yes, because the owner knew of the dog's dangerous propensity and the bite matched that risk
Answer C: No, because domestic animals can never create strict liability
Answer D: Yes, because all dog owners are strictly liable for all dog bites
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The dog was domestic, but the owner knew it lunged and snapped at delivery drivers. The later bite to a delivery driver was within that known propensity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A prior bite is not required if other conduct gave the owner reason to know of the dangerous propensity. C: Domestic animals can support strict liability when the owner knows or should know of dangerous propensities. D: The correct rule depends on known dangerous propensity, not automatic liability for every dog bite.
lb_rule_reference: An owner of a domestic animal may be strictly liable when the owner knows or has reason to know of the animal's dangerous propensity and the harm results from that propensity.
