Outline: Spousal Privileges
Question: Solomon is tried in federal court for armed robbery. The prosecution calls Solomon's current spouse, Abigail. Abigail saw Solomon hide a revolver behind a loose wall panel in their home, but Solomon said nothing to her when he did it. Abigail refuses to testify against Solomon. What is the correct federal spousal-privilege analysis?
Answer A: The marital communications privilege protects Abigail's testimony because anything a spouse sees in the marital home is a confidential communication.
Answer B: No privilege applies because Abigail's proposed testimony concerns an observation rather than a communication.
Answer C: The observation is not a confidential marital communication, but Abigail may refuse adverse testimony under the federal spousal testimonial privilege because she is the witness-spouse in a current marriage.
Answer D: Solomon may prevent Abigail from testifying because the defendant-spouse controls the federal adverse spousal testimonial privilege.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The confidential marital communications privilege protects confidential communications, not every observed act. Abigail's observation of Solomon hiding the revolver is not a communication. But in a federal criminal case, the adverse spousal testimonial privilege belongs to the witness-spouse, and a current spouse may refuse to testify adversely. Abigail, not Solomon, controls that testimonial privilege.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it merges observation with communication. A spouse's silent act is not automatically a confidential marital communication. B: B is wrong because it correctly rejects the communications privilege but forgets the separate adverse testimonial privilege. D: D is wrong because federal law places the adverse-testimonial choice with the witness-spouse, not the defendant-spouse.
lb_rule_reference: Observation is not marital communication; witness-spouse may invoke federal adverse testimonial privilege
lb_student_diagnostic: Student either collapses the two spousal privileges or overcorrects by denying testimonial privilege once the communication privilege fails.
