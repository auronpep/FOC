Question: Two negligent drivers caused a single indivisible injury to a pedestrian. The pedestrian settled with Driver A for $40,000. The written agreement released Driver A only and expressly reserved claims against Driver B. The jurisdiction follows the modern rule that a release of one joint tortfeasor does not release others unless the agreement says so. The pedestrian then sued Driver B. What is the likely effect of the settlement in the pedestrian's negligence claim against Driver B?
Answer A: The claim against Driver B is barred because any release of one joint tortfeasor releases all joint tortfeasors
Answer B: The claim against Driver B may proceed, but any judgment must be reduced to prevent double recovery
Answer C: The claim against Driver B may proceed only if Driver A was less than 50 percent at fault
Answer D: The claim against Driver B is barred only if Driver A already paid less than his fair share
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The release covered only Driver A and reserved claims against Driver B. The pedestrian may sue Driver B, subject to a settlement credit under the jurisdiction's allocation rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The jurisdiction follows the modern rule, and the agreement expressly reserved claims against Driver B. The settlement affects credit, not the existence of the remaining claim. C: The release question turns on agreement wording and the no-double-recovery credit, not a threshold percentage of Driver A's fault. D: Under joint and several liability, plaintiff recovery and defendant contribution are separate. A low settlement may affect credits or contribution rules, but it does not automatically bar the claim.
lb_rule_reference: A settlement with one joint tortfeasor does not automatically release other tortfeasors unless the release so provides or the jurisdiction follows that rule; the plaintiff receives a credit to prevent double recovery.
