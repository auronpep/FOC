Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Same facts as CR-076: Agent Reyes, working undercover, approached Leonard and proposed that they jointly sell stolen electronics. Leonard agreed and provided a list of items he could supply. Agent Reyes never intended to complete any sale. The jurisdiction has enacted the Model Penal Code. Leonard is charged with conspiracy to sell stolen property. Under the MPC unilateral conspiracy rule, is Leonard guilty of conspiracy?
Answer A: No, because there was no genuine bilateral agreement
Answer B: Yes, because under the MPC only Leonard's agreement and criminal intent need to be proved
Answer C: No, because the MPC requires an overt act and no overt act is alleged
Answer D: Yes, but only because Leonard was the one who initiated the criminal scheme
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: MPC § 5.03 adopts the unilateral theory of conspiracy. The crime is defined in terms of what the individual defendant agrees to do, not the genuine intent of all parties. Leonard genuinely agreed with what he believed to be another person willing to commit the crime. That is enough. The fact that Reyes was feigning consent does not prevent Leonard's conviction because the MPC focuses solely on Leonard's agreement and purpose. This is the principal doctrinal difference from common-law bilateral conspiracy.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: This is an MPC jurisdiction. The MPC deliberately abandoned the bilateral requirement. Under MPC § 5.03, a person is guilty of conspiracy if he agrees with another to commit a crime. The other party's genuine intent is irrelevant; only the defendant's agreement and intent matter. C: Providing a list of items available for the illegal sale is itself an overt act in furtherance of the conspiracy. The overt-act requirement is satisfied. The stronger issue is the bilateral/unilateral distinction. Moreover, this answer would defeat the charge under both MPC and common law, so it is not the rule being tested. D: Under the MPC unilateral rule, it does not matter who initiated the scheme. Any person who genuinely agrees to commit a crime is guilty of conspiracy regardless of who proposed it first. The initiation is irrelevant to guilt.
lb_rule_reference: Under the MPC, conspiracy is unilateral: a person is guilty if he agrees with another to commit a crime, even if that other person feigns agreement or is acquitted; only the agreeing defendant's genuine intent is required
