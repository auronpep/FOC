Outline: Creation
Question: Deborah signed and delivered a deed granting Daniel a permanent easement across Deborah's land. Daniel did not record the deed and did not visibly use the easement. Deborah later sold the land to Samuel, who paid value and had no actual or inquiry notice of Daniel's easement. The jurisdiction's recording act protects purchasers for value who take without notice. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Daniel's easement was never created because an easement is void until recorded.
Answer B: Samuel is automatically bound because all easements bind all later purchasers, whether or not they had notice.
Answer C: Daniel owns fee title to the easement strip because an unrecorded easement converts into title against a later purchaser.
Answer D: Daniel's easement was created between Deborah and Daniel, but it may not bind Samuel if Samuel is protected by the recording act as a purchaser without notice.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Creation and enforceability against successors are separate questions. The signed and delivered deed created an easement between Deborah and Daniel, but an unrecorded, nonvisible easement may be cut off against a protected purchaser for value without notice under the recording act.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because recording is not required for creation between the original parties. B: B is wrong because successor enforcement can depend on notice and recording-act protection. C: C is wrong because an easement remains a use right, not fee title to the strip.
