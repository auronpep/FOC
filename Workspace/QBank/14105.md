


BARMATRIX Q#: 14105
Outline_code: 92080105
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: A lender sued her borrower in federal court for breach of a promise to repay, with interest, the $80,000 that she had borrowed. The court had diversity jurisdiction. The borrower admitted that she received an $80,000 loan from the lender but claimed that the loan contract was illegal because the rate of interest provided for in the parties' contract was usurious and that she had agreed to pay it only under duress. Under state law, the usurious character of the interest would entitle the borrower to repay at a reduced rate of interest, and duress is a complete defense to a breach of contract action. Both the lender and the borrower filed cross-motions for summary judgment. The borrower's summary judgment motion, which sought a judgment of no liability to the lender, was supported by her affidavit, which sufficiently demonstrated that she signed the contract with the lender under duress and that the contract was, in fact, usurious. Is the court likely to grant the borrower's motion for summary judgment?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the motion and affidavit demonstrate that she signed the contract with the lender under duress.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the motion and affidavit demonstrate that the contract with the lender was usurious.
Answer C: C: No, because the borrower may not obtain a judgment in her favor on the basis of the lender's affirmative defenses.
Answer D: D: No, because the borrower failed to establish that there are no genuine issues of material fact.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. The court may grant the borrower's motion because state law indicates that duress is a complete defense to a breach of contract action.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. Under state law, the usurious character of the interest would entitle the borrower only to repay at a reduced rate of interest, not to be released from liability altogether. C is incorrect. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure (FRCP) 56 does permit a defendant to obtain a judgment in its favor on the basis of affirmative defenses. D is incorrect. The fact that there are cross-motions for summary judgment does not necessarily establish that there are genuine issues of material fact. The borrower presented an unrefuted affidavit in support of her motion, so the court may grant the borrower's motion because of state law providing that duress is a complete defense to a breach of contract action.
