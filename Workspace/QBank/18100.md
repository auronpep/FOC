Question: In a federal assault trial, Miriam asks witness Joshua, “What color was the traffic light?” Joshua answers, “It was green, and Elijah had just failed a drug test.” No question asked about drugs. Elijah immediately objects and moves to strike the answer. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the motion because any answer to a proper question is admissible in full.
Answer B: Strike the entire answer, including the statement that the light was green.
Answer C: Deny the motion because only the lawyer who asked the question may object to a nonresponsive answer.
Answer D: Strike the volunteered drug-test statement and, if requested, instruct the jury to disregard it.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness's volunteered, nonresponsive inadmissible matter may be stricken. The proper cure is targeted: strike the nonresponsive drug-test statement, not necessarily the responsive answer about the light.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a proper question does not make volunteered inadmissible material admissible. B is overbroad because the responsive portion may remain. B: A is wrong because a proper question does not make volunteered inadmissible material admissible. B is overbroad because the responsive portion may remain. C is wrong because the opposing party may move to strike prejudicial nonresponsive matter. C: C is wrong because the opposing party may move to strike prejudicial nonresponsive matter.
