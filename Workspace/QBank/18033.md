Question: In a federal civil jury trial, the parties do not stipulate to a nonunanimous verdict. The court seats eight jurors. After deliberations, seven jurors agree on liability and one juror dissents. The plaintiff asks the court to accept the verdict because a strong majority agreed. May the court accept the seven-to-one verdict?
Answer A: No, because a federal civil verdict must be unanimous unless the parties stipulate otherwise
Answer B: Yes, because any verdict by more than half of the jurors is enough in federal civil court
Answer C: Yes, because eight jurors is too many and the dissenting juror should be ignored
Answer D: No, because federal civil juries must always have exactly 12 members
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: With no stipulation, unanimity is required even though the jury size is within the permitted range.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A simple majority is not the default federal civil standard. C: Eight jurors is within the federal civil range, and each juror counts. D: Federal civil juries may have between 6 and 12 members.
lb_rule_reference: A federal civil jury must have at least 6 and no more than 12 members, and the verdict must be unanimous unless the parties stipulate otherwise.
