


BARMATRIX Q#: 22703
Outline_code: 43020201
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Judicial Review
Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: The State A legislature enacted a statute that permanently removed from parental custody any child whose parents were convicted of a drug crime. A man and a woman had three children. The man was convicted of possession of methamphetamine with the intent to distribute. The state sued to terminate both parents' custody rights to the three children because of the man's conviction. At trial, the woman argued that the new statute was unconstitutional as applied. The court told the woman that she had the burden to show that the statute's application to her was not rationally related to a legitimate state interest. Is the trial court correct?
Answer A: A: No, because the woman has a fundamental right to live with her children.
Answer B: B: No, because the state had the burden of showing that the statute is rationally related to a legitimate government interest.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the woman is arguing that the statute is unconstitutional as applied to her.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the woman's claim will determine who has custody of minor children.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A person has a fundamental right to live with members of his or her own family. If a statute infringes on a fundamental right, a court reviewing the statute uses strict scrutiny analysis. Under strict scrutiny, the government must show that the infringement is necessary to advance a compelling state interest. That burden cannot be placed on the party opposing the statute. Here, the court incorrectly placed the burden of persuasion on the woman because her claim involved the fundamental right to live with her children. B is incorrect because the burden on the government when it infringes on fundamental rights is strict scrutiny, not merely the showing of a rational basis. C and D are incorrect because the burden cannot be placed on the woman since it involves a fundamental right.
