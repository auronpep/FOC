Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A manufacturer sold a table saw with the intended blade guard installed. The buyer's employer later removed the guard to speed production and rewired the saw so it would run with the guard absent. Months later, an employee using the altered saw cut his hand. Inspection showed the saw matched the manufacturer's specifications when sold. The employee sues the manufacturer under strict products liability for a manufacturing defect. Is the employee likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim for a manufacturing defect against the manufacturer?
Answer A: Yes, because the saw was dangerous when it injured the employee
Answer B: No, because the saw matched intended specifications when sold and the dangerous condition came from a later substantial alteration
Answer C: Yes, because all table saws should be designed so guards can never be removed
Answer D: No, because manufacturers are never liable once any third party alters a product after sale
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The saw did not leave the manufacturer as a rogue unit. The employer's later removal and rewiring created the condition that caused the injury, defeating this manufacturing-defect theory.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Manufacturing defect asks whether the product departed from intended design when it left the defendant's control. The dangerous condition was created by a later substantial alteration. C: The call asks manufacturing defect. A design-defect claim would require a linewide design analysis, but the manufacturing claim fails because this unit conformed when sold. D: Some alterations may be foreseeable or unrelated to causation. The narrower reason here is that the later substantial alteration created the dangerous condition and the saw conformed when sold.
lb_rule_reference: A manufacturing-defect claim requires that the unit departed from intended design when it left the defendant's control; a later substantial alteration can defeat that defect theory.
