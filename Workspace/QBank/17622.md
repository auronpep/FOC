Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: After Miranda warnings, Sofia said, "I want a lawyer." Detectives immediately stopped questioning and left her alone in an interview room. Thirty minutes later, Sofia knocked on the door and said, "I changed my mind. I want to tell you what happened." Detectives reread the warnings, Sofia said she understood and wanted to talk, and she confessed. Is Sofia's confession admissible under Miranda?
Answer A: No, because a request for counsel permanently bars all later police questioning
Answer B: No, because detectives should have waited until a lawyer was physically present
Answer C: Yes, because Sofia reinitiated conversation and then knowingly and voluntarily waived her rights
Answer D: Yes, because Sofia's original request for counsel was ambiguous
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Police honored the counsel request by stopping. Sofia later initiated further communication, received renewed warnings, acknowledged understanding, and chose to confess. That sequence supports admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The bar prevents police from reinitiating. It does not prevent the suspect from initiating further conversation and waiving rights. B: Counsel presence is required for police-initiated interrogation after invocation. Sofia herself reinitiated and then waived after fresh warnings. D: "I want a lawyer" was clear. The admissibility rests on suspect reinitiation and a later valid waiver, not ambiguity.
lb_rule_reference: After a clear request for counsel, police may not initiate interrogation, but the suspect may reinitiate communication and then knowingly and voluntarily waive Miranda rights.
