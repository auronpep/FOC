Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Robbery
Question: Owen is indicted for armed robbery of a convenience store and counsel is appointed. Detectives later visit Owen without counsel and say they only want to ask whether he committed "the theft from the same store without the weapon." They obtain no waiver. Owen admits he took the store's money during the incident. Should Owen's statement be suppressed under the Sixth Amendment?
Answer A: Yes, because the questioning concerned the same charged criminal episode and a same-offense variant after attachment
Answer B: No, because theft and armed robbery use different labels
Answer C: No, because Owen was not in custody during the conversation
Answer D: Yes, because police may not question an indicted defendant about any unrelated crime
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The government cannot avoid the attached counsel right by relabeling the charged robbery as a lesser theft from the same incident. Deliberate elicitation without counsel or waiver violates the Sixth Amendment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Offense specificity does not permit questioning about the same charged offense under a narrower label when the right has attached. C: Sixth Amendment deliberate elicitation after attachment does not depend on custody. D: The right is offense-specific. The problem is that this was the same charged offense in substance, not that all unrelated questioning is barred.
lb_rule_reference: Once the Sixth Amendment attaches to a charged offense, the government may not deliberately elicit statements about that same offense, including lesser-included or same-elements variants, without counsel or valid waiver.
