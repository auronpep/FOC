Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: Abraham orally agreed to sell a small parcel of land to Sarah for $90,000. Sarah paid $20,000, moved onto the parcel with Abraham's knowledge, and built a well and fence that substantially improved the land. Abraham then refused to sign a deed and asserted the Statute of Frauds. Sarah seeks specific enforcement of the oral land-sale agreement. What is the best argument for Sarah?
Answer A: The agreement is enforceable because payment alone always removes a land contract from the Statute of Frauds.
Answer B: The agreement is unenforceable because land-sale agreements can never be enforced without a signed writing.
Answer C: The agreement may be specifically enforced because Sarah's possession and substantial improvements are part performance pointing to the oral land contract.
Answer D: The agreement is enforceable only if Abraham orally admitted the contract in court.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A land-sale contract is generally within the Statute of Frauds, but equity may enforce an oral land contract when the buyer's acts of part performance are strongly referable to the alleged contract. Sarah's possession plus substantial improvements supply the classic part-performance route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because payment alone is usually not enough for land part performance. B: B is wrong because land contracts are not categorically unenforceable when equitable part performance applies. D: D is wrong because a judicial admission may matter in some contexts, but it is not the only possible path; the decisive route here is land part performance.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law land contract SOF / part performance
