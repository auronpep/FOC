Question: In a federal vandalism trial, John calls Luke to prove who broke a window. John's lawyer asks Luke, “What did Lydia tell you about who broke the window?” Lydia is not testifying, and John offers the answer to prove that the named person broke the window. The opposing party objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the question calls for hearsay.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because all questions asking what someone said are irrelevant.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Luke can repeat any statement he personally heard.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because the question is not leading.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The question calls for Luke to repeat Lydia's out-of-court statement to prove the truth of the assertion identifying the window breaker. That is hearsay unless a route admits it, and none is supplied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because out-of-court statements can be relevant or admissible for nontruth purposes or under exceptions. C: C is wrong because personally hearing the statement does not avoid hearsay when it is offered for truth. D is irrelevant; not leading does not make hearsay admissible. D: B is wrong because out-of-court statements can be relevant or admissible for nontruth purposes or under exceptions. C is wrong because personally hearing the statement does not avoid hearsay when it is offered for truth. D is irrelevant; not leading does not make hearsay admissible.
