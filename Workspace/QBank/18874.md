Question: A state terminates disability benefits after a paper review. The recipient has a statutory entitlement to benefits if medically eligible and argues that an in-person hearing was required before termination. The state argues written submissions are enough because in-person hearings are costly. Which framework should a court use to decide what process was due?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny, because any property deprivation is automatically reviewed as a fundamental-right burden
Answer B: A fixed rule that an oral hearing is always required before any benefit ends
Answer C: Mathews balancing: private interest, risk of erroneous deprivation and value of safeguards, and government interest
Answer D: Rational basis review of whether the benefit program is a wise policy
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The protected benefit entitlement triggers the flexible process-balancing inquiry.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Procedural due process asks what procedures are required, not whether a fundamental right was burdened. B: Due process is flexible and uses balancing, not a universal oral-hearing rule. D: The challenge is to termination procedure, not substantive policy validity.
lb_rule_reference: Once a protected interest is found, the process due is generally assessed by balancing the private interest, the risk of erroneous deprivation and value of additional safeguards, and the government's interest.
