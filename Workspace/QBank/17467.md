Question: In a federal auto-theft trial, Samuel testifies that the fleeing car was blue. Identity is disputed. The prosecution offers an authenticated traffic-camera video showing the fleeing car was red, matching the defendant's car. Samuel objects that the video is merely extrinsic impeachment. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the video because extrinsic contradiction is always barred.
Answer B: Admit the video only to show Samuel's credibility, not as substantive evidence.
Answer C: Admit the video both as substantive evidence on identity and to contradict Samuel's testimony.
Answer D: Exclude the video unless Samuel first admits that the color of the car is important.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The video is not offered merely to contradict Samuel on a collateral matter. The car's color bears directly on identity, a disputed issue. If authenticated, the video is independently admissible as substantive evidence and also contradicts Samuel's testimony.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because extrinsic contradiction is not always barred. B: B is wrong because the video has independent substantive relevance to identity. D: D is wrong because materiality does not depend on Samuel's admission.
lb_rule_reference: Material contradiction / substantive and impeachment use
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests material contradiction and the difference between collateral impeachment and independent substantive proof.
