Question: A jury awards $20,000 in compensatory damages and $25 million in punitive damages for a single economic tort with no physical harm. Comparable state civil penalties are modest. The defendant raises a due process challenge. What should the court consider?
Answer A: Nothing, because punitive damages are entirely within jury discretion
Answer B: Reprehensibility, ratio to actual harm, and comparable civil penalties
Answer C: Strict scrutiny because money is a fundamental right
Answer D: Mathews balancing of hearing procedures only
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: These guideposts evaluate whether the punitive award is grossly excessive.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Due process imposes constitutional limits on grossly excessive punitive awards. C: Punitive-damages review uses due-process excessiveness guideposts, not fundamental-right strict scrutiny. D: The challenge is to the substantive excessiveness of the award, not notice or hearing procedure.
lb_rule_reference: The Due Process Clause limits grossly excessive punitive damages, considering reprehensibility, ratio, and comparable civil penalties.
