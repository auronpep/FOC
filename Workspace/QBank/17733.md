Question: Mary agreed to buy Joseph's field, provided that Mary's surveyor approved the boundary lines by June 10. The approval clause was included because Mary wanted protection against buying less land than expected. The surveyor failed to issue the report by June 10, but Mary told Joseph she was satisfied and ready to close anyway. Joseph refused to close, arguing that the surveyor condition had failed. What is the best result?
Answer A: Joseph may refuse to close because any failed condition makes both parties' duties disappear.
Answer B: Joseph may refuse to close only if the surveyor's delay was Mary's fault.
Answer C: Mary cannot waive the condition because waivers require new consideration.
Answer D: Mary may waive the condition inserted for her protection, and Joseph cannot rely on its nonoccurrence to avoid selling.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A condition included for one party's protection may generally be waived by the protected party. The surveyor-approval condition protected Mary, and she chose to proceed. Joseph, the nonbeneficiary of the condition, cannot use the nonoccurrence to escape his selling duty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the effect of nonoccurrence depends on whose duty the condition protects and whether it is waived. B: B is wrong because Joseph cannot invoke this buyer-protection condition merely by showing delay. C: C is wrong because waiver of a condition does not require new consideration in this setting.
lb_rule_reference: Condition beneficiary / waiver
