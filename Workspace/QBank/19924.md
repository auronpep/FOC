Question: Peter contracted with John to manage Peter's farm. The contract required John to pay Peter's mother Mary $1,000 per month “while Mary lives in Peter's farmhouse.” Mary moved out of the farmhouse after six months. John stopped making payments. Mary sues John for payments after she moved out. What is the likely result?
Answer A: Mary can recover because she was named in the contract.
Answer B: Mary can recover because beneficiaries are not bound by contract conditions that refer to them.
Answer C: Mary can recover because she was not a party to the management contract.
Answer D: Mary cannot recover the later payments because the contract limited John's payment duty to months when Mary lived in the farmhouse.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Mary was an intended beneficiary of the payment term, but her right is limited by the contract's own scope. The promise required payments only while Mary lived in the farmhouse, so John's duty for later payments did not mature after she moved out.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because being named does not remove express limits. B: B is wrong because beneficiaries take subject to conditions and scope terms. C: C is wrong because nonparty status does not free Mary from the limits of the contract she seeks to enforce.
