Question: Martha orally agreed to buy a lot from Isaac for $60,000. Martha paid Isaac $8,000 as a deposit but never took possession, made no improvements, and received no deed. Isaac refused to convey. Martha sues for specific performance of the land-sale contract and, alternatively, return of her $8,000. What is the best statement of the likely consequence?
Answer A: Martha can specifically enforce the land sale because any payment removes a land contract from the Statute of Frauds.
Answer B: The oral land-sale contract is void, so Martha cannot recover the deposit under any theory.
Answer C: Martha can specifically enforce the land sale only if she proves the oral terms by two witnesses.
Answer D: The oral land-sale contract is generally unenforceable for specific performance, but Martha may have a restitutionary claim for the deposit.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Without a sufficient writing or part-performance facts strongly referable to the land sale, the Statute of Frauds generally bars specific enforcement of the oral land contract. But the usual consequence is not that the transaction is void for all purposes; restitution may be available to return benefits conferred.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because payment alone usually is not enough for land part performance. B: B is wrong because unenforceability of the contract action does not automatically bar restitution. C: C is wrong because witness count is not the land SOF rule.
