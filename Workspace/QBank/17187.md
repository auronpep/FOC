Outline: Burglary
Question: At night, Tomas pries open a bedroom window of another person's house. Standing outside, he inserts a hooked pole through the window and uses it to pull a jewelry box from a dresser. No part of Tomas's body crosses the window frame. Is the entry element of common-law burglary satisfied?
Answer A: No, because no part of Tomas's body entered the house
Answer B: No, because Tomas did not damage the window frame
Answer C: Yes, because Tomas inserted a tool into the dwelling to commit the theft
Answer D: Yes, but only if Tomas later entered the house to retrieve the pole
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The hooked pole entered the dwelling and was used to accomplish the intended felony inside. That satisfies entry.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: An instrument inserted to commit the felony can satisfy entry. B: Opening or prying a closed window can satisfy breaking without damage, and the tool insertion satisfies entry. D: No later bodily entry is required when the felony-committing instrument entered.
lb_rule_reference: For common-law burglary, entry is satisfied when any part of the body or an instrument used to commit the felony enters the dwelling.
