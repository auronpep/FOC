


BARMATRIX Q#: 18373
Outline_code: 44040304
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Intermediate scrutiny (quasi-suspect) classifications
Question: The plaintiff challenges the constitutionality of a state law that provides that no contraceptive device that requires insertion into a cavity of the human body may be sold without a prescription. If the only argument used by the plaintiff is that the statute violates the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment because only women use such devices, which of the following would be the most effective argument in opposition to the plaintiff's claim?
Answer A: A: Gender is not a suspect classification.
Answer B: B: The statute has a rational basis because devices inserted into the human body are more likely to cause harm than devices manufactured solely for external use.
Answer C: C: The right to protect citizens against their own lack of judgment is included in the state's police powers.
Answer D: D: The statute bears a substantial relationship to an important government interest because devices inserted into the human body are more likely to cause harm than devices manufactured solely for external use.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A is incorrect because the fact that a system of classification does or does not discriminate against a suspect class determines the standard to be applied, but it does not itself determine whether the statute is constitutional. B is incorrect because a rational basis is not sufficient to make constitutional a statute that discriminates on the basis of gender. C is incorrect because police powers, like all other powers of the state, may not be exercised in a way that is inconsistent with the United States Constitution.
