Question: In a writing signed by both parties on December 1, a man agreed to buy from a distributor a gasoline engine for $1,000, delivery to be made on the following February 1. Through a secretarial error, the writing called for delivery on March 1, but neither party noticed the error until February 1. Before signing the agreement, the man and the distributor orally agreed that the contract of sale would be effective only if the man notified the distributor in writing no later than January 2 that the man had arranged to resell the engine to a third person. Otherwise, they agreed orally, "There is no deal." On December 15, the man entered into a contract with a mechanic to resell the engine to the mechanic at a profit. On December 16, the man notified the distributor by telephone of the man's resale agreement with the mechanic, and explained that a written notice was not feasible because the man's secretary was ill. The distributor replied, "That's okay. I'll get the engine to you on February 1, as we agreed." Having learned, however, that the engine had increased in value about 75% since December 1, the distributor renounced the agreement on February 1. If the man sues the distributor on February 2 for breach of contract, which of the following concepts best supports the man's claim?
Answer A: A: Substantial performance.
Answer B: B: Nonoccurrence of a condition subsequent.
Answer C: C: Waiver of condition.
Answer D: D: Novation of buyers.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. The non-occurrence of the express condition to provide written notice of re-sale by January 2 would normally discharge the parties' duties under the contract, however, the condition was excused when the distributor verbally waived the condition by agreeing to send the engine without written notice. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Generally, express conditions must be strictly satisfied, and substantial performance will not be enough. B is incorrect. The condition here is a condition precedent, not a condition subsequent. Moreover, excuse by non- occurrence is not applicable because that arises when a party ignores the non-occurrence of a condition and performs anyway. D is incorrect. There is no indication of a novation here.
