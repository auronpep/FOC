Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Abraham signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey my ranch to Sarah and to the person I later choose, each to receive one-half.” Sarah was alive and accepted the deed. Two weeks later, Abraham told Isaac that Isaac was the person he chose, but Abraham signed no new deed. Who owns the ranch?
Answer A: Sarah and Isaac each own one-half, because Abraham later identified Isaac.
Answer B: Sarah owns the entire ranch, because she was the only grantee named at delivery.
Answer C: Abraham owns the entire ranch, because one attempted grantee was not identified.
Answer D: Sarah owns one-half, and Abraham retains the other one-half.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is ascertainable grantee identity at delivery. Sarah was a living, identified grantee for one-half and takes that share. The other attempted grantee was not identified or ascertainable when the deed was delivered, and Abraham's later oral selection without a new deed does not transfer that share. Abraham retains the other one-half.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because later oral identification does not cure an unascertainable grantee in the delivered deed. B: B is wrong because Sarah was given only one-half. C: C is wrong because the failed half does not defeat the valid half conveyed to Sarah.
lb_rule_reference: Partial invalid share / future selection not grantee identity
