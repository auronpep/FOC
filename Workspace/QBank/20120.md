Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal robbery trial, Hannah testifies that she heard a masked robber say, “Open the drawer.” Hannah had worked with David for two years and says the robber's voice sounded like David's, although the store alarm was ringing at the same time. The prosecutor offers Hannah's identification of the voice. David objects that Hannah is not certain. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because voice identification requires certainty.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless a voice-recognition expert confirms Hannah's opinion.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Hannah's prior familiarity with David's voice supplies a basis; the alarm affects weight.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the prosecutor also introduces a recording of the robbery.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness may identify a voice based on hearing it at any time under circumstances connecting it to the speaker. Hannah's workplace familiarity supplies the foundation. Her uncertainty and the alarm affect weight, not admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because voice identification does not require certainty. B: B is wrong because lay voice identification can be sufficient. D: D is wrong because a recording is not required when the witness testifies from personal perception.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(5)
lb_student_diagnostic: Certainty threshold error: student treats weakness in voice recognition as exclusion rather than weight.
