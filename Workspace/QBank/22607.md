


BARMATRIX Q#: 22607
Outline_code: 44040300
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Equal Protection Clause
Question: State A university created a new merit program to attract in- state residents and keep its “best and brightest” from leaving the state. If a state high school student was accepted to the program, that student got free tuition, a travel stipend, a special dorm, and priority in choosing his or her classes. A high school student who was a legal resident of the state but not a United States citizen applied to the program. The university denied the student's application for the sole reason that the student was not a United States citizen. The university believed that noncitizens would be unlikely to stay in the state, which was the goal of the program, and the program had a limited number of available seats. The student sued, arguing that the denial of her application was unconstitutional. Is the student's denial from the program constitutional?
Answer A: A: Yes, because resident aliens may be denied state benefits.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the denial was rationally related to a legitimate state purpose.
Answer C: C: No, because the denial violated the Privileges and Immunities Clause.
Answer D: D: No, because there was insufficient justification for discriminating against resident aliens.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Although a state may discriminate against aliens in situations where citizenship is an essential part, such as when discriminating against an alien holding public office or being a police officer, all other situations must pass strict scrutiny analysis because alienage is a suspect classification. Here, while the state believes resident aliens are less likely to remain in the state, this is not a compelling justification for keeping resident aliens out of the program. Consequently, the student's denial will not pass strict scrutiny. A is incorrect because the state cannot always deny resident aliens state benefits based on their alienage alone. B is incorrect because rational basis analysis would only apply if citizenship was an essential part of the situation. C is incorrect because the Privileges and Immunities Clause protects against discrimination against residents of other states, and the student is a State A resident.
