Subtopic: Defenses to Enforceability
Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: Rachel orally agrees to buy 700 sacks of grain from Samuel for $7,000. Rachel signs a purchase order and sends it to Samuel. Samuel does not sign anything, does not deliver, and is not a merchant. Rachel sues Samuel for breach. Samuel asserts the Statute of Frauds. What is the best result?
Answer A: Samuel has a Statute of Frauds defense because the only signed writing was signed by Rachel, not by Samuel, the party against whom enforcement is sought.
Answer B: Rachel can enforce the contract because one party's signature is always enough regardless of who is sued.
Answer C: Rachel can enforce the contract because grain is perishable.
Answer D: Samuel has no defense because the purchase order was sent to him.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the ordinary UCC SOF rule, the writing must be signed by the party against whom enforcement is sought, unless an exception applies. Rachel seeks enforcement against Samuel, but Samuel did not sign and no merchant-confirmation or other exception is present.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the relevant signature is the party to be charged. C: C is wrong because perishability is not a general UCC §2-201 exception. D: D is wrong because receipt of a purchase order by a nonmerchant seller does not by itself satisfy the SOF against that seller.
lb_rule_reference: SOF signature by party to be charged
