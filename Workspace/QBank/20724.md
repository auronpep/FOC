Question: In a federal automobile trial, Noah calls Joanna, who saw the collision. Noah's lawyer asks, “Was Peter negligent when he entered the intersection?” Peter objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because Joanna saw the collision.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because lay witnesses may always decide negligence.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because the question calls for a legal conclusion rather than helpful lay observations.
Answer D: Sustain the objection because eyewitnesses may not testify in negligence cases.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Joanna may describe what she saw and may give helpful lay opinions based on perception, but asking whether Peter was “negligent” calls for a legal conclusion that invades the jury's role.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because perception supports factual testimony but not a legal conclusion. B: B is wrong because lay opinion is limited to helpful perception-based opinions, not legal labels. D: D is wrong because eyewitnesses may testify to what they perceived.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 701 / legal conclusion by lay witness
