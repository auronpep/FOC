Question: Abigail gave Nathan the exclusive right to book her speaking engagements and negotiate sponsorship appearances for one year. The written agreement set Nathan's commission percentage but did not set a minimum number of bookings. Nathan did nothing for six months. Abigail sued, and Nathan argued that no effort duty was written. Which statement is least likely to be accepted?
Answer A: UCC Article 2 automatically governs because the agreement grants an exclusive commercial right.
Answer B: A court may imply reasonable or best efforts from an exclusive agency arrangement even without a numeric booking quota.
Answer C: The absence of a fixed quota does not automatically make the exclusive agency illusory.
Answer D: Because the transaction concerns services and intangible bookings, any custom or effort evidence must be routed through common-law interpretation rather than Article 2 as the direct source.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: UCC Article 2 governs transactions in goods. An exclusive booking and sponsorship agency is a services/intangible arrangement, even though it is commercial. The trigger facts are speaking engagements, sponsorship appearances, commissions, and no sale of goods. The decisive lock is source law: a common-law exclusive-agency/best-efforts route may matter, but Article 2's exclusive-dealing rule does not automatically control this services contract.
