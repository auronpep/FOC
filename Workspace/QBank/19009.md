Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Noah agreed in a signed writing to sell Sarah 1,000 jars of oil. The writing contained a merger clause and was silent about whether Sarah could return jars with chipped labels. In four prior oil contracts between the same parties, Noah accepted returns of jars with chipped labels without objection. Sarah offers the prior course of dealing to explain the silent return issue. How should the court rule under UCC Article 2?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence because a merger clause bars all course-of-dealing evidence.
Answer B: Admit the evidence if it reasonably explains or supplements the agreement and does not contradict an express term.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence because course of dealing can never be used in a goods contract.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if the written contract is first rescinded.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC 2-202, a final written expression may be explained or supplemented by course of dealing, usage of trade, or course of performance. A merger clause is not always an absolute bar to such meaning evidence if the evidence is reasonably consistent with the writing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it treats the merger clause as absolute under the UCC. C: C is wrong because Article 2 expressly recognizes course-of-dealing evidence. D: D is wrong because the evidence explains or supplements the agreement; rescission is not required.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-202 / course evidence despite final writing
