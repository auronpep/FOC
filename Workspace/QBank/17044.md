Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A collector kept a venomous exotic snake in a locked terrarium. The snake had never escaped or bitten anyone. When a repair worker lawfully entered the collector's study, the snake escaped through a cracked vent and bit the worker. In the worker's strict-liability action, how should the court rule?
Answer A: For the worker, because a venomous bite is a characteristic danger of keeping that wild animal
Answer B: For the collector, because the snake had never bitten anyone before
Answer C: For the collector, because the worker was injured while performing paid work
Answer D: For the worker only if the collector knew the vent was cracked
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The keeper is strictly liable for harm caused by the wild animal's characteristic dangerous propensity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Prior harm is not required for wild animals. C: The worker's paid status does not remove strict liability for characteristic wild-animal harm. D: Knowledge of the defective vent would support negligence, but strict liability turns on the wild animal and characteristic harm.
lb_rule_reference: The keeper of a wild animal is strictly liable for injuries caused by the animal's characteristic dangerous propensity; the plaintiff need not show that this individual animal had previously caused harm.
