Question: The plaintiff shows emails from nonprivileged sources suggesting that Daniel may have used his lawyer to draft false invoices. The plaintiff asks the judge to review a small set of Daniel's lawyer emails in camera to determine whether the crime-fraud exception applies. Daniel argues that any review would itself destroy privilege. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: The judge must publicly disclose the emails before deciding privilege.
Answer B: The judge may not look at the emails unless Daniel first waives privilege.
Answer C: The judge must deny review because allegations of fraud can never justify in camera review.
Answer D: The judge may conduct in camera review if the threshold showing justifies it, without making the emails public merely by reviewing them.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Courts may use in camera review to evaluate privilege claims, including crime-fraud issues, when an adequate threshold showing is made. Judicial review under seal does not itself equal public disclosure or waiver. The decisive axis is procedure for testing privilege.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This defeats the purpose of in camera review and would disclose before deciding privilege. B: B is wrong because Waiver is not required for the court to test a privilege claim under appropriate procedure. C: C is wrong because Fraud allegations do not automatically defeat privilege, but they can support limited review when adequately grounded.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / in camera privilege review
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is confusing court screening of privilege with disclosure to the opposing party.
