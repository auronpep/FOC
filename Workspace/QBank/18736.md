Question: In a federal theft trial, Lydia is charged with stealing a sealed box from a locked office. Lydia denies having access. The prosecutor offers testimony that Lydia had been issued a master key to the office suite the week before the theft. Lydia objects that many other employees also had keys. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony because Lydia's key access tends to make her opportunity to steal the box more probable, even if others also had access.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because opportunity evidence is relevant only if the defendant was the only person with access.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony because a key does not prove Lydia opened the office.
Answer D: Admit the testimony only if the prosecutor proves the key was used on the night of the theft.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Access to the locked office is circumstantial evidence of opportunity. Others' access and the lack of direct proof affect weight, not threshold relevance.
