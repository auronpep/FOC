Question: Daniel bought a ticket to a concert at an arena owned by John. The ticket gave Daniel the right to enter the arena and sit in a specified seat for that evening's concert. Before Daniel entered, John revoked the ticket without justification and offered to refund the price. Daniel claims that the ticket gave him an easement in the arena. Which is the best classification of Daniel's right?
Answer A: An easement appurtenant because the ticket identified a specific seat.
Answer B: A profit because Daniel paid value for entry.
Answer C: A license, though Daniel may have a contract claim if the revocation breached the ticket agreement.
Answer D: Title by adverse possession because Daniel intended to occupy the seat during the concert.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A ticket generally creates a license to enter and use property for a limited purpose. It does not create an easement or title interest merely because the holder paid value or received an assigned seat. Any wrongful revocation is usually analyzed as a contract issue, not as ownership of an easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an easement appurtenant requires a dominant parcel, and Daniel has none. B: B is wrong because a profit is a right to remove resources from land. D: D is wrong because intended temporary attendance is not adverse possession.
