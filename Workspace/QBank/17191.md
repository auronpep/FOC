Question: Sarah signed a writing to buy from Isaac “one gray donkey, with delivery next Friday.” Before signing, Isaac orally said two things: first, that he would include a new saddle; second, that Sarah could take a brown donkey instead of the gray one. The writing appears final but not complete and exclusive. Sarah offers both oral statements. Which result is most accurate?
Answer A: Both oral statements are admissible because the writing is not complete and exclusive.
Answer B: Only the saddle promise is admissible because it supplements the writing, while the brown-donkey option contradicts the express gray-donkey term.
Answer C: Only the brown-donkey option is admissible because it concerns the main subject of the contract.
Answer D: Neither oral statement is admissible because any prior oral statement is barred after signing.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Each oral term must be routed separately. The saddle term is a consistent additional term and may supplement a partial integration. The brown-donkey option contradicts the express written term identifying the gray donkey.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because partial integration does not permit contradictory terms. C: C is wrong because the brown-donkey option contradicts the written subject. D: D is wrong because consistent additional terms may supplement a partial integration.
lb_rule_reference: Split oral package / one supplement and one contradiction
