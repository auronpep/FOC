Question: A state imposes a special fee on all furniture manufactured outside the state before it may be sold locally. Identical in-state furniture pays no fee. The state says the fee raises money for local schools. What is the likely DCC result?
Answer A: The fee is valid because states may raise revenue by taxing imports
Answer B: The fee is likely invalid because it discriminates against out-of-state furniture
Answer C: The fee receives Pike balancing because taxes are always evenhanded burdens
Answer D: The fee is valid because furniture is not commerce until a sale occurs
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The tax burden falls only on out-of-state goods and protects local manufacturers.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Taxing authority does not permit discrimination against out-of-state goods. C: A tax that facially targets out-of-state goods is discriminatory, not merely incidental. D: Goods moving into a state for sale are part of interstate commerce.
lb_rule_reference: A state tax that discriminates against out-of-state goods or sellers is generally invalid under the Dormant Commerce Clause unless a narrow justification applies.
