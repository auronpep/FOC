Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff brings a federal discrimination claim and a related state-law claim under a newly enacted state statute that state courts have not interpreted. The state-law claim shares facts with the federal claim, but resolving it would require deciding a novel issue of state regulatory policy. Must the federal court exercise supplemental jurisdiction over the state-law claim?
Answer A: Yes, because relatedness makes supplemental jurisdiction mandatory
Answer B: Yes, because federal courts are always better suited to decide related claims together
Answer C: No, the court may decline supplemental jurisdiction because the claim raises a novel or complex state-law issue
Answer D: No, because related state-law claims can never be heard in federal court
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Novel or complex state-law questions are a statutory reason for discretionary decline.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1367(c) gives the court discretion to decline in specified circumstances. B: Efficiency matters, but Congress allows decline for novel or complex state-law issues. D: Related state-law claims can be heard; the point is discretionary decline, not categorical exclusion.
lb_rule_reference: A federal court may decline supplemental jurisdiction when the state-law claim raises a novel or complex issue of state law.
