Question: Moses hired Daniel to build a small medical office. The contract required a fire-rated rear exit because the office would serve patients. Daniel completed most of the office but omitted the fire-rated exit and installed an ordinary locked door instead. The building could not lawfully be used as a medical office without the required exit. Daniel demanded the full contract price less the cost of replacing the door. What is the best result?
Answer A: Daniel substantially performed because most of the building was finished.
Answer B: Moses must pay the full contract price because the defect can be priced.
Answer C: Moses must pay only if Daniel proves Moses waived the written specifications before construction began.
Answer D: Moses's duty to pay the contract price has not matured because the omitted fire-rated exit is a material failure going to the purpose of the contract.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Substantial performance does not exist when the defect is material and goes to the essence of the bargain. The required fire-rated exit was central to lawful use of the medical office. Daniel's failure prevented substantial performance, so Moses's payment duty for the contract price did not mature.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because percentage completion does not control when the missing feature is central to the contract's purpose. B: B is wrong because an easily priced defect can still be material if it defeats the intended use. C: C is wrong because the issue is materiality and substantial performance, not waiver before construction.
