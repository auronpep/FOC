Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: During a dispute over a parking spot, Omar repeatedly kicked Sylvia in the ribs and stomach with steel-toed boots after she fell to the ground. Omar did not intend to kill Sylvia — he later told police he "just wanted to hurt her real bad" and stopped kicking when she became motionless. Sylvia died three hours later from internal hemorrhaging caused by the blows. Omar had no prior relationship with Sylvia. Can Omar be convicted of murder?
Answer A: No, because Omar lacked intent to kill, which is required for murder
Answer B: Yes, for first-degree murder, because the prolonged beating shows premeditation
Answer C: Yes, for murder, because intent to inflict grievous bodily harm is a recognized form of malice aforethought
Answer D: Yes, for involuntary manslaughter, because Omar did not intend to kill
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: At common law, malice aforethought encompasses four states of mind: (1) intent to kill, (2) intent to inflict grievous bodily harm, (3) depraved-heart recklessness, and (4) intent to commit a felony (felony murder). Omar's intent to "hurt her real bad" — evidenced by stomping on a fallen woman with steel-toed boots — satisfies the grievous bodily harm form of malice. Death resulting from that intent establishes murder (second-degree, absent premeditation).
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: At common law, malice aforethought includes not only intent to kill but also intent to inflict grievous bodily harm. Intent to cause serious bodily injury that results in death is sufficient for murder. Omar's stated intent to "hurt her real bad" combined with repeated stomping with steel-toed boots constitutes intent to inflict grievous bodily harm. B: Premeditation requires prior deliberation about the killing — a decision to kill formed through cool reflection. There is no evidence Omar thought about killing Sylvia before or during the attack. Continued assault during a fight does not constitute premeditation toward a killing. The proper charge is second-degree murder under the grievous bodily harm theory. D: Involuntary manslaughter applies to unintended killings caused by criminal negligence or an unlawful act. It does not apply when the defendant intentionally inflicts grievous bodily harm that happens to cause death. That conduct elevates the charge to murder under the grievous bodily harm theory. Involuntary manslaughter is the wrong charge because it understates Omar's culpability.
lb_rule_reference: At common law, intent to inflict grievous bodily harm (serious bodily injury) is a sufficient form of malice aforethought; if death results, the killing is murder even without intent to kill
