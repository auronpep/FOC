Question: In a federal robbery trial, the store owner Daniel offers a surveillance video showing the counter area. Daniel testifies that the store's camera system records continuously, that the video file was copied from the system, and that the file fairly shows what the system recorded at the relevant time. The defendant objects that no eyewitness to the recording process testified. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Exclude the video because only an eyewitness to the robbery can authenticate it.
Answer B: Exclude the video because videos are writings and therefore inadmissible unless the original camera is brought to court.
Answer C: Admit the video if the testimony about the recording process is sufficient to support a finding that the video is what Daniel claims.
Answer D: Admit the video only as impeachment evidence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Video evidence can be authenticated through evidence describing the process or system and showing that it produces an accurate result. An eyewitness to the event is not the only authentication route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A overdemands eyewitness authentication. B is a best-evidence false positive; the camera itself need not be produced. D misclassifies the video's use.
