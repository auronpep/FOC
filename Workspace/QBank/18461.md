Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Battery
Question: Nina angrily threw a pebble toward Leo's shoulder intending to make him think he would be hit. Leo ducked, and the pebble struck and cracked Maya's tablet lying on a nearby bench. Nina did not know Maya or intend to damage the tablet. Maya sues Nina for trespass to chattels. Can transferred intent satisfy the intent element for Maya's chattel claim?
Answer A: No, because Nina intended a person tort, not a property tort
Answer B: No, because Nina did not know Maya owned the tablet
Answer C: Yes, because Nina intended a covered assault and the completed invasion was a covered chattel tort
Answer D: Yes, but only because Nina acted negligently by throwing the pebble
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Nina intended to cause apprehension of contact, and the pebble caused actual damage to chattel. Both are within the transferred-intent family.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Trespass to chattels is in the traditional transferred-intent circuit with battery, assault, false imprisonment, and trespass to land. B: Intent can transfer from the intended protected invasion to the actual protected invasion inside the circuit. D: The intentional-tort route does not depend on ordinary negligence when transferred intent supplies the intent element.
lb_rule_reference: Transferred intent can operate within the traditional circuit when an intended assault or battery results in trespass to chattels.
