Question: Diane and Eric robbed a convenience store at gunpoint. As they fled, a police officer arrived and ordered them to stop. Eric pointed his gun at the officer, and the officer shot and killed Eric in self-defense. Diane was apprehended nearby moments later. The prosecution charged Diane with felony murder for Eric's death. The jurisdiction follows the common-law agency theory of felony murder. Is Diane guilty of felony murder for Eric's death?
Answer A: Yes, because Eric's death occurred during the commission of the robbery Diane was participating in
Answer B: Yes, because Diane proximately caused Eric's death by initiating the robbery
Answer C: No, because under the agency theory, felony murder does not apply when a third party — not a co-felon — performs the killing
Answer D: No, because Eric's own conduct (pointing a gun at the officer) was a superseding intervening cause
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The agency theory limits felony murder to killings committed by a co-felon acting as the criminal enterprise's agent in furtherance of the felony. When an innocent party or law enforcement kills a co-felon during the felony, that killing is not attributable to the surviving felon under the agency theory. Here, the officer — a third party resisting the crime — killed Eric. Diane is not liable for felony murder under the agency theory.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under the agency theory, felony murder applies only to killings committed by a co-felon acting as the "agent" of the criminal enterprise in furtherance of the felony. When the killing is performed by a third party (here, a police officer) resisting the felony, the surviving felon is not vicariously liable under the agency theory. The question specifies this jurisdiction uses the agency theory. B: But-for causation alone does not establish felony murder liability under the agency theory. The agency theory specifically rejects proximate cause as the basis for felony murder and instead requires that the killing be performed by a co-felon in furtherance of the crime. B describes the proximate cause theory, which some jurisdictions apply, but the question specifies the agency theory jurisdiction. D: While intervening cause analysis is available in some homicide contexts, the agency theory does not turn on causation analysis at all. The correct basis for Diane's non-liability is the structural limit of the agency theory — not Eric's choice to escalate. D reaches the right result but through the wrong doctrine; on an exam, the tested doctrine is the agency theory limit, not intervening cause.
lb_rule_reference: Under the agency theory (majority common-law rule), felony murder liability does not attach to a surviving felon when the killing is performed by a third party (police or victim) in resistance to the felony; only killings by a co-felon in furtherance of the crime trigger liability
