Question: During discovery, the district court orders a company to produce emails over its attorney-client privilege objection. The company files an immediate appeal, invoking the collateral order doctrine and arguing that disclosure would be harmful. Is the order automatically appealable under the collateral order doctrine?
Answer A: Yes, because every privilege ruling is immediately appealable
Answer B: Yes, because discovery orders are always final decisions
Answer C: No, because an adverse privilege discovery order generally must await final judgment or another proper review mechanism
Answer D: No, because attorney-client privilege does not exist in federal court
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The collateral order doctrine is narrow and does not automatically cover ordinary privilege production orders.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ordinary privilege discovery orders generally are not collateral-order appeals. B: Discovery orders are usually interlocutory and reviewed later. D: The privilege exists; the issue is immediate appellate jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: A discovery order adverse to attorney-client privilege generally is not immediately appealable as a collateral order; review usually waits for final judgment or another proper route.
