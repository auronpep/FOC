Question: A state allows manufacturers to sell appliances in the state only if they certify that the price charged in every other state during the same month is no lower than the in-state price. The state says it is regulating only sales within its borders. What is the strongest DCC objection?
Answer A: The law directly controls out-of-state pricing and risks impermissible extraterritorial regulation
Answer B: The law is valid because a state may control all prices nationwide once a seller enters its market
Answer C: The law is valid because price rules never affect commerce
Answer D: Article IV privileges and immunities is the only relevant doctrine
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The certification ties access to the in-state market to prices charged wholly elsewhere.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: State power over in-state transactions does not let it regulate nationwide prices. C: Price controls are commercial regulation. D: The plaintiff could be a manufacturer, and the problem is regulation of interstate commerce.
lb_rule_reference: A state law is suspect when it directly controls commerce occurring wholly outside the state's borders, such as by tying in-state sales to prices charged elsewhere.
