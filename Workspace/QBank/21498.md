Question: A local newspaper negligently but falsely reported that a private engineer falsified water-safety data for a city project. The story concerned public drinking water. The engineer proved actual injury to reputation but did not prove the newspaper knew the story was false or recklessly disregarded truth. The engineer seeks presumed damages. Can the engineer recover presumed damages?
Answer A: No, because presumed damages in this public-concern case require actual malice
Answer B: Yes, because negligence always permits presumed damages
Answer C: Yes, because water safety is not a public concern
Answer D: No, because private plaintiffs can never recover in public-concern defamation cases
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A private plaintiff may recover actual damages on negligence, but presumed damages for public-concern speech require actual malice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Negligence may support actual damages, not presumed or punitive damages in this setting. C: City drinking-water safety is a public concern. D: Private plaintiffs can recover actual damages with the required fault; the issue is presumed damages.
lb_rule_reference: In a public-concern defamation case, a private plaintiff must prove actual malice to recover presumed or punitive damages, though negligence may support actual damages.
