MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Cause in fact (actual cause)
Question: A scooter manufacturer sold a model with a linewide brake design that overheated on long downhill rides, causing delayed stopping. A feasible vented brake design would have reduced the risk at modest cost. The manufacturer knew riders often descended long hills in city commuting. A rider took the scooter downhill too fast despite a speed warning, the brakes faded, and the rider hit a parked car. A jury finds the design defective, assigns 70 percent responsibility to the manufacturer and 30 percent to the rider, and the jurisdiction applies pure comparative fault to strict products liability. The rider seeks full damages from the manufacturer. How should damages be handled in the rider's strict products liability claim against the manufacturer?
Answer A: The rider recovers nothing because any misuse of a product bars strict products liability
Answer B: The rider recovers 70 percent of damages because pure comparative fault reduces recovery by the rider's 30 percent share
Answer C: The rider recovers full damages because comparative fault never applies to strict products liability
Answer D: The rider recovers nothing because the speed warning shifts all responsibility to the user
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The jurisdiction applies pure comparative fault to strict products liability, and the jury assigned 30 percent responsibility to the rider. Recovery is reduced, not barred.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Foreseeable misuse does not automatically bar products liability, especially where the jurisdiction applies comparative fault. The jury found the defective design was still 70 percent responsible. C: The facts state that this jurisdiction applies pure comparative fault to strict products liability. The rider's 30 percent responsibility reduces recovery. D: A warning does not automatically shift all responsibility to the user when a defective design remains a legal cause. The jury allocated responsibility under comparative fault.
lb_rule_reference: In a comparative-fault jurisdiction, plaintiff fault may reduce recovery in a products claim, but foreseeable misuse does not automatically bar liability.
