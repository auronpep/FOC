Outline_code: 95070705
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Preliminary injunctions, permanent injunctions, and temporary restraining orders
Question: A woman obtained a temporary restraining order (TRO) in federal court that prevented her business partner from accessing the partnership's assets for 14 days or until a preliminary injunction hearing could take place. Because the preliminary injunction hearing could not be scheduled within the 14- day period, the court extended the TRO for another 30 days. The partner appealed the court's extension, and the woman moved to dismiss the appeal. Is the court likely to dismiss the appeal?
Answer A: A: Yes, because a TRO is not appealable.
Answer B: B: Yes, because there has not been a final judgment.
Answer C: C: No, because the extension made the TRO equivalent to a preliminary injunction.
Answer D: D: No, because a TRO is automatically appealable.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Temporary restraining orders (TRO) are governed by Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(b). A TRO is a short- term pretrial injunction and is intended to be a stop- gap measure to keep the status quo until the court holds a hearing on whether or not to grant a preliminary injunction. Because TROs are not final orders, they cannot be appealed. However, if a court extends a TRO beyond the 14- day time limit provided in the Rules, the TRO is treated as an appealable preliminary injunction under 28 U.S.C. §1292(a)(1), which permits appeals of interlocutory orders “granting, continuing, modifying, refusing or dissolving injunctions, or refusing to dissolve or modify injunctions.” Here, because the TRO has been extended past 14 days, the partner can appeal the TRO. Therefore, A, B, and D are incorrect.
