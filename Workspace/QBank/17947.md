Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: An officer stopped Nora for weaving across the lane line. The officer kept Nora at the roadside, asked for license and registration, and asked, "How many drinks have you had tonight?" Nora answered, "Three beers." The stop lasted six minutes before the officer arrested her. No Miranda warnings were given before the roadside question. Should Nora's answer be suppressed under Miranda?
Answer A: Yes, because any police question during a stop requires Miranda warnings
Answer B: Yes, because traffic stops are seizures under the Fourth Amendment
Answer C: No, because the brief roadside stop had not become Miranda custody when Nora answered
Answer D: No, because Miranda applies only after formal charges are filed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The stop was short, public, and typical of a traffic investigation. Although the officer asked an incriminating question, Miranda did not apply before custody comparable to formal arrest.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Miranda requires custody plus interrogation. Ordinary roadside traffic questioning is generally temporary and not comparable to formal arrest. B: Fourth Amendment seizure does not equal Miranda custody. Miranda custody requires restraint comparable to formal arrest. D: Miranda can apply before charges if there is custodial interrogation. The reason it does not apply here is lack of custody.
lb_rule_reference: Miranda warnings are required only for custodial interrogation; a brief roadside traffic stop usually is not custody comparable to formal arrest.
