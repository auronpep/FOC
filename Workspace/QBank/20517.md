Question: Joseph signed a deed stating, “For love and affection, I convey my cottage to Mary.” Joseph delivered the deed to Mary and said, “The cottage is yours now.” Mary accepted. Joseph later argued that the deed failed because Mary paid nothing. Who owns the cottage?
Answer A: Mary, because a delivered deed can be valid even though the conveyance is a gift.
Answer B: Joseph, because every deed must be supported by bargained-for consideration.
Answer C: Joseph, because love and affection can support a will but never a deed.
Answer D: Mary only if she paid at least nominal consideration after accepting the deed.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is execution and delivery rather than bargained-for consideration. Joseph signed and delivered a writing with present transfer words, Mary was a living grantee, and Mary accepted. The gift character of the deed does not block title from passing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because deed validity does not require contract consideration. C: C is wrong because a gift deed can convey land if the deed formalities and delivery are satisfied. D: D is wrong because nominal payment after delivery is not needed to make the deed effective.
lb_rule_reference: Gift deed / no consideration required
