Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Miriam managed a shop in one city. A different shop owner, Boaz, promised her a managerial job “for as long as both sides are satisfied” and told her she would begin in two months. In reasonable reliance, Miriam spent $4,000 moving and gave up her current apartment. Before she began work, Boaz withdrew the offer. The position would have been terminable at will after she started. If the court enforces the promise by promissory estoppel, what recovery is most appropriate?
Answer A: All salary Miriam expected to earn until retirement.
Answer B: The $4,000 moving expense and other reliance loss necessary to avoid injustice.
Answer C: Nothing, because promissory estoppel never applies to employment promises.
Answer D: The reasonable value of services Miriam would have performed for Boaz.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Promissory estoppel may enforce a promise to the extent necessary to avoid injustice. Because the job was effectively at will, full future salary would overstate the remedy. The better measure is reliance loss, such as the reasonable moving expense caused by the promise.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because promissory-estoppel relief may be limited, and an at-will-style job promise does not justify retirement-level expectation damages. C: C is wrong because employment promises can support reliance relief in appropriate cases. D: D is wrong because Miriam never performed services for Boaz, so reasonable value of services is not the right measure.
lb_rule_reference: Promissory estoppel remedy limited as justice requires
