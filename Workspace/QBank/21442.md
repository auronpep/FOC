Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: Caleb orally agreed to sell his farm to Lydia. No single document contains all terms. Lydia has a signed letter from Caleb stating, “I will sell you my north farm for the price we discussed,” and a separate unsigned email chain between them clearly identifying the price as $200,000 and the closing date. Caleb denies enforceability under the Statute of Frauds. Can the documents together satisfy the writing requirement?
Answer A: No, because all essential terms must appear in one signed document.
Answer B: No, because the unsigned email chain can never be considered for any purpose.
Answer C: No, because letters to the buyer cannot satisfy the Statute of Frauds.
Answer D: Yes, if the writings are sufficiently connected and the signed letter, together with the related writings, reasonably identifies the contract and the party to be charged.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The Statute of Frauds may be satisfied by multiple writings if they are sufficiently connected and at least one is signed by the party to be charged. A single document need not contain every detail if the writings collectively supply the required memorandum.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because multiple writings can satisfy the memorandum requirement. B: B is wrong because unsigned writings may be read with a signed writing when sufficiently connected. C: C is wrong because a signed writing sent to the buyer can satisfy the writing requirement against the seller.
