Question: Final judgment is entered on May 1 in a federal civil case. On May 20, the losing party files a timely Rule 59(e) motion to alter or amend the judgment. The court denies the motion on June 10. The winning party argues the appeal deadline ran from May 1 and expired while the Rule 59 motion was pending. What is the best response?
Answer A: The timely Rule 59(e) motion tolls the appeal clock, which runs from disposition of the motion
Answer B: Post-judgment motions never affect appeal time
Answer C: The motion eliminates the right to appeal
Answer D: The appeal deadline runs from the date the complaint was filed
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Timely specified post-judgment motions restart the notice-of-appeal period after they are resolved.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Some timely motions do toll the clock. C: A tolling motion preserves rather than destroys appeal timing. D: The clock is keyed to judgment and qualifying post-judgment motions.
lb_rule_reference: Timely specified post-judgment motions affect notice-of-appeal deadlines.
