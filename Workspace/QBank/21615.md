Question: Abraham contracted with a builder to construct a small apartment for Abraham's mother, Sarah, on Abraham's land. Abraham promised to provide access to the land and pay progress amounts as required. After the builder purchased materials, Abraham locked the gate and refused to allow work to begin. Sarah then sued the builder, claiming she was the intended beneficiary of the apartment promise. What is the builder's strongest defense?
Answer A: Sarah cannot ever sue because intended beneficiaries have no contract rights.
Answer B: The builder may assert Abraham's material breach because Sarah's rights are subject to defenses arising from Abraham's contract.
Answer C: The builder is liable to Sarah because a third-party beneficiary's rights are always independent of the promisee's performance.
Answer D: The builder is discharged only if Sarah personally locked the gate.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An intended beneficiary's rights are generally subject to defenses the promisor has against the promisee under the contract. Abraham's refusal to provide access materially prevented the builder's performance. The builder can assert that derivative defense against Sarah's claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because intended beneficiaries can sometimes enforce contracts. C: C is wrong because the beneficiary does not receive rights free of contract defenses between promisor and promisee. D: D is wrong because the relevant breach is Abraham's, not Sarah's personal conduct.
lb_rule_reference: Third-party beneficiary derivative defense / constructive condition
