Question: In a federal battery trial, Aaron asks Ruth, “Did you see Moses enter the room?” Ruth answers, “Yes, and I heard someone in the hallway say Moses had a knife.” Moses moves to strike Ruth's entire answer. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Grant the motion because any answer containing hearsay must be completely removed.
Answer B: Deny the motion because hearsay can never be stricken after it is heard.
Answer C: Strike only the volunteered hallway statement if it is inadmissible, while allowing the responsive observation that Ruth saw Moses enter.
Answer D: Deny the motion because Ruth is the only witness to enter the room.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A motion to strike should be targeted. Ruth's statement that she saw Moses enter answered the question and is based on personal knowledge. The added hallway statement is the portion that may be stricken if inadmissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the responsive portion need not be removed. B: B is wrong because inadmissible hearsay can be struck by timely motion. D: D is wrong because witness uniqueness does not answer the motion-to-strike issue.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(1); FRE 611(a) / overbroad motion to strike
