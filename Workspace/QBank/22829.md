


BARMATRIX Q#: 22829
Outline_code: 94050100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Venue
Question: A woman, a resident of State A, was hit by a car driven by a man, a resident of State B. The accident occurred in State A, very close to the border of State C. The woman sued the man in federal district court in State B based on diversity of citizenship. All of the witnesses to the accident lived in State C, where the woman was hospitalized and treated for her injuries. Because State B is very large, it was difficult for the man and the witnesses to appear before the court in State B. Consequently, the man sought to have the case transferred to the State C federal court. Will the court transfer the woman's claim to the State C federal court?
Answer A: A: Yes, because it was difficult for the man and witnesses to appear before the court in State B.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the woman was hospitalized and treated for her injuries in State C.
Answer C: C: No, because the man did not reside in State C and no events giving rise to the claim occurred there.
Answer D: D: No, because the woman chose to bring her suit in State B.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under 28 U.S.C. §1391, venue is proper in (a) the judicial district where any defendant resides, so long as all defendants are residents of the state in which the district is located; (b) a judicial district where a substantial part of the events giving rise to the claim occurred; or, (c) if no district in the United States satisfies either (a) or (b), a judicial district where any defendant is subject to the court's personal jurisdiction in regard to such action. Here, venue would be proper in State B, where the man resided, or State A, where the accident occurred. Importantly, 28 U.S.C. §1404(a) states that “a district court may transfer any civil action to any other district or division where it might have been brought or to any district or division to which all parties have consented.” Because State C is not a proper venue under 28 U.S.C. §1391, it would be improper for the court to transfer the case there. Therefore, A and B are incorrect. D is incorrect because venue is not wholly dependent on where the plaintiff chooses to bring suit.
