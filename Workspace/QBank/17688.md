Outline: Roles of judge and jury
Question: In a federal arson trial, the prosecutor offers a surveillance video from a warehouse. Naomi, the warehouse manager, testifies that the camera records the loading dock and that she recognizes Jacob's walk and jacket in the video. Jacob objects that the image is blurry and that the jury could not reliably identify him. How should the court rule on the objection?
Answer A: Exclude the video because the judge must personally find that the person shown is Jacob before the jury may see it.
Answer B: Admit the video if there is sufficient evidence for a reasonable jury to find that it shows Jacob, leaving the identification weight to the jury.
Answer C: Admit the video only if Jacob admits that he is the person shown.
Answer D: Exclude the video because identity questions are always for the jury and therefore cannot be screened by the judge.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: For conditional relevance and authentication-style issues, the judge screens for enough evidence to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims. Naomi's testimony supplies a threshold basis. The jury then decides whether the blurry image actually identifies Jacob and what weight to give it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the judge need not personally decide the ultimate identity question as a merits fact. C: C is wrong because authentication does not require an admission by the opposing party. D: D is wrong because the judge still performs a threshold screening role before the jury weighs the evidence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / judge screens, jury weighs
