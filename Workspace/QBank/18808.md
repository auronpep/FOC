Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: In a robbery trial, Samuel testifies that he saw Rebecca driving the getaway car at night from across the street. Rebecca offers evidence that Samuel had forgotten his prescription glasses that evening. The prosecutor objects that Samuel's eyesight is collateral and does not prove who drove the car. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence, because only facts about Rebecca can be relevant.
Answer B: Admit the evidence, because Samuel's ability to perceive the driver has some tendency to affect the weight of his identification testimony.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence unless Samuel admits he could not see the driver.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if Rebecca calls an eye doctor.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Evidence affecting a witness's perception can be relevant because credibility and reliability of testimony matter to consequential facts. The evidence need not prove the driver's identity directly.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevance can include facts bearing on a witness's ability to observe. C: C is wrong because the witness need not concede the defect for the evidence to be relevant. D: D is wrong because expert testimony is not always required to show a basic perception issue.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is thinking relevance must point directly to the party rather than to the reliability of proof.
