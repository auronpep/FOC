Question: David conveyed Cedaracre “to Solomon and his heirs, but if Cedaracre is used for retail sales during Solomon's life, then to Nathan and his heirs.” Solomon later conveyed Cedaracre to Samuel by valid deed. During Solomon's life, Samuel opened a retail store on Cedaracre. Who owns Cedaracre after Samuel opens the store?
Answer A: Nathan, because Samuel took Solomon's estate subject to Nathan's executory interest.
Answer B: Samuel, because Solomon's deed to Samuel did not repeat the retail-sales condition.
Answer C: David, because a breach of a condition always returns title to the grantor.
Answer D: Solomon, because Samuel's violation voided Solomon's deed to Samuel.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Nathan owns Cedaracre. Solomon had a fee simple subject to executory limitation and could transfer that defeasible estate to Samuel. Samuel took subject to Nathan's shifting executory interest. When Samuel used the land for retail sales during Solomon's life, the estate automatically shifted to Nathan.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a later deed cannot eliminate an existing executory limitation by omitting it. C: C is wrong because the future interest was given to Nathan, not retained by David. D: D is wrong because the violation does not restore title to Solomon; it triggers the gift-over to Nathan.
lb_rule_reference: Transfer of defeasible fee / automatic shift to third party
