Question: In a federal premises-liability trial, Elijah testifies that he saw Hannah walk toward the north stairway before she fell. Hannah's lawyer asks Elijah, “Why did Hannah choose the north stairway instead of the elevator?” Elijah has not spoken with Hannah about her reason. The defendant objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because Elijah saw Hannah walk toward the stairway.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because a witness may always explain another person's motive.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because Hannah's reason is irrelevant in every premises case.
Answer D: Sustain the objection because the question calls for speculation about Hannah's reason.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Elijah may testify to what he observed, but without a basis for knowing Hannah's reason, the question asks him to speculate about her motive.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because observing conduct does not establish knowledge of internal motive. B: B is wrong because witnesses cannot simply infer another person's reasons without a foundation. C is overbroad; Hannah's reason could be relevant in some cases, but this witness lacks a basis.
