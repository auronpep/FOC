Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Joshua signed a deed conveying Ridgeacre to Hannah and placed it with Ezra, an independent escrow holder. Joshua instructed Ezra to deliver the deed to Hannah only if Hannah paid the full purchase price by Friday, and Joshua retained no right to retrieve the deed. On Thursday, before paying, Hannah demanded the deed and claimed that title had already passed because Joshua had surrendered control to Ezra. Is Hannah correct?
Answer A: Yes, because surrendering control to any third party immediately transfers title to the grantee.
Answer B: Yes, because the deed was signed and Hannah was named as grantee.
Answer C: No, because the deed was placed in escrow and title would not pass to Hannah until the payment condition was satisfied.
Answer D: No, but only because Hannah had not yet recorded the deed.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is conditional escrow. Joshua surrendered control to an independent escrow holder, but the delivery instruction made Hannah's payment a condition precedent to delivery to her. Before that condition was satisfied, title had not passed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because escrow delivery can delay title until the condition occurs. B: B is wrong because signature and named-grantee status do not replace delivery. D: D is wrong because the problem is unsatisfied delivery condition, not recording.
lb_rule_reference: Escrow delivery does not pass title before the escrow condition is satisfied
