Question: Abigail and Benjamin are both merchants who deal in lamps. They orally agree that Benjamin will buy 500 lamps from Abigail. The same day, Abigail sends Benjamin a signed email confirming “500 lamps at $8 each.” Benjamin receives the email, has reason to know its contents, and sends no objection. Three weeks later, Benjamin refuses to buy. What is the best UCC Statute of Frauds analysis?
Answer A: The email cannot satisfy the Statute of Frauds against Benjamin because Benjamin did not sign it.
Answer B: The email binds only Abigail because she signed it.
Answer C: The email may satisfy the Statute of Frauds against Benjamin because it was a sufficient merchant confirmation and Benjamin failed to object in writing within 10 days.
Answer D: The email is ineffective because emails can never be writings under the UCC.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Between merchants, a signed confirmation sufficient against the sender can satisfy the SOF against the recipient if the recipient has reason to know its contents and does not give written notice of objection within 10 days after receipt. Benjamin's silence beyond that period allows the confirmation route.
