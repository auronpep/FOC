Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A company staged a large fireworks show in a small downtown plaza under a valid city permit. The crew used careful launch procedures. A shell burst lower than expected, and sparks burned a spectator standing in the designated viewing area. If the spectator sues for strict liability, which answer is best?
Answer A: The company is strictly liable because the burn resulted from the explosive spark risk that made the display abnormal in that setting
Answer B: The company is not liable because it had a valid permit
Answer C: The company is not liable because the spectator stood in the designated viewing area
Answer D: The company is strictly liable only if the shell was negligently loaded
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The permit and careful procedures do not remove strict liability for harm within the fireworks risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A permit may authorize the display, but it does not bar strict liability for resulting tort harm. C: The facts do not show assumption of a hidden or forbidden risk; the burn came from the display itself. D: Negligent loading is unnecessary when strict liability for an abnormally dangerous display applies.
lb_rule_reference: A fireworks display may be abnormally dangerous in context, and strict liability covers burn injuries caused by sparks or explosions from the display.
