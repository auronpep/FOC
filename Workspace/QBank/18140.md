Question: Congress passes an appropriations bill that funds several named infrastructure projects. The President signs the bill, then issues a statement canceling one project because the President believes it is wasteful. The statement directs the Treasury not to release the funds for that project. The sponsor of the project sues. Which constitutional principle is most directly implicated?
Answer A: The President may cancel any spending item after signing a bill because spending is an executive function
Answer B: The cancellation is constitutionally suspect because the President is attempting to change enacted law outside bicameralism and presentment
Answer C: The cancellation is valid because the President could have vetoed the whole bill
Answer D: The cancellation is valid only if the project is located in one state
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Once the President signs the bill, the statute has legal effect as enacted. Canceling a discrete statutory item is a change in law that must follow the constitutional process.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Administration of spending does not include unilateral power to amend or repeal part of a statute after signing it. C: The existence of a whole-bill veto does not create a post-signing item veto. D: The defect is separation of powers and presentment, not the project's location.
lb_rule_reference: After signing a bill, the President generally may not unilaterally cancel or amend parts of the statute; changing enacted law requires the constitutionally prescribed legislative process.
