MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jury Trials
Outline: Jury Instructions
Question: A man sued a corporation in federal court for negligence. During trial, the corporation submitted a proposed jury instruction on contributory negligence. Before instructing the jury, the judge told the parties of the instructions she would give. The instructions did not include any instruction on contributory negligence. Neither party objected and the judge gave the jury her instructions. The jury ruled in the man's favor, and the judge entered judgment on the verdict. The corporation wanted to appeal the verdict on the ground that the court should have used its contributory negligence instruction. May the corporation appeal the verdict?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the corporation submitted a proposed instruction on contributory negligence.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the judge did not give any instruction on contributory negligence.
Answer C: C: No, because the corporation did not object after the judge told the parties of the instructions she intended to give.
Answer D: D: No, because the corporation did not object before the jury reached a verdict.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 51(c)(2)(A), a party who objects to a jury instruction must do so before the challenged instruction is given to the jury. Since the corporation did not do so, it cannot now challenge the instruction on appeal. Therefore, A, B, and D are incorrect.
