Question: A restaurant signed a contract with a supplier for four espresso machines, installation, and a short training session. Most of the price was allocated to the machines, and the signed contract required delivery to the restaurant's downtown location. It also stated that modifications had to be in a signed writing. After signing, the restaurant orally asked the supplier to deliver and install the machines at a new uptown location instead. The supplier rerouted the shipment, installed the machines uptown, and the restaurant used them for a month. In a separate later phone call, the supplier also said it would provide six months of free menu consulting, but the supplier never performed the consulting and the restaurant did not materially rely on that promise. Which later understanding can most plausibly affect the parties' obligations despite the signed no-oral-modification clause?
Answer A: Both the uptown delivery/install change and the free menu-consulting promise.
Answer B: The uptown delivery/install change only.
Answer C: The free menu-consulting promise only.
Answer D: Neither later understanding.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that later understandings must be split by source law, performance, and reliance. The predominant transaction is a goods contract with incidental installation/training, so Article 2 governs the delivery/install change. Although the signed no-oral-modification clause matters, the restaurant's post-signing request, the supplier's rerouting and installation, and the restaurant's month-long use can support waiver. The free consulting promise is different: it was not signed, performed, accepted, or materially relied upon. The decisive lock is split routing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it treats the performed delivery/install change and the unperformed consulting promise as identical. C: C is wrong because the consulting promise has no signed writing, performance, or reliance support. D: D is wrong because the performed and accepted uptown delivery/install change can plausibly operate as waiver despite the NOM clause.
lb_rule_reference: Predominant goods source law / UCC 2-209 split waiver
