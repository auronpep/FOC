Outline: Scope
Question: A recorded easement allowed Elizabeth, the owner of Upper Lot, to use a wooden stairway on Jacob's Lower Lot to reach the public sidewalk. Several boards rotted, making the stairway unsafe. Elizabeth gave Jacob reasonable notice and offered to replace only the rotten boards at her own expense. The work would not widen or relocate the stairway. May Jacob prevent Elizabeth from entering Lower Lot to make the repair?
Answer A: Yes, because an easement holder may pass over the servient land but may never repair the easement area.
Answer B: Yes, because repair rights must always be separately stated in the deed.
Answer C: No, because an easement gives Elizabeth full control over all of Lower Lot.
Answer D: No, because the easement holder may make reasonable repairs necessary to enjoy the easement without materially increasing the servient burden.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The holder of an easement may ordinarily do what is reasonably necessary to maintain and enjoy the easement, provided the work stays within the easement's scope and does not unreasonably burden the servient estate. Replacing rotten boards after notice at the holder's expense is a reasonable maintenance use.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because passage rights often include reasonable maintenance needed for passage. B: B is wrong because reasonable maintenance may be implied from the easement's use. C: C is wrong because an easement is a limited use right, not ownership of the servient estate. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Easement maintenance / reasonable repair
lb_student_diagnostic: The recurring trap is treating an easement as either bare passage only or full servient-land control.
