Question: Leo planted a bomb in a commercial airliner to destroy the aircraft and kill a specific passenger, Victor, whom he hated. The plane carried 200 other passengers and crew. Leo was indifferent to their fates — he did not want them to die, but he did not care if they did. The bomb exploded and killed all 201 people aboard. The prosecution charges Leo with 201 counts of murder under a statute that imposes murder liability for killing with either purpose or knowledge. Under the Model Penal Code, what is Leo's mental state with respect to the deaths of the 200 passengers and crew other than Victor?
Answer A: Purpose, because Leo planted the bomb knowing that all passengers would die
Answer B: Knowledge, because Leo was aware that detonating the bomb was practically certain to kill all passengers and crew
Answer C: Recklessness, because Leo consciously disregarded the risk of killing the other passengers
Answer D: Negligence, because Leo should have considered the impact of his actions on innocent passengers
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: MPC knowledge, as applied to results, means the actor is aware that his conduct is practically certain to cause the result. Leo knew with certainty that a bomb explosion on a fully occupied aircraft would kill everyone aboard. He did not desire their deaths (that would be purpose), but he was fully aware of the near-certain outcome. Under the MPC, this is knowledge. Critically, the MPC treats knowledge as equivalent to purpose for most murder statutes, so Leo is liable for the murder of all 201 victims — 1 with purpose and 200 with knowledge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: MPC purpose requires that the defendant's conscious object was to cause the result. Leo did not want the 200 others to die — his goal was Victor's death. Wanting a result and knowing it will happen are distinct mental states under the MPC. A defendant who is indifferent to a result he knows will occur acts with knowledge, not purpose. C: Recklessness applies when the result is a risk, not a certainty. MPC recklessness involves conscious disregard of a substantial and unjustifiable risk that a result might occur. Here, Leo knew with practical certainty that the bomb would kill everyone on the plane — the result was not a risk but a virtual certainty. Where the defendant knows the result is practically certain, the MPC characterizes that as knowledge, not recklessness. Recklessness is reserved for cases where the defendant gambles on a risk that might or might not materialize. D: MPC negligence is an objective standard — the defendant failed to be aware of a risk that a reasonable person would have perceived. Negligence applies when the defendant lacked subjective awareness of the risk. Leo subjectively knew — with certainty — that all passengers would die. An actor who is actually, subjectively aware of a practical certainty cannot be characterized as merely negligent. Negligence is the lowest MPC culpability tier and is reserved for actors who are oblivious, not those who have actual knowledge.
lb_rule_reference: Under the MPC, purpose means the defendant's conscious object was to cause the result; knowledge means the defendant was aware that his conduct was practically certain to cause the result, even without desiring it
