Subtopic: Remedies
Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Sarah contracted with Daniel, the operator of a seasonal market, for booth rights and required permits for Sarah's first food kiosk. Daniel repudiated before the market opened. Sarah cannot prove projected profits with reasonable certainty because the kiosk had no reliable sales history. After the contract was made, Sarah reasonably spent $14,000 on equipment rentals, $6,000 on launch advertising, and $5,000 on reusable signs. The signs remain usable in a later kiosk venture and have $2,000 in remaining value. What may Sarah recover as reliance damages?
Answer A: $23,000.
Answer B: $25,000.
Answer C: Nothing, because the projected profits are speculative.
Answer D: $60,000, Sarah's hoped-for profits from the first season.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is reliance under common law after repudiation. Because Sarah's expectation profits are too speculative, the formula is reasonable expenditures made in reliance minus value retained: $14,000 equipment rentals + $6,000 advertising + $5,000 signs − $2,000 remaining sign value = $23,000. The limiting gates are certainty and offset, and the result scope is limited net reliance. The answer does not decide expectation profits or restitution because the record does not prove profits and Daniel did not receive the expenditures as a benefit.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it omits the $2,000 retained-value offset, creating overcompensation under WAL-REM-008. C: C is wrong because speculative expectation does not bar reliance damages for proven expenditures. D: D is wrong because it awards speculative expectation profits, a WAL-REM-029 reliance/speculation confusion.
lb_rule_reference: Common law reliance damages — reasonable expenditures minus value retained when expectation is too speculative
