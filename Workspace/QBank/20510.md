Question: David agreed in a signed contract to sell Miriam “Grade A dates” for resale. The contract contained a merger clause. In the regional date trade, “Grade A” regularly means dates packed in moisture-proof crates. David delivered the dates in ordinary sacks. Miriam offers trade-usage evidence about the crate meaning. Should the evidence be admitted? Should the evidence be admitted?
Answer A: No, because a merger clause bars all trade-usage evidence.
Answer B: Yes, because under Article 2, usage of trade may explain or supplement a final writing if reasonably consistent with its terms.
Answer C: No, because trade usage is admissible only when the writing has no merger clause and no quality term.
Answer D: Yes, but only to prove damages, not to interpret the written term.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC 2-202 and 1-303, usage of trade may explain or supplement final written terms if reasonably consistent. The evidence gives commercial meaning to “Grade A” and does not necessarily contradict the writing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because merger clauses do not automatically bar UCC course or usage evidence. C: C is wrong because usage can explain terms that exist in the writing. D: D is wrong because the primary purpose is interpretation of the contract term.
