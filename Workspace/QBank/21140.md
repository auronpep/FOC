Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Presumptions and inferences
Question: In a federal civil action, Hannah claims that Samuel received a notice terminating a lease. Hannah proves that the notice was properly mailed. Samuel testifies that he never received it. Hannah asks the judge to instruct the jury: “If you find the notice was properly mailed, you must find that Samuel received it unless Samuel persuades you otherwise.” How should the court rule?
Answer A: Give the instruction because Samuel's denial puts his credibility at issue.
Answer B: Give the instruction because the presumption is about a writing and the jury should receive the whole notice file.
Answer C: Refuse the instruction because it improperly shifts the burden of persuasion and makes the inference mandatory.
Answer D: Refuse the instruction only if Samuel claims a privilege not to testify about receipt.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A federal civil presumption may shift the burden of production, but it does not shift the burden of persuasion. The proposed instruction makes receipt mandatory unless Samuel persuades the jury otherwise, so it overstates the presumption's effect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because credibility does not turn a production presumption into a persuasion-shifting instruction. B: B is wrong because completeness of the notice file is not the issue. D: D is wrong because no privilege is needed to object to the burden-shifting instruction.
lb_rule_reference: Mandatory presumption / civil FRE 301
