Outline: Duty to mitigate
Question: Rachel agreed to buy 1,000 microcontrollers from Samuel for $20 each, with delivery due May 1. Samuel repudiated on April 10. At the time and place Rachel learned of the breach, equivalent microcontrollers were available in time for $24 each. Rachel spent $500 on a reasonable search and inspection. Instead of buying the equivalent microcontrollers, Rachel bought inferior chips for $21 each, paid $2,000 to rework them, and lost $30,000 in resale profit when the inferior chips failed. What is Rachel's maximum Article 2 recovery from Samuel?
Answer A: Nothing, because Rachel failed to cover reasonably.
Answer B: $33,500, consisting of the inferior-chip premium, rework cost, lost resale profit, and search expense.
Answer C: $4,500, the market differential plus reasonable search and inspection expense.
Answer D: $3,500, Rachel's actual inferior-chip premium, rework cost, and search expense.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under UCC goods law, with Rachel as buyer and Samuel as seller. The breach posture is seller repudiation. Because equivalent goods were available when Rachel learned of the breach, the Article 2 market measure supplies the baseline: 1,000 × ($24 market price − $20 contract price) = $4,000, plus $500 in reasonable incidental search and inspection expense, for $4,500. The $30,000 resale profit was reasonably preventable by obtaining equivalent goods, and the inferior-chip rework costs resulted from an unreasonable substitute choice. The limiting gates are mitigation, causation, and offset, and the result scope is limited.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because failure to cover reasonably does not eliminate all Article 2 damages, a WAL-REM-009 error. B: B is wrong because it stacks preventable consequential profit and unreasonable rework costs with the proper measure, triggering WAL-REM-008 and WAL-REM-003. D: D is wrong because it uses Rachel's unreasonable inferior-cover costs as the formula baseline instead of the market differential for equivalent goods.
lb_rule_reference: UCC buyer market damages and mitigation — market differential plus incidentals, excluding preventable consequential loss
