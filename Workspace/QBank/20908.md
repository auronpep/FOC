Question: A negligent cyclist struck a pedestrian. The pedestrian's longtime roommate and close friend watched the collision from a bench, was not personally endangered, and suffered severe distress. The jurisdiction limits bystander NIED to close family members who contemporaneously observe serious injury. Is the friend likely to recover for bystander NIED?
Answer A: Yes, because the friend saw the injury happen
Answer B: Yes, because genuine distress is enough
Answer C: No, because the friend is not within the close-family class required by the rule
Answer D: No, because emotional distress can never be recovered by bystanders
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The friend witnessed the injury but lacks the relationship status the jurisdiction requires for bystander NIED.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The stated rule also requires a close family relationship. B: Genuine distress does not erase the jurisdiction's close-family limit. D: Some bystander claims are recognized; this one fails because the relationship limit is not met.
lb_rule_reference: Bystander NIED limits usually exclude unrelated friends even if they witness serious injury and suffer genuine distress.
