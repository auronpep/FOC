Outline: Rule of completeness
Question: In Moses's trial for arson, the prosecutor plays a ten-second excerpt from Moses's recorded interview: “I bought gasoline that night.” The next sentence on the recording is, “for my generator after the power failed.” Moses immediately asks that the next sentence be played before the prosecutor continues. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the request because Moses can play the rest of the recording only during the defense case.
Answer B: Grant the request if the next sentence is needed to prevent the excerpt from misleading the jury.
Answer C: Deny the request because the jury can infer from cross-examination that Moses had an innocent explanation.
Answer D: Grant the request only if the prosecutor first agrees that the second sentence is true.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 106 permits the adverse party to require introduction of a completing part at the time the initial part is introduced when fairness requires same-time context. The next sentence directly explains the purpose for buying gasoline and may be necessary to prevent the excerpt from misleading the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because waiting until the defense case may not adequately correct a misleading impression created immediately. C: C is wrong because later cross-examination is not always an adequate substitute for contemporaneous completion. D: D is wrong because the proponent of the initial excerpt need not agree that the completing statement is true.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 106 / timing of completion
