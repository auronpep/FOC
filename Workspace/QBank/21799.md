Question: Paul signed a deed conveying Harboracre to Timothy. Timothy was out of town, so Paul handed the deed to John and said, “Timothy owns Harboracre now. Hold this deed for him until he returns tonight, and do not return it to me.” John agreed. Timothy accepted the deed that evening. Was the deed delivered?
Answer A: No, because a deed held by a third party can never be delivered.
Answer B: Yes, because Paul expressed present intent to pass title and placed the deed beyond his control for Timothy's benefit.
Answer C: No, because Timothy was not physically present when Paul handed over the deed.
Answer D: Yes, but only from the moment Timothy later recorded the deed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is present intent plus surrender of control. Paul said Timothy owned the land now, gave the deed to John for Timothy, and barred return to Paul. Holding the deed for the grantee's convenience did not defeat delivery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because third-party delivery can be effective. C: C is wrong because the grantee's physical presence at handoff is not always required. D: D is wrong because recording is not the event that creates delivery between these parties.
lb_rule_reference: A third party holding for the grantee's convenience can receive effective delivery when the grantor gives up control
