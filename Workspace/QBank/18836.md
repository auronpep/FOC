Outline: Lay opinion testimony
Question: In a federal prosecution for reckless driving, Ruth testifies that she saw Peter stumble out of a tavern, smelled alcohol on his breath, and heard his slurred speech. The prosecutor asks Ruth whether Peter appeared drunk. Peter objects that Ruth is not an expert. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because intoxication can be described only by an expert.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because lay witnesses may never state opinions.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if Ruth administered a chemical test.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because the opinion is rationally based on Ruth's perception and helpful to understanding her testimony.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A lay witness may give an opinion rationally based on perception and helpful to the factfinder. A firsthand observation that someone appeared drunk is a common lay shorthand opinion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because expert testimony is not required for ordinary observed intoxication. B: B is wrong because lay opinions are permitted when Rule 701 is satisfied. C: C is wrong because a chemical test is not required for this lay observation.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 701 / perception-based lay opinion
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests lay-opinion channel versus expert-only false positive.
