Question: In a federal robbery trial, witnesses say the robber wore a bright purple cloak. The prosecutor offers evidence that Ezra was stopped fifteen minutes later three blocks away wearing a bright purple cloak. Ezra objects that other people could own purple cloaks. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence because the cloak does not conclusively identify Ezra as the robber.
Answer B: Admit the evidence because the distinctive cloak and timing have some tendency to identify Ezra; other possible owners affect weight.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence unless the cloak is admitted as a physical exhibit.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if no other purple cloak existed in the city.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The distinctive cloak, timing, and location give the evidence probative value on identity. The possibility that someone else owned a purple cloak weakens but does not eliminate relevance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because identity evidence need not be conclusive. C: C is wrong because testimony about the cloak can be relevant even if the cloak itself is not offered. D: D is wrong because uniqueness is not required for threshold relevance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401; FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Student requires absolute uniqueness rather than recognizing partial identification value.
