Question: A prisoner loses access to the recreation room for one afternoon after arguing with a guard. The sanction does not affect good-time credits, parole eligibility, sentence length, or placement conditions beyond that ordinary restriction. The prisoner claims a full due process hearing was constitutionally required. What is the state's strongest response?
Answer A: Every prison discipline decision requires the same process as a criminal trial
Answer B: The restriction likely does not create a liberty interest because it is ordinary and does not affect sentence duration
Answer C: Due process applies because any loss of comfort in prison is a liberty deprivation
Answer D: Due process never applies inside prisons
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A brief ordinary recreation restriction is not the kind of atypical significant hardship that triggers due process protections.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Prison context uses a protected-liberty-interest gate, not full criminal trial procedure for every sanction. C: Ordinary incidents of prison life do not automatically create a protected liberty interest. D: Prisoners can have due process claims when discipline affects protected liberty interests.
lb_rule_reference: Prison discipline implicates a procedural due process liberty interest when it imposes an atypical and significant hardship compared with ordinary prison life or affects sentence duration.
