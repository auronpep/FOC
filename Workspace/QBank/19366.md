Question: A seller signs and sends Esther a note stating, “I agree to sell Esther 500 wool blankets for delivery next month.” The note does not state the price. Esther accepts, but the seller later refuses to deliver and asserts the Statute of Frauds because the price is missing. Is the signed note sufficient under the UCC Statute of Frauds?
Answer A: Yes, because a writing can be sufficient even if it omits price, so long as it indicates a contract and states the quantity.
Answer B: Yes, but only if Esther proves the parties later agreed on a price in a signed writing.
Answer C: No, because a goods contract writing must state every essential term, including price.
Answer D: No, because the note was signed only by the seller and not by Esther.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: For a UCC sale of goods of $500 or more, the writing must indicate a contract for sale, be signed by the party to be charged, and state a quantity. A missing price does not make the memorandum insufficient; the UCC can supply a reasonable price if the parties intended to contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the missing price does not require a separate signed price writing. C: C is wrong because UCC §2-201 does not require every term. D: D is wrong because the seller is the party against whom enforcement is sought and signed the writing.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 — goods SOF and open price
