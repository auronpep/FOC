Outline: Taxing and spending power
Question: Congress offers states federal funds to repair interstate bridges. The statute conditions the funds on each state changing its public-school history curriculum. The curriculum condition is clearly stated and not independently unconstitutional. What is the strongest spending-power objection?
Answer A: The condition may be invalid because it is unrelated to the federal interest in bridge repair funding
Answer B: The condition is valid because any clearly stated funding condition is constitutional
Answer C: The condition is invalid only if the state proves Congress acted with bad motive
Answer D: The condition is valid because education is an area of state police power
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Spending conditions must be germane to the federal interest in the funded program.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Clarity is necessary but not sufficient; relatedness also matters. C: The relatedness problem exists even without proving subjective bad motive. D: State police power does not make an unrelated federal funding condition valid.
lb_rule_reference: A condition on federal funds must be related to the federal interest in the particular national project or program.
