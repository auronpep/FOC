Question: Nora is charged by information with armed burglary. At a preliminary hearing, the prosecutor presents witnesses and the magistrate determines whether probable cause supports holding Nora for trial. Nora asks for appointed counsel, but the court proceeds without appointing anyone. Important cross-examination opportunities are lost. Did the hearing implicate Nora's Sixth Amendment right to counsel?
Answer A: Yes, because formal proceedings had begun and the preliminary hearing was a critical stage
Answer B: No, because the Sixth Amendment applies only during the jury trial itself
Answer C: No, because a preliminary hearing concerns probable cause rather than guilt
Answer D: Yes, but only because Nora expressly asked for counsel at the hearing
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A formal information had initiated adversary proceedings. A preliminary hearing with witness testimony and probable-cause consequences is a critical stage at which counsel is required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The right extends to critical pretrial stages after attachment, including proceedings where counsel can preserve rights and test evidence. C: A stage can be critical even if it is not the final guilt decision. Witness testing and binding-over consequences matter. D: After attachment at a critical stage, the Sixth Amendment right does not depend solely on a request. The charged defendant is entitled to counsel.
lb_rule_reference: Once adversary proceedings have begun, the Sixth Amendment right to counsel applies at critical stages where counsel's assistance is needed to protect the accused's rights.
