Question: Two hunters simultaneously fired negligently toward a hiker. One pellet struck the hiker, but the pellet cannot be traced to either hunter. Both hunters are defendants, and the evidence shows no one else could have fired the pellet. What is the best causation rule?
Answer A: The hiker automatically loses because he cannot identify which hunter fired the pellet
Answer B: The burden may shift to the negligent hunters to prove they did not cause the injury
Answer C: Both hunters are strictly liable even if neither acted negligently
Answer D: The hiker must prove that both pellets struck him
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Both possible negligent tortfeasors are before the court, and one of them caused the harm.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Alternative liability addresses this proof problem when the required conditions are met. C: The doctrine depends on negligent actors, not strict liability for innocent conduct. D: The point is that only one pellet caused the indivisible injury.
lb_rule_reference: Alternative liability may shift the burden to negligent defendants when all possible tortfeasors are before the court and only one caused the indivisible injury.
