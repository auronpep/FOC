Question: Attorney Rachel emailed testifying expert Luke. One paragraph gave Rachel's mental impressions about trial themes. Another identified measurements Rachel provided and told Luke to assume the machine ran for six hours. The opponent seeks the email in discovery. Which ruling is most accurate?
Answer A: The entire email is discoverable because Luke will testify.
Answer B: The entire email is protected because all attorney-expert communications are immune.
Answer C: Communications revealing counsel's mental impressions are protected, but communications identifying facts, data, or assumptions counsel provided and the expert considered or relied on may be discoverable.
Answer D: The email is discoverable only if Rachel also sent it to the client.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 26(b)(4)(C) generally protects attorney communications with a report-writing testifying expert, but not communications about compensation, facts or data supplied and considered, or assumptions supplied and relied on. The decisive axis is protected attorney theory versus discoverable expert inputs.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This overopens expert discovery and ignores the protection for attorney-expert communications. B: B is wrong because This overprotects the email and ignores the facts/data/assumptions exceptions. D: A is wrong because This overopens expert discovery and ignores the protection for attorney-expert communications. B is wrong because This overprotects the email and ignores the facts/data/assumptions exceptions. C is wrong because Correct answer D is wrong because Client transmission is irrelevant to the expert-communication discovery rule.
