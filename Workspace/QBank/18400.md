Question: Leon approaches Victor outside a bank and says: "Give me your wallet, or I'll track down your brother and hurt him next month." Victor, frightened, hands over his wallet. Leon takes it and walks away. Is Leon guilty of robbery?
Answer A: Yes, because Leon used intimidation to obtain Victor's wallet
Answer B: Yes, because the threat was directed at a family member, which courts treat as equivalent to a direct threat
Answer C: No, because the threat was of future harm, not immediate bodily harm, so the intimidation element of robbery is not satisfied
Answer D: No, because Victor voluntarily handed over the wallet, negating the "taking" element of robbery
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Common-law robbery requires a trespassory taking from the person or presence of another by force or intimidation. The intimidation element demands an immediate threat of bodily harm — one that puts the victim in fear of imminent physical injury. Leon threatened harm to occur "next month," which is neither imminent nor immediate. This kind of threat may support a charge of extortion or blackmail (obtaining property by threats of future harm), but it does not satisfy robbery's requirement of immediate intimidation. Leon is guilty of extortion/blackmail and larceny, not robbery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Robbery requires intimidation by an immediate threat of bodily harm — typically to the victim or a close family member present at the scene. Leon's threat was both temporally removed (future harm) and directed at someone not present. Common-law robbery requires the threat to create fear of immediate, imminent harm. A threat to cause harm "next month" does not create the immediate apprehension of bodily harm required. B: Even setting aside the third-party-victim issue, the threat here was of future harm ("next month"), not immediate harm. The immediacy requirement is independently fatal to a robbery charge. Moreover, the extension to family-member threats typically applies only when the family member is present at the scene or when the threat creates immediate fear of present harm to the victim. D: Voluntary surrender under intimidation still constitutes a "taking" for robbery purposes when the intimidation is sufficient — the coerced surrender is not truly voluntary. The problem with robbery here is not the voluntariness of Victor's surrender, but rather that the threat did not meet the immediacy requirement. Coerced surrender under a sufficient immediate threat does satisfy the taking element of robbery.
lb_rule_reference: Robbery requires that force or intimidation consist of an immediate threat of bodily harm. A threat to cause harm at some future time, or to harm someone other than the victim, does not satisfy the intimidation element of robbery.
