MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A company was fumigating a warehouse with a toxic gas. While walking around the outside of the warehouse before any gas was released, a delivery driver slipped on an ordinary wet ladder left near the loading dock and broke his wrist. The driver sues the company for strict liability based on the fumigation. Is the company strictly liable?
Answer A: Yes, because fumigation with toxic gas is abnormally dangerous
Answer B: Yes, because the injury happened at the fumigation site
Answer C: No, because the injury did not result from exposure to, escape of, or other special risk of the fumigation
Answer D: No, because strict liability never applies before an activity begins
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The fall was unrelated to the danger that makes fumigation abnormal.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Even if the activity is abnormally dangerous, the injury must result from the abnormal risk. B: The site connection is not enough; the wet ladder was an ordinary premises risk. D: Timing is not the decisive rule; the decisive point is that the harm was outside the abnormal risk.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity does not extend to injuries unrelated to the special danger, such as an ordinary slip or fall.
