Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Isaac was unable to write because of an injury. In Isaac's presence, and at Isaac's express direction, Ezra wrote Isaac's name at the end of a deed conveying Isaac's field to Rebecca. Isaac then said, “That is my signature for this deed,” delivered the deed to Rebecca, and Rebecca accepted. Ezra had no separate written power of attorney. Who owns the field?
Answer A: Isaac, because only Isaac's own handwriting could sign the deed.
Answer B: Isaac, because Ezra lacked a separate written power of attorney.
Answer C: Rebecca, because Ezra acted as Isaac's hand in Isaac's presence and at Isaac's direction, satisfying the grantor-signature gate.
Answer D: Rebecca only if Ezra also signed as a witness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantor authentication. A signature may be made by another as the grantor's hand when the grantor is present, directs the act, and adopts the signature. This is not a separate agency conveyance requiring Ezra to exercise independent authority; Isaac adopted the signature and delivered the deed, so title passes to Rebecca.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the grantor's own handwriting is not required when the signature is made and adopted at the grantor's direction. B: B is wrong because the scribe did not act under independent land-conveyance authority; he wrote Isaac's name as Isaac's directed hand. D: D is wrong because a witness signature is not required for baseline deed validity on these facts.
