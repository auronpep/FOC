Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Exclusionary Rule
Question: Officers unlawfully entered a warehouse and saw stolen medical equipment. They left without touching anything. A different detective, unaware of the entry, then submitted an affidavit based only on shipping records, a cooperating witness, and surveillance video. A magistrate issued a warrant, and officers seized the same equipment. The defendant moves to suppress. Should the equipment be suppressed?
Answer A: Yes, because once officers saw the equipment unlawfully, the evidence was permanently tainted
Answer B: Yes, because independent source applies only to witness testimony, not physical evidence
Answer C: No, because the warrant affidavit was based on information independent of the unlawful entry
Answer D: No, because any later warrant automatically cures an earlier illegal search
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The later warrant rested solely on records, a witness, and surveillance unrelated to the unlawful entry. The equipment was obtained through an independent lawful source, so suppression is not required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Fruit analysis permits admission when the later seizure rests on a genuinely independent lawful source. B: The doctrine can apply to physical evidence when the lawful source is independent. D: A later warrant helps only if it is not based on the unlawful search and was not prompted by it in a tainted way.
lb_rule_reference: The independent-source doctrine admits evidence when police later obtain it through a lawful source independent of the constitutional violation.
