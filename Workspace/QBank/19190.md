Outline: Marital Communications Privilege
Question: Isaac and Rebekah divorced before trial. In a federal civil action, Rebekah is asked whether, during the marriage, she saw Isaac sign a false invoice at their kitchen table. She is not asked to repeat anything Isaac said. Isaac objects under the marital communications privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because Rebekah learned the fact while married to Isaac.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because all domestic conduct is deemed communicative between spouses.
Answer C: Overrule the objection, because the privilege protects confidential communications, not a spouse's observation of conduct.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only because the case is civil rather than criminal.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Marital communications privilege protects confidential communications, not every fact learned during marriage. Rebekah is asked about what she observed Isaac do, not what Isaac privately communicated. The decisive axis is observation versus communication.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This turns marriage into a blanket fact privilege. The privilege is narrower. B: B is wrong because Observed conduct is not automatically a confidential communication merely because it occurs at home. D: D is wrong because Civil posture is not the controlling reason. The same observation/communication split matters in criminal cases too.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / observation not communication
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating all knowledge gained from a spouse as privileged rather than identifying the protected object.
