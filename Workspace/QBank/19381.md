Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Peter signed and delivered to Paul a deed conveying a warehouse. The deed contained all required deed terms, and Paul accepted it. Paul put the deed in a drawer and never recorded it. No third party claims the warehouse. Peter later says the deed was ineffective because it was never recorded. Who owns the warehouse?
Answer A: Peter, because an unrecorded deed is void.
Answer B: Peter, until Paul records the deed.
Answer C: Peter, because the recorder's stamp is a required deed formality.
Answer D: Paul, because recording is not required for a valid deed to pass title between grantor and grantee.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive gate is validity between the parties, not recording priority. A deed that satisfies the required formalities and is delivered and accepted can pass title even if it is never recorded. With no competing third party, title belongs to Paul.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an unrecorded deed is not void for that reason alone. B: B is wrong because recording is not a condition to title passing between Peter and Paul. C: C is wrong because a recorder's stamp is not a deed-validity formality.
