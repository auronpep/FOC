Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Abraham agreed in a signed writing to sell Sarah 500 portable water filters for resale in Sarah's store. The writing was final but silent about the packaging standard. Sarah offers evidence that in this trade, “retail-ready filters” means individually boxed filters with printed instructions. Abraham objects that the writing is final and that trade usage cannot be considered. Which source-law route best governs the dispute about the packaging term?
Answer A: Common law governs because the dispute concerns interpretation rather than formation, so trade usage is barred by the final writing.
Answer B: UCC Article 2 governs because the contract is for movable goods, and trade usage may be used to explain or supplement the writing if consistent with it.
Answer C: Real-property law governs because the filters will be displayed in a store.
Answer D: The Statute of Frauds governs because any evidence about packaging must itself be signed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The transaction is a sale of movable goods, so UCC Article 2 is the source-law lock. Under the UCC route, trade usage may explain or supplement a final writing when it is reasonably consistent with the express terms. The first decision is not whether the writing is final in the abstract, but whether Article 2 supplies the meaning architecture.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it imports a rigid common-law PER route into a goods contract. C: C is wrong because the buyer's store location does not turn movable filters into real property. D: D is wrong because the question asks meaning of a term, not whether a contract satisfies a writing requirement.
