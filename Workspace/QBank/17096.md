Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Types of Jury Verdicts
Question: A man sued a company in federal district court in State A for injuries he received after riding on one of the company's rollercoasters. The case was tried in front of a seven-member jury. The jury returned a verdict 5-2 in favor of the man.&#10;&#10;May a verdict be entered in the man's favor?
Answer A: A: No, because the verdict was not unanimous.
Answer B: B: No, because the jury had less than 12 members.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the majority of the jury ruled in the man's favor.
Answer D: D: Yes, because a super majority ruled in the man's favor.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 48, “a jury must begin with at least six and no more than 12 members.” In addition, “unless the parties stipulate otherwise, the verdict must be unanimous and must be returned by a jury of at least six members.” Since there is no indication the parties stipulated otherwise, the jury's verdict has to be unanimous before it can be entered in the man's favor. Therefore, C and D are incorrect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect because, as stated above, a jury can have between 6 and 12 members.
