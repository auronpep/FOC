


BARMATRIX Q#: 22215
Outline_code: 33040301
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Hearsay and Circumstances of Its Admissibility
Outline: Requiring unavailability of declarant
Question: A man and a woman were injured when the rental car they were driving crashed into a tree. The man and the woman sued the rental company, claiming that the rental company had improperly repaired the car and damaged the car's accelerator. According to the man and woman, the car spontaneously sped up right before it hit the tree. Before trial, the woman discovered that she had cancer. Knowing that she was not going to survive until the trial occurred, she agreed to be deposed by the rental company. The woman died shortly thereafter. At trial, the rental company argued that the man was contributorily negligent and drove with his foot on the accelerator before the car hit the tree. The man offered into evidence the woman's deposition testimony, where she stated, “The car just sped up by itself and he kept saying that he wasn't touching anything but the brake.” Is the woman's statement admissible?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the woman made the statement knowing that she was dying.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the woman made the statement during the deposition.
Answer C: C: No, because the woman's statement did not appear to be challenged during the deposition.
Answer D: D: No, because the woman's statement is inadmissible hearsay.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 804(b)(1), testimony given by a witness in another hearing or taken in a deposition is admissible if the witness is unavailable and the party against whom the testimony is offered had an opportunity to examine the witness. Here, the woman has passed away and the deposition was taken by the rental company, so the testimony is admissible. A is incorrect because a dying declaration requires that the declarant make a statement regarding the circumstances of his or her death. Here, the woman was dying of cancer, which was not caused by the accident. C is incorrect because the opposing party only has to have the opportunity to examine the witness. Whether the rental company took advantage of the opportunity is irrelevant. D is incorrect because the woman's testimony meets the requirements of the former testimony exception.
