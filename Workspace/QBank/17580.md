Question: Jacob privately asked his lawyer, Esther, how to backdate a lease so that a bank would believe the lease existed before a loan application. Jacob sought detailed drafting advice to make the false date look authentic. Is the communication protected by attorney-client privilege?
Answer A: Yes, because Jacob spoke privately to a lawyer for legal advice.
Answer B: Yes, unless Esther knew the bank would be misled.
Answer C: No, because Jacob sought the lawyer's assistance to further a future fraud.
Answer D: No, because attorney-client privilege never applies to lease documents.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The crime-fraud exception removes protection when a client seeks legal advice or assistance to further future or ongoing crime or fraud. Jacob was not merely confessing past conduct; he sought help making a false document to mislead a bank. The decisive axis is future fraud purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is attorney-confidentiality absolutism. Privacy and legal advice do not protect communications made to further fraud. B: B is wrong because This adds a false attorney-knowledge condition. The client's fraudulent purpose is the key. D: A is wrong because This is attorney-confidentiality absolutism.
