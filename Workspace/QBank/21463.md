Outline: Freedom of speech and expression
Question: A city council meeting includes a public-comment period limited to the proposed zoning ordinance on the agenda. The mayor cuts off a speaker who begins discussing unrelated national elections. The mayor then allows supporters and opponents of the zoning ordinance to speak. Is the topic limit itself likely unconstitutional?
Answer A: Yes, because public-comment periods must allow speech on every topic
Answer B: Yes, because cutting off any speaker is viewpoint discrimination
Answer C: No, because limiting comments to the zoning ordinance is a reasonable topic limit in a limited forum
Answer D: No, because city council meetings are private forums outside the First Amendment
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The city kept the forum within its defined subject and did not suppress a viewpoint on that subject.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A limited forum may be confined to the meeting's agenda topic. B: The speaker was cut off for being off topic, while both sides of the zoning issue were allowed. D: A government-created comment period implicates forum doctrine even though it may be limited.
lb_rule_reference: A public meeting comment period is commonly a limited public forum; topic limits may be reasonable, but viewpoint discrimination within the allowed topic is unconstitutional.
