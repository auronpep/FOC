Question: A city ordinance prohibits all open flames in public parks during a severe drought. A religious group that uses small candles during outdoor worship challenges the ordinance under the Free Exercise Clause. The ordinance does not mention religion and applies to all park users. What standard most likely applies?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny, because any incidental burden on religious practice requires an exemption
Answer B: Rational basis, because the ordinance is neutral and generally applicable
Answer C: The ordinance is invalid per se because religious conduct can never be regulated
Answer D: The ordinance is valid only if the city proves no less restrictive alternative exists
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The law applies to all open flames without religious targeting or individualized exemptions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Neutral and generally applicable laws usually do not trigger strict scrutiny merely because they incidentally burden religious practice. C: Religious conduct may be incidentally burdened by neutral generally applicable laws. D: That burden is not triggered by a neutral generally applicable rule.
lb_rule_reference: A neutral and generally applicable law that incidentally burdens religious practice usually receives rational basis review under the Free Exercise Clause.
