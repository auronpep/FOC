Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A camping knife was sold with a sharp exposed blade and a protective sheath. The blade was visible and functioned as an ordinary knife blade. A camper removed the sheath and cut a finger while slicing rope. The packaging did not say, "Sharp blade can cut skin." The camper sues the manufacturer under strict products liability for failure to warn. Is the camper likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim for failure to warn?
Answer A: Yes, because the knife did not expressly warn that a sharp blade can cut skin
Answer B: No, because the risk that an exposed knife blade can cut skin is obvious and requires no warning
Answer C: Yes, because every product capable of causing physical injury must carry a warning label
Answer D: No, because warnings are relevant only to prescription products
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The danger was visible, ordinary, and integral to the product's function. Failure-to-warn liability does not require labels for obvious knife-cut risks.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Manufacturers generally need not warn of obvious dangers ordinary users already understand. The cutting risk of an exposed knife blade is patent. C: The warning duty is limited. Obvious risks generally do not require warnings, and dangerousness alone does not create a duty to warn against every injury. D: Warnings apply broadly in products cases, not only to prescription products. This claim fails because the risk was obvious.
lb_rule_reference: A manufacturer generally has no duty to warn of open and obvious dangers that ordinary users already understand.
