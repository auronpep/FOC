Question: In a federal accident case, Ezra, a tow-truck driver, testifies that he saw a broken brake line hanging under Anna's truck immediately after the crash. Ezra is then asked two questions: first, whether the line appeared broken when he saw it; second, whether the line failed because of internal metal fatigue. Ezra has no engineering training. The defendant objects to both opinions. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude both opinions because Ezra is not an engineer.
Answer B: Admit both opinions because Ezra saw the brake line after the crash.
Answer C: Admit the opinion that the line appeared broken, but exclude the metal-fatigue opinion as specialized causation testimony.
Answer D: Exclude the broken-line observation, but admit the metal-fatigue opinion because causation is for the jury.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Ezra may give a lay opinion based on his perception that the line appeared broken. But explaining that the line failed because of internal metal fatigue requires specialized engineering knowledge. The court should split the opinions by basis and subject matter.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A under-admits by treating ordinary visual observation as expert-only. B over-admits by treating specialized causation as lay opinion.
