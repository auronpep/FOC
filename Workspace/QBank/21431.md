Question: In a federal personal-injury trial, Mary claims severe facial scarring. She offers a clear color photograph taken one week after the accident. The defendant objects that the photograph is unpleasant to view. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the photograph because any unpleasant photograph is unfairly prejudicial.
Answer B: Admit the photograph if it fairly depicts the injury and its probative value is not substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice.
Answer C: Exclude the photograph unless Mary has no medical witness.
Answer D: Admit the photograph only for impeachment.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Photographs can be probative evidence of injury. Unpleasantness is not enough; exclusion requires the Rule 403 dangers to substantially outweigh probative value.
