Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Joshua signed and delivered to Miriam a deed conveying Lot 4. The deed identified the parties and property and used present words of transfer. Miriam accepted the deed. The county recorder refused to record it because Joshua had not acknowledged it before a notary. In a dispute only between Joshua and Miriam, who owns Lot 4?
Answer A: Joshua, because the recorder refused to record the deed.
Answer B: Joshua, because acknowledgment before a notary is always required for deed validity.
Answer C: Miriam, because acknowledgment may affect recording but is not required for validity between the parties.
Answer D: No one, until a court orders the recorder to accept the deed.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is deed execution and delivery, not recordability. Acknowledgment may be required for recording, but it is not ordinarily required for the deed to be valid between grantor and grantee. Title passes to Miriam.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the recorder's refusal does not undo a valid transfer between the parties. B: B is wrong because acknowledgment is not always a validity requirement. D: D is wrong because title can pass before and without a court order concerning recording.
