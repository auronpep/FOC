Question: Abraham hired Moses to build a courtyard. The signed contract stated that Moses would install the “premium stone package” for $90,000 and contained a merger clause stating that the writing was the parties' complete and exclusive agreement. After a dispute arose, Abraham offered evidence that, during negotiations, both parties used “premium stone package” to mean limestone rather than marble. Should the court admit the evidence? Should the court admit the evidence?
Answer A: Exclude it because a merger clause bars all prior negotiation evidence.
Answer B: Admit it to explain the meaning of the ambiguous phrase, not to add a new obligation.
Answer C: Exclude it because the term appears inside a complete written agreement.
Answer D: Admit it only if Abraham first proves that Moses breached the contract.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Even a complete integration does not bar extrinsic evidence offered to explain the meaning of an ambiguous term. The evidence is not offered to add a new term; it is offered to determine what the written phrase “premium stone package” means.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because merger clauses bar added or contradictory terms, not all explanation of ambiguous language. C: C is wrong because integration does not eliminate ambiguity analysis. D: D is wrong because admissibility of interpretive evidence does not depend on first proving breach.
