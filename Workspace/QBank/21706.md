Question: Officer Park arrested Cal and read him the complete Miranda warnings from a card. Cal nodded and said, "Yeah, I got it." Officer Park then began asking questions about a robbery. Cal answered for the next forty minutes, ultimately admitting he was at the scene. Cal never said "I waive my rights" and never signed a waiver form. He now argues his statements should be suppressed because he did not expressly waive his rights. Did Cal validly waive his Miranda rights?
Answer A: No, because a valid Miranda waiver requires an express oral or written statement of waiver
Answer B: No, because Cal invoked his right to silence by remaining mostly silent for the initial portion of the interview
Answer C: Yes, because Cal impliedly waived his rights by acknowledging the warnings and voluntarily answering questions for forty minutes
Answer D: Yes, because Cal said "Yeah, I got it," which constituted an express waiver of his Miranda rights
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under *Berghuis v. Thompkins* (2010), a suspect who is given Miranda warnings and who then voluntarily answers police questions has impliedly waived the right to remain silent. The waiver need not be express. The prosecution must show the waiver was knowing, voluntary, and intelligent. Here, Cal was warned, acknowledged understanding, and answered questions voluntarily for a substantial period. This establishes implied waiver. His statements are admissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Supreme Court in *Berghuis v. Thompkins* (2010) explicitly held that an express oral or written waiver is not required. A waiver may be implied from the totality of circumstances. Receiving warnings, acknowledging understanding, and then voluntarily answering questions is sufficient to establish implied waiver. B: Invocation of Miranda rights must be unambiguous. A suspect who simply does not say "I waive" but proceeds to answer questions has not made an unambiguous invocation. Ambiguous conduct is treated as non-invocation under *Berghuis v. Thompkins*. Cal's nodding and saying "Yeah, I got it" followed by answering questions is consistent with waiver, not invocation. D: "Yeah, I got it" is more accurately characterized as acknowledgment of understanding, not an express waiver of rights. This matters because the correct analysis is implied waiver based on the totality of circumstances. Mischaracterizing an acknowledgment of understanding as an express waiver conflates two distinct things. The conclusion is correct but the reasoning is inaccurate.
lb_rule_reference: A suspect may impliedly waive Miranda rights by receiving warnings, indicating understanding, and then voluntarily responding to questioning; no express written or oral waiver is required.
