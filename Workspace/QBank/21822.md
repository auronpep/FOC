Outline: Rule of completeness
Question: Peter is charged with making a false report. The prosecutor reads from Peter's written statement: “I saw Joshua take the money.” The same sentence continues, “but I was guessing because I was across the street and could not see his hands.” Peter asks the court to require the prosecutor to read the rest of the sentence. The prosecutor objects that the remainder is Peter's hearsay. What should the court do?
Answer A: Exclude the remainder because a defendant's own written statement is always hearsay when offered by the defendant.
Answer B: Require the remainder to be read if fairness requires it to avoid distorting the part the prosecutor introduced.
Answer C: Exclude the remainder because Peter may cross-examine the officer who took the statement instead.
Answer D: Require the prosecutor to read every statement Peter made during the interview.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The prosecutor's excerpt suggests a direct eyewitness accusation. The remainder of the same sentence materially changes that meaning. If fairness requires completion, the court may require the completing part at that time and the hearsay objection does not defeat completion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 106 can permit necessary completion over a hearsay objection. C: C is wrong because cross-examination later may not adequately correct an immediate distortion. D: D is wrong because only the portion needed to correct the misleading excerpt is required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 106 / hearsay objection to completing statement
