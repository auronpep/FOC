Question: Isaac agreed in a signed land-sale contract to buy Naomi's vacant lot. The contract contained a merger clause, stated that the lot was sold “as is,” and said Naomi had no duty to make repairs. Before signing, Naomi told Isaac that she had never received an engineering report about the retaining wall, even though she had received a report warning that the wall was unstable. Naomi also orally promised before signing that she would rebuild the wall at her own expense. Isaac seeks to offer both statements after discovering the report. Which evidence is admissible under the parol-evidence framework?
Answer A: Both the concealed-report evidence and the oral repair promise.
Answer B: Neither item, because the contract has a merger clause and an as-is clause.
Answer C: The concealed-report evidence only, if offered to prove fraud or avoid the contract rather than to add a repair term.
Answer D: The oral repair promise only, because it concerns performance rather than contract validity.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that parol evidence does not bar evidence offered to prove fraud, mistake, duress, or another basis to avoid the contract, but it can bar a prior oral promise offered to add a term contradicting a complete written agreement. The trigger facts are the merger clause, as-is/no-repair language, concealed engineering report, and separate oral repair promise. The decisive lock is offered purpose. The fraud evidence is used to attack enforceability; the repair promise adds a repair duty inconsistent with the writing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it fails to split the two pieces of evidence and admits a contradictory repair term. B: B is wrong because it treats the merger clause as barring defense/avoidance evidence. D: D is wrong because the oral repair promise is the prior term most directly contradicted by the written no-repair/as-is language.
