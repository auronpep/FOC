Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: During a pickup basketball game, Rafi accused Owen of cheating. Owen immediately grabbed a knife from his gym bag, shouted, "I'll end this now," and stabbed Rafi once in the chest. Rafi died. There was no evidence that Owen had previously planned to harm Rafi or paused to reflect before stabbing him. What is the most serious homicide offense supported by these facts?
Answer A: First-degree murder, because Owen intended to kill when he stabbed Rafi in the chest
Answer B: Voluntary manslaughter, because Rafi's accusation provoked Owen
Answer C: Murder, but not first-degree murder, because Owen intentionally killed without proven premeditation and deliberation
Answer D: Involuntary manslaughter, because Owen acted impulsively rather than deliberately
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A purposeful stabbing to the chest supports malice murder. But first-degree murder requires premeditation and deliberation, and the facts provide no planning, waiting, cool reflection, or other degree trigger. The most serious supported offense is murder short of first degree.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intent to kill is not the same as premeditation and deliberation. The facts show an immediate eruption with no prior planning or reflective pause. B: Mere words and accusations are not adequate provocation at common law. The facts reduce first degree, but they do not reduce murder to voluntary manslaughter. D: Impulse defeats deliberation, not malice. Owen intentionally used deadly force aimed at a vital area, which supports murder.
lb_rule_reference: Intent to kill supplies malice for murder, but first-degree murder requires the additional element of premeditation and deliberation.
