MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Freedom of religion
Question: The state passed a law providing free textbooks to K- 12 students in all public and private schools in the state. A private elementary school in the state was founded in the 1960s as a response to school desegregation. Since its founding, the school has only admitted white students. A parent group sued the state, arguing that the distribution of free textbooks to the elementary school was unconstitutional. Is the court likely to find that the distribution of free textbooks to the elementary school is unconstitutional?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the state may not give aid to private schools.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the state cannot give significant aid to schools that practice racial discrimination.
Answer C: C: No, because the book program is neutral and gives books to all public and private schools in the state.
Answer D: D: No, because the book program serves a legitimate state interest.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: In Norwood v. Harrison, 413 U.S. 455 (1973), the United States Supreme Court found a free book program unconstitutional when the state gave free books to private schools that practiced racial discrimination. A is incorrect because the state can aid private schools so long as that aid does not support racial discrimination. C is incorrect because the fact that the books are given to all schools in the state does not save the program from being unconstitutional. D is incorrect because while education is a legitimate government interest, the government's interest in education would not allow it to give aid to discriminatory schools.
