Question: In a federal fraud trial, the prosecutor offers an email allegedly written by Ezra. The email contains details known to Ezra and was sent from an account bearing Ezra's name. Ezra denies writing it. The judge finds enough evidence for a reasonable jury to find that Ezra wrote the email and admits it. If Ezra requests an instruction on the authorship issue, what should the court do?
Answer A: Tell the jury it must treat the email as written by Ezra because the judge admitted it.
Answer B: Tell the jury it may consider the email only if it finds that Ezra wrote or authorized it.
Answer C: Tell the jury to disregard the email because Ezra denied writing it.
Answer D: Tell the jury that authentication is always decided finally by the judge and never considered by the jury.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Authenticity may function as a conditional fact. The judge screens for sufficient evidence to support a finding, but the jury may decide whether the condition is actually satisfied and then give the evidence appropriate weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because admission after threshold screening does not compel the jury to find authorship. C: C is wrong because a denial does not automatically defeat admissibility if sufficient supporting evidence exists. D: D is wrong because the judge's screening role does not eliminate the jury's role in weighing whether the item is what the proponent claims.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / authenticity as conditional fact
