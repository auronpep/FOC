Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: A merchant seller orally agreed to sell 800 baskets to Rachel, who was buying them for a one-time family event and was not a merchant. The seller sent Rachel a signed confirmation. Rachel received it and did not object in writing within 10 days. The seller later sued Rachel for the price after Rachel refused delivery. Does the seller's confirmation satisfy UCC §2-201 against Rachel under the merchant-confirmation rule?
Answer A: Yes, because Rachel received the confirmation and failed to object in writing within 10 days.
Answer B: Yes, because only the sender of a confirmation must be a merchant.
Answer C: Yes, but only if the baskets were shipped before Rachel refused delivery.
Answer D: No, because the merchant-confirmation rule applies between merchants, and Rachel is not a merchant.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The merchant-confirmation rule applies between merchants. A signed confirmation by one merchant can satisfy the writing requirement against a silent merchant recipient, but it does not bind a nonmerchant recipient under that special rule. Another signed writing by Rachel or a separate exception would be needed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because failure to object within 10 days matters only under the between-merchants rule. B: B is wrong because both parties must be merchants for the special confirmation rule. C: C is wrong because shipment may raise other UCC issues, but it does not make Rachel a merchant recipient.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 / merchant confirmation merchant status
