Question: In a federal wage case, Lydia offers a summary chart of 300 handwritten time sheets. She has not authenticated the time sheets and admits that some were prepared by unknown persons. Lydia refuses to make the time sheets available for inspection. The employer objects to the summary. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the chart because summaries are always admissible when records are numerous.
Answer B: Admit the chart if Lydia promises that the totals are accurate.
Answer C: Exclude the chart because Rule 1006 requires voluminous admissible materials and access to the underlying originals or duplicates.
Answer D: Exclude the chart only if the employer proves every time sheet is false.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A Rule 1006 summary depends on admissible underlying materials that cannot conveniently be examined and on making the originals or duplicates available to other parties. Those safeguards are missing.
