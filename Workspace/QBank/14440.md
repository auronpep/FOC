Question: A debtor owed a lender $1,500. The statute of limitations barred recovery on the claim. The debtor wrote to the lender, stating, “I promise to pay you $500 if you will extinguish the debt.” The lender agreed. Is the debtor's promise to pay the lender $500 enforceable?
Answer A: A: No, because the debtor made no promise not to plead the statute of limitations as a defense.
Answer B: B: No, because there was no consideration for the debtor's promise.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the debtor's promise provided a benefit to the lender.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the debtor's promise to pay part of the barred antecedent debt needs no consideration to be enforceable.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. The enforcement of a debtor's promise is a long-established exception to the pre-existing duty rule. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The debtor still promised to pay the $500, implying that there was no reliance on the statute of limitations. B is incorrect. A promise to pay a debt after the running of the statute of limitations will be enforceable. C is incorrect. The benefit to the lender alone is not enough to make this promise enforceable.
