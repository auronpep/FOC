Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A homeowner owned a dog known to bite when cornered. A neighbor knew about the dog's biting history. During a visit, the neighbor repeatedly poked the dog with a stick while the dog was trapped against a fence. The dog bit the neighbor's hand. If the neighbor sues for strict liability, which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The homeowner is automatically liable because the dog had a known dangerous propensity
Answer B: The homeowner is not liable because dogs are domestic animals
Answer C: The neighbor's deliberate provocation of the known risk can defeat or reduce strict-liability recovery
Answer D: The neighbor's conduct matters only in a negligence action and never in strict liability
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The neighbor knowingly triggered the precise animal danger, so a defense applies.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Known propensity supports the claim, but the plaintiff's knowing provocation can be a defense. B: Domestic-animal owners can be strictly liable when they know of a dangerous propensity. D: Animal strict liability can be subject to defenses based on knowing, voluntary, or provoking conduct.
lb_rule_reference: Even when an animal owner would otherwise face strict liability, a plaintiff's knowing provocation or assumption of the animal risk can defeat or reduce recovery.
