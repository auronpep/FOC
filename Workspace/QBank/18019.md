Question: Isaac bought a grain silo from a manufacturer under a signed contract stating that the silo would be “delivered and set on the concrete pad.” The contract was silent about anchor bolts. After the manufacturer placed the silo on the pad but did not anchor it, Isaac claimed that the phrase required anchoring. Which additional fact would most strengthen Isaac's Content/Meaning argument?
Answer A: Before signing, a salesperson told Isaac, “We handle everything,” but the final writing did not repeat that phrase.
Answer B: A different buyer once paid a different installer separately to anchor a silo.
Answer C: In the grain-silo trade, “delivered and set on the concrete pad” ordinarily includes anchoring the silo to the pad, and the manufacturer regularly deals in that trade.
Answer D: Isaac lost money because the unanchored silo could not be used during harvest.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a trade usage can give particular meaning to a contract term when the usage is established and reasonably consistent with the writing. The trigger facts are a goods contract with the phrase “delivered and set” and silence about anchoring. The decisive lock is offered purpose: Isaac needs a meaning source that defines the written scope. C supplies that source directly.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is tempting because it is seller-side language, but it is a prior broad oral statement and is weaker than established trade meaning.
