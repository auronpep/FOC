Outline: Psychotherapist-patient
Question: Lydia brings a federal employment-discrimination action against her employer. During discovery, the employer subpoenas confidential notes from Naomi, a licensed clinical social worker who provided Lydia psychotherapy for anxiety caused by the workplace events. The notes record Lydia's statements to Naomi during diagnosis and treatment. Lydia has not disclosed the notes to anyone else and has not waived any privilege. Are the therapy notes protected by a federal privilege?
Answer A: No. Federal common law does not recognize any privilege for communications made in a medical or counseling setting.
Answer B: Yes, but only if State A has enacted a statutory privilege covering licensed clinical social workers.
Answer C: No. The privilege depends on whether the communications occurred in a hospital, not whether they were made for psychotherapy.
Answer D: Yes. Federal common law recognizes a psychotherapist-patient privilege for confidential communications with a licensed psychotherapist, including qualifying licensed clinical social workers, made in the course of diagnosis or treatment.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under federal privilege law, confidential communications between a patient and a licensed psychotherapist in the course of diagnosis or treatment are protected. The privilege also extends to qualifying licensed clinical social workers providing psychotherapy. Lydia's statements were made confidentially to Naomi during diagnosis and treatment, and no waiver is stated. The decisive axis is recognized federal privilege scope, not the broader medical-setting label.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it collapses physician-patient and psychotherapist-patient privileges. Federal law generally lacks a broad physician-patient privilege, but it recognizes psychotherapist-patient privilege. B: B is wrong because this is a federal-question case using federal privilege law, and the federal psychotherapist privilege is independently recognized under Rule 501 common law. C: C is wrong because the privilege turns on confidential psychotherapy for diagnosis or treatment by a protected professional, not hospital location.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 + Jaffee psychotherapist-patient privilege
lb_student_diagnostic: Student has a recognition-map gap and treats all medical or counseling relationships as either all privileged or all unprivileged.
