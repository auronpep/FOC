Outline: Refreshing recollection
Question: In a federal civil trial, Jeremiah forgets the exact words he heard Rachel say during a meeting. On the stand, he reviews a typed summary that he prepared the next day. After reviewing it, Jeremiah says, “Now I remember Rachel said, ‘Send the shipment tonight.'” Opposing counsel objects that the typed summary itself is not the original recording of the meeting and therefore Jeremiah cannot testify to the words. The typed summary was used only to refresh memory. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the original recording is required whenever a witness testifies to words spoken at a meeting.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because a summary can refresh memory only if it qualifies as a duplicate under the original-writing rule.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Jeremiah is testifying from refreshed present memory, not offering the typed summary to prove its contents.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the typed summary is admitted as a recorded recollection.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When a writing is used only to refresh a witness's memory and the witness then testifies from present recollection, the writing itself is not being offered to prove its contents. The original-writing rule is not triggered merely because the witness refreshed memory from a summary. Opposing counsel may have Rule 612 inspection and cross-examination rights if the writing was used while testifying.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the witness is not proving the contents of a recording; he is testifying from memory of what he heard. B: B is wrong because duplicate status is not the issue. D: D is wrong because recorded recollection is needed only if the witness lacks present memory and the 803(5) foundation is satisfied.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 612 and FRE 1002 / original-writing false positive
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests original-writing false positives in the refresh context.
