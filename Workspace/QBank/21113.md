Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: An employee files a complaint in federal court against her employer. Count I asserts a federal employment discrimination claim under Title VII, seeking $30,000 in damages. Count II asserts a state-law wrongful termination claim arising from the same termination decision and based on the same facts as Count I, seeking an additional $20,000. Both counts concern the employer's decision to fire the employee at the same time, on the same day, for stated reasons that the employee contends were pretextual. The employer moves to dismiss Count II for lack of subject matter jurisdiction, arguing that $20,000 does not satisfy the $75,000 amount-in-controversy requirement. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Should the court dismiss Count II?
Answer A: Yes, because $20,000 does not satisfy the $75,000 amount-in-controversy requirement for diversity jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because supplemental jurisdiction under § 1367 does not apply to employment cases — those require independent federal subject matter jurisdiction over each claim
Answer C: No, because the state-law claim shares a common nucleus of operative fact with the Title VII claim, bringing it within § 1367(a) supplemental jurisdiction
Answer D: No, because the plaintiff may aggregate the Title VII and state claims to reach $50,000 in total, which together satisfy a reduced threshold for pendant claims
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under § 1367(a), a federal court has supplemental jurisdiction over all claims so related to the anchor claim that they form part of the same case or controversy under Article III — meaning they share a common nucleus of operative fact. Both Count I and Count II arise from the same employer decision to terminate the same employee at the same time for the same stated reasons. They share a common nucleus of operative fact. Supplemental jurisdiction under § 1367(a) applies and the motion to dismiss Count II should be denied. No independent AIC or independent jurisdictional basis is required for the supplemental claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The state-law claim is not being asserted on diversity grounds — it is being maintained under supplemental jurisdiction anchored to the Title VII federal claim. Section 1367(a) does not impose an independent AIC requirement. If the claim shares a common nucleus of operative fact with a claim that independently invokes federal jurisdiction, supplemental jurisdiction attaches without satisfying § 1332. B: Section 1367(a) provides supplemental jurisdiction over all claims that share a common nucleus of operative fact with the anchor federal claim, with no subject-matter exception for employment cases. Title VII provides the anchor for federal-question jurisdiction; § 1367(a) reaches the state wrongful-termination claim because it arises from the same termination event. D: There is no reduced AIC threshold for supplemental jurisdiction claims. Section 1367(a) imposes no amount-in-controversy requirement at all; the anchor claim must independently satisfy its own jurisdictional requirements, but supplemental claims need only meet the common-nucleus test. Neither aggregation nor a reduced dollar figure is part of § 1367 analysis.
