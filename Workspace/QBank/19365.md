Question: A store manager completes a routine daily safety checklist before any accident occurs. Months later, after a customer sues, the store sends the checklist to defense counsel. The customer requests the checklist in discovery. The store claims it is work product because counsel now has it. Is the checklist protected as work product on these facts?
Answer A: Yes, because any document in a lawyer's file is work product
Answer B: Yes, because safety information is always privileged
Answer C: No, because it was prepared in the ordinary course of business before litigation was anticipated
Answer D: No, because work product is never protected in federal court
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The checklist was not prepared because of litigation or for trial preparation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Possession by counsel does not transform an ordinary business record into work product. B: Safety information is not privileged as a category. D: Work product is protected when the doctrine's requirements are met.
lb_rule_reference: Materials prepared in the ordinary course of business are not work product merely because they are later sent to a lawyer.
