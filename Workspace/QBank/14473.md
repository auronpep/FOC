Question: On June 1, a seller and a buyer contracted in writing for the sale and purchase of the seller's cattle ranch (a large single tract) and to close the transaction on December 1. On October 1, the buyer told the seller, "I'm increasingly unhappy about our June 1 contract because of the current cattle market, and do not intend to buy your ranch unless I'm legally obligated to do so." If the seller sues the buyer on October 15 for breach of contract, the seller will probably
Answer A: A: win, because the buyer committed a total breach by anticipatory repudiation on October 1.
Answer B: B: win, because the buyer's October 1 statement created reasonable grounds for the seller's insecurity with respect to the buyer's performance.
Answer C: C: lose, because the parties contracted for the sale and conveyance of a single tract, and the seller cannot bring suit for breach of such a contract prior to the agreed closing date.
Answer D: D: lose, because the buyer's October 1 statement to the seller was neither a repudiation nor a present breach of the June 1 contract.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. Anticipatory repudiation requires an unequivocal statement of unwillingness to perform on a contractual obligation, not mere doubt or uncertainty. Here, the buyer expressed serious doubt and may have established insecurity, but did not amount to a full repudiation. This was also not a breach because the time for performance had not yet passed. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The buyer's statement, although raising serious doubts about his intention to perform, was not sufficiently unequivocal to be an anticipatory repudiation. B is incorrect. Reasonable grounds for insecurity are not alone sufficient to entitle the seller to treat the contract as breached. C is incorrect. The buyer's obligation is not due until the closing date, which means this was not a breach. However, this answer is incomplete. The seller could have brought suit on October 15 if the buyer's statement had been sufficiently unequivocal to amount to a repudiation of the contract.
