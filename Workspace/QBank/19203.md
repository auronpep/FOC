Question: A city official, Nathan, promised that the city would let Joel use a public hall for a charity concert without charge. Joel reasonably spent $6,000 advertising the concert. Before the concert, the city withdrew the promise. Joel cannot prove ticket revenue with reasonable certainty. If Joel proves promissory estoppel, what remedy is most likely?
Answer A: The market rental value of the hall for the entire season.
Answer B: All profits Joel hoped the concert would earn.
Answer C: The $6,000 reasonable advertising expenditure, if necessary to avoid injustice.
Answer D: Nothing, because a promise made without bargain consideration can never support a remedy.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Promissory estoppel can supply a remedy when a promise reasonably induces reliance and enforcement is necessary to avoid injustice. The remedy may be limited to reliance, here the $6,000 advertising expense, because expected profits are uncertain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Joel did not bargain for or lose use of the hall for an entire season. B: B is wrong because the hoped-for profits are not proven with reasonable certainty. D: D is wrong because promissory estoppel can support relief despite lack of bargain consideration.
lb_rule_reference: Promissory estoppel remedy as justice requires
