Outline: Gap-Fillers
Question: Naomi bought a commercial bakery oven from Isaac's equipment company. The signed agreement allocated most of the price to the oven itself and a small amount to ordinary installation. It described the purchase as a “turnkey oven package” but did not define that phrase. Naomi withheld payment after Isaac installed the oven but did not perform a start-up burn-in. Naomi offered evidence that, in the commercial bakery equipment trade, “turnkey oven package” ordinarily includes one start-up burn-in. Which Content/Meaning route is best?
Answer A: Common-law services rules control because installation was included, so the trade usage is barred by the final writing.
Answer B: Article 2 controls because the predominant purpose was the sale of goods, and the trade usage may explain the technical package term if established and consistent.
Answer C: The court should ignore source law and apply a default gap-filler requiring payment only after perfect installation.
Answer D: The usage is admissible only if Naomi proves that Isaac committed fraud when he omitted the burn-in.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a mixed transaction is routed by its predominant purpose, and a predominantly goods transaction uses Article 2 meaning sources. The trigger facts are that most of the price was for the oven and installation was incidental, plus the technical phrase “turnkey oven package.” The decisive locks are source law and purpose: Naomi offers usage to explain the package term, not to prove fraud or a default rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because incidental installation does not make the transaction a services contract. C: C is wrong because a gap-filler should not be used before checking agreement meaning sources. D: D is wrong because fraud is not required for interpretive trade-usage evidence.
