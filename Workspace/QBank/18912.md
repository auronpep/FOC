Question: A resident organized rallies, gave interviews, and ran a website opposing a proposed highway. A newspaper then falsely reported that she had secretly accepted money from a rival developer to oppose the project. The article concerned the highway controversy. What plaintiff-status rule most likely applies?
Answer A: She may be a limited-purpose public figure for statements related to the highway controversy
Answer B: She is a public figure for every topic for the rest of her life
Answer C: She cannot be any public figure because she is not famous nationwide
Answer D: She is automatically a private plaintiff because she is a resident
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: She voluntarily sought influence in a public controversy, so actual malice may be required for related statements.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Limited-purpose status is tied to the controversy and related statements. C: Limited-purpose public figures need not be general celebrities. D: A private resident can become a limited-purpose public figure by injecting herself into a controversy.
lb_rule_reference: A person who voluntarily injects herself into a public controversy to influence its outcome may be a limited-purpose public figure for statements related to that controversy.
