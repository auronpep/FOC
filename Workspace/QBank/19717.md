Question: In a federal arson trial, the prosecutor proves that Solomon bought gasoline one hour before the fire and was seen walking away from the building. Solomon asks the judge to instruct the jury that circumstantial evidence is weaker than direct evidence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Give the instruction because circumstantial proof is always legally weaker than direct proof.
Answer B: Give the instruction only if the prosecution has no eyewitness to the fire being set.
Answer C: Refuse the instruction because circumstantial evidence is an accepted proof type and the jury decides its weight.
Answer D: Refuse the instruction only because the gasoline purchase is direct evidence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Circumstantial evidence can be probative and is not categorically inferior to direct evidence. The jury weighs the evidence; the court should not instruct that circumstantial proof is legally weaker.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A states a false hierarchy. B incorrectly requires direct evidence. D mislabels the gasoline purchase; it is circumstantial proof of arson.
