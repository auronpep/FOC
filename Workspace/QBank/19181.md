Question: A state official asks a federal court to declare that a newly enacted state law is constitutional. No one has threatened suit, no enforcement action is pending, and the named defendant agrees with the official that the law is valid. What is the best Article III objection?
Answer A: The request seeks an advisory opinion because there is no real adverse dispute
Answer B: The court must answer because governments are entitled to advance approval of laws
Answer C: The case is moot because the law has already been enacted
Answer D: The case is a political question because a state law is involved
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal courts decide concrete disputes between adverse parties, not abstract requests for legal approval.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Article III does not allow federal courts to give abstract legal advice. C: Mootness is not the issue; there was no concrete adverse dispute to begin with. D: Courts can review state laws in proper cases; the defect here is lack of adversity and concrete dispute.
lb_rule_reference: Federal courts do not issue advisory opinions; a real case or controversy requires adverse parties and a dispute the court's judgment can affect.
