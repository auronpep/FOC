Subtopic: Defenses to Enforceability
Outline: Mistake / Lack of Mutual Assent
Question: A builder agrees to construct a retaining wall on Caleb's land for a fixed price. Before signing, both the builder and Caleb know that no soil test has been done and that subsurface rock is possible. The builder says, “I will take the chance and keep the fixed price.” The soil later contains hard rock, increasing the builder's cost substantially. May the builder avoid the contract for mistake?
Answer A: Yes, because both parties did not know the true soil condition.
Answer B: No, because the builder was aware of limited knowledge and accepted the risk before contracting.
Answer C: Yes, because increased cost always makes performance impracticable.
Answer D: No, but only because land-improvement contracts are governed by the UCC.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A party bears the risk of mistake when the party is aware of limited knowledge but treats that knowledge as sufficient. The builder knew the soil condition was uncertain and expressly took the chance in a fixed-price contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because lack of knowledge is not enough when a party consciously accepts the uncertainty. C: C is wrong because increased cost does not automatically excuse performance, especially where the party assumed the risk. D: D is wrong because construction services are not governed by Article 2 as a sale of goods.
lb_rule_reference: Conscious ignorance / risk allocation
