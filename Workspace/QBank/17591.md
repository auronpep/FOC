Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal arson prosecution, Deborah testifies that she never saw Daniel carry gasoline into the warehouse. Before trial, Deborah testified before a grand jury under oath that she saw Daniel carry two gasoline cans into the warehouse. Deborah is now on the stand and subject to cross-examination. The prosecutor offers the grand jury testimony. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Admit the grand jury testimony only to impeach Deborah because grand jury testimony is not a deposition.
Answer B: Admit the grand jury testimony both to impeach Deborah and to prove that Daniel carried the gasoline cans.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony because prior inconsistent statements are admissible only in civil cases.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony because Deborah is available to testify live.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Grand jury testimony is testimony under penalty of perjury in a proceeding. Because Deborah testifies at trial, is subject to cross-examination, and her grand jury testimony is inconsistent with her trial testimony, it qualifies as a federal nonhearsay prior inconsistent statement and may be used substantively as well as for impeachment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the federal rule includes prior statements under oath at trials, hearings, other proceedings, and depositions; it is not limited to depositions. C: C is wrong because the rule applies in criminal as well as civil cases. D: D is wrong because Deborah's live testimony and cross-examination opportunity are required for the prior-statement route.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 801(d)(1)(A) / grand jury prior inconsistent statement
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests recognition that grand jury testimony can satisfy the sworn-proceeding requirement.
