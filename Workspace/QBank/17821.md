Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: A retailer, Esther, orders 100 identical clay jars from a merchant seller, Noah. The order is silent about how the seller may accept. Noah promptly ships exactly 100 conforming jars without first sending a written acceptance. Esther receives the shipment but says there was no contract because Noah never promised to ship. Under Article 2, what is the best result?
Answer A: No contract formed because a seller must always promise before shipping.
Answer B: No contract formed because a written acceptance is required for every goods sale.
Answer C: No contract formed because shipment is performance, not acceptance.
Answer D: A contract formed because Noah accepted the goods order by promptly shipping conforming goods.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An order to buy goods generally invites acceptance by prompt shipment as well as by a prompt promise to ship, unless the offer unambiguously says otherwise. Noah accepted by shipping conforming goods.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because shipment can itself accept a goods order. B: B is wrong because a separate written acceptance is not always required for formation. C: C is wrong because under Article 2, shipment can be both acceptance and performance.
lb_rule_reference: UCC_goods
lb_student_diagnostic: Student missed that shipment can be an acceptance method under Article 2.
