Question: A delivery cart sold by a commercial retailer had a defective wheel assembly when sold. During ordinary use in a store aisle, the wheel broke off, the loaded cart tipped, and boxes struck a customer who had not bought or used the cart. Can the customer be a proper strict-products plaintiff?
Answer A: No, because the customer did not buy or use the cart
Answer B: Yes, because a foreseeable bystander physically injured by the defect may sue
Answer C: No, because strict products liability protects only economic expectations
Answer D: Yes, but only if the customer proves the retailer inspected negligently
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The defect made ordinary product use dangerous to nearby customers. The customer suffered physical injury within the product-risk field.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Foreseeable bystanders injured by defective products can be protected. Purchase and privity are not required. C: Strict products liability protects against tort-type physical harm to persons or property. D: Strict liability does not require negligent inspection by the retailer.
lb_rule_reference: Strict products liability can protect foreseeable users and bystanders injured by a defective product; privity or purchase is not required.
