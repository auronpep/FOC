Question: A woman and man were charged with murder. Each gave a confession to the police that implicated both of them. The woman later retracted her confession claiming that it was coerced. The woman and man were tried together. The prosecutor offered both confessions into evidence. The woman and man objected. After a hearing, the trial judge found that both confessions were voluntary and admitted both into evidence. The woman testified at trial, and the man did not. She claimed that her confession was false and the result of coercion. Both defendants were convicted. On appeal, the woman contends her conviction should be reversed because of the admission into evidence of the man's confession. The woman's contention is
Answer A: A: correct, because the man did not testify at trial.
Answer B: B: correct, whether or not the man testified at trial.
Answer C: C: incorrect, because the woman testified on her own behalf.
Answer D: D: incorrect, because the woman's own confession was properly admitted into evidence.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. Use of the non-testifying co-defendant's confession against the accused woman, without providing her the opportunity to confront him about the substance of the confession, violates her rights under the Confrontation Clause.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. If the man had testified, the woman would have had the opportunity to cross-examine him about the confession. Such an opportunity would satisfy the requirements of the Confrontation Clause. If he had testified, then the man's confession could have been properly used against the woman. C is incorrect. The woman must be afforded the opportunity to confront the man about the confession, beyond testifying herself. The woman's testimony on her own behalf is irrelevant to whether she had the opportunity to confront incriminating statements made by the non-testifying co-defendant. D is incorrect. Admission of the woman's confession does not prevent the constitutional violation, nor is it relevant to the fact that the non-testifying co-defendant's confession was used against her, in violation of the Confrontation Clause.
