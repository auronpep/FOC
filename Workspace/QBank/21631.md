Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: David signed a deed conveying Riveracre to Samuel as a gift. David personally delivered the deed to the county recorder with a written request that it be recorded immediately because “Riveracre now belongs to Samuel.” Samuel did not know about the deed until a week later, when he thanked David and kept the recorded copy. Did title pass to Samuel?
Answer A: No, because Samuel did not physically receive the deed before it was recorded.
Answer B: No, because Samuel did not pay consideration for Riveracre.
Answer C: Yes, because David's recording request showed present delivery intent and Samuel's acceptance of the beneficial deed was presumed and later confirmed.
Answer D: Yes, but only from the moment Samuel first learned about the deed, not from David's delivery for recording.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery and acceptance locks are present intent plus presumed acceptance. David's recording request stated that Riveracre belonged to Samuel now. A beneficial gift deed may be accepted without prior awareness, and Samuel later confirmed acceptance, so title passed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because physical receipt by the grantee is not always required. B: B is wrong because consideration is not required for a gift deed. D: D is wrong because later awareness confirms acceptance; it does not make grantor recording legally irrelevant where delivery intent already existed.
lb_rule_reference: Grantor recording can evidence delivery, and acceptance of a beneficial deed is presumed absent rejection
