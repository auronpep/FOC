Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: Nina sued Delta Hauling for injuries from a warehouse accident and lost after a full trial. Final judgment was entered for Delta Hauling. Delta Hauling later merged into Metro Logistics, which assumed Delta Hauling's assets and liabilities. Nina then sues Metro Logistics for the same injuries from the same warehouse accident. Is the second suit likely barred by claim preclusion?
Answer A: Yes, because Metro Logistics is a successor in privity with the prior defendant and the claim arises from the same transaction
Answer B: Yes, but only because issue preclusion applies to every fact from the first trial
Answer C: No, because Metro Logistics was not named in the first complaint
Answer D: No, because claim preclusion never applies to corporate successors
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Claim preclusion reaches parties and privies; a merger successor does not create a new transaction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The stronger bar is claim preclusion, and issue preclusion has separate actual-litigation and essentiality requirements. C: A formal name change or successor relationship can satisfy the privity requirement. D: Successor or privy status can support preclusion when due-process limits are satisfied.
lb_rule_reference: Claim preclusion can bind parties and their privies when a valid final merits judgment already resolved a claim arising from the same transaction.
