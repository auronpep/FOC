Question: In a federal car-crash trial, David offers a foam-board diagram of the intersection while his witness Leah explains where the vehicles were. The diagram is not drawn to scale, but Leah testifies that it fairly shows the relative positions she is describing. The opponent objects that the diagram is not the actual intersection. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Exclude the diagram because a demonstrative aid must be the real object it depicts.
Answer B: Allow the diagram to be used to illustrate Leah's testimony if the court finds it helpful and not misleading.
Answer C: Admit the diagram as conclusive proof of the exact distances between the vehicles.
Answer D: Exclude the diagram unless David proves the diagram satisfies the original-writing rule.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A diagram used to help a witness explain testimony is demonstrative or illustrative. It need not be the actual scene or drawn to scale if it is helpful and not misleading; the court can limit its use.
