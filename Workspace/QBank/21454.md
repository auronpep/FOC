Outline: Joinder of claims
Question: A plaintiff sues a delivery company for injuries from a collision. The company wants to bring in the truck manufacturer, alleging the manufacturer's defective brakes caused the plaintiff's injuries. The delivery company does not claim the manufacturer must indemnify or reimburse the company if the plaintiff wins. Is Rule 14 impleader proper on these facts?
Answer A: Yes, because any party the defendant blames may be impleaded
Answer B: Yes, because Rule 14 permits every related claim arising from the same accident
Answer C: No, because impleader requires a claim that the nonparty is or may be liable to the defending party for all or part of the plaintiff's claim
Answer D: No, because third-party practice is available only to plaintiffs
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Without indemnity, contribution, or similar derivative liability to the delivery company, Rule 14 impleader is not the correct device.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 14 requires derivative liability to the defendant, not merely direct liability to the plaintiff. B: Transactional relatedness alone is not enough for impleader. D: Defending parties commonly use Rule 14 as third-party plaintiffs.
lb_rule_reference: A defending party may implead a nonparty who is or may be liable to it for all or part of the plaintiff's claim, such as indemnity or contribution.
