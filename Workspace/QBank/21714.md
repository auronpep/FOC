Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Service of Process
Question: Congress creates a federal civil claim and expressly authorizes nationwide service of process for that claim. Plaintiff files the federal claim in a federal court in State A and serves Defendant, a State B corporation with extensive United States contacts but few State A contacts. Defendant argues that State A's long-arm statute does not reach it. What is the best analysis of the personal-jurisdiction hook?
Answer A: The court must rely only on State A's long-arm statute because every federal case uses the forum state's reach
Answer B: The federal nationwide-service statute may provide the authorization route, so the analysis is not limited to State A contacts
Answer C: Nationwide service means personal jurisdiction exists over any defendant anywhere in the world without due process limits
Answer D: The claim must be dismissed unless Plaintiff also proves diversity jurisdiction
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A federal statute authorizing nationwide service changes the Rule 4(k) route and prevents exclusive reliance on the state long-arm statute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 4(k)(1)(C) recognizes federal statutes that authorize service and jurisdiction. C: Statutory authorization does not eliminate constitutional limits. D: The facts already describe a federal statutory claim. The issue is personal jurisdiction and service authorization.
lb_rule_reference: When a federal statute authorizes nationwide service of process, Rule 4(k)(1)(C) can support personal jurisdiction, and the due-process focus is not simply the defendant's contacts with the forum state.
