Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Foreseeable plaintiffs
Question: A delivery company negligently flew a heavy package drone over a crowded sidewalk. The drone lost power and dropped its package toward pedestrians directly below. A pedestrian standing under the flight path dove aside and fractured a wrist. Was the pedestrian within the class of plaintiffs to whom the company owed a negligence duty?
Answer A: Yes, because people directly under the heavy drone were foreseeably endangered before the package fell
Answer B: No, because the pedestrian was not the package recipient
Answer C: No, because duty is decided only after seeing who was actually hit
Answer D: Yes, but only because drone flights are strictly liable activities
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The company's careless flight created a foreseeable risk of physical harm to pedestrians in the drop zone.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Negligence duty is not limited to the contracting recipient when nearby pedestrians are foreseeably endangered. C: Duty is assessed before the accident by foreseeable risk, not by hindsight injury. D: The call asks negligence duty. The pedestrian is foreseeable without turning the activity into strict liability.
lb_rule_reference: A negligence duty is owed to plaintiffs foreseeably within the risk field created before the accident.
