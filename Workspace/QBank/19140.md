Question: A newspaper published an editorial in which an editor asserted that a candidate for high political office was a user of illegal drugs. The accusation was untrue. The editor acted unreasonably in not investigating the accusation before publishing it; however, the editor honestly believed that the accusation was true. The candidate sued the editor for defamation. Is the candidate entitled to recover? Is the candidate entitled to recover?
Answer A: No, because the accusation appeared in an editorial and was, therefore, merely an opinion.
Answer B: No, because the editor honestly believed that the accusation was true.
Answer C: Yes, because calling someone an illegal drug user is defamatory per se.
Answer D: Yes, because the accusation was false and was injurious to the candidate's reputation.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. In a defamation action that involves a matter of public concern - here, a claim brought by a candidate for public office - the plaintiff must establish more than mere negligence with regard to the truth or falsity of the allegedly defamatory statement of fact. The plaintiff must establish that the defendant acted with actual malice. Here, the candidate cannot establish actual malice on the part of the editor in publishing the statement because the editor honestly believed the accusation was true.

additional_info: Defamation requires defamatory language, of or concerning the plaintiff, publication to a third person, and reputational damage. When the plaintiff is a public figure or the matter is of public concern, falsity and fault are also required. Under New York Times v. Sullivan, a public official or public-figure plaintiff must prove actual malice by showing knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard as to truth or falsity. Under Gertz, a private plaintiff in a public-concern case may recover on negligence with actual harm. This item turns on the public-candidate actual-malice gate: the editor was negligent in failing to investigate, but honestly believed the accusation was true, so the candidate does not recover.
