Outline: Defamation
Question: A magazine disliked a candidate for mayor and rushed a story falsely saying the candidate missed several council meetings. The reporter relied on an outdated attendance list and failed to call the candidate before publication. The reporter honestly believed the list was current and had no serious doubts about the story. The candidate sues for defamation. Does the candidate likely prove actual malice?
Answer A: Yes, because the magazine disliked the candidate
Answer B: Yes, because the reporter failed to investigate perfectly
Answer C: No, because the facts show negligence and ill will, but not knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard
Answer D: No, because candidates can never recover for false statements during campaigns
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The reporter relied on an outdated list and disliked the candidate, but honestly believed the list was current and had no serious doubts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Actual malice is knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard for truth, not dislike by itself. B: Failure to investigate can be negligence without actual malice unless the facts show serious doubts or reckless disregard. D: Candidates can recover if they prove falsity and actual malice. The problem here is insufficient actual malice.
lb_rule_reference: Actual malice means knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard for truth; poor investigation or ill will alone is not enough.
