Question: A state law makes it a crime to "hold or teach the belief that civil law is subordinate to divine law." The state argues it may regulate conduct inspired by that belief and therefore may also ban the belief itself. What is the best constitutional response?
Answer A: The law is unconstitutional because the government may not punish religious belief as such
Answer B: The law is valid if the state has a rational basis for discouraging unlawful conduct
Answer C: The law is valid because Free Exercise protects only organized churches
Answer D: The law is invalid only if it also violates the Establishment Clause
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Religious belief is absolutely protected against government punishment; conduct is analyzed separately.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The statute punishes belief itself, not conduct. C: Free Exercise protects individuals and religious beliefs, not only institutions. D: Punishing religious belief violates Free Exercise on its own.
lb_rule_reference: The government may not punish religious belief as such, but religiously motivated conduct can be regulated under applicable constitutional standards.
