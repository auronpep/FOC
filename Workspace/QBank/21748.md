Outline: Attorney-client
Question: Daniel sues Miriam in federal court under diversity jurisdiction for breach of a State A contract. State A law supplies the rule of decision and recognizes the attorney-client privilege. During federal discovery, Daniel's lawyer inadvertently produces one email in which Daniel asked his lawyer for legal advice about the contract. Before production, Daniel's lawyers used a reasonable privilege-screening process. The next day, after discovering the mistake, they notify Miriam and demand return of the email. Miriam argues that the production waives privilege over all communications on the same subject. How should the court analyze the privilege and waiver issues?
Answer A: State law governs both the existence of privilege and all waiver consequences, so Federal Rule of Evidence 502 has no role in the federal discovery dispute.
Answer B: State law supplies the initial privilege rule, but Rule 502 governs the waiver consequences of the disclosure in the federal proceeding; on these facts, the inadvertent disclosure should not operate as a waiver.
Answer C: Federal common law supplies the initial attorney-client privilege because the case is in federal court, and Rule 502 creates the privilege independently of state law.
Answer D: The production automatically waives the entire subject matter because an email was actually disclosed, even though the disclosure was inadvertent and promptly corrected.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 501 sends the initial privilege question to state law when state law supplies the rule of decision for the civil claim. Rule 502 separately governs waiver consequences for attorney-client or work-product material disclosed in a federal proceeding, and it applies notwithstanding Rule 501. Under Rule 502(b), an inadvertent disclosure does not operate as a waiver if the holder took reasonable steps to prevent disclosure and promptly took reasonable steps to rectify the error. The decisive axis is separating initial privilege from federal waiver effect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it treats state privilege law as controlling every waiver consequence. Rule 502 has a controlling federal waiver function in the circumstances it covers. C: C is wrong because Rule 502 does not create the underlying attorney-client privilege. It defines waiver consequences for communications or information covered by privilege or work-product protection. D: D is wrong because Rule 502 rejects automatic waiver for qualifying inadvertent disclosures and restricts subject-matter waiver to defined intentional-disclosure/fairness circumstances.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 + FRE 502
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats source law and disclosure waiver as one undifferentiated privilege rule.
