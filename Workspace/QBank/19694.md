MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Cause in fact (actual cause)
Question: Two recreational archers ignored range rules and fired arrows toward a target while a worker was still collecting arrows behind it. One arrow struck the worker in the shoulder. Both archers fired at nearly the same time, both used identical arrows from the same rental bin, and the injured worker cannot identify which archer's arrow hit him. The worker sues both archers for negligence, and both archers are before the court. In the worker's negligence claim against the archers, how should the court treat causation?
Answer A: The worker must lose because he cannot identify the exact archer whose arrow hit him
Answer B: The burden may shift to each archer to prove that his arrow did not cause the injury
Answer C: Each archer is liable only for half of the wound because causation is uncertain
Answer D: The worker may use market-share liability because the arrows were identical
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Both defendants acted negligently, both are before the court, and one caused the indivisible injury. Those facts fit alternative liability, allowing the burden to shift to the defendants.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Alternative liability exists for the narrow case where all defendants before the court acted negligently, one caused the indivisible injury, and plaintiff cannot identify which one. C: The issue is causal uncertainty, not apportionment of a divisible injury. Alternative liability shifts the causation burden; it does not automatically divide a single wound into shares. D: Market-share liability is a narrow products doctrine usually tied to fungible products and market shares. This is a simultaneous-negligence alternative-liability problem, not a product-market allocation case.
lb_rule_reference: When all defendants before the court acted negligently and one caused an indivisible injury, alternative liability may shift the burden to defendants to prove they did not cause the harm.
