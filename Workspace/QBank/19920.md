internal_id: TR-061
Outline_code: 61020401
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Cause in fact (actual cause)
Question: A grocery store employee carelessly left a stack of empty crates in a narrow aisle. A customer walking down the aisle saw the crates, stepped around them, and did not touch them. At that same moment, a ceiling sprinkler pipe that had been properly installed and inspected burst without warning, dumping water onto the floor. The customer slipped on the sudden water and broke her wrist. The evidence shows that the customer would have slipped on the water even if the crates had never been in the aisle. Is the customer likely to prevail in a negligence claim against the grocery store based on the crates?
Answer A: Yes, because leaving crates in a narrow customer aisle was negligent conduct
Answer B: Yes, because the sprinkler burst was not the customer's fault
Answer C: No, because the crates were not an actual cause of the customer's fall
Answer D: No, because the store owed no duty to customers in its aisles
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The undisputed facts show the customer would have slipped on the sudden water even if the crates had not been there. Because the breach did not change the outcome, the crates were not a but-for cause, and no multiple-sufficient-cause exception applies.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Negligence requires duty, breach, actual cause, proximate cause, and damages. A breach is not enough when the facts show the same injury would have occurred at the same time without the crates. B: Plaintiff innocence does not prove defendant causation. The customer still must show that the store's breach caused the injury. D: A store generally owes customers reasonable care regarding aisle conditions. The defect in the claim is actual cause, not duty.
lb_rule_reference: A negligent breach is not an actual cause of harm if the same harm would have occurred at the same time even without the breach; substantial-factor analysis is reserved for multiple sufficient causes.
