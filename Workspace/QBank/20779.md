Question: A seller sues Caleb for breach of an alleged oral contract to buy 100 bronze lamps for $10,000. In sworn testimony, Caleb admits, “I agreed to buy 40 bronze lamps, but never 100.” No writing exists and no goods were delivered or paid for. Under the UCC Statute of Frauds, what is the maximum quantity enforceable based on Caleb's admission?
Answer A: None, because admissions never satisfy the goods Statute of Frauds.
Answer B: All 100 lamps, because Caleb admitted that some contract existed.
Answer C: 40 lamps, because enforcement based on an admission is limited to the quantity admitted.
Answer D: 100 lamps, but only if the seller proves the oral quantity by clear and convincing evidence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A goods contract that lacks a sufficient writing can be enforceable to the extent the party against whom enforcement is sought admits in court that a contract was made. But enforcement cannot exceed the quantity admitted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the UCC recognizes an admission exception. B: B is wrong because admitting some quantity does not open enforcement for the full alleged oral quantity. D: D is wrong because higher proof of the oral quantity does not bypass the admitted-quantity cap.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(3)(b) — admission exception
