


BARMATRIX Q#: 22134
Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: An elderly woman entered into a written lease for a house. Because she was elderly, the woman insisted that the lease include a provision that required the landlord to make any necessary repairs within a reasonable time. Three months into the lease, a tree limb fell and hit the house's front steps, causing them to crumble. The woman informed the landlord that the steps were broken and she did not feel safe leaving the house. After two weeks, the landlord had done nothing. The local news reported a hurricane was approaching, so the woman decided that she needed to go to the store to get milk and bread. When she tried to go down the front steps, she fell and broke her hip. The woman sued the landlord for her injuries. Is the woman entitled to damages?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the landlord agreed to make any necessary repairs within a reasonable time.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the steps were broken by an act of God.
Answer C: C: No, because the woman assumed the risk by using the stairs.
Answer D: D: No, because the landlord did not cause the crumbling of the steps.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Generally, a landlord is not responsible for injuries caused by the premises' condition. However, if the landlord has made a covenant to repair, he or she is responsible for those injuries. Here, the landlord breached the lease agreement by not repairing the steps, so the woman can sue him under the breach itself and because she was injured by a condition of the premises. B is incorrect because the fact that the steps were broken by the falling branch would not change the analysis. C is incorrect because the landlord breached his promise to repair. D is incorrect because it doesn't matter whether the landlord caused the steps to crumble.
