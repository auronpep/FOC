Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Abraham owned Lot 7 in Canaan County. On ordinary notebook paper, Abraham wrote, “I convey Lot 7 in Canaan County to Sarah,” signed his name, and handed the paper to Sarah intending to transfer the land immediately. Sarah accepted the paper but paid nothing. The paper was not notarized or recorded. Who owns Lot 7?
Answer A: Sarah, because the writing contained the required deed formalities and was delivered with present intent.
Answer B: Abraham, because Sarah paid no consideration for the conveyance.
Answer C: Abraham, because the paper was not notarized or recorded.
Answer D: Abraham, because a deed must be on a formal printed deed form.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is deed formalities. The writing identifies the grantor and grantee, uses present words of transfer, describes the property, is signed by Abraham, and was delivered with present intent. Consideration, notarization, formal deed paper, and recording are not validity requirements between the parties, so title passes to Sarah.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because consideration is not required for deed validity. C: C is wrong because acknowledgment and recording affect proof, recordability, or priority, not validity between Abraham and Sarah. D: D is wrong because ordinary paper can satisfy the writing requirement if the deed terms are present.
lb_rule_reference: Deed formalities / no consideration
