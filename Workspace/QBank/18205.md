Outline: Psychotherapist-patient
Question: Solomon attended a workplace seminar led by Amos, a motivational coach who was not licensed as a mental-health professional and was not providing therapy. During a break, Solomon told Amos, “I moved company funds into my account.” In a federal fraud case, Solomon claims psychotherapist-patient privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection, because the conversation was not a confidential psychotherapy communication with a covered treatment professional.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because any mental-health-related setting is privileged.
Answer C: Sustain the objection, because Solomon subjectively felt stressed when he spoke.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Amos later repeated the statement to the employer.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Psychotherapist-patient privilege requires a protected therapeutic relationship and confidential treatment communication. A statement to an unlicensed motivational coach at a workplace seminar does not fit that route. The decisive axis is professional role and treatment purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because This is the medical/mental-health setting shortcut. The setting alone does not create privilege. C: C is wrong because The speaker's stress does not substitute for a protected therapy relationship. D: D is wrong because Later repetition is not the reason the privilege fails; it failed at the relationship and purpose gates.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / treatment-purpose boundary
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating therapeutic flavor or workplace counseling language as enough for psychotherapist privilege.
