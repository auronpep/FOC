Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Asher bought goods from Tamar for resale. Tamar failed to deliver. Asher reasonably spent $900 inspecting substitute goods and $600 transporting replacement goods after cover. Asher labels these amounts “reliance damages.” What is the best classification of these amounts under Article 2?
Answer A: Reliance damages, because Asher spent money after relying on the contract.
Answer B: Restitution, because Tamar received a benefit from Asher's inspection and transportation.
Answer C: Liquidated damages, because the amounts are specific and easy to calculate.
Answer D: Incidental damages, because they are reasonable expenses connected with inspection, transportation, and effecting cover after the seller's breach.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Article 2, reasonable expenses incurred in inspection, transportation, care, custody, or effecting cover after a seller's breach are incidental damages. They may be recoverable, but the better classification is incidental damages, not reliance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the statutory goods remedy more precisely classifies these as incidental damages. B: B is wrong because Tamar did not receive a benefit from Asher's expenses. C: C is wrong because calculability does not make damages liquidated.
