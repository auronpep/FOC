internal_id: TR-253

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Affirmative duties to act
Question: A hiker saw an adult stranger slip into a shallow pond and struggle. The hiker had not caused the danger, had no special relationship with the stranger, and did not begin a rescue. The hiker could have thrown a nearby rope with little risk but walked away. The stranger was injured and sues the hiker for negligence. Is the hiker liable for failing to rescue?
Answer A: Yes, because a reasonable person would have thrown the rope
Answer B: Yes, because ability to help always creates a duty
Answer C: No, because no affirmative-duty exception applies
Answer D: No, because negligence never applies to omissions
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The hiker did not create the peril, start a rescue, have a special relationship, or violate a supplied statute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Negligence requires a legal duty. The facts supply no affirmative-duty exception. B: Ability alone does not create a legal duty to rescue a stranger. D: Omissions can be negligent when an affirmative-duty exception applies. None applies here.
lb_rule_reference: A person generally has no duty to rescue or aid a stranger absent risk creation, undertaking, special relationship, statute, or another recognized exception.
