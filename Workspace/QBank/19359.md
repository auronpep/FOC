internal_id: TR-052
Outline_code: 61020301
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Falling below the standard of care
Question: A lawyer agreed to file a client's appeal. Expert testimony established that comparable lawyers use a docketing system that flags appeal deadlines twice. The lawyer kept the deadline only on a sticky note, misplaced it, and filed the appeal one week late. The appeal was dismissed. The client sues the lawyer for professional negligence. Is the client likely to establish breach in the professional negligence claim against the lawyer?
Answer A: Yes, because expert evidence shows the lawyer fell below the care used by comparable lawyers
Answer B: No, because a lawyer is liable only when the lawyer intentionally harms the client
Answer C: No, because courts judge lawyers only by ordinary lay common sense, not expert professional practice
Answer D: Yes, because every lost appeal automatically proves the lawyer was negligent
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The professional standard is supplied by comparable lawyers' accepted care. Missing the appeal deadline after using a plainly deficient tracking method can establish breach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Professional negligence does not require intent. It requires falling below the applicable professional standard and causing harm. C: Professionals are judged by the knowledge, skill, and care of comparable professionals. Expert testimony can establish that standard. D: A lost appeal alone is not automatic malpractice. The correct reason is the lawyer's breach of the professional docketing standard.
lb_rule_reference: A professional must use the knowledge, skill, and care of comparable professionals; breach is shown by falling below that professional standard, not by ordinary lay preference.
