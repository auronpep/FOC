Question: Naomi conveyed Greenacre “to Ruth so long as Ruth operates a bakery on the land during Ruth's life, and if Ruth stops operating a bakery during Ruth's life, then to Boaz.” Ruth later stopped operating the bakery and converted the property into a residence. Who owns Greenacre after Ruth stops operating the bakery?
Answer A: Naomi, because “so long as” always leaves a possibility of reverter in the grantor.
Answer B: Ruth, unless Naomi exercises a right of entry.
Answer C: Ruth, because the bakery language is merely a statement of purpose.
Answer D: Boaz, because the deed gave the future interest to a third party and the estate automatically shifted.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Boaz owns Greenacre. Although “so long as” is durational language, the deed does not retain the future interest in Naomi. It gives the future interest to Boaz, a third party. Ruth therefore had a fee simple subject to executory limitation, and title shifted automatically to Boaz when the condition occurred.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a possibility of reverter belongs to the grantor, and Naomi did not keep the future interest. B: B is wrong because no right of entry is reserved to Naomi. C: C is wrong because the deed contains operative limitation language and an express gift-over.
