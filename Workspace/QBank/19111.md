Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: A Texas consultant entered a three-year service agreement with a Kansas software company. The consultant negotiated with Kansas executives, agreed to weekly calls with Kansas engineers, sent monthly reports to Kansas, used Kansas-hosted systems, and was paid from Kansas. The consultant allegedly breached by failing to provide promised integration work. The software company sued in Kansas. Kansas's long-arm statute reaches the constitutional limit. Does the consultant have a strong personal-jurisdiction objection?
Answer A: Yes, because a contract with an out-of-state party can never create forum contacts
Answer B: Yes, because the consultant never physically entered Kansas
Answer C: No, because the consultant deliberately entered a continuing relationship centered on Kansas and the claim arises from that relationship
Answer D: No, but only because diversity jurisdiction exists
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The consultant's ongoing Kansas-directed duties, negotiations, reporting, systems use, and payment stream support purposeful availment for a breach claim arising from that relationship.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The rule is not categorical. The ongoing relationship, future obligations, and performance directed to Kansas matter. B: Physical entry is not required when the defendant deliberately creates continuing forum obligations. D: SMJ does not establish PJ.
lb_rule_reference: Specific jurisdiction may rest on prior negotiations, contemplated future consequences, contract terms, and actual course of dealing connecting the defendant to the forum.
