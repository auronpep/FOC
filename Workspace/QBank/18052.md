Question: In a federal assault trial, the government offers a knife allegedly found near Jacob shortly after the attack. An officer testifies that she marked the knife and placed it in an evidence bag. Another officer testifies that he retrieved the marked bag from the evidence locker the next morning. Jacob objects because no witness testifies about who moved the bag from the patrol car to the locker. How should the court treat the objection?
Answer A: Exclude the knife because any gap in chain of custody destroys admissibility.
Answer B: Admit the knife if the evidence is sufficient to support a finding that it is the same item, leaving gaps for the jury to weigh.
Answer C: Admit the knife only if the judge finds beyond a reasonable doubt that no tampering occurred.
Answer D: Exclude the knife because physical evidence requires a perfect chain of custody.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court screens for a sufficient basis from which a reasonable juror could find the item is what the proponent claims. Minor chain gaps usually affect weight, not admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and D are wrong because a perfect chain is not required. C is wrong because the judge does not apply a beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard to authenticate physical evidence. C: C is wrong because the judge does not apply a beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard to authenticate physical evidence. D: A and D are wrong because a perfect chain is not required. C is wrong because the judge does not apply a beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard to authenticate physical evidence.
