Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Nonresponsive answers
Question: In a federal negligence trial, Eve calls Noah to testify about a collision with Adam. Eve's lawyer asks, “What color was the traffic light when Adam entered the intersection?” Noah answers, “It was red for Adam, and Adam had told me the day before that he never obeys traffic lights.” Adam immediately moves to strike the statement about what he had said the day before. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant the motion as to the volunteered statement about Adam's prior comment, while leaving the answer about the light in evidence.
Answer B: Grant the motion and strike Noah's entire testimony because part of one answer went beyond the question.
Answer C: Deny the motion because any answer given by a witness must remain before the jury once spoken.
Answer D: Deny the motion because the question was not leading.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A timely motion to strike can remove a nonresponsive volunteered portion of an answer, especially when that portion injects inadmissible or unfairly prejudicial matter. Noah's answer about the light responded to the question; the additional statement about Adam's prior comment went beyond the question and is the portion to strike.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the remedy should be tailored; the responsive part of the answer need not be stricken. C: C is wrong because inadmissible or nonresponsive matter can be struck after an answer. D: D is wrong because the issue is the witness's volunteered answer, not the form of the question.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(1); FRE 611(a) / partial motion to strike
