Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: Martha sues a store after a metal shelf bracket broke. Before the store discarded the bracket, Martha's investigator photographed it from several angles. The investigator testifies that the photos accurately show the bracket as he saw it that day. The store objects that the actual bracket is the best evidence and must be produced. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the original physical object is always required before photographs may be used.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Martha proves the store discarded the bracket in bad faith.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the original-writing rule does not require the physical object when authenticated photos are offered to show its appearance.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the investigator is qualified as a metallurgy expert.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The original-writing rule applies when proving the contents of a writing, recording, or photograph. It does not require production of a physical object as the 'original.' The investigator's testimony can authenticate the photos as accurate depictions of the bracket.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a physical object is not a writing, recording, or photograph whose contents are being proved. B: B is wrong because bad faith is not a condition for using authenticated photographs to show the object's appearance. D: D is wrong because the investigator need not be an expert to authenticate photos he took and observed.
