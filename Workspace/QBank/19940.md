Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Rivermark Tools is incorporated and headquartered in Ohio. To sell tools in Nevada, Rivermark registered to do business and appointed a Nevada agent for service of process. The Nevada registration statute says only that registered companies must maintain an agent for service; it does not state that registration is consent to general jurisdiction. A plaintiff sued Rivermark in Nevada over an accident that occurred in Ohio involving a tool bought in Ohio. Does the service-agent appointment automatically create general jurisdiction in Nevada?
Answer A: Yes, because appointing a service agent always consents to all-purpose jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because any company selling in Nevada is at home there
Answer C: No, the appointment makes service possible but does not automatically establish general jurisdiction for an unrelated Ohio accident
Answer D: No, because service on a registered agent is always invalid
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The statute only required an agent for service. Without clear consent or at-home status, Nevada lacks all-purpose jurisdiction for an unrelated Ohio claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Service and jurisdiction are related but distinct. The statute did not clearly convert registration into all-purpose consent. B: Ordinary business registration and sales do not make a corporation at home. D: Service on a properly appointed agent can be valid; the defect is treating service as automatic general jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: Service mechanics and personal-jurisdiction consent are separate; a registered agent may make service effective without automatically creating general jurisdiction for unrelated claims.
