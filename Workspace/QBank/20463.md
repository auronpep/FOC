Question: Isaac owned a pasture but was traveling far away. By telephone, Isaac told Jacob, “Sign my name to a deed conveying the pasture to Leah.” Jacob signed a deed, “Isaac, by Jacob as agent,” delivered it to Leah, and Leah accepted. Isaac had never given Jacob written authority to sign a deed. Who owns the pasture?
Answer A: Isaac, because an agent who executes a deed for a landowner must have written authority.
Answer B: Leah, because Isaac orally authorized Jacob to sign the deed.
Answer C: Leah, because the deed named Isaac as grantor and Jacob as agent.
Answer D: Leah only if she paid value and had no notice of the missing written authority.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed signed through an agent, and the decisive validity gate is the agent's authority to execute a land conveyance. Authority to sign a deed for the owner must be in writing. Jacob had only oral authority, so his signature did not bind Isaac and title remains with Isaac.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because ordinary oral agency is not enough for executing a deed. C: C is wrong because labeling Jacob as agent does not supply written authority from Isaac. D: D is wrong because BFP and recording ideas cannot cure a deed-signing authority defect at the validity gate.
lb_rule_reference: Written authority required for agent execution of deed
