Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Judgments and Verdicts
Question: A buyer sued a seller in federal court for breach of contract. The case was tried without a jury and was based solely on documentary evidence. At the close of evidence, the judge stated, “Judgment shall be entered for the buyer.” The judgment was so entered. What would be the seller's best argument for persuading an appellate court to reverse the court's judgment?
Answer A: A: The court erred by not providing findings and conclusions.
Answer B: B: The court's judgment was clearly erroneous because it was based solely on documentary evidence.
Answer C: C: The court erred by not giving the seller an opportunity to submit proposed findings and conclusions.
Answer D: D: The court erred by not requiring the buyer to submit proposed findings and conclusions.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Fed. R. Civ. P. 52(a)(1) requires a court trying an action on the facts without a jury to “find the facts specially and state its conclusions of law separately.” Here, the court entered judgment without providing anything. B is incorrect because the judgment could be based solely on documentary evidence. C and D are incorrect because the court must provide the findings and conclusions itself.
