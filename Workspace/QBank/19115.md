Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Solicitation
Question: Kim and Lou drove together to a convenience store. Lou told Kim he was going to rob the place. Kim said "go ahead — I've got your back" and remained in the car as a lookout. Lou entered the store and robbed the clerk at gunpoint. Kim drove Lou away when Lou returned to the car. Kim is charged as an accomplice to robbery. Which theory best supports Kim's accomplice liability?
Answer A: Kim is not liable because she never entered the store or touched the clerk
Answer B: Kim is liable because she was present at the scene and thus automatically complicit
Answer C: Kim is liable because she verbally encouraged Lou, acted as a lookout, and drove the getaway car
Answer D: Kim is liable only as a principal because her getaway driving was the most important contribution
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Accomplice liability requires that the defendant, with intent to promote or facilitate the offense, aided, counseled, or encouraged the principal. Kim satisfies all elements: (1) she verbally encouraged Lou before the robbery ("go ahead — I've got your back"), demonstrating intent that the crime occur; (2) she served as a lookout, providing concrete assistance; (3) she drove the getaway car, facilitating escape. Any one of these acts, with the required intent, would suffice. The combination makes the case clear.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Accomplice liability does not require direct physical participation. Assistance by remaining outside as a lookout and providing words of encouragement ("I've got your back") before and during the crime, coupled with the getaway driving, constitutes aiding and abetting. Physical presence at the point of crime is not required. B: Mere presence at the scene of a crime — even knowing a crime is occurring — is insufficient for accomplice liability. The law requires active assistance or encouragement. Kim's liability flows from her verbal encouragement, her lookout role, and the getaway driving — not from mere presence. D: Kim cannot be a principal-in-the-first-degree because she was not the direct perpetrator who committed the actus reus of the robbery (the confrontation and demand). She is most accurately charged as a principal in the second degree (or accomplice/aider and abettor). The importance of her contribution doesn't change the category.
lb_rule_reference: A person is liable as an accomplice if, with intent to promote or facilitate the offense, he aids, abets, counsels, or encourages the principal; mere presence at the scene is insufficient, but verbal encouragement during the commission of the crime establishes accomplice liability
