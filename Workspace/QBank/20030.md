Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: A federal diversity action is filed in the District of North Franklin. The defendant properly impleads a third-party defendant under Rule 14. The third-party defendant lives in a neighboring state and is served 72 miles from the federal courthouse. The forum state's long-arm statute would not otherwise reach the third-party defendant. The third-party defendant moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. What is the best response?
Answer A: The motion must be granted because federal courts can only use the forum state's long-arm statute
Answer B: The motion should be denied if the party was joined under Rule 14 and served within 100 miles of the issuing court
Answer C: The motion should be denied because the 100-mile bulge applies to every defendant in every federal case
Answer D: The motion should be granted because a third-party defendant can never be subject to personal jurisdiction
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts fit Rule 4(k)(1)(B): Rule 14 party, service within 100 miles, and federal-court action.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 4(k)(1)(B) creates a separate 100-mile path for certain Rule 14 and Rule 19 parties. C: The rule is limited to specified joined parties, not original defendants generally. D: Rule 14 parties can be within PJ through ordinary or special Rule 4(k) paths.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 4(k)(1)(B) can establish jurisdiction over a party joined under Rule 14 or Rule 19 who is served within 100 miles of where the summons issued.
