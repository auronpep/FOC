Question: A state follows the traditional livestock-trespass rule. A rancher's cattle escaped through a gate that an unknown person had left open and wandered onto a neighbor's vegetable field, destroying a row of crops. The neighbor sues the rancher for strict liability. What is the likely result?
Answer A: The neighbor recovers because the state imposes strict liability for foreseeable crop damage from trespassing livestock
Answer B: The neighbor loses unless the rancher personally left the gate open
Answer C: The neighbor loses because cattle are domestic animals
Answer D: The neighbor recovers only if the cattle had previously damaged crops
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The stated rule places the loss on the livestock owner for property damage from straying livestock.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Personal fault is not required under the traditional livestock-trespass strict-liability rule stated in the facts. C: The livestock-trespass rule is a distinct animal-liability rule for property damage. D: The claim rests on trespassing livestock, not on a known dangerous propensity of a particular animal.
lb_rule_reference: In jurisdictions retaining the livestock-trespass rule, an owner may be strictly liable for foreseeable property damage caused when livestock stray onto another's land.
