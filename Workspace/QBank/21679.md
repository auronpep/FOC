Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal negligence trial, Naomi testifies that she never complained about the store's loose stair before her fall. The defendant immediately calls Silas, who will testify that Naomi said the week before the fall, “I told the manager the stair was loose.” Naomi was not asked about that alleged statement before Silas was called. The plaintiff objects. What should the court do?
Answer A: Admit Silas's testimony immediately because any inconsistent statement may be proved by another witness.
Answer B: Exclude Silas's testimony unless Naomi is first given an opportunity to explain or deny the statement and the plaintiff has an opportunity to examine her about it, unless the court orders otherwise.
Answer C: Exclude Silas's testimony permanently because extrinsic evidence of a prior inconsistent statement is always barred.
Answer D: Admit Silas's testimony only if Naomi's alleged statement was made under oath.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Extrinsic evidence of a witness's prior inconsistent statement generally may not be admitted until the witness has an opportunity to explain or deny the statement and the adverse party has an opportunity to examine the witness about it, unless the court orders otherwise. Naomi has not yet been given that opportunity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 613(b) controls the timing of extrinsic proof. C: C is wrong because extrinsic proof can be admitted when the rule's opportunity requirements are met. D: D is wrong because oath is not required for impeachment use; oath matters for substantive use under Rule 801(d)(1)(A).
lb_rule_reference: FRE 613(b) / extrinsic evidence opportunity requirement
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests extrinsic prior-inconsistent-statement timing under current Rule 613(b).
