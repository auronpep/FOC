Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Acceptable testimony
Question: Daniel is charged with entering a locked warehouse on Thursday night. The prosecutor offers testimony that Daniel was seen outside the same warehouse late Wednesday night, looking through the front window. Daniel objects that being there on Wednesday does not prove he entered on Thursday. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence, because conduct on Wednesday cannot be relevant to conduct on Thursday.
Answer B: Admit the evidence, because Daniel's presence and interest in the warehouse the night before has some tendency to show opportunity or preparation.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence unless the witness saw Daniel carrying burglary tools.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if the prosecutor proves Daniel returned on Thursday.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Daniel's presence and conduct near the warehouse shortly before the charged entry make opportunity or preparation somewhat more probable. The evidence is weak, but that weakness goes to weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because nearby-in-time conduct can have a logical link to opportunity or preparation. C: C is wrong because additional incriminating facts are not required for basic relevance. D: D is wrong because the evidence need not independently prove the charged entry.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is demanding that opportunity evidence prove the ultimate event.
