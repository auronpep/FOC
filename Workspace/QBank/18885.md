MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Trespass to chattel
Question: At a conference table, Attendee picked up another person's phone without permission, glanced at the lock screen for three seconds to check the time, and immediately put it back. The phone was not moved from the table, damaged, unlocked, or made unavailable to the owner. The owner sues for trespass to chattels. Is the owner likely to prevail?
Answer A: Yes, because any intentional touching of another's chattel is actionable
Answer B: Yes, because the owner would have been offended if she saw the touching
Answer C: No, because the brief harmless glance caused no dispossession, impairment, deprivation, or harm
Answer D: No, because only land can be trespassed on
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The phone remained available and undamaged. The facts show no meaningful interference with the chattel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Trespass to chattels requires some legally significant interference, such as dispossession, impairment, deprivation of use, or harm. B: Trespass to chattels protects chattel interests, not personal dignity alone. D: Trespass to chattels exists. This claim fails because the interference was too slight.
lb_rule_reference: Trespass to chattels requires dispossession, impairment, deprivation of use, or harm; a brief harmless touching or use may be insufficient.
