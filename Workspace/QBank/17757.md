Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: A plaintiff files a federal complaint alleging, "The defendant acted unlawfully, violated my rights, and caused me damages." The complaint names no transaction, date, conduct, duty, or injury facts. The defendant moves to dismiss under Rule 12(b)(6), arguing that the complaint is only a legal conclusion. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the motion because federal notice pleading never requires factual allegations
Answer B: Grant the motion because the complaint contains only conclusory labels
Answer C: Grant the motion only if the court lacks subject matter jurisdiction
Answer D: Convert the motion to summary judgment because the plaintiff has no evidence yet
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 12(b)(6) tests whether the complaint states a plausible claim. This complaint gives no factual basis from which the court can infer liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 8 is liberal, but it still requires factual content showing a plausible entitlement to relief. A bare legal conclusion does not satisfy that standard. C: The stated motion is failure to state a claim, not lack of subject matter jurisdiction. A complaint can be jurisdictionally proper and still fail Rule 8. D: Rule 12(b)(6) tests pleaded facts, not proof. No conversion is needed when the court evaluates the complaint itself.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 8(a) requires a short and plain statement showing entitlement to relief; labels, conclusions, and naked assertions are not enough under the federal plausibility standard.
