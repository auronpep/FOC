Question: David is charged with assault. Before arrest, David privately gave his lawyer, Sarah, a bloody knife and said, “This is the knife I used.” The prosecution subpoenas Sarah for the knife but does not ask Sarah to repeat David's statement. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Require production of the knife, because the physical object is not itself a privileged communication, while David's statement to Sarah remains protected absent an exception.
Answer B: Quash the subpoena entirely, because anything a client gives a lawyer for legal advice becomes privileged.
Answer C: Require Sarah to produce the knife and testify that David said he used it, because physical evidence destroys privilege for all related statements.
Answer D: Quash the subpoena unless the prosecution proves it cannot obtain another knife by other means.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications, not preexisting physical evidence. Sarah may not be compelled to disclose the privileged statement merely because she received the knife, but the knife itself is not transformed into privileged matter. The decisive axis is object versus communication.
