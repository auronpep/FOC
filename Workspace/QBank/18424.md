Question: Samuel granted an electric company “an easement to place and maintain underground electric lines within the five-foot strip along the west boundary of Samuel's land.” The company later placed a large above-ground transformer twenty feet east of the strip because that location was easier for its workers to reach. The transformer was not needed to keep the underground lines within the five-foot strip functioning. Samuel sues to require removal of the transformer. Which is the best result?
Answer A: The company may keep the transformer because utility easements always cover whatever land is convenient for the utility.
Answer B: The company may keep the transformer because underground-line language includes all above-ground equipment.
Answer C: Samuel should prevail because the grant fixed a five-foot underground-line easement and the transformer is outside that scope.
Answer D: Samuel should prevail only if the transformer destroys all economic value of his land.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When an express easement specifies both purpose and location, the holder must stay within those limits unless the grant or applicable law provides otherwise. A transformer outside the five-foot underground-line strip and unnecessary to that line exceeds the granted scope.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because utility convenience does not rewrite a fixed easement. B: B is wrong because underground-line language does not automatically authorize unrelated above-ground equipment outside the strip. C is correct because the grant controls location and purpose. D: D is wrong because this is an easement-scope dispute, not a takings claim.
lb_rule_reference: Specified location / equipment outside strip
