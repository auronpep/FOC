Question: Mary signed and delivered a deed labeled “Gift Deed” conveying her garden to Elizabeth. The deed contained present transfer words, accurately described the garden, and Elizabeth accepted. Mary later argued that a gift deed can pass only an unenforceable promise unless the grantee pays value. Who owns the garden?
Answer A: Mary, because a deed labeled “Gift Deed” does not transfer legal title.
Answer B: Mary, because Elizabeth paid no value and therefore cannot receive title.
Answer C: Elizabeth, because a gift deed can pass legal title if the required deed formalities and delivery are satisfied.
Answer D: Elizabeth only if the gift deed is later replaced by a warranty deed.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a gift deed, and the decisive deed-type lock is whether it satisfies ordinary deed validity and delivery requirements. Mary signed and delivered a deed with present transfer words and a sufficient description, and Elizabeth accepted. The gift label and lack of value do not prevent legal title from passing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a gift deed can be an effective conveyance. B: B is wrong because grantee value is not required for deed validity. D: D is wrong because a later warranty deed is unnecessary to validate the gift deed.
lb_rule_reference: Gift deed can pass legal title when delivered
