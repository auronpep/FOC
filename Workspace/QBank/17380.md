Question: Noah owned Lot 1, which had an express recorded easement over Abigail's Lot 2. During a friendly conversation, Noah told Abigail, “I probably will not use the easement anymore.” Noah did not sign a release, did not block his own access, and did not otherwise act inconsistently with keeping the easement. Abigail later claimed that Noah had released the easement. Is the easement terminated by release?
Answer A: Yes, because any oral statement of future nonuse releases an easement.
Answer B: Yes, because nonuse alone is abandonment as a matter of law.
Answer C: No, because release of an easement generally requires a written release or equivalent legally sufficient act, and Noah's casual oral statement was not enough.
Answer D: No, because an express recorded easement can never be terminated.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement may be released, but because it is an interest in land, an express release generally must satisfy the statute of frauds or be supported by another legally sufficient termination doctrine. Noah made only a casual oral statement and took no abandonment-type action.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a casual oral statement is not an effective release. B: B is wrong because nonuse alone does not establish abandonment. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because express recorded easements can terminate through release, merger, abandonment, expiration, or other recognized routes.
lb_rule_reference: Release of easement / statute of frauds
lb_student_diagnostic: The recurring trap is treating loose oral language as either formal release or automatic abandonment.
