Question: A state senate plan gives one rural district 60,000 residents and one urban district 190,000 residents. The state offers no coherent administrative, geographic, or representational justification for the population gap. What is the strongest Equal Protection objection?
Answer A: The plan violates one-person-one-vote principles because state legislative districts must have substantially equal populations
Answer B: The claim is a political question because all districting cases are nonjusticiable
Answer C: The plan receives rational basis because rural voters are not a suspect class
Answer D: The plan is valid if each district elects exactly one senator
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A large unexplained population deviation dilutes votes in the larger district.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Malapportionment and one-person-one-vote claims are justiciable. C: The issue is vote dilution and population equality, not suspect-class status. D: Equal Protection also requires substantially equal population so votes carry roughly equal weight.
lb_rule_reference: Equal Protection requires substantially equal population in state legislative districts; large unjustified deviations violate one-person-one-vote principles.
