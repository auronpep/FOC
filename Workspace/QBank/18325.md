Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Andrew owned a shop. He signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey the shop to Peter son of Jonah.” The intended grantee's legal name was Simon Peter, and he was commonly known in the town as Peter son of Jonah. No other person fit that description. Simon Peter accepted the deed. Who owns the shop?
Answer A: Simon Peter, because the deed identifies him with reasonable certainty.
Answer B: Andrew, because the deed did not use Simon Peter's complete legal name.
Answer C: Andrew, because a nickname or known name can never identify a grantee.
Answer D: Simon Peter only if he records an affidavit explaining the name difference.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantee identity. A grantee may be identified by a known name or description if the person is reasonably certain. The phrase “Peter son of Jonah” uniquely identifies Simon Peter, so the name variation does not prevent title from passing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because complete legal names are not always required. C: C is wrong because a known name or descriptive identifier can be enough. D: D is wrong because recording an explanatory affidavit is not a condition to validity between Andrew and Simon Peter.
lb_rule_reference: Grantee identity / alias or known name
