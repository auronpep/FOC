


BARMATRIX Q#: 22743
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A woman was traveling cross country when she stopped at a fine art painting shop in State A. She saw a painting signed by a famous artist she greatly admired, who had passed away recently at his home in State B. The store owner saw the woman admiring the painting and offered to sell it to her for 10 percent less than the stickered price. The woman refused, saying she'd love to buy it, but she was still a poor student just traveling through on her way to State C. The owner asked for the woman's phone number in case anything changed on the price of the painting. The owner said he had never stepped foot out of State A and had never done any business across state lines, but he hoped to see her again if she ever came back. Two weeks later, the owner called the woman at her home in State C and offered to sell her the painting for 50 percent off – at $80,000, it was a steal. The woman was thrilled to buy the painting at that price, but when it arrived at her home in State C she immediately discovered that the painting was a fake. The woman filed suit against the owner in State C. The owner moved to dismiss the woman's claim in State C for lack of jurisdiction. Does the State C court have jurisdiction over the claim?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the owner called the woman in State C to make the sale.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the owner made the sale knowing that the woman was only traveling through and lived in State C.
Answer C: C: No, because the owner never left State A.
Answer D: D: No, because a single phone call is not enough to grant the State C court jurisdiction.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: If a seller makes a call into a state for the specific purpose of making a sale and does in fact make that sale, the call has created sufficient minimum contacts to support the state's jurisdiction over that claim. Thus, State C would have jurisdiction over the claim. B is incorrect because the fact the owner knew the woman was just traveling through would not be enough to support jurisdiction in State C. C is incorrect because the owner could still be sued in another state even if he never physically left his home state. D is incorrect because a single phone call can show sufficient market activity in a state to give a state jurisdiction over a claim.
