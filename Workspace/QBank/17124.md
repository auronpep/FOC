Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: In a federal personal-injury trial, Martha offers a photograph of a stairway. Samuel testifies that he saw the stairway the morning after the fall and that the photograph fairly and accurately shows the stairway as it appeared then. The defendant objects that Samuel did not take the photograph. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the photograph because a witness with knowledge may authenticate it as a fair and accurate depiction.
Answer B: Exclude the photograph because only the photographer can authenticate it.
Answer C: Exclude the photograph because a photograph is inadmissible unless it is the original image file.
Answer D: Admit the photograph only if an expert explains the camera process.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A photograph may be authenticated by testimony from a witness with knowledge that it fairly and accurately depicts the scene. The authenticating witness need not be the photographer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the photographer is not required. C: C is wrong because the original-writing rule is not triggered merely by offering a photo as an illustrative depiction authenticated by a witness. D: D is wrong because ordinary photographs usually do not require expert process testimony.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(1) / photograph fair-and-accurate testimony
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests photograph foundation through a witness with knowledge.
