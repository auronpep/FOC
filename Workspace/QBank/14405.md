Question: On June 1, a seller received a mail order from a buyer requesting prompt shipment of a specified computer model at the seller's current catalog price. On June 2, the seller mailed to the buyer a letter accepting the order and assuring the buyer that the computer would be shipped on June 3. On June 3, the seller realized that he was out of that computer model, shipped a different computer model to the buyer, and mailed a separate notice of accommodation. On June 5, the buyer received the seller's June 2 letter and the different computer model, but not the notice of accommodation. On June 5, which of the following is a correct statement of the parties' legal rights and duties?
Answer A: A: The buyer can either accept or reject the different computer model and, in either event, recover damages, if any, for breach of contract.
Answer B: B: The buyer can either accept or reject the different computer model, but if he rejects it, he will thereby waive any remedy for breach of contract.
Answer C: C: The seller's prompt shipment of nonconforming goods constituted an acceptance of the buyer's offer, thereby creating a contract for the sale of the different computer model.
Answer D: D: The seller's notice of accommodation was timely mailed, and his shipment of the different computer model constituted a counteroffer.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. As of June 5, having received a letter accepting the buyer's offer to purchase the correct computers along with the incorrect computers, the buyer had the right to accept or reject the shipment. The mailed notice of accommodation is a distraction from those dispositive facts. Whether the buyer accepts or rejects the shipment, he may sue for breach of contract because of the failure to ship goods conforming to the original contract. B
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. The buyer can rightfully reject or accept the non-conforming goods, but a rejection would not waive the right to sue for the seller's breach. The seller had accepted the original offer but tendered non-conforming goods, and which gave him the right to sue for the seller's breach. C is incorrect. On the contrary, it was the seller's June 2 acceptance letter that created a contract based on the buyer's original offer, and the shipment of non-conforming goods amounted to a breach of that contract. D is incorrect. The mailed notice of accommodation was irrelevant. As of June 5, the seller had accepted the original offer and mailed non-conforming goods, which gives the buyer the right to sue for breach whether he accepts or rejects the shipment.
