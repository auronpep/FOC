Outline: Assumption of risk
Question: A chemical company was neutralizing an unstable compound in a fenced exclusion zone marked with signs warning of toxic vapor. A photographer read the signs, climbed the fence to get close-up pictures, inhaled vapor, and became ill. In the photographer's strict-liability suit, what is the company's best defense?
Answer A: The company used reasonable care in posting the warning signs
Answer B: The photographer knowingly and voluntarily encountered the specific toxic-vapor risk
Answer C: The company cannot be strictly liable because chemical work is always useful to the public
Answer D: The photographer loses only if he intended to become ill
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Assumption of the particular abnormal risk can bar or reduce recovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Due care alone is not a defense to strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity. C: Usefulness is relevant to the abnormal-danger analysis but does not categorically bar strict liability. D: Assumption of risk requires knowing exposure to the risk, not intent to suffer injury.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff who knowingly and voluntarily exposes himself to the particular abnormal risk may be barred from strict-liability recovery.
