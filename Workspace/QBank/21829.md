Question: In a federal negligence trial, Ruth is asked whether Noah drove through a red light. Ruth answers that she did not see the intersection but that Leah told her, “Noah ran the red light.” Noah objects. The plaintiff offers Ruth's testimony to prove that Noah ran the red light. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because Ruth personally heard Leah make the statement.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because a witness may testify to any statement the witness personally heard.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because Ruth lacks personal knowledge of the red light and would be relaying Leah's out-of-court statement for its truth.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Leah is unavailable to testify.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Ruth has personal knowledge that Leah made a statement, but she does not have personal knowledge of whether Noah ran the red light. Because the statement is offered to prove the truth of Leah's assertion, the testimony also creates a hearsay problem. The personal-knowledge objection properly blocks Ruth from proving the underlying event through Leah's statement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B are wrong because personal knowledge of hearing a statement is not personal knowledge of the event asserted in the statement. D is wrong because unavailability alone does not create admissibility, and the problem is Ruth's lack of personal knowledge plus hearsay use. B: A and B are wrong because personal knowledge of hearing a statement is not personal knowledge of the event asserted in the statement. D is wrong because unavailability alone does not create admissibility, and the problem is Ruth's lack of personal knowledge plus hearsay use. D: D is wrong because unavailability alone does not create admissibility, and the problem is Ruth's lack of personal knowledge plus hearsay use.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 and 801 / hearsay versus personal knowledge
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the trap of substituting personal knowledge of a statement for personal knowledge of the event asserted.
