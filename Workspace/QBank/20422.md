Question: Esther hired Samuel to design three separate signs for her shops: $2,000 for the north shop sign, $2,000 for the south shop sign, and $2,000 for the market sign, each payable on delivery of that sign. Samuel delivered the north and south shop signs, both of which Esther accepted. Before Samuel began the market sign, Esther cancelled the project and refused to pay for any signs. What is Samuel most likely entitled to recover?
Answer A: Nothing, because all three signs were part of one overall project.
Answer B: The reasonable value of his work only, because a divisible contract cannot use stated prices.
Answer C: The stated contract price for the two completed and accepted signs, plus any appropriate damages from the cancellation.
Answer D: The full $6,000 contract price because Esther cancelled before the third sign was started.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Separate prices and payment on delivery of each sign make the contract divisible. Samuel completed and delivered two units, and Esther accepted them. His right to the stated prices for those units matured, though the unmade third sign is a separate consequence issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the separate unit prices and payment triggers defeat the all-or-nothing approach. B: B is wrong because completed divisible units can be compensated at the contract rate. D: D is wrong because Samuel did not perform the third sign and does not automatically receive its full price.
