MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Malicious prosecution
Question: A vendor sued a former distributor for stealing inventory. Before filing, the vendor's own audit showed the inventory had been shipped to the wrong warehouse and that the distributor had not taken it. The vendor filed anyway to pressure the distributor to drop a separate pricing complaint. The court later entered judgment for the distributor on the merits. The distributor then sued the vendor for malicious prosecution. Is the distributor likely to prevail on the malicious prosecution claim?
Answer A: Yes, because the vendor initiated proceedings without probable cause, acted with an improper purpose, and the case ended in the distributor's favor
Answer B: No, because malicious prosecution requires misuse of a subpoena or other process after the case begins
Answer C: No, because losing a lawsuit never creates tort liability
Answer D: Yes, but only because the vendor's lawsuit caused the distributor economic loss
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The audit undermined probable cause, the pressure motive supports malice, and judgment for the distributor supplies favorable termination.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Misuse of process after issuance is abuse of process. Malicious prosecution targets wrongful initiation of proceedings with favorable termination. C: Losing alone is not enough, but the facts also show no probable cause, malice, and favorable termination. D: Damages alone do not establish malicious prosecution. The decisive elements are lack of probable cause, malice, and favorable termination.
lb_rule_reference: Malicious prosecution requires initiation of proceedings without probable cause, with malice, and favorable termination for the later plaintiff.
