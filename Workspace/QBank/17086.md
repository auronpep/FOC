Question: In a federal civil trial, Martha testifies for the defendant. The plaintiff seeks to impeach Martha with a three-year-old conviction for fraud. Martha shows that the conviction was annulled last year based on a finding that she was innocent. The plaintiff argues that fraud is a dishonesty crime. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the conviction because fraud always involves dishonesty.
Answer B: Admit the conviction because the annulment affects only punishment, not impeachment.
Answer C: Exclude the conviction because a conviction annulled based on a finding of innocence is not admissible for impeachment.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction only if Martha is a criminal defendant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 609(c), evidence of a conviction is not admissible if the conviction was the subject of a pardon, annulment, or equivalent procedure based on a finding of innocence. That exclusion applies even though the original offense involved fraud.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the innocence-based annulment defeats use of the conviction. B: B is wrong because Rule 609(c) addresses impeachment admissibility directly. D: D is wrong because the innocence-based exclusion is not limited to criminal defendants.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(c) / pardon based on innocence
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the innocence-based annulment route and rejects dishonesty-label overuse.
