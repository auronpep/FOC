Question: Minutes after a robbery, police detain a suspect one block away carrying the victim's bag. The victim had seen the robber face-to-face under bright lights. Police bring the victim to the scene, where the victim immediately identifies the suspect. The defendant moves to suppress the showup. What is the best answer?
Answer A: Suppression is automatic because one-person showups are always unconstitutional
Answer B: The identification is likely admissible because it was immediate and reliability factors are strong
Answer C: Suppression is required because the suspect was detained by police
Answer D: The identification is admissible only if counsel was present
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The witness had a close, well-lit view, the identification occurred minutes later, and the suspect was detained nearby with the victim's bag. Those facts support reliability despite suggestiveness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Suggestiveness does not automatically require suppression. Courts consider necessity and reliability. C: Detention is common in showups. The issue is whether the procedure created a substantial likelihood of misidentification. D: Counsel rules depend on attachment and corporeal lineup posture. The facts are an immediate pretrial showup tested under due process reliability.
lb_rule_reference: A one-person showup is suggestive, but due process suppression depends on whether the procedure was unnecessarily suggestive and created a substantial likelihood of misidentification under reliability factors.
