Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: Police arrested Talia, questioned her without Miranda warnings, and obtained a voluntary statement that she drove the getaway car. The court excluded the statement from the prosecution's case-in-chief. At trial, Talia testified, "I was never near the car." The prosecutor seeks to use the unwarned statement only to impeach that testimony. May the prosecutor use Talia's unwarned but voluntary statement for impeachment?
Answer A: No, because any Miranda violation bars the statement for every purpose
Answer B: No, because impeachment use would punish Talia for exercising the right to testify
Answer C: Yes, because the statement was voluntary and is offered only to impeach inconsistent testimony
Answer D: Yes, because an unwarned statement is fully admissible whenever it is truthful
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The Miranda violation keeps the statement out of the prosecution's affirmative case. Because it was not coerced and Talia testified inconsistently, it may be used for impeachment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A voluntary Miranda-violating statement is excluded from the case-in-chief but may be used to impeach inconsistent testimony. B: The impeachment exception prevents a defendant from using Miranda as a license to commit perjury, as long as the statement was voluntary. D: Truthfulness is not the Miranda remedy test. The statement remains barred from the case-in-chief and is admissible only for the limited impeachment purpose.
lb_rule_reference: Voluntary unwarned statements are barred from the case-in-chief under Miranda, but may be used for impeachment if the defendant testifies inconsistently.
