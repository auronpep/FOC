Question: Daniel agreed in a signed contract to buy 300 restaurant mixers from Samuel, with delivery to Daniel's central warehouse by June 1. The contract stated that changes required a signed writing. Two weeks before delivery, Daniel told Samuel that the warehouse floor had failed inspection and asked Samuel to deliver the mixers directly to three restaurant locations instead. Samuel rerouted trucks, paid added nonrefundable freight charges, and delivered the first two location shipments, which Daniel accepted. When the third location shipment arrived, Daniel rejected it, arguing that the written contract required delivery only to the central warehouse and that there was no signed amendment. Can Daniel reject the third shipment on that ground?
Answer A: Yes, because the parol evidence rule bars Samuel from proving Daniel's oral delivery-location request.
Answer B: Yes, because a signed no-oral-modification clause makes later oral requests legally irrelevant even after performance and reliance.
Answer C: Probably no, because Daniel's later request, Samuel's reliance, and Daniel's acceptance of the first two location deliveries can support waiver, making retraction unjust.
Answer D: Probably no, but only if Samuel proves a trade usage that delivery locations are always flexible in restaurant-equipment contracts.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that an attempted modification that does not satisfy a signed-writing clause may still operate as a waiver, and waiver affecting future performance may be unretractable if retraction would be unjust after material reliance. The trigger facts are Daniel's post-signing request, Samuel's added nonrefundable freight, and Daniel's acceptance of the first two changed deliveries. The decisive locks are timing and reliance: Samuel is not using a prior oral term to vary the writing; he is relying on later conduct that changed the operative delivery practice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it commits a PER timing error; the delivery-location request occurred after the signed writing. B: B is wrong because no-oral-modification language is relevant but not absolute when later conduct supports waiver and reliance. D: D is wrong because the parties' own later request, reliance, and acceptance supply the stronger route; trade usage is not required.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-209(4), (5) / waiver and reliance
