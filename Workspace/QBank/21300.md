Outline: Erie Doctrine
Question: After a federal diversity trial, the court enters a money judgment for the plaintiff. The parties agree state law governs the contract claim, but they dispute the rate for interest accruing after entry of the federal judgment. A federal post-judgment-interest statute sets the rate. Which source governs post-judgment interest?
Answer A: State law, because the underlying claim is a state contract claim
Answer B: Federal law, because a federal statute directly controls post-judgment interest
Answer C: The jury's chosen rate, because interest is always a fact question
Answer D: No interest, because diversity judgments cannot include interest
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When a valid federal statute answers the issue, the federal court applies it rather than unguided Erie balancing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Post-judgment interest on a federal judgment is governed by the federal statute on point. C: The legal source sets the rate; the jury does not choose a post-judgment statutory rate. D: Federal judgments can carry post-judgment interest; the question is which source supplies it.
lb_rule_reference: Post-judgment interest on a federal district court money judgment is governed by federal statute, including in diversity cases.
