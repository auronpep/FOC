MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Solicitation
Question: Margaret, hoping to collect life insurance proceeds, approached Victor, her neighbor, and asked him to kill her husband. Margaret explained that she would pay Victor $50,000 after the deed was done. Victor was horrified and immediately went to the police to report the conversation. Margaret never repeated the request or took any further action. Margaret is charged with solicitation to commit murder. Is Margaret guilty of solicitation?
Answer A: No, because Victor refused and therefore no agreement was formed
Answer B: No, because Margaret voluntarily abandoned the plan after Victor's refusal
Answer C: Yes, because solicitation is complete when the request is communicated with intent that the crime be committed
Answer D: Yes, but only if the prosecution proves Margaret had a plan to pay Victor
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Criminal solicitation is complete at the moment the defendant communicates the request or encouragement with the specific intent that the solicited person commit the crime. Margaret asked Victor to kill her husband and offered payment, demonstrating specific intent that the murder be carried out. Victor's refusal, his reporting to police, and Margaret's subsequent inaction are all irrelevant to whether solicitation was committed. The crime was complete when Margaret finished speaking.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Solicitation does not require agreement. The offense is the act of entreating another to commit a crime. Victor's refusal is irrelevant to solicitation liability. The crime was complete the moment Margaret communicated her request to Victor. B: There is no abandonment defense to solicitation because the offense was complete at the moment of communication. One cannot abandon a completed crime. Moreover, Margaret did not "abandon" in any meaningful sense — she simply did not repeat the request after Victor refused. D: Solicitation requires specific intent that the crime be committed, not proof of a payment arrangement. While the offer of payment is strong evidence of intent, the offense does not require a payment plan as a separate element. A bare verbal request to commit a crime, with the required intent, is sufficient.
lb_rule_reference: Criminal solicitation is complete when the defendant communicates a request or encouragement to another person to commit a crime, with the specific intent that the crime be committed; the solicited person's response is not an element
