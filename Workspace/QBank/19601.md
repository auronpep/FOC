Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A newspaper published a truthful article about a public marathon and included a photo of a runner crossing the finish line. The runner did not consent. The photo appeared only inside the article, not in an advertisement or on merchandise. Is the newspaper likely liable for appropriation?
Answer A: No, because the photo was used in a newsworthy editorial article rather than to promote a separate product
Answer B: Yes, because any publication of a photo without consent is appropriation
Answer C: Yes, because newspapers make money from articles
Answer D: No, but only because the runner was famous
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: News and editorial uses are generally not appropriation merely because the publication is sold.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Newsworthy or editorial use is different from commercial identity promotion. C: Profit from news publishing does not automatically turn editorial use into appropriation. D: The facts do not say the runner was famous; the key is newsworthy editorial use.
lb_rule_reference: Newsworthy, editorial, or incidental use of a person's likeness is usually not appropriation merely because the publisher sells its news product.
