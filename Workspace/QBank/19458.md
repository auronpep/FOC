Question: Daniel contracted with Hannah to buy Hannah's small shop. Daniel promised in the contract to pay Hannah's sister Leah $6,000. Hannah delivered the shop and signed all transfer papers. The contract required Hannah to use black ink on the transfer forms; one form was signed in blue ink, but the filing office accepted it without objection. Daniel refused to pay Leah, arguing that Hannah failed to comply exactly with the ink requirement. Can Leah recover from Daniel?
Answer A: Yes, because Daniel has no contract defense that materially defeats his duty to pay Leah.
Answer B: No, because any technical failure by Hannah defeats Leah's beneficiary rights.
Answer C: No, because Leah was not the promisee.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Leah proves Daniel subjectively intended to make her a gift.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A promisor may assert real defenses against a beneficiary, but the asserted defect must actually affect the duty. The blue-ink signature was accepted and did not materially impair Daniel's bargain, so it does not defeat Leah's right to the promised payment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a technical defect that does not excuse the promisor's duty is not a complete defense. C: C is wrong because intended beneficiaries are not promisees. D: D is wrong because objective contract intent and direct payment matter; subjective gift motive is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — defense must actually excuse duty
