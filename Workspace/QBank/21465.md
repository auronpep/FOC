Question: Joshua executed and delivered a valid deed conveying Hillacre “to Caleb when Caleb marries.” Caleb is unmarried. The deed contains no other disposition of Hillacre. Who has the present possessory estate immediately after delivery of the deed?
Answer A: Caleb, because delivery of the deed gives him immediate possession.
Answer B: Caleb and Joshua as tenants in common until Caleb marries.
Answer C: No one, because the deed creates a gap in possession.
Answer D: Joshua, subject to Caleb's springing executory interest.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Joshua retains the present possessory estate, subject to Caleb's springing executory interest. Caleb does not take possession unless and until he marries. The law does not leave a gap in possession; the grantor keeps the estate until the springing interest becomes possessory.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because deed delivery does not eliminate the condition precedent. B: B is wrong because the deed does not create co-ownership. C: C is wrong because the grantor retains the present estate during the waiting period.
lb_rule_reference: Springing executory interest / grantor retains present estate
