internal_id: TR-051
Outline_code: 61020301
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Falling below the standard of care
Question: A doctor recommended an elective diagnostic procedure using a sedative. The doctor performed the procedure skillfully, but did not tell the patient that the sedative carried a material risk of prolonged respiratory distress for patients with the patient's known condition. The patient would have chosen a different test if told of that risk. The risk occurred and caused injury. The patient sues the doctor for negligence based on lack of informed consent. Is the patient likely to prevail on the negligence claim for lack of informed consent?
Answer A: No, because the doctor performed the procedure skillfully
Answer B: Yes, because the doctor failed to disclose a material risk that would have changed the patient's decision
Answer C: No, because doctors need disclose only risks that are certain to occur
Answer D: Yes, because every undisclosed risk makes the doctor liable if any injury follows
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The undisclosed risk was material for this patient, the patient would have declined or chosen an alternative if informed, and the undisclosed risk caused the injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Lack of informed consent is distinct from negligent performance. A skillfully performed procedure can still involve negligent nondisclosure of a material risk. C: The rule turns on material risks, not only certain harms. A risk can require disclosure even though it is not certain. D: Informed consent does not require disclosure of every remote risk, and the plaintiff still must show materiality, decision causation, and injury from the undisclosed risk.
lb_rule_reference: Medical informed consent can be breached when a physician fails to disclose a material treatment risk and the patient would have declined the treatment if informed.
