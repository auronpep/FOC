Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A building owner hired an independent contractor to remove a load-bearing wall next to a busy sidewalk while the building remained open. The work required shoring and controlled debris removal to prevent collapse. The contractor skipped required shoring, and a section of wall fell onto a pedestrian. The owner argues that the contractor controlled all demolition methods. Is the owner likely liable to the pedestrian under an independent-contractor exception?
Answer A: No, because the contractor controlled the demolition methods
Answer B: Yes, because removing a load-bearing wall beside pedestrians involved a peculiar risk requiring special precautions
Answer C: No, unless the owner personally instructed the contractor to skip shoring
Answer D: Yes, but only because all construction work is abnormally dangerous strict-liability work
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The contracted work posed a special risk of collapse unless shoring and debris precautions were used. That supports the inherently dangerous activity exception to the independent-contractor rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The inherently dangerous work exception can impose liability despite contractor control when special precautions are needed to address a peculiar risk. C: The exception can apply even without the owner's personal instruction because the risk inheres in the delegated work. D: The question concerns an independent-contractor exception for inherently dangerous work, not strict liability for all construction.
lb_rule_reference: A principal may be liable for an independent contractor's negligence when the contracted work creates a peculiar risk of serious harm unless special precautions are taken.
