


BARMATRIX Q#: 22805
Outline_code: 92080104
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motions to dismiss
Question: A plaintiff sued a defendant in federal court. The defendant moved to dismiss the complaint for failure to state a claim. In response, the plaintiff voluntarily dismissed the action. The plaintiff then filed a second action that alleged the same claims but fixed the pleading defects outlined in the defendant's motion to dismiss. The defendant moved to dismiss the second action, and the plaintiff again voluntarily dismissed the second action. The plaintiff then filed a third action that alleged the same claims but addressed other pleading defects noted in the defendant's second motion. The defendant moved to dismiss the third action. Is the court likely to grant the defendant's motion?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the plaintiff's initial complaint was materially incomplete or incorrect.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the plaintiff's previously dismissed actions operated as an adjudication on the merits of the plaintiff's claim.
Answer C: C: No, because the plaintiff fixed the pleading defects outlined in the defendant's motions to dismiss.
Answer D: D: No, because the plaintiff voluntarily dismissed the first and second actions before the defendant moved for summary judgment on either of them.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Fed. R. Civ. P. 41(a)(1)(B) states that if a plaintiff voluntary dismisses a claim that the plaintiff has previously voluntarily dismissed, the “notice of dismissal operates as an adjudication on the merits.” Here, since the plaintiff already voluntarily dismissed his claim once, his second voluntary dismissal acts as an adjudication on the merits. A is incorrect because there was no indication that the plaintiff's initial complaint was materially incomplete or incorrect. C is incorrect because the plaintiff had previously voluntarily dismissed the complaint. D is incorrect because the fact that the defendant did not file for summary judgment did not change the analysis.
