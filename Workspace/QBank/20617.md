Question: Abigail owned Lot 3 near a public road. Elizabeth owned Lot 7, which was separated from Lot 3 by another parcel but used the route over Lot 3 for deliveries to Lot 7. Abigail signed a recorded grant giving “the owner of Lot 7 a right to cross the marked lane on Lot 3 for deliveries to Lot 7.” How should the crossing right be classified?
Answer A: It must be in gross because Lot 3 and Lot 7 are not adjoining parcels.
Answer B: It may be appurtenant because the grant identifies Lot 7 as the benefited dominant parcel, even though the parcels are not contiguous.
Answer C: It is a profit because delivery trucks may carry goods.
Answer D: It is only a license because the grant benefits deliveries rather than Elizabeth personally.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An appurtenant easement requires a benefited dominant parcel and a burdened servient parcel, but the parcels need not always be contiguous. The grant identifies the owner of Lot 7 and states that the lane is for deliveries to Lot 7.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because contiguity is not the decisive requirement for appurtenance. C: C is wrong because carrying goods in a truck is not removing resources from Lot 3. D: D is wrong because a recorded grant tied to Lot 7 can create a property right, not merely revocable permission.
lb_rule_reference: Appurtenant easement / contiguity not required
