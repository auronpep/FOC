Outline: Reliance damages
Question: David hired Esther to produce a promotional video for a new online course. David repudiated before filming. Before the repudiation, Esther reasonably spent $12,000 securing the location and $8,000 hiring a crew. Esther's claimed $30,000 in expected distribution profits is not supported by reliable sales data. Esther asks for the expenditures plus the expected profits. What is the proper recovery?
Answer A: $50,000, because both the expenditures and hoped-for profits followed from David's breach.
Answer B: $20,000, because reliance protects the proven expenditures but does not stack speculative profits.
Answer C: $30,000, because expectation damages are the preferred contract remedy.
Answer D: Nothing, because the expected profits are speculative.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is reliance under common law. The breach posture is repudiation, the loss category is proven preparation expenditures, and the limiting gate is certainty. Because the profits are speculative, Esther may recover $12,000 + $8,000 = $20,000 as reliance, but she cannot stack reliance with unproven expectation profits. The result scope is limited, and the answer does not decide consequential damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it stacks reliance expenditures and speculative profits, a WAL-REM-008 overcompensation error. C: C is wrong because expectation profits fail the certainty gate here. D: D is wrong because speculative profits do not require nominal-only recovery when reliance damages are proven.
lb_rule_reference: Common law reliance damages — reliance as alternative to speculative expectation, not cumulative recovery
