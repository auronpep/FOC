Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: Abraham is charged with burning a warehouse. The prosecutor offers evidence that Abraham disabled the warehouse's smoke alarm two days before the fire. Abraham objects that disabling the alarm does not prove he started the fire. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence, because disabling the alarm shortly before the fire has some tendency to show preparation or opportunity.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence, because it does not directly prove who lit the fire.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence unless the prosecutor proves Abraham disabled the alarm for the purpose of arson.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if the alarm would have prevented all damage.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Disabling the alarm shortly before the fire makes preparation or opportunity somewhat more probable. The evidence need not directly prove ignition or Abraham's subjective purpose to be relevant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because circumstantial preparation evidence can be relevant. C: C is wrong because the prosecutor need not prove the precise purpose behind the act before the evidence has probative value. D: D is wrong because the relevance does not depend on whether the alarm would have prevented all damage.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is demanding direct proof from circumstantial preparation evidence.
