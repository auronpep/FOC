Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Paul orally agreed to buy 200 tablets from Peter for $6 each. Peter delivered 50 tablets. Paul immediately inspected them, found that they did not match the oral specifications, and seasonably rejected them, returning the tablets the same day. Peter sues for the price of all 200 tablets. Peter argues that delivery of 50 tablets takes the entire contract outside the Statute of Frauds. Is he correct?
Answer A: No. Goods that are merely received and seasonably rejected are not received and accepted, and delivery of 50 tablets does not enforce the entire oral contract.
Answer B: Yes. Any physical delivery of goods validates the entire oral contract.
Answer C: Yes, but only for all 200 tablets because the original oral quantity controls over the Statute of Frauds.
Answer D: No, because UCC §2-201 never permits enforcement without a signed writing.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The UCC exception applies to goods that have been received and accepted. Paul seasonably rejected and returned the goods, so Peter cannot use the received-and-accepted exception for those goods, much less for the entire oral quantity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because mere delivery is not acceptance. C: C is wrong because oral quantity cannot expand enforcement beyond a valid writing or exception. D: D is wrong because UCC §2-201 contains exceptions, but this fact pattern does not satisfy the received-and-accepted route.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 / received but rejected goods
