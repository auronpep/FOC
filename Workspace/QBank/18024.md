Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: Every model of a folding exercise bench was manufactured exactly according to the company's plans. The plans placed the rear support so close to the center that the bench tipped backward during ordinary presses by users within the advertised weight limit. A wider rear support was inexpensive and would not impair use. Which theory best matches these facts?
Answer A: Manufacturing defect, because the bench that injured the plaintiff was dangerous
Answer B: Design defect, because the intended design created an avoidable tipping risk across the product line
Answer C: Failure to warn only, because a warning label is always the exclusive remedy for exercise equipment
Answer D: No products claim because the product was built exactly as planned
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff attacks the product-line design, supported by ordinary use and a feasible safer alternative.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The bench did not depart from specifications. The hazard was in the intended design of every unit. C: A warning does not automatically displace a feasible design-defect claim. D: Exact compliance with an unsafe intended design can be the reason the claim is design defect.
lb_rule_reference: A design defect challenges the safety of the intended product line, not a one-off departure from specifications.
