Question: An employee sues a city in federal court under a federal civil-rights statute. During discovery, the city invokes a state-law municipal-review privilege that federal common law does not recognize. The disputed evidence is relevant to the federal claim. Which privilege source governs?
Answer A: State privilege law, because any civil case can borrow state evidence law
Answer B: Federal privilege law, because the evidence is relevant to a federal claim
Answer C: The defendant's home-state privilege law, because the defendant is a city
Answer D: No privilege analysis is needed because federal claims allow all relevant evidence
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim arises under federal law, so federal common-law privilege governs absent a controlling statute or rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: For a federal claim, federal privilege law supplies the ordinary rule. C: Party status does not override the federal source of the claim. D: Federal privilege law still applies; the issue is whether federal law recognizes the asserted privilege.
lb_rule_reference: In a federal-question case, federal common-law privilege governs privilege issues unless a statute, rule, or constitutional provision provides otherwise.
