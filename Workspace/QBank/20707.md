Outline: Mode and order of witnesses
Question: In a federal civil rights trial, the defendant is halfway through direct examination of Aaron. The judge learns that Rebekah, a nonparty medical witness for the plaintiff, must leave immediately for an emergency surgery and cannot return for months. The plaintiff asks to interrupt Aaron's testimony briefly to take Rebekah's testimony. The defendant objects that a party may never interrupt another party's witness. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because once a witness begins testifying, no other witness may be called until that witness is fully completed.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless the defendant agrees to the interruption.
Answer C: Overrule the objection if the interruption is a reasonable way to receive important testimony without unfairly impairing the defendant's examination of Aaron.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because witness availability always requires interrupting the current witness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may exercise reasonable control over the order of witnesses. A brief interruption to preserve important testimony from an unavailable witness may be permitted if the court protects the opposing party from unfair prejudice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the order of witnesses is not that rigid. B: B is wrong because the judge's reasonable control does not require party consent. D: D is wrong because witness availability is important but not automatic; the ruling must remain reasonable and fair.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / interrupting witness order for unavoidable witness
