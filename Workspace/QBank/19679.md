Question: Moses speaks little English. To obtain legal advice, he met privately with his lawyer, Ruth, while Aaron interpreted. Aaron was not a friend or witness; he was present only so Moses and Ruth could communicate accurately. Does Aaron's presence destroy attorney-client privilege?
Answer A: No, because Aaron was reasonably necessary to facilitate the confidential legal consultation.
Answer B: Yes, because the presence of any third person waives attorney-client privilege.
Answer C: Yes, unless Aaron was also a licensed lawyer.
Answer D: No, but only if Aaron later refuses to testify.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Confidentiality is not destroyed when a third person is reasonably necessary to facilitate the legal consultation, such as an interpreter. Aaron's role preserved, rather than defeated, the confidential channel. The decisive axis is necessary agent versus unnecessary observer.
