Question: Joseph told Esther that she could build a one-lane driveway across Joseph's land to reach Esther's home. Esther paved a narrow residential driveway in reliance on the statement. Years later, Esther bought an adjoining commercial parcel and began using the same driveway for delivery trucks serving that parcel. What is the best result?
Answer A: Esther may use the driveway for any land she later owns because estoppel creates unlimited access.
Answer B: Any estoppel-based right is limited by the permission and reliance, so delivery-truck access to the adjoining commercial parcel may be an overburden.
Answer C: Joseph may revoke all use because estoppel rights always expire once the driveway is built.
Answer D: Esther owns the driveway strip because she paved it.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement by estoppel is limited by the representation and the reliance that justified making the license irrevocable. The promise and reliance concerned residential access to Esther's home, not truck access for a later-acquired commercial parcel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because estoppel does not create unlimited use for extra land. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because estoppel may prevent revocation of the relied-on use. D: D is wrong because paving may support a use right, not title.
lb_rule_reference: Estoppel scope limited by reliance and promise
lb_student_diagnostic: The recurring trap is proving estoppel and then forgetting to limit the resulting right's scope.
