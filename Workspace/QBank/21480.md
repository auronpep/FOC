Question: Mary owned a used piano and was not in the business of selling pianos. She offered to sell the piano to John for $2,000. John paid Mary $50 in exchange for Mary's promise to keep the offer open for ten days. On day three, Mary told John she revoked the offer. John accepted on day five. What is the best result?
Answer A: Mary's revocation was ineffective because John paid consideration for an option to keep the offer open.
Answer B: Mary's revocation was ineffective only because she was a merchant under Article 2's firm-offer rule.
Answer C: Mary's revocation was effective because nonmerchant offers can never be made irrevocable.
Answer D: Mary's revocation was effective because John's reliance is presumed insufficient whenever the subject is goods.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Although Mary was not a merchant and therefore did not create an Article 2 firm offer, John paid separate consideration for Mary's promise to hold the offer open. That option consideration made the offer irrevocable during the ten-day option period, and John accepted within that period.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because Mary was not a merchant and the result does not depend on Article 2's firm-offer rule. C: C is wrong because nonmerchant offers can be made irrevocable by an option supported by consideration. D: D is wrong because the decisive route is option consideration, not presumed reliance.
