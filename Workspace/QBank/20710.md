MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Diversity Jurisdiction
Question: A homeowner files a federal court action under diversity jurisdiction seeking only a permanent injunction to prevent a neighboring corporation from operating industrial equipment within 100 feet of the homeowner's property. The homeowner claims the noise and vibrations constitute a nuisance. The homeowner does not seek monetary damages. The homeowner's property is worth $250,000. An independent appraiser concludes that if the corporation's equipment continues to operate, the homeowner's property value will decline by $60,000. The corporation moves to dismiss for failure to satisfy the $75,000 AIC requirement. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Does the amount in controversy satisfy § 1332?
Answer A: No, because the plaintiff seeks no money damages, and the amount in controversy is zero when only injunctive relief is requested
Answer B: No, because the projected $60,000 property value decline does not exceed $75,000, and the homeowner has not demanded more than that
Answer C: Yes, because the value of the homeowner's property interest ($250,000) — the right being protected by the injunction — satisfies the § 1332 threshold
Answer D: Yes, because the defendant corporation's cost of complying with the injunction — if it exceeds $75,000 — also satisfies the AIC threshold under the defendant-viewpoint rule
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When the plaintiff seeks injunctive relief and no monetary damages, the amount in controversy is the value of the object of the litigation — the right being protected or the harm the injunction would prevent. The homeowner's property, worth $250,000, is the right being protected. The injunction is sought to prevent diminution of a $250,000 asset. That value substantially exceeds the $75,000 threshold, and the AIC requirement is met. The motion to dismiss should be denied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The AIC for injunctive relief is the value of the right at stake — typically the economic value of obtaining or avoiding the injunction. Here the injunction, if denied, would cause a $60,000 decline in property value, and the property itself is worth $250,000. Either the value of the right protected or the value of the property interest may satisfy the threshold. B: The $60,000 projected decline is one measure of value, but the AIC can also be measured by the value of the property right being protected — here, the $250,000 property. Where the property itself is the subject of the nuisance and the injunction would protect it from diminution, courts may use the value of the whole right at stake. Courts sometimes use either the plaintiff's gain or the defendant's burden from the injunction. On these facts, the homeowner's property interest substantially exceeds $75,000. D: This answer is not wrong as a standalone proposition — some courts do use the defendant-viewpoint test — but it is incomplete and the stated premise requires facts not established in the question (the corporation's compliance costs are not specified). The simpler and clearer basis for satisfying the threshold is the value of the plaintiff's property interest. This answer reaches the right outcome by an unnecessary and speculative path.
lb_rule_reference: In a suit for injunctive relief, the AIC for § 1332 purposes is the value of the object of the litigation — typically the economic value of the right being protected or the cost of complying with or being subject to the injunction.
