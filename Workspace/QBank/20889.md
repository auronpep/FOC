Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police have a valid arrest warrant for Dana. They go to Dana's apartment at 7 a.m., see Dana's car in the assigned space, hear Dana's voice inside, and enter after knocking and announcing. They arrest Dana in the living room. Was the entry lawful under the Fourth Amendment?
Answer A: No, because police always need a search warrant to enter any home
Answer B: Yes, because police had a valid arrest warrant and reason to believe Dana was inside Dana's own home
Answer C: No, unless police also had probable cause to search for evidence
Answer D: Yes, but only because police saw the car outside
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The car, voice, and time of day support reason to believe Dana was home, and the warrant authorized arrest there.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: An arrest warrant authorizes entry into the suspect's own home when officers have reason to believe the suspect is inside. C: The entry was to arrest Dana under an arrest warrant, not to search for evidence. D: The full reason-to-believe analysis includes all facts, not a single required fact.
lb_rule_reference: An arrest warrant permits police to enter the suspect's own home when they have reason to believe the suspect is inside.
