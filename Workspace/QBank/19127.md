Question: Daniel granted Esther a written easement “to install, maintain, repair, and replace one underground water line” across Daniel's field. Years later, the line leaked. Esther gave Daniel reasonable notice and proposed to enter the field for one day with a small excavator to repair the line, restoring the surface afterward. Daniel argues that the easement allowed only the original installation, not later repair access. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Daniel is correct because repair rights must always be granted in a separate document after the line fails.
Answer B: Daniel is correct because any entry with equipment converts an easement into fee ownership.
Answer C: Esther may enter reasonably to repair the line because the written easement expressly includes maintenance, repair, and replacement rights.
Answer D: Esther may enter only if she first proves an easement by necessity.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The express language grants rights to install, maintain, repair, and replace the water line. Reasonable temporary entry necessary to perform that work is within the scope of the granted utility easement, provided the work does not materially increase the burden beyond the grant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the original grant already includes repair rights. B: B is wrong because use of equipment to exercise the easement does not convey fee title. D: D is wrong because necessity is irrelevant when an express utility easement exists.
lb_rule_reference: Utility easement / install, maintain, repair, replace
