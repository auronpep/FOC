Question: A manufacturer sold a lightweight step ladder to retail stores. Customer testing showed that ordinary users often stood on the top cap for brief tasks, even though a small sticker said "Do not stand here." The top cap flexed because of a design choice that saved weight. A homeowner stood on the top cap to change a bulb, the cap collapsed, and she fell. The manufacturer argues misuse is a complete defense. How should the court treat misuse in the homeowner's products liability claim?
Answer A: Misuse completely bars recovery because the sticker told users not to stand on the top cap
Answer B: Misuse is irrelevant because comparative fault never applies in strict products cases
Answer C: Misuse does not automatically bar recovery because standing on the top cap was foreseeable and may bear on defect, warning, causation, or comparative responsibility
Answer D: Misuse bars recovery only if the homeowner intended to damage the ladder
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The manufacturer knew ordinary users stood on the top cap. Foreseeable misuse is folded into product design and warning analysis rather than serving as an automatic complete defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A warning and misuse do not automatically defeat products liability when the misuse was foreseeable and may have needed design or warning treatment. B: Many jurisdictions consider misuse, assumption of risk, or comparative responsibility in products cases. The issue is not irrelevant. D: Product misuse can matter without intent to damage the product. The correct issue is foreseeability and causal role, not intent to break the product.
lb_rule_reference: Product misuse is not a complete defense when the misuse is foreseeable and should be considered in design, warning, causation, or comparative responsibility analysis.
