Question: Joseph signed a deed conveying Vineyardacre to Benjamin. Joseph handed the deed to Benjamin and said, “Read this over. I may decide next week whether to give you the land, but it is not yours now.” Benjamin kept the paper and later claimed title. What is the best result?
Answer A: Benjamin owns Vineyardacre because physical possession of the deed is conclusive delivery.
Answer B: Joseph owns Vineyardacre because Joseph's words show no present intent to transfer title when he handed over the deed.
Answer C: Benjamin owns Vineyardacre only if he records the deed before Joseph changes his mind.
Answer D: Joseph owns Vineyardacre only because Benjamin paid no consideration.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is the grantor's present intent to transfer title. Joseph's handoff was for review and expressly stated that the land was not Benjamin's yet. Physical possession of the paper did not create delivery without present transfer intent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because delivery is not proved conclusively by physical possession. C: C is wrong because recording cannot cure lack of delivery. D: D is wrong because consideration is not the delivery lock.
lb_rule_reference: Physical handoff is not delivery without present intent to pass title
