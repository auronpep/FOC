


BARMATRIX Q#: 22630
Outline_code: 42010202
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Chief executive
Question: An Air Force management analyst's job was eliminated when the department he worked in was reorganized. Two years prior, the analyst had testified before Congress about the misuse of funds surrounding the Air Force's new plane. His testimony was very negative in regard to the Air Force, and the analyst's superiors were very unhappy. Following the analyst's dismissal, the President publicly claimed that he decided to fire the analyst for disrespecting the Air Force two years ago. The White House later retracted the President's statement. The analyst sued the President in federal district court on the grounds that he was improperly dismissed because of his negative comments before Congress. The analyst sought civil damages. The President moved to dismiss the analyst's claim. Should the court dismiss the analyst's claim?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the President has absolute immunity from civil damages when acting in his official capacity.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the President is Commander- in- Chief of the armed forces.
Answer C: C: No, because the analyst can claim a personal and concrete injury from the President's actions.
Answer D: D: No, because the analyst acted as a public whistleblower.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The President has absolute immunity from suits for civil damages for any actions taken in his official capacity. Nixon v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 731 (1982). Here, since the firing and statement occurred while the President was acting in his official capacity, he is absolutely immune from a civil suit for damages. B is incorrect because the President's immunity is not based on his position as Commanderin- Chief. C and D are incorrect because of the President's immunity.
