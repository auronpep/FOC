internal_id: TR-150

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Abuse of process
Question: A distributor had a plausible contract claim against a retailer and filed suit. After filing, the distributor served a broad subpoena on the retailer's unrelated parent company and privately offered to withdraw it only if the retailer paid a separate old debt not part of the lawsuit. Is the retailer likely to prevail on an abuse of process claim against the distributor?
Answer A: No, because the distributor had probable cause to file the contract lawsuit
Answer B: Yes, because the distributor used a subpoena to coerce payment of an unrelated debt
Answer C: No, because abuse of process requires the prior lawsuit to end in the retailer's favor
Answer D: Yes, but only if the subpoena contained defamatory statements
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The subpoena was legal process used for an ulterior purpose outside the lawsuit's proper objectives.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Abuse of process can exist even when the lawsuit was properly filed if process is later used for an ulterior purpose. C: Favorable termination is not an abuse-of-process element. D: Abuse of process turns on improper use of legal process, not defamatory content.
lb_rule_reference: Abuse of process targets use of legal process for an ulterior purpose after process issues, even if the underlying claim had probable cause.
