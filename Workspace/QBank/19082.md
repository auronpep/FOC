Question: A grand jury indicted Marcus on armed robbery charges. Three days after the indictment, detectives assembled a six-person in-person lineup and asked the victim to view it. Neither Marcus nor the prosecutors were notified, and no attorney was present. The victim identified Marcus from the lineup. At trial, the prosecution sought to introduce the lineup identification through the detective's testimony and to have the victim make an in-court identification. Was the lineup identification obtained in violation of Marcus's constitutional rights?
Answer A: No, because Marcus had not yet been arraigned, so the right to counsel had not yet attached
Answer B: No, because the victim's identification was voluntary and not coerced by police
Answer C: Yes, because Marcus had a Sixth Amendment right to have counsel present at the post-indictment in-person lineup, and conducting it without notice to counsel violated that right
Answer D: No, because lineups are not a critical stage of the prosecution since they occur outside of court
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under *United States v. Wade* and *Gilbert v. California*, a post-indictment in-person lineup is a "critical stage" of the prosecution at which the Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches. Because Marcus had already been indicted, the lineup required the presence of, or a valid waiver by, his attorney. Conducting the lineup without notifying counsel violated the Sixth Amendment. The remedy is exclusion of the out-of-court identification and suppression of any in-court identification unless the prosecution proves an independent source for the witness's ability to identify Marcus.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches at the initiation of formal charges — indictment, information, or arraignment — whichever occurs first. Indictment is sufficient. Marcus was already indicted, so the right had attached before the lineup occurred. B: The right to counsel at a post-indictment lineup is not about coercion; it is a structural right under *Wade* designed to protect against the subtle suggestiveness inherent in lineups. The absence of overt coercion is irrelevant to the Sixth Amendment analysis. D: Wade* expressly held that a post-indictment in-person lineup IS a critical stage of the prosecution, even though it occurs out of court. The risk of irreparable mistaken identification is precisely why counsel's presence is required.
