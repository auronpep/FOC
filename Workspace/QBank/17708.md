Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Subsequent remedial measures
Question: A customer falls when a loose stair tread shifts inside a store. The next morning, the store bolts the tread into place. At trial, the customer offers evidence of the repair to prove that the store had negligently maintained the stair before the fall. The store does not dispute that it controlled the stair or that repair was feasible. Should the repair evidence be admitted for the customer's stated purpose?
Answer A: Yes, because a repair after an accident is an opposing-party admission that the earlier condition was unsafe.
Answer B: No, because a subsequent remedial measure is not admissible to prove negligence or culpable conduct.
Answer C: Yes, because a repair is always admissible to prove feasibility, even when feasibility is not disputed.
Answer D: No, but only if the store first admits that the stair was negligently maintained.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 407 bars evidence of measures taken after an injury-causing event when the evidence is offered to prove negligence, culpable conduct, product defect, or need for warning. The customer offers the repair for the prohibited fault purpose. Because ownership, control, and feasibility are not disputed, the permitted-purpose routes do not control.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 407 rejects the admission-by-repair inference for fault purposes. C: C is wrong because feasibility is a permitted purpose only when genuinely disputed and when the evidence is offered for that purpose. D: D is wrong because exclusion does not depend on the store admitting negligence; it depends on the proponent's prohibited use of the repair evidence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 407 subsequent remedial measures barred for fault purpose
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats a later repair as ordinary relevance or an admission instead of running the public-policy purpose screen.
