Outline: Expectation damages
Question: David contracted with Abigail to provide a portable oven for David's weekly bread stall. Abigail breached. David could have rented an equivalent oven from another supplier for the week for $600, which would have allowed him to operate normally. David refused to rent it and lost $4,000 in net profits for the week. Renting the substitute would have been reasonable. What lost-profit damages may David recover?
Answer A: $4,000, because Abigail's breach caused the stall to close.
Answer B: Nothing, because any failure to mitigate eliminates all contract damages.
Answer C: $600, the reasonable cost that would have avoided the lost profits.
Answer D: $4,600, the lost profits plus the substitute rental cost David did not incur.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A nonbreaching party cannot recover losses that could have been avoided by reasonable mitigation. If a $600 substitute would have prevented $4,000 in lost profits, David's recovery for that loss is limited to the reasonable substitute cost.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores the avoidable-consequences limit. B: B is wrong because failure to mitigate limits avoidable losses; it does not erase all damages. D: D is wrong because David did not incur the rental cost and cannot stack it on unrecoverable avoidable profits.
