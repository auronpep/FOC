Question: In a federal tax prosecution, the government calls Dr. Luke, an emergency-room physician, to testify about statements Ruth made while being treated for a cut hand. Dr. Luke was not providing psychotherapy or mental-health treatment. Ruth objects under federal physician-patient privilege. How should the court rule under the federal default?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because all confidential medical treatment statements are privileged in federal court.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because Dr. Luke is a physician and therefore within the psychotherapist privilege.
Answer C: Overrule the objection, because federal common law recognizes psychotherapist-patient privilege but not a general physician-patient privilege on these facts.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Ruth's statements were not made for medical treatment.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal common law recognizes a psychotherapist-patient privilege, but the federal default does not include a general physician-patient privilege. Dr. Luke was treating a physical injury, not providing psychotherapy. The decisive axis is recognized relationship under the governing source law.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is the medical-setting shortcut. Confidential medical care is not automatically privileged under federal common law. B: B is wrong because A physician is not treated as a psychotherapist merely by being a medical professional. D: D is wrong because Treatment purpose may matter for hearsay, but it does not create a general federal physician-patient privilege.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / no general federal physician-patient privilege
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is converting medical confidentiality or hearsay treatment logic into an evidence privilege.
