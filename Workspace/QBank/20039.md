Question: Moses contracted with David to sell David a grain store. The contract required David to deliver 200 sacks of grain to Ruth, Moses's sister. Moses then wrote Ruth, “David promised to deliver grain to you as part of our sale. Please confirm that you accept that arrangement.” Ruth replied, “I accept and will rely on that delivery for my winter supply.” Before the delivery date, Moses and David agreed to cancel the grain-delivery clause. May Ruth enforce David's promise to deliver the grain?
Answer A: No, because Ruth was not named as a contracting party.
Answer B: No, because Ruth did not provide consideration to David.
Answer C: Yes, but only if Moses separately assigned the grain store contract to Ruth.
Answer D: Yes, because Ruth manifested assent to the beneficiary promise at Moses's request before the attempted cancellation.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Ruth was the direct recipient of performance and manifested assent to the promise after Moses requested her acceptance. That assent vested her rights before Moses and David attempted to cancel the beneficiary clause.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because intended beneficiaries are not contracting parties. B: B is wrong because consideration from Ruth is not required. C: C is wrong because Ruth enforces as a beneficiary, not as an assignee of Moses's contract rights.
