Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: In a federal murder trial, the defendant Abigail admits that the victim died and that the victim was the person named in the indictment. The prosecutor offers a long video of the victim's childhood, family celebrations, and funeral, stating that it will help the jury see the victim as a real person. Abigail objects under Rule 403. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the video because all evidence about the victim is automatically relevant.
Answer B: Admit the video because emotional force is never a Rule 403 concern.
Answer C: Exclude the video only if Abigail proves the victim's family asked the prosecutor to offer it.
Answer D: Exclude the video if its slight probative value is substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair emotional prejudice.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A long emotional video with little bearing on disputed issues may invite the jury to decide on sympathy or emotion rather than facts. If its limited probative value is substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice, exclusion is proper.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the evidence must have meaningful probative value on a consequential issue and survive Rule 403. B: B is wrong because unfair emotional prejudice is a classic Rule 403 concern. C: C is wrong because the family's request is not the Rule 403 test.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403
lb_student_diagnostic: Student mistakes emotional narrative for probative proof.
