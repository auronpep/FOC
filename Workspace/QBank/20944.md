Outline: Freedom of religion
Question: A city grant program reimburses religious nonprofits for building repairs only if city officials decide the building is used for "true worship rather than community activity." Officials deny a grant to a congregation because its services include meals, counseling, and music that officials label "mostly social." The congregation challenges the criteria. What is the strongest constitutional problem?
Answer A: The criteria invite officials to make theological judgments about what counts as true worship
Answer B: The criteria are valid because city officials need some way to rank religious activities
Answer C: The criteria are valid because community meals can never be religious
Answer D: The criteria are invalid only if the city prefers one named denomination in the ordinance text
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The program requires government officials to evaluate religious meaning and centrality, which the First Amendment strongly disfavors.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Administrative needs do not authorize theological line-drawing by government officials. C: Government cannot decide that a practice lacks religious significance because officials view it as social. D: Theological line-drawing can be unconstitutional even without naming a denomination.
lb_rule_reference: Government may not make benefits turn on theological judgments about which activities are sufficiently religious or spiritually central.
