Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: Abigail sues a pharmacy after becoming ill from a sealed bottle of tablets. She offers the unopened bottle she bought that day. The bottle bears the pharmacy's printed label, the prescription number, Abigail's name, and the pharmacy's address. The pharmacy objects that Abigail must call the employee who printed the label to authenticate the bottle. How should the court rule on the authentication objection?
Answer A: Sustain it because only the label-printing employee can authenticate the label.
Answer B: Overrule it because the business label and surrounding circumstances can identify the bottle's origin.
Answer C: Sustain it because the label is hearsay and cannot be used for any purpose.
Answer D: Overrule it only if Abigail proves the tablets' chemical composition first.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The label and circumstances provide a permissible source-identification foundation. A trade or business label may authenticate origin, and Abigail's purchase testimony supplies additional connection. Chemical composition is a separate issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the label-printing employee is not the only authentication source. C: C is wrong because the label is being used to identify the bottle and source, not simply as a testimonial assertion. D: D is wrong because chemical composition is not required to authenticate the bottle's origin.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 902(7); FRE 901(a)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student demands a specific foundation witness instead of recognizing trade-label and circumstantial authentication.
