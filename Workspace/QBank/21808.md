Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Roles of judge and jury
Question: In a federal robbery trial, the prosecutor offers a surveillance video that allegedly shows Aaron entering a store five minutes before the robbery. The store manager testifies that the camera automatically records the store entrance, that the video came from that camera, and that the timestamp matches the store's system. Aaron argues that the video might have been edited and should not go to the jury unless the judge first finds that it is authentic by a preponderance of the evidence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the video unless the judge finds by a preponderance of the evidence that the video is authentic.
Answer B: Admit the video if there is evidence sufficient for a reasonable juror to find that the video is what the prosecutor claims.
Answer C: Admit the video only if Aaron first has an opportunity to cross-examine the person who installed the camera.
Answer D: Exclude the video because any possibility of editing makes authentication impossible.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Authentication is a conditional-relevance question. The judge screens for evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what its proponent claims. The jury then decides what weight to give the authentication proof.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because conditional relevance does not require the judge to find authenticity by a preponderance. C: C is wrong because the installer is not the only possible authenticating witness. D: D is wrong because possible editing may affect weight, but does not automatically bar admissibility if threshold evidence supports authenticity.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b), 901
