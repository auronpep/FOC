Question: A grocery store invited customers into its produce department. For forty minutes, a clear puddle from a leaking refrigeration hose spread behind a display where shoppers could not easily see it. The store had no inspection schedule for that aisle. A customer slipped in the puddle while reaching for fruit. The customer sues the store for negligence. Is the customer likely to establish the store's duty in the negligence claim?
Answer A: Yes, because a customer is an invitee and the store owed reasonable inspection and care for hidden hazards
Answer B: No, because the store is liable only for hazards it actually knew about
Answer C: No, because a spill in a store is an ordinary risk customers must always notice for themselves
Answer D: Yes, because stores are strictly liable for any injury to a customer on the premises
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A customer entering for the store's business is an invitee. The store owed reasonable care, including reasonable inspection for hazards it should discover.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: That narrower warning duty fits licensees more closely. Invitees receive reasonable-care protection, including inspection for hazards the possessor should discover. C: The puddle was clear and hidden behind a display. A land possessor's invitee duty does not disappear merely because the danger is on the floor. D: The claim is negligence. The store owes a duty of reasonable care to invitees, but liability still depends on breach, causation, and damages.
lb_rule_reference: A land possessor owes an invitee reasonable care, including reasonable inspection and protection from hazards the possessor knows or should discover.
