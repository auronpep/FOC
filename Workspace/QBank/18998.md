Question: A state builds a state-owned courthouse and uses state funds to buy stone for the project. In awarding its own purchase contract, the state gives a preference to stone quarried in the state. An out-of-state quarry sues under the DCC. What is the state's strongest defense?
Answer A: The state is acting as a market participant in buying stone for its own project
Answer B: The preference is invalid because a state may never favor local sellers
Answer C: The preference is valid only if Congress approved this exact contract
Answer D: The preference must be analyzed under strict scrutiny because stone is speech
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The state is purchasing goods for itself, not regulating private purchasers generally.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The market participant exception can permit local preference in the state's own market participation. C: Market participation is a separate exception. D: The dispute is a procurement preference, not speech regulation.
lb_rule_reference: When a state acts as a market participant, such as buying goods or hiring for its own project, it may favor its own citizens or businesses in that market.
