internal_id: CP-041
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff sues a defendant in federal court on three separate state-law claims: a $40,000 breach-of-contract claim, a $20,000 fraud claim, and a $17,000 conversion claim. The claims are factually unrelated to each other. The defendant moves to dismiss, arguing that none of the individual claims exceeds $75,000 and therefore the federal court lacks jurisdiction. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Should the court dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because each claim must independently exceed $75,000 and none of the plaintiff's three claims does so
Answer B: No, because the plaintiff may aggregate her three claims against the single defendant, reaching $77,000 in controversy
Answer C: Yes, because the claims are factually unrelated and therefore cannot be treated as a single controversy for AIC purposes
Answer D: No, because the plaintiff's fraud claim alone likely exceeds $75,000 in actual damages once punitive damages are considered
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A single plaintiff asserting multiple claims against a single defendant may aggregate all claims to satisfy the § 1332 amount-in-controversy requirement, regardless of whether the claims arise from the same transaction or are factually related. The three claims total $40,000 + $20,000 + $17,000 = $77,000, which exceeds the $75,000 threshold. The court should deny the motion to dismiss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A single plaintiff may aggregate all of her claims against a single defendant to determine whether the AIC threshold is met — even when the claims are factually unrelated. Here the three claims total $77,000, which exceeds $75,000. Aggregation is available and the threshold is met. C: Aggregation of a single plaintiff's claims against a single defendant is permitted regardless of factual relationship. The common-transaction limitation applies to multi-plaintiff aggregation, not single-plaintiff aggregation. A single plaintiff may always aggregate her own claims against one defendant. D: The question asks about the claims as pleaded: $20,000 for fraud. The AIC analysis looks at what the plaintiff claims in good faith, not speculation about what a jury might award. The correct path is aggregation of the three as-pleaded amounts, not inference about potential punitive awards not mentioned in the fact pattern.
lb_rule_reference: A single plaintiff may aggregate all claims against a single defendant to meet the § 1332 amount-in-controversy requirement; multiple plaintiffs generally cannot aggregate their separate claims.
