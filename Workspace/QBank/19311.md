Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Ruth hired Jacob to paint her house. The signed writing stated the color, price, and completion date, but did not mention cleanup. The writing did not say it was complete and exclusive. Ruth offers evidence that, before signing, Jacob orally promised to remove all paint cans and drop cloths after finishing. Jacob objects under the parol evidence rule. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because a consistent additional cleanup term may supplement a final but partial writing.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because every oral statement made before signing is barred.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence because cleanup relates to the same painting project and therefore contradicts the writing.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if Ruth proves the written contract was never valid.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A final writing may be only a partial integration. A prior consistent additional term can supplement a partially integrated agreement if it does not contradict the writing and the writing was not intended as complete and exclusive.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the parol evidence rule does not bar every prior statement. C: C is wrong because a cleanup promise may supplement the painting terms without contradicting color, price, or date. D: D is wrong because Ruth is not trying to avoid the contract; she is trying to supplement a partial writing.
lb_rule_reference: Partial integration / consistent additional term
