Question: Naomi sold a bakery to Joseph. The contract stated, “Seller promises to give Buyer all existing oven-maintenance manuals within five days after closing.” Naomi delivered the bakery at closing but sent the manuals two days late. Joseph could operate the ovens and suffered no loss from the delay. Joseph refused to make the first installment payment, arguing that Naomi breached first. Who is most likely to prevail?
Answer A: Joseph, because any seller breach excuses all buyer payment duties.
Answer B: Joseph, because post-closing manuals are always express conditions to payment.
Answer C: Naomi, because the late manuals were at most a minor covenant breach and did not discharge Joseph's matured payment duty.
Answer D: Naomi, but only if Joseph expressly waived the manual-delivery promise before closing.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A covenant breach does not automatically excuse the other party's performance. The contract used promise language, the manuals were delivered slightly late, and the delay caused no material failure of the bargain. Joseph's payment duty had matured, though he could seek any proven damages from the minor breach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every breach discharges the other party's duty. B: B is wrong because the language and facts do not make the manuals an express condition to payment. D: D is wrong because waiver is unnecessary when the breach is nonmaterial and the duty has matured.
