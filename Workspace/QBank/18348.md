Question: Noah signed a deed conveying Barnacre to Rachel. Noah placed the deed in his own locked safe and told Rachel, “After I die, you may take the deed if I have not destroyed it before then.” Noah kept the only key and never gave the deed to Rachel or to anyone acting for her. Noah died with the deed still in the safe. Who owns Barnacre?
Answer A: Rachel, because Noah signed a deed naming her as grantee.
Answer B: Noah's estate, because Noah retained control over the deed and never delivered it with present intent to pass title.
Answer C: Rachel, because Noah died before destroying the deed.
Answer D: Rachel only if she records the deed before Noah's estate opens probate.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is surrender of control plus present intent. Noah kept the deed in his own safe, kept the only key, and reserved the practical power to destroy or withhold the deed. That retained control defeats delivery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because signing alone is not delivery. C: C is wrong because failure to destroy the deed before death does not create lifetime delivery. D: D is wrong because recordation after death cannot cure nondelivery.
lb_rule_reference: Keeping a deed under grantor control with power to change course defeats delivery
