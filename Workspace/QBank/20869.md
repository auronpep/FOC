Question: A supermarket is in a section of town where there are sometimes street fights and where pedestrians are occasionally the victims of pickpockets and muggers. In recognition of the unusual number of robberies in the area, the supermarket posted signs in the store that read: "Warning: There are pickpockets and muggers at work in this part of the city. The supermarket is not responsible for the acts of criminals." Other than posting the signs, the supermarket took no other precautions to prevent criminal activity on the premises. One evening, a customer drove to the supermarket to see about a special on turkeys that the supermarket was advertising. She decided that the turkeys were too large and left the store without purchasing anything. In the parking lot, she was attacked by an unknown man who raped her and then ran away. If the customer sues the supermarket, the result should be for the If the customer sues the supermarket, the result should be for the
Answer A: plaintiff, because the supermarket failed to take reasonable steps to protect customers against criminal attack in its parking lot.
Answer B: plaintiff, because the supermarket is liable for harm to business invitees on its premises.
Answer C: defendant, because the warning signs were visible to the customer.
Answer D: defendant, because the rapist was the proximate cause of the customer's injuries.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. The supermarket had a duty to do more than post a warning. The fact that it was aware of ongoing violent crimes in the area meant that it had a duty to take reasonable affirmative steps to keep its customers safe against foreseeable dangers by third persons.

additional_info: An invitee enters the premises in response to an express or implied invitation of the landowner, including store customers. A landowner owes an invitee reasonable and ordinary care in keeping the property reasonably safe. In situations involving tortious acts of third persons, a business owner may still have a duty to exercise reasonable care, including reasonable security measures to prevent attacks or thefts against customers. Thus, a duty to warn in this scenario will not be sufficient; the landowner will likely have to take affirmative steps to prevent the known danger from causing harm.
