Question: Gia sold sandwiches from a sidewalk cart without the required city permit, a misdemeanor. A customer bought a sandwich, ate it without incident, and walked two blocks away. While crossing against a traffic light, the customer was struck by a speeding driver and died. Gia was charged with involuntary manslaughter based on the unlicensed sale. Is Gia guilty of misdemeanor manslaughter?
Answer A: Yes, because Gia was committing a misdemeanor when she sold the sandwich
Answer B: Yes, because any death after a misdemeanor is involuntary manslaughter
Answer C: No, because the unlicensed sale did not actually or proximately cause the traffic death
Answer D: No, because misdemeanor manslaughter applies only to violent misdemeanors
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The customer's death resulted from a traffic collision after the sale was complete. The permit violation did not create or increase the risk that caused death. Causation is missing, so misdemeanor manslaughter fails.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The misdemeanor must cause the death. The traffic collision, not the lack of a permit, caused the customer's death. B: Misdemeanor manslaughter is not strict liability for all later deaths. Actual and proximate cause remain required. D: The decisive problem is causation. Some unlawful-act manslaughter questions consider dangerousness, but here the death was not caused by the unlawful sale at all.
lb_rule_reference: Misdemeanor manslaughter requires the unlawful act to actually and proximately cause death; the misdemeanor label alone is not enough.
