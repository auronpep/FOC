MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Assault
Question: During a parking dispute, Driver stepped within two feet of Neighbor, raised a fist, and said, "Move that cone right now or I will hit you." Driver was blocking the only path around the car, and Neighbor reasonably believed Driver would strike immediately if she did not comply. Driver never touched Neighbor. Neighbor sues Driver for assault. Is Neighbor likely to prevail?
Answer A: No, because Driver's words were conditional
Answer B: No, because Driver never made physical contact
Answer C: Yes, because Driver intentionally created reasonable apprehension of imminent contact
Answer D: Yes, but only because Neighbor was actually afraid
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Driver was close, raised a fist, blocked the path, and threatened immediate contact unless Neighbor complied. The conditional wording did not remove imminence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Conditional words can still create imminent apprehension when the defendant appears ready to act immediately. B: Assault requires reasonable apprehension of imminent contact, not actual contact. D: Assault turns on reasonable apprehension of imminent contact. Fear alone is not the element.
lb_rule_reference: A conditional threat can be assault when it creates reasonable apprehension of imminent harmful or offensive contact and the defendant appears able to carry it out.
