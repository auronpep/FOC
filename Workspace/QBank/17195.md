Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A hiker fell on a trail. A bystander announced that he was a trained medic, told others not to call emergency services, and began treating the hiker. He then left to take a phone call without calling for help. The delay worsened the hiker's injury. Which duty analysis is strongest?
Answer A: The bystander had no duty because he had no initial obligation to help
Answer B: The bystander is liable only if he successfully completed the rescue
Answer C: The bystander owed reasonable care after undertaking aid and inducing others not to call
Answer D: The bystander is strictly liable because he claimed medical training
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: His conduct created reliance and delayed other help. That supports a duty to act reasonably in the rescue effort.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Once he undertook aid and induced reliance by stopping others from calling, he had to use reasonable care. B: The duty is reasonable care in the undertaking, not a duty to achieve a perfect result. D: Reliance supports a negligence duty, not strict liability.
lb_rule_reference: A voluntary undertaking can create a duty to use reasonable care, especially when others rely on it or the undertaking worsens the risk.
