Outline: Joinder of parties
Question: A diversity case is pending between a plaintiff from Ohio and a defendant from Michigan. The court determines that a required party is also from Ohio, so joining that party would destroy complete diversity. What should the court do next?
Answer A: Automatically dismiss because any required party who cannot be joined is indispensable
Answer B: Ignore the required party because diversity cases never allow required-party analysis
Answer C: Apply Rule 19(b) to decide whether the action should proceed without the party or be dismissed
Answer D: Join the party anyway because joinder rules override complete diversity
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When joinder is not feasible because it would destroy jurisdiction, the court applies Rule 19(b)'s equity-and-good-conscience factors.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Dismissal depends on Rule 19(b) factors after feasibility fails. B: Rule 19 still applies in diversity cases. D: Joinder rules do not override subject matter jurisdiction limits.
lb_rule_reference: Required-party status does not end the analysis. If joinder is not feasible, the court applies Rule 19(b) factors to decide whether the action should proceed in equity and good conscience.
