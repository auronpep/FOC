Question: Paul orally agrees to buy 1,000 bronze cups from John for $5 each. Paul sends John $1,000, which the parties identify as payment for 200 cups, and John accepts the payment. No writing is signed. Paul then refuses to buy any cups, and John sues for all 1,000. How much of the oral goods contract is enforceable under the payment exception?
Answer A: None, because payment can never substitute for a signed writing.
Answer B: All 1,000 cups, because any part payment validates the whole contract.
Answer C: Only the 200 cups for which payment was made and accepted.
Answer D: All 1,000 cups if John can prove the oral agreement by clear and convincing evidence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 2-201(3)(c) makes an otherwise valid oral goods contract enforceable with respect to goods for which payment has been made and accepted. The payment was for 200 cups, so the exception supports enforcement only for that quantity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because payment made and accepted is a UCC exception. B: B is wrong because part payment does not automatically validate the entire oral quantity. D: D is wrong because heightened proof does not replace the statutory limit.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(3)(c)
