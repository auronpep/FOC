Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Consent
Question: Plaintiff files a federal product-liability action in District B, where the product exploded and witnesses are located. Defendant manufactured and sold the product only in State A and did not target, ship to, advertise in, or service customers in State B. Defendant timely moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. Plaintiff responds that venue is proper in District B because a substantial part of the events occurred there. What is the best response to Plaintiff's venue argument?
Answer A: Proper venue in District B automatically establishes personal jurisdiction there
Answer B: Proper venue may matter, but Plaintiff still must show personal jurisdiction or a waiver/consent basis
Answer C: Venue is always improper unless personal jurisdiction already exists
Answer D: Personal jurisdiction exists because witnesses are located in District B
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Venue does not cure missing personal jurisdiction. Defendant timely objected, so Plaintiff needs a PJ basis.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Venue and personal jurisdiction are independent. Venue facts do not supply defendant-created minimum contacts. C: Venue and PJ have separate standards. A district may be venue-proper but still lack personal jurisdiction over a defendant. D: Convenience and witness location do not create defendant minimum contacts.
lb_rule_reference: Proper venue does not itself establish personal jurisdiction. The plaintiff must satisfy both venue and personal-jurisdiction requirements unless the defendant waives or consents.
