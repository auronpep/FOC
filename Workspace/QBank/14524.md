Question: A high-volume, pleasure-boat retailer entered into with a boater, a written contract signed by both parties, to sell the boater a power boat for $12,000. The manufacturer's price of the boat delivered to the retailer was $9,500. As the contract provided, the boater paid the retailer $4,000 in advance and promised to pay the full balance upon delivery of the boat. The contract contained no provision for liquidated damages. Prior to the agreed delivery date, the boater notified the retailer that he would be financially unable to conclude the purchase; the retailer thereupon resold the same boat that the boater had ordered to a third person for $12,000. If the boater sues the retailer for restitution of the $4,000 advance payment, which of the following should the court decide?
Answer A: A: The boater's claim should be denied, because, as the party in default, he is deemed to have lost any right to restitution of a benefit conferred on the retailer.
Answer B: B: The boater's claim should be denied, because, but for his repudiation, the retailer would have made a profit on two boat-sales instead of one.
Answer C: C: The boater's claim should be upheld in the amount of $4,000 minus the amount of the retailer's lost profit under its contract with the boater.
Answer D: D: The boater's claims should be upheld in the amount of $3,500 ($4,000 minus $500 as statutory damages under the UCC).
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. The boater will only recover the $4,000 MINUS the "lost volume" seller's lost profits from selling one boat instead of two. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. It would be an unjust enrichment for the retailer, as a lost volume seller, to retain an amount greater than his losses from having one fewer customer. B is incorrect. Even though the seller is entitled to lost profits from selling only one boat rather than two, this is the extent of damages that should be awarded to him. The boater should recover the remainder of the deposit amount, once those lost profits are awarded to the seller. D is incorrect. Because this is a case involving a lost volume seller, the seller will recover the amount of lost profits from the $4,000, with the remainder going to the boater for restitution.
