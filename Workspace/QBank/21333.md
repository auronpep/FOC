Outline: Refreshing recollection
Question: In a federal contract trial, Deborah refreshes her memory on the stand with a three-page memorandum. Only the second paragraph on page one relates to the delivery date Deborah just described. Opposing counsel Ezra inspects the memorandum and asks to introduce all three pages into evidence. Deborah's lawyer objects to the unrelated portions. What should the court do?
Answer A: Allow Ezra to introduce all three pages because any writing used to refresh memory becomes available in full.
Answer B: Allow Ezra to introduce only portions that relate to Deborah's testimony, subject to deletion of unrelated matter.
Answer C: Exclude the entire memorandum because refreshing writings may never be introduced by the adverse party.
Answer D: Admit the entire memorandum only if Deborah's lawyer first offers it as a business record.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 612 gives the adverse party the right to inspect, cross-examine, and introduce in evidence any portion that relates to the witness's testimony. It does not make unrelated portions admissible. The court should limit use to related portions and delete unrelated matter if necessary.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 612 does not authorize wholesale admission of unrelated material. C: C is wrong because related portions may be introduced by the adverse party. D: D is wrong because the adverse party's Rule 612 use is not dependent on the proponent's business-record foundation.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 612(b) / related portions only
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests related-portion limits and avoids automatic whole-document admission.
