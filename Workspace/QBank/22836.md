


BARMATRIX Q#: 22836
Outline_code: 95070403
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Scope of discovery
Question: A man sued his employer for injuries caused by exposure to toxic chemicals. The man's suit was in federal district court based on diversity. Because of the complicated nature of the case, the employer believed that the man was likely to call several expert witnesses at trial. The employer was very concerned about certain experts in the field and wanted to know who was likely to testify on the man's behalf. How can the employer discover the identities of the expert witnesses?
Answer A: A: File a discovery request asking for the names of the expert witnesses.
Answer B: B: File a discovery request asking to see the man's trial plan.
Answer C: C: File a request with the court to learn the names of approved trial witnesses.
Answer D: D: Wait until the man tells the employer the names of his expert witnesses.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(2)(A), a party is required to disclose the identity of any expert witnesses. Because the man is required to disclose, the employer simply has to wait until the man tells him. A, B, and C are incorrect because the employer does not need to do anything to discover the identities of the man's expert witnesses.
