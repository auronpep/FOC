Question: Daniel agreed to paint all 20 rooms of Leah's guesthouse for a lump sum of $40,000, payable “when the work is complete.” The contract did not allocate separate prices by room. Daniel painted eight rooms, then demanded $16,000 as the contract price for the rooms completed. Leah refused. Who is most likely to prevail on Daniel's claim for immediate contract payment?
Answer A: Leah, because the contract was entire and completion or substantial completion was a constructive condition to the lump-sum payment duty.
Answer B: Daniel, because each room is automatically a separate divisible unit.
Answer C: Daniel, because partial work always makes a proportional contract payment immediately due.
Answer D: Leah, but only because Daniel failed to obtain a written certificate of completion.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The contract stated one lump sum payable on completion and did not allocate separate unit prices. Daniel had not substantially completed the whole job, so Leah's duty to pay the contract price had not matured. Daniel may have another theory in restitution depending on circumstances, but not immediate contract-price payment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because rooms are not automatically separate contracts absent allocation or structure. C: C is wrong because partial work does not automatically mature a lump-sum payment duty. D: D is wrong because no certificate term is stated; the issue is the constructive condition of completion.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law constructive conditions / entire contract
