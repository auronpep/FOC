Outline: Refreshing recollection
Question: In a federal civil trial, Abigail uses a maintenance log to refresh her memory while testifying. The judge orders the log produced for opposing counsel, but Abigail's lawyer says the log was left at the office and cannot be produced that day. Opposing counsel asks the court to strike Abigail's testimony automatically. What is the proper ruling?
Answer A: The court must strike Abigail's testimony because any failure to produce a refreshing writing requires striking the witness's testimony.
Answer B: The court may issue an appropriate order, but automatic striking is not required in this civil case.
Answer C: The court must admit the log's contents as true because Abigail used the log while testifying.
Answer D: The court must deny any relief because Rule 612 applies only in criminal prosecutions.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: If a writing is not produced or delivered as ordered under Rule 612, the court may issue any appropriate order. The mandatory strike-or-mistrial consequence applies when the prosecution does not comply in a criminal case. This is a civil case, so the court has remedial discretion rather than an automatic duty to strike.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it imports the criminal-prosecution consequence into a civil case. C: C is wrong because the writing's contents are not automatically admitted as true. D: D is wrong because Rule 612 applies in civil and criminal cases.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 612(c) / failure to produce in civil case
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests modal force and civil/criminal consequence under Rule 612(c).
