


BARMATRIX Q#: 22808
Outline_code: 95070704
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Default judgment
Question: A company sued a man for negligence in federal court alleging that the man's electrical work caused a large fire. The man filed a motion to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction. The court denied the man's motion. The man did not file any other response.Sixty days later, the company asked the clerk to enter default. The clerk did so. The company then applied for the entry of a default judgment and served the man with written notice three days before the default judgment hearing. After the hearing, the court entered a default judgment in the company's favor. Ten days later, the man filed a motion to set aside the default judgment. Is the court likely to grant the man's motion?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the man challenged the court's jurisdiction over the claim.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the man did not receive adequate notice of the hearing.
Answer C: C: No, because the man did not file any other response after the denial of his motion to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction.
Answer D: D: No, because the man did not file his motion to set aside the default judgment within seven days.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 55(b)(2), “[i] f the party against whom a default judgment is sought has appeared personally or by a representative, that party or its representative must be served with written notice of the application at least 7 days before the hearing.” Here, the man appeared before the court when he filed his motion to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction, and the company only served the man three days before the hearing. A is incorrect because the man's jurisdictional challenge would not change the analysis. C and D are incorrect because the man did not receive sufficient notice.
