Question: A salon used its ordinary, nondefective hair dryer while styling a customer's hair. The stylist carelessly held the hot dryer too close to the customer's scalp for several minutes, causing a burn. The customer sues the salon under strict products liability. Is strict products liability the best theory?
Answer A: Yes, because a product was involved in the injury
Answer B: No, because the claim sounds in negligent service rather than a product defect
Answer C: Yes, because service providers are always treated as product sellers
Answer D: No, because strict products liability requires privity with the manufacturer
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The dryer was ordinary and nondefective. The injury resulted from the stylist's use of it during a service.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The facts point to careless service, not a defective product supplied to the customer. C: Using a product while providing a service does not automatically place the service provider in the products distribution chain for the injury. D: The correct reason is no defective product supplied by this defendant, not a privity requirement.
lb_rule_reference: A pure service provider is not strictly liable in products liability when the injury arises from negligent service rather than a defective product supplied in commerce.
