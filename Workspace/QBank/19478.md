Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Moses contracted to buy 200 machines from a seller for $400 each. The seller repudiated. Reasonable substitutes were available for $430 each, but Moses chose not to purchase them because he no longer needed the machines immediately. At trial, Moses seeks the difference between the market price when he learned of the breach and the contract price. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Moses may seek market damages even though he did not cover.
Answer B: Moses is barred from any damages because reasonable substitutes were available.
Answer C: Moses may recover only nominal damages because cover was possible.
Answer D: Moses may recover the full $86,000 market value of the substitute machines.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Cover is optional under Article 2. A buyer's failure to cover within the cover rule does not bar another remedy, including market damages under the buyer's market-damages provision.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because available cover does not eliminate the buyer's market remedy. C: C is wrong because the availability of cover does not reduce the claim to nominal damages. D: D is wrong because market damages are a differential measure, not the full value of substitute goods.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-712(3); UCC §2-713
