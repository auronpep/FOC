Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Trial judge’s discretion to control order and presentation of evidence
Question: In a federal contract trial, Joshua asks Naomi, “Did you receive the signed invoice?” Naomi answers, “No.” Joshua asks the same question again three more times without changing the wording or laying any new foundation. Naomi's counsel objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the court may prevent repetitive questioning that wastes time.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because a party may repeat any relevant question until the witness changes the answer.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if the repeated question is also leading.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because repetition goes only to the jury's view of the witness's credibility.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may control questioning to avoid wasting time and needless repetition. Once the witness has answered the same question, the judge may stop counsel from repeating it without a new basis.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because relevance does not permit unlimited repetition. C: C is wrong because repeated questioning can be controlled even if it is not leading. D: D is wrong because trial control, not merely jury credibility assessment, is at issue.
