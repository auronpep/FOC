


BARMATRIX Q#: 22159
Outline_code: 32020100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: A woman sued a grocery store after slipping on a puddle in the frozen food aisle. The woman offered the testimony of her doctor's receptionist. The receptionist would testify that the woman told her she needed a doctor's appointment because she had been suffering tremendous nerve pain in her hand and elbow for several days after the fall. Is the testimony admissible?
Answer A: A: Yes, as a statement used for the purposes of medical treatment.
Answer B: B: Yes, as a statement of present sense impression.
Answer C: C: No, because the receptionist is not a medical professional.
Answer D: D: No, because there's no indication that the receptionist is unavailable.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The receptionist's testimony is admissible under the hearsay exception for statements made for the purposes of medical treatment or diagnosis. The exception applies to medical professionals and their agents, even if those agents are not themselves medical professionals. Consequently, the woman's statements regarding the need for a medical appointment are admissible. B is incorrect because present sense impression applies to statements made while the declarant was perceiving something or immediately thereafter. Here, the woman had been having the pain for a while, so A is a better answer. C is incorrect because the receptionist does not need to be a medical professional for the exception to apply. D is incorrect because the medical statement exception does not require the declarant to be unavailable.
