MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A company delivered sealed drums of a dangerous chemical to a plant. On the way, the delivery truck stopped at a red light. A distracted motorist rear-ended the truck, and a pedestrian was injured by the collision. The drums remained sealed and did not leak. Can the pedestrian recover from the company under strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity?
Answer A: Yes, because the truck was carrying dangerous chemicals
Answer B: Yes, because strict liability applies to all accidents connected to dangerous materials
Answer C: No, because the injury was an ordinary traffic-collision harm, not harm from the dangerous chemical
Answer D: No, because strict liability is never available for transporting chemicals
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The abnormal chemical risk did not materialize because the drums remained sealed and caused no exposure or explosion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The mere presence of dangerous chemicals does not make every traffic injury a strict-liability harm. B: Strict liability is limited to harms caused by the abnormal risk. D: Transportation can present abnormal risks in some settings, but these facts involve only ordinary collision harm.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability for handling dangerous materials does not cover ordinary harms, such as a routine traffic collision, unless the harm results from the abnormal risk of the dangerous material.
