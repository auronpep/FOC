Question: A city fires a licensed childcare worker and publicly states that she abused children. The city also reports the accusation to the state licensing board, which bars her from childcare work unless the report is cleared. She denies the accusation and asks for a name-clearing hearing. Which result is most likely?
Answer A: No hearing is required because reputation injury is never relevant
Answer B: A name-clearing opportunity is likely required because the accusation changed her legal work status
Answer C: Strict scrutiny applies because childcare is a fundamental right of employment
Answer D: No process is due because government employers may publish any reason for termination
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The public accusation plus licensing consequence implicates a liberty interest in pursuing an occupation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: This is stigma plus a legal bar to childcare work, not reputation alone. C: The remedy is procedural: an opportunity to clear her name, not substantive fundamental-right review. D: Public false charges tied to legal status can trigger procedural safeguards.
lb_rule_reference: Government defamation plus alteration of legal status or loss of a right can create a liberty interest requiring a chance to clear one's name.
