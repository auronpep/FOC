


BARMATRIX Q#: 22552
subject_display: Contracts
topic: Performance, Breach, and Discharge
subtopic: Prospective inability to perform: effect on other party
difficulty: 56050403
Stem / Fact Pattern: An attorney decided to invest $4,000 in art for her office. On November 1, she decided to purchase a painting from the defendant, with delivery to be made to the attorney on December 28 after the painting was framed. On November 15, the attorney learned that the defendant had accidentally sold the painting to another buyer. Based on this knowledge, the attorney gave another art dealer $4,000 to purchase another painting. On November 30, the defendant learned of the mistake and got the painting back from the other buyer. On December 28, when the defendant arrived at the attorney’s office with the framed painting, the attorney refused to accept it. The attorney instituted an action against the defendant on December 29 seeking specific performance of the November 1 agreement. Is the attorney entitled to specific performance?
Answer A: A: No, because the attorney refused to accept delivery of the painting on December 28.
Answer B: B: No, because the attorney has already succeeded in investing $4,000 in art for her office.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the painting is unique.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the defendant’s sale to the other person was an anticipatory repudiation of its contract with the attorney.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Although the attorney’s refusal to accept the painting is excused by her reinvestment of the money in reliance on of the defendant’s prospective inability to perform, her refusal of the defendant’s tender discharges the defendant from further obligation to perform. B is incorrect since the defendant’s obligation to deliver is not dependent on the attorney’s reason for making the purchase. C and D are incorrect since the attorney’s refusal to accept delivery constitutes a waiver of any specific performance rights that she otherwise might have had.
