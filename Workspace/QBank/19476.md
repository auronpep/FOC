Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A building owner hired a licensed company to fumigate a warehouse with a highly toxic gas. Warning signs were posted, and the applicators used standard tarps. Overnight, gas escaped through a shared wall into a neighboring studio and injured an artist. The artist sues the building owner for strict liability. What is the strongest strict-liability analysis?
Answer A: The owner may be strictly liable if the fumigation was abnormally dangerous and the gas injury was within that risk
Answer B: The owner cannot be strictly liable because a licensed contractor did the work carefully
Answer C: The owner is liable for all injuries anywhere in the building during the month after fumigation
Answer D: The artist must prove the owner personally applied the gas
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Highly toxic gas fumigation can be an abnormally dangerous activity in context, and escaping gas injury is the risk that makes the activity abnormal.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Delegation and careful performance do not necessarily avoid strict liability for abnormally dangerous activity harm. C: Liability is tied to harm from the escaping gas risk, not every later injury in the building. D: Strict liability can attach to one who carries on or causes an abnormally dangerous activity despite using contractors.
lb_rule_reference: Fumigation with highly dangerous gas may be abnormally dangerous when the gas escapes and injures neighboring occupants.
