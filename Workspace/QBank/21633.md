Question: A warehouse driver negligently backed a forklift toward a visitor. The visitor jumped aside before contact. The visitor was startled but suffered no physical injury, no emotional symptoms, no medical expense, no lost time, and no property damage. The visitor sues the driver for negligence and asks for nominal damages. What is the driver's strongest defense to the negligence claim?
Answer A: The visitor may recover nominal damages because the driver breached a duty of care
Answer B: The visitor may recover because every near miss creates presumed damages
Answer C: The negligence claim fails because the facts show no actual injury or compensable loss
Answer D: The visitor may recover punitive damages because the driver used a forklift
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The driver breached a duty, but negligence liability also requires actual harm caused by the breach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Negligence requires actual harm; nominal damages do not make breach alone actionable. B: Presumed or nominal damages are not the ordinary negligence measure absent actual loss. D: The facts do not show actual injury or the kind of aggravated misconduct needed for punitive damages.
lb_rule_reference: Negligence is not actionable on breach alone; the plaintiff must prove actual personal injury, property damage, or other compensable loss.
