Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Defamation
Question: A food blogger wrote, "I watched the chef drop a clean spoon, rinse it, and keep cooking. Based on that, I think the chef is careless." The facts described were true. The chef sued for defamation, arguing that the word "careless" harmed his reputation. Is the blogger likely liable for defamation?
Answer A: No, because the statement was an opinion based on disclosed true facts
Answer B: Yes, because any negative opinion about job performance is defamatory
Answer C: Yes, because the blogger published the statement online
Answer D: No, but only because restaurants are public figures
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The reader received the factual basis and could judge the opinion; no undisclosed false fact was implied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Defamation requires a false factual assertion or an opinion implying one. C: Publication alone is not enough; the statement must be actionable fact and false. D: The better reason is fact versus opinion, not plaintiff status.
lb_rule_reference: A statement of pure opinion based on fully disclosed facts is usually not actionable defamation because it does not imply an undisclosed false factual assertion.
