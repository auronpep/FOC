Question: A state allows only state residents to sit for its bar exam. A citizen of another state meets all education and character requirements and agrees to submit to local discipline, but is denied solely because she will remain a nonresident. What is the best Article IV analysis?
Answer A: The exclusion is suspect because practicing law is a common calling protected for nonresident citizens
Answer B: The exclusion is valid automatically because each state controls its own bar
Answer C: The exclusion violates DCC only if the lawyer represents corporations
Answer D: The exclusion is valid because Article IV protects only recreational privileges
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The state discriminates against a nonresident citizen in access to a livelihood without an apparent substantial reason tied to the exclusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: State licensing authority does not permit unjustified nonresident exclusion from a common calling. C: The direct issue is nonresident citizen access to a profession under Article IV. D: Article IV is strongest for important civil privileges such as livelihood, not mere recreation.
lb_rule_reference: A state may not exclude nonresident citizens from practicing law solely because of nonresidency unless it has a substantial reason closely related to the discrimination.
