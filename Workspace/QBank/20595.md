Outline: False pretenses
Question: Nell buys a used laptop from Omar and promises to pay him next Friday. At the time, Nell honestly expects to have the money. The next week she loses her job, decides not to pay, and keeps the laptop. Is Nell guilty of false pretenses based on the original promise?
Answer A: Yes, because any broken promise to pay for property is false pretenses
Answer B: Yes, because failure to pay later proves the promise was false when made
Answer C: No, because she honestly intended to pay when she promised future payment
Answer D: No, because title to personal property can never pass before payment
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts negate a false present intent at the time of the promise.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A later-broken promise honestly made is not a false present or past fact. B: Later nonperformance alone does not prove present fraudulent intent. D: The issue is false representation, not a categorical title rule.
lb_rule_reference: A promise about future performance is not false pretenses if the defendant honestly intended to perform when the promise was made.
