Question: While hunting at dusk, Petra saw movement in brush near a marked trail. Without identifying the target, she fired once, believing it was a deer. The shot killed a hiker. Petra had ignored a safety rule requiring hunters to confirm the target before firing. What is the best homicide classification?
Answer A: Murder, because a person died from Petra's gunshot
Answer B: Involuntary manslaughter, because Petra's criminal negligence caused the hiker's death
Answer C: No homicide liability, because Petra honestly believed she was shooting at a deer
Answer D: Voluntary manslaughter, because Petra acted under sudden excitement
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Petra violated a basic firearm safety rule by firing without confirming her target near a marked trail. That gross negligence caused death, supporting involuntary manslaughter.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The facts show negligent failure to identify a target, not intent or conscious extreme indifference to human life. C: A genuine mistake can still be criminally negligent when the defendant takes an unjustified lethal risk. D: Voluntary manslaughter requires adequate provocation and heat of passion, not careless target identification.
lb_rule_reference: Criminally negligent conduct causing death supports involuntary manslaughter when the risk is serious but does not show the extreme conscious disregard required for depraved-heart murder.
