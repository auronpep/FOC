Question: Defendant moves to dismiss under Rule 12(b)(6). The court relies on defendant's new witness affidavits that are not attached to or incorporated in the complaint. The plaintiff had no notice that the court would treat the motion as summary judgment. What is the procedural problem?
Answer A: There is no problem because judges may consider any evidence on Rule 12(b)(6)
Answer B: The court should exclude the material or convert the motion and give a reasonable opportunity to respond
Answer C: The motion automatically becomes JMOL
Answer D: The court must enter default against defendant
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Considering outside material triggers Rule 12(d) conversion safeguards.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 12(b)(6) usually tests the pleading, not outside evidence. C: JMOL is a trial motion under Rule 50. D: Default is unrelated to outside-evidence conversion.
lb_rule_reference: If matters outside the pleadings are presented and not excluded on a Rule 12(b)(6) or 12(c) motion, the motion must be treated as one for summary judgment with reasonable opportunity to present pertinent material.
