Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Roles of judge and jury
Question: In a federal criminal trial, Peter moves to suppress a statement he made to officers. At the suppression hearing, Peter testifies only that officers questioned him before giving warnings. The prosecutor then tries to cross-examine Peter about where he was on the night of the crime. How should the court rule on that cross-examination?
Answer A: Permit it because any defendant who testifies waives all limits on cross-examination.
Answer B: Permit it because the jury is not present.
Answer C: Bar it because Peter testified only on a preliminary question and does not become subject to cross-examination on other issues in the case.
Answer D: Bar it only if Peter also invokes the privilege against self-incrimination.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A defendant who testifies on a preliminary question does not, by doing so, become subject to cross-examination on other issues in the case.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because preliminary-question testimony does not open all merits issues. B: B is wrong because the absence of the jury does not expand cross-examination beyond the preliminary matter. D: D is wrong because the evidence rule itself limits cross-examination in this setting.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(d)
