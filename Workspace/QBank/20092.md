Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: Joseph is charged with stealing cash from a workplace safe. The prosecutor offers evidence that Joseph received three overdue rent notices during the week before the cash disappeared. Joseph objects that rent notices do not prove theft. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the notices, because motive evidence is irrelevant unless it proves the crime by itself.
Answer B: Admit the notices, because financial pressure has some tendency to show motive.
Answer C: Exclude the notices unless the landlord testifies that Joseph actually read them.
Answer D: Admit the notices only if the prosecutor proves Joseph had no other way to pay rent.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The overdue rent notices have some tendency to show financial pressure and motive. They do not prove theft alone, but relevance does not require that level of proof.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because motive evidence can be relevant even when it does not independently prove the offense. C: C is wrong because receipt of the notices may be enough to create a logical link, assuming the proponent can establish the relevant foundation. D: D is wrong because the prosecution need not prove Joseph had no other funds before the evidence becomes relevant.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is demanding that motive evidence prove conduct conclusively.
