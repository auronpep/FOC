Outline_code: 82010402
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Duties and remedies
Question: The tenant rented commercial office space from the landlord for a rent of $3,000 a month. As part of the lease, the tenant was given the right to exclusive occupancy of the basement of the building to use as storage. When the tenant moved into the building, it found that the entire basement was filled with building materials belonging to the landlord. The landlord promised to move the materials, and the tenant kept sending requests to the landlord to do so. After six months without the materials being removed from the basement, the tenant withheld the entire seventh month's rent but remained in the premises. The landlord sued the tenant for the month's $3,000 rent. The landlord should recover
Answer A: A: $0.
Answer B: B: $3,000 minus that part of the rent that can be apportioned to the basement.
Answer C: C: $3,000 minus the cost of cleaning the basement.
Answer D: D: $3,000.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: If the landlord makes it physically impossible for the tenant to occupy some portion of the premises, the lease has been breached. This is called an actual partial eviction. In that case, the tenant is entitled to withhold the entire rent, not just the part attributable to the portion of the premises from which the tenant has been actually evicted. Importantly, the tenant can withhold rent without moving out of the rest of the premises. Here, since it is physically impossible for the tenant to occupy the basement, the landlord has committed an actual partial eviction of the tenant. Therefore, the tenant is entitled to withhold all of the month's rent, and he does not need to move out of the premises.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B, C, and D are therefore incorrect.
