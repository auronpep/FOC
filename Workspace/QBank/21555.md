Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: A New York plaintiff sues two Texas defendants in federal court under diversity for injuries from a warehouse explosion. Defendant 1 asserts a state-law crossclaim against Defendant 2 for contribution arising from the same explosion. The crossclaim is for $30,000. May the federal court hear Defendant 1's crossclaim?
Answer A: No, because the crossclaim is below $75,000
Answer B: Yes, because the defendant crossclaim shares the same explosion facts and Section 1367(b)'s plaintiff-claim restriction does not bar it
Answer C: No, because Section 1367(b) bars all supplemental claims in diversity cases
Answer D: Yes, but only if the plaintiff consents
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The crossclaim forms part of the same case and is asserted by a defendant, not a plaintiff.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Supplemental claims do not need an independent amount threshold. C: The statute is narrower than that. D: Consent cannot create or defeat statutory SMJ.
lb_rule_reference: Related crossclaims between defendants can fall within supplemental jurisdiction when they share a common nucleus of operative fact.
