Question: A delivery driver leaves his assigned route for an hour to visit a friend across town. After the visit, he drives back toward the next assigned delivery stop. Two blocks before reaching that stop, he negligently causes a collision. Is the employer's strongest argument against vicarious liability likely successful?
Answer A: Yes, because once an employee starts a frolic, scope of employment can never resume that day
Answer B: Yes, because driving is never within the scope of employment if the employee earlier had a personal motive
Answer C: No, because the driver had likely reentered the scope by returning to the assigned delivery route
Answer D: No, because employers are strictly liable for every accident involving a company vehicle
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Although the visit was a frolic, the collision occurred after the driver resumed the employer's mission.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Scope can resume when the employee returns to the employer's business. B: The relevant question is the employee's activity at the time of the tort. D: The scope analysis still matters.
lb_rule_reference: An employee who substantially abandons the employer's business is outside the scope during the frolic, but may reenter the scope after returning to the employment mission.
