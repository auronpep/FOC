Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A chemical plant stored a large volume of highly toxic gas in tanks on site. A valve was opened during storage operations, gas escaped, and neighboring residents suffered inhalation injuries. The residents sue under products liability, but no one alleges a defect in a product sold or distributed to them. What is the best classification?
Answer A: Products liability, because a chemical substance was involved
Answer B: Abnormally dangerous activity may be the better lane if the harm came from the toxic storage risk
Answer C: Products liability automatically applies because strict liability governs every dangerous substance
Answer D: No tort theory is possible because no consumer bought the gas
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The injury arose from escape of toxic gas during storage operations. Without a sold defective product, the product-liability lane is the wrong box.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The facts focus on storage and escape from an activity, not a defect in a product supplied to plaintiffs. C: Strict liability doctrines still require the right gate: product defect, animal, or abnormally dangerous activity. D: A non-products strict-liability or negligence theory may still exist. The absence of a product sale does not erase all tort theories.
lb_rule_reference: Product-adjacent facts still require a product defect; harm from escape of stored toxic chemicals may instead test abnormally dangerous activity risk-scope.
