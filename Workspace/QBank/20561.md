MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: In a federal assault trial, Caleb claims that Levi started the fight. To show Levi's temper, Caleb offers testimony from six neighbors about a complicated property-line dispute Levi had with a different neighbor three years earlier. The testimony would require several days of evidence about surveys, fences, and unrelated lawsuits. What is the strongest Rule 403 basis for excluding the evidence?
Answer A: The evidence would risk confusing the issues and wasting time on a collateral mini-trial.
Answer B: The evidence is inadmissible because all prior disputes are hearsay.
Answer C: The evidence is inadmissible because Rule 403 excludes all evidence of a person's temper.
Answer D: The evidence must be admitted because it has at least some probative value.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Even if the evidence has some probative value, Rule 403 permits exclusion when that value is substantially outweighed by confusion of the issues or waste of time. A multi-day trial about a collateral property dispute risks distracting the jury from the charged assault.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the problem is not that all prior disputes are hearsay. C: C is wrong because Rule 403 is a balancing rule, not a categorical bar on temperament evidence. D: D is wrong because relevance is only the first step; Rule 403 can exclude relevant evidence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403
lb_student_diagnostic: Student ignores the mini-trial and confusion counterweights.
