


BARMATRIX Q#: 22806
Outline_code: 92080105
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: A farmer filed a negligence claim against a paint factory in federal court. The farmer alleged that chemical runoff from the factory was poisoning his crops. After discovery, the factory moved for summary judgment. In support of its motion, the factory submitted a memorandum identifying facts that it claimed were not in dispute. It also cited and attached a report from a hydrologist who stated that any contamination found on the farmer's land could not have come from chemical runoff from the factory.What is the best way for the farmer to raise a genuine dispute of material fact?
Answer A: A: Submit a report from the farmer's expert hydrologist that contradicts the findings in the factory hydrologist's report.
Answer B: B: Submit the farmer's records showing the current level of pollution on the farmer's land.
Answer C: C: Submit an affidavit from the farmer's attorney detailing his conversations with the factory's hydrologist.
Answer D: D: Submit an affidavit from the farmer's expert hydrologist with findings that contradict the report of the factory's hydrologist.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The best way for the farmer to challenge the motion for summary judgment and raise a genuine issue of material fact is for the farmer's expert hydrologist to submit an affidavit directly contradicting the findings of the factory's hydrologist. Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(c)(4). A is incorrect because the expert hydrologist needs to submit an affidavit, not simply a report. B is incorrect because the current level of pollution on the farmer's land would have no bearing on the farmer's claim that the factory was the source of the pollution. C is incorrect because whatever conversations the farmer's attorney had with the factory's hydrologist would not necessarily contradict the factory hydrologist's statements regarding the alleged pollution.
