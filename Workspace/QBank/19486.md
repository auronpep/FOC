Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Res ipsa loquitur
Question: A sealed ceiling light fixture in a restaurant's dining room fell onto a customer's table during dinner. The restaurant alone maintained the fixture. The customer did not touch it and has no direct evidence of the specific maintenance error. What is the likely effect of res ipsa loquitur?
Answer A: It permits an inference that the restaurant was negligent, but it does not automatically compel judgment
Answer B: It makes the restaurant strictly liable for the falling fixture
Answer C: It cannot apply because the customer lacks direct proof of the exact maintenance error
Answer D: It cannot apply unless the customer proves the fixture was defective when manufactured
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A falling maintained fixture is the kind of event that ordinarily suggests negligence, and the restaurant had responsibility. Res ipsa usually permits an inference rather than automatic liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Res ipsa is an evidentiary inference in negligence, not strict liability. C: Res ipsa exists for cases where direct evidence is unavailable but the circumstances support an inference. D: The claim is about restaurant maintenance responsibility. A manufacturing defect is not required for res ipsa.
lb_rule_reference: Res ipsa loquitur allows, but usually does not compel, an inference of negligence from certain accident circumstances.
