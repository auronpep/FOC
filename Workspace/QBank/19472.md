Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Trespass to land
Question: To mark a possible driveway, a developer tied bright survey string between two stakes. Wanting a straight line, the developer intentionally placed one stake and three feet of string across a neighbor's lawn. The string was removed the next morning and left no mark. The neighbor sues the developer for trespass to land. Is the neighbor likely to prevail in a trespass to land claim against the developer?
Answer A: No, because no person walked onto the neighbor's lawn
Answer B: No, because the string caused no visible damage
Answer C: Yes, because the developer intentionally placed a physical object on the neighbor's land
Answer D: No, unless the developer knew the exact property boundary
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The developer intentionally caused a stake and string to enter the neighbor's land. That physical invasion supports trespass despite its short duration and lack of damage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Trespass can occur through an object intentionally placed on another's land. B: Lack of damage does not defeat trespass to land. The tort protects possession from unauthorized physical invasion. D: Trespass requires intent to place the object where it was placed, not knowledge that the location belongs to another.
lb_rule_reference: A physical object intentionally placed on another's land can constitute trespass even when the entry is minor and harmless.
