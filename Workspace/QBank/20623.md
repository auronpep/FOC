Question: A state agency is sued over a permit issued to a mining company. The agency intends to defend the permit, but it must also consider statewide environmental and administrative policies. The mining company moves to intervene, arguing that the permit is central to its only operating site and that the agency may settle on terms that protect broader state interests while sacrificing the company's project. How should the court analyze adequacy of representation for intervention of right?
Answer A: Adequacy is automatically satisfied because the agency and the company both want the permit upheld
Answer B: Adequacy is automatically lacking whenever a private party disagrees with a government litigation strategy
Answer C: The court should consider whether the agency may adequately protect the company's specific permit interest, despite their aligned goal
Answer D: Adequacy is irrelevant if the company has a property or economic interest in the permit
Correct Answer: C
lb_rule_reference: For intervention of right, the proposed intervenor must show that existing parties may not adequately represent its interest; aligned objectives can matter, but a distinct narrower interest may support inadequacy.
