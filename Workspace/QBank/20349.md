Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Ezra manufactured custom chairs. Elizabeth repudiated a contract to buy 100 chairs for $200 each. Ezra had a per-chair variable cost of $120 and allocated fixed overhead of $20 per chair. Ezra could not use the market-price formula to put himself in the position performance would have done. No resale proceeds are involved. Under the seller-profit measure, what profit component is included before any incidental damages?
Answer A: $8,000, because profit includes the $80 margin per chair, including reasonable overhead, multiplied by 100.
Answer B: $6,000, because fixed overhead must always be subtracted from the seller's profit.
Answer C: $20,000, the full contract price.
Answer D: $12,000, Ezra's variable production cost.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When the ordinary market measure is inadequate, the seller may recover the profit, including reasonable overhead, that full performance would have produced. The margin is $200 minus $120, or $80 per chair, for 100 chairs: $8,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because reasonable overhead is included in the profit measure rather than automatically deducted. C: C is wrong because the full contract price overcompensates when the seller seeks profit rather than price. D: D is wrong because production cost is not the profit component.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-708(2) / profit including reasonable overhead
