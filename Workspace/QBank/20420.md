Question: A cyclist loaned her bicycle to a friend for one afternoon. Instead of returning it, the friend sold the bicycle to a stranger and kept the money. The cyclist later located the bicycle, but the stranger refused to return it without payment. The cyclist sues the friend for conversion. Is conversion the best claim?
Answer A: Yes, because selling the bicycle was serious dominion inconsistent with the cyclist's rights
Answer B: No, because the friend initially received the bicycle with permission
Answer C: No, because the bicycle was not destroyed
Answer D: Yes, but only for repair-cost damages
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Unauthorized sale is serious interference and supports full-value conversion damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Lawful initial possession can become conversion when the defendant later asserts wrongful dominion. C: Conversion can occur by sale or serious dominion even without destruction. D: Conversion generally permits full market value, not just repair cost.
lb_rule_reference: Conversion requires serious interference with another's chattel, such as unauthorized sale or dominion, warranting full market value damages.
