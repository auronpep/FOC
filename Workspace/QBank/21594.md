Question: Solomon signed and delivered a deed conveying his store building to Peter. Peter handed Solomon a check for the purchase price. The deed did not state that delivery was conditional on the check clearing. Peter accepted the deed, but the check later bounced. Who owns the store building?
Answer A: Solomon, because Peter's payment failed after delivery.
Answer B: Solomon, because a deed for a sale is invalid unless the price is actually collected.
Answer C: Peter, because the delivered deed passed title; Solomon's nonpayment claim is a separate remedy issue.
Answer D: Peter only if he pays the price before recording the deed.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is delivery of a properly executed instrument, not later collection of the purchase price. Solomon delivered the deed without making delivery conditional on the check clearing, and Peter accepted. The bounced check may support a money claim or equitable remedy, but it does not undo the deed-validity lock.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because failure of payment after unconditional delivery does not automatically return title. B: B is wrong because deed validity is not dependent on actual collection of consideration. D: D is wrong because recording is not the condition that makes the deed valid between Solomon and Peter.
lb_rule_reference: Failure of consideration after delivery
