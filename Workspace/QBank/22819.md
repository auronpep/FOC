


BARMATRIX Q#: 22819
Outline_code: 94030100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Removal to Federal Court
Question: A man sued his employer in state court, asserting discrimination claims under both state and federal law. The man's attorney had a terrible relationship with the judge to whom the case was assigned. Because he wanted to avoid problems with the judge, the man's attorney promptly removed the claim to federal court on the basis of federal question jurisdiction. The employer moved to remand. How should the federal court rule?
Answer A: A: Retain the case to avoid the risk of an unfair trial.
Answer B: B: Retain the case because the man alleged federal claims.
Answer C: C: Remand the state claims.
Answer D: D: Remand the entire case.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under 28 U.S.C. §1441(a), only defendants can remove a state action to federal court. Here, the plaintiff removed the action because he was afraid of judicial bias. Consequently, the entire case should be remanded back to the state court. Therefore, A, B, and C are incorrect.
