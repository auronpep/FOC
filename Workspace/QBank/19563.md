MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: False imprisonment
Question: A librarian closed a study room at night after checking the sign-out sheet, which incorrectly showed that no one remained inside. A student had fallen asleep behind a tall shelf. The librarian locked the door and left. The student woke up an hour later, could not leave, and missed an exam review. The student sues the librarian for false imprisonment. Is the student likely to prevail in a false imprisonment claim against the librarian?
Answer A: Yes, because the student was actually confined in a locked room
Answer B: No, because the librarian lacked intent to confine the student
Answer C: No, because the student was asleep when the door was locked
Answer D: Yes, because missing an exam review supplies the necessary harm
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The librarian accidentally locked the student in after a mistaken check. Without intent to confine, false imprisonment is not established.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: False imprisonment requires intentional confinement. The librarian did not know the student was inside and did not intend to confine anyone. C: Awareness during confinement can be enough once the student woke up. The stronger reason is lack of defendant intent. D: Harm does not replace the intentional-confinement element. Accidental confinement may suggest negligence, but not false imprisonment.
lb_rule_reference: False imprisonment is an intentional tort; accidental confinement is not false imprisonment without intent to confine or transferred intent.
