Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Amendment of Pleadings
Question: A plaintiff timely sues a driver for negligence arising from a collision at a particular intersection on June 1. After the limitations period expires, the plaintiff seeks to amend to add a negligent entrustment theory against the same defendant based on the same car, driver, and collision. The defendant objects that the new theory is late. Does the amendment relate back?
Answer A: No, because any amendment after limitations expires is barred
Answer B: No, because a new legal theory can never relate back
Answer C: Yes, because the new theory arises from the same collision described in the original complaint
Answer D: Yes, but only if the defendant expressly waives the statute of limitations
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The original complaint gave notice of the collision and factual transaction at issue. The negligent entrustment theory arises from the same occurrence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 15(c) allows relation back for certain amendments. The timing question does not end the analysis. B: The key is whether the amendment arises from the same conduct, transaction, or occurrence. New legal labels can relate back when the factual core is the same. D: Relation back can solve the problem without express waiver when Rule 15(c)'s requirements are met.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 15(c)(1)(B) permits relation back for an amendment asserting a claim or defense arising out of the conduct, transaction, or occurrence set out or attempted to be set out in the original pleading.
