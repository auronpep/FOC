Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Elijah signed a deed conveying Millacre to Elisha. Elijah sent the original deed to the county recorder with a signed note stating, “Please record this deed. I have conveyed Millacre to Elisha as of today.” Elisha was away on a trip and did not physically receive the deed before recording. Assuming Elisha never rejected the conveyance, who owns Millacre?
Answer A: Elisha, because Elijah's act of recording with present-transfer intent can satisfy delivery, and acceptance of the beneficial deed is presumed.
Answer B: Elijah, because title cannot pass unless the grantee personally holds the deed before recording.
Answer C: Elijah, because acceptance must always be signed by the grantee on the deed itself.
Answer D: Elisha only if Elijah also gave Elisha a separate bill of sale for the land.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is present intent shown by Elijah's recording instruction. A manual handoff to Elisha was not required. Because the conveyance was beneficial and Elisha did not reject it, acceptance is presumed and title passed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because delivery can occur without the grantee's physical possession. C: C is wrong because grantee signature or written acceptance on the deed is not generally required. D: D is wrong because land title transfers by deed, not a bill of sale.
lb_rule_reference: No hand-to-hand transfer is required if the grantor records the deed with present intent to convey
