Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: After Jade is indicted for fraud, her codefendant agrees to cooperate with prosecutors. At their direction, the codefendant visits Jade and repeatedly asks how she created the false invoices. Jade answers. Her lawyer is not present and Jade did not waive counsel. Should the statements be suppressed under the Sixth Amendment?
Answer A: Yes, because a government-directed codefendant deliberately elicited statements about the charged offense after indictment
Answer B: No, because codefendants are always private actors for Sixth Amendment purposes
Answer C: No, because Jade was not in a police station
Answer D: Yes, because all conversations with codefendants are inadmissible after indictment
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The codefendant acted as a government agent and intentionally questioned Jade about the charged fraud after the right had attached.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A private person can become a government agent when acting at government direction. C: Massiah does not require custody or a stationhouse interview. D: Suppression depends on government agency and deliberate elicitation, not merely codefendant status.
lb_rule_reference: After attachment, a cooperating codefendant acting at government direction can be a government agent for Massiah if the codefendant deliberately elicits statements about the charged offense.
