Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: An undercover officer meets with Arman and Diego about selling stolen medical devices. The officer only pretends to agree. Arman and Diego, however, genuinely agree with each other to steal and sell the devices and assign roles for the next day. The jurisdiction follows common-law bilateral conspiracy. Can Arman and Diego be guilty of conspiracy?
Answer A: No, because an undercover officer was present and never truly agreed
Answer B: Yes, because two genuine participants agreed to pursue the criminal objective
Answer C: No, because the devices were not yet stolen when the agreement was made
Answer D: Yes, but only under the MPC unilateral rule
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Common-law bilateral conspiracy requires at least two persons who truly agree and intend the crime. Arman and Diego satisfy that requirement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Arman and Diego genuinely agreed with each other. The officer's feigned agreement is irrelevant once two real participants share the criminal objective. C: Conspiracy is complete with the required agreement, subject to any overt-act requirement not raised here. D: The common-law bilateral rule is satisfied because Arman and Diego are two genuine participants.
lb_rule_reference: Under the common-law bilateral rule, an undercover officer's feigned agreement does not defeat conspiracy if at least two other participants genuinely agree to the criminal objective.
