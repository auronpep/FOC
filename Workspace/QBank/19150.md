Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A warehouse employee finished breakfast at home and drove his own car toward the warehouse for a regular shift. He was not carrying company property, was not paid for travel time, and had not been asked to make any stop for the employer. Two blocks from the warehouse, he negligently changed lanes and struck a cyclist. The cyclist sues the warehouse owner for vicarious liability, arguing that the employee was on his way to work and the accident happened near the warehouse. Is the cyclist likely to prevail on a vicarious liability claim against the warehouse owner under respondeat superior?
Answer A: Yes, because the employee was traveling to work and the accident occurred near the employer's premises
Answer B: No, because ordinary commuting without a special employer purpose is outside the scope of employment
Answer C: No, because employers are never liable for automobile accidents unless they own the vehicle
Answer D: Yes, because the employee's negligence occurred shortly before the shift began
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The employee was not on duty, was not paid for travel, used his own car, and was not serving an employer errand. The going-and-coming rule clips respondeat superior liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ordinary commuting in the employee's own car, unpaid and without an employer errand, is generally outside the scope of employment. Physical proximity to the workplace does not make the commute employer service. C: Vehicle ownership is not the controlling rule. An employer can be vicariously liable for an employee's driving within scope even in a non-owned vehicle; this claim fails because the trip was an ordinary commute. D: Time is only one coordinate. Without paid travel, employer control, a work errand, or a special benefit to the employer, the ordinary commute remains outside scope.
lb_rule_reference: Ordinary commuting to or from work is generally outside the scope of employment unless the trip itself serves the employer in a special way.
