Question: A store placed the only checkout line so customers had to walk around a clearly visible floor opening. The store knew customers carrying large boxes could not see their feet and had no practical alternate route. A customer carrying a box stepped into the opening. Does the obviousness of the opening automatically eliminate the store's duty?
Answer A: Yes, because open and obvious hazards never require precautions
Answer B: Yes, because customers carrying boxes become trespassers
Answer C: No, because the store should anticipate customers would encounter the opening despite its visibility
Answer D: No, because stores are strictly liable for checkout injuries
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The store created a route where customers with boxes had to face the hazard. The risk remained foreseeable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A possessor may still owe precautions when it should anticipate invitees will encounter an obvious risk. B: The customer remains an invitee using the checkout route. D: The duty is reasonable care, not strict liability.
lb_rule_reference: An open and obvious danger may still require precautions when the possessor should anticipate invitees will encounter it despite the danger.
