Outline: Pardon
Question: A mayor is convicted in federal court of bribing a federal inspector and is also indicted in state court for falsifying state campaign records. The President issues a pardon stating that the mayor is forgiven for "all legal consequences arising from the bribery matter." The mayor argues that the pardon also bars the state prosecution and a private civil suit by a contractor. Which result is most accurate?
Answer A: The pardon bars the federal conviction but does not bar the state prosecution or private civil suit
Answer B: The pardon bars all state and federal proceedings because it uses broad language
Answer C: The pardon bars the state prosecution because bribery of a federal inspector is involved
Answer D: The pardon bars the civil suit because civil liability is less severe than criminal punishment
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The President's pardon power extends to federal offenses. It does not cancel state criminal charges or private civil liability, even if those matters arise from related conduct.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The President cannot expand Article II power by wording the pardon broadly. State crimes and private civil claims remain outside the federal pardon power. C: The state charge is brought by a separate sovereign under state law. A federal pardon does not extinguish state criminal liability. D: Pardon power addresses federal offenses, not private civil remedies. The relative severity of the consequence is not the constitutional line.
lb_rule_reference: The President may pardon federal offenses, but the pardon power does not reach state crimes, civil liability, or impeachment consequences.
