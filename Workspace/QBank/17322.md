MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Commerce power
Question: A state allows in-state wineries to ship directly to consumers but forbids out-of-state wineries from doing the same. The state defends the rule by citing the Twenty-first Amendment and a general interest in alcohol regulation, but it identifies no nondiscriminatory reason for treating out-of-state wineries worse. What is the likely DCC result?
Answer A: The rule is automatically valid because alcohol is involved
Answer B: The rule is suspect because it discriminates against out-of-state wineries without a sufficient justification
Answer C: The rule receives rational basis because alcohol is not commerce
Answer D: The rule violates Article IV only if the wineries are natural persons
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The state treats in-state and out-of-state wineries differently, and the Twenty-first Amendment does not automatically save that discrimination.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Alcohol authority is not a blanket shield for economic protectionism. C: Alcohol is commerce, and discriminatory alcohol regulations can trigger DCC scrutiny. D: This is a DCC commerce discrimination problem; Article IV citizen limits do not replace it.
lb_rule_reference: The Twenty-first Amendment gives states authority over alcohol, but it does not automatically save protectionist discrimination against interstate commerce.
