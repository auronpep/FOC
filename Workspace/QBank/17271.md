


BARMATRIX Q#: 17271
Outline_code: 56050403
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Contracts
Subtopic: Performance, Breach, and Discharge
Outline: Anticipatory repudiation
Question: An attorney decided to invest $4,000 in art for her office. On November 1, she decided to purchase a painting from the defendant, with delivery to be made to the attorney on December 28 after the painting was framed. On November 15, the attorney learned that the defendant had accidentally sold the painting to another buyer. Based on this knowledge, the attorney gave another art dealer $4,000 to purchase another painting. On November 30, the defendant learned of the mistake and got the painting back from the other buyer. On December 28, when the defendant arrived at the attorney's office with the framed painting, the attorney refused to accept it. The attorney instituted an action against the defendant on December 29 seeking specific performance of the November 1 agreement. With respect to the attorney's demand for specific performance, a court is most likely to find for
Answer A: A: the defendant, since the attorney refused to accept delivery of the painting on December 28.
Answer B: B: the defendant, since the attorney has already succeeded in investing $4,000 in art for her office.
Answer C: C: the attorney, since the painting is unique.
Answer D: D: the attorney, since the defendant's sale to the other person was an anticipatory repudiation of its contract with the attorney.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Although the attorney's refusal to accept the painting is excused by her reinvestment of the money in reliance on of the defendant's prospective inability to perform, her refusal of the defendant's tender discharges the defendant from further obligation to perform.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect since the defendant's obligation to deliver is not dependent on the attorney's reason for making the purchase. C and D are incorrect since the attorney's refusal to accept delivery constitutes a waiver of any specific performance rights that she otherwise might have had.
