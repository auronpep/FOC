Question: A town bans "ritual animal killing conducted outside licensed food facilities" but allows hunting, pest control, farm slaughter, veterinary euthanasia, and secular animal processing. The town enacted the rule after public meetings focused on stopping a particular religious ceremony. A religious group challenges the ordinance. Which statement best describes the Free Exercise issue?
Answer A: Rational basis applies because the ordinance avoids naming the religion
Answer B: Strict scrutiny likely applies because the ordinance appears to target religious conduct while preserving comparable secular conduct
Answer C: The law is invalid only if officials admit hostility to religion in the ordinance text
Answer D: The law is valid because animal welfare always defeats Free Exercise objections
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The many secular exceptions and meeting history support a religious-targeting inference. The law is not neutral or generally applicable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Neutrality can fail when the structure and history show targeting of religious conduct. C: Targeting can be shown by design, exemptions, and context; explicit textual hostility is not required. D: Even a compelling interest must be pursued through appropriately tailored means once strict scrutiny applies.
lb_rule_reference: A law that is drafted to target religious conduct is not neutral and triggers strict scrutiny even if it avoids naming a religion.
