Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: A small-claims tribunal resolves a $200 dispute after a five-minute hearing with no discovery and no right to counsel. A later federal case involving the same factual issue seeks millions of dollars. The party who lost in small claims argues that issue preclusion would be unfair. Which answer is best?
Answer A: Issue preclusion is automatic because the same factual issue appeared
Answer B: The court should consider whether the party had a full and fair opportunity and adequate incentive to litigate
Answer C: Claim preclusion automatically applies because damages are higher now
Answer D: Issue preclusion never applies to tribunal decisions
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Issue preclusion includes fairness and opportunity limits.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Same issue alone is not enough. C: The question concerns an issue and the parties/opportunity, not claim splitting. D: Some tribunal decisions can have preclusive effect depending on governing law and procedures.
lb_rule_reference: Even when an issue was decided, fairness and opportunity to litigate remain part of issue-preclusion analysis.
