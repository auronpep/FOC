Question: Isaac owned Lot 1 and Lot 2. Unknown to anyone except Isaac, a buried drainpipe from Lot 1 crossed beneath Lot 2. There were no surface drains, cleanouts, markers, recorded plans, or other visible signs of the pipe. Isaac sold Lot 2 to Rachel and later sold Lot 1 to Joseph. Joseph then discovered the pipe and claimed an implied easement by prior use across Rachel's land. What is the best result?
Answer A: Joseph has an implied easement because any underground utility that existed before severance is automatically apparent.
Answer B: Joseph has an easement by necessity because underground drainage is always strictly necessary.
Answer C: Joseph has a profit because the pipe carries water from one parcel to another.
Answer D: Joseph likely has no implied easement by prior use because the use was not apparent or reasonably discoverable at severance.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A prior-use easement generally requires an apparent, continuous use that is reasonably necessary at severance. A completely hidden underground pipe with no visible or recorded indicators is not apparent or reasonably discoverable on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because existence alone is not the apparent-use requirement. B: B is wrong because drainage is not automatically strict necessity, and the claim is framed as prior use. C: C is wrong because a profit is a right to remove resources from land. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Apparent use requirement
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating every preexisting physical condition as apparent even when no buyer could reasonably detect it.
