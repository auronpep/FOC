Question: A plaintiff sues for emotional-distress damages and alleges continuing psychiatric injury. The defendant emails the plaintiff's lawyer a notice stating that the plaintiff must appear tomorrow for a psychiatric examination, without seeking a court order or showing good cause. What is the best objection?
Answer A: The defendant may compel the exam by notice alone because mental condition is relevant
Answer B: The exam can be ordered only if the plaintiff's condition is in controversy and good cause is shown
Answer C: Mental examinations are never allowed in civil discovery
Answer D: The exam is automatic because the plaintiff requested damages
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The claimed psychiatric injury may put condition in controversy, but the defendant must use the Rule 35 order procedure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 35 requires a court order and good cause, not just relevance and notice. C: Rule 35 permits mental examinations when its requirements are satisfied. D: Even when condition is in controversy, the movant must show good cause and obtain an order.
lb_rule_reference: A court may order a party whose physical or mental condition is in controversy to submit to an exam only for good cause and on notice specifying the exam details.
