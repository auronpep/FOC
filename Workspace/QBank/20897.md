Question: Solomon is charged with burning his warehouse. The prosecutor offers evidence that Solomon increased the warehouse's fire-insurance coverage six weeks before the fire. Solomon objects that business owners often adjust insurance for innocent reasons. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence because innocent explanations are possible.
Answer B: Admit the evidence because it has some tendency to show motive; innocent business explanations affect weight.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence because insurance evidence is never admissible.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if the insurer denied Solomon's claim.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The insurance increase is circumstantial evidence of possible financial motive. Innocent explanations may reduce its persuasive force but do not make it irrelevant. The evidence is not offered to prove negligence through insurance coverage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because alternative innocent explanations do not automatically bar relevant motive evidence. C: C is wrong because insurance evidence is not categorically inadmissible for every purpose. D: D is wrong because motive evidence does not depend on the insurer denying the claim.
