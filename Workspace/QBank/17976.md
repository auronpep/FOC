Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Attorney-client
Question: Rachel sues Luke in federal court. During document production, Rachel's lawyer Daniel inadvertently produces one email in which Rachel asked Daniel for legal advice about the dispute. Before production, Daniel's team used a reasonable privilege-screening process for the volume of documents. The next afternoon, after discovering the error, Daniel notifies Luke, identifies the email, and demands that it be returned or sequestered. Luke argues that the production waived attorney-client privilege. May Luke use the email on the ground that privilege was waived by production?
Answer A: Yes. Any actual production of a privileged email in discovery automatically waives the privilege.
Answer B: Yes. Production of one privileged email automatically waives privilege over all communications on the same subject.
Answer C: No. If the disclosure was inadvertent and Rachel took reasonable prevention and prompt-rectification steps, the production does not operate as a waiver.
Answer D: No, but only because a court order had already been entered stating that no disclosure could ever waive privilege.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 502(b) rejects automatic waiver for qualifying inadvertent disclosures in a federal proceeding. Rachel's lawyer used a reasonable screening process and promptly demanded return or sequestration after discovering the mistake. The decisive axis is not the mere fact of disclosure; it is whether the Rule 502(b) prevention and rectification conditions are satisfied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because actual production is not automatically waiver under Rule 502(b) when the disclosure was inadvertent, reasonable precautions were taken, and prompt rectification followed. B: B is wrong because subject-matter waiver is not automatic. Rule 502(a) limits subject-matter waiver to intentional disclosures where fairness requires considering related material together. D: D is wrong because a Rule 502(d) court order can provide additional protection, but it is not the only way to avoid waiver. Rule 502(b) itself supplies a no-waiver rule when its conditions are met.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 502(b) inadvertent disclosure
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats completed production as a one-step waiver and misses the Rule 502(b) architecture.
