Question: An innkeeper, who had no previous experience in the motel or commercial laundry business and who knew nothing about the trade usages of either business, bought a motel and signed an agreement with a laundry company for the motel's laundry services. The agreement was for a term of one year and provided for “daily service at $500 a week.” From their conversations during negotiation, the laundry company owner knew that the innkeeper expected laundry services seven days a week. When the laundry company refused to pick up the motel's laundry on two successive Sundays and indicated that it would never do so, the innkeeper canceled the agreement. The laundry company sued the innkeeper for breach of contract. At trial, clear evidence was introduced to show that in the commercial laundry business “daily service” did not include service on Sundays. Is the laundry company likely to succeed in its action?
Answer A: A: No, because the laundry company knew the meaning the innkeeper attached to “daily service,” and therefore the innkeeper's meaning will control.
Answer B: B: No, because the parties attached materially different meanings to “daily service,” and therefore no contract was formed.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the parol evidence rule will not permit the innkeeper to prove the meaning he attached to “daily service.”
Answer D: D: Yes, because the trade usage will control the interpretation of “daily service.”
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. The laundry company knew that the innkeeper thought "daily service" included Sundays, even though it was contrary to industry standards. And the innkeeper, unaware of the industry standard, did not, therefore, know that the laundry company attached a different meaning to the term. In this scenario, the innkeeper's meaning will control, as the innocent, unknowing party. B
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. This is not a situation where the parties attach such different meanings to a material term that no contract was formed. This is because the laundry company knew about the discrepancy in interpretations between the parties. In that scenario, the innkeeper's meaning will govern, as stated above. C is incorrect. The Parol Evidence Rule (PER) only bars a party's attempt to contradict or add a term to an integrated writing. But here, the innkeeper would be the party seeking to introduce earlier evidence to affirm that "daily service" logically means to anticipate service every day. Thus, the PER would only apply if the laundry service were the party introducing evidence, not the innkeeper. D is incorrect. Trade usage is not applicable to make meaning of the term "daily service" here, because the laundry company was aware of the innkeeper's different interpretation and the innkeeper was unaware of that fact, the innkeeper's meaning will prevail.
