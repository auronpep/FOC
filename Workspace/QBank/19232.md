Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: Raquel, while heavily intoxicated, decided to fire her pistol into a crowded outdoor concert venue from a hillside approximately 100 yards away, shooting randomly into the crowd of several hundred people just to "watch everyone scatter." One bullet struck a bystander in the head, killing him instantly. Raquel knew the venue was packed with people. She was not aiming at any individual and had no intent to kill anyone. What is the most serious homicide charge for which Raquel can be convicted?
Answer A: First-degree murder, because Raquel fired into a crowd knowing people were present
Answer B: Involuntary manslaughter, because Raquel did not intend to kill anyone
Answer C: Murder (depraved-heart), because Raquel consciously disregarded an extreme and unjustifiable risk to human life
Answer D: Second-degree murder, because Raquel intended to fire the gun even if not to kill
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Depraved-heart murder is an unintentional killing committed with malice aforethought in the form of extreme recklessness — a conscious disregard of a severe and unjustifiable risk that manifests a depraved indifference to human life. Raquel knew hundreds of people were present, chose to fire randomly into the crowd for amusement, and subjectively recognized the lethal risk. This is the paradigm case: extreme risk, conscious disregard, and conduct showing indifference to human life.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: First-degree murder requires premeditation and deliberation — a prior decision to kill a specific victim after cool reflection. Raquel had no intent to kill anyone. Knowing people are present and firing randomly does not satisfy premeditation; it satisfies extreme recklessness, which is the hallmark of depraved-heart murder, not first-degree murder. B: Involuntary manslaughter involves criminal negligence or a misdemeanor/low-level unlawful act causing death — not conscious, subjective awareness of an extreme and unjustifiable risk. Raquel knew the venue was packed and fired anyway for entertainment. That is conscious disregard of a grave risk, not mere negligence. The degree of risk and subjective awareness elevate this to depraved-heart murder. D: Second-degree murder in its classic form requires an intent to kill (or intent to cause grievous bodily harm). Raquel had no intent to kill or seriously injure anyone. While D reaches a murder charge, it misidentifies the theory. The proper charge is depraved-heart murder, which is a form of second-degree murder but rests on extreme recklessness, not intent. D's stated rationale ("intended to fire") is legally insufficient to establish second-degree murder as normally defined.
lb_rule_reference: Depraved-heart murder requires conscious disregard of an extreme risk to human life that is unjustifiable; this is distinguished from involuntary manslaughter by the degree of risk and the defendant's subjective awareness of it
