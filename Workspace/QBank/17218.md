Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A family dog had always been calm around guests and had never growled, snapped, lunged, or bitten anyone. During a backyard cookout, a guest accidentally stepped on the dog's tail. The startled dog bit the guest's hand. The guest sues only for strict liability, not negligence. Is the owner likely strictly liable under common-law domestic-animal rules?
Answer A: Yes, because dog owners are strictly liable whenever their dogs bite
Answer B: No, because the owner lacked knowledge or reason to know of a dangerous propensity
Answer C: Yes, because the guest was on the owner's land when bitten
Answer D: No, because strict liability never applies to any animal kept as a pet
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The dog was domestic and had no history of dangerous conduct. The unexpected first bite does not support strict liability on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common-law strict liability for domestic animals requires known dangerous propensity. The facts negate prior warning signs. C: The call asks strict animal liability, not premises negligence. Land location does not replace the dangerous-propensity requirement. D: Domestic pets can create strict liability when the owner knows of dangerous propensities.
lb_rule_reference: A domestic pet's first unexpected bite does not create common-law strict liability absent owner knowledge or reason to know of dangerous propensity.
