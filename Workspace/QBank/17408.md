Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A fifteen-year-old operated a rented speedboat on a crowded lake. While adjusting the music controls, he failed to watch the channel and struck a docked fishing boat. A passenger on the fishing boat was injured. The passenger sues the teenager for negligence. What standard of care applies in the passenger's negligence claim against the teenager?
Answer A: The teenager is always judged by the care of a reasonable child of like age and experience
Answer B: The teenager is held to the standard of a reasonably prudent adult operating a speedboat under similar circumstances
Answer C: The teenager is held to his own subjective level of boating experience
Answer D: The teenager cannot be liable because minors are immune from negligence claims
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Although the defendant is fifteen, speedboat operation is an adult activity, so the adult reasonable-person standard applies.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Operating a speedboat on a crowded lake is an adult activity. A child engaged in an adult activity is held to the adult standard. C: The adult-activity rule does not lower the standard for subjective inexperience. It applies the adult objective standard. D: Minors can be liable for negligence. The issue is which standard applies.
lb_rule_reference: Children are usually held to a child standard, but a child engaged in an adult activity is held to the adult reasonable-person standard.
