internal_id: CP-053

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A state contractor sues a federal agency's private contractor for negligence in performing work on a federal construction project. The state negligence claim requires proving that the defendant deviated from the applicable standard of care. The plaintiff's complaint alleges that the standard of care is established by federal safety regulations promulgated by the federal agency, and that the defendant violated those regulations. The defendant moves to keep the case in federal court after removal, arguing that the necessity of interpreting federal safety regulations embedded in the state negligence standard of care establishes federal-question jurisdiction. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Does § 1331 federal-question jurisdiction exist over this state-law negligence claim?
Answer A: Yes, because any state claim that requires interpretation of federal regulations necessarily arises under federal law for § 1331 purposes
Answer B: No, because the state negligence cause of action is created by state law, and the federal element appears only as evidence of the standard of care, not as a necessary element of the claim
Answer C: Yes, because the defendant removed the case and removal cures any deficiency in the plaintiff's federal-question pleading
Answer D: No, because § 1331 is limited to claims created by a federal statute; a state negligence claim can never satisfy § 1331 regardless of how many federal elements it contains
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under *Grable*, an embedded federal element can support § 1331 jurisdiction only when it is a necessary, actually disputed, and substantial element of the state-created cause of action, and when exercising federal jurisdiction would not disrupt the balance of federal and state responsibilities. Here, the federal safety regulations serve as evidence of the applicable standard of care — they help prove what the standard was — but the cause of action itself is state-law negligence. The federal issue is not a necessary element; it is one way to prove a state-law element. *Grable* does not extend that far. The case belongs in state court.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under *Grable*, jurisdiction exists only when the federal issue is necessary, actually disputed, and substantial, and when exercising federal jurisdiction would not upset the balance of federal and state judicial responsibilities. Routine reference to federal regulations as evidence of a standard of care does not make the claim "arise under" federal law — otherwise any negligence per se case based on a federal regulation would be in federal court, which would massively displace state court jurisdiction in ways Congress did not intend. C: Removal does not create federal subject matter jurisdiction — it only transfers a case that already has (or is claimed to have) federal jurisdiction from state to federal court. If original jurisdiction does not exist under § 1331 or § 1332, removal is improper and the case must be remanded. Removal and jurisdiction are separate inquiries. D: Grable* itself demonstrates that a state-created cause of action can support § 1331 jurisdiction under the right circumstances. The *Grable* claim was state law (quiet title), yet the Court found § 1331 jurisdiction because the federal issue (IRS notice requirements) was necessary, actually disputed, and substantial. The "state claim can never satisfy § 1331" position is wrong.
lb_rule_reference: Under Grable & Sons Metal Products, Inc. v. Darue Engineering, a state law claim may arise under federal law for § 1331 purposes when the state-law claim necessarily raises a stated federal issue, the federal issue is actually disputed and substantial, and a federal forum may entertain it without disturbing any congressionally approved balance of federal and state judicial responsibilities.
