Question: Before any dispute was reasonably anticipated, a company's email system automatically deleted routine server logs under a standard 30-day retention policy. Months later, litigation begins and the plaintiff seeks sanctions for the missing logs. What is the best argument against sanctions?
Answer A: The logs were ESI lost through the routine, good-faith operation of an information system before a preservation duty arose
Answer B: ESI can never support discovery sanctions
Answer C: Sanctions are mandatory because relevant ESI is missing
Answer D: The company is protected only if it proves the logs would have hurt its own case
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The safe-harbor principle protects routine good-faith loss absent exceptional circumstances.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: ESI loss can support sanctions when preservation duties and rule requirements are met. C: Sanctions do not follow automatically from any missing ESI. D: The routine good-faith operation principle does not require proving the content would be self-harming.
lb_rule_reference: Absent exceptional circumstances, a court may not impose sanctions for failing to provide ESI lost because of the routine, good-faith operation of an electronic information system.
