Question: In a federal forgery trial, the government offers an allegedly forged receipt and two letters that Daniel admits he wrote. The government asks the jury to compare the receipt signature with the admitted letters. Daniel objects that no handwriting expert has testified. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because only an expert may compare handwriting.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the judge must decide whether the signatures match before the jury can see them.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Daniel saw the receipt being signed.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because the trier of fact may compare the challenged writing with authenticated specimens.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 901 permits comparison by an expert witness or by the trier of fact with an authenticated specimen. Daniel's admitted letters are authenticated specimens, so expert testimony is not required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the trier of fact may compare. B: B is wrong because the judge screens for sufficient foundation; the ultimate comparison is for the trier. C: C is wrong because eyewitness testimony to the signing is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(3) / trier comparison with authenticated specimen
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the trier-comparison route for handwriting authentication.
