Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Privileges
Question: During a sidewalk argument, Pat raised a fist and stepped toward Riley. Riley backed away, and Pat lowered his fist, turned around, and began walking away. Ten seconds later, Riley ran after Pat and punched him in the back "so he would learn not to threaten people." Pat sues Riley for battery. Can Riley rely on self-defense?
Answer A: Yes, because Pat threatened Riley first
Answer B: Yes, because Riley used only nondeadly force
Answer C: No, because Riley's punch was retaliation after any imminent threat had passed
Answer D: No, because self-defense is available only inside the defendant's home
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Pat lowered his fist, turned away, and left. Riley's later punch was punitive, not defensive against an imminent contact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Self-defense is limited to force reasonably necessary against an imminent threat. It does not justify retaliation after the threat ends. B: Even nondeadly force must be necessary and timely. The threat had ended. D: Self-defense can apply outside the home. This defense fails because the force was retaliatory and untimely.
lb_rule_reference: Self-defense does not privilege retaliatory force after the imminent threat has ended.
