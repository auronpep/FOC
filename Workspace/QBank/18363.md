Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police obtained a warrant to search a suspect's apartment for evidence of drug trafficking. The supporting affidavit established probable cause based on controlled buys. The warrant authorized police to search the apartment at 1420 Elm Street, Apt. 4B, and to seize "any and all items related to illegal activity." While executing the warrant, officers found cocaine, a pistol, and business records containing evidence of an unrelated mail fraud scheme. The suspect moves to suppress all three items. Which of the following best describes the admissibility of the seized items?
Answer A: All three items are admissible because the warrant established probable cause and was issued by a neutral magistrate
Answer B: All three items are suppressible because the particularity clause was violated
Answer C: The cocaine is likely admissible as within the scope of the warrant's probable-cause basis; the business records are suppressible as beyond the warrant's scope and not in plain view
Answer D: The pistol and business records are suppressible, but the cocaine is admissible because it was the specific contraband sought
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The warrant's particularity clause was defective as written ("any and all items"), but courts interpret warrants in light of the supporting affidavit, which established probable cause for drug trafficking evidence. Cocaine plainly falls within that scope. The pistol could be admissible under plain view during a lawful search if its incriminating nature was immediately apparent. The business records, however, are unrelated to drug trafficking, were not immediately apparent as contraband, and fall outside any exception — they are suppressible. The business records are the clearest suppression candidate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A valid warrant also requires particularity — the items to be seized must be described with sufficient specificity to limit police discretion. "Any and all items related to illegal activity" is a general warrant condemned by the Fourth Amendment. Items falling outside the specific probable-cause basis (cocaine in a drug trafficking warrant) may be suppressible unless plain view or another exception applies. B: Courts sever invalid portions of warrants. The cocaine — the item within the probable-cause basis of the drug trafficking warrant — may survive under the portions of the warrant that were particularized by context and the supporting affidavit. The pistol may be admissible under plain view if immediately apparent as evidence of a crime. A wholesale suppression ruling overstates the remedy. D: The pistol may survive under plain view: if officers were lawfully in the apartment executing a valid portion of the warrant and the pistol's incriminating nature was immediately apparent, it is admissible. Lumping the pistol with the business records ignores the plain view doctrine.
lb_rule_reference: A warrant must describe with particularity both the place to be searched and the items to be seized; a general warrant authorizing seizure of "any evidence of crime" is constitutionally invalid
