Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Habit or custom practice
Question: In a federal insurance case, the insurer must show that it mailed a cancellation notice to Abraham. The mailroom supervisor, Rachel, testifies that every cancellation notice is printed with a barcode, scanned by Rachel's team, and placed in outgoing mail the same day; company records show Abraham's notice received the outgoing-mail scan. Rachel does not personally remember this notice. Abraham objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because Rachel lacks memory of Abraham's particular notice.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because routine practice is character evidence.
Answer C: Admit the testimony because organizational routine practice and the scan record can support an inference that the notice was mailed.
Answer D: Admit the testimony only if the postal worker who collected the mail testifies.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Evidence of an organization's routine practice may be admitted to prove conduct in accordance with that routine. Rachel's testimony about the routine, combined with the scan record, lays a foundation for mailing even without personal memory of the particular notice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because routine practice can prove conduct without personal memory of the specific event. B: B is wrong because routine practice is not character evidence. D: D is wrong because postal-worker testimony is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 406 / routine practice as foundation for mailing
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests routine-practice foundation and rejects personal-memory over-demand.
