Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Ruth sues Samuel after a bus collision, claiming the collision caused persistent shoulder pain. Ruth offers her own testimony that she had never experienced shoulder pain before the collision. Samuel objects that Ruth might have injured her shoulder later while gardening. Is Ruth's proposed testimony admissible?
Answer A: No, because Ruth is not a medical expert on causation.
Answer B: Yes, because her lack of prior shoulder pain has some tendency to support her causation theory, even though Samuel may argue another cause.
Answer C: No, because evidence with a possible alternative explanation is inadmissible.
Answer D: Yes, but only to impeach Samuel if he testifies that Ruth had shoulder pain before the accident.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Ruth may testify from personal knowledge that she had no prior shoulder pain. That fact is probative of whether the collision caused the injury. Later-gardening possibilities go to weight, not admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Ruth is testifying to her own prior condition, not offering a medical expert opinion. C: C is wrong because an alternative explanation does not make probative evidence inadmissible. D: D is wrong because the testimony is substantive circumstantial evidence on causation, not only impeachment.
