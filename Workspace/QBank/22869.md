


BARMATRIX Q#: 22869
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A woman, a citizen of State A, sued a man, a citizen of State B, for $80,000 in injuries after he accidentally hit her with his car while the man was visiting State A for a conference. The woman brought suit in State A federal court. The man argued that he was not subject to personal jurisdiction in State A. His only contacts with the state had been attending the conference, which was paid for by his company; a semester of college in State A 20 years ago, where he lived on campus; and a bank account from his college days in State A that he had forgotten to close and which had $110 in it. Does the State A federal court have jurisdiction over the man?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the man hit the woman in State A.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the man had a bank account with sufficient funds to bring him under State A jurisdiction.
Answer C: C: No, because the man had insufficient personal contacts with State A.
Answer D: D: No, because the woman is a citizen of State A.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A state will have specific personal jurisdiction over a defendant when a claim arises from the defendant's contact with the jurisdiction. Here, the man hit the woman in State A, so State A will have personal jurisdiction over the defendant for this specific cause of action. B is incorrect because the single forgotten bank account is likely not enough to give State A personal jurisdiction. C is incorrect because State A has jurisdiction over this particular claim since it occurred in State A. D is incorrect because the woman's citizenship has nothing to do with whether the State A court would have jurisdiction over the man.
