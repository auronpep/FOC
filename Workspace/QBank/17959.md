MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Two officers approach Leo on a public sidewalk at noon. They do not block his path, display weapons, touch him, or use commanding language. One officer asks whether Leo will answer a few questions. Leo agrees and, while speaking, reveals contraband in his open hand. Was Leo seized when the officers first approached and asked questions?
Answer A: No, because a reasonable person in Leo's position would have felt free to decline and leave
Answer B: Yes, because any police question creates a Fourth Amendment seizure
Answer C: Yes, because two officers were present
Answer D: No, because sidewalk encounters are never regulated by the Fourth Amendment
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Police may approach a person in public and ask questions without creating a seizure if they do not use coercive restraint or authority.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The Fourth Amendment seizure test asks whether a reasonable person would feel free to leave, not whether an officer asked a question. C: Officer number is only one factor. Here there was no blocking, command, weapon display, or physical restraint. D: A sidewalk encounter can become a seizure if officers restrain liberty through force or authority. These facts simply do not cross that line.
lb_rule_reference: A seizure occurs only when, under the totality of circumstances, a reasonable person would not feel free to leave or end the encounter.
