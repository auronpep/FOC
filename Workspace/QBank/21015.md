Question: A water treatment plant occasionally emits odors that substantially interfere with a nearby owner's use of land. Shutting the plant immediately would leave the town without safe drinking water for months. The owner proves private nuisance and seeks an immediate injunction closing the plant. What is the court's best approach to the requested injunction?
Answer A: Grant it automatically because nuisance liability has been established
Answer B: Deny all relief because the plant serves an important public function
Answer C: Balance hardships and public interest, potentially awarding damages or delayed relief instead of immediate closure
Answer D: Treat the odor as negligence only, because nuisance has no damages remedy
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Courts may tailor nuisance remedies when an injunction would impose disproportionate hardship.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Equitable relief is not automatic. B: Public utility may affect the remedy, but it does not erase liability. D: Nuisance may support damages and equitable remedies.
lb_rule_reference: Even when nuisance liability exists, a court may balance hardships and public interests before granting an injunction, and may award damages instead.
