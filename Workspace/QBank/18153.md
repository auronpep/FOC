Question: Abraham agreed to buy Leah's house if Abraham obtained a mortgage commitment by July 15. Abraham promptly applied to three banks and truthfully supplied all requested documents, but every bank rejected the application by July 15. Abraham refused to buy the house. If Leah sues Abraham for breach, what is the likely result?
Answer A: Leah wins because Abraham promised to buy the house.
Answer B: Leah wins because failure to obtain financing is always the buyer's risk.
Answer C: Abraham wins because his duty to buy did not mature when the financing condition failed despite his good-faith efforts.
Answer D: Abraham wins only if he proves the banks acted negligently in rejecting his application.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The financing commitment was a condition precedent to Abraham's duty to buy. Because Abraham made good-faith efforts and the condition did not occur, his duty never matured. Refusing to perform after a failed condition is not breach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the promise to buy was conditional. B: B is wrong because a properly drafted financing condition can allocate the risk of financing failure. D: D is wrong because the buyer need not prove bank negligence; the relevant facts are the condition and his good-faith efforts.
lb_rule_reference: Express condition precedent
