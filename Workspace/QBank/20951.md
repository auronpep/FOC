Outline: Form and scope of examination
Question: In a federal civil trial, Jonathan testifies on direct only about seeing a blue car enter a parking lot. On cross-examination, opposing counsel asks Jonathan about a separate contract dispute between the parties that was never mentioned on direct and does not bear on Jonathan's credibility. The examining party objects that the question is beyond the scope of direct. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because cross-examination may cover any relevant matter as of right.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless the court, in its discretion, permits inquiry into the new matter.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because cross-examination may never include credibility questions.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Jonathan is an adverse party.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Cross-examination should not go beyond the subject matter of direct examination and matters affecting credibility unless the court allows inquiry into additional matters. The separate contract dispute is new substantive matter and not credibility on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because cross is not unlimited as of right. C: C is wrong because credibility is a proper cross-examination subject. D: D is wrong because adverse-party status is not the sole route for expanded cross.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(b) / scope of cross-examination
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests scope-of-cross limits and court-discretion routing.
