Outline: Battery
Question: Councilwoman Vera was giving a public speech when a political opponent, Griff, walked up behind her and deliberately poured a cup of cold water over her head. Vera was not physically hurt—she suffered no cuts, bruises, or lasting effects—but she was humiliated in front of the audience and her clothing was soaked. Griff claims he cannot be guilty of battery because Vera was not injured. Which of the following most accurately states whether Griff committed battery against Vera?
Answer A: Griff did not commit battery because Vera suffered no physical injury
Answer B: Griff did not commit battery because the contact was with water, not his body or an instrument he wielded
Answer C: Griff committed battery because he intentionally applied unlawful force that was offensive to a person of ordinary sensibilities
Answer D: Griff committed battery only if he intended to humiliate Vera rather than merely protest her policies
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Common-law battery requires: (1) an unlawful (2) application of force (3) to the person of another, that is either harmful or offensive to a person of ordinary sensibilities. Griff deliberately poured water on Vera without consent. The contact was not socially permitted (not like a handshake or incidental crowd contact) and would be offensive to any reasonable person. Physical injury is not an element. All components are satisfied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common-law battery does not require physical injury. The offense is complete upon any unlawful application of force that is either harmful or offensive to a reasonable person. Deliberately pouring water on someone against their will is offensive contact satisfying the battery element. B: Battery encompasses any unlawful force applied through any means, including objects, instruments, or substances put in motion by the defendant. Deliberately directing water onto a person is an application of force just as a thrown object would be. D: Battery at common law requires intent to make the contact, not a specific purpose behind the contact. Griff intended to pour water on Vera — that volitional act is sufficient. His motive (protest, humiliation, or prank) is irrelevant to the prima facie case.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law battery is satisfied by any unlawful application of force that is either harmful OR offensive to a person of ordinary sensibilities; physical injury is not required
