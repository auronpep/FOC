Question: A seller agrees to sell Sarah a used well pump and states in the contract, “Seller guarantees that the pump produces at least 200 gallons per hour.” Both parties honestly believe the pump meets that standard. In fact, before the contract was signed, the pump could produce only 90 gallons per hour. The seller argues that the contract should be avoided for mutual mistake. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The seller may avoid the contract because both parties were mistaken.
Answer B: The seller may avoid the contract because the buyer also believed the pump was adequate.
Answer C: The contract is void for lack of consideration.
Answer D: The seller may not use mutual mistake to escape a risk the seller expressly assumed by guaranteeing the pump's capacity.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Mutual mistake does not allow a party to avoid a risk that the party assumed by agreement. By guaranteeing pump capacity, the seller allocated that risk to itself. Sarah may rely on the contract rather than lose the guarantee through mistake doctrine.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B are wrong because mutual error is not enough when the party seeking avoidance bore the risk. C is wrong because consideration exists; the dispute is over risk allocation and enforceability of the guarantee.
