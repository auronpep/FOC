Question: After litigation is reasonably anticipated, a company fails to suspend deletion of relevant chat messages. Some messages are lost and cannot be restored, and the plaintiff shows prejudice. There is no evidence the company intended to deprive the plaintiff of the messages. What is the proper sanction framework?
Answer A: The court may order measures no greater than necessary to cure the prejudice
Answer B: The court must instruct the jury that the lost messages were unfavorable
Answer C: The court must dismiss the action whenever any ESI is lost
Answer D: The court may do nothing because ESI is not discoverable
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Prejudice without intent supports curative measures, not the severe intent-based sanctions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: An adverse-inference instruction requires the separate intent-to-deprive finding. C: Case-ending sanctions require more than mere loss and prejudice. D: ESI is discoverable when it falls within the discovery scope.
lb_rule_reference: If ESI that should have been preserved is lost because reasonable steps were not taken and cannot be restored or replaced, the court may order measures no greater than necessary to cure prejudice.
