Question: Mara is indicted for a bank robbery. While she is in custody, detectives give Miranda warnings and question her about an unrelated warehouse arson that has not been charged. Mara waives Miranda and admits the arson. She seeks suppression under the Sixth Amendment because counsel had been appointed on the robbery. What is the best Sixth Amendment answer?
Answer A: Suppression is required because appointment of counsel on one charge bars questioning about every uncharged crime
Answer B: The Sixth Amendment does not bar the arson statement merely because counsel was appointed on the robbery
Answer C: Suppression is required because custody alone makes the Sixth Amendment apply to all questioning
Answer D: The statement is admissible only if the arson occurred before the robbery
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The arson was a different uncharged offense. If Mara validly waived Miranda, the Sixth Amendment robbery counsel right does not itself suppress the arson statement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Sixth Amendment is offense-specific. Attachment on robbery does not automatically attach to an unrelated uncharged arson. C: Custody triggers Miranda analysis, not Sixth Amendment attachment for all offenses. D: The issue is offense specificity and attachment, not which crime occurred first.
lb_rule_reference: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel is offense-specific; attachment on one charged offense does not automatically bar questioning about a different uncharged offense.
