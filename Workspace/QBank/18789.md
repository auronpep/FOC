Question: Abraham hired Noah to renovate a bakery for a lump sum of $60,000, payable “upon completion of the renovation.” After finishing demolition, framing, and some plumbing, Noah demanded $30,000 because he had completed about half of the work. Abraham refused to pay until the renovation was complete. Noah stopped work and sued for breach of the contract price. Who is most likely to prevail on the claim for immediate contract payment?
Answer A: Noah, because a contractor is always entitled to proportional contract payments as work progresses.
Answer B: Noah, because partial performance automatically makes the owner's full payment duty mature.
Answer C: Abraham, because completion was a constructive condition to the lump-sum payment duty and Noah had not substantially completed the renovation.
Answer D: Abraham, but only because Noah failed to make an express written demand for payment before stopping work.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under common law, when a work contract provides a lump-sum payment upon completion, substantial completion is ordinarily a constructive condition to the owner's payment duty. Noah had completed only preliminary and partial work, not substantial performance of the renovation. Abraham's duty to pay the contract price had not matured.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because progress payments are not implied merely because work has begun. B: B is wrong because partial performance is not the same as substantial performance. D: D is wrong because the issue is payment-duty maturity, not the form of Noah's demand.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law constructive conditions / payment on completion
