Outline: Consequential damages
Question: Judah contracted with Deborah to supply custom display shelves for $10,000. Two weeks after the contract was formed, Judah signed a separate contract to use the shelves in a trade-show booth expected to produce $50,000 in profits. Judah told Deborah about the trade show only after Deborah had already breached the shelf contract. Judah seeks the $50,000 expected trade-show profits. Are those profits recoverable from Deborah?
Answer A: Yes, because Deborah learned of the trade-show profits before trial.
Answer B: No, because the special lost profits were not within Deborah's reason to know at the time of contracting.
Answer C: Yes, because all losses that actually follow a breach are recoverable.
Answer D: No, because lost profits are recoverable only in UCC goods cases.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Consequential lost profits require foreseeability at the time of contracting, not merely at breach or before trial. Deborah did not have reason to know of the later trade-show contract when the shelf contract was made.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because later knowledge after formation does not satisfy the foreseeability timing gate. C: C is wrong because causation alone does not make a special loss recoverable. D: D is wrong because common-law contracts may allow lost profits when the relevant gates are met.
lb_rule_reference: Consequential damages / time of foreseeability
