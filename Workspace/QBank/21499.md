MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Manslaughter
Question: Eli took a loaded handgun to a cabin weekend. Wanting to make his friends laugh, he spun the gun on a table after removing the magazine but without checking whether a round remained in the chamber. The gun discharged and killed a friend standing nearby. Eli was shocked and had not pointed the gun at anyone. What is the best homicide classification?
Answer A: No homicide liability, because Eli did not know a round remained in the chamber
Answer B: Murder, because any death caused by a loaded gun is malice
Answer C: Involuntary manslaughter, because Eli's grossly negligent firearm handling caused death
Answer D: Voluntary manslaughter, because Eli acted in the heat of excitement
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Eli created a substantial and unjustified risk by manipulating a gun without checking the chamber. His conduct caused death but lacks the malice required for murder. Involuntary manslaughter is the best classification.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Criminal negligence can support involuntary manslaughter when a defendant creates a gross and unjustified risk of death. Handling a firearm this way without checking the chamber is more than ordinary accident. B: The facts show gross negligence, not intent to kill, intent to seriously injure, or conscious extreme indifference to life. Murder overstates the mental state. D: Voluntary manslaughter requires adequate provocation and heat of passion; those facts are absent. This is a negligence-based homicide issue.
lb_rule_reference: Involuntary manslaughter can be based on criminal negligence or recklessness that causes death but does not rise to malice.
