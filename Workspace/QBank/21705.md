Outline: Clergy and penitent
Question: Naomi met privately with Pastor Aaron for spiritual counseling but asked her friend Ruth to sit in “for support.” Ruth was not needed to translate or assist the counseling. Naomi confessed to altering checks. In a later federal case, Naomi seeks to bar Ruth from testifying about the confession. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because the meeting occurred with clergy.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because any supportive friend is treated as part of the clergy privilege.
Answer C: Overrule the objection, because Naomi voluntarily disclosed the statement in the presence of an unnecessary third party.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Pastor Aaron first waives the privilege.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Confidentiality is required. A voluntary disclosure in front of an unnecessary friend defeats the privilege as to that friend's testimony. The decisive axis is third-party presence and necessity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Clergy involvement does not eliminate the confidentiality requirement. B: B is wrong because The facts say Ruth was not needed for the communication or counseling. D: D is wrong because The issue is Naomi's nonconfidential disclosure to Ruth, not Pastor Aaron's waiver.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / unnecessary third party in clergy communication
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is missing the same third-party confidentiality gate that appears in attorney-client, marital, psychotherapist, and clergy settings.
