Outline_code: 71040200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Four men are charged with conspiracy to commit a series of bank robberies. Nine successful bank robberies took place during the period of the charged conspiracy. Because the robbers wore masks and gloves and stole the bank surveillance tapes, no direct identification of the robbers by the witnesses has been made. Some circumstantial evidence ties each of the men to the overall conspiracy. During cross-examination, a prosecution witness testified that one defendant was in jail on other charges during six of the robberies. That defendant's lawyer has moved for a judgment of acquittal at the close of the government's case. Should the motion be granted?
Answer A: A: No, because a conspirator is not required to agree to all of the objects of the conspiracy.
Answer B: B: No, because a conspirator need not be present at the commission of each crime conspired upon.
Answer C: C: Yes, provided the defendant has complied with the rule requiring pretrial notice of alibi.
Answer D: D: Yes, regardless of compliance with the alibi rule, because the government is bound by exculpatory evidence elicited during its case in chief.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: (B)is the best response, because a co-conspirator need not be present at the commission of each crime in order to be guilty of a conspiracy. Furthermore, the arrest of one co-conspirator does not automatically terminate the conspiracy when other co-conspirators continue to carry out the mission of the conspiracy. Accordingly, the jailed defendant may still be guilty of conspiracy, and a judgment of acquittal at the close of the government's case is improper on these facts. (A)is not the best response, because conspirators must agree to the joint pursuit of unlawful ends. More importantly, it is irrelevant factually, as the defendant is raising a defense based on his failure to be present (i.e., he was in jail), not on his failure to agree to all of the objects of the conspiracy. As a co-conspirator need not be present at the commission of each crime in order to be guilty of a conspiracy, a judgment of acquittal at the close of the government's case is improper on these facts. (C)is not the best response, because even if the defendant had complied with the rule requiring pretrial notice of alibi, a judgment of acquittal will not be entered. A co-conspirator need not be present at the commission of each crime in order to be guilty of a conspiracy. Accordingly, the jailed defendant may still be guilty of conspiracy, and a judgment of acquittal at the close of the government's case is improper on these facts. (D)is not the best response, because the government is not “bound by exculpatory evidence elicited during its case-in-chief.” The government is free to contest such evidence and is in no way bound by it. Furthermore, even if the government were so bound, a judgment of acquittal will not be entered.
