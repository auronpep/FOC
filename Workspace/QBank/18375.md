Outline: Psychotherapist-patient
Question: In a federal antitrust trial, a nonparty witness is asked to disclose what he told a licensed psychotherapist during confidential treatment for anxiety. The treatment has no direct connection to the antitrust issues. The witness asserts a psychotherapist-patient privilege under federal common law. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the privilege because no privilege exists unless Congress codifies it in a Federal Rule of Evidence.
Answer B: Overrule the privilege because all medical or therapy conversations are treated alike under federal common law.
Answer C: Sustain the privilege only if the state where therapy occurred has enacted a psychotherapist-patient statute.
Answer D: Sustain the privilege because federal common law recognizes a psychotherapist-patient privilege for confidential treatment communications.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal common law under Rule 501 recognizes a psychotherapist-patient privilege for confidential treatment communications. This item tests the recognized-relationship gate, not a general medical-setting privilege. The licensed psychotherapist treatment communication is the protected object.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 501 allows privileges to develop under federal common law; codification in a specific evidence rule is not required. B: B is wrong because This collapses psychotherapist privilege into a general medical privilege. Federal common law treats the two differently. C: C is wrong because This imports state law into a federal antitrust case without a state-law rule of decision.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / recognized federal relationship privileges
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is either under-recognizing federal common-law privileges or overgeneralizing medical confidentiality.
