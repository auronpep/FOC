Outline: Larceny
Question: Owen lent his neighbor Dana $500 cash in a handshake deal three months ago. Dana has repeatedly refused to repay the loan. One afternoon while Dana is mowing her lawn, Owen walks into Dana's unlocked garage and takes a power drill worth approximately $500 that he knows belongs to Dana. Owen intends to keep the drill until Dana repays the debt, believing he has a legal right to "offset" the debt by taking property of equivalent value. Is Owen guilty of larceny?
Answer A: Yes, because Owen took Dana's property without her consent
Answer B: Yes, because Owen's self-help remedy is illegal under civil law
Answer C: No, because Owen's sincere belief in his right to take equivalent value negates the trespassory-taking element
Answer D: No, because the drill's value is equal to the debt, so Owen took nothing of net value
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Larceny requires a trespassory taking, meaning the defendant must take without a sincere belief in a legal right to the property. The common-law claim-of-right doctrine provides that a defendant who genuinely (even if mistakenly) believes he is entitled to the specific property or its monetary equivalent cannot form the mens rea required for a trespassory taking. Owen's belief that he could offset Dana's debt by taking property of equivalent value, however legally incorrect under civil law, negates the trespassory element and therefore defeats a larceny charge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Lack of consent is a necessary but not sufficient condition for a trespassory taking. Where a defendant sincerely believes he has a legal right to the specific property (or its equivalent value), the claim-of-right doctrine negates the trespassory element even though the owner did not consent. B: Criminal liability for larceny is analyzed under criminal law standards. The civil illegality of self-help does not automatically satisfy the criminal law's trespassory-taking requirement. The claim-of-right doctrine operates independently of civil remedies. D: Larceny does not require that the defendant profit in excess of what is owed. The correct reason to acquit is the claim-of-right doctrine (subjective belief), not a value-offset theory. A person who takes property worth more than the debt would still potentially qualify for the claim-of-right defense on the sincerely-believed-amount, but the value-equivalence theory is not a standalone defense.
lb_rule_reference: A taking is not trespassory if the defendant sincerely believes he has a legal right to the specific property taken, even if that belief is mistaken.
