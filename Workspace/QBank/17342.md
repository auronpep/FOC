Subtopic: Defenses to Enforceability
Outline: Mistake / Lack of Mutual Assent
Question: A farmer sells several sealed jars of old coins to Daniel, a professional coin dealer. The farmer says he does not know whether the coins are rare or common. Daniel briefly examines the jars and says, “I am buying based on my own judgment.” Both parties later learn that most of the coins are common and worth far less than Daniel expected. Daniel seeks rescission for mutual mistake. What is the best defense to Daniel's claim?
Answer A: The farmer committed fraud because he did not prove the coins were rare.
Answer B: Daniel may rescind because both parties were uncertain.
Answer C: Daniel assumed the risk by purchasing based on his own expert judgment despite known uncertainty.
Answer D: Daniel may rescind because a professional buyer can never assume risk when the seller is a farmer.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A party may bear the risk of mistake through conscious ignorance and through the parties' allocation of decision responsibility. Daniel was the professional evaluator, knew the uncertainty, and bought based on his own judgment. That defeats rescission for mistake.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the farmer's uncertainty and lack of proof were disclosed rather than concealed. B: B is wrong because uncertainty plus risk assumption does not support rescission. D: D is wrong because professional expertise can support allocating risk to the professional buyer.
lb_rule_reference: Expertise-based risk allocation
