Question: A grand jury returned an indictment against Marcus for bank robbery on a Tuesday. Two days later, before Marcus had been arraigned and before he had retained an attorney, a detective visited Marcus at his home and asked him about the robbery. Marcus was not in custody. Without any Miranda warnings, Marcus voluntarily told the detective where he had hidden the money. Marcus now moves to suppress that statement. Should the court suppress Marcus's statement?
Answer A: No, because Marcus was not in custody when he made the statement
Answer B: No, because Marcus had not yet retained an attorney
Answer C: Yes, because the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had attached and Marcus was questioned without counsel present
Answer D: No, because Marcus validly waived his rights by voluntarily answering the detective's questions
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches at the commencement of formal adversarial proceedings. A grand jury indictment is such a proceeding. Once the right attached, the government could not deliberately elicit statements from Marcus in the absence of counsel or a valid waiver. Marcus never waived, so the statement must be suppressed under *Massiah v. United States* and its progeny.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel does not require custody. It attaches at indictment and applies to any deliberate elicitation, in custody or not. B: The right attaches upon indictment regardless of whether counsel has been appointed or retained. Marcus was entitled to counsel from the moment the indictment issued. D: A valid Sixth Amendment waiver requires that the defendant know of the right and intentionally relinquish it. No Miranda-style advisement was given, and nothing indicates Marcus knew he had a Sixth Amendment right to counsel at that moment. Voluntariness alone does not constitute waiver of Sixth Amendment rights.
lb_rule_reference: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches at the commencement of formal adversarial proceedings — indictment, information, arraignment, preliminary hearing, or formal charge
