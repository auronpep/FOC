Question: Isaac owned a recorded easement to cross Rebecca's land. Isaac did not use the easement for 18 years because he preferred a newer public road. He never signed a release, never said he was giving up the easement, and never built anything blocking his own use of the route. Has the easement been abandoned solely because of Isaac's long nonuse?
Answer A: Yes, because any nonuse for more than 10 years automatically abandons an easement.
Answer B: Yes, because preferring a public road is the same as releasing the easement.
Answer C: No, but only because recorded easements can terminate only by condemnation.
Answer D: No, because abandonment generally requires nonuse plus intent or conduct showing relinquishment; nonuse alone is insufficient.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Abandonment of an easement generally requires nonuse combined with intent to relinquish the right or conduct inconsistent with continued ownership. Isaac's preference for another route and long nonuse, without more, do not by themselves abandon the easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because nonuse alone is not abandonment. B: B is wrong because using a better road is not the same as release. C: C is wrong because easements can terminate through multiple doctrines. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Abandonment / nonuse plus intent
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is counting years of nonuse without finding intent or inconsistent conduct.
