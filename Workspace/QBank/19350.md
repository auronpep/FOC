Outline: Joinder of claims
Question: A buyer sues a seller in federal court for fraud, seeking damages, and also seeks rescission of the contract. Both requests turn on whether the seller knowingly made a false statement. The seller asks the judge to decide the rescission claim first and find that no false statement occurred. What is the strongest objection to the seller's proposed sequence?
Answer A: Equitable claims must always be dismissed when legal claims are present
Answer B: The jury must decide every issue in the case, including final equitable relief
Answer C: The judge must decide first because rescission is equitable
Answer D: The common fact issue should be tried to the jury first because it also controls the legal damages claim
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The Seventh Amendment protects the jury's role on common facts underlying legal relief.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Legal and equitable claims may coexist; the sequencing protects the jury right. B: The court may decide equitable relief, but common factual issues tied to legal claims should not be decided first by the judge. C: Equitable classification does not allow the judge to preempt common facts that carry a jury right on damages.
lb_rule_reference: When legal and equitable claims share factual issues, the jury generally decides the common facts for the legal claim before the court resolves equitable relief.
