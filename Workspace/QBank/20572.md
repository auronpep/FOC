Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff, a citizen of State A, sues Defendant, a citizen of State B, in federal court for more than the jurisdictional amount. Plaintiff then seeks to add a related claim against Third Party, a citizen of State A, under Rule 20. Plaintiff argues that the claim arises from the same accident and that Rule 20 permits joinder. What is the best jurisdictional analysis?
Answer A: The claim may proceed because Rule 20 permits related parties to be joined in one action
Answer B: The claim may proceed because supplemental jurisdiction always covers claims arising from the same case or controversy
Answer C: The court must separately assess subject-matter jurisdiction, and Section 1367(b) likely bars the plaintiff's claim against the nondiverse Rule 20 party
Answer D: The claim is allowed if Third Party agrees to waive the diversity problem
Correct Answer: C
lb_rule_reference: A joinder rule may permit a party or claim procedurally, but the court still needs subject-matter jurisdiction; in diversity cases, Section 1367(b) can bar certain plaintiff claims against parties joined under Rules 14, 19, 20, or 24 when inconsistent with diversity.
