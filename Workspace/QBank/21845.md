Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: Mason enters a locked office at night intending to steal any payroll envelopes he finds inside. He carries a bag and pry tool. The desk drawer is empty, so Mason takes nothing. He is charged with attempted larceny. Does Mason's conditional plan defeat attempt intent?
Answer A: Yes, because he intended to steal only if payroll envelopes were present
Answer B: No, because he intended to steal the envelopes if present and acted toward that objective
Answer C: Yes, because no payroll envelopes existed in the drawer
Answer D: No, but only because Mason carried a bag
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Mason entered the office with tools and a bag intending to take payroll envelopes if he found them. The empty drawer creates factual failure, not lack of intent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Conditional intent can satisfy attempt. Mason intended to steal payroll envelopes if he found them and took a substantial step toward that theft. C: The empty drawer is factual impossibility. It does not defeat attempt if intent and a sufficient act are present. D: The bag is evidence, but the rule is that conditional intent can still be intent for attempt.
lb_rule_reference: Conditional intent can satisfy attempt when the defendant intends to commit the target crime if a condition occurs and takes a substantial step toward that conditional plan.
