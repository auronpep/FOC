Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: A retailer orally agreed to buy 900 tablets from Lydia, a merchant seller. They agreed on quantity and delivery date but left the price open, intending to use the market price at delivery. No writing was signed, no goods were delivered, no payment was made, and neither party admitted the contract in court. Lydia refused to perform. Can the retailer enforce the oral contract?
Answer A: Yes, because the UCC can supply a reasonable price.
Answer B: Yes, because leaving price open proves the parties intended flexibility rather than a final writing.
Answer C: No, because the contract is for goods of $500 or more and no signed writing or UCC §2-201 exception is present.
Answer D: No, but only because no delivery date was stated in writing.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An open price does not necessarily prevent formation under the UCC, but it does not eliminate the Statute of Frauds. For a sale of goods priced at $500 or more, UCC §2-201 generally requires a sufficient signed writing unless an exception applies. No exception appears here.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the UCC's open-price rule does not replace the Statute of Frauds. B: B is wrong because intent to use market price does not supply a signed writing. D: D is wrong because the problem is no writing or exception, not merely the delivery date.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 / open price no-writing distinction
