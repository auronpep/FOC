Question: In a federal securities-fraud case, a nonparty accountant is asked to disclose statements she made to a licensed clinical psychologist during confidential therapy. The statements concern the accountant's panic attacks and do not involve threats or future crimes. The accountant asserts privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the privilege because federal common law recognizes only attorney-client and spousal privileges.
Answer B: Sustain the privilege because confidential psychotherapist-patient treatment communications are protected under federal common law.
Answer C: Overrule the privilege because medical confidentiality is never an evidence privilege.
Answer D: Sustain the privilege only if the accountant is a party to the case.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal common law recognizes a psychotherapist-patient privilege for confidential treatment communications. The fact that the holder is a nonparty does not eliminate the privilege. The decisive axis is protected relationship plus confidential treatment communication.
