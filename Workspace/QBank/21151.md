Outline: Robbery
Question: Milo is arraigned on a robbery charge and counsel is appointed. Later that week, a detective visits Milo's apartment, tells him she wants "his side of the robbery," and asks detailed questions. The detective gives no warnings and never mentions counsel. Milo admits planning the robbery. He was not in custody during the conversation. Should Milo's robbery statement be suppressed?
Answer A: Yes, because the government deliberately elicited a statement about the charged offense after attachment without counsel or waiver
Answer B: No, because Milo was not in custody when he spoke
Answer C: No, because Milo voluntarily answered the questions
Answer D: Yes, because police may never contact a represented defendant for any reason
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Arraignment attached the Sixth Amendment right. The detective deliberately elicited statements about the charged robbery, and the facts show no valid waiver.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The Sixth Amendment deliberate-elicitation rule does not require custody once formal proceedings have begun. C: Voluntary speech alone does not establish a knowing and intelligent waiver of the attached Sixth Amendment right. D: The Sixth Amendment is offense-specific and can be waived. The precise defect here is deliberate elicitation about the charged offense without counsel or waiver.
lb_rule_reference: A post-charge statement deliberately elicited from the accused in counsel's absence is suppressible unless the accused validly waived the Sixth Amendment right.
