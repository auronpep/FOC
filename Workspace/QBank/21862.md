Question: Rebecca held a recorded easement to use a lane across Esau's land. Esau built a locked fence across the lane and openly used the lane area as part of his garden for 12 years. Rebecca knew of the fence and made no attempt to use the lane or object. The jurisdiction recognizes extinguishment of easements by prescription, and the prescriptive period is 10 years. What is the best result?
Answer A: The easement necessarily continues because recorded easements cannot be lost by prescription.
Answer B: The easement may be extinguished because Esau's open, adverse obstruction prevented use for longer than the prescriptive period.
Answer C: The easement continues because nonuse alone can never affect easement rights, regardless of adverse obstruction.
Answer D: Esau automatically owns Rebecca's dominant estate because he blocked the lane.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Some jurisdictions allow an easement to be extinguished by prescription when the servient owner openly and adversely obstructs the easement for the statutory period. Esau's locked fence and garden use prevented the easement's use for longer than the 10-year period with Rebecca's knowledge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because recording does not make an easement immune from all termination doctrines. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because the facts include adverse obstruction, not mere nonuse. D: D is wrong because blocking an easement route does not transfer the dominant estate.
lb_rule_reference: Extinguishment by prescription
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is applying the nonuse-alone rule even when the servient owner has made an adverse, open obstruction for the statutory period.
