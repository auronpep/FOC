Question: Isaac hired Ruth to build a fence for $15,000, payable on completion. Ruth completed 80% of the fence. Before she could finish, Isaac ordered her off the property because he had decided to use a different contractor. Isaac refused to pay, arguing that Ruth had not completed the fence. What is the best result?
Answer A: Isaac owes nothing because completion was a condition to payment and the fence was incomplete.
Answer B: Isaac owes only if Ruth proves she would have completed the fence with perfect workmanship.
Answer C: Isaac may invoke the completion condition because an owner can always insist on literal completion.
Answer D: Isaac cannot rely on noncompletion caused by his own prevention and may be liable for breach, with Ruth entitled to appropriate contract damages.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Although completion was a constructive condition to payment, a party who prevents performance cannot rely on the nonoccurrence of the condition caused by that prevention. Isaac barred Ruth from completing the fence. He may be liable for breach and appropriate damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Isaac caused the noncompletion. B: B is wrong because Ruth need not prove perfect hypothetical performance to defeat Isaac's prevention defense. C: C is wrong because a party cannot insist on a condition it prevented.
lb_rule_reference: Prevention of constructive condition / breach by prevention
