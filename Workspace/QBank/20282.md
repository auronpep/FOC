Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: Leah is prosecuted for assault with a knife. An officer found a small knife in Leah's coat pocket five minutes after the assault. A medical examiner will testify that the victim's wound is consistent with that type of knife, but that many similar knives could have caused the wound. Leah moves to exclude the knife as irrelevant. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the knife, because the medical examiner cannot say it probably caused the wound.
Answer B: Admit the knife, because it has some tendency to connect Leah to the assault; the possibility of other similar knives goes to weight.
Answer C: Exclude the knife, because evidence is relevant only if it proves the disputed fact by a preponderance.
Answer D: Admit the knife only if the prosecution proves that no other similar knife was in the area.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The knife has some tendency to make Leah's use of a knife more probable. Relevance does not require probability, certainty, or exclusion of every other possible weapon. The weakness of the link is for the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the examiner need not identify the knife as the probable weapon to make it relevant. C: C is wrong because the relevance standard is lower than the merits burden. D: D is wrong because the prosecutor need not rule out every other possible knife at the admissibility stage.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is converting a weak-probative-link problem into an exclusion rule.
