Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Affirmative duties to act
Question: A bus passenger suffered a visible medical emergency while the bus was stopped outside a hospital. The driver knew the passenger needed help but refused to call emergency services or allow another passenger to do so because the bus was behind schedule. The delay worsened the passenger's condition. Did the driver and bus company owe an affirmative duty to use reasonable care?
Answer A: Yes, because a common carrier-passenger relationship can create a duty to aid
Answer B: Yes, because every person near a medical emergency has a duty to act
Answer C: No, because the bus company did not cause the medical emergency
Answer D: No, because schedule delays excuse all rescue duties
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A common carrier owes passengers reasonable care, and the driver knew of the emergency.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The duty is based on the carrier-passenger special relationship, not a universal rescue duty. C: Special relationship is another exception. The carrier did not need to cause the emergency to owe reasonable aid. D: Schedule concerns do not eliminate the duty of reasonable care to passengers in known danger.
lb_rule_reference: Common carriers owe passengers a duty of reasonable care, including reasonable aid or protection when the carrier knows or should know of danger.
