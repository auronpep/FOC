Question: Two event venues repeatedly blocked a public bike path with crowd-control fences on weekend nights. A cyclist suffered a unique shoulder injury when forced into traffic. The cyclist settled with Venue A for $15,000 under an agreement that released only Venue A. The cyclist's total damages were later found to be $80,000 in a public nuisance and negligence action against Venue B. The jurisdiction applies a pro tanto credit for settlements. What is the likely effect of Venue A's settlement in the cyclist's public nuisance and negligence claim against Venue B?
Answer A: Venue B is released because both venues contributed to the same public nuisance
Answer B: Venue B remains liable for any recoverable damages after crediting the $15,000 settlement
Answer C: Venue B owes the full $80,000 because public nuisance is a wrong against the community
Answer D: Venue B owes nothing unless Venue A was insolvent
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The settlement did not release Venue B, but it must be credited against the total damages to prevent double recovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The agreement released only Venue A. A partial settlement with one tortfeasor does not automatically release another under the stated facts. C: The cyclist is seeking damages for a special injury. Settlement credit still applies to prevent more than one recovery for that injury. D: The settlement resolved Venue A's share as between plaintiff and Venue A. Venue B's remaining liability turns on credit and remaining damages, not Venue A's solvency.
lb_rule_reference: A settlement or release in a multi-defendant nuisance case must be tracked separately from full satisfaction, remaining claims, and contribution or credit among tortfeasors.
