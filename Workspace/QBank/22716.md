Outline_code: 43020201
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Judicial Review
Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: Sixty- five percent of city police officers identified as racial minorities. In the city itself, 80 percent of city residents were members of racial minorities. In order to be promoted to a leadership position in the police department, an applicant had to take and pass a one- day multiple choice exam. Historically, 75 percent of white applicants passed, while only 20 percent of minority applicants passed. As a result, 80 percent of leadership positions in the police department were held by white officers. A potential leadership applicant sued, arguing that the required test was unconstitutional. Is the police leadership test unconstitutional?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the test has a clear disparate impact on racial minorities.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the state cannot show a compelling state interest for the test.
Answer C: C: No, because there is no evidence that the test is the result of intentional discrimination.
Answer D: D: No, because a state has absolute authority to regulate law enforcement through its police powers.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: In the absence of evidence of discriminatory intent, race- neutral qualifications for state jobs do not trigger either intermediate or strict scrutiny. Importantly, a disproportionate impact on racial minorities by itself is not enough to trigger heightened scrutiny. Here, while the test has a disproportionate impact on racial minorities, there is no evidence that it is anything other than a neutral test that happens to have a disproportionate impact. As such, the test is constitutional. A is incorrect because a disparate impact by itself is not enough to make the test unconstitutional. B is incorrect because “compelling state interest” refers to strict scrutiny analysis, which this test would not trigger. D is incorrect because a state's police power does not refer to power over law enforcement personnel.
