Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: In the answer, Jacob omits a Rule 12(b)(6) failure-to-state-a-claim defense. After the pleadings close, he moves for judgment on the pleadings, arguing the complaint still fails to state a claim. Is the defense automatically waived by omission from the answer?
Answer A: Yes, all Rule 12 defenses are waived if omitted from the first response
Answer B: Yes, failure to state a claim must be raised before answering or never
Answer C: No, failure to state a claim can be raised by Rule 12(c) after the pleadings close
Answer D: No, because subject-matter jurisdiction and failure to state a claim are the same
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: It is later-preserved, unlike personal jurisdiction, venue, process, and service objections.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 12 defenses have different waiver rules. B: Rule 12(h)(2) preserves it for later listed stages. D: They are distinct, even though both can be raised later than some defenses.
lb_rule_reference: Failure to state a claim may be raised in a pleading, by Rule 12(c), or at trial.
