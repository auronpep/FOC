Subtopic: Remedies
Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Mary, a merchant retailer, contracted to sell Paul a generator for $10,000. Mary's cost for the generator was $8,400. Paul repudiated before delivery. Mary had enough inventory and sales capacity to sell generators to both Paul and another buyer. Mary later sold the same model generator to John for $10,000. No incidental damages are claimed. What may Mary recover from Paul?
Answer A: Nothing, because Mary resold the generator for the same price.
Answer B: $10,000, because Paul breached the sales contract.
Answer C: $1,600, because Mary lost the profit she would have made on an additional sale to Paul.
Answer D: Only the difference between the contract price and the resale price.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under UCC Article 2 for a seller facing buyer repudiation of goods. Mary is a lost-volume seller because she had inventory and capacity to make both sales, so the resale did not replace Paul's sale. The formula is anticipated profit: $10,000 contract price − $8,400 cost = $1,600. The result scope is limited to lost profit because no incidental damages are claimed, and the answer does not allow an action for the price.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it uses WAL-REM-011, the resale-erases-lost-volume trap. B: B is wrong because it overuses an action for the price even though Paul did not accept the goods. D: D is wrong because it underuses lost-volume analysis; the contract-resale differential is zero but the lost profit remains.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-708(2) lost-volume seller — anticipated profit despite resale
