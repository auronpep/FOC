Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motion to strike
Question: A defendant answers a federal complaint and asserts as an affirmative defense, "The plaintiff's claim is barred because the plaintiff is a bad neighbor." The alleged neighbor conduct has no legal connection to the contract claim. The plaintiff moves under Rule 12(f) to strike the defense as insufficient and immaterial. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the motion because courts can never strike material from pleadings
Answer B: Grant the motion because the asserted defense is legally insufficient and immaterial
Answer C: Convert the motion to summary judgment because all Rule 12 motions require evidence
Answer D: Grant default judgment for the plaintiff because the defense is weak
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The answer labels irrelevant neighbor behavior as an affirmative defense to a contract claim. Rule 12(f) permits striking insufficient or immaterial defenses.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 12(f) expressly authorizes striking certain improper matter from pleadings. C: Rule 12(f) evaluates the pleading matter itself. No conversion is required to strike an insufficient defense. D: Striking an insufficient defense does not automatically establish the plaintiff's claim or justify default judgment.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 12(f) permits the court to strike from a pleading an insufficient defense or redundant, immaterial, impertinent, or scandalous matter.
