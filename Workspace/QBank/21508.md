Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Contribution and indemnity
Question: A delivery employee negligently caused a crash while making deliveries for his employer. The injured plaintiff sued the employer only, relying on respondeat superior. The employer was not independently negligent but paid the full judgment to the plaintiff. The employer then seeks to recover the full amount from the employee whose negligence caused the crash. Which claim, contribution or indemnity, best fits the employer's post-judgment claim against the employee?
Answer A: Contribution, because contribution always shifts the entire judgment to one tortfeasor
Answer B: Indemnity, because the employer's liability was vicarious and the employee was the active wrongdoer
Answer C: Satisfaction, because satisfaction gives the employer a claim against the employee
Answer D: Release, because paying the plaintiff released the employee from all responsibility to the employer
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The employer was liable only through respondeat superior and was not independently negligent. Indemnity is the mechanism that can shift the entire loss to the primarily responsible employee.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Contribution usually shares loss according to responsibility. A full shift from a vicariously liable employer to the actively negligent employee is indemnity. C: Satisfaction describes the plaintiff's full recovery on the judgment; it is not the employer's reimbursement theory against the active tortfeasor. D: Payment satisfying the plaintiff does not automatically bar an indemnity claim by the vicariously liable party against the active wrongdoer.
lb_rule_reference: Contribution apportions loss among joint tortfeasors, while indemnity can shift the entire loss to the party primarily responsible when another party's liability is only vicarious or passive.
