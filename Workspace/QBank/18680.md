Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Joseph contracted with Esther to create a custom inventory program for her shop. Esther repudiated before Joseph delivered any part of the program. Joseph had reasonably spent $15,000 paying programmers and renting servers for the project. Because the program was never delivered, Esther received no benefit from Joseph's work. Joseph cannot prove his expected profit with certainty. What is Joseph's best damages theory?
Answer A: Restitution measured by Esther's benefit, because Joseph spent money on the project.
Answer B: Reliance damages measured by Joseph's reasonable expenditures made in preparation for performance.
Answer C: The full contract price, because every repudiation entitles the nonbreaching party to the price.
Answer D: No recovery, because Esther received no benefit.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages focus on the injured party's expenditures made in reliance on the contract. Restitution focuses on the benefit conferred on the defendant. Because Esther received no benefit but Joseph reasonably spent $15,000 preparing to perform and cannot prove expectation, reliance is the correct theory.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because restitution would be measured by Esther's benefit, and she received none. C: C is wrong because an action for the full price is not the ordinary remedy for this services repudiation. D: D is wrong because reliance can compensate Joseph even if Esther received no benefit.
