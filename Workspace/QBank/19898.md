MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Self-defense
Question: Sofia suffers from a delusional disorder. She believes a neighbor is spiritually corrupt and that destroying his car would be morally righteous. Before smashing the car windows, Sofia tells a friend, "The police will arrest me for this because the law calls it vandalism, but heaven wants it done." The jurisdiction applies M'Naghten and defines "wrong" as legally wrong. Is Sofia likely to succeed on an insanity defense?
Answer A: Yes, because Sofia believed the act was morally justified
Answer B: Yes, because any delusional disorder is enough for insanity
Answer C: No, because she knew the act was legally wrong in a legal-wrongfulness M'Naghten jurisdiction
Answer D: No, because M'Naghten requires proof that the defendant could not control her conduct
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: M'Naghten asks whether, because of mental disease, the defendant did not know the nature and quality of the act or did not know it was wrong. This jurisdiction defines wrongfulness legally. Sofia knew she was damaging property and knew the law forbade it. Her moral delusion does not satisfy the legal-wrongfulness prong.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The jurisdiction defines wrongfulness as legal wrongfulness. Sofia knew the law prohibited the act and expected arrest. B: Mental disease is not enough. The disease must cause the precise incapacity required by the applicable test. D: M'Naghten is a cognitive test. Control inability belongs to irresistible-impulse or MPC-style formulations, not pure M'Naghten.
lb_rule_reference: In a jurisdiction defining wrongfulness as legal wrongfulness, M'Naghten is not satisfied when the defendant knew the act was prohibited by law
