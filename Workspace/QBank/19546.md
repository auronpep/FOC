Question: A tenant was injured by a single ceiling collapse caused by a plumber's negligent repair and a landlord's negligent failure to inspect. The tenant settled with the plumber for $25,000 and expressly reserved claims against the landlord. A jury later assessed total damages at $100,000. The jurisdiction uses a dollar-for-dollar settlement credit. In the tenant's negligence claim against the landlord, what is the likely effect of the plumber settlement?
Answer A: The landlord owes the full $100,000 because the tenant reserved claims against the landlord
Answer B: The landlord owes $75,000, reflecting the $100,000 damages minus the $25,000 settlement credit
Answer C: The landlord owes nothing because settlement with one tortfeasor satisfies the entire claim
Answer D: The landlord owes only if the plumber was less than 50 percent at fault
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The jurisdiction supplies a dollar-for-dollar credit. The settlement does not release the landlord, but it reduces the remaining recoverable judgment to avoid double recovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Reserving claims prevents release of the landlord; it does not let the tenant recover more than total damages. The credit reduces the landlord's exposure. C: A partial settlement is not full satisfaction unless it equals or is intended as full recovery. The agreement reserved claims, and damages exceed the settlement. D: The stated jurisdiction uses a dollar-for-dollar settlement credit. The plumber's percentage is not the controlling credit rule in this question.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff's settlement with one tortfeasor reduces the recoverable judgment against nonsettling tortfeasors under the applicable credit rule so that the plaintiff does not obtain double recovery.
