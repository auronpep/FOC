Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: A homeowner sues a federal agency for approving a report that criticized development near a river. She asks the court to order the agency to withdraw the report, arguing that private banks might then choose to finance her project. No bank is a party, and no bank has promised to lend if the report is withdrawn. What is the main standing problem?
Answer A: Redressability is speculative because relief depends on independent bank decisions
Answer B: Standing exists because a court order might improve the plaintiff's chances
Answer C: Standing exists because environmental reports always injure property owners
Answer D: The case is a political question because banks influence development policy
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The requested order does not itself finance the project, and the needed bank decisions are uncertain nonparty choices.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Redressability requires likely relief, not a speculative possibility of help through nonparties. C: The plaintiff still must show concrete injury, traceability, and likely redress. D: The defect is Article III redressability, not textual commitment to another branch or lack of standards.
lb_rule_reference: Redressability requires that the requested court order likely remedy the plaintiff's injury; speculative independent choices by nonparties can break the chain.
