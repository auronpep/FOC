Question: A county places a winter display in front of the courthouse. It includes a creche donated decades ago, a menorah, a winter festival sign, lights, and secular holiday figures. The display has appeared in similar form for many years. A resident argues the creche alone makes the display unconstitutional. What is the best Establishment Clause analysis?
Answer A: The display is unconstitutional per se because it includes a religious symbol on public property
Answer B: The court should evaluate the display's full context, including history, setting, and surrounding secular or plural elements
Answer C: The display is always valid because any long-standing religious display is constitutional
Answer D: The display is valid only if each religious group in the county gets a separate symbol
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Establishment analysis for displays does not turn on a single symbol in isolation. The whole context matters.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Context matters; religious symbols are not automatically invalid on public property. C: History is relevant but does not automatically validate every display, especially if context shows coercion or government favoritism. D: The Constitution does not require an inventory of every religion in each holiday display.
lb_rule_reference: Religious symbols in public displays are evaluated in context, including history and surrounding secular or commemorative elements; a symbol is not automatically invalid.
