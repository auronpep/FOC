Outline_code: 44040100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: State Action Requirement
Question: A farmer owned a large chicken farm next to a private airport that was often used by the United States Air Force for training. During the training exercises, Air Force pilots often flew very low over the farm. The noise from the planes frightened the farm animals and resulted in the deaths of several chickens. Eventually, the noise because so severe that the farmer had to abandon his business. The farmer sued the United States, arguing that the government had taken his property without just compensation. Did the Air Force's actions result in a compensable taking?
Answer A: A: No, because the training flights took off from a private airport.
Answer B: B: No, because the training flights did not adversely affect all potential uses of the land.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the low training flights caused the death of several chickens.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the farmer owned the airspace above his farm.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A taking occurs when government action causes a direct and immediate interference with a landowner's use and enjoyment of the land. United States v. Causby, 328 U.S. 256 (1946). Here, the low training flights have interfered with the farmer's ability to raise chickens, so a compensable taking has occurred under the Fifth Amendment. A is incorrect because the fact that the flights take off from a private airport does not change the analysis. B is incorrect because a taking can still occur even if the government does not adversely affect all potential uses of the land. D is incorrect because the farmer would not own all of the airspace above his farm, and the real issue is whether the government has interfered with the farmer's use of the land.
