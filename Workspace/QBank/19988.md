Question: A recorded easement gave David a 20-foot-wide access strip across Esther's land. Esther built a permanent fence that openly occupied the outer 8 feet of the strip, leaving a 12-foot lane open. David knew of the fence, used only the remaining 12-foot lane, and made no objection for 12 years. The jurisdiction recognizes partial extinguishment of easements by prescription, and the prescriptive period is 10 years. What is the best result?
Answer A: The entire 20-foot easement necessarily survives because partial prescription is impossible.
Answer B: Esther may have extinguished David's easement as to the fenced 8-foot portion, while the remaining 12-foot lane may continue.
Answer C: Esther automatically acquired fee title to David's dominant parcel.
Answer D: The entire easement terminated because any obstruction to any part of an easement destroys all of it.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Where recognized, prescription against an easement can extinguish the right to the extent of the adverse obstruction. Esther openly and adversely occupied 8 feet of the 20-foot strip for longer than the statutory period, so the easement may be narrowed as to that portion while the unobstructed portion continues.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the facts state the jurisdiction recognizes partial extinguishment. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because blocking part of an easement does not transfer the dominant parcel. D: D is wrong because partial obstruction need not terminate the entire easement.
lb_rule_reference: Partial extinguishment by adverse obstruction
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is choosing an all-or-nothing result when the facts support partial loss limited to the obstructed portion.
