MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Consent
Question: A plaintiff files a state-court action against Defendant A and Defendant B, both of whom have been properly served. The case satisfies all requirements for diversity-based removal. Defendant A, within the 30-day removal window, files a notice of removal without consulting Defendant B. Defendant B objects to removal and did not join in or consent to the notice of removal. Defendant A argues that it can remove unilaterally because Defendant A has an independent right to access federal court. The plaintiff moves to remand within 30 days of the notice of removal, relying solely on Defendant B's failure to join. The governing forum is initially state court, now removed, under the MBE baseline. Should the court remand the case?
Answer A: No, because each defendant has an independent right to remove to federal court and Defendant A's removal is valid without Defendant B's participation
Answer B: Yes, the court should remand because Defendant B did not join in or consent to removal, violating the unanimity requirement under § 1446(b)(2)(A)
Answer C: No, because Defendant B's failure to consent is a defect in the notice of removal, not a lack of subject matter jurisdiction, and Defendant B can ratify the removal after the fact
Answer D: No, because the plaintiff's motion to remand is untimely — the 30-day window to challenge removal runs from service of the original complaint, not from the notice of removal
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1446(b)(2)(A) requires that all defendants who have been properly joined and served must join in or consent to removal. Defendant B was properly served but did not join in or consent to Defendant A's removal notice. This is a procedural defect in removal. The plaintiff timely moved to remand within 30 days of the notice of removal, preserving this objection under § 1447(c). The court should remand the case.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1446(b)(2)(A) codifies the unanimity rule: all defendants who have been properly joined and served must join in or consent to the notice of removal. Unilateral removal by one defendant is a procedural defect. There is no independent individual removal right when co-defendants exist. C: While the defect is procedural (not jurisdictional), a defendant cannot retroactively ratify a removal notice after the 30-day removal window has closed. The window for filing a compliant removal notice is 30 days; if all defendants do not join within that window, the removal is defective and remand is required when timely sought. Here, the plaintiff timely sought remand and the defect has not been cured within the window. D: Under § 1447(c), a motion to remand based on a defect other than lack of subject matter jurisdiction must be made within 30 days after the filing of the notice of removal — not within 30 days of service of the original complaint. The plaintiff here moved within 30 days of the notice of removal, which is timely.
lb_rule_reference: 28 U.S.C. § 1446(b)(2)(A) — all defendants who have been properly joined and served must join in or consent to removal; unilateral removal by one defendant over the other's objection is a procedural defect.
