Outline: Scope
Question: Martha held a recorded easement across David's land. David later sought a construction loan secured by a mortgage on the land. To help David obtain the loan, Martha signed a written subordination agreement stating that her easement would be junior to the new mortgage. The lender recorded the mortgage and later properly foreclosed junior interests. The foreclosure buyer refuses Martha access. Which result is most accurate?
Answer A: Martha's easement necessarily survives because it was originally recorded before the mortgage.
Answer B: Martha's easement necessarily survives because easements cannot be subordinated.
Answer C: Martha's easement may be extinguished because she subordinated it to the mortgage that was later properly foreclosed.
Answer D: Martha now owns the mortgaged land because foreclosure of a subordinated easement transfers fee title to the easement holder.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A senior easement holder may agree to subordinate the easement to a later mortgage. Once subordinated, the easement can be treated as junior to that mortgage, and proper foreclosure of the mortgage may extinguish the subordinated easement interest.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because original recording priority can be altered by subordination. B: B is wrong because easement interests can be subordinated by agreement. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because extinguishment or priority loss does not transfer fee title to the easement holder.
lb_rule_reference: Subordination agreement / foreclosure effect
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is relying on the original recording order without processing the later subordination agreement.
