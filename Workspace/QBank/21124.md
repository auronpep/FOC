Question: Solomon contracted to buy 50 commercial ovens from a seller for $2,000 each. The seller wrongfully failed to deliver. Solomon promptly bought substitute ovens of similar capacity and quality for $2,150 each. The substitute ovens were a different brand, but they reasonably served the same business need. No incidental or consequential damages are claimed. What may Solomon recover?
Answer A: Nothing, because cover goods must be identical in brand and model.
Answer B: $100,000, the original contract price.
Answer C: $107,500, the full substitute purchase price.
Answer D: $7,500, the reasonable cover-price differential.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Cover requires a good-faith, reasonable substitute purchase without unreasonable delay; the substitute goods need not be identical if they reasonably serve the same need. The cover differential is $150 per oven × 50 = $7,500.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the substitute need not be identical. B: B is wrong because the original contract price is not the buyer's damages. C: C is wrong because cover damages are the differential, not the full substitute price.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-712
