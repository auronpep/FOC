Question: In a federal traffic trial, Lydia testifies that the traffic light was green when Mark entered the intersection. On cross-examination, Mark's lawyer asks, “When you saw Mark enter against the red light, where were you standing?” Lydia's lawyer objects. What is the best objection?
Answer A: The question is hearsay.
Answer B: The question is leading.
Answer C: The question calls for expert opinion.
Answer D: The question misstates the witness's testimony.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The question asserts that Lydia saw Mark enter against a red light, but Lydia testified that the light was green. The best objection is that the question misstates the evidence or testimony.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the defect is not an out-of-court statement. B is not the best objection; leading is generally permitted on cross. B: A is wrong because the defect is not an out-of-court statement. B is not the best objection; leading is generally permitted on cross. C is wrong because no expert opinion is being requested. C: C is wrong because no expert opinion is being requested.
