


BARMATRIX Q#: 17103
Outline_code: 96060100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Law Applied by Federal Courts (Erie)
Outline: Erie Doctrine
Question: A doctor, a citizen of State A, sued a pharmaceutical company, incorporated in State B and with its primary place of business in State B, in federal court in State B. The doctor claimed $500,000 in damages based on breach of contract and tortious interference claims, arguing the actions of the company made it impossible for him to make necessary contracts with medical suppliers in State C, where he also had a practice.&#10;&#10;In deciding the doctor's claims, what law regarding contracts and torts should the court apply?
Answer A: A: The law of State A, because a plaintiff is the master of his or her claim.
Answer B: B: The law of State B, because jurisdiction is based on diversity.
Answer C: C: The law of State C, because this is where the claim arose.
Answer D: D: The federal common law, because the doctor chose to sue in federal court.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When exercising jurisdiction based on diversity, a federal court is required to apply the substantive law of the state in which it is sitting. Here, the federal court is sitting in State B.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect because the plaintiff does not get to choose which laws the federal court will follow simply because he or she brought the claim. C is incorrect because choice of law issues are not based upon what state the claim arose in. D is incorrect because although there is federal common law, federal courts cannot use federal common law to decide state law claims.
