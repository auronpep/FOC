MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: Two employees discussed a personal financial problem at normal volume in an open break room while several coworkers were present. A supervisor standing nearby heard the conversation without using any device, trick, or hidden access. The employees later sued for intrusion upon seclusion. Are the employees likely to prevail on an intrusion upon seclusion claim against the supervisor?
Answer A: Yes, because the topic was personal and embarrassing
Answer B: No, because the supervisor merely overheard an open conversation in a shared workplace area
Answer C: Yes, because intrusion is established whenever there is no consent to listen
Answer D: No, because privacy torts require a written publication
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: There was no highly offensive invasion of seclusion because the conversation occurred audibly in an open break room.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The employees discussed it openly in a common area where others were present; the supervisor did not intrude into a private space or communication. C: Lack of consent alone does not establish a highly offensive intrusion into a private matter. D: Intrusion does not require publication; the claim fails because no private seclusion was invaded.
lb_rule_reference: Intrusion requires invasion of a private place or matter; listening to a conversation knowingly held in an open area usually is not enough.
