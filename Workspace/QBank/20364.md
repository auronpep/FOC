Question: Elijah told Elisha that Elisha could construct a footbridge over a stream on Elijah's land to reach Elisha's cabin. Elisha built the bridge at substantial expense. Elijah later tried to revoke all access. Elisha asks the court to declare that he owns the land under the bridge in fee simple. What should the court most likely do?
Answer A: Declare Elisha the fee owner because reliance always transfers title.
Answer B: Deny all relief because bridges can never be the subject of land-use rights.
Answer C: Declare Elijah the owner and require Elisha to remove the bridge despite Elijah's promise and Elisha's reliance.
Answer D: Recognize, if appropriate, an irrevocable license or easement by estoppel limited to the bridge use rather than fee title.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Estoppel can protect a relied-on use, but it does not ordinarily transfer fee title to the servient land. The remedy should match the permission and reliance: continued bridge access, if the estoppel elements are met.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reliance does not automatically convey fee title. B: B is wrong because bridges or access structures can be within a land-use right. C: C is wrong because substantial reliance may make revocation inequitable. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Estoppel remedy is use right, not title
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is overcorrecting from revocable license to full ownership instead of selecting the narrow use-right remedy.
