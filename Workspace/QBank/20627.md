Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Diversity Jurisdiction
Question: On January 1, Olivia, a citizen of Maine, files a state-law tort action in federal court against Ben, also a citizen of Maine, seeking $120,000. On February 1, Ben permanently moves to Vermont and changes his domicile before filing any response. Does Ben's move create diversity jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because diversity is measured when the defendant responds
Answer B: No, because both parties were Maine citizens when the complaint was filed
Answer C: Yes, because any later change in citizenship controls if it occurs before trial
Answer D: No, because tort claims can never be heard under diversity
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Complete diversity was absent at filing, and the later move does not retroactively create jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The relevant time is filing, not response. C: Later citizenship changes usually do not affect diversity jurisdiction. D: State tort claims can be heard under diversity if citizenship and amount requirements are met.
lb_rule_reference: Diversity citizenship is determined when the complaint is filed.
