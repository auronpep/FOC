Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Assault
Question: From across a locked courthouse lobby, a litigant shouted at an opposing witness, "When this hearing ends tomorrow, I will punch you." Security officers stood between them, and the litigant made no movement toward the witness. The witness was frightened and avoided the hearing the next day. The witness sues for assault. Is the litigant liable for assault?
Answer A: No, because the statement threatened future contact rather than imminent contact
Answer B: Yes, because the witness was actually frightened
Answer C: Yes, because words alone always create assault when they mention punching
Answer D: No, but only because the witness was not physically touched
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The words point to tomorrow, and the facts negate present ability or immediate threatened contact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Fear alone is not enough; assault requires apprehension of imminent contact. C: Words can matter, but the threat must create reasonable imminent apprehension. D: Assault does not require contact; this answer reaches the result for the wrong reason.
lb_rule_reference: Assault requires reasonable apprehension of imminent harmful or offensive contact; a future threat, without present ability or immediacy, is not enough.
