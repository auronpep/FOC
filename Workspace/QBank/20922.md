Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: In a federal battery trial, John claims that a blow from Goliath could not have broken his wrist. John asks to stage a courtroom demonstration in which a large actor wearing a costume similar to Goliath's strikes a mannequin while dramatic sound effects play. Goliath objects under Rule 403. Which ruling is most appropriate?
Answer A: Admit the demonstration because demonstrative evidence is always helpful.
Answer B: Admit the demonstration only if the actor is the same height as Goliath.
Answer C: Exclude or limit the demonstration if its theatrical features and dissimilarity risk misleading the jury or causing unfair prejudice that substantially outweighs its probative value.
Answer D: Exclude the demonstration because all demonstrations are inadmissible unless they recreate the exact event.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Demonstrative evidence may be useful, but theatrical presentation and dissimilar conditions can mislead the jury or invite an improper emotional response. Rule 403 allows exclusion or limitation if those risks substantially outweigh probative value.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because demonstrative evidence is subject to Rule 403. B: B is wrong because matching height alone does not cure theatrical or misleading features. D: D is wrong because demonstrations need not perfectly recreate the event, but they must not mislead or unfairly prejudice.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats demonstrative aids as either automatically admissible or automatically barred.
