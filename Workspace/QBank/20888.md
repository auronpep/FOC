Question: Police arrest Dana for robbery, take her to the station, give Miranda warnings, and question her before any indictment, information, arraignment, or preliminary hearing. Dana waives her Miranda rights and admits she drove the getaway car. She later moves to suppress solely on the ground that the Sixth Amendment required counsel during the interview. Should the statement be suppressed on Sixth Amendment grounds?
Answer A: No, because formal adversary proceedings had not yet begun, so the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had not attached
Answer B: Yes, because custody automatically triggers both Miranda and the Sixth Amendment right to counsel
Answer C: Yes, because police questioned Dana about a serious felony
Answer D: No, because the Sixth Amendment never applies to police questioning before trial
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Arrest and custody alone do not attach the Sixth Amendment. Because no formal charge or comparable adversary proceeding had begun, Dana's Sixth Amendment right was not yet in play.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Miranda is Fifth Amendment doctrine. The Sixth Amendment turns on formal adversary proceedings, not custody alone. C: Offense seriousness does not attach the Sixth Amendment before formal proceedings begin. D: After attachment, deliberate elicitation during questioning can violate the Sixth Amendment. This answer is too broad.
lb_rule_reference: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches only when formal adversary proceedings begin, such as indictment, information, arraignment, preliminary hearing, or formal charge.
