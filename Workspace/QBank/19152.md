Question: Miriam sells Joseph an old silver cup. Both say before signing that they do not know whether it was made by a famous artisan or by an ordinary shop. Joseph says, “That uncertainty is why I am paying a lower price; I will take the chance.” After signing, Joseph learns the cup was made by an ordinary shop and is worth less than it would have been if it were famous. Can Joseph rescind for mutual mistake?
Answer A: Yes, because both parties lacked certainty about the cup's maker.
Answer B: Yes, because a mistaken belief about authenticity always permits rescission.
Answer C: No, because Joseph consciously accepted the authenticity risk as part of the bargain.
Answer D: No, but only because Miriam also lacked knowledge.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A party who knows there is uncertainty and decides to proceed anyway may bear the risk of mistake. Joseph expressly treated the unknown maker as part of the bargain and paid a lower price because of that risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because uncertainty plus an express decision to take the chance defeats mistake. B: B is wrong because authenticity mistakes can support rescission only if the risk was not allocated to the party seeking avoidance. D: D is wrong because Miriam's lack of knowledge alone is not the reason; Joseph's assumption of risk is decisive.
lb_rule_reference: Known uncertainty / authenticity risk
