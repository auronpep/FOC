Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal bribery case, the prosecutor plays an authenticated recording of an anonymous caller. Deborah, who worked beside Paul for five years, listens to the recording and testifies that the caller's voice is Paul's. Paul objects that Deborah did not hear the call live. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because voice identification must be based on hearing the call live.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because a familiar witness may identify a voice heard through a recording.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because only an expert may identify a recorded voice.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the recording is not offered for its contents.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Voice identification may be based on a voice heard firsthand or through mechanical or electronic transmission or recording, if the witness has an adequate basis for familiarity. Deborah's workplace familiarity supplies that basis.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because live hearing of the call is not required. C: C is wrong because a lay witness with familiarity can identify a voice. D: D is wrong because voice identification and use of the recording's contents are separate questions.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(5)
lb_student_diagnostic: Live-hearing and expert-only traps.
