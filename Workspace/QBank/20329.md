Question: A furniture store employee was assigned to drive a store van from the warehouse to a customer. Instead, he drove six miles in the opposite direction to watch his friend's softball game. While circling the stadium parking lot before the game, he negligently struck a pedestrian. The employee intended to make the delivery after the game. Is the furniture store likely vicariously liable under respondeat superior for the pedestrian's negligence injury?
Answer A: Yes, because the employee was driving the store's van during work hours
Answer B: No, because the employee was on a substantial personal frolic and had not returned to the delivery route
Answer C: No, because employers are never liable for employee driving accidents unless they personally directed the route
Answer D: Yes, because the employee intended eventually to complete the delivery
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The accident occurred during a substantial personal departure from the assigned delivery. The plan to resume work later does not place the employee back within scope at the time of the crash.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Scope depends on time, place, purpose, and degree of deviation. A six-mile trip to attend a game is a substantial personal frolic. C: Employers often are liable for employee driving within scope. This employer avoids liability because of the frolic, not because route direction is always required. D: Scope is measured when the tort occurs. Intent to return later does not make the stadium trip a detour.
lb_rule_reference: A substantial personal departure from authorized time, place, and purpose is a frolic outside the scope of employment until the employee returns to the employer's business.
