Question: A nonparty files a timely motion for permissive intervention and shows that its claim shares a common legal question with the main action. The court finds intervention would require reopening extensive discovery and would substantially prejudice the original parties. May the court deny permissive intervention?
Answer A: No, because a common question makes permissive intervention automatic
Answer B: Yes, because the court may consider undue delay or prejudice to the original parties
Answer C: No, unless the proposed intervenor lacks subject-matter jurisdiction
Answer D: Yes, but only if all existing parties jointly object
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 24(b) directs the court to consider delay and prejudice when deciding permissive intervention.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common question is not enough; the court considers delay and prejudice. C: Even with jurisdiction, permissive intervention may be denied for delay or prejudice. D: The court's discretion does not require unanimous party objection.
lb_rule_reference: Permissive intervention requires a timely motion and a claim or defense sharing a common question with the main action; the court must consider whether intervention will unduly delay or prejudice adjudication of the original parties' rights.
