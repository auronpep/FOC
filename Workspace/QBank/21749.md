Question: Moses owned a single tract of land. He conveyed the interior portion to Ruth and retained the surrounding land. At the moment of conveyance, the interior portion had no access to any public road except by crossing Moses's retained land. The deed did not mention access. Ruth seeks a right to cross Moses's retained land to reach the public road. Which result is most accurate?
Answer A: Ruth has an easement by necessity because the parcel was landlocked when the common tract was severed.
Answer B: Ruth has no easement because the deed did not expressly grant one.
Answer C: Ruth has only a revocable license because access across another's land can never be implied.
Answer D: Ruth has an implied easement by prior use only if Moses had previously used an existing visible road across the retained land.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement by necessity may arise when a common owner severs land and one resulting parcel is landlocked at the time of severance. The doctrine implies access over the land of the common grantor or grantee when strict necessity exists.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because necessity may imply access despite silence in the deed. C: C is wrong because an easement by necessity is a property interest, not merely a revocable license. D: D is wrong because prior use is a separate implied-easement route; a necessity easement does not require a preexisting visible road.
lb_rule_reference: Easement by necessity / strict necessity at severance
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating deed silence as fatal even when severance created a landlocked parcel.
