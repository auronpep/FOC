Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Deborah agreed to manufacture custom signs for David for $90,000. Deborah expected total production costs of $72,000, so the expected profit was $18,000. David repudiated after Deborah reasonably incurred $70,000 in costs. The signs were specially made, had no regular market, and could be sold only for $8,000 in salvage. What is Deborah's proper recovery under the special-goods expectation formula?
Answer A: $80,000.
Answer B: $62,000.
Answer C: $18,000.
Answer D: $90,000.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under UCC Article 2 after buyer repudiation of specially manufactured goods. Because ordinary market or resale damages are inadequate, the formula is expected profit plus costs reasonably incurred minus salvage proceeds: $18,000 + $70,000 − $8,000 = $80,000. The limiting gate is offset for salvage and saved costs, and the result scope is partial expectation. The answer does not award the price because the goods were not accepted and the price-action categories are not the basis of recovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it awards cost minus salvage but omits the expected profit. C: C is wrong because it awards profit only and ignores reasonable costs already incurred. D: D is wrong because it overuses the contract price and ignores the UCC expectation formula and salvage offset.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-708(2) — profit plus reasonable costs incurred minus salvage when market damages are inadequate
