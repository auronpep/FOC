Question: An airport authority sells ad space inside secure boarding areas. It rejects all campaign ads but accepts travel, restaurant, and tourism ads. The rule applies to all candidates and all political parties. A candidate challenges the refusal. What is the likely forum analysis?
Answer A: The secure boarding area is a traditional public forum, so the ad rule is automatically invalid
Answer B: The ad space is likely a nonpublic forum where reasonable, viewpoint-neutral limits on campaign ads may be valid
Answer C: The rule is viewpoint discrimination because it rejects all campaign ads equally
Answer D: The rule is valid only if it satisfies strict scrutiny
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A categorical ban on campaign ads in a nonpublic forum can be reasonable if viewpoint neutral.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Airports and secure boarding areas are not traditional public forums. C: Rejecting an entire subject category is not the same as favoring one side of a debate. D: Nonpublic forum restrictions generally need reasonableness and viewpoint neutrality, not strict scrutiny.
lb_rule_reference: In a nonpublic forum, the government may impose reasonable, viewpoint-neutral restrictions.
