Question: Mary owned Lot 1 with a recorded easement across Lot 2. The city later bought Lot 2 from its owner by ordinary deed, not through condemnation. The deed to the city did not mention Mary's recorded easement. The city then told Mary that the easement ended because the servient land was now publicly owned. Which result is most accurate?
Answer A: The easement ended automatically because all private easements vanish when a city buys the servient land.
Answer B: The easement ended because the city's deed did not repeat it.
Answer C: The easement generally continues because an ordinary purchaser, including the city, takes subject to an existing recorded easement unless the easement is lawfully condemned or otherwise terminated.
Answer D: Mary now owns Lot 2 in fee simple because her easement was recorded first.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An ordinary purchase of servient land does not by itself extinguish an existing recorded easement. The city may acquire land subject to private property interests unless it condemns or otherwise lawfully terminates them. The deed's silence does not erase the recorded easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because public ownership alone does not automatically destroy private easements. B: B is wrong because the deed need not repeat a recorded easement for it to survive. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because an easement is not fee title.
lb_rule_reference: Government ownership / easement survives ordinary purchase
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating government acquisition as condemnation. The source lock asks whether the city merely bought the land or formally took the easement.
