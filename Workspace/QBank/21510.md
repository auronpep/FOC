MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Falling below the standard of care
Question: An architect designed a roof using a method accepted by comparable architects at the time. An unusually intense storm later damaged the roof. The owner offers no expert testimony that the design fell below professional standards and argues that the damage itself proves malpractice. Is the poor result alone enough to establish professional breach?
Answer A: Yes, because any failed professional project proves negligence
Answer B: Yes, because architects are judged by ordinary lay intuition only
Answer C: No, because the plaintiff needs proof that the architect fell below comparable professional care
Answer D: No, because professionals are never liable for negligence
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Without evidence that accepted professional practice was breached, the damage alone does not establish malpractice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A poor result alone does not prove the professional fell below comparable professional care. B: Professional conduct is measured against comparable professional knowledge and skill. D: Professionals can be liable when they fall below the professional standard. The facts do not show that here.
lb_rule_reference: Professionals are judged by the knowledge, skill, and care of comparable professionals; a poor result alone does not establish breach.
