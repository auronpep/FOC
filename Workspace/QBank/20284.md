Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Levi contracted with Hannah to stage a traveling exhibit. Hannah breached. Levi proves that he spent $4,000 on exhibit permits before the breach. Levi also testifies that he turned down another possible project that he believes might have produced $25,000, but he has no signed contract, no customer records, and no reliable profit data for that project. What is the strongest recoverable reliance amount on these facts?
Answer A: $29,000, because Levi relied by spending money and passing up another project.
Answer B: $25,000, because lost opportunities are always reliance damages.
Answer C: $4,000, because the permit expenses are proven reliance expenditures and the alleged lost opportunity is too speculative.
Answer D: Nothing, because reliance damages are unavailable when a plaintiff mentions a lost opportunity.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages cover proven expenditures made in reliance. A claimed lost opportunity may require proof of causation and certainty. Levi's possible project lacks reliable proof, while the $4,000 permit expense is concrete reliance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it adds a speculative opportunity value. B: B is wrong because lost opportunities are not automatically reliance damages. D: D is wrong because the speculative opportunity does not defeat the proven permit expenses.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance damages / lost opportunity decoy
