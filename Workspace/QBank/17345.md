Question: A gang leader pointed a gun at Quinn and said, "Shoot the witness now or I will kill you." Quinn believed the threat was immediate and real. Quinn took the gun the leader handed him and intentionally shot the witness, killing her. Quinn was charged with murder. Is duress a complete defense to the murder charge at common law?
Answer A: Yes, because Quinn reasonably feared immediate death
Answer B: Yes, because Quinn did not choose the situation voluntarily
Answer C: No, because duress is not a complete defense to intentional murder at common law
Answer D: No, because duress is available only when the threat comes from a natural force rather than a person
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Quinn intentionally killed the witness. Although the threat might satisfy many ordinary duress requirements, common law generally withholds duress as a complete defense to intentional murder. Quinn may not obtain a complete acquittal on that basis.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common law generally does not allow duress as a complete defense to intentional murder. The homicide limitation blocks the defense even when the threat is grave. B: Lack of voluntary exposure may be relevant to duress generally, but it does not overcome the common-law rule barring duress as a complete defense to intentional murder. D: Duress is the doctrine for human threats. The defense fails here because of the intentional-murder limitation, not because the threat came from a person.
lb_rule_reference: At common law, duress is generally not a complete defense to intentional murder, even when the defendant acts under threat of death.
