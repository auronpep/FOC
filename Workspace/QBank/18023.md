Outline: Scope
Question: Mary owned Blackacre, which had an appurtenant driveway easement over Peter's land. Mary later bought adjoining Whiteacre, which had no easement over Peter's land. Mary began using the driveway over Peter's land to serve both Blackacre and Whiteacre. The added Whiteacre traffic substantially increased use of the driveway. May Mary use the easement to benefit Whiteacre?
Answer A: Yes, because Mary owns both Blackacre and Whiteacre.
Answer B: Yes, because any appurtenant easement may be used for all land owned by the dominant owner.
Answer C: No, but only if Peter proves adverse possession of the driveway.
Answer D: No, because an appurtenant easement may not be expanded to benefit non-dominant land or materially overburden the servient estate.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Mary may not use the easement to benefit Whiteacre. An appurtenant easement is tied to the dominant estate, here Blackacre. Using it to serve non-dominant land, especially with a substantial increase in burden, exceeds the easement's scope.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because common ownership of adjacent land does not expand the easement's dominant estate. B: B is wrong because the easement benefits the parcel identified as dominant, not every parcel owned by the same person. C: C is wrong because the issue is overburdening, not adverse possession.
lb_rule_reference: Appurtenant easement / no benefit to non-dominant land
