Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: HelmetCo manufactures motorcycle helmets in State A. It appoints a State B distributor, pays for State B motorcycle-event booths, and provides State B warranty service for the same helmet model. A State B rider buys that model from the distributor and is injured in State B when the helmet allegedly fails. The rider sues HelmetCo in State B. What is the best analysis of specific personal jurisdiction?
Answer A: Jurisdiction is supported because HelmetCo deliberately served the State B market for the same product and the claim arose from that market activity
Answer B: Jurisdiction fails because a product manufacturer can be sued only where it is incorporated or headquartered
Answer C: Jurisdiction exists only because HelmetCo could foresee that any helmet might travel to State B
Answer D: Jurisdiction fails because Plaintiff's injury occurred after the helmet left HelmetCo's factory
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The distributor, advertising, warranty service, and same-product injury supply purposeful forum targeting and relatedness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Specific jurisdiction can arise from claim-linked forum targeting even when general jurisdiction is absent. C: The stronger basis is not bare foreseeability; it is deliberate service of the State B market. D: Specific jurisdiction does not require injury at the factory. The claim relates to HelmetCo's forum-directed product activity.
lb_rule_reference: Specific jurisdiction is stronger when a manufacturer targets or serves the forum market through distributors, advertising, support, or other deliberate forum-directed conduct and the claim arises from that product activity.
