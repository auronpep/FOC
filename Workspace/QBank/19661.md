Question: David contracted with Naomi to buy Naomi's bakery. In that sale contract, David promised to pay Naomi's creditor Hannah $7,000. Naomi fully performed the bakery sale. David also had a separate landscaping contract with Naomi, and Naomi breached that landscaping contract. David refuses to pay Hannah, arguing that Naomi owes him damages under the landscaping contract. May David use Naomi's separate landscaping breach to avoid paying Hannah under the bakery sale contract?
Answer A: Yes, because any claim David has against Naomi may be asserted against Hannah.
Answer B: Yes, because an intended beneficiary is always responsible for all debts of the promisee.
Answer C: No, because Hannah's beneficiary claim under the bakery contract is not defeated by David's unrelated claim against Naomi from a separate contract.
Answer D: No, but only because Hannah gave consideration directly to David.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A promisor may assert defenses that arise from the contract the beneficiary seeks to enforce. David's unrelated claim against Naomi under a separate landscaping contract does not defeat Hannah's right under the bakery sale contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because beneficiary rights are not automatically subject to every unrelated claim against the promisee. B: B is wrong because an intended beneficiary is not a guarantor of the promisee's separate obligations. D: D is wrong because beneficiary consideration is not the reason Hannah can enforce.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — defense must arise from relevant contract
