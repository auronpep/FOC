Question: Sarah owned a large tract with an interior cabin parcel and an outer ring of land touching the public road. Sarah conveyed the entire outer ring to Jacob but retained the interior cabin parcel. After the conveyance, Sarah's retained parcel had no legal access to the public road except by crossing the land conveyed to Jacob. The deed did not mention access. Under the majority rule, what is Sarah's best claim?
Answer A: Sarah has no claim because a grantor can never receive an implied access right over land she conveyed.
Answer B: Sarah has no claim unless she proves a visible road existed before the conveyance.
Answer C: Sarah may have an easement by necessity because the conveyance landlocked her retained parcel at severance.
Answer D: Sarah has only a revocable license because all implied rights retained by grantors are personal.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement by necessity may arise when severance of a common tract leaves either the conveyed parcel or the retained parcel without legal access. Under the majority rule stated in the call, Sarah may claim access by necessity because her retained parcel was landlocked at severance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the majority rule does not categorically bar an implied necessity easement for retained land. B: B is wrong because visible prior use is required for prior-use implication, not for necessity. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because an easement by necessity is a property interest, not merely a revocable license.
lb_rule_reference: Easement by necessity for retained land
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is applying prior-use requirements to a strict necessity fact pattern.
