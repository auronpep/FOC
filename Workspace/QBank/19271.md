Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: A statute makes it a crime to sell a restricted chemical to a person under 18, regardless of whether the seller knows the buyer's age. Lee agrees to sell the chemical to Maya, who is actually an adult undercover officer. Lee believes Maya is 20. Before the sale occurs, police arrest Lee and charge him with attempted sale of the chemical to a minor. Under the common-law attempt rule, is Lee guilty of attempt?
Answer A: Yes, because the completed offense would not require Lee to know the buyer's age
Answer B: No, because Lee did not intend to sell the chemical to a person under 18
Answer C: Yes, because Maya was an undercover officer rather than a real buyer
Answer D: No, because attempt liability is impossible whenever the completed offense is strict liability
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Attempt requires proof that Lee specifically intended to commit the target crime. Because the target crime depends on sale to a minor and Lee believed the buyer was an adult, the attempt mens rea is missing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Attempt is a specific-intent crime. For an attempted strict-liability offense, the defendant must intend the facts that would make the conduct criminal. Lee did not believe Maya was under 18. C: The undercover status is not the reason. The problem is Lee's lack of intent as to the age circumstance. D: Attempt liability can exist for a strict-liability target if the defendant specifically intends the circumstance that would make the completed conduct criminal.
lb_rule_reference: Attempt requires the specific intent to commit the target crime, including intent as to the circumstance that makes the target conduct criminal, even when the completed offense would impose strict liability.
