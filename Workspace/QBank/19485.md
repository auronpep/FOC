Question: A driver negligently broke a pedestrian's wrist. A doctor prescribed inexpensive therapy that would have prevented stiffness. The pedestrian unreasonably ignored the therapy, causing additional stiffness and two extra months of lost work. The driver admits liability for the initial fracture but challenges the later losses. What is the likely effect of the pedestrian's refusal?
Answer A: The pedestrian is barred from all recovery for the wrist injury
Answer B: The pedestrian may recover everything because the driver caused the first injury
Answer C: Recovery may be reduced by the stiffness and lost work that reasonable therapy would have avoided
Answer D: The pedestrian may recover only nominal damages because damages became uncertain
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The pedestrian keeps damages for the original fracture but may lose avoidable damages caused by unreasonable failure to mitigate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Failure to mitigate affects avoidable additional damages, not the entire original claim. B: The plaintiff must take reasonable mitigation steps. Avoidable later losses can be excluded. D: The original physical injury supports compensatory damages; mitigation only limits avoidable added loss.
lb_rule_reference: When a plaintiff unreasonably fails to mitigate after injury, recovery is reduced by the damages that reasonable mitigation would have avoided, not barred entirely.
