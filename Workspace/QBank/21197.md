Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: Guards removed Lina from her cell, handcuffed her to a wall in a locked interview room, and told her she could not return to her unit until detectives finished questioning her about a new assault charge. Detectives questioned her for three hours without Miranda warnings, and she confessed. Should Lina's confession be suppressed under Miranda?
Answer A: Yes, because the added restraints made the interrogation custodial and no warnings were given
Answer B: Yes, because all questioning of prisoners requires counsel to be present
Answer C: No, because Lina was already in prison before the questioning began
Answer D: No, because Miranda applies only to police stations, not prisons
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The handcuffing, locked room, refusal to let Lina return, and prolonged questioning created interrogation pressure beyond ordinary incarceration. Miranda warnings were required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Counsel is not automatically required for every prison question. The issue is custodial interrogation without warnings. C: Prison status does not end the inquiry. Added restraints can create Miranda custody for the new interrogation. D: Miranda depends on custodial interrogation, not a stationhouse location.
lb_rule_reference: An inmate is in Miranda custody for a new interrogation when the setting adds restraint comparable to formal arrest for that investigation.
