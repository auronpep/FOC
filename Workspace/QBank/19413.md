Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Andrew signed a deed conveying Pastureacre to Philip. Andrew placed the deed in a sealed envelope, handed it to Philip, and said, “Carry this to my title company for safekeeping. I am not delivering it to you, and Pastureacre is still mine.” Philip opened the envelope and recorded the deed. Who owns Pastureacre?
Answer A: Philip, because the named grantee physically received the deed.
Answer B: Philip, because recording conclusively proves delivery.
Answer C: Philip, unless Andrew demanded the deed back before it was recorded.
Answer D: Andrew, because Philip received the deed only as a messenger and Andrew expressly negated present delivery.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is present intent. Andrew used Philip only as a messenger and expressly said the deed was not being delivered and that the land remained Andrew's. Philip's physical custody and recording did not create delivery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because physical custody by the named grantee is not conclusive when the grantor's words show a messenger purpose. B: B is wrong because recording is not conclusive proof of delivery. C: C is wrong because the defect was lack of present delivery, not merely failure to demand return.
lb_rule_reference: Giving the deed to the named grantee for a limited messenger purpose is not delivery if the grantor expressly negates present transfer
