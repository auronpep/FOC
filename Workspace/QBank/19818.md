Question: David signed a deed granting Solomon “a permanent right to drive across the paved strip on David's land to reach Solomon's field.” The deed does not give Solomon any right to remove timber, crops, minerals, or other resources from David's land. How is Solomon's right best classified?
Answer A: A negative easement because David must avoid interfering with Solomon.
Answer B: A profit because the right increases the usefulness of Solomon's field.
Answer C: An affirmative easement because Solomon may physically use part of David's land for access.
Answer D: A real covenant because the deed describes a permanent land-use arrangement.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An affirmative easement gives the holder the right to enter or use another's land for a limited purpose. Solomon may drive across the paved strip on David's land, so the right is an affirmative easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a negative easement restrains the servient owner's use rather than allowing the holder to enter or use the land. B: B is wrong because a profit involves removing resources from land. D: D is wrong because the deed grants a use right, not merely a promise enforceable as a covenant.
lb_rule_reference: Affirmative easement / right to use servient land
