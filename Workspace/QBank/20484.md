Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police unlawfully searched a parolee's jacket and found a firearm. Prosecutors declined new criminal charges, but the parole board seeks to use the firearm evidence at a parole revocation hearing. The parolee moves to exclude the firearm from the revocation hearing solely because of the unlawful search. Is exclusion required in the parole revocation hearing?
Answer A: Yes, because every Fourth Amendment violation triggers exclusion in every proceeding
Answer B: Yes, because parole revocation is more serious than a criminal trial
Answer C: No, because the exclusionary rule generally does not bar the evidence in parole revocation proceedings
Answer D: No, because parolees have no Fourth Amendment rights at all
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Even assuming the search violated the Fourth Amendment, exclusion is generally not required in parole revocation proceedings. The remedy available in a criminal trial does not automatically transfer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The exclusionary rule is limited by proceeding type. It generally does not require exclusion in parole revocation hearings. B: Consequence alone does not make the exclusionary rule apply with full trial force. D: Parolees do not lose all Fourth Amendment protection. The issue here is the limited exclusionary remedy.
lb_rule_reference: Evidence obtained in violation of the Fourth Amendment is generally not excluded from parole revocation proceedings.
