Question: Noah owned an undivided one-half interest in a parcel. Noah signed and delivered a quitclaim deed to Ruth stating that he quitclaimed “all my right, title, and interest” in the parcel to Ruth. Ruth accepted. What did Ruth receive from Noah?
Answer A: Nothing, because a quitclaim deed is not a deed of conveyance.
Answer B: A promise that Noah had good title to the entire parcel.
Answer C: The entire parcel, because quitclaim deeds always convey full ownership.
Answer D: Whatever interest Noah had in the parcel, but no general warranty that Noah owned more.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a quitclaim deed, and the decisive deed-type lock is the interest conveyed. A quitclaim deed can convey whatever right, title, and interest the grantor actually has, but it does not provide the covenants associated with a warranty deed. Ruth therefore received Noah's one-half interest, not a warranty of the whole parcel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a quitclaim deed can be an effective conveyance. B: B is wrong because a quitclaim does not generally promise good title. C: C is wrong because Noah could convey only the interest he owned.
lb_rule_reference: Quitclaim deed conveys whatever interest grantor has, without title covenants
