Question: A state temporarily bars importation of cattle from a region experiencing a contagious livestock disease. The record shows no reliable inspection can detect the disease during its incubation period, and the ban ends when the outbreak is contained. What is the state's strongest defense?
Answer A: A genuine disease quarantine with no reasonable nondiscriminatory alternative can survive DCC scrutiny
Answer B: Discrimination against out-of-state goods is always invalid, with no exceptions
Answer C: The ban is valid because states may always prefer local producers
Answer D: The ban must be analyzed only under Article IV privileges and immunities
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The state has a legitimate health purpose and evidence that inspection cannot solve the problem.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The state can justify discrimination in rare cases with no reasonable nondiscriminatory alternative. C: The valid reason is disease prevention without alternatives, not local preference. D: The fact pattern concerns goods in commerce, not nonresident citizens' fundamental privileges.
lb_rule_reference: A state may justify a discriminatory health restriction if it proves a legitimate local purpose, such as preventing disease, and no reasonable nondiscriminatory alternative.
