


BARMATRIX Q#: 18151
Outline_code: 44040604
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Eminent domain—Takings Clause
Question: The city, as part of an economic development plan for the city's waterfront area, condemned 15 houses owned by the plaintiffs. The properties were not blighted and were not rundown in any way. The plan was to give the properties to a private developer to create new office space. The plaintiffs challenged the city's action. The court should rule in favor of
Answer A: A: the plaintiffs, because the houses were being given to a private party.
Answer B: B: the plaintiffs, because the houses were in good repair.
Answer C: C: the city, because the houses were condemned as part of an economic development plan.
Answer D: D: the city, because the condemnation resulted in mere land use regulation.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the Fifth Amendment “Takings” Clause, the government cannot take property without fair compensation. Importantly, private property can only be taken for a public use. Consequently, the government cannot simply take private property and hand it to another private owner without some sort of public use. However, the property does not have to be generally open to the public after a taking, and it can be given over to a private party so long as the public can be expected to receive some benefit from its use. Here, since the houses were condemned as part of an economic development plan benefiting the public, the condemnation was a legal taking even though a private party was developing the property.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A and B are therefore incorrect. In some circumstances, a land use regulation can result in a taking. D is therefore incorrect.
