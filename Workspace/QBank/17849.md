Question: In a diversity action arising from a boat collision, two defendants are citizens of the same state. One defendant asserts a crossclaim against the other for contribution arising from the same collision. The crossclaim seeks less than the amount-in-controversy threshold. What is the best jurisdictional analysis?
Answer A: The crossclaim fails because every claim in diversity must independently satisfy complete diversity and the amount requirement
Answer B: The crossclaim is allowed procedurally and likely falls within supplemental jurisdiction because it arises from the same collision
Answer C: The crossclaim is jurisdictionally proper because Rule 13(g) creates federal subject-matter jurisdiction
Answer D: The crossclaim is barred by Section 1367(b) because it is asserted by a defendant
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 13(g) permits the crossclaim, and its shared factual nucleus supports supplemental jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A same-transaction crossclaim may be supported by supplemental jurisdiction. C: Joinder rules do not create subject-matter jurisdiction. D: The key statutory limits target specified claims by plaintiffs or persons seeking to join as plaintiffs, not this defendant crossclaim.
lb_rule_reference: A related crossclaim that forms part of the same case or controversy may usually fall within supplemental jurisdiction, but the joinder rule itself does not supply jurisdiction.
