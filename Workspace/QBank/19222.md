Question: A cyclist was injured when an old custom racing frame cracked. Over the years, three local shops had modified different parts of the frame using different materials and methods. The cyclist cannot identify which modification caused the crack and sues all three shops under market-share liability. Is market-share liability likely appropriate?
Answer A: Yes, because the plaintiff cannot identify the exact causal shop
Answer B: Yes, because all defendants worked on the same bicycle
Answer C: No, because custom nonfungible modifications do not fit market-share liability
Answer D: No, because causation uncertainty always defeats every negligence claim
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The frame work involved different modifications and methods. The plaintiff's uncertainty does not justify market-share liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The doctrine is narrow and generally tied to fungible products. Custom modifications by different shops are not fungible market shares. B: Working on the same object does not make the conduct or product fungible. D: Some doctrines address causal uncertainty. This particular market-share theory does not fit.
lb_rule_reference: Market-share liability is narrow and generally limited to fungible products where identifying the manufacturer is impossible.
