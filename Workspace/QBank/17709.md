Question: Jon, a citizen of Utah, sues Kara, a citizen of Idaho, in federal court. Jon asserts a $50,000 contract claim and an unrelated $35,000 property-damage claim against Kara. Both claims are brought by Jon against Kara. Is the amount in controversy satisfied?
Answer A: Yes, because one plaintiff may aggregate multiple claims against one defendant to exceed $75,000
Answer B: No, because unrelated claims can never be aggregated
Answer C: No, because each individual claim must exceed $75,000
Answer D: Yes, but only because the claims arise under federal law
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Jon's claims total $85,000, so the amount requirement is met.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Relatedness is not required for a single plaintiff aggregating against one defendant. C: That is not the rule for one plaintiff's claims against one defendant. D: The stem is testing diversity amount aggregation, not federal question.
lb_rule_reference: For Section 1332, one plaintiff may aggregate all claims against one defendant, related or unrelated, to exceed $75,000.
