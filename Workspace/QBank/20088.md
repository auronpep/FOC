Question: In a federal civil trial, Amos asks Lydia on direct, “Did you inspect the pump and tell Mark it was safe before noon?” Mark objects that the question is compound. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because both parts of the question concern the same pump.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the question combines multiple facts in a way that could make the answer unclear.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because compound questions are allowed on direct but not on cross.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Lydia answers both parts incorrectly.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may control the form of questions to avoid confusion. A compound question asks multiple things at once—inspection, statement to Mark, safety, and timing—so an answer may be unclear. The objection should be sustained.
