Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Roles of judge and jury
Question: In a products-liability case, Sarah offers Deborah as an expert on brake design. Deborah's résumé shows twenty years designing commercial braking systems. Isaac objects that Deborah's résumé contains hearsay statements from former employers and that the jury, not the judge, should decide whether Deborah is qualified. Who decides whether Deborah is qualified to testify as an expert?
Answer A: The judge decides qualification and may consider the résumé for that preliminary purpose.
Answer B: The judge decides qualification but may consider only testimony admissible under the hearsay rules.
Answer C: The jury decides qualification because expert credibility is always for the jury.
Answer D: The jury decides qualification unless both parties agree that the judge may decide it.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The judge decides preliminary questions about witness qualification, including expert qualification. In deciding that gate, the judge is not bound by the evidence rules except privilege.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the judge may consider otherwise inadmissible information when resolving preliminary admissibility questions. C: C is wrong because the jury may weigh an admitted expert's testimony, but qualification is for the court. D: D is wrong because party consent is not needed for the court to decide expert qualification.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a), 702
