


BARMATRIX Q#: 22848
Outline_code: 95070705
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Preliminary injunctions, permanent injunctions, and temporary restraining orders
Question: A windfarm company built a large wind turbine next to a cattle ranch. The rancher believed that the sound from the turbine was upsetting his cattle and causing them not to breed. The rancher filed for a preliminary injunction to stop the turbine. The court denied the injunction and decided to hear the case on the merits. The rancher, who is losing several thousands of dollars a day, comes to you for advice. What is the best course of action for the rancher to take?
Answer A: A: Immediately appeal the denial of the injunction.
Answer B: B: Appeal after the court has issued a final order.
Answer C: C: File a new lawsuit requesting damages from the windfarm company.
Answer D: D: Move for a temporary restraining order after discovery is completed.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Interlocutory orders denying an injunction are immediately appealable, which is an exception to the general rule that interlocutory orders are unappealable because they are not final. 28 U.S.C. §1292(a). Since the rancher is losing several thousands of dollars a day, the quickest course of action would be for him to immediately appeal the denial of the injunction. B is incorrect because the rancher does not have to wait for a final order under the circumstances. C is incorrect because filing a new lawsuit for damages will not stop the harm being done to his cattle. D is incorrect because the rancher does not have to wait until discovery is completed to do something.
