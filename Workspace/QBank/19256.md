Question: Daniel promised Esther in a written contract that he would pay Esther's creditor, John, $4,000. Esther mailed John a copy of the contract. John read it but did nothing in response and made no change in position. Before John demanded payment or filed suit, Daniel and Esther agreed to reduce the payment to John to $2,000. What amount may John enforce against Daniel?
Answer A: $4,000, because learning of the contract automatically vested John's rights.
Answer B: $2,000, because John had not vested his rights before the original parties modified the promise.
Answer C: $4,000, because creditor beneficiaries are always immune from later modification.
Answer D: Nothing, because creditors can never be intended beneficiaries.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: John may have been an intended creditor beneficiary, but merely receiving and reading the contract did not itself vest his rights. Without reliance, assent requested by a party, suit, or similar vesting event before the modification, John takes only the modified promise.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because awareness alone is not the same as vesting. C: C is wrong because creditor-beneficiary status does not automatically bar pre-vesting modification. D: D is wrong because creditors can be intended beneficiaries.
