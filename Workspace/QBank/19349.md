Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Solomon owned Lot 5. He signed a writing that said, “I want Elijah to have Lot 5 after I am gone. This letter explains my wish.” Solomon handed the writing to Elijah, who kept it. Solomon is still alive. Elijah claims that he owns Lot 5 now. Who owns Lot 5?
Answer A: Solomon, because the writing expresses a future wish and lacks present words of conveyance.
Answer B: Elijah, because the writing is signed and identifies the property.
Answer C: Elijah, because physical delivery of the writing substitutes for words of transfer.
Answer D: Elijah, because a deed need not be recorded to be valid.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is operative words of present transfer. The writing expresses Solomon's future wish, not a present conveyance of title. Because the instrument does not presently transfer the land, title remains with Solomon.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a signed writing and property identification are not enough without words of transfer. C: C is wrong because delivery cannot supply missing operative conveyance language. D: D is wrong because the no-recording rule is true but irrelevant when the instrument is not a deed of present transfer.
