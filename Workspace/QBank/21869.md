Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police have an arrest warrant for Victor. They receive a tip that Victor is visiting a friend's apartment. Without consent, exigency, or a search warrant for the apartment, officers enter the friend's home and arrest Victor in a bedroom. Was the entry into the friend's apartment lawful?
Answer A: Yes, because an arrest warrant follows the suspect into any home
Answer B: No, because police needed a search warrant for the third party's home or a valid exception
Answer C: Yes, because the tip gave police reasonable suspicion Victor was inside
Answer D: No, but only because Victor was found in a bedroom rather than the living room
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The arrest warrant authorized arrest of Victor but did not authorize warrantless entry into a third party's home absent consent or exigency.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A third party's home has its own Fourth Amendment protection. An arrest warrant alone is not enough. C: Reasonable suspicion does not authorize home entry to arrest someone in a third party's home. D: The entry problem exists before reaching room-by-room scope.
lb_rule_reference: An arrest warrant for a suspect does not by itself authorize entry into a third party's home; police generally need a search warrant or an exception.
