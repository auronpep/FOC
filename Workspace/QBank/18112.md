Question: In a federal employment trial, Silas testifies that he heard Lydia tell a supervisor, “I resign.” A court reporter happened to transcribe the meeting. Lydia objects that Silas may not testify unless the transcript is introduced. Silas is testifying from memory of what he heard. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the transcript is the best evidence of the conversation.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Silas has reviewed the transcript.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Silas's firsthand testimony about what he heard is a proper proof vehicle and does not require the transcript.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the transcript is unavailable.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness with personal knowledge may testify to an oral statement heard. The existence of a transcript does not bar live testimony when the transcript's contents are not the proof vehicle.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and D are original-writing false positives. B creates an irrelevant review requirement. B: A and D are original-writing false positives. B creates an irrelevant review requirement. D: A and D are original-writing false positives. B creates an irrelevant review requirement.
