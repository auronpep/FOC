Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A quarry posted guards and signs warning that blasting would occur at noon and that no one should enter the marked blast zone. A trespasser read the signs, waited until the warning siren began, and climbed over the barrier to retrieve a lost drone. A blast sent debris that injured him. The trespasser sues for strict liability. Which issue most directly limits the strict-liability claim?
Answer A: The quarry used reasonable care by posting signs, so strict liability automatically disappears
Answer B: The trespasser's knowing entry into the warned blast zone may limit or bar recovery
Answer C: The quarry is liable because strict liability has no defenses
Answer D: The trespasser wins only if he proves the blasting crew was negligent
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff knowingly entered the precise danger zone during the blast warning. Assumption of the abnormal risk is the key limiting issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Due care is not the central strict-liability defense. The stronger issue is the plaintiff's knowing and voluntary encounter with the abnormal risk. C: Strict liability still has scope limits and can recognize plaintiff conduct defenses such as knowing assumption of risk. D: The claim is strict liability for blasting; negligence is not the required theory.
lb_rule_reference: Knowing and voluntary assumption of the abnormal risk may limit or bar recovery in strict liability for abnormally dangerous activities.
