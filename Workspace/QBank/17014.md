Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: Hannah sues Caleb after a stairway collapse, claiming the collapse caused her knee injury. Caleb offers a photograph from two weeks before the collapse showing Hannah using a knee brace. Hannah objects that the photograph does not prove her current injury was preexisting because people use braces for many reasons. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the photograph, because it does not conclusively prove that Hannah's current injury predated the collapse.
Answer B: Exclude the photograph, because a photograph can never show medical causation.
Answer C: Admit the photograph only if Caleb calls a doctor to explain it before the jury sees it.
Answer D: Admit the photograph, because it has some tendency to show a possible preexisting knee condition; Hannah's explanation goes to weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The knee-brace photograph has some tendency to make a preexisting condition more probable, which is consequential to causation and damages. It need not conclusively establish the condition.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because conclusiveness is not required for relevance. B: B is wrong because photographs can be circumstantial evidence of physical condition. C: C is wrong because expert testimony is not always required for threshold relevance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is demanding medical certainty before admitting circumstantial condition evidence.
