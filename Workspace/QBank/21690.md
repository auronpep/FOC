Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Solicitation
Question: Rafi asks an undercover officer to steal a painting that Rafi believes is hanging in a private gallery. Unknown to Rafi, the painting has already been moved to a police evidence room and is not at the gallery. The officer never intends to steal anything. Rafi is charged with solicitation to commit burglary. What is the best answer?
Answer A: Rafi is not guilty because the painting was not actually in the gallery
Answer B: Rafi is guilty because he intentionally asked another person to commit what he believed would be a burglary
Answer C: Rafi is not guilty because undercover officers cannot be solicited
Answer D: Rafi is guilty only if the officer entered the gallery
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rafi communicated a request that the officer commit a burglary. The painting's absence and the officer's true intent do not defeat completed solicitation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Solicitation focuses on the intentional request. If the facts were as Rafi believed, the requested conduct would be criminal. C: Solicitation does not require the solicited person to be willing or culpable. D: Solicitation is complete upon the intentional communication of the request.
lb_rule_reference: Factual impossibility is not a defense to solicitation when the defendant intentionally asks another person to commit conduct the defendant believes would be criminal.
