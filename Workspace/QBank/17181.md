Question: In a federal antitrust trial, Zion Ltd. designates Joseph as its Rule 615 representative. Zion also wants Rachel, another employee who will testify, to remain at counsel table because she knows the company's pricing records. The opponent requests sequestration. Zion makes no showing that Rachel is essential to presenting its defense. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Permit both Joseph and Rachel to remain because a corporation may keep all employee-witnesses in the courtroom.
Answer B: Permit Joseph to remain as the designated representative, but exclude Rachel unless Zion shows that her presence is essential or another exception applies.
Answer C: Exclude both Joseph and Rachel because a corporation is not a natural person.
Answer D: Permit Rachel to remain automatically because any employee who knows records is exempt.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A non-natural party may have one designated officer or employee representative exempt from exclusion. Additional witness-employees are not automatically exempt; they must fit another exception, such as being essential to presenting the claim or defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A overexpands the representative exception. C ignores the entity representative exception. D treats knowledge of records as automatic essentiality.
