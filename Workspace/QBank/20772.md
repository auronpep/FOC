Question: Noah contracted with a contractor to renovate a home for Noah's mother, Deborah. The contract stated that the contractor would make the home wheelchair accessible for Deborah and that “any claim to enforce this contract must first be submitted to arbitration.” The contractor failed to build the ramp. Deborah sued the contractor in court as an intended beneficiary, without seeking arbitration. What is the contractor's strongest argument?
Answer A: Deborah cannot ever enforce the contract because only parties are bound by arbitration clauses.
Answer B: Deborah can sue in court because she was not a party to the contract procedure clause.
Answer C: Deborah can sue in court only if Noah assigns his rights to her.
Answer D: Deborah's claim arises under the contract and is subject to the contract's arbitration requirement.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A beneficiary who sues to enforce a contract generally takes the benefit subject to the contract's limits and procedures. Deborah cannot claim the ramp promise while avoiding the arbitration requirement in the same contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an intended beneficiary may enforce the contract, but subject to its terms. B: B is wrong because beneficiary status does not let Deborah cherry-pick only favorable terms. C: C is wrong because assignment is not necessary for beneficiary enforcement.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — enforcing contract subject to procedure terms
