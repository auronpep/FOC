Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: In a federal fraud trial, the prosecutor offers evidence that Solomon texted a witness, “If you testify about the forged invoices, you will regret it.” Solomon objects that the threat makes him look bad and will prejudice the jury. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the text because threats are always character evidence.
Answer B: Exclude the text because all evidence showing consciousness of guilt is unfairly prejudicial.
Answer C: Admit the text if its probative value on consciousness of guilt is not substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice.
Answer D: Admit the text only if Solomon admits he sent it for the purpose of silencing the witness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A threat to a witness can be highly probative of consciousness of guilt. The evidence is damaging, but it is not excluded merely for that reason. The court still balances probative value against unfair prejudice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the evidence is offered for consciousness of guilt, not merely character. B: B is wrong because consciousness-of-guilt evidence is not categorically excluded. D: D is wrong because the proponent need not obtain Solomon's admission to the threatening purpose.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403
lb_student_diagnostic: Student overuses the unfair-prejudice label against strongly probative conduct.
