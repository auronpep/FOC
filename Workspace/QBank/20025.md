Question: In a federal negligence trial, Abraham stipulates that he owned the delivery van involved in the collision. Later, Sarah offers the stipulation and also argues that ownership proves Abraham was negligent. Abraham objects to the negligence argument. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Allow the stipulation to prove ownership but not as conclusive proof of negligent driving.
Answer B: Exclude the stipulation because ownership stipulations are hearsay.
Answer C: Allow the stipulation as conclusive proof of every disputed issue.
Answer D: Exclude the stipulation unless a witness with personal knowledge signs it in court.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A stipulation can establish the specific fact stipulated—ownership—but it does not automatically prove negligence or every other element.
