Subtopic: Rights in Land
Outline: Running with the land
Question: Boaz and Naomi owned adjoining lots served by a private road already open to both lots under a separate recorded easement. In a later deed, Boaz promised that “Boaz and successors as owners of Lot 2 shall pay one-half of the reasonable cost of maintaining the private road.” The later deed did not grant any new right to enter or cross land. What is the best classification of the later deed promise?
Answer A: An easement because every road-related provision is an easement.
Answer B: A revocable license because the promise concerns use of a road.
Answer C: An affirmative real covenant because it is a promise to pay land-related maintenance costs, not a new use right.
Answer D: A profit because the road increases the value of both lots.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A real covenant is a land-related promise enforceable by damages if running requirements are met. The later deed promise requires successors to pay maintenance costs; it does not create a new right to enter or use land.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because road provisions can be covenants when they require payment rather than grant use. B: B is wrong because this is a deed promise, not mere permission. D: D is wrong because increased value is not resource removal.
