Question: In a federal employment case, Lydia testifies that her supervisor never mentioned age when refusing to hire her. After Lydia is given an opportunity to explain, the defendant calls Martha, who testifies that Lydia previously said, “The supervisor told me he wanted younger workers.” The prior statement was not made under oath at any proceeding. The court admits Martha's testimony only to impeach Lydia. The defendant asks the court to tell the jury it may use the statement to prove the supervisor's words. Lydia requests a limiting instruction. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Tell the jury it may use the prior statement for any purpose because it was admitted in evidence.
Answer B: Tell the jury it may use the statement only to evaluate Lydia's credibility, not to prove the supervisor made the statement.
Answer C: Exclude Martha's testimony entirely because prior inconsistent statements are never admissible.
Answer D: Give no instruction because limiting instructions are always discretionary.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The prior statement was admitted only for impeachment and was not substantively admissible under the federal prior inconsistent statement rule. Under FRE 105, when evidence is admissible for one purpose but not another, the court must restrict the evidence to its proper scope and instruct the jury accordingly on timely request.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because admission for impeachment does not automatically authorize substantive use. C: C is wrong because prior inconsistent statements can be admitted to impeach. D: D is wrong because Rule 105 makes a limiting instruction mandatory on timely request.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 105 / limiting instruction for impeachment-only material
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests limiting-purpose control after impeachment-only evidence is admitted.
