Outline: Definition
Question: In a federal negligence trial, Paul claims that Adam's cart struck him in a warehouse. Paul offers testimony that immediately after the incident Adam said, “I should have watched where I was going.” Adam objects on the sole ground that the statement is irrelevant because another rule might later exclude some post-accident statements. How should the court rule on relevance?
Answer A: Exclude the statement as irrelevant because post-accident statements can never make negligence more probable.
Answer B: Exclude the statement as irrelevant unless it qualifies under a hearsay exception.
Answer C: Admit the statement as relevant because it tends to make Adam's lack of care more probable, although another rule may separately affect admissibility.
Answer D: Admit the statement only if Adam repeats it in court.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: At the relevance stage, the statement tends to make negligent conduct more probable. Other admissibility questions, such as hearsay or policy exclusions, are separate from threshold relevance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because post-event statements can have probative value. B confuses relevance with hearsay admissibility. B: A is wrong because post-event statements can have probative value. B confuses relevance with hearsay admissibility. D is wrong because relevance does not require in-court repetition. D: D is wrong because relevance does not require in-court repetition.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 and 402 / relevance despite possible separate exclusion
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests relevance as the first gate and separates Rule 401 from other admissibility limits.
