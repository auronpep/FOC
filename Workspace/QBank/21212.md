Question: David received an “exclusive dealership” for Esther's solar inverters in the northern region. The written agreement listed products, dealer discounts, and territory, but did not expressly say whether Esther could make direct sales to commercial customers in the region. Esther later sold directly to several regional customers. Which fact best supports David's Contract Content/Meaning argument that the direct sales violated the agreement?
Answer A: David's dealership revenue declined after Esther's direct sales.
Answer B: Before signing, Esther told David she “probably would not need” to make direct sales.
Answer C: David paid rent for a larger showroom after receiving the dealership.
Answer D: In the solar-inverter trade, an “exclusive dealership” in a region ordinarily means that all sales in the region go through the dealer unless the agreement states a direct-sales carve-out.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Established trade usage may give particular meaning to a commercial term and may explain or supplement an exclusive-dealing goods agreement if reasonably consistent with the writing. The trigger facts are the written exclusive dealership, silence about direct sales, and the need to define the content of territorial exclusivity. The decisive lock is best meaning source: a regular trade usage defining exclusive dealership directly supports the disputed term.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it is a damages or causation fact, not the best fact for contract meaning. B: B is wrong because it is a prior oral statement and is weaker because it may be barred or treated as uncertain. C: C is wrong because it may show reliance or business impact, but it does not define the term.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-306(2) exclusive dealing; UCC 1-303 usage of trade; best-source selection
