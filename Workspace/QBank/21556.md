Question: Benjamin agreed in a signed writing to deliver hay to Deborah “by harvest.” The writing did not define “harvest.” Before signing, Benjamin emailed Deborah that in their valley “harvest” meant the end of September, when the last cutting of hay was normally complete. Deborah offers the email to show the delivery date meant September 30. Should the email be admitted?
Answer A: Yes, if the term “by harvest” is ambiguous and the email explains the parties' intended meaning.
Answer B: No, because every email sent before a final writing is excluded.
Answer C: No, because delivery dates can never be explained by extrinsic evidence.
Answer D: Yes, but only to prove Benjamin acted in bad faith.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The email is offered to explain an ambiguous delivery term, not to add an inconsistent term. Extrinsic evidence can be used to resolve the meaning of an ambiguous phrase.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because prior communications may be admissible for explanation. C: C is wrong because time terms can be ambiguous and may require interpretation. D: D is wrong because the email is relevant to contract meaning, not only to bad faith.
