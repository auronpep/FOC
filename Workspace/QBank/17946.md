Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Esther hired Daniel to perform at a festival for a fee of $20,000. Daniel repudiated. Esther proved that ticket sales lost because of Daniel's absence would have generated $90,000 in additional revenue. Esther also proved that, because Daniel did not perform, she saved the $20,000 fee and $10,000 in staging and security expenses tied only to Daniel's act. Assuming the lost ticket revenue is foreseeable and reasonably certain, what is Esther's expectation recovery for the lost event profits?
Answer A: $60,000.
Answer B: $90,000.
Answer C: $110,000.
Answer D: $20,000.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Expectation damages put Esther in the position she would have occupied had Daniel performed, without overcompensation. The lost revenue is $90,000, but Esther avoided $30,000 in performance-related costs, leaving $60,000 in lost net profit.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it awards gross revenue without subtracting saved costs. C: C is wrong because it adds Daniel's unpaid fee rather than treating it as a saved cost. D: D is wrong because the unpaid performance fee is not the lost-profit measure.
lb_rule_reference: Expectation damages / lost event profits
