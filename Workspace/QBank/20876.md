Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police respond to a 911 call reporting screams from an apartment. They enter without a warrant and find an injured resident in the kitchen. After paramedics remove the resident and officers confirm no one else is inside, an officer opens bedroom drawers looking for evidence of assault and finds drugs. Does the emergency-aid exception justify the drawer search?
Answer A: Yes, because the original entry was justified by an emergency
Answer B: No, because once the emergency ended, opening drawers for evidence required a warrant or separate exception
Answer C: Yes, because officers may search every room after any 911 call
Answer D: No, because emergency aid never permits warrantless entry
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The injured person was removed and the apartment was secured. The later search was investigatory.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The emergency justified entry and safety actions, not a later drawer search after the emergency ended. C: Even protective actions are limited by the emergency or safety rationale. D: Emergency aid can justify warrantless entry; the problem is the later drawer search.
lb_rule_reference: Emergency entry permits action tied to the emergency, but once the emergency ends, a further evidence search needs a warrant or another exception.
