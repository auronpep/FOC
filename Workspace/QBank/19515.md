MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: A plaintiff is injured by a machine. Before limitations expires, she knows both the manufacturer and distributor may be liable. To keep litigation simple, she sues only the manufacturer. After limitations expires, discovery shows the distributor may have deeper insurance. The plaintiff moves to amend to add the distributor and claims relation back. Should the amendment relate back under the mistake route?
Answer A: Yes, because the distributor knew the accident happened
Answer B: Yes, because the claim arises from the same machine accident
Answer C: No, unless the manufacturer consents to adding the distributor
Answer D: No, because the plaintiff made a deliberate choice not to sue a known party
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff knew the distributor's identity and possible role before limitations expired but chose not to sue it. That is not a mistake concerning identity under Rule 15(c)(1)(C).
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Notice alone is not enough. The distributor also must have known it was omitted because of a mistake concerning identity. B: Same transaction is necessary but not sufficient for changing parties. The mistake requirement is missing. C: Relation back depends on the proposed new party's notice and mistake facts, not the old defendant's consent.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 15(c)(1)(C) requires that the added party knew or should have known it would have been sued but for a mistake concerning the proper party's identity.
