Question: In a federal contract trial, Luke testifies for Mary. On cross-examination, the opponent asks Luke, “Did you lie on a loan application last year by falsely stating your income?” Mary objects that the question concerns a specific act that did not result in a conviction. The opponent has a good-faith basis for the question. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because specific acts may never be mentioned unless they resulted in a conviction.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the opponent must first prove the loan application with extrinsic evidence.
Answer C: Overrule the objection in the court's discretion because the question concerns conduct probative of Luke's character for truthfulness.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Luke has first offered reputation testimony for his own truthfulness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 608(b) bars extrinsic proof of specific instances used solely to attack truthfulness, but the court may allow inquiry on cross-examination into specific conduct if it is probative of the witness's character for truthfulness or untruthfulness. Lying on a loan application is probative of truthfulness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 608(b) allows discretionary cross-examination about some nonconviction acts. B: B is wrong because extrinsic evidence is not the required route; indeed it is barred if used solely for truthfulness. D: D is wrong because the inquiry may attack Luke's truthfulness without Luke first offering truthful-character evidence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(b) / specific act inquiry on cross-examination
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests cross-examination inquiry versus extrinsic proof for truthfulness-specific acts.
