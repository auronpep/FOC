Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Isaac, while alive, signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey my farm to my heirs.” Isaac had two living sons, Jacob and Esau, and no spouse. Isaac intended Jacob and Esau to receive the farm immediately, but the deed did not name them or otherwise identify them as the grantees. Who owns the farm immediately after delivery?
Answer A: Jacob and Esau, because they would likely be Isaac's heirs if Isaac died that day.
Answer B: Jacob alone, because he was the favored son.
Answer C: Isaac, because a living person has no heirs and the deed does not identify present grantees with reasonable certainty.
Answer D: Isaac's future estate, because title must wait until Isaac dies.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is ascertainable grantee identity at delivery. A living person has no heirs in the technical sense, and the deed did not identify Jacob and Esau as present grantees. Isaac's private intent cannot cure the uncertainty, so title remains with Isaac.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because probable future heirs are not the same as presently ascertainable grantees. B: B is wrong because family preference does not supply deed language. D: D is wrong because the deed attempted a present conveyance but failed for lack of ascertainable grantees; title does not move to a future estate.
lb_rule_reference: Heirs of living person / unascertainable grantee
