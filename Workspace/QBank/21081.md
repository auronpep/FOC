Question: Carla robbed a pharmacy at gunpoint. After taking the drugs and cash, she fled on foot through a parking lot. A loss-prevention officer from an adjacent store witnessed the robbery through a window and pursued her. During the chase — thirty seconds after leaving the pharmacy — Carla shot and killed the loss-prevention officer to avoid capture. Carla argued that the robbery was complete before the shooting. Is Carla guilty of felony murder for the loss-prevention officer's death?
Answer A: No, because the robbery was complete when Carla left the pharmacy, and the shooting occurred after the underlying felony ended
Answer B: No, because the loss-prevention officer was not a victim of the underlying robbery and therefore falls outside the felony murder predicate
Answer C: Yes, because the killing occurred during Carla's immediate flight before reaching a place of temporary safety, which is within the scope of felony murder
Answer D: Yes, because the shooting itself constitutes a new and independent qualifying felony (assault with a deadly weapon) that separately triggers felony murder
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The temporal and geographic scope of felony murder extends through the flight phase until the defendant reaches a place of temporary safety. Thirty seconds into a foot chase in a parking lot adjacent to the robbery scene, Carla had not reached any place of safety. The killing was committed during immediate flight and directly to facilitate escape. Felony murder applies.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Felony murder extends through the escape phase until the defendant reaches a place of temporary safety. Carla was thirty seconds into a foot chase, in the parking lot, with no place of safety in sight. The criminal enterprise was ongoing. The killing during immediate flight before reaching safety falls within the scope of felony murder. B: Felony murder does not require that the person killed be the direct victim of the predicate crime. Any killing occurring in furtherance of or during the commission (including escape) of the qualifying felony is covered. The officer was pursuing Carla in the immediate aftermath of the robbery; the killing was in furtherance of completing the escape. D: The killing during the escape from the robbery is properly analyzed as part of the original robbery's felony murder scope — not as a new and independent predicate felony. Moreover, using the shooting itself as a predicate would raise merger doctrine concerns (the shooting merges with the homicide). The correct analysis is that the killing falls within the scope of the original robbery's felony murder liability during the escape phase.
lb_rule_reference: Felony murder liability extends through the escape phase of a felony until the defendant reaches a place of temporary safety; a killing committed while fleeing from a qualifying felony before reaching safety triggers felony murder
