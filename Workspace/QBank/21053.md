Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Jonathan and Rachel negotiated a contract for catering services. They exchanged a draft labeled “discussion draft only” and never signed a final writing. At trial, Jonathan offers evidence of the parties' oral agreement about dessert service. Rachel objects that the parol evidence rule bars the oral evidence because a draft existed. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the oral evidence because any draft triggers the parol evidence rule.
Answer B: Admit the oral evidence because the parol evidence rule applies only after the parties adopt a writing as a final expression of their agreement.
Answer C: Exclude the oral evidence unless the draft contains a merger clause.
Answer D: Admit the oral evidence only if the contract involved goods.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The parol evidence rule depends on a writing adopted as a final expression of the parties' agreement. A discussion draft that was never adopted as final does not trigger the bar.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the existence of a draft is not enough. C: C is wrong because a merger clause is not needed to admit evidence when there is no final writing. D: D is wrong because the no-final-writing principle is not limited to goods contracts.
lb_rule_reference: No final writing / PER not triggered
lb_student_diagnostic: Student mistakes a negotiation draft for an integrated final writing.
