Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A debt collector secretly placed a recording device in a consumer's closed therapy room to learn why she had missed payments. The collector listened to the session but never shared the recording with anyone. Is the consumer likely to prevail on an intrusion upon seclusion claim against the collector?
Answer A: No, because the collector did not publish the recording
Answer B: Yes, because secretly recording a private therapy session is a highly offensive intrusion
Answer C: No, because truth is a defense to privacy claims
Answer D: Yes, but only if the consumer proves reputational harm
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The collector intentionally invaded a private therapy setting in a highly offensive way, and no later disclosure is required for intrusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intrusion is complete upon a highly offensive invasion of a private matter; publication is not an element. C: Intrusion concerns the offensive invasion itself, not falsity or truth of the information obtained. D: Intrusion protects seclusion and private affairs, not only reputation.
lb_rule_reference: Intrusion requires an intentional, highly offensive invasion of a private place, conversation, or matter; publication is not required.
