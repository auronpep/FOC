Question: Carla came home and found her spouse in bed with another person. She screamed and left the house. She spent the next four hours at a friend's apartment, repeatedly saying she needed to "settle the score." She then returned home with a gun and killed her spouse. Is Carla entitled to voluntary manslaughter mitigation?
Answer A: Yes, because discovering adultery is adequate provocation under common-law framing
Answer B: Yes, because Carla remained angry throughout the four hours
Answer C: No, because the delay and revenge planning gave a reasonable cooling period before the killing
Answer D: No, because adultery can never be legally adequate provocation
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Even assuming adequate provocation at the moment of discovery, Carla left, waited for hours, discussed revenge, obtained a gun, and returned. Those facts defeat the no-cooling requirement and support murder rather than voluntary manslaughter.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Adequate provocation is only one element. A reasonable cooling period defeats mitigation, and Carla's delay plus revenge statements show cooling and retaliation. B: The law also asks whether a reasonable person would have cooled off. Sustained anger alone cannot preserve mitigation after a significant delay and planning. D: The better answer accepts the possible adequacy of the initial provocation but denies mitigation because of cooling time. D states too broad a rule for a common-law question.
lb_rule_reference: Even adequate provocation does not reduce murder to voluntary manslaughter if a reasonable person would have cooled off before the killing or if the defendant actually cooled and acted in revenge.
