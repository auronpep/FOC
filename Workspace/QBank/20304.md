Outline: Conviction of crime
Question: In a federal robbery prosecution, David testifies in his own defense. The prosecutor seeks to impeach David with a five-year-old felony conviction for aggravated assault. David objects. The court finds that the conviction has some probative value for credibility, but that its prejudicial effect to David is equal to that probative value. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the conviction because all felony convictions of testifying defendants are automatically admissible.
Answer B: Admit the conviction because the conviction is less than ten years old.
Answer C: Exclude the conviction because, for a criminal defendant witness, the probative value must outweigh its prejudicial effect to the defendant.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction only if aggravated assault involved dishonesty or false statement.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When the witness is the criminal defendant, a felony conviction under FRE 609(a)(1)(B) is admissible only if the probative value of the evidence outweighs its prejudicial effect to that defendant. Equal probative value and prejudice is not enough.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because defendant-witness felony convictions receive a stricter balancing rule. B: B is wrong because being within ten years does not eliminate the defendant-specific balancing requirement. D: D is wrong because the felony route does not require dishonesty, but it does require the probative value to outweigh prejudice to the defendant.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(a)(1)(B) / criminal defendant witness balancing
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the defendant-witness balancing standard and the modal-force difference between nondefendant and defendant witnesses.
