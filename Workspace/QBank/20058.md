Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Abraham hired Moses to renovate a house. After negotiations, they signed a detailed written agreement stating that Moses would complete the work for $80,000. Before signing, Moses orally said, “I will do the same work for $70,000 if you pay quickly.” The writing contains no reference to the $70,000 figure. After Moses finishes the work, Abraham offers evidence of the prior oral $70,000 statement to reduce the contract price. How should the court treat the evidence?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because prior oral statements are always admissible to show what the parties really intended.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because it contradicts the final written price term.
Answer C: Admit the evidence because the oral statement was made before the written contract was signed.
Answer D: Exclude the entire written agreement because a prior oral price term exists.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When parties adopt a final written agreement, evidence of a prior or contemporaneous agreement may not be used to contradict a term included in the writing. The written agreement sets the $80,000 price. Abraham's offered oral $70,000 price directly contradicts that term.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the parol evidence rule exists precisely to restrict certain prior or contemporaneous statements. C: C is wrong because the statement's pre-signing timing makes it parol evidence; it does not make it admissible. D: D is wrong because the inconsistent oral statement does not invalidate the writing.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law PER / final integrated writing / contradiction
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats prior negotiation evidence as freely admissible even when it contradicts an integrated price term.
