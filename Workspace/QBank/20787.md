Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Jacob signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey my meadow to Leah and Rachel.” Leah and Rachel were both alive and accepted the deed. The deed did not state the size of each share. Jacob later claimed that the deed failed because it did not allocate percentages between Leah and Rachel. What is the best result?
Answer A: Jacob keeps the meadow, because a deed to multiple grantees must state precise percentages.
Answer B: Leah and Rachel take title, with their shares supplied by the default rule rather than by invalidating the deed.
Answer C: Leah takes the entire meadow because she was named first.
Answer D: Rachel takes the entire meadow because the deed did not expressly reject survivorship.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is whether the grantees are living and ascertainable. Leah and Rachel are named living grantees, so the deed does not fail merely because it omits precise percentages. The law supplies a default ownership form or equal shares rather than invalidating the conveyance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because unstated percentages do not make named living grantees unascertainable. C: C is wrong because being named first does not give Leah the whole meadow. D: D is wrong because the absence of percentage language does not give Rachel the entire property.
