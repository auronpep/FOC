Question: To impress friends, Devon drove a stolen sports car at high speed down a packed downtown street at closing time. He swerved around pedestrians and shouted that everyone would "jump out of the way." He lost control, struck a pedestrian, and killed her. What is the most serious homicide offense supported?
Answer A: Involuntary manslaughter only, because Devon did not intend to hit anyone
Answer B: Murder, because Devon consciously disregarded an extreme risk to human life
Answer C: First-degree murder, because using a stolen car during a death is felony murder
Answer D: No homicide liability, because the pedestrian could have avoided the car
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Driving at high speed through a packed downtown street while aware pedestrians are present creates a grave risk of death. Devon's statement shows awareness and disregard. Those facts support depraved-heart murder.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Depraved-heart murder does not require a desire to kill. Devon consciously created an extreme risk of death in a crowded area and disregarded it. C: The call asks the most supported homicide offense on these facts. The clearer route is depraved-heart murder; the facts do not establish a qualifying independent felony-murder predicate and connection. D: A warning does not erase the extreme risk Devon created. The pedestrian's failure to escape is not a superseding cause.
lb_rule_reference: Depraved-heart murder requires reckless conduct that creates a very high risk of death and shows extreme indifference to human life.
