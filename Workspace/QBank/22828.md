


BARMATRIX Q#: 22828
Outline_code: 92080105
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: A woman sued a car company in federal court for injuries she sustained in a car accident. The company answered and denied all liability. The woman's attorney deposed the company's president, and the company's attorney deposed the woman. The company then moved for summary judgment on the ground that the woman had no evidence showing that the company was at fault. What is the woman's best response to the company's motion?
Answer A: A: Argue that the motion should be denied because the company failed to attach any evidence that the company was not at fault.
Answer B: B: Argue that the motion should be denied because more time is needed for discovery, and attach a declaration describing the desired discovery.
Answer C: C: File a cross- motion for summary judgment because the company failed to provide any evidence that the company was not at fault.
Answer D: D: File a demand for a jury trial because the woman's claim is based in negligence.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: In discussing summary judgment, Fed. R. Civ. P. 56 (d) states that “[i]f a nonmovant shows by affidavit or declaration that, for specified reasons, it cannot present facts essential to justify its opposition, the court may: (1) defer considering the motion or deny it; (2) allow time to obtain affidavits or declarations or to take discovery; or (3) issue any other appropriate order.” Here, since there have only been two depositions, and it appears that neither side has gained any appropriate evidence, the woman should ask the court for more time to discover the evidence necessary for her claim. A is incorrect because the court considers the evidence in the record, not evidence attached to the motion. C is incorrect because a cross- motion for summary judgment would not negate the company's summary judgment motion. D is incorrect because a demand for a jury trial would not negate the company's summary judgment motion.
