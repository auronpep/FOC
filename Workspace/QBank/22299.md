


BARMATRIX Q#: 22299
Outline_code: 72060200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Justification Defenses
Question: The police believed a student was dealing drugs at the local high school. A police officer called the school and asked the principal to call the student into her office and search his backpack. The principal called the student in and asked him if she could search his backpack. The student didn't say anything in response. The principal then told the student that if he didn't respond to her questions, she was going to search his backpack. Again, the student didn't say anything. The principal then searched the student's backpack and found a large supply of marijuana. At trial for drug possession, the student moved to suppress the marijuana. Should the court grant the student's motion?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the police officer asked the principal to search the student's backpack.
Answer B: B: Yes, because there was no probable cause for the search of the student's backpack.
Answer C: C: No, because the search was conducted by the principal.
Answer D: D: No, because the student consented to the search with his silence.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When a private individual conducts a search at the direction of a government agent, the search is subject to the same Fourth Amendment scrutiny as if that search was conducted by the government agent himself or herself. Here, for the police officer to lawfully conduct the search, he would need sufficient probable cause. Probable cause for a search requires a reasonable belief that evidence of a crime will be discovered in the object searched. However, there was no probable cause for the search because the student was simply called out from class and there was no indication of a reasonable belief that evidence would be found in the student's backpack. Since there was no probable cause, the search conducted by the principal at the officer's request was unlawful. A is incorrect because a search directed by police would be lawful if there was sufficient probable cause. This makes B a better answer. C is incorrect because the principal conducted the search at the direction of the police. D is incorrect because mere silence would not amount to adequate consent to a search.
