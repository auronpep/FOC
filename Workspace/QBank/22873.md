


BARMATRIX Q#: 22873
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: Dozens of homes were damaged when a dam released a large amount of water without warning and flooded a river dividing States A and B. The owner of the dam was a citizen of State A. The majority of the homeowners were citizens of State A, although almost a third of the homeowners were citizens of State B. The homeowners filed a class action in federal court arguing that the dam owner was negligent when it released the water. Although homeowners in State A suffered the most damage in the flood, two homeowners from State B were named as class representatives. The dam owner moved to dismiss, arguing that the federal court did not have subject matter jurisdiction over the class action. How should the court rule?
Answer A: A: Grant the motion, because the majority of homeowners were citizens of State A.
Answer B: B: Grant the motion, because State A homeowners suffered the most damage.
Answer C: C: Deny the motion, because the claim involved an occurrence on a border between two states.
Answer D: D: Deny the motion, because the class representatives were citizens of State B.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A court has subject matter jurisdiction based on diversity if the amount in controversy is over $75,000 and the plaintiff and defendant are citizens of different states. In determining the citizenship of a class action, only the citizenship of the class representatives is considered. Here, since the class representatives are citizens of State B and the dam owner is a citizen of State A, there is complete diversity of citizenship. A and B are incorrect because the citizenship of the class would not be determined by where the majority of homeowners live or who suffered the most damage. C is incorrect because subject matter jurisdiction would not exist simply because the claim involved an occurrence on a border between two states.
