Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A technician was told to deliver a replacement part to a client on her way to lunch. She chose a restaurant near the client, carried the part in her car, and negligently injured a pedestrian while turning into the client's parking lot before lunch. Is the technician's employer likely vicariously liable?
Answer A: Yes, because the accident occurred while she was substantially performing the assigned delivery
Answer B: Yes, because employers are liable for all employee driving
Answer C: No, because any planned lunch makes the entire trip personal
Answer D: No, because delivery trips are always independent contractor activities
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The personal lunch motive does not remove a work-purpose delivery from scope.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Scope still matters. C: A mixed motive does not automatically create a frolic. D: Nothing in the facts makes her an independent contractor.
lb_rule_reference: A trip can remain within scope when the employee is substantially serving the employer, even if the employee also has a personal motive.
