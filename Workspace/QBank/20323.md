Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Rape
Question: Brennan, age 24, met Cassidy at a college bar that required proof of age for entry. Cassidy, who had used a fake identification card, told Brennan she was 20 years old. Cassidy was in fact 15. Brennan, reasonably relying on Cassidy's representation and the bar's ID check, engaged in consensual sexual intercourse with Cassidy. The age of consent in the jurisdiction is 16. Brennan is charged with statutory rape. Under the traditional common-law approach to statutory rape, which of the following is correct?
Answer A: Brennan is not guilty because he reasonably believed Cassidy was 20 years old and above the age of consent
Answer B: Brennan is not guilty because the intercourse was consensual and Cassidy was not harmed
Answer C: Brennan is guilty because statutory rape is a strict liability offense and a reasonable mistake as to the victim's age is not a defense
Answer D: Brennan is guilty, but the bar's negligence in accepting the fake ID is a complete defense that the bar can assert on Brennan's behalf
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Statutory rape at common law and under most traditional statutes is a strict liability crime with respect to the victim's age. The elements are: (1) sexual intercourse (2) with a person below the statutory age of consent. No mens rea as to age is required. The common law rejects the mistake-of-age defense even when the mistake is entirely reasonable, based on a deliberate legislative policy choice to protect minors categorically. Cassidy was 15 — below the jurisdiction's age of consent of 16 — and the elements of the offense are met.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Statutory rape is a strict liability offense. Under the traditional common-law rule, the defendant's mental state — even a reasonable and good-faith belief about age — is irrelevant. There is no mens rea requirement as to the victim's age. The strict liability rule exists precisely because legislatures chose to protect minors even at the cost of convicting those who acted reasonably. B: Statutory rape criminalizes sexual intercourse with a person below the age of consent regardless of actual consent. The premise of the offense is that minors under a certain age cannot legally consent as a matter of law. Whether Cassidy subjectively agreed or was physically unharmed is legally irrelevant to the charge. D: Criminal defenses are personal to the defendant. A third party's negligence or wrongdoing cannot constitute a defense on behalf of a defendant. Even if the bar were separately liable (which raises different legal questions), that liability has no bearing on Brennan's guilt for statutory rape. Additionally, strict liability means Brennan's own lack of fault provides no defense — a fortiori, someone else's fault provides none.
