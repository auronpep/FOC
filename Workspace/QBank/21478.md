Question: In a civil fraud trial, the plaintiff introduces bank records strongly suggesting that the defendant diverted partnership funds. When asked whether he diverted the funds, the defendant validly invokes the Fifth Amendment because a criminal investigation is pending. The plaintiff asks for an instruction that the jury may, but need not, draw an adverse inference from the refusal. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the instruction because no adverse inference may ever follow a valid Fifth Amendment invocation.
Answer B: Permit a civil adverse-inference instruction, so long as it is permissive and otherwise proper.
Answer C: Give a mandatory instruction that the jury must find the fact against the defendant.
Answer D: Strike the bank records because the defendant invoked the privilege.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: In civil litigation, a valid Fifth Amendment invocation may still permit an adverse inference against a party, unlike the criminal defendant context. The instruction should be permissive, not mandatory. The decisive axis is privilege validity versus civil consequence.
