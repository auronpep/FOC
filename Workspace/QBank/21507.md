Question: David signed and delivered a deed stating, “For one silver coin, I convey my vineyard to Jonathan.” Jonathan accepted the deed, but the coin was never handed over. David later argued that the deed failed because even the nominal consideration was unpaid. Who owns the vineyard?
Answer A: Jonathan, because nonpayment of nominal consideration does not defeat an otherwise valid delivered deed.
Answer B: David, because even nominal consideration must actually be paid before title can pass.
Answer C: David, because a deed for nominal consideration is treated as a will.
Answer D: Jonathan only if he pays the coin before David records a revocation.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is delivery of an executed instrument, not payment of nominal consideration. David signed and delivered a deed with present transfer words, and Jonathan accepted. The missing silver coin may affect a separate promise, but it does not defeat title.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because consideration is not required for deed validity. C: C is wrong because nominal consideration does not make a present deed testamentary. D: D is wrong because the grantor cannot revoke a delivered deed by recording a later objection.
lb_rule_reference: Nominal consideration not paid does not defeat deed
