MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Praxis Consulting Group is incorporated and headquartered in the State of Rendell. Praxis hired Niamh, a resident of the State of Colwick, specifically because Niamh would work from her Colwick home office serving Colwick-based clients — a growing market Praxis wanted to enter. Praxis sent Niamh her laptop and client materials to Colwick. Praxis's Rendell managers regularly emailed and called Niamh in Colwick to oversee her work, and Praxis invoiced Colwick clients for Niamh's services. Niamh's employment agreement contains a 2-year noncompete clause, a Rendell choice-of-law clause, and a clause providing that "any dispute shall be resolved in the courts of Rendell." Niamh left Praxis and began working for a Colwick-based competitor. Praxis filed a noncompete enforcement action in Rendell federal court. Niamh moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. Does the Rendell federal court have specific personal jurisdiction over Niamh?
Answer A: No, because Niamh is a Colwick resident who worked entirely in Colwick — her activities were in Colwick, not Rendell, so she has no Rendell contacts
Answer B: No, because choice-of-law and forum-selection clauses in employment agreements are generally unenforceable against employees in their home states
Answer C: Yes, because the forum-selection clause in Niamh's employment agreement constitutes her advance consent to personal jurisdiction in Rendell, and Praxis's noncompete claim arises directly from that agreement
Answer D: Yes, because Praxis's Rendell headquarters generates employment contracts for all employees, making Rendell the proper forum for any employment dispute
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The employment agreement contains an express forum-selection clause designating Rendell courts. Under consent-based PJ doctrine, a party who contractually agrees in advance to litigate in a specified forum has consented to personal jurisdiction there for claims covered by the clause. The noncompete claim arises from the employment agreement — the clause covers it. Additionally, under *Burger King* totality analysis, Niamh entered a long-term employment relationship with a Rendell company, under Rendell law, with Rendell-based supervision — sufficient purposeful availment independently. Either basis — consent via forum-selection clause or purposeful availment — supports Rendell jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under *Burger King*, the contacts analysis is not limited to physical performance location. Niamh deliberately entered a long-term employment agreement with a Rendell company, under Rendell law, with an express forum-selection clause designating Rendell courts. Niamh knowingly entered a legal and commercial relationship centered in Rendell. These are the kinds of contacts *Burger King* found sufficient: a franchise agreement negotiated with and supervised from a Florida headquarters, governed by Florida law. The employment relationship here is analogous. Moreover, the forum-selection clause is direct consent to Rendell jurisdiction. B: There is no general federal rule invalidating forum-selection clauses in employment agreements. Some states have specific statutes limiting enforcement (California has one for sales representatives, for example), but there is no categorical federal prohibition. The question asks about Rendell federal court jurisdiction — not state-law contract enforcement. Moreover, *Atlantic Marine Construction Co.* established that valid forum-selection clauses are strongly enforceable in federal courts under Rule 1404(a). Without more specific facts about a state statute invalidating this clause, it should be treated as enforceable. D: The fact that Praxis generates contracts from Rendell does not automatically make Rendell the proper forum for all employment disputes. The PJ analysis depends on the defendant's contacts — here, Niamh's contacts with Rendell. The correct analysis examines Niamh's deliberate entry into a Rendell-centered relationship (the forum-selection clause and the *Burger King* totality) — not merely where Praxis's paperwork originates. An employer's home-state contract generation is not a categorical rule making the employer's state the proper forum for all disputes.
lb_rule_reference: Enforcement of a noncompete against a former employee in the employee's home state requires that the employer purposefully availed itself of that state, considering where the employee worked, where the employer directed the employee's activities, the employer's own forum contacts, and the choice-of-law and choice-of-forum provisions in the employment agreement. *Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz* (1985) factors applied to employment context.
