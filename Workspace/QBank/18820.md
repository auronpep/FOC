Question: A town board opens monthly meetings with volunteer prayers from local clergy. The town invites speakers from different faiths and does not require audience participation. One prayer includes sectarian language. A resident sues, arguing that any sectarian reference in a legislative prayer violates the Establishment Clause. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The prayer is unconstitutional solely because it contains sectarian language
Answer B: The practice is not automatically invalid; the court should evaluate history, neutrality, and coercion
Answer C: The practice is always valid because legislative prayer has historical roots
Answer D: The practice is valid only if every prayer is written by the town attorney
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Legislative prayer has a historical foundation. The relevant inquiry includes coercion and whether the practice is exploited to proselytize or denigrate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Legislative-prayer doctrine looks to historical practice and whether the practice proselytizes, denigrates, or coerces, not a per se ban on sectarian references. C: Historical practice does not authorize coercion, discrimination, or proselytizing by the government. D: Legislative prayer need not be scripted by government counsel; excessive control could create its own problems.
lb_rule_reference: Legislative prayer can be consistent with historical practice, but the government may not exploit it to proselytize, denigrate, or coerce participation.
