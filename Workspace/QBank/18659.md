Subtopic: Remedies
Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Ruth contracted to buy 1,000 identical tablets from Boaz for $50 each. Boaz wrongfully failed to deliver. Ruth immediately and in good faith bought reasonable substitute tablets for $58 each. Because the substitute seller was closer to Ruth, Ruth saved $1,000 in freight she would have owed under the original contract. Ruth also reasonably spent $2,000 arranging the substitute purchase. What is Ruth's proper expectation-damages recovery from Boaz?
Answer A: $8,000, the difference between the cover price and the contract price.
Answer B: $9,000, the cover-price difference plus incidental expenses less freight saved.
Answer C: $10,000, the cover-price difference plus incidental expenses, without subtracting freight saved.
Answer D: $58,000, the full cost of the substitute purchase.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The buyer's cover measure is the cost of reasonable cover minus the contract price, plus incidental or consequential damages, less expenses saved because of the seller's breach. The cover differential is $8,000 (($58 - $50) × 1,000). Adding $2,000 incidental expenses and subtracting $1,000 saved freight gives $9,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it omits recoverable incidental expenses and the saved-expense adjustment. C: C is wrong because it adds incidentals but fails to subtract expenses saved by the breach. D: D is wrong because cover damages are not the full substitute purchase price; the measure is the differential plus authorized additions and offsets.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-712; UCC §2-715
