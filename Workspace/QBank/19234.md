Question: In a federal insurance dispute, Miriam seeks to prove the exact wording of a policy exclusion. She calls Aaron, who says, “I read the policy last year, and it excluded roof damage.” The insurer objects and asks that the policy itself or a proper duplicate be produced. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because any witness may testify from memory about a document's exact legal terms.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because Miriam is trying to prove the content of a writing, so the original-writing rule requires the policy or an acceptable substitute.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the policy is circumstantial evidence only.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Aaron is not a lawyer.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When the proponent seeks to prove the contents of a writing, the original-writing rule requires the original or a permitted substitute. Aaron's memory of the policy's wording is not the proper proof channel absent an exception.
