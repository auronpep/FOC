Question: A recorded deed gave a utility company “a perpetual easement in gross to maintain a water line across David's parcel.” Sarah later bought David's parcel with record notice of the easement. Sarah argues that she is not burdened because the utility company owns no dominant parcel. What is the best answer?
Answer A: Sarah is not burdened because an easement without a dominant parcel is void.
Answer B: Sarah owns the water line because she bought the burdened parcel.
Answer C: Sarah is burdened by the recorded easement in gross even though the utility company owns no dominant parcel.
Answer D: Sarah is burdened only if the utility company proves horizontal privity with David.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement in gross can burden land even though it benefits a person or entity rather than a dominant parcel. The recorded utility easement gives notice to Sarah as servient successor, so lack of a dominant parcel is not a defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because in-gross easements do not require a dominant parcel. B: B is wrong because buying the servient land does not transfer ownership of the easement holder's utility right. D: D is wrong because horizontal privity is a covenant-burden issue, not the classification requirement for a recorded easement in gross.
lb_rule_reference: Easement in gross / successor servient owner
