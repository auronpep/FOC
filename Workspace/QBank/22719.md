


BARMATRIX Q#: 22719
Outline_code: 41030400
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Relations Between Federal and State Governments
Outline: Tenth Amendment and the Scope of State Authority
Question: After a national study found that State A ranked last in childhood health and welfare, the state enacted several new laws to encourage children to take up healthy outdoor activities. One of those laws made hunting and fishing licenses free for all State A children. Children from other states had to pay the same cost as adults, which was $250. A State B resident challenged the new hunting and fishing license law, arguing that it was unconstitutional. Should the court rule in the State B resident's favor?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the law violates the Privileges and Immunities Clause.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the law violates the Equal Protection Clause.
Answer C: C: No, because the law is within the police power of the state.
Answer D: D: No, because childhood health and welfare is a compelling state interest.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A state has an interest in protecting its natural resources, and it may regulate noncommercial recreational activities that use its natural resources without violating the Constitution. See Baldwin v. Montana Fish and Game Commission, 436 U.S. 371 (1977). Hunting and fishing licenses involve a recreational, noncommercial activity using State A's natural resources, so the law is constitutional. A is incorrect because privileges and immunities only apply to economic discrimination. B is incorrect because only rational basis review would apply since there is no suspect class being discriminated against. A law like this would easily pass rational basis review. D is incorrect because “compelling state interest” refers to strict scrutiny review, which would not apply here.
