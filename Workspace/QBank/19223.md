Question: A parts supplier sold components to David under a signed contract requiring payment within ten days of each invoice. For the first three months, the supplier accepted David's payments thirty days after invoice without objection. The supplier then sent David a clear notice stating that, beginning with the next shipment, strict ten-day payment would be required. David made the next payment thirty days after invoice. The supplier suspended further shipments. What is the supplier's best response to David's argument that the supplier waived the ten-day payment term forever?
Answer A: A waiver of a payment term can never be retracted once late payments have been accepted.
Answer B: A waiver affecting future performance may be retracted by reasonable notice unless retraction would be unjust because of a material reliance change.
Answer C: The parol evidence rule bars evidence of the accepted late payments because the written contract set a ten-day deadline.
Answer D: The supplier's notice retroactively makes the first three late payments breaches.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a waiver affecting an executory portion of a UCC contract may be retracted by reasonable notification unless retraction would be unjust in light of material reliance. The trigger facts are the accepted late payments, the clear prospective notice, and the next shipment/payment cycle. The decisive lock is waiver retraction: prior acceptance may waive strict timing for earlier payments, but it does not necessarily waive the term forever.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it makes waiver irrevocable in all circumstances. C: C is wrong because course performance after signing is not barred as parol evidence and may show waiver. D: D is wrong because retraction is prospective; it does not retroactively convert accepted late payments into breaches.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-209(5); UCC 1-303(f) / waiver retraction
