Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Malicious prosecution
Question: A creditor files a valid collection lawsuit against a debtor. After obtaining a subpoena in that case, the creditor uses the subpoena to demand unrelated trade secrets from the debtor's new employer, threatening to embarrass the debtor unless the debtor sells land to the creditor. The debtor sues for misuse of legal process. Which theory best fits these facts?
Answer A: Abuse of process, because valid process was used for an improper collateral purpose after issuance
Answer B: Malicious prosecution, because every aggressive litigation tactic is malicious prosecution
Answer C: Defamation, because subpoenas are always public statements of fact
Answer D: No tort is possible because the original collection lawsuit was valid
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The issue is misuse of process to pressure an unrelated land sale.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Malicious prosecution targets wrongful initiation and requires additional elements such as favorable termination. C: The wrong described is misuse of process, not reputational publication. D: Abuse of process can arise even when the process was validly issued.
lb_rule_reference: Abuse of process focuses on using legal process for an improper collateral purpose after it is issued; malicious prosecution focuses on wrongfully initiating proceedings without probable cause and with favorable termination.
