Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A landlord sued a tenant in federal court for unpaid rent under state contract law. The complaint alleges only breach of the lease. The tenant is expected to defend by arguing that a federal housing statute makes the lease provision unenforceable. The landlord asserts federal-question jurisdiction because the federal statute will likely be disputed. Does the federal court have Section 1331 jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because an anticipated federal defense creates federal-question jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because the tenant will probably cite federal law in the answer
Answer C: No, because the landlord's own claim is a state-law rent claim and the federal issue is only defensive
Answer D: No, but only because the amount in controversy is below $75,000
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff's affirmative claim is created by state contract law. The anticipated federal defense does not create Section 1331 jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Federal defenses do not appear on the well-pleaded complaint for Section 1331 purposes. B: The answer and defenses do not supply original federal-question jurisdiction. D: Amount in controversy belongs to diversity, not federal-question jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: Under the well-pleaded complaint rule, Section 1331 jurisdiction exists only when federal law creates the plaintiff's claim or the plaintiff's claim necessarily raises a substantial disputed federal issue.
