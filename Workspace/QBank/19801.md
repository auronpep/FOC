Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: Three siblings, all citizens of different states from the defendant trustee, sue in federal court to recover a single $120,000 trust distribution allegedly owed jointly to them as one undivided fund. No sibling has a separate fixed share unless the fund is first recovered. May the siblings aggregate the amount of the trust distribution?
Answer A: No, because multiple plaintiffs can never aggregate
Answer B: No, because each sibling must independently claim more than $75,000
Answer C: Yes, because they assert a common undivided right to a single $120,000 fund
Answer D: Yes, because all trust cases automatically arise under federal law
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The amount can be aggregated when plaintiffs enforce one common undivided interest.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A common undivided right is an exception to the ordinary no-aggregation rule. B: The claim concerns one undivided fund, not separate individual damages. D: Trust facts do not automatically create federal-question jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: Aggregation is allowed for a common undivided interest, such as a jointly owned fund or property right, but not for separate individual damages.
