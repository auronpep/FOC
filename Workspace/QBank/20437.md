Question: A state reimburses parents for bus fares used to transport children to accredited schools, public or private. Parents choose the school, and funds are paid according to the same distance formula regardless of religion. Some reimbursements go to parents whose children attend religious schools. Does the reimbursement program automatically violate the Establishment Clause?
Answer A: Yes, because any public benefit that helps families using religious schools is an establishment of religion
Answer B: No, because the program is neutral and aid reaches religious schools only through private parental choice
Answer C: Yes, unless the state excludes all religious schools from the reimbursement program
Answer D: No, but only if every religious school promises not to teach religion
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The benefit is available on equal terms and is directed by parents, not by state preference for religion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Neutral aid reaching religious schools through private parental choice is not automatically unconstitutional. C: The Establishment Clause does not require exclusion from neutral public benefits solely because a school is religious. D: The neutral transportation benefit is not a direct subsidy for religious instruction.
lb_rule_reference: Neutral public benefits that reach religious schools through private choice do not automatically violate the Establishment Clause.
