Outline: Opinion or reputation
Question: In a federal civil trial, Abigail testifies that she saw a collision. The defendant calls Noah to testify that Abigail has a reputation for being careless and hot-tempered. The defendant offers this testimony only to attack Abigail's credibility as a witness. Abigail objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because any negative character trait may be used to attack credibility.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because Rule 608(a) permits opinion or reputation evidence only about a witness's character for truthfulness or untruthfulness.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because careless people are less likely to observe accurately.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Abigail first testified that she is peaceful.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 608(a) allows reputation or opinion evidence to attack or support a witness's character for truthfulness, not general bad character or unrelated traits like carelessness or temper. The offered testimony is not character for untruthfulness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because witness-character impeachment under Rule 608(a) is limited to truthfulness or untruthfulness. C: C is wrong because the testimony attacks a character trait, not a specific perception defect tied to the event. D: D is wrong because the issue is the wrong trait, not whether Abigail opened the door to peacefulness.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(a) / wrong character trait
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests trait discipline: not every negative reputation is truthfulness impeachment.
