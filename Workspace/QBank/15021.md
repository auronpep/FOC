Question: A man owns Townacres in fee simple, and a woman owns the adjoining Greenacres in fee simple. The man has kept the lawns and trees on Townacres trimmed and neat. The woman "lets nature take its course" at Greenacres. The result on Greenacres is a tangle of underbrush, fallen trees, and standing trees that are in danger of losing limbs. Many of the trees on Greenacres are near Townacres. In the past, debris and large limbs have been blown from Greenacres onto Townacres. By local standards Greenacres is an eyesore that depresses market values of real property in the vicinity, but the condition of Greenacres violates no applicable laws or ordinances. The man demanded that the woman keep the trees near Townacres trimmed. The woman refused. The man brought an appropriate action against the woman to require her to abate what the man alleges to be a nuisance. In the lawsuit, the only issue is whether the condition of Greenacres constitutes a nuisance. The strongest argument that the man can present is that the condition of Greenacres
Answer A: A: has an adverse impact on real estate values.
Answer B: B: poses a danger to the occupants of Townacres.
Answer C: C: violates community aesthetic standards.
Answer D: D: cannot otherwise be challenged under any law or ordinance.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. The man's only claim would be under a private nuisance, arguing that the woman's property is creating a danger to others by being so overgrown. The man must show that a more serious interference is occurring as a result of the woman's actions, such as a threat to the safety of those on his land.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. An impact of property values would not interfere with the man's right to use his own land, and is not a valid basis for a private nuisance claim. C is incorrect. There is no covenant or equitable servitude requiring landowners to keep their property to a particular aesthetic standard mentioned in the fact pattern. A violation of aesthetic standards alone is not enough to establish a private nuisance claim. D is incorrect. A nuisance claim is not warranted by the mere absence of other available claims.
