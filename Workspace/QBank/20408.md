Question: Abraham owned Hillacre. Hillacre had an appurtenant driveway easement over Valleyacre, which Sarah owned. Abraham later bought Valleyacre in fee simple, so he owned both Hillacre and Valleyacre at the same time. Two years later, Abraham sold Valleyacre to Isaac. The deed to Isaac did not mention the driveway easement. After the sale to Isaac, may Abraham use the old driveway easement over Valleyacre?
Answer A: Yes, because an easement appurtenant can never be terminated by common ownership.
Answer B: No, because the easement terminated by merger when Abraham owned both the dominant and servient estates.
Answer C: Yes, because the easement automatically revived when Abraham sold Valleyacre to Isaac.
Answer D: No, but only because Isaac recorded his deed before Abraham recorded a new easement.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Abraham may not use the old easement. When the same person owns both the dominant estate and the servient estate in the same right, the easement terminates by merger. Once terminated, the easement does not automatically revive when the parcels are later separated; it must be recreated by grant, implication, prescription, or another valid method.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because unity of ownership can terminate an easement by merger. C: C is wrong because a terminated easement does not automatically revive after later severance. D: D is wrong because the issue is merger, not recording priority.
lb_rule_reference: Easement merger / unity of dominant and servient estates
