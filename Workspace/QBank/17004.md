Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Battery
Question: A phone maker ran an ad truthfully stating that a competitor's older model had a shorter battery life in independent tests. The competitor lost sales but cannot identify any false statement in the ad. Is the competitor likely to recover for injurious falsehood?
Answer A: Yes, because any comparative ad that causes lost sales is tortious
Answer B: Yes, because truth is not a defense to business torts
Answer C: No, because the ad was truthful and injurious falsehood requires a false disparaging statement
Answer D: No, because injurious falsehood applies only to statements about land
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The tort does not punish truthful comparison.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Lost sales alone do not replace falsity. B: Falsity is central to injurious falsehood. D: It can apply to products and business interests, but falsity is missing here.
lb_rule_reference: Injurious falsehood requires falsity; true or nonactionable comparative statements do not become tortious merely because they reduce sales.
