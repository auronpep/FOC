


BARMATRIX Q#: 19815
Outline_code: 61020304
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Res ipsa loquitur
Question: That evening, the woman was placing the bottle on the dinner table when the bottle exploded. The woman was cut by flying glass. In an action by the woman against the company, may she successfully rely on the doctrine of res ipsa loquitur?
Answer A: A: Yes, because it applies in exploding bottle cases.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the company was in exclusive control of the bottling process.
Answer C: C: No, because the bottles were knocked over by the officers.
Answer D: D: No, because the bottles were not in the company's possession at the time the woman's injury occurred.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An inference of negligence may be established under the doctrine of res ipsa loquitur when the accident was one that would not ordinarily have occurred without negligence, and the circumstances eliminate the probability that the negligence was anyone's but the defendant's. Bottles don't usually explode unless the company that was in exclusive control of the bottling process acted negligently, so res ipsa frequently is applied in exploding bottle cases. But where, as here, some other event was as likely a cause of the explosion, res ipsa cannot be applied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A and B are therefore incorrect. D is incorrect because the fact that the bottles were not in the defendant's possession when the accident occurred is not in itself sufficient to eliminate the probability that the negligence was anyone's other than the defendant's.
