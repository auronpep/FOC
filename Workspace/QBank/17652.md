Question: In a federal criminal assault trial, Isaiah testifies in his own defense. The prosecutor seeks to impeach Isaiah with a juvenile adjudication for making false statements to police. The prosecutor argues that an adult conviction for the same offense would be admissible under Rule 609(a)(2). Isaiah objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the adjudication because false statements are always admissible to impeach.
Answer B: Admit the adjudication if the court finds it necessary to fairly determine guilt or innocence.
Answer C: Exclude the adjudication because Rule 609(d) does not permit a juvenile adjudication to be used against the accused who testifies.
Answer D: Exclude the adjudication only if more than 10 years have passed since the adjudication.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 609(d) permits juvenile adjudications only when the adjudication is of a witness other than the defendant, along with other requirements. Isaiah is the accused, so his juvenile adjudication is not admissible under Rule 609(d).
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the juvenile-adjudication rule limits use even for false-statement offenses. B: B is wrong because the necessity condition does not overcome the defendant-witness exclusion. D: D is wrong because the bar here comes from Rule 609(d), not the 10-year rule.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(d) / juvenile adjudication of criminal defendant excluded
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests juvenile adjudication limits when the witness is the accused.
