Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Freedom of religion
Question: A city offers small grants to neighborhood groups that publish newsletters about community service. A church-run food pantry applies to publish a newsletter describing volunteer opportunities and explaining that service is part of its faith. The city denies the grant solely because the newsletter includes religious reasons for service. Secular groups with moral or political viewpoints receive grants. What is the pantry's strongest argument?
Answer A: The city may deny the grant because any public funds reaching religious expression violate the Establishment Clause
Answer B: The denial likely discriminates against a religious viewpoint in a neutral private-speech funding program
Answer C: The city may deny the grant because religious speech is a subject category, not a viewpoint
Answer D: The pantry wins only if the city intended to promote religion
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The program funds private newsletters on community service. Excluding the religious explanation while allowing secular viewpoints is viewpoint discrimination.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Neutral benefits for private speech do not automatically establish religion, and religious viewpoints cannot be singled out for exclusion. C: When the topic is community service, a religious reason for service is a viewpoint on an allowed subject. D: The claim is exclusion of private religious viewpoint, not government promotion of religion.
lb_rule_reference: When government creates a neutral program for private speech, excluding otherwise eligible speech because it expresses a religious viewpoint can violate the First Amendment.
