Outline: Appointment and removal
Question: Congress creates a board of administrative adjudicators within the Department of Energy. Each adjudicator can take testimony and issue initial decisions, but the Secretary of Energy may review any decision, reverse it, and remove an adjudicator for good cause. The statute authorizes the Secretary to appoint the adjudicators. A company argues that the adjudicators must be nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate. How should the court most likely rule on the appointment challenge?
Answer A: The appointments are unconstitutional because every officer of the United States requires Senate confirmation
Answer B: The appointments are likely valid because Congress may vest appointment of inferior officers in a department head
Answer C: The appointments are valid only if the President personally signs each appointment certificate
Answer D: The appointments are unconstitutional because administrative adjudicators can never be officers
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The adjudicators appear inferior because their decisions are reviewable by the Secretary and they are removable for cause by the Secretary. Congress may vest appointment of inferior officers in department heads.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Congress may allow department heads to appoint inferior officers. Senate confirmation is required for principal officers, not every officer. C: The Constitution expressly permits Congress to vest appointment of inferior officers in department heads. D: Agency adjudicators who exercise significant authority can be officers. The issue is the permissible appointment route.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may vest appointment of inferior officers in the President alone, courts of law, or department heads; principal officers require presidential nomination and Senate confirmation.
