Subtopic: Remedies
Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Abraham agreed to build a small guesthouse for Sarah for $90,000. Abraham reasonably expected to spend $72,000 on labor and materials. Before Abraham bought any materials or began work, Sarah repudiated the contract without excuse. Abraham had no other losses and was able to avoid all performance costs. What is Abraham's proper expectation recovery?
Answer A: $90,000, because Sarah repudiated the contract before performance.
Answer B: $72,000, because Abraham expected to spend that amount performing.
Answer C: $18,000, because Abraham recovers the profit he would have earned after subtracting costs saved.
Answer D: Nothing, because Abraham had not yet begun work when Sarah repudiated.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation. The goal is to put Abraham in the position he would have occupied had Sarah performed, without overcompensating him. Because Abraham avoided all $72,000 in costs, his loss is the $18,000 profit he would have earned on the $90,000 contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because awarding the full price would ignore the $72,000 in costs Abraham saved. B: B is wrong because expected cost is not the nonbreaching builder's loss. D: D is wrong because an owner's repudiation can cause lost profit even before the contractor begins work.
lb_rule_reference: Common law expectation damages; cost saved offset
