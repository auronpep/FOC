Question: In a federal contract trial, Leah calls Nathan, a former employee, expecting him to identify delivery logs. On direct examination, Nathan repeatedly evades simple questions and says, “I do not want to help Leah.” Leah asks to treat Nathan as hostile and to use leading questions. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the request because leading questions are never allowed on direct examination.
Answer B: Grant the request if the court finds leading questions are necessary to develop Nathan's testimony in light of his hostility.
Answer C: Deny the request unless Nathan is an adverse party.
Answer D: Grant the request only if Nathan has been convicted of a crime of dishonesty.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may allow leading questions on direct when necessary to develop a witness's testimony, including with a hostile or unwilling witness. Nathan's evasiveness and expressed unwillingness support allowing leading questions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is too absolute. C ignores that hostility in fact may justify leading even if the witness is not an adverse party.
