Question: Deborah hired Samuel to perform a certified safety review of a warehouse. The signed services agreement contained a merger clause and required Samuel to provide a “certified safety review,” but it did not list his license number. Before signing, Samuel stated that he held the required certification. He in fact knew that his certification had expired. Deborah seeks to introduce Samuel's statement and licensing records to rescind the agreement for fraud, not to add a new service term. Should the court exclude the evidence under the parol evidence rule?
Answer A: Yes, because the merger clause bars every pre-signing statement related to the transaction.
Answer B: Yes, because a party may use fraud evidence only after proving a breach of the written service term.
Answer C: No, because the evidence is offered to avoid the contract for fraud rather than to vary the writing's service obligations.
Answer D: No, but only because the Statute of Frauds does not apply to safety reviews.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that parol evidence does not bar evidence offered to prove fraud, mistake, duress, or another basis to avoid the agreement. The trigger facts are Samuel's pre-signing certification statement, his expired certification, and Deborah's stated purpose of rescission for fraud. The decisive lock is offered purpose. The evidence attacks enforceability; it is not offered to add or contradict a service duty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a merger clause does not automatically bar avoidance evidence. B: B is wrong because fraud avoidance is not dependent on first proving breach of the written term. D: D is wrong because the Statute of Frauds point is not the reason the evidence escapes the parol-evidence bar.
