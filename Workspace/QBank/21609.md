Outline: Nuisance
Question: A homeowner works nights and sleeps during the day. A neighbor operates a lawful woodworking shop in a detached garage from noon to 3 p.m. on weekends. The saw noise is audible but comparable to ordinary lawn equipment and does not disturb other nearby residents. The homeowner sues for private nuisance, alleging that the weekend noise prevents him from sleeping. Is the homeowner likely to prevail on private nuisance?
Answer A: Yes, because any activity that interferes with this homeowner's actual sleep schedule is a nuisance
Answer B: Yes, because a landowner's right to quiet enjoyment always defeats a neighbor's productive use of land
Answer C: No, because the noise is not shown to substantially interfere with an ordinary person's use and enjoyment of land
Answer D: No, because nuisance claims are limited to physical invasions of land
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A hypersensitive plaintiff does not convert ordinary neighborhood noise into private nuisance without objective substantial interference.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The interference must be substantial under an objective standard, not merely serious to a uniquely situated plaintiff. B: Nuisance balances competing land uses and requires substantial, unreasonable interference. D: Nuisance can involve intangible interferences such as noise, odor, or vibration.
lb_rule_reference: Private nuisance requires a substantial and unreasonable interference with use and enjoyment of land, judged by an ordinary-person standard rather than a uniquely sensitive plaintiff's reaction alone.
