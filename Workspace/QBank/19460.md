Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A plaintiff sues a manufacturer only for negligent product design after a scooter accident. The manufacturer shows that, when the scooter was designed, it used reasonable testing, followed available safety standards, and reasonably rejected a proposed change that would create greater risks. The plaintiff argues that proof of reasonable care is irrelevant because products cases are strict liability. How should the court treat the manufacturer's reasonable-care evidence?
Answer A: It is relevant because the pleaded theory is negligent product design
Answer B: It is always irrelevant in every products case
Answer C: It automatically defeats any strict-products claim even if the product was defective
Answer D: It matters only if the injured user had contractual privity with the manufacturer
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Theory matters. Reasonable-care evidence is central to negligence even though it would not be a complete answer to a properly framed strict-products defect claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: It may be irrelevant to strict liability fault, but not to negligence. C: Reasonable care is not necessarily a complete strict-liability defense. D: Privity is not the reason reasonable-care evidence matters.
lb_rule_reference: In a negligent-products claim, the defendant's reasonable care in design, testing, or manufacture is relevant; strict products liability does not require proving defendant carelessness.
