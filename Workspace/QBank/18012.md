Question: David owned Lot 1 and Lot 2. A water line serving the house on Lot 1 crossed beneath Lot 2. Before severance, the line had visible shutoff covers, meter boxes, and utility markers on Lot 2. David sold Lot 1 to Esther and later sold Lot 2 to Noah. The deed to Esther did not mention the water line. Esther claims an implied easement by prior use for the water line. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The claim fails because underground utilities can never be apparent.
Answer B: The claim may succeed because the visible covers, boxes, and markers made the continuous utility use reasonably discoverable at severance.
Answer C: The claim fails because water lines are profits rather than easements.
Answer D: The claim succeeds only if Lot 1 has no road access.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A use can be apparent when it is visible or reasonably discoverable on inspection. Although the water line was underground, the visible covers, boxes, and markers indicated its presence, and the line continuously served Lot 1 before severance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because underground utilities are not categorically nonapparent when visible indicators exist. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because a utility line is a use right, not a profit to remove resources. D: D is wrong because road access is not the test for a prior-use utility easement.
lb_rule_reference: Apparent prior use may include visible utility indicators
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is using an all-or-nothing rule for underground utilities instead of asking whether the use was reasonably discoverable.
