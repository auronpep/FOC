Question: Joshua granted Rachel “a right of ingress and egress over the gravel road to reach Rachel's house.” After a heavy snowstorm, Rachel hired Joseph to plow the gravel road at Rachel's expense so she could reach her house. Joseph used ordinary equipment, stayed within the road, and did not damage Joshua's land. Joshua objected that the deed did not expressly mention snow removal. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Joshua may bar all snow removal because maintenance must be stated separately from ingress and egress.
Answer B: Rachel may arrange reasonable snow removal within the road because it is incidental to enjoying the access easement.
Answer C: Rachel may pave and widen the road permanently because snow makes gravel inconvenient.
Answer D: Joshua may terminate the easement because weather temporarily prevented use.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement holder may make reasonable repairs or maintenance necessary to enjoy the easement, so long as the work stays within scope and does not unreasonably burden the servient estate. Ordinary snow removal within the road at Rachel's expense is incidental to ingress and egress.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reasonable maintenance may be incidental to access. B is correct because the work is limited, necessary, and nondamaging. C: C is wrong because maintenance does not authorize permanent widening or paving. D: D is wrong because temporary weather obstruction does not terminate the easement.
