Outline: Strict scrutiny (suspect) classifications
Question: A state legislature enacts a statute barring any person who was born in a specified foreign nation from serving as a licensed contractor working on state-funded infrastructure projects. The legislature's declared purpose is protecting state security from foreign espionage. A licensed contractor born in the specified nation, who has been a U.S. citizen for 15 years, challenges the statute. Under Equal Protection analysis, which standard of review applies to the national-origin-based contractor exclusion, and what must the government show?
Answer A: Intermediate scrutiny, because national origin is a quasi-suspect classification similar to sex
Answer B: Rational basis, because the state has a legitimate interest in infrastructure security and may draw reasonable lines about who performs sensitive work
Answer C: Strict scrutiny, because national origin is a suspect classification; the government must show a compelling interest and that the birth-nation exclusion is narrowly tailored to serve that interest — broad categorical exclusions based on birth nation are unlikely to survive
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies, but the statute automatically survives because national security is a compelling governmental interest that always justifies national-origin-based restrictions
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: National origin is a suspect classification triggering strict scrutiny. The government must demonstrate a compelling state interest and narrow tailoring. A blanket categorical exclusion of persons born in a specified nation — applied to U.S. citizens who have been naturalized for years — is unlikely to satisfy narrow tailoring because it does not distinguish between individuals who pose security risks and those who do not. Less restrictive means, such as background checks or security clearance requirements, would likely be available.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: National origin is a suspect classification, not a quasi-suspect classification. It triggers strict scrutiny, the same tier as race. The Supreme Court has consistently treated national origin as fully suspect, requiring the government to show a compelling interest and narrow tailoring. B: The classification is based on national origin — the nation in which the contractor was born — which is a suspect classification regardless of the state's stated purpose. Rational basis is the wrong standard. The government bears the burden under strict scrutiny to show a compelling interest and narrow tailoring. D: Even when national security is a compelling interest, the means must be narrowly tailored. A categorical exclusion of all persons born in a particular country is likely not narrowly tailored because it does not track actual security risk and sweeps in persons with no security-relevant ties to that country. Compelling interest is necessary but not sufficient.
lb_rule_reference: National origin is a suspect classification under the Equal Protection Clause. Laws that classify individuals by national origin are subject to strict scrutiny: the government must show a compelling interest served by narrowly tailored means.
