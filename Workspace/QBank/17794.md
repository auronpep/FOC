Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Commerce power
Question: A federal statute regulates interstate sale of nursery plants but says nothing about allowing states to favor local nurseries. A state then bans out-of-state nurseries from selling plants in the state. The state argues that because Congress regulated the field without forbidding the ban, Congress authorized it. Is the state correct?
Answer A: Yes, any federal regulation counts as authorization for state discrimination
Answer B: Yes, because states have unlimited power over plants
Answer C: No, federal silence is not clear authorization for the discriminatory state ban
Answer D: No, but only because corporations cannot sue under the DCC
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The state must point to a clear congressional authorization before the DCC limit is displaced.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Clear authorization is required; silence or ambiguity does not authorize protectionism. B: Local regulatory interest does not create congressional authorization or excuse discrimination. D: Corporations can assert DCC burdens; the defect is lack of clear authorization.
lb_rule_reference: Congressional authorization of state discrimination or burdens must be clear; mere federal silence or ambiguous regulation is not enough.
