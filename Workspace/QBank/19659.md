Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: Esther sues Jacob in federal court for injuries from a fall in Jacob's store. Esther has the burden to prove that a wet floor caused her fall. Jacob introduces testimony that Esther had fainted earlier that morning and may have fallen because she fainted again in the store. What is the effect of Jacob's testimony?
Answer A: It shifts the burden of persuasion to Esther to disprove fainting.
Answer B: It requires the court to direct a verdict for Jacob unless Esther produces a medical expert.
Answer C: It gives the jury an alternative-cause fact to consider, but Esther retains the burden of persuasion on causation.
Answer D: It is inadmissible because a defendant may not introduce evidence of an alternative cause.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Jacob's evidence may be relevant to causation, but it does not shift the burden of persuasion. Esther remains responsible for persuading the jury that the wet floor caused her injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because contrary evidence does not shift the ultimate burden of persuasion. B: B is wrong because Esther is not automatically required to produce a medical expert merely because Jacob offers another causal theory. D: D is wrong because alternative-cause evidence can be relevant.
