Outline: Expectation damages
Question: John contracted with Rachel to deliver design files for a bid package. Rachel breached, and John missed the bid deadline. John seeks the profit he says he would have earned from winning the project. The project owner had not promised John the job, five firms were bidding, and John offers no evidence of his likelihood of winning beyond his own statement that he was “sure” he would have been selected. What is the best measure of John's lost-profit recovery for the missed project?
Answer A: The full profit John hoped to make, because Rachel caused him to miss the bid.
Answer B: One-fifth of the expected profit, because five firms bid.
Answer C: The project owner's full contract price.
Answer D: No lost profits for the missed project, because John's chance of winning and profit amount are too speculative on these facts.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A lost opportunity can support damages only if causation and amount are proven with reasonable certainty. John has not shown with sufficient reliability that he would have won or what net profit he would have made.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because causation of the missed deadline does not prove the lost contract. B: B is wrong because dividing by the number of bidders is arbitrary, not reasonable certainty. C: C is wrong because the project owner's price is not John's net profit.
lb_rule_reference: Lost profits / speculative opportunity
