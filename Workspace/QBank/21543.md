Question: Two diverse plaintiffs sue one defendant in federal court after the same accident. Plaintiff One seeks $45,000 for vehicle damage. Plaintiff Two seeks $40,000 for separate personal injuries. Their claims are separate and neither plaintiff alone exceeds $75,000. Can the plaintiffs aggregate their separate claims to satisfy the amount requirement?
Answer A: Yes, because claims from the same accident are always aggregated
Answer B: Yes, because the total demand is $85,000
Answer C: No, because separate plaintiffs' separate claims usually cannot be aggregated
Answer D: No, because diversity cases can never involve multiple plaintiffs
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Each plaintiff has a separate claim below the threshold, and no common undivided right is present.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Same accident does not make separate plaintiffs' separate claims a common undivided interest. B: Amount aggregation depends on party posture, not just total arithmetic. D: Multiple plaintiffs are allowed if jurisdictional requirements are met.
lb_rule_reference: Multiple plaintiffs may not aggregate separate claims unless they enforce a common undivided interest.
