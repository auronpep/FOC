MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: At a party, Troy picked up a revolver, loaded one chamber with a round, and pressured his friend Miles to play Russian roulette by telling him anyone who refused was a "coward." Miles, wanting to impress the crowd, agreed. Troy handed Miles the revolver, Miles pulled the trigger, and the gun fired. Miles was killed instantly. Troy had orchestrated and pressured the game. What is the most serious homicide offense for which Troy can be convicted?
Answer A: No crime, because Miles voluntarily chose to participate and assumed the risk of death
Answer B: Involuntary manslaughter, because Troy did not pull the trigger and Miles made the final choice to fire
Answer C: Murder (depraved-heart), because Troy consciously disregarded an extreme and unjustifiable risk to Miles's life by engineering and pressuring the game
Answer D: Second-degree murder, because Troy intended to cause Miles extreme fear, which constitutes intent to cause serious bodily harm
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Depraved-heart murder requires a killing committed with malice in the form of conscious disregard of an extreme, unjustifiable risk to human life that manifests a depraved indifference to life. Russian roulette has an approximately 1-in-6 chance of instant death. Troy loaded the gun, organized the game, and used social coercion to induce Miles to participate. He consciously disregarded an extreme risk. Victim participation does not reduce criminal liability in homicide. Troy is guilty of murder.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Victim consent and voluntary assumption of risk are not defenses to criminal homicide. The criminal law does not permit one person to cause another's death simply because the victim agreed to play a lethal game. Moreover, Troy actively engineered the game and used social pressure to induce Miles's participation. Victim participation does not negate the defendant's culpability. B: Depraved-heart murder does not require that the defendant be the direct physical cause of death. Troy loaded the gun, organized the game, and pressured Miles into participating. He created an extreme and unjustifiable risk of death and acted with conscious disregard for Miles's life. This is paradigmatic depraved-heart malice, and the charge is murder, not manslaughter. D: The correct theory is depraved-heart murder (extreme recklessness), not intent-to-injure murder. Psychological fear is not grievous bodily harm. D reaches a murder conviction but through the wrong theory. The tested doctrine is depraved-heart murder based on conscious disregard of extreme risk.
lb_rule_reference: Voluntary participation by the victim in a dangerous activity does not negate depraved-heart murder liability; a defendant who forces or strongly induces another to engage in an act with an extreme risk of death can be guilty of murder when death results
