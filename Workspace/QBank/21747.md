Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Velos Automotive Inc. manufactures and sells the Velos Trident SUV in all 50 states, including the State of Corten. Velos sells thousands of Trident SUVs annually through Corten dealerships, runs Corten-targeted advertising, and maintains service agreements with Corten repair shops. The particular Velos Trident involved in the accident at issue in this lawsuit was manufactured in Ohio and first sold by an Ohio dealership to a buyer who later moved to Corten. The vehicle was then involved in an accident in Corten, allegedly caused by a defective steering mechanism. The Corten resident injured in the accident sued Velos Automotive in Corten federal court. Velos moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction, arguing that the specific vehicle involved in the accident was not sold in Corten and Velos therefore lacked the necessary forum contact for specific jurisdiction over this claim. How should the court rule on Velos's motion?
Answer A: Grant the motion, because the specific steering-defect claim must arise directly from a vehicle sold in Corten for specific jurisdiction to attach
Answer B: Grant the motion, because Velos's Corten contacts are general-jurisdiction contacts (ongoing sales across the state), not specific-jurisdiction contacts for this particular claim
Answer C: Deny the motion, because Velos extensively sells the Trident SUV in Corten, maintains Corten service relationships, and targets Corten consumers with advertising — the claim "relates to" these forum contacts even though this specific vehicle was not sold there
Answer D: Deny the motion, because any defendant with nationwide sales is subject to personal jurisdiction in every state for any product-liability claim
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Ford Motor Co.* directly controls. The Court held that when a manufacturer extensively sells, advertises, and services the same model in a state, and an injury claim involves that model used in that state, specific jurisdiction is proper even if the injury-causing vehicle was not sold there. Velos sells thousands of Tridents in Corten, runs Corten advertising, and maintains Corten service relationships. The steering-defect claim relates to Velos's purposeful Corten contacts. Both the long-arm and due-process prongs are satisfied; the motion should be denied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court* (2021) rejected the strict but-for causation requirement for specific jurisdiction. The Court held that Velos-equivalent facts — a manufacturer that extensively sells the same model in the forum but whose specific injury-causing vehicle was sold elsewhere — satisfy specific jurisdiction's "relate to" prong. Velos regularly sells Trident SUVs in Corten, advertises there, and maintains service relationships there. The claim relates to Corten because it involves the same type of product Velos purposefully sells into the forum. B: The general/specific PJ distinction turns on whether the claim relates to the forum contacts, not on how regular those contacts are. Under *Ford Motor Co.*, the fact that Velos regularly sells the same type of product in Corten for the same uses that generated this claim is precisely what makes specific jurisdiction appropriate. Systematic sales of the claim-related product type in the forum can satisfy specific jurisdiction even if the frequency of those contacts might otherwise look like general-jurisdiction territory. D: Ford Motor Co.* was a narrow ruling, not a declaration that nationwide sellers are subject to PJ everywhere for any claim. The Court emphasized the substantial and regular in-forum contacts of the specific type relevant to the claim. A manufacturer that sells a different product line in the forum but not the model at issue would not satisfy the "relate to" standard under the same analysis. Nationwide sales do not automatically create nationwide specific jurisdiction for all product-liability suits.
lb_rule_reference: Specific jurisdiction requires that the claim arise out of or relate to the defendant's forum contacts. After *Ford Motor Co.* (2021), "relate to" means a close enough relationship between the claim and the forum contacts — it does not require that the specific injury-causing product was sold in the forum, only that the defendant regularly sold the same type of product in the forum and the injury claim involves that type of product used in the forum.
