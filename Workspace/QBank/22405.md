


BARMATRIX Q#: 22405
Outline_code: 74020101
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: A man and his friend decided to burn the friend's house down to get the insurance money. The friend told the man that he would be out of town that Friday, and that the man could burn the house down then. However, on the agreed- upon Friday, the friend got drunk at a local bar and passed out in his house. The man set fire to the house as planned and accidentally killed the friend. If the man is charged with murder, what should the result be?
Answer A: A: Guilty, because the man set the fire.
Answer B: B: Guilty, because the man committed insurance fraud.
Answer C: C: Not guilty, because the man reasonably believed the friend was out of town.
Answer D: D: Not guilty, because the man set the fire as part of a fraudulent scheme with the friend.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the common law felony- murder rule, a person who kills another during the commission of a dangerous felony is guilty of murder. Here, arson is a dangerous felony, and the friend died during its commission, so the man is guilty of murder. B is incorrect because insurance fraud is not a dangerous felony. C and D are incorrect because they do not change the fact that the friend was killed during the man's commission of a dangerous felony.
