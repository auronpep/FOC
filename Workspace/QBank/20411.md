Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Taxing and spending power
Question: Congress requires certain businesses to pay a modest annual assessment if they do not submit voluntary energy-efficiency reports. The assessment is collected with federal tax returns, produces revenue, and carries no criminal sanction or scienter requirement. Congress calls it a "penalty." What is the best taxing-power analysis?
Answer A: The exaction cannot be a tax because Congress labeled it a penalty
Answer B: The exaction may be sustained as a tax because it is collected like a tax, is modest, raises revenue, and lacks punitive features
Answer C: The exaction cannot be a tax because all taxes influence behavior
Answer D: The exaction is valid only if it also regulates interstate commerce
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The payment functions like a tax despite the penalty label.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Function matters more than label in taxing-power analysis. C: Taxes often influence behavior; that alone does not invalidate them. D: Taxing power is an independent enumerated authority.
lb_rule_reference: A federal exaction may be sustained under the taxing power when it functions like a tax; statutory labels do not control.
