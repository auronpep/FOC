Outline_code: 32020100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: A man was injured while riding a rollercoaster at an amusement park. The man sued the park for negligence. During trial, the man sought to call the Chief Engineer of the State Amusements Commission. The man wanted the engineer to testify about an official report the engineer made after the accident. Specifically, the man wanted the engineer to read a quote from the rollercoaster operator who said the man was injured because the operator had forgotten to make necessary repairs. Is the report admissible?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the operator's statement was in a public record.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the operator's statement is a past recollection recorded.
Answer C: C: No, because there is no indication that the operator is unavailable.
Answer D: D: No, because the operator's statement is inadmissible hearsay.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 803(8), the record or statement of a public official is an exception to the hearsay rule. Since an employer is vicariously liable for the negligence of an employee committed within the scope of employment, statements tending to establish that the accident resulted from the employee's negligence are relevant in the plaintiff's action against the defendant. The evidence should thus be admitted unless excluded under the hearsay rule. Hearsay is an out- of- court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted. These facts raise what is sometimes called a multiple- level hearsay problem (i.e., a problem involving an out- of- court statement that contains another out- of- court statement). This is so because the operator's statement was not made during the negligence trial and so is an “out- of- court” statement, and because the evidence of his statement is contained in a report that was also not made as part of the negligence trial and so is an “out- of- court” statement. For multiple- level hearsay (i.e., the report containing the operator's statement) to be admissible, each level must be separately admissible. The first level of hearsay is the testimony by the operator. Under the common law, statements by an employee are admissible against the employer only if the employee had the authority to make them. However, FRE 801(d)(2)(D) requires only that the employee's statement concerned a matter within the scope of his or her employment and was made while the employment relationship existed. The operator's statement is therefore a vicarious admission, which is an exception to the hearsay rule at common law and is not hearsay at all under the FRE. The second level of hearsay is the report. Since it was made by a public official (the engineer), regarding “matters observed pursuant to duty imposed by law as to which matters there was a duty to report” (that the operator made the statement), the report qualifies as a public record or report under FRE 803(8).Here, the operator made a statement within the scope of his employment with the park, which is now being used against the park itself. The engineer took the operator's statement as part of his official report, which brings the statement under the public records exception. B is incorrect because a past recollection recorded is used to aid a witness who has forgotten the subject of his or her testimony. Here, the operator is not testifying as a witness, and there is no indication that he forgot his statement. C is incorrect because the public records exception does not require the declarant to be unavailable. D is incorrect because the statement qualifies under the public records exception.
