Outline: Conviction of crime
Question: In a federal criminal trial for theft, Ezra testifies in his own defense. The court properly admits Ezra's prior conviction for making a false statement to impeach his credibility. Ezra asks the court to instruct the jury that it may consider the conviction only in evaluating Ezra's credibility and not as evidence that he committed the charged theft. What should the court do?
Answer A: Refuse the instruction because once the conviction is admitted, the jury may use it for any purpose.
Answer B: Refuse the instruction because limiting instructions are available only in civil cases.
Answer C: Give the instruction because the conviction was admitted for impeachment, not to prove propensity or guilt of the charged offense.
Answer D: Give the instruction only if the prior conviction is more than ten years old.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When evidence is admitted for one purpose but not another, FRE 105 requires the court, on timely request, to restrict the evidence to its proper scope and instruct the jury accordingly. A conviction admitted under Rule 609 is used to evaluate credibility, not to prove that the defendant committed the charged crime.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because impeachment evidence does not become substantive propensity evidence merely because admitted. B: B is wrong because limiting instructions are not limited to civil cases. D: D is wrong because the need for a limiting instruction does not depend on the old-conviction rule.
