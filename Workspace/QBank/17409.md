Outline: Scope of discovery
Question: A plaintiff discloses her treating orthopedic doctor to testify about observations, diagnosis, and treatment made during the plaintiff's hospital care. The doctor was not hired to prepare litigation opinions. The defendant moves to exclude the doctor because no full retained-expert report was served. What is the best response?
Answer A: Exclude the doctor automatically because every expert witness must provide the same full written report
Answer B: Allow the testimony if the disclosure requirements for a non-retained treating expert were satisfied
Answer C: Exclude the doctor because treating physicians can never give opinion testimony
Answer D: Allow the testimony only if the defendant first deposes the doctor
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A treating physician may testify from treatment-based observations without being a retained expert required to serve a full report.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Retained experts and treating physicians are not subject to identical report requirements. C: Treating physicians may provide opinions formed during treatment if properly disclosed. D: A deposition may be available, but it is not the condition that determines whether the retained-expert report rule applies.
lb_rule_reference: A witness retained or specially employed to provide expert testimony must provide a written report, while a treating physician testifying from treatment may be disclosed without the same retained-expert report requirement.
