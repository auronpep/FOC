Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: A California manufacturer deliberately sold specialized equipment to dozens of Montana mines over five years, sent technicians to Montana twice, and maintained a Montana service hotline. A Montana mine worker was injured by that equipment and sued in Montana. The manufacturer argues that litigating in Montana is inconvenient because its records and executives are in California. How should the court treat the manufacturer's fairness argument?
Answer A: Dismiss because any inconvenience to the defendant defeats personal jurisdiction
Answer B: Dismiss because records in another state always control the fairness analysis
Answer C: Deny dismissal because strong Montana-directed equipment sales and service contacts make ordinary inconvenience insufficient
Answer D: Deny dismissal only if the manufacturer is incorporated in Montana
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim arises from a forum-served product line and deliberate Montana contacts. Ordinary litigation burden does not make jurisdiction unreasonable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Inconvenience alone rarely defeats jurisdiction when purposeful suit-related contacts are strong. B: They do not automatically overcome deliberate forum contacts; they may be venue or transfer facts too. D: Specific jurisdiction can exist without incorporation when the claim relates to purposeful forum contacts.
lb_rule_reference: Once minimum contacts and relatedness exist, the defendant must make a compelling fairness case based on burden, forum interest, plaintiff interest, efficient resolution, and shared policy interests.
