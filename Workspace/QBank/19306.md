Question: In a federal criminal case, the government orders Simon to stand in a lineup and wear a jacket recovered near the crime scene. Simon objects under the Fifth Amendment, arguing that appearing in the lineup and wearing the jacket may incriminate him. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because any compelled act that helps the prosecution is testimonial.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because displaying physical characteristics and wearing an item for identification is not compelled testimonial communication.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Simon first waives his right not to testify.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the jacket is independently authenticated by a written confession.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The Fifth Amendment protects against compelled testimonial communications, not compelled participation in physical identification procedures such as lineups or wearing clothing for comparison. The decisive axis is testimonial communication versus physical display.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This confuses incriminating effect with testimonial compulsion. C: C is wrong because No waiver is required because Simon is not being compelled to testify. D: D is wrong because Authentication of the jacket is a separate evidence issue and does not control the Fifth Amendment objection.
lb_rule_reference: Fifth Amendment / object production versus testimonial explanation
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating all compelled physical displays as compelled self-incrimination.
