Question: Joseph signed a writing stating, “I promise to convey my vineyard to Lydia next month.” He handed the writing to Lydia. The writing identified the vineyard and was signed by Joseph. Lydia argues that title passed immediately when she received the writing. Who owns the vineyard now?
Answer A: Joseph, because a promise to convey in the future is not itself a present deed of conveyance.
Answer B: Lydia, because the writing identified the land and was signed by Joseph.
Answer C: Lydia, because delivery of any signed land writing passes title.
Answer D: Lydia, but only if she records the writing before next month.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is operative words of present transfer. A promise to convey in the future may raise contract issues, but it is not itself a present deed transferring title. Title remains with Joseph unless and until a valid conveyance occurs.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because signature and land identification do not replace present conveyance language. C: C is wrong because delivery of a signed land writing is not enough if the writing is only a promise. D: D is wrong because recording cannot turn a future promise into a present deed.
