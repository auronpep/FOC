MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Battery
Question: During a crowded auction, a bidder became irritated when another bidder stood in front of him. To make the bidder move, he deliberately snapped a rolled catalog against the bidder's neck. The contact stung for a moment but left no mark. The bidder had not consented to the touching. The bidder sues for battery. Is the bidder likely to prevail?
Answer A: No, because the defendant did not intend to injure the bidder
Answer B: No, because a rolled catalog is not part of the defendant's body
Answer C: Yes, because the defendant intentionally caused offensive contact with the bidder's person
Answer D: Yes, but only if the bidder saw the catalog before it touched him
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The defendant deliberately snapped the catalog against the bidder's neck to move him. That is intentional offensive contact even without lasting injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Battery does not require intent to injure. Intent to cause harmful or offensive contact is enough. B: Battery can be committed through an object intentionally used to make offensive contact. D: Battery requires contact, not apprehension before contact. The completed offensive contact supports the claim.
lb_rule_reference: Battery intent is satisfied when the defendant intends harmful or offensive contact, even if the defendant does not intend physical injury.
