Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: “Necessary and Proper” Clause power
Question: Congress enacts a statute requiring every household to adopt a federally approved recycling schedule. The statute is justified only as "necessary and proper to solve a national problem." It is not tied to commerce, taxing, spending, federal property, or any other enumerated power. What is the strongest constitutional objection?
Answer A: Necessary and Proper is not freestanding; Congress must connect the law to an enumerated power
Answer B: The statute is valid because national usefulness is enough under Necessary and Proper
Answer C: The statute is valid because household schedules are never local
Answer D: The statute is invalid only if states have already enacted recycling laws
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The clause supplies means to execute other powers, not a general federal police power.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: National usefulness does not replace the enumerated-power requirement. C: Nationwide application is not the same as enumerated federal authority. D: The threshold issue is Congress's affirmative power.
lb_rule_reference: Necessary and Proper is not an independent source of federal police power; it must carry into execution another enumerated federal power.
