Question: Jacob and Esau owned Lot 1 as tenants in common. Lot 1 benefited from a recorded access easement over Mary's Lot 2. Without Esau's consent, Jacob signed a document purporting to “release the entire easement over Lot 2.” Mary then barred Esau from using the access route. What is Esau's best argument?
Answer A: Jacob's unilateral release automatically extinguished the entire easement because any co-owner may destroy all appurtenant rights.
Answer B: Jacob could not unilaterally release the entire easement as against Esau's co-ownership interest in the dominant estate.
Answer C: The easement became an easement in gross owned personally by Mary.
Answer D: The easement terminated because co-owned dominant land can never hold appurtenant easements.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A co-owner ordinarily cannot unilaterally destroy an appurtenant easement for the entire dominant estate to the prejudice of another co-owner. Jacob's act may affect Jacob's own position, but it does not automatically extinguish Esau's access right tied to his co-ownership interest.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because one co-owner generally cannot unilaterally release the whole easement for all co-owners. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because Mary owns the servient estate; the access right did not become her personal in-gross right. D: D is wrong because co-owned land may benefit from an appurtenant easement.
lb_rule_reference: Co-owner release limits / appurtenant easement
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is routing the question only through release doctrine and ignoring the burden/benefit ownership lock.
