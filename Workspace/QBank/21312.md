Question: Esther hired Benjamin's software company to host an inventory platform. The signed agreement stated that “service credits are available for missed uptime targets,” but it did not say whether credits were the exclusive remedy. After a long outage, Esther sued for lost profits. Benjamin argues that service credits were the sole remedy. Which additional fact most strengthens Benjamin's Content/Meaning argument?
Answer A: Esther lost several large sales during the outage.
Answer B: Benjamin's salesperson orally said during negotiations that the company “usually works things out with credits.”
Answer C: The signed agreement incorporated a service-level appendix that stated, “service credits are the sole and exclusive remedy for missed uptime targets.”
Answer D: Benjamin fixed the outage within two days after Esther complained.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a remedy dispute can turn first on the meaning and incorporated content of the contract. The trigger facts are an ambiguous service-credit clause and the question's request for the best fact supporting exclusivity. The decisive lock is meaning source. An incorporated service-level appendix that expressly states sole and exclusive remedy is the strongest content source. It defines the remedy term rather than merely proving harm, negotiations, or later cure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is tempting because it supports damages, but damages do not define whether the remedy was exclusive.
