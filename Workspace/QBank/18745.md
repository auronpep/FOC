Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Sarah sues Isaac after a stair collapse, claiming the accident caused chronic knee pain. Isaac offers Sarah's medical record from six months before the collapse showing treatment for knee pain. Sarah objects that the record does not prove her current pain has the same cause. Assuming the record is otherwise admissible, how should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the record because prior knee pain has some tendency to bear on causation and damages.
Answer B: Exclude the record because it does not conclusively prove the cause of Sarah's current pain.
Answer C: Exclude the record because only post-accident medical records can be relevant.
Answer D: Admit the record only if Isaac proves Sarah's current pain is identical to the earlier pain.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A prior condition involving the same body part can bear on causation and damages. It need not conclusively establish that the current pain has the same cause; the relationship and strength of the inference are for the factfinder.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because relevance does not require conclusive proof. C: C is wrong because pre-accident condition evidence can be highly probative. D: D is wrong because identical symptoms are not required for threshold relevance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Confusing relevance with conclusive medical causation.
