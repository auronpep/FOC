Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Vera and Omar agree to burglarize a warehouse. The next morning, before any overt act is required in the jurisdiction, Vera tells Omar she is backing out and goes home. Police arrest both that afternoon based on recorded conversations from the night before. Vera is charged with conspiracy to commit burglary. Is Vera's withdrawal a defense to the conspiracy charge?
Answer A: Yes, because she withdrew before any burglary occurred
Answer B: No, because the conspiracy was already complete when Vera agreed to the burglary plan
Answer C: Yes, because withdrawal is always a complete defense to every inchoate crime
Answer D: No, but only because Omar did not accept her withdrawal
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: In a jurisdiction that does not require an overt act, the conspiracy offense is complete at agreement. Withdrawal may affect future liability, but not the conspiracy already formed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Conspiracy is complete upon the agreement in this jurisdiction. The later withdrawal does not erase the completed agreement offense. C: Withdrawal rules vary by offense and consequence. It generally does not undo a completed conspiracy. D: Acceptance by a co-conspirator is not the reason. The completed conspiracy charge remains because the agreement already occurred.
lb_rule_reference: Withdrawal generally is not a defense to the conspiracy already completed by agreement; it may affect later substantive liability or the limitations period.
