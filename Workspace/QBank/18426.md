Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Caleb owned a cottage bordered by two adjoining owners, Joshua on the east and Daniel on the west. Caleb signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey my cottage to my neighbor.” The deed gave no other description of the grantee. Caleb privately meant Joshua, but he did not say so in the deed or otherwise create a selection method. Who owns the cottage?
Answer A: Joshua, because Caleb privately intended him to be the grantee.
Answer B: Caleb, because the deed does not identify an ascertainable grantee.
Answer C: Joshua and Daniel as equal tenants in common, because both were neighbors.
Answer D: Whichever neighbor records the deed first.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantee identity. A grantee must be named or reasonably ascertainable. Because “my neighbor” could refer to Joshua or Daniel and the deed provides no way to choose between them, Caleb's private intent cannot cure the uncertainty. Title remains with Caleb.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because private, unstated intent does not identify the grantee. C: C is wrong because the deed did not convey to both neighbors. D: D is wrong because recording cannot choose an otherwise unascertainable grantee.
lb_rule_reference: Grantee uncertainty / private intent cannot cure
