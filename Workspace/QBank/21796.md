Outline: Psychotherapist-patient
Question: Hannah met with Dr. Miriam, a licensed psychotherapist. Hannah brought her friend Naomi into the session for moral support even though Naomi was not needed for treatment or communication. With Naomi present, Hannah admitted hiding a weapon. In a federal case, Hannah claims psychotherapist-patient privilege over the statement as against Naomi's testimony. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because the statement was made in a therapist's office.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because friends are always necessary to therapy.
Answer C: Overrule the objection as to Naomi's testimony, because Hannah voluntarily disclosed the statement in the presence of an unnecessary third party.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Dr. Miriam also testifies.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The privilege depends on confidentiality. By speaking in the presence of an unnecessary friend, Hannah did not keep the communication confidential as to Naomi. The decisive axis is voluntary third-party disclosure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because The therapist's office is not enough without confidentiality. B: B is wrong because A third party may preserve privilege only when reasonably necessary; the facts say Naomi was not needed. D: D is wrong because The therapist's testimony is irrelevant to whether Naomi heard a nonconfidential disclosure.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / psychotherapist confidentiality
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is letting the treatment setting obscure the third-party confidentiality gate.
