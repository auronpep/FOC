Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Duress
Question: Harlan, the manager of a small pawn shop, suspected his employee Delia of stealing cash from the register. He called Delia into a back room and told her: "You are not leaving this room until you confess. If you try to walk out that door, I will call the police and have you arrested for felony theft — and your children will see you taken away in handcuffs." The door was unlocked. Delia, believing Harlan would carry out the threat, remained in the room for two hours before Harlan finally let her leave. Delia later denied stealing anything, and no charges were filed. Which of the following best describes whether Harlan committed false imprisonment of Delia?
Answer A: No, because the door was unlocked and Delia could have left at any time
Answer B: No, because Harlan's threat was to call the police — a lawful act — not to use physical force against Delia
Answer C: Yes, because Harlan confined Delia through a credible threat that caused her to reasonably believe she could not leave, satisfying the confinement element of false imprisonment
Answer D: Yes, but only if Harlan's underlying accusation of theft was false
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: False imprisonment at common law is the intentional, unlawful confinement of another within fixed boundaries with no reasonable means of escape. Confinement may be effected by physical barriers, actual force, or threats of force or immediate harm. Harlan told Delia she could not leave and backed the command with a credible, immediate threat. Delia stayed because she reasonably believed she had no lawful choice. Two hours of compelled presence constitutes confinement. That the door was physically unlocked does not negate confinement achieved by duress.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: False imprisonment does not require physical barriers. Confinement achieved through threats of immediate harm—including threats of arrest—can satisfy the confinement element if the victim reasonably believed the threat would be carried out and complied because of it. The unlocked door is irrelevant when the victim was prevented from using it by a credible threat. B: Even a threat of a technically lawful act can constitute the coercive force required for false imprisonment if it is used to compel the victim to remain against her will. The key question is whether the victim was unreasonably confined, not whether the threatened act was itself lawful. Confinement by moral pressure or threat of immediate harm (including threatened police involvement under coercive circumstances) can satisfy the element. D: The truth or falsity of the underlying accusation is irrelevant to whether false imprisonment occurred. Private citizens have no authority to detain employees for investigation the way law enforcement might (and even police must follow constitutional rules). Harlan had no legal privilege to confine Delia regardless of whether she stole. The shopkeeper's privilege, which requires reasonable grounds and limited duration and force, does not extend to employer-employee interrogations of this kind.
lb_rule_reference: False imprisonment can be accomplished by physical barriers, physical force, threats of force, or other duress; confinement by threat of immediate harm satisfies the confinement element even without physical restraint
