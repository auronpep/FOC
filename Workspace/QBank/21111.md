Outline: Conditions
Question: Ruth agreed to buy a warehouse from Samuel. The contract stated, “Buyer's duty to purchase is subject to the city issuing a storage permit by June 1.” The permit application required Ruth to submit a site plan. Ruth found a cheaper warehouse two weeks later and deliberately never submitted the plan. On June 2, Ruth told Samuel that the permit condition failed and that she had no duty to buy. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Ruth is excused because the permit was not issued by June 1.
Answer B: Ruth is excused unless Samuel proves that the city certainly would have issued the permit.
Answer C: Ruth cannot rely on the failed permit condition because she deliberately failed to make the good-faith efforts needed to satisfy it.
Answer D: Ruth can rely on the condition because express conditions are never affected by a party's conduct.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The permit was an express condition to Ruth's duty, but a party cannot avoid a duty by deliberately preventing or failing to pursue the condition on which that duty depends. Ruth's noncooperation excuses the condition and prevents her from using its nonoccurrence as an escape.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because literal nonoccurrence is not enough when the party seeking protection caused the nonoccurrence. B: B is wrong because Samuel need not prove certain approval when Ruth deliberately withheld the required application materials. D: D is wrong because express conditions remain subject to prevention, cooperation, and good-faith principles.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law express condition / good-faith efforts
