Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A homeowner's dog was known to chew shoes but had never bitten, lunged, or knocked anyone down. During a backyard gathering, the dog wagged its tail against a low table, spilling a drink on a guest's phone. The guest sues the homeowner for strict liability based on the dog's known shoe-chewing habit. Should the guest prevail?
Answer A: Yes, because any known bad habit by a domestic animal creates strict liability for all later harm
Answer B: Yes, because the dog caused property damage rather than personal injury
Answer C: No, because the spilled drink did not result from the dog's known shoe-chewing propensity
Answer D: No, because domestic-animal owners can never be liable for property damage
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Strict liability for a domestic animal is tied to the scope of the known dangerous tendency.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The known habit must be a dangerous propensity that caused the type of harm at issue. B: The property label does not supply the missing link to a known dangerous propensity. D: Domestic-animal liability can exist, but these facts do not show harm from a known dangerous propensity.
lb_rule_reference: Domestic-animal strict liability is limited to harm caused by the known dangerous propensity; unrelated ordinary animal behavior is not enough.
