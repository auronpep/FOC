Question: In a federal property-damage trial, Daniel sues for the cost of repairing his fence after a delivery truck hit it. Daniel seeks only repair costs. Daniel offers testimony that he has seven grandchildren who enjoy visiting his yard. The delivery company objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony because all background facts about Daniel's life are relevant.
Answer B: Admit the testimony because sympathy facts always help the jury understand damages.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony because the grandchildren evidence does not make the repair cost, liability, or any defense more or less probable.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony only if the delivery company admits it struck the fence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Because Daniel seeks only repair costs, the number of grandchildren who visit the yard does not make liability, repair amount, or any defense more or less probable. It is not a fact of consequence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B treat sympathy as relevance. D is wrong because even if liability is disputed, this fact does not logically bear on the disputed issues.
