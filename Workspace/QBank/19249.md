Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Mode and order of witnesses
Question: In a federal civil trial, Naomi is eighty-eight years old and becomes short of breath during testimony. The judge allows Naomi to testify in short segments with breaks, and directs counsel to avoid repeating questions Naomi has already answered. The defendant objects that the judge is helping Naomi's side by changing the normal rhythm of testimony. How should the objection be resolved?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because all witnesses must testify without breaks once examination begins.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because the court may reasonably control examination to make testimony effective and protect a witness from undue hardship.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Naomi is a party.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only because elderly witnesses are always presumed truthful.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may reasonably control the mode of examination to make the testimony effective and protect witnesses from harassment, undue embarrassment, or undue hardship. Allowing breaks and preventing needless repetition can be proper trial control.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because there is no absolute no-break rule. C: C is wrong because witness-protection control is not limited to parties. D: D is wrong because the ruling concerns mode of presentation, not a presumption of truthfulness.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / breaks for vulnerable witness
