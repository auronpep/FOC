Question: Suspecting that a defendant had slain his wife, police detectives persuaded one of the defendant's employees to remove a drinking glass from the defendant's office so that it could be used for fingerprint comparisons with a knife found near the body. The fingerprints matched. The prosecutor announced that he would present comparisons and evidence to the grand jury. The defendant's lawyer immediately filed a motion to suppress the evidence of the fingerprint comparisons to bar its consideration by the grand jury, contending that the evidence was illegally acquired. The motion should be
Answer A: A: granted, because, if there were no probable cause, the grand jury should not consider the evidence.
Answer B: B: granted, because the employee was acting as a police agent and his seizure of the glass without a warrant was unconstitutional.
Answer C: C: denied, because motions based on the exclusionary rule are premature in grand jury proceedings.
Answer D: D: denied, because the glass was removed from the defendant's possession by a private citizen and not a police officer.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. A motion to exclude illegally-obtained evidence is premature during grand jury proceedings and it should be denied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The motion is premature; the grand jury is allowed to consider almost all kinds of evidence (fingerprints in this case), even if there was no probable cause for obtaining it. B is incorrect. The motion is premature, and on that basis should be denied despite whether the employee's conduct was constitutional. D is incorrect. The motion should be denied not because of the Fourth Amendment violation, but because it is premature.
