Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Freedom of religion
Question: Public schools require all students to attend a literature unit that includes stories from many cultures. Parents object on religious grounds to one story but do not allege that students must affirm any belief, participate in worship, or violate a religious rule. The school does not target religion and applies the curriculum to all students. What is the best constitutional analysis?
Answer A: The parents automatically receive an opt-out because any religious objection vetoes public-school curriculum
Answer B: The school likely may apply the neutral curriculum because the facts show exposure, not compelled affirmation or religiously targeted coercion
Answer C: The school always wins because parental religious objections are irrelevant in public education
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies solely because the story mentions religion
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: On these facts, the curriculum is general and neutral, and the students are not forced to affirm or violate a religious practice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Religious objection alone does not automatically override a neutral curriculum absent coercion, targeting, or substantial burden. C: Some policies can burden Free Exercise or parental rights if they coerce religious violation or target religion. D: Mentioning religious material in a neutral curriculum is not the same as targeting or burdening religious exercise.
lb_rule_reference: Religious objection to public-school curriculum does not automatically create a constitutional opt-out; courts look for coercion, substantial burden, targeting, or forced affirmation.
