MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff files a state-court action asserting only state-law breach of contract. The defendant files an answer and a federal counterclaim under a federal statute that would independently establish federal-question jurisdiction. The defendant does not remove. The plaintiff, seeing the federal counterclaim, files a notice of removal to federal court, arguing that the defendant's federal counterclaim has made the action removable and that either party may remove when a federal question is in the case. The governing forum is initially state court; the issue is whether removal to federal court is proper under the MBE baseline. Is removal proper?
Answer A: Yes, because a federal counterclaim raises a federal question, and either party may remove once a federal question appears anywhere in the litigation
Answer B: No, because only defendants may remove under § 1441(a), and a plaintiff has no statutory right to remove
Answer C: Yes, because the action is now jointly removable: the plaintiff's original state-law claim and the defendant's federal counterclaim together establish subject matter jurisdiction that would not have existed at the time of original filing
Answer D: Yes, because the plaintiff's original filing in state court constitutes a waiver of venue objections, and either party may elect federal court jurisdiction after waiver
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1441(a) grants the right of removal exclusively to "the defendant or the defendants." A plaintiff who originally chose to file in state court has no right to remove even if the case subsequently develops federal dimensions. Additionally, even if a defendant sought removal, the federal counterclaim would not support it because the well-pleaded complaint rule requires the federal question to appear in the plaintiff's complaint. Neither problem is present from the plaintiff's perspective — the plaintiff cannot remove at all.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Two rules defeat this removal attempt. First, only defendants may remove under § 1441(a); plaintiffs have no statutory right of removal. Second, under the well-pleaded complaint rule, jurisdiction is assessed from the plaintiff's complaint — not from counterclaims. A federal counterclaim does not make the case removable even if the defendant filed it. C: There is no joint removability doctrine. Section 1441 grants removal rights to defendants, not plaintiffs. The presence of a federal counterclaim does not cure the plaintiff's lack of standing to remove, nor does it retroactively transform the action into one that was originally federal. D: Waiving a venue objection is entirely unrelated to removal rights. Waiver of venue objections does not grant either party the right to remove — and removal is not an election-by-either-party mechanism. Removal is a defendant's statutory right under § 1441, which cannot be exercised by plaintiffs under any theory.
lb_rule_reference: 28 U.S.C. § 1441(a) — any civil action filed in state court may be removed to federal district court by the defendant if the action could have been brought originally in federal court.
