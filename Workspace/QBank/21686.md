Question: After a workplace injury, the corporation's general counsel asked Andrew, a warehouse employee who saw the accident, to meet privately so counsel could give legal advice to the corporation. Andrew was told the interview was confidential and that counsel represented the corporation, not Andrew personally. Is Andrew's communication categorically outside corporate attorney-client privilege because he is not senior management?
Answer A: Yes, because only control-group executives can make privileged corporate communications.
Answer B: Yes, because eyewitness facts can never be communicated privilegedly to corporate counsel.
Answer C: No, because communications by employees to corporate counsel may be privileged when made confidentially for the purpose of obtaining legal advice for the corporation.
Answer D: No, but only if Andrew personally retained the corporation's counsel.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Corporate attorney-client privilege is not categorically limited to senior management. Employee communications to corporate counsel may be privileged when made at counsel's direction, within the employee's corporate role, confidentially, and for legal advice to the corporation. The decisive axis is legal-advice purpose and corporate representation, not job title alone.
