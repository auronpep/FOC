Question: A negligent truck driver crashed into a traffic signal at an empty intersection. A pedestrian standing safely behind a concrete barrier across the street saw the crash and became frightened, but the truck never came near the pedestrian. The pedestrian sues for standalone NIED in a zone-of-danger jurisdiction. What is the best result?
Answer A: The pedestrian recovers because any frightening accident supports NIED
Answer B: The pedestrian recovers because physical impact is not required
Answer C: The pedestrian does not recover because the pedestrian was outside the zone of danger
Answer D: The pedestrian does not recover because emotional distress is never recoverable in negligence
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The truck never endangered the plaintiff personally, so the zone-of-danger gate is not satisfied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Zone-of-danger recovery requires personal danger, not merely observing a frightening event. B: Lack of impact is not enough; the plaintiff also must be within the zone of personal danger. D: Some NIED claims are recognized; this one fails because the specific zone-of-danger limit is missing.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff outside the zone of personal danger generally cannot recover under a zone-of-danger NIED rule merely because the event was frightening.
