Outline: Judicial notice
Question: David is on trial in federal court for fraud. The prosecutor asks the judge to take judicial notice that David was convicted of forgery in a state case. Instead of requiring a certified record or other reliable source, the judge privately telephones a state-court clerk whom the judge knows personally. The clerk says, “I remember that conviction.” The judge immediately tells the jury that the conviction has been judicially noticed. Was this a proper use of judicial notice?
Answer A: Yes, because a judge may rely on any source the judge personally trusts.
Answer B: Yes, because criminal convictions are always automatically noticed.
Answer C: No, but only because judicial notice is unavailable in criminal cases.
Answer D: No, because the court relied on a private, informal source rather than a source whose accuracy could not reasonably be questioned and did not give a proper opportunity to be heard.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Judicial notice requires a fact not subject to reasonable dispute because it is generally known or can be accurately and readily determined from a reliable source. A private phone call to a remembered source is not the proper reliable-source route, and the parties are entitled to be heard on the propriety of notice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because personal trust in a private source is not the Rule 201 standard. B: B is wrong because even court-related facts require a proper basis and procedure. C: C is wrong because judicial notice can occur in criminal cases, but the jury instruction consequences differ.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 201(b), (e) / source quality and opportunity to be heard
