Outline: Spousal Privileges
Question: Moses is prosecuted in federal court for arson. The prosecution calls Miriam, Moses's current wife, to testify that she saw Moses buy gasoline the night before the fire. Miriam says she is willing to testify. Moses objects and argues that a defendant may prevent adverse testimony by a spouse. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because the defendant-spouse controls adverse spousal testimony.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because the adverse spousal testimonial privilege belongs to the witness-spouse, who may choose to testify.
Answer C: Sustain the objection, because observed conduct during marriage is a confidential marital communication.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Miriam and Moses were separated when she saw the gasoline purchase.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the federal common-law rule, the adverse spousal testimonial privilege is held by the witness-spouse. Miriam may refuse to testify adversely, but Moses cannot stop her from testifying if she is willing. The decisive axis is holder control, not marital status alone.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is holder inversion. Trammel vests the federal adverse testimonial privilege in the witness-spouse, not the defendant-spouse. C: C is wrong because This collapses the two spousal privileges. Buying gasoline is an observed act, not a confidential communication between spouses. D: D is wrong because Separation is not the operative fact here. The controlling fact is that Miriam is the holder and is willing to testify.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / Trammel witness-spouse holder
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is giving the accused spouse veto power instead of identifying the witness-spouse as the holder.
