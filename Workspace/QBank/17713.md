Question: Rachel, a textile designer, gave Paul the exclusive right to license her fabric patterns to clothing manufacturers for one year. The agreement gave Paul a commission on licenses he obtained but did not require a minimum number of licenses. Paul placed the pattern files in storage, made no calls, and later argued that he owed no duty because the writing did not use the phrase “best efforts.” Can Rachel recover for Paul's failure to pursue licenses?
Answer A: No, because an exclusive agency without a fixed quota is always illusory.
Answer B: Yes, if the court implies a reasonable or best-efforts obligation from the exclusive agency relationship.
Answer C: No, because Article 2 controls only if Paul was selling finished fabric goods.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Rachel proves a prior oral promise by Paul to contact manufacturers.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An exclusive agency or exclusive right under common law can carry an implied reasonable or best-efforts obligation, even though the exact UCC goods rule does not control. The trigger facts are the exclusive licensing right, Paul's commission structure, and his total failure to pursue licenses. The decisive lock is source law plus term status: this is not an Article 2 goods sale, but the exclusive agency term can supply effort content.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because exclusivity can support an implied effort obligation and is not automatically illusory. C: C is wrong because it states a source-law limitation but ignores the common-law route that supports Rachel. D: D is wrong because the effort obligation can arise from the written exclusive agency itself rather than a prior oral promise.
