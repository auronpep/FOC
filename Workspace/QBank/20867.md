Question: Peter is prosecuted in federal court for passing forged checks. Martha, Peter's wife, has been living apart from him for six months, but they are still legally married. Martha wants to testify that she watched Peter mail the checks. Peter objects under the adverse spousal testimonial privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection, because Martha is the witness-spouse and is willing to testify despite the current legal marriage.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because legal separation is enough for Peter to control Martha's testimony.
Answer C: Sustain the objection, because the privilege is stronger when the spouses are separated.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only because Martha observed the mailing rather than hearing a statement.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A current legal marriage exists, but the federal adverse spousal testimonial privilege is held by the witness-spouse. Martha may choose to testify; Peter cannot bar her testimony. The decisive axis is holder control, not whether the spouses are happily living together.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because This gives the defendant-spouse control and treats separation as a holder shift. Neither is the federal rule. C: C is wrong because The privilege does not become defendant-controlled because the marriage is strained. D: D is wrong because The observation/communication distinction matters for marital communications, but the decisive reason here is Martha's willingness as the holder.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / witness-spouse holder and current marriage
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is using marital harmony as a free-floating policy instead of applying holder and current-marriage rules.
