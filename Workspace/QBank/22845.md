


BARMATRIX Q#: 22845
Outline_code: 95070300
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Multiple Parties and Claims
Question: A woman died leaving an insurance policy worth $80,000. The policy stated that the proceeds should be paid to “my favorite nephew.” The woman had three nephews, and all three claimed the money. The insurance company was a citizen of State A, while one nephew was a citizen of State A, one nephew was a citizen of State B, and one nephew was a citizen of State C. The insurance company knew it had no rights to the money, so it wanted to bring an interpleader action in federal court to determine who owned the money. May the insurance company do so?
Answer A: A: No, because there was no federal subject matter jurisdiction over the claim.
Answer B: B: No, because the insurance company had no rights to the property at issue.
Answer C: C: Yes, because there was complete diversity between the nephews.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the insurance company had no rights to the property at issue.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 22 and 28 U.S.C. §1335, a person who is in possession of property claimed by more than one party (known as a “stakeholder”) can file an interpleader action to require the property claimants to litigate amongst themselves to determine who owns the property. In this way, the stakeholder can avoid liability. However, normal subject matter rules would apply. Here, the plaintiff would be the insurance company and the defendants would be the nephews. While there is over $75,000 in controversy, there is no federal question involved and the insurance company and one of the nephews are both citizens of State A. Consequently, the insurance company could not bring its interpleader action in federal court. B is incorrect because an interpleader action is specifically designed for situations where the person in possession of property has no rights to the property. C is incorrect because the question is whether there was diversity between the insurance company and the nephews, not merely the nephews among themselves. D is incorrect because of the lack of subject matter jurisdiction.
