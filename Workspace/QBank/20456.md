Question: Jacob is sued for negligently repairing a stair railing. To show he was careful when repairing the railing, Jacob offers evidence that he regularly donates grain to poor families in his village. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because generous people are less likely to be negligent.
Answer B: Admit the evidence because any good act is relevant to civil liability.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence because charitable giving does not make careful stair repair more or less probable.
Answer D: Exclude the evidence only if the plaintiff proves Jacob donated grain for selfish reasons.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The charitable giving does not have a logical connection to whether Jacob exercised reasonable care in repairing the railing. It is not merely weak; it is directed at no consequential fact through a valid inferential chain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because generosity is not a logical proxy for careful repair work. B: B is wrong because a good act is not automatically relevant to any civil dispute. D: D is wrong because the problem is lack of probative link, not Jacob's motive for giving.

lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats general moral praise as relevant to a specific conduct issue.
