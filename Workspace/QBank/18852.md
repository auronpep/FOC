Question: In a federal warehouse-injury case, Gideon denies that he controlled the forklift that injured Samuel. Samuel offers a monthly storage invoice addressed to Gideon for that forklift during the month of the accident. Gideon objects that the invoice does not prove he was driving the forklift. How should the court rule on relevance?
Answer A: Exclude the invoice because control can be proved only by eyewitness testimony.
Answer B: Exclude the invoice because it does not prove Gideon personally operated the forklift.
Answer C: Admit the invoice only if Gideon admits that the billing address was correct.
Answer D: Admit the invoice because it tends to make Gideon's ownership or control of the forklift more probable, even if other proof is needed to show operation.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The invoice tends to make ownership or control more probable, which is a fact of consequence if Gideon denies control. It need not prove that Gideon personally drove the forklift to be relevant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because control may be proved circumstantially. B wrongly focuses only on operation rather than ownership or control.
