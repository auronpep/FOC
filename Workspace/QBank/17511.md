Outline: Diversity Jurisdiction
Question: Spouse A, a citizen of State A, sues Spouse B, a citizen of State B, in federal court. Spouse A asks the federal court to grant a divorce decree, set child custody, and award alimony. The amount at stake exceeds $75,000. Should the federal court exercise diversity jurisdiction over the requested decree?
Answer A: Yes, because the spouses are citizens of different states and the amount exceeds $75,000
Answer B: Yes, because family-law disputes are state-law claims and can always be heard under diversity
Answer C: No, because the suit seeks divorce, alimony, and child-custody relief within the domestic relations exception
Answer D: No, but only because the spouses were once citizens of the same state
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal courts generally do not issue domestic relations decrees under diversity jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The domestic relations exception limits federal diversity jurisdiction over divorce, alimony, and custody decrees. B: Some state-law claims can be heard in diversity, but divorce and custody decrees fall within the domestic relations exception. D: The current facts state diverse citizenship. The obstacle is the domestic relations exception.
lb_rule_reference: The domestic relations exception generally bars federal diversity jurisdiction over suits seeking divorce, alimony, or child-custody decrees.
