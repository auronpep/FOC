Question: Leah granted a water company “a permanent easement to install and maintain one underground water line within the marked ten-foot strip across Leah's land.” Years later, the line leaked. The company gave Leah reasonable notice and proposed to enter the ten-foot strip for one day with small equipment to repair the leak. The work would not enlarge the line or move it outside the strip. Leah refuses entry, arguing that the easement mentions a water line but not workers or equipment. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Leah may exclude the company because maintenance must be separately stated in a second deed.
Answer B: The company may enter reasonably to maintain and repair the water line within the granted strip.
Answer C: The company may move the line anywhere on Leah's land because maintenance is needed.
Answer D: The company may take title to the ten-foot strip because an easement for utilities is equivalent to fee ownership.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A utility easement that expressly includes installation and maintenance carries reasonable incidental rights necessary to maintain the utility within the granted area. The proposed one-day entry within the strip to repair the leak fits the maintenance purpose and does not enlarge the burden.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reasonable maintenance access may be implied from the maintenance grant. B is correct because the repair is within the express utility purpose and location. C: C is wrong because maintenance does not allow relocation anywhere on the servient land. D: D is wrong because an easement is a use right, not fee title.
lb_rule_reference: Utility easement / reasonable maintenance access
