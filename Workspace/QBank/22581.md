


BARMATRIX Q#: 22581
Outline_code: 55020100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Contracts
Subtopic: Formation of Contracts
Outline: Offer and acceptance
Question: A homeowner told a contractor that the homeowner would pay the contractor $7,500 if the contractor replaced the homeowner's deck with a new one. The contractor replied, “Sounds good. Let me get stuff together.” The homeowner responded, “Whatever's cool. Just let me know if I have to hire someone else.” A week later, the contractor pulled into the homeowner's driveway and unloaded everything he needed to replace the deck. The homeowner arrived home just as the contractor was picking up his hammer and yelled, “Don't do anything! I'm hiring my son- in- law to do it!” The contractor responded, “But I bought wood and nails for the deck!” The contractor sued the homeowner for breach of contract. If the court rules in the contractor's favor, which of the following is its most likely reason for doing so?
Answer A: A: The contractor accepted a unilateral contract when he unloaded everything he needed at the house.
Answer B: B: The contractor accepted the offer when he bought wood and nails for the deck.
Answer C: C: The contractor accepted a bilateral contract when he said, “Sounds good.”
Answer D: D: The homeowner's offer became irrevocable when the contractor bought wood and nails for the deck.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A unilateral offer is irrevocable once the offeree begins performance. If the court rules in the contractor's favor, it is most likely because it finds that the contractor's act of unloading the things necessary for the job is the beginning of performance. B is incorrect because purchasing materials is likely to be seen as mere preparation, not acceptance by performance. C is incorrect because the contractor's statement “sounds good” is not a sufficiently clear statement to bind the contractor if the court finds that the parties intended to form a bilateral contract. D is incorrect because mere preparation for performance would not make the offer irrevocable.
