Question: Peter testifies as a witness in a federal civil trial. On cross-examination, counsel asks Peter whether he recently filed a false tax return, solely to impeach his credibility. A truthful answer could expose Peter to criminal tax charges. Peter invokes the Fifth Amendment. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Reject the invocation, because impeachment questions are never privileged.
Answer B: Reject the invocation, because credibility is always relevant.
Answer C: Allow the invocation, because the question calls for testimonial information that could incriminate Peter even though it is asked for impeachment.
Answer D: Allow the invocation only if Peter is a party rather than a witness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The Fifth Amendment can apply to any compelled testimonial answer that presents a real risk of incrimination. Impeachment purpose and relevance do not eliminate the privilege. The decisive axis is incriminating testimonial content, not the evidentiary use.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This creates an impeachment exception that does not exist. B: B is wrong because Relevance to credibility does not override self-incrimination privilege. D: D is wrong because Nonparty witnesses may also invoke the privilege when the answer could incriminate them.
lb_rule_reference: Fifth Amendment / impeachment does not defeat privilege
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating impeachment value as a privilege override.
