Question: Leah hired a caterer for a banquet under a signed services contract that listed the dinner menu and required signed written changes. After signing, two oral understandings occurred. First, Leah asked the caterer to add a breakfast station for an additional $2,000; the caterer hired extra staff, bought ingredients, and served the breakfast. Second, Leah asked the caterer to reduce the original dinner price by $1,000 because the event budget was tight; the caterer said “fine,” but there was no dispute, new consideration, or reliance tied to that reduction. After the banquet, Leah paid the reduced dinner price and refused to pay for the breakfast station. Which later oral understanding is most plausibly enforceable?
Answer A: Both the breakfast-station addition and the dinner-price reduction.
Answer B: The breakfast-station addition only.
Answer C: The dinner-price reduction only.
Answer D: Neither later understanding.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that later oral changes must be routed term by term. The breakfast-station request involved added service, extra staffing, purchased ingredients, performance, and Leah's acceptance at the event. That supports modification, waiver, or reliance despite the written-change clause. The dinner-price reduction is different: it was a gratuitous reduction of an existing price with no dispute, new consideration, performance route, or reliance. The decisive lock is split routing of later understandings.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it treats the performed extra service and unsupported price reduction as legally identical. C: C is wrong because the price reduction is the weaker understanding; it lacks the performance and reliance facts that support the breakfast station. D: D is wrong because the written-change clause does not automatically defeat the performed, accepted extra service.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law modification / split later understandings
