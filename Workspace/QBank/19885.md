Question: In a federal negligence trial, Martha testifies that she saw Rachel fall from a ladder and hold her head. Martha has no medical training. Rachel asks Martha, “Did Rachel suffer a concussion from the fall?” The defendant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because Martha saw Rachel fall.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because a lay witness may state any opinion based on personal knowledge.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if Rachel later calls a doctor.
Answer D: Sustain the objection because diagnosing a concussion requires medical or specialized knowledge.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Martha may describe what she observed, such as the fall, Rachel's conduct, pain, confusion, or visible injury. But diagnosing a concussion requires specialized medical knowledge within expert-testimony rules, so the lay-opinion question should be excluded.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B are wrong because seeing the fall supports factual observations, not a medical diagnosis.
