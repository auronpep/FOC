Outline: Joinder of parties
Question: In a federal diversity action, the court determines that an absentee is required under Rule 19(a), but joining the absentee would destroy subject-matter jurisdiction. The defendant moves to dismiss immediately. What should the court do next?
Answer A: Automatically dismiss because a required party can never be absent
Answer B: Automatically proceed because federal courts never dismiss for absent parties
Answer C: Decide whether, in equity and good conscience, the action should proceed among existing parties or be dismissed
Answer D: Ignore the absentee because subject-matter jurisdiction always overrides Rule 19
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 19(b) provides the next analysis when a required party cannot feasibly be joined.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: If joinder is not feasible, the court must apply Rule 19(b). B: Dismissal may be required if the Rule 19(b) factors show the case cannot proceed fairly. D: Jurisdiction explains why joinder is not feasible; it does not eliminate the Rule 19(b) decision.
lb_rule_reference: If a required party cannot be joined, the court decides under Rule 19(b) whether in equity and good conscience the action should proceed or be dismissed.
