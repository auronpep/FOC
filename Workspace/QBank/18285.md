Question: In a federal criminal trial, Michael is prosecuted for robbery. The prosecution's key eyewitness, James, testifies that Michael was the robber. Michael seeks to impeach James with a juvenile adjudication for perjury. The court finds that an adult conviction for the same offense would be admissible to attack credibility and that the evidence is necessary to fairly determine guilt or innocence. James objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the adjudication because juvenile adjudications are never admissible under Rule 609.
Answer B: Exclude the adjudication because James is not the criminal defendant.
Answer C: Admit the adjudication because it is offered in a criminal case against a witness other than the defendant, an adult conviction would be admissible, and admission is necessary to fairly determine guilt or innocence.
Answer D: Admit the adjudication in any civil or criminal case if it involved dishonesty.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 609(d) allows juvenile adjudication evidence only in narrow circumstances: it must be offered in a criminal case, involve a witness other than the defendant, be an offense for which an adult conviction would be admissible, and be necessary to fairly determine guilt or innocence. Those conditions are satisfied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 609(d) contains a narrow exception. B: B is wrong because the rule requires that the witness be someone other than the defendant. D: D is wrong because juvenile adjudications are not freely admissible in civil cases or whenever dishonesty appears.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(d) / juvenile adjudication
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the narrow juvenile-adjudication route and its criminal-case/nondefendant-witness limits.
