MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Battery
Question: At a banquet, a guest saw one attendee suddenly raise a glass bottle above another attendee's head while shouting. Reasonably believing an attack was about to occur, the guest pulled the bottle-holder backward by the shoulders. The bottle-holder had actually been starting a loud toast and was not attacking anyone. The bottle-holder sues the guest for battery. Can the guest defeat the bottle-holder's battery claim by relying on defense of others?
Answer A: No, because the bottle-holder was not actually attacking anyone
Answer B: Yes, because the guest reasonably believed another person faced imminent contact and used proportionate force
Answer C: No, because defense of others applies only to family members
Answer D: Yes, because any force is allowed when protecting another person
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The raised bottle and shouting reasonably appeared to threaten imminent contact, and pulling the person backward was proportionate nondeadly force.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Defense of others can apply when the defendant reasonably believes another person faces imminent contact, even if that belief is mistaken. C: The privilege is not limited to family members under the general rule. Reasonable belief and proportionality are the key limits. D: The force must still be reasonable and proportionate. The guest wins because the force was limited, not because all force is allowed.
lb_rule_reference: Defense of others permits reasonable proportionate force based on a reasonable belief that another person faces imminent harmful or offensive contact.
