Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff sues Defendant in State A court. Defendant believes State A lacks personal jurisdiction but removes the case to federal court on diversity grounds. In its first federal Rule 12 motion after removal, Defendant moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. Plaintiff argues that removal itself waived the objection. How should the court treat the waiver argument?
Answer A: Accept it because removing a case is always a general appearance that waives personal jurisdiction
Answer B: Reject it because Defendant raised personal jurisdiction in the first Rule 12 motion after removal
Answer C: Accept it because diversity jurisdiction makes all personal-jurisdiction objections irrelevant
Answer D: Reject it only if Defendant also proves that venue is improper
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The objection was preserved by asserting it in the first required Rule 12 response. Removal itself is not a waiver.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Removal alone does not waive a personal-jurisdiction objection if the defendant timely raises it in federal court. C: SMJ and PJ remain separate. Diversity does not cure lack of personal jurisdiction. D: The issue is waiver of a personal-jurisdiction defense, not whether venue is proper.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant does not waive a personal-jurisdiction objection merely by removing a case to federal court, but must still raise the objection in the required Rule 12 posture.
