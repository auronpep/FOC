Question: After Oliver is indicted for armed robbery, police deliberately question him without waiver about the same incident, calling it "simple theft" instead of robbery. Theft is a lesser-included offense of the charged robbery. Oliver makes incriminating statements. What is Oliver's best Sixth Amendment argument?
Answer A: Police avoided the Sixth Amendment because they used a different label for the questioning
Answer B: The questioning implicated the attached Sixth Amendment right because theft was a lesser-included offense of the charged robbery
Answer C: The statement is admissible because police did not ask about a weapon
Answer D: The statement is admissible because Sixth Amendment rights are never offense-specific
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The theft questioning concerned the same offense under the elements test. Deliberate elicitation without counsel or waiver violates the attached right.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Labels do not control. A lesser-included offense is the same offense for this purpose. C: A lesser-included offense remains covered because it is included within the charged offense. D: The right is offense-specific, but that specificity includes same offenses and lesser-included offenses.
lb_rule_reference: Offense-specific Sixth Amendment protection extends to charged offenses and offenses that are the same under the elements test, including lesser-included offenses.
