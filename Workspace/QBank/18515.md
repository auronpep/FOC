Outline: Marital Communications Privilege
Question: Before his death, Solomon privately told his wife Deborah during their marriage that he had hidden a signed release in a safe. After Solomon dies, a federal civil action involving his estate seeks Deborah's testimony about the statement. The estate asserts marital communications privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection, because a privilege always ends when the speaker dies.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because only criminal defendants can assert marital communications privilege.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if Deborah is unwilling to testify.
Answer D: Sustain the objection, because confidential marital communications may remain protected after the communicating spouse's death.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The marital communications privilege can survive the death of the communicating spouse. The requested testimony repeats Solomon's confidential statement to Deborah during marriage. The decisive axis is privilege survival, not witness willingness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is the death-ends-privilege trap. Survival rules prevent privileged confidences from automatically evaporating. B: B is wrong because Marital communications privilege is not limited to criminal defendants. C: C is wrong because Deborah's willingness is not decisive where the communication privilege is validly asserted.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / survival of marital communications privilege
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating death or witness willingness as automatically destroying the communication privilege.
