Question: In a federal fraud trial, the prosecutor offers a relevant email from Deborah to prove that she knew invoices were false. The email also contains one unrelated sentence calling Deborah's assistant “a thief and a drunk.” Deborah objects under Rule 403. What is the most appropriate response?
Answer A: Admit the email in redacted form or with an appropriate limiting instruction if that addresses the improper-prejudice risk while preserving the probative portion.
Answer B: Exclude the entire email because any unfairly prejudicial sentence contaminates the whole exhibit.
Answer C: Admit the entire email because relevant evidence cannot be redacted.
Answer D: Exclude the email only if the assistant testifies at trial.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 403 balancing can be managed through redaction or limiting instructions when they preserve probative evidence while reducing unfair prejudice or misuse. The court need not choose only between admitting everything and excluding everything.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because partial redaction may cure the prejudice while retaining probative value. C: C is wrong because relevant exhibits may be limited to their proper scope. D: D is wrong because the assistant's testimony is not the condition for redacting unfairly prejudicial material.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403; FRE 105
lb_student_diagnostic: Student misses intermediate controls under 403/105.
