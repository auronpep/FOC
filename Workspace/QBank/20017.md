Question: Solomon writes to a supplier, “For the next 30 days, if you sell tools on credit to Noah, I will guarantee payment up to $15,000.” The supplier receives the letter and sells Noah $5,000 of tools on credit on day 10 and $6,000 on day 20. Noah does not pay. What is the best formation analysis?
Answer A: No guaranty formed because the supplier never promised Solomon that it would sell tools to Noah.
Answer B: No guaranty formed because every credit sale required a separate written notice before delivery.
Answer C: No guaranty formed because Solomon did not receive any direct benefit from the sales.
Answer D: The supplier accepted the unilateral guaranty offer by extending credit within the stated period, at least as to the credit actually extended within the limit.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Solomon invited acceptance by performance—selling tools to Noah on credit during the stated period. The supplier performed by extending credit within 30 days and within the guaranteed amount.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a return promise was not required when the offer invited credit extension. B: B is wrong because the offer did not impose a separate notice condition. C: C is wrong because the requested extension of credit is the bargained-for acceptance, even if Solomon did not receive the tools.
lb_rule_reference: surety/common_law
lb_student_diagnostic: Student demanded a bilateral promise or direct benefit in a performance-accepted guaranty.
