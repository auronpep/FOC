Outline: Liquidated damages
Question: Deborah contracted with John to install a cooling system for a records facility by June 1 for $90,000. The contract required John to pay $900 per day for late completion. When the contract was made, the parties reasonably expected that delayed access, staff rescheduling, and disruption to stored records would be difficult to measure, and $900 per day was a reasonable estimate. John completed the work 10 days late. Deborah proved $5,000 in actual extra staffing costs. What is Deborah's best recovery for the delay?
Answer A: $5,000, because actual damages replace a liquidated amount once actual loss can be proved.
Answer B: Nothing, because Deborah did not prove lost revenue from the late completion.
Answer C: $14,000, because Deborah may add the $5,000 actual cost to the $9,000 stipulated delay amount.
Answer D: $9,000, because the reasonable $900-per-day clause applies to the 10-day delay.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under common law, measured by an enforceable delay liquidated-damages clause. The breach posture is delayed performance, and the loss category is stipulated delay loss. The penalty gate is satisfied because the delay losses were difficult to measure at contracting and the per-day amount was reasonable. The formula is $900 per day × 10 days late = $9,000. The result scope is limited to the stipulated delay recovery; Deborah does not stack the same actual delay costs on top of it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it treats later actual damages as automatically replacing a valid liquidated amount, a WAL-REM-015 penalty shortcut. B: B is wrong because hard-to-prove delay loss can be liquidated without proof of lost revenue. C: C is wrong because it stacks actual delay costs with the liquidated measure for the same loss, triggering WAL-REM-008.
lb_rule_reference: Common law construction delay liquidated damages — reasonable per-day estimate for hard-to-measure delay loss is enforceable
