MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: Priya intended to shoot and kill her former supervisor, Dana, as Dana walked down a hallway. Priya fired at Dana but missed; the bullet struck a bystander, Wallace, who was walking in the hallway and died instantly. Priya had no quarrel with Wallace and did not know he was present. Priya was apprehended at the scene. Is Priya guilty of murder for Wallace's death?
Answer A: No, because Priya did not intend to kill Wallace and lacked any malice toward him
Answer B: No, because the shot that killed Wallace was a miss — an accident — rather than an intentional act directed at him
Answer C: Yes, because under the doctrine of transferred intent, Priya's intent to kill Dana transfers to Wallace, making her guilty of murder
Answer D: Yes, but only for involuntary manslaughter, because the killing of Wallace was accidental
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The doctrine of transferred intent provides that when a defendant acts with intent to kill Person A but kills Person B instead, the intent is transferred to the unintended victim. This doctrine prevents defendants from escaping murder liability simply because they missed their intended target and hit someone else. Priya had premeditated intent to kill Dana. That intent transfers to Wallace. She is guilty of murder (and, depending on whether premeditation attaches, potentially first-degree murder) for Wallace's death.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The doctrine of transferred intent holds that when a defendant intends to kill one person and accidentally kills another, the intent to kill transfers to the unintended victim. Priya had the intent to kill (directed at Dana); that intent transfers to Wallace for purposes of murder liability. Absence of personal animosity toward the specific victim killed is not a defense. B: The "accidental" nature of Wallace's death describes how the bullet traveled, not the defendant's intent. Priya intentionally fired a deadly weapon with intent to kill a human being. The transferable intent was present and active at the moment of firing. That it traveled to the wrong target does not eliminate the intentional nature of the act or the murder charge. D: Involuntary manslaughter covers unintentional killings resulting from criminal negligence or an unlawful act — not killings resulting from a deliberate act to kill a human being. Priya acted with intent to kill; transferred intent elevates this to murder, not manslaughter. The fact that the intended victim survived does not reduce the charge against Priya for the victim who died.
lb_rule_reference: Under the doctrine of transferred intent, when a defendant intends to kill Person A but accidentally kills Person B, the intent to kill transfers to the unintended victim; the defendant is guilty of murder of the unintended victim
