MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Foreseeable plaintiffs
Question: A counselor met with a client who said, "Tomorrow morning I am going to hit my former supervisor at the bus stop outside the mill." The counselor knew the supervisor's name and workplace. The counselor did not warn the supervisor or notify anyone. The next morning, the client attacked the supervisor at that bus stop. The supervisor sues the counselor for negligence. Is the supervisor likely to establish a duty in the negligence claim against the counselor?
Answer A: No, because a person is never liable for harm physically caused by another adult
Answer B: Yes, because the counselor had a relationship and specific knowledge of an identifiable, preventable threat
Answer C: Yes, because any violent statement by a client creates a duty to warn the public at large
Answer D: No, because the counselor did not have physical custody of the client
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The threat named the victim, time, and place, and the counselor had a professional relationship with the dangerous actor. Those facts can create a duty to warn or otherwise act reasonably.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The rule has exceptions. A counseling relationship plus a specific threat to an identifiable victim can create a duty to take reasonable protective steps. C: The duty is supported by the specificity and identifiability of the threatened victim, not by every vague violent statement or a duty to the whole public. D: Physical custody is not the only possible control link. A special relationship and specific warning information may support a reasonable warning duty.
lb_rule_reference: A duty to warn or protect may arise when a special relationship gives the defendant knowledge of a specific, foreseeable, and preventable threat to an identifiable victim.
