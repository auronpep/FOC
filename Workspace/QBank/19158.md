Outline: Payment of medical expenses
Question: After a patron fell on a wet floor, the restaurant owner said, “I forgot to put out the warning sign, and I will pay your emergency-room bill.” At trial, the patron offers both parts of the statement to prove negligence. No settlement demand or release was discussed. Which ruling is most accurate under the Federal Rules?
Answer A: Exclude both parts because any statement accompanying a medical-payment offer is protected.
Answer B: Admit both parts because the owner's promise to pay is an opposing-party statement.
Answer C: Exclude the promise to pay medical expenses to prove liability, but do not exclude the separate fault statement under Rule 409.
Answer D: Exclude the fault statement but admit the promise to pay medical expenses.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 409 bars offers or promises to pay medical expenses when offered to prove liability, but it does not bar accompanying factual admissions merely because they accompany a medical-payment offer. No Rule 408 settlement trigger appears. The decisive axis is statement splitting within the policy container.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This imports Rule 408-style statement protection into Rule 409. B: B is wrong because Opposing-party statement status does not make the medical-payment promise admissible for liability. D: A is wrong because This imports Rule 408-style statement protection into Rule 409. B is wrong because Opposing-party statement status does not make the medical-payment promise admissible for liability. C is wrong because Correct answer D is wrong because This reverses the split. The promise to pay is barred for liability; the separate fault statement is not barred by Rule 409.
