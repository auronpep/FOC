Question: A customer fell on a store's entry ramp during a rainstorm. The next day, the store installed additional traction strips. The store admits the strips were feasible before the fall but denies that reasonable care required them under the prior conditions. For breach, what is the best use of the later installation?
Answer A: It conclusively proves the store breached its duty before the fall
Answer B: It may be relevant only to feasibility if feasibility is genuinely disputed, but it is not a substitute for the breach analysis
Answer C: It is always irrelevant to every issue because it happened after the accident
Answer D: It proves the customer was contributorily negligent because the store changed the ramp after the fall
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The central question remains whether reasonable care required the precaution before the injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A later repair does not automatically establish that the earlier conduct was unreasonable. C: Later action can matter for limited purposes in some settings. D: The later installation does not establish the customer's lack of care.
lb_rule_reference: Later repairs may show feasibility when disputed, but they are not themselves proof that the earlier condition breached the duty of reasonable care.
