Outline: Roles of judge and jury
Question: In a federal contract trial, Abraham asks his witness Isaac, “Tell the jury everything that happened in the two years before the contract was signed.” Rebecca objects that the question calls for a long narrative. Abraham says the witness should be free to tell the story in his own way. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because a witness may always answer in narrative form on direct examination.
Answer B: Sustain the objection and require Abraham to ask more focused questions if the broad narrative would make the proof inefficient or confusing.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if the answer would contain hearsay.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because the jury, not the judge, controls the form of direct examination.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The judge may control the mode of examination to make the presentation effective and avoid wasting time or confusion. A broad narrative question can be narrowed even if some narrative testimony might otherwise be allowed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because narrative form is not an absolute right. C: C is wrong because the problem is form and control, not only hearsay. D: D is wrong because the court controls the mode and order of examining witnesses.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / narrative control
