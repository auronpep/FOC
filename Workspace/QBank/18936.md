Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Attorney-client
Question: Esther meets privately with lawyer Nathan to discuss a civil fraud claim. Esther brings Mary, a close friend, into the meeting because Esther is nervous and wants moral support. Mary is not an interpreter, paralegal, accountant, or other person needed for Nathan to give legal advice. Later, the opposing party subpoenas Mary to testify about what Esther said to Nathan at the meeting. How should the court rule on Esther's attorney-client privilege objection to Mary's testimony?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because Esther trusted Mary and expected Mary not to repeat the conversation.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because a communication made in a lawyer's office is privileged whenever the lawyer is present.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Esther voluntarily disclosed the communication in the presence of an unnecessary third party, so confidentiality was not preserved for that communication.
Answer D: Sustain the objection if Mary's testimony would be especially damaging, because need and prejudice determine whether confidentiality is waived.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications made for legal advice. Confidentiality is not preserved merely because the third party is trusted or emotionally helpful. Mary had no role needed to obtain legal advice, so Esther's voluntary disclosure in Mary's presence prevents or waives confidentiality for that communication. The decisive axis is necessary-agent status, not subjective trust or evidentiary importance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because subjective trust is not enough. The law distinguishes necessary agents from unnecessary third parties; a trusted friend can still destroy confidentiality. B: B is wrong because lawyer presence is not a privilege shortcut. The communication must be confidential, and unnecessary third-party presence defeats that requirement. D: D is wrong because waiver does not turn on whether the testimony is damaging. Relevance, prejudice, or litigation need does not recreate a privileged channel after unnecessary disclosure.
lb_rule_reference: Voluntary unnecessary disclosure waives attorney-client confidentiality
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats personal trust as equivalent to legal confidentiality.
