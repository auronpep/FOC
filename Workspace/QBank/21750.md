Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: A federal prosecutor offers testimony that Ezra received a call from an unknown number. The caller said, “I am the one you are looking for,” and asked where the meeting would be. Ezra had never spoken with Gideon, did not recognize the voice, and the caller gave no details known uniquely to Gideon. The prosecutor claims Gideon made the call. Gideon objects for lack of authentication. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because any call related to the case is relevant.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because the jury may always speculate about the caller's identity.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only because the call was not recorded.
Answer D: Sustain the objection because there is insufficient evidence to support a finding that Gideon was the caller.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Relevance depends on connecting the call to Gideon. The facts provide no assigned-number evidence, voice recognition, distinctive knowledge, or other identifying circumstances. The foundation is insufficient.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevance requires a connection to the consequential person or event. B: B is wrong because authentication requires evidence sufficient to support a finding, not speculation. C: C is wrong because the absence of a recording is not the reason; the problem is lack of identifying evidence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(5)-(6)
lb_student_diagnostic: Relevance-overbreadth trap: student treats case-related content as enough without identifying the speaker.
