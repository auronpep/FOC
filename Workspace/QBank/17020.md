Question: Congress enacts a statute prohibiting military personnel from being used to search private warehouses inside the United States unless Congress has declared war or enacted a specific domestic-security authorization. Without such authorization, the President orders military units to search privately owned warehouses for cyber equipment allegedly linked to foreign sabotage. The warehouse owners seek an injunction. What is the best constitutional analysis?
Answer A: The order is valid because Commander in Chief authority always overrides domestic statutory limits during national-security investigations
Answer B: The order is likely invalid because Congress has forbidden the domestic use of military searches absent conditions not present here
Answer C: The order is valid because foreign sabotage makes the issue a political question
Answer D: The order is valid only if the military finds actual contraband during the searches
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Congress specifically limited domestic military searches and the statutory conditions are not met. The President's national-security rationale does not automatically overcome Congress's contrary domestic rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Commander in Chief authority does not automatically authorize domestic searches contrary to Congress's statutory limits. The President is acting at the lowest ebb under Youngstown. C: Courts can decide whether executive action conflicts with a statute. The presence of foreign-affairs facts does not automatically make the dispute nonjusticiable. D: Constitutional authority is assessed when the President acts, not by whether later evidence is found.
lb_rule_reference: Commander in Chief authority is strongest in military command, but it does not automatically authorize the President to make domestic law contrary to Congress.
