Outline: Incorporation of Bill of Rights through the Fourteenth Amendment
Question: Congress finds a pattern of unconstitutional state discrimination against a protected class and enacts a limited damages remedy against state agencies for closely related violations. A state argues Congress can never create remedies against states under the Fourteenth Amendment. What is the best response?
Answer A: Congress may enact congruent and proportional remedies to enforce Fourteenth Amendment rights against state action
Answer B: Congress may freely redefine what the Fourteenth Amendment means
Answer C: Congress may use Section 5 only against private conduct with no state involvement
Answer D: Congress lacks all power because states are never subject to federal remedies
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 5 gives Congress enforcement power when the remedy targets constitutional violations and is properly tailored.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Enforcement power does not include redefining the substantive right. C: Section 5 enforces Fourteenth Amendment limits on state action. D: Proper Section 5 legislation can support certain remedies against states.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may enforce Fourteenth Amendment rights against state action through congruent and proportional remedies, but it may not redefine the substantive meaning of those rights.
