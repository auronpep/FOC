Outline: Presumptions and inferences
Question: In a federal civil action, Ruth claims that Boaz received a written cancellation notice. Ruth offers proof that her clerk addressed the envelope to Boaz, placed proper postage on it, and mailed it. Boaz testifies that he never received the notice. At the close of evidence, Ruth asks the court to instruct the jury that it must find Boaz received the notice. What instruction is proper?
Answer A: The jury must find receipt because proof of proper mailing creates a conclusive presumption.
Answer B: The jury may infer receipt from the mailing evidence, but it is not required to do so and Ruth retains the burden of persuasion.
Answer C: The jury must disregard the mailing evidence because Boaz denied receipt.
Answer D: The court should admit the proof only for impeachment and give no instruction about receipt.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: In a federal civil case, a presumption generally shifts the burden of production but not the burden of persuasion. Once contrary evidence is introduced, the jury may still infer receipt from the mailing proof, but it is not compelled to find receipt.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the presumption is not conclusive. C: C is wrong because Boaz's denial creates a fact issue; it does not erase the mailing evidence. D: D is wrong because the mailing evidence is substantive evidence on receipt, not merely impeachment.
