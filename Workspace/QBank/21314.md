Question: Moses told Ruth, “You may cross my pasture path to reach the river as a neighborly favor.” Ruth used the path openly for 15 years. She always closed the gate as Moses requested. The jurisdiction's prescriptive period is 10 years. Moses later revoked permission and blocked the path. Does Ruth have a prescriptive easement?
Answer A: Yes, because any open use lasting longer than the prescriptive period is adverse as a matter of law.
Answer B: No, because Ruth's use began and continued with Moses's permission, defeating adversity.
Answer C: Yes, because closing the gate showed Ruth controlled the path exclusively.
Answer D: No, but only because river-access easements are invalid unless created by deed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Prescription requires adverse use. Ruth's use was expressly permissive from the start and complied with Moses's condition. Long duration does not convert permissive use into adverse use unless Ruth clearly repudiates the permission.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because duration and openness do not replace adversity. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because closing a gate as requested confirms permission rather than hostile control. D: D is wrong because river access may be the subject of an easement; the defect is permission.
lb_rule_reference: Permission defeats adversity
lb_student_diagnostic: The repair signal is failure to search for permission before counting years.
