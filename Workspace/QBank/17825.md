Question: Ruth signed and delivered a deed stating, “For ten thousand dollars paid, I convey my field to Boaz.” Boaz accepted the deed, but no money was ever paid. Ruth then asserted that the deed was void because the consideration recital was inaccurate. Who owns the field?
Answer A: Ruth, because the deed was void as soon as the stated consideration was not actually paid.
Answer B: Ruth, because Boaz did not sign the deed promising to pay the price.
Answer C: Boaz, because nonpayment of consideration does not defeat an otherwise valid delivered deed.
Answer D: Boaz only if he records the deed before Ruth sues.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is not the truth of the consideration recital. Ruth signed and delivered a deed with present transfer words, and Boaz accepted. Nonpayment may create a separate claim, but it does not undo the title-transfer lock.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an inaccurate or unpaid consideration recital does not automatically void a delivered deed. B: B is wrong because the grantee's signature is not required to receive title by deed. D: D is wrong because recording cannot be used as the event that makes the deed valid between Ruth and Boaz.
lb_rule_reference: False or unpaid consideration recital does not void delivered deed
