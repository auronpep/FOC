Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Mode and order of witnesses
Question: In a federal negligence trial, Rachel plans to prove liability first and damages second. Her damages witness, Elijah, must leave the courthouse at noon for surgery. Rachel asks to call Elijah first, before any liability witness. Jacob objects that a plaintiff must prove liability before damages. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a party must always present evidence in the logical order of the elements of the claim.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Jacob consents to the change in witness order.
Answer C: Overrule the objection if calling Elijah first is a reasonable way to manage the trial without unfair prejudice.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because a party has an absolute right to call witnesses in any order.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court has broad authority to exercise reasonable control over the order of presenting evidence. Allowing a witness with a genuine availability problem to testify out of the usual order is within that discretion if it does not unfairly prejudice the opposing party.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the rules do not require a rigid element-by-element order of proof. B: B is wrong because the judge's trial-control authority does not depend on opposing counsel's consent. D: D is wrong because the party's preferred order is not absolute; the judge controls order and presentation reasonably.
