


BARMATRIX Q#: 22786
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A man and woman sued a restaurant in State A federal court as joint plaintiffs under Rule 20. The man sued the restaurant for $10,000 in damages under the federal Safe Food Act after he was hospitalized for accidentally eating a fried rat. The woman sued the restaurant for $20,000, claiming negligent infliction of emotional distress under state law because she was sitting at the next table when the man ate the rat and she was now so traumatized that she could no longer sleep. The man was a citizen of State A, the woman was a citizen of State B, and the restaurant was incorporated in State A and had its principal place of business in State C. The restaurant moved to dismiss the woman's claim for lack of jurisdiction. Should the court grant the motion?
Answer A: A: No, because the man's claim and the woman's claim arose from the same transaction or occurrence.
Answer B: B: No, because the woman's claim arose when the restaurant violated the federal Safe Food Act.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the woman is only claiming $20,000 in damages.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the woman's claim is based on a state claim of negligent infliction of emotional distress.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under 28 U.S.C. §1367, if a court has proper jurisdiction over one claim, it has supplemental jurisdiction over any claim arising from the same transaction or occurrence. Here, the federal court has jurisdiction over the man's claim because he is suing under a federal act. While the woman is bringing a state law claim, it arises from the same instance of the man accidentally eating the rat. Thus, the federal court has supplemental jurisdiction over the woman's claim and the court should not dismiss it. B is incorrect because the woman is filing a state law claim, even if her injury was caused by the restaurant potentially violating a federal claim. C and D are incorrect because the federal court would have supplemental jurisdiction over the woman's claim even if it did not meet federal court requirements on its own.
