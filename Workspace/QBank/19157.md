Question: After an explosion, Sarah's lawyer obtained a recorded statement from eyewitness John in anticipation of litigation. John died before the plaintiff could interview him. The recording is the only substantial equivalent of John's account. The lawyer's separate cover memo contains mental impressions about John's credibility. What should the court do?
Answer A: Compel both the recording and the lawyer's cover memo because John is unavailable.
Answer B: Deny all discovery because work product can never be discovered.
Answer C: Order production of the ordinary witness statement if substantial need and undue hardship are shown, but protect the lawyer's mental impressions.
Answer D: Order production only if Sarah first waives attorney-client privilege.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Ordinary work product may be discovered on a showing of substantial need and inability to obtain the substantial equivalent without undue hardship. Even when production is ordered, the court must protect mental impressions, conclusions, opinions, and legal theories. The decisive axis is ordinary versus opinion work product.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This underprotects opinion work product by treating the cover memo like the witness recording. B: B is wrong because This overprotects ordinary work product and ignores the substantial-need route. D: A is wrong because This underprotects opinion work product by treating the cover memo like the witness recording. B is wrong because This overprotects ordinary work product and ignores the substantial-need route. C is wrong because Correct answer D is wrong because Attorney-client waiver is not the required route for ordinary work-product discovery.
lb_rule_reference: FRCP 26(b)(3)(A)-(B) / substantial need and opinion protection
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is not splitting ordinary factual work product from opinion work product.
