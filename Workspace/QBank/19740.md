Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Rebecca sues Reuben after a ladder collapsed. Rebecca offers testimony from Hannah that, an hour before the accident, Hannah said in Reuben's presence, “This ladder has a cracked side rail.” Reuben denies hearing the statement. Hannah testifies that Reuben was standing beside her and looking at the ladder when she spoke. Reuben objects that the statement is irrelevant unless he heard it. What should the court do?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because Reuben denies hearing the warning.
Answer B: Admit the testimony only if the judge conclusively finds that Reuben heard the warning.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony because the warning is hearsay for the truth that the ladder was cracked.
Answer D: Admit the testimony if there is sufficient evidence for a reasonable jury to find that Reuben heard it, with the jury deciding that condition.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The warning is relevant to notice only if Reuben heard it. Hannah's testimony provides enough support for a jury to find that condition. The judge screens for sufficient support; the jury decides whether the condition exists.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Reuben's denial creates a jury issue if sufficient contrary evidence supports hearing. B: B is wrong because the judge need not conclusively decide the conditional fact. C: C is wrong because the statement is offered to show notice, not to prove the ladder actually was cracked.
