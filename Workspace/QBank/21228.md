Question: Voters sue their state in federal court, alleging that the state constitution's initiative process gives too much power to direct democracy and therefore deprives the state of a republican form of government. They rely only on the Guarantee Clause and ask the court to invalidate the initiative system. What is the strongest basis for dismissal?
Answer A: The claim is likely nonjusticiable because it rests on the Guarantee Clause and lacks judicially manageable standards
Answer B: The claim must be heard because any challenge to election structure is automatically justiciable
Answer C: The claim fails only because voters never have standing in election cases
Answer D: The claim is justiciable because political controversy itself creates a federal case
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Guarantee Clause claims about republican government structure are generally treated as political questions committed outside ordinary judicial review.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Some election-related claims are justiciable, but a claim resting solely on the Guarantee Clause is generally nonjusticiable. C: The key defect here is political question under the Guarantee Clause, not a categorical ban on voter standing. D: Political controversy does not supply manageable legal standards or overcome textual commitment concerns.
lb_rule_reference: Claims resting solely on the Guarantee Clause's republican-form-of-government requirement are generally nonjusticiable political questions.
