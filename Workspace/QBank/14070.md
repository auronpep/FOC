


BARMATRIX Q#: 14070
Outline_code: 91090200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jury Trials
Outline: Demand for Jury Trial
Question: A farmer injured by a machine sued the machine's manufacturer on his own behalf and as a representative of a class of individuals injured by the same model machine during the past five years. The farmer alleged that the machine was defectively designed and manufactured because the machine failed to shut off when the key was removed from the ignition. Therefore, the machine subjected its users to a risk of bodily injury. The suit was brought in federal court and satisfied the requirements for diversity jurisdiction. The farmer sought compensatory damages both for himself and for the members of the class. He timely filed a request for a jury trial on his own behalf and on behalf of the class. Historically, class actions are devices available exclusively in equity. The farmer requested that the court "certify" the action to proceed as a class action. In response, the machine manufacturer moved to strike the farmer's jury demand. Is the farmer entitled to a jury trial?
Answer A: A: Yes, on both the issues of the class certification and the underlying merits of the action against the manufacturer.
Answer B: B: Yes, but only on the underlying merits of the action against the manufacturer.
Answer C: C: Yes, but only on the issue of class certification.
Answer D: D: No, on neither the class certification issue nor the underlying merits of the action.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. The farmer's lawsuit against the manufacturer for defective design and manufacture, in which the farmer seeks monetary damages, is an action of law. Since the requirements for class action derive exclusively from equity, the issue regarding whether the requirements for class certification have been met is reserved for a judge and not a jury. Therefore, in this case, a jury could only determine the factual issues bearing on the merits of the action.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The claim underlying the farmer's class action is an action of law that a jury may determine. However, the requirements for class action certification derive from equity. As a result, the farmer's request that the action proceeds as a class action is an issue to be determined by a judge and not a jury. Therefore, the court cannot grant a jury trial for both issues. C is incorrect. In this case, the requirements from class action certification derive exclusively from equity. Therefore, the court cannot award a jury trial to determine whether the requirements for class certification are met. D is incorrect. As explained above, the jury should hear the merits of the claim, but not the class certification issue.
