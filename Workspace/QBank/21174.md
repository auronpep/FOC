Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Isaac signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey my orchard to Rebekah.” Unknown to Isaac, Rebekah had died the month before delivery. Rebekah's will left all of her property to Rachel. Rachel claims the orchard under Rebekah's will. Who owns the orchard?
Answer A: Rachel, because Rebekah's will transferred all property described in instruments naming Rebekah.
Answer B: Rebekah's estate, because the deed was delivered to a named grantee.
Answer C: Rachel, because Isaac intended a gift and consideration was unnecessary.
Answer D: Isaac, because no title vested in Rebekah when the deed was delivered to a dead grantee.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is a living grantee capable of taking title. A deed cannot vest title in a person who is already dead at delivery. Because Rebekah took nothing, nothing passed through her will to Rachel, and title remains with Isaac.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a will cannot transfer title that never vested in the decedent. B: B is wrong because naming a dead person is not enough to vest title in an estate. C: C is wrong because the no-consideration rule does not cure the absence of a living grantee.
lb_rule_reference: Living grantee / title cannot vest in dead person
