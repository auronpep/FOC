MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers conducted an illegal warrantless search of a suspect's apartment and discovered a victim's body hidden in a storage closet. Independent of the illegal search, a massive grid search involving 200 volunteers was already systematically covering the exact same area of the city and was estimated to reach the apartment building within the hour. The suspect moves to suppress evidence relating to the discovery of the body. Should the court suppress the evidence of the body's discovery?
Answer A: Yes, because the body was discovered as a direct result of the illegal search, making it an inadmissible fruit of the poisonous tree
Answer B: Yes, because inevitable discovery applies only to tangible physical evidence, not to testimonial or location-based evidence
Answer C: No, because the government can show by a preponderance of the evidence that the ongoing volunteer grid search would have inevitably discovered the body through lawful means
Answer D: No, because the good-faith exception to the exclusionary rule saves the evidence since the officers believed they were conducting a lawful search
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Nix v. Williams established the inevitable discovery exception: evidence discovered through an unconstitutional search is admissible if the government demonstrates by a preponderance of evidence that the evidence would have been discovered inevitably through independent, lawful investigative activity. The ongoing grid search of 200 volunteers, systematically covering the area and minutes away, satisfies this standard. The government does not need to show the lawful search had actually reached the evidence — only that it would have done so with certainty. Evidence of the body and its location is admissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The inevitable discovery exception (Nix v. Williams) allows admission of evidence that was the fruit of an illegal search if the government proves by a preponderance that the evidence would have been discovered through independent lawful means regardless of the violation. The ongoing volunteer grid search was lawfully organized and would have reached the apartment imminently. B: Nix v. Williams itself involved the discovery of a murder victim's body — location-based evidence. The Supreme Court imposed no categorical limit on the type of evidence eligible for the inevitable discovery exception. It applies to any evidence that would have been found through independent lawful investigation. D: The Leon good-faith exception applies when officers reasonably rely on a facially valid warrant later found defective. It does not apply to warrantless searches where officers simply believed (incorrectly) that their search was lawful. The correct exception here is inevitable discovery, not good faith.
lb_rule_reference: Evidence discovered as a result of an unconstitutional search is not suppressible if the government can demonstrate by a preponderance of the evidence that the evidence would inevitably have been discovered through independent, lawful investigative procedures (Nix v. Williams)
