Stem / Fact Pattern: A state statute prohibits the killing of any animal “in a manner which causes unnecessary pain or suffering of said animal.” The defendant is prosecuted for violating the statute by strangling a chicken as part of a religious ritual in which he participated. The defendant defends on the ground that the state statute as applied in his case unconstitutionally interferes with his free exercise of religion. Which of the following may the court NOT consider in determining the constitutionality of the statute?
Answer A: A: Whether the statute is necessary to protect a compelling state interest.
Answer B: B: Whether the religious belief that requires the strangling of a chicken is reasonable.
Answer C: C: Whether the religious ritual involving the strangling of chickens has been practiced for a long period of time.
Answer D: D: Whether the defendant is sincere in the religious belief that requires the strangling of a chicken.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Basic to freedom of religion is the rule that a court may not inquire into the truth or reasonableness of a particular religious belief. A is incorrect because in free exercise cases, the state’s pursuit of a compelling state interest would certainly be sufficient (though not necessary) to lead to the upholding of the statute. In Wisconsin v. Yoder, 406 U.S. 205 (1972), the United States Supreme Court examined the sincerity of a claimant’s religious beliefs in determining whether a state statute as applied violated the Free Exercise Clause. In doing so, the court referred to the fact that the belief involved was of longstanding tradition. C and D are therefore incorrect.
