Question: A small lender hired an accountant to prepare a financial report for one stated purpose: helping the lender decide whether to make a loan to a named borrower. The accountant carelessly overstated the borrower's inventory. The lender relied on the report, made the loan, and lost money when the borrower defaulted. No physical injury or property damage occurred. The lender sues the accountant for negligent misrepresentation. Is the lender likely barred from recovering economic loss in its negligent misrepresentation claim against the accountant?
Answer A: Yes, because pure economic loss is never recoverable in any tort
Answer B: Yes, because the lender must prove physical damage to its own property
Answer C: No, because the accountant supplied information for this lender's limited and foreseeable reliance
Answer D: No, because every foreseeable user of a financial report may recover
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The accountant prepared the report for the named lender and the specific loan decision. That special information-supply relationship can support recovery for economic loss in negligent misrepresentation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Pure economic loss may be recoverable when a professional supplies information for a limited class and a specific transaction, creating a recognized duty. B: Property damage is not the only recognized path. A negligent misrepresentation claim can allow economic recovery when the duty is based on supplied information for limited reliance. D: The duty is limited; it does not extend to every foreseeable reader. The lender can recover because it was the named, intended user for a specific transaction.
lb_rule_reference: Pure economic loss may be recoverable in a negligent misrepresentation or professional information-supply claim when the defendant owed a duty to a limited, foreseeable class relying on the information.
