Question: Mary contracted to buy 1,000 jars from Joseph, all to be delivered on May 1. The signed contract stated that modifications had to be in a signed writing. After signing, Mary orally asked Joseph to deliver 500 jars on April 15 and 500 jars on May 15 because her shelves were not ready. Joseph delivered the first 500 jars on April 15, and Mary accepted and paid for them. Before the second shipment, Mary tried to cancel and objected when Joseph offered evidence of her oral split-shipment request and her acceptance of the first shipment. For admissibility purposes, how should the court treat Joseph's evidence?
Answer A: Exclude it because the parol evidence rule bars any oral statement that differs from the signed delivery term.
Answer B: Admit it only if the judge first concludes that a binding written modification existed.
Answer C: Admit it for the limited purpose of showing later waiver or attempted modification; the evidence's ultimate legal effect is a separate question.
Answer D: Exclude it because only an accord and satisfaction can affect a signed delivery term after breach.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that later conduct and statements may be admitted to show waiver or attempted modification under Article 2, even if a signed-writing requirement prevents formal modification. The trigger facts are Mary's post-signing split-shipment request and her acceptance of the first altered shipment. The decisive lock is call discipline: the call asks admissibility, not whether Joseph has conclusively proved the final merits of waiver or unretractable reliance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it applies PER to post-signing conduct. B: B is wrong because admissibility for waiver does not require the judge to find a completed written modification first. D: D is wrong because accord and satisfaction is a discharge route; Joseph is offering evidence of later waiver/modification of delivery timing.
