Outline: Plea negotiations and withdrawn plea offers
Question: A defendant and his lawyer met with the prosecutor to discuss a possible guilty plea. During the meeting, the defendant said, “I delivered the package, but I want probation.” No plea agreement was reached. At trial, the prosecutor offers the statement against the defendant in the government's case-in-chief. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the statement because it was made during plea discussions with an attorney for the prosecuting authority and no resulting guilty plea occurred.
Answer B: Admit the statement because no guilty plea was entered.
Answer C: Admit the statement because plea discussions are protected only after a written plea agreement is signed.
Answer D: Exclude the statement only if it was made under oath in court.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 410 bars statements made during plea discussions with an attorney for the prosecuting authority when the discussions do not result in a guilty plea or result in a later-withdrawn guilty plea. The prosecutor cannot use the statement against the defendant in the case-in-chief.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A is wrong because Correct answer B is wrong because Rule 410 protects failed plea discussions; no entered plea is required. C is wrong because A signed plea agreement is not required for Rule 410 protection. D is wrong because Statements in Rule 11 proceedings are one protected category, but prosecutor plea discussions are separately protected. C: C is wrong because A signed plea agreement is not required for Rule 410 protection. D: D is wrong because Statements in Rule 11 proceedings are one protected category, but prosecutor plea discussions are separately protected.
