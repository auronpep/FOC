Question: A warehouse supervisor handed forklift keys to a new worker who had never driven a forklift and had just failed the safety test. The supervisor told him to move pallets through a busy loading area. The worker lost control and struck a visitor. What is the strongest duty theory against the supervisor?
Answer A: Negligent entrustment, because the supervisor gave a dangerous machine to a known incompetent operator
Answer B: No duty, because only the forklift driver physically hit the visitor
Answer C: No duty unless the supervisor owned the forklift personally
Answer D: Strict liability, because forklifts are always abnormally dangerous
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The supervisor knew the worker was untrained and had failed the test, yet entrusted him with a forklift in a busy area.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The supervisor's own entrustment can be negligent when it foreseeably enables unsafe operation. C: The key fact is control over access and knowledge of incompetence, not personal title. D: This is ordinary negligent entrustment, not strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity.
lb_rule_reference: Entrusting a dangerous instrumentality to a person known or likely to be incompetent can create a negligence duty.
