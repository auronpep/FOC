Question: In a trade-secret case, the plaintiff seeks design documents that are relevant and proportional. The defendant does not claim privilege but shows the documents contain sensitive proprietary formulas. The defendant asks the court to limit disclosure to outside counsel and experts. What is the court's best response?
Answer A: Deny protection because nonprivileged relevant documents must always be produced without restrictions
Answer B: Deny all discovery because trade-secret material is categorically immune from production
Answer C: Enter an appropriate protective order if good cause supports limiting access while allowing the relevant discovery
Answer D: Compel production only if the plaintiff first proves the trade secret is invalid
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule allows the court to protect against undue burden, oppression, or confidentiality harm through tailored conditions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A protective order can regulate the manner and recipients of discovery for good cause. B: Confidentiality supports protection, not automatic total withholding. D: Discovery does not require the requesting party to prove the merits first.
lb_rule_reference: For good cause, the court may issue a protective order to protect a party or person from annoyance, embarrassment, oppression, or undue burden or expense.
