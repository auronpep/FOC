Question: Samuel signed and recorded an instrument granting a communications company “a perpetual easement to install, inspect, repair, and replace fiber-optic cables beneath Samuel's land.” The company owns no neighboring parcel benefited by the cables. What is the best classification of the company's interest?
Answer A: An easement appurtenant because every easement must benefit a neighboring parcel.
Answer B: A commercial easement in gross because the right benefits the company without a dominant parcel.
Answer C: A real covenant because the company may repair equipment.
Answer D: A revocable license because companies cannot hold easements.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement in gross benefits a person or entity rather than a dominant parcel. Utility and communications rights are classic commercial in-gross easements. The lack of company-owned neighboring land does not defeat the easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because easements in gross do not require a dominant parcel. C: C is wrong because the instrument grants a use right in land, not merely a promise. D: D is wrong because an entity can hold a commercial easement.
lb_rule_reference: Commercial easement in gross
