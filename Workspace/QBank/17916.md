Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Rebekah agreed to pay Boaz $70,000 to repair a barn, with payment due only on completion. Boaz wrongfully abandoned the job after partial work but before any payment was due. Rebekah reasonably hired another contractor to finish the promised repair work for $60,000. Rebekah proves no delay damages or other loss. What direct expectation damages may Rebekah recover from Boaz?
Answer A: $70,000, because Boaz breached the repair contract.
Answer B: $60,000, because that was the cost to finish the work.
Answer C: $10,000, because the substitute cost was $10,000 less than the original price.
Answer D: Nothing, because the substitute completion cost was less than the unpaid contract balance and Rebekah proved no other loss.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Expectation damages prevent overcompensation. Rebekah expected to pay $70,000 for the completed barn repair and ultimately obtained completion for $60,000. Without delay damages or another proven loss, she has no direct expectation damages from the breach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because breach alone does not justify the full contract price as damages. B: B is wrong because Rebekah would have paid Boaz $70,000 if the contract had been performed. C: C is wrong because the lower substitute cost is a savings to Rebekah, not a loss.
lb_rule_reference: Common law expectation damages; no overcompensation
