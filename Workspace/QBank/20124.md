Question: Solomon built a lawful two-story home on his lot. The home blocked sunlight and a mountain view from Abigail's adjoining lot. Abigail had no written easement, covenant, subdivision restriction, or statute protecting sunlight or views. Abigail sues to force Solomon to lower the house. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Abigail has an implied negative easement for sunlight because her lot previously received sunlight.
Answer B: Abigail has no enforceable right to sunlight or view absent an easement, covenant, statute, or other recognized source of right.
Answer C: Abigail has an implied easement for a view because the view increased her property value.
Answer D: Abigail can recover in nuisance merely because the new home is unattractive to her.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: At common law, there is generally no automatic right to light, air, or view across neighboring land. Such a right usually must come from an easement, covenant, statute, or other recognized legal source. The facts remove each such source.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because courts do not freely imply broad negative easements for sunlight. C: C is wrong because increased property value does not create a view easement. D: D is wrong because aesthetic displeasure alone is not enough for nuisance liability.
lb_rule_reference: No automatic right to light, air, or view
