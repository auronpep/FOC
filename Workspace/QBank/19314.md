Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Job was leaving town during a flood. He signed a deed conveying Farmacre to Leah, handed it to Leah, and said, “Keep this safe for me until I return. I am not giving you the farm.” Leah kept the deed and later claimed title because Job had handed her the signed instrument. Who owns Farmacre?
Answer A: Job, because Leah held the deed only for safekeeping and Job expressly denied present intent to transfer title.
Answer B: Leah, because delivery is complete whenever the grantor voluntarily hands the deed to the named grantee.
Answer C: Leah, because Job's reason for leaving town made the deed a valid gift.
Answer D: Job only if Leah failed to record the deed before Job returned.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is present transfer intent. Job's words made Leah a safekeeping custodian, not a grantee receiving title. Physical handoff of the signed instrument did not pass title where Job expressly said he was not giving her the farm.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because physical handoff is not conclusive delivery. C: C is wrong because emergency circumstances do not override the grantor's stated lack of transfer intent. D: D is wrong because recording cannot cure lack of delivery.
lb_rule_reference: Safekeeping custody is not delivery when the grantor says title is not passing
