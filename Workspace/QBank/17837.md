Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: Ruth orally agreed to work as Naomi's caretaker “for the rest of Naomi's life” at a stated monthly wage. Naomi died eight months later, after Ruth had performed for the full eight months. Naomi's estate argues that the agreement was unenforceable because it was not in writing and might have lasted longer than one year. Is the estate's Statute of Frauds defense valid?
Answer A: No, because the agreement could have been fully performed within one year if Naomi died within that period.
Answer B: Yes, because any contract expected to last more than one year must be in writing.
Answer C: Yes, because employment and service contracts are always within the Statute of Frauds.
Answer D: No, but only because Ruth fully performed after the first year.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The one-year provision applies only when the agreement, by its terms, cannot possibly be fully performed within one year. A promise to serve for a person's lifetime can be fully performed within one year if that person dies within the year, so the one-year Statute of Frauds category does not apply.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because expected or likely duration is not the test. C: C is wrong because services contracts are not automatically within the Statute of Frauds. D: D is wrong because the key reason is possible performance within one year, not full performance after a year.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law one-year provision
