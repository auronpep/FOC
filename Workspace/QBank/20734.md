Question: A city reimburses nonprofit preschools for playground resurfacing if they meet safety standards. A church-run preschool meets every standard but is denied reimbursement solely because it is operated by a church. What is the best constitutional argument for the preschool?
Answer A: Denying the benefit solely because of religious status burdens Free Exercise
Answer B: The city must exclude the preschool because any benefit to a church is establishment
Answer C: The preschool loses because religious organizations have no rights in public-benefit programs
Answer D: The preschool wins only if the city funds religious instruction directly
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A neutral public safety benefit cannot generally exclude an otherwise eligible entity merely because it is religious.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The Establishment Clause does not require categorical religious-status exclusions from neutral benefits. C: Government benefits cannot be distributed on unconstitutional religious-status terms. D: The exclusion is based on religious status, even though the benefit is secular safety resurfacing.
lb_rule_reference: Excluding an otherwise eligible entity from a public benefit solely because of religious status can violate Free Exercise.
