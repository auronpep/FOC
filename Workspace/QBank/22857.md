


BARMATRIX Q#: 22857
Outline_code: 94020000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: A truck driver and a motorcyclist were in an accident in State A. The truck driver was a citizen of State A, and the motorcyclist was a citizen of State B. The motorcyclist was driving cross- country for fun and had never been to State A before the accident. Immediately after the accident, he flew home to State B for treatment. The truck driver sued the motorcyclist in State A federal court, claiming the accident had caused $100,000 in damages. The motorcyclist filed a motion to dismiss, arguing that the court lacked personal jurisdiction. How should the court rule?
Answer A: A: Deny the motion, because the motorcyclist had sufficient contacts with State A.
Answer B: B: Deny the motion, because the accident occurred in State A.
Answer C: C: Grant the motion, because the truck driver was a citizen of State A.
Answer D: D: Grant the motion, because the motorcyclist had insufficient contacts with State A.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: There are two types of personal jurisdiction. “Specific” jurisdiction arises when there is a close connection between the plaintiff's cause of action and the defendant's contacts with the jurisdiction. “General” jurisdiction arises when the defendant is “essentially at home” in the jurisdiction through repeated contacts with that jurisdiction. International Shoe Co. v. Washington, 326 U.S. 310 (1945). Here, the State A federal court would have specific jurisdiction over the claim because the accident occurred in State A. A is incorrect because a single visit to a state during a cross- country trip is unlikely to be sufficient contacts for personal jurisdiction. C is incorrect because the fact that the truck driver was a citizen of State A does not change the analysis. D is incorrect because the accident occurred in State A.
