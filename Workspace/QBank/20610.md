Outline: Defamation
Question: A supervisor drafted a letter falsely accusing an employee of theft. Before sending it to anyone, the supervisor handed the only copy to the employee and said, "This is what I was going to send." No one else saw or heard the accusation. Is there defamation publication?
Answer A: No, because the accusation was communicated only to the plaintiff
Answer B: Yes, because the supervisor wrote the accusation down
Answer C: Yes, because the accusation was serious
Answer D: No, but only because the employee did not lose money
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Publication means communication to a third party; showing the plaintiff alone is not enough.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Writing can be libel if communicated to a third party, but writing alone is not publication. C: Seriousness does not replace the publication element. D: The publication element fails before damages are reached.
lb_rule_reference: Defamation publication requires communication to at least one third person; a defamatory statement shown only to the plaintiff is not published.
