Stem / Fact Pattern: A woman, a citizen of State A, underwent surgery in State B. During the surgery, a medical device that was being implanted in her back snapped and damaged her spine. The woman sued the manufacturer of the device in federal district court in State B and sued the doctor and the hospital in State B state court. The manufacturer moved to dismiss the woman’s action for failure to join the doctor and hospital as necessary parties under Fed. R. Civ. P. 19(a). Should the federal district court dismiss the woman’s case?
Answer A: A: No, because the doctor and hospital are joint tortfeasors.
Answer B: B: No, because the doctor and hospital are potential defendants in the woman’s action.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the doctor and hospital are necessary and indispensable parties.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the doctor and hospital are likely jointly and severally liable.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Joint tortfeasors with potential joint- and- several liability are permissive parties to an action. Consequently, they are not “necessary and indispensable” parties subject to compulsory joinder under Fed. R. Civ. P. 19. Temple v. Synthes Corp., 498 U.S. 5 (1990). Therefore, the federal case should not be dismissed simply because the woman failed to join the doctor and the hospital. B is incorrect because the mere fact that the doctor and hospital are potential defendants does not make them subject to Fed. R. Civ. P. 19. C is incorrect because the United States Supreme Court has held that joint tortfeasors are not necessary and indispensable parties. D is incorrect because the fact that the doctor and hospital are likely jointly and severally liable does not make them parties necessary for the federal adjudication.
