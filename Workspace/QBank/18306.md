Question: Moses owned a valid easement across Joseph's land to reach a river. Moses stopped using the route, removed the only bridge on his side of the easement, built a permanent wall across his own entrance to the route, and wrote to Joseph that he was permanently giving up the easement. Years later, Moses tried to use the old route again. Is the easement still enforceable?
Answer A: No, because Moses's nonuse plus clear acts and statements showing intent to relinquish the easement amounted to abandonment.
Answer B: No, because any easement terminates automatically when unused for one year.
Answer C: Yes, because an easement can be terminated only by a recorded deed from the easement holder.
Answer D: Yes, because abandonment is impossible unless Joseph pays Moses consideration.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The easement is not enforceable. Abandonment requires more than mere nonuse; it requires intent to relinquish the easement, usually shown by statements or conduct inconsistent with continued ownership. Moses's wall, bridge removal, and written statement show abandonment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because nonuse alone does not automatically terminate an easement after a fixed short period. C: C is wrong because a formal release is one termination method, but abandonment may also terminate an easement. D: D is wrong because abandonment does not require consideration from the servient owner.
lb_rule_reference: Easement abandonment / intent to relinquish
