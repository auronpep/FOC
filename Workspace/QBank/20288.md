Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Solomon owned a 90-acre farm. He signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey ten acres of my 90-acre farm to Nathan.” The deed did not identify which ten acres, provide a method for selecting them, or attach a map. Solomon privately intended Nathan to receive the ten acres near the well, but the deed did not say that. What is the result?
Answer A: No title passes, because the deed does not describe the land conveyed with reasonable certainty.
Answer B: Nathan receives the ten acres near the well, because Solomon privately intended that location.
Answer C: Nathan receives any ten acres he chooses after recording the deed.
Answer D: Nathan receives an undivided one-ninth interest in the whole farm.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is property description. A deed must identify the land conveyed with reasonable certainty or provide an objective selection method. “Ten acres of my 90-acre farm” gives no way to determine which land was conveyed, and Solomon's private intent cannot cure the description. No title passes by this deed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because private, unstated intent cannot supply the missing land description. C: C is wrong because recording does not create a selection method. D: D is wrong because the deed purports to convey a physical ten-acre portion, not a fractional share of the entire farm.
