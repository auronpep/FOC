Question: Miriam sues Nathan's grocery store after slipping on grapes in an aisle. The disputed issues are whether grapes were on the floor long enough for the store to discover them and whether Miriam was injured. Nathan offers evidence that Miriam was cited for speeding two years earlier. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the citation because it tends to show Miriam is careless generally.
Answer B: Exclude the citation because it does not make any fact of consequence in the slip-and-fall case more or less probable.
Answer C: Admit the citation because all prior negligent conduct is relevant in civil cases.
Answer D: Exclude the citation only if Miriam first proves she paid the fine.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The speeding citation does not logically bear on the store's notice of grapes, the presence of grapes, or Miriam's injury. It invites a general-carelessness inference rather than proving a consequential fact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because general carelessness is not a proper relevance link to the disputed slip-and-fall facts. C: C is wrong because prior negligent conduct is not automatically relevant. D: D is wrong because whether Miriam paid the fine has no bearing on the missing connection.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Using a broad character-like inference instead of identifying a fact of consequence.
