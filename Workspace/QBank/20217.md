Outline: Liquidated damages
Question: Miriam contracted with Isaac to maintain an apartment building for $6,000 per month. The contract stated that Isaac would owe $40,000 as liquidated damages for any breach of any duty, including late submission of a monthly maintenance report. At the time of contracting, any loss from a late report was easy to measure. Isaac submitted one report two days late, causing Miriam to pay a $200 administrative fee and no other loss. What should Miriam recover for the late report?
Answer A: $40,000, because the contract expressly calls that amount liquidated damages.
Answer B: $200, because the broad $40,000 clause is an unenforceable penalty as applied to this readily measurable minor breach.
Answer C: Nothing, because a two-day report delay is too minor to support contract damages.
Answer D: $40,200, because Miriam may recover both the liquidated amount and the administrative fee.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under a services contract. The breach posture is a minor breach, and the loss category is a readily measurable administrative fee. The penalty gate invalidates the $40,000 clause because it imposes one large amount for any breach, including trivial breaches, where the expected loss was easy to measure and grossly smaller than the stipulated sum. The formula after excluding the penalty is the proved actual loss: $200. The result scope is limited actual recovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the liquidated-damages label does not prevent penalty analysis, triggering WAL-REM-015. C: C is wrong because minor breach can still produce limited actual damages; nominal damages or no recovery is not required when a $200 loss is proved. D: D is wrong because it stacks an invalid stipulated amount with actual damages and overcompensates Miriam, triggering WAL-REM-008.
lb_rule_reference: Common law penalty doctrine — one large amount for any breach, including trivial breaches with measurable loss, is unenforceable as a penalty
