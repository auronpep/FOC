internal_id: TR-234
Outline_code: 61020402
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A store employee negligently left a mop bucket near the front entrance, creating a slip risk for customers. Before anyone slipped, a stranger grabbed the bucket, carried it across the street, and intentionally threw it through an unrelated office window. The office owner sues the store for the broken window. Is the store likely liable for the window damage?
Answer A: Yes, because the window would not have broken but for the bucket being left near the entrance
Answer B: Yes, because stores are responsible for all misuse of objects on their premises
Answer C: No, because the intentional window-smashing was extraordinary and outside the slip risk
Answer D: No, because property damage is never a negligence injury
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The breached precaution guarded against entrance slips, not an intentional attack on an unrelated office window. The stranger's act is superseding.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: But-for cause is not enough. The intentional window-smashing is outside the slip risk that made leaving the bucket negligent. B: Negligence liability is limited by proximate cause and scope of risk. D: Property damage can be recoverable. This claim fails because the harm was outside the risk.
lb_rule_reference: An extraordinary intentional act outside the risk created by the defendant's breach may be a superseding cause.
