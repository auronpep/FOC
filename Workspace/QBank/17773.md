MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff, a citizen of Idaho, files a diversity action in federal court against a defendant, a citizen of Montana. The plaintiff's complaint seeks $50,000 in compensatory damages for breach of contract. The defendant files an answer asserting a counterclaim for $100,000 against the plaintiff, arising from a different contract dispute between the parties. The defendant argues that because the combined claims in the case exceed $75,000, the amount-in-controversy requirement is satisfied. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Does the AIC requirement under § 1332 support jurisdiction over the plaintiff's original claim?
Answer A: Yes, because the defendant's $100,000 counterclaim can be added to the plaintiff's $50,000 claim, bringing the total controversy to $150,000 and satisfying the AIC threshold
Answer B: No, because the plaintiff's $50,000 claim does not independently exceed the $75,000 threshold, and the defendant's counterclaim cannot inflate the plaintiff's AIC
Answer C: Yes, because the defendant's counterclaim brings the total amount in dispute over $75,000, and the purpose of the AIC threshold is to screen trivial cases — this case is clearly not trivial
Answer D: No, but the defendant may independently assert jurisdiction over the counterclaim under § 1332 if the counterclaim meets the threshold, treating the counterclaim as a separate action
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The amount in controversy under § 1332 is determined from the plaintiff's complaint at the time of filing. The plaintiff claims $50,000 — below the $75,000 threshold. The defendant's $100,000 counterclaim does not increase the plaintiff's AIC; the AIC requirement focuses on what the plaintiff is seeking, not on the total dollar amount of all claims pending in the case. Because the plaintiff's claim falls short of $75,000, the court lacks § 1332 jurisdiction over the plaintiff's original claim, and the action must be dismissed or remanded. (Note: the defendant's counterclaim, if it independently qualifies under § 1332, would be a separate jurisdictional analysis.)
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The AIC is measured from the plaintiff's perspective and from the plaintiff's complaint at the time of filing. A defendant's counterclaim does not supplement the plaintiff's AIC. The plaintiff's claim of $50,000 is measured on its own; adding the counterclaim to inflate the plaintiff's AIC is not permitted. The plaintiff's claim must independently exceed $75,000. C: The AIC rule is applied technically, not purposively. Courts do not use the policy rationale to override the rule that the AIC is measured from the plaintiff's complaint alone. The fact that a large counterclaim is pending does not satisfy a threshold that the plaintiff's own claim failed to meet. D: The question asks specifically about jurisdiction over the plaintiff's original claim. The plaintiff's $50,000 claim lacks the required AIC and must be dismissed. What happens to the defendant's counterclaim is a separate issue not posed by the question. An answer that is non-responsive to the question asked — even if technically defensible in isolation — is wrong on a bar exam.
lb_rule_reference: The AIC under § 1332 is determined from the plaintiff's complaint at the time of filing; the defendant's counterclaim does not supplement or inflate the plaintiff's AIC.
