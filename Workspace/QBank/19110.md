Question: Hannah orally agrees to buy 600 wooden crates from Samuel for $6,000. Samuel delivers 150 crates. After a reasonable opportunity to inspect them, Hannah tells Samuel, “I will keep these 150 crates, but I am not taking the rest.” No writing was signed. Samuel sues to enforce the entire 600-crate agreement. What is the best UCC Statute of Frauds result?
Answer A: The contract is enforceable for the 150 crates Hannah received and accepted, but not for the remaining 450 absent another exception.
Answer B: The contract is enforceable for all 600 crates because acceptance of any goods validates the entire contract.
Answer C: The contract is unenforceable for all crates because no writing was signed.
Answer D: The contract is enforceable for all 600 crates only if Samuel is a merchant.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: An oral goods contract can be enforced with respect to goods that have been received and accepted. Hannah accepted 150 crates after inspection, so that part falls within the exception. The remaining crates are not covered merely by partial acceptance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because acceptance of part does not validate the entire alleged quantity. C: C is wrong because received-and-accepted goods are a statutory exception. D: D is wrong because this exception does not depend on merchant status.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §§2-201(3)(c), 2-606
