Question: In federal discovery, Paul withholds 200 emails and states only, “All withheld emails are privileged.” Paul gives no dates, participants, general subject descriptions, or basis that would allow the requesting party to assess the claim without seeing the contents. What should the court require?
Answer A: Nothing more, because saying “privileged” is enough to preserve the claim.
Answer B: Immediate production of every email, because an inadequate log permanently destroys privilege.
Answer C: Paul must expressly claim the privilege and describe the nature of the withheld communications in a way that permits assessment without revealing privileged information.
Answer D: The judge must submit the emails to the jury to decide privilege.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A party withholding discoverable information on privilege or trial-preparation grounds must expressly make the claim and describe the nature of the material without revealing protected information, enough for others to assess the claim. The decisive axis is privilege procedure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is privilege-log omission. A bare slogan does not satisfy the procedural claim requirement. B: B is wrong because This overstates the consequence. The court may require a proper log or resolve disputes without automatic permanent waiver on these facts. D: A is wrong because This is privilege-log omission. A bare slogan does not satisfy the procedural claim requirement. B is wrong because This overstates the consequence. The court may require a proper log or resolve disputes without automatic permanent waiver on these facts. C is wrong because Correct answer D is wrong because Privilege is a court question, not a jury merits question.
lb_rule_reference: FRCP 26(b)(5)(A) / privilege log
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is invoking privilege as a conclusion rather than satisfying the procedural gate for withheld material.
