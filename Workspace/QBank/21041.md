Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Taxing and spending power
Question: Congress enacts a statute making it a federal crime for any parent to let a minor child skip school more than five times in a semester. The statute contains no jurisdictional element, does not regulate goods or services, and is justified in the findings as necessary because school attendance is a serious national concern. What is the strongest constitutional objection?
Answer A: The statute is valid because education is an important national issue that requires uniform federal treatment
Answer B: The statute is invalid unless Congress can connect it to an enumerated power such as commerce, taxing, spending, or Section 5 enforcement
Answer C: The statute is valid because Congress shares the states' general police power when regulating family and education issues
Answer D: The statute is invalid only if it violates a fundamental right
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Congress needs an enumerated authority hook; education and truancy are traditionally state police-power subjects unless tied to a federal source of power.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Importance alone does not give Congress a general police power. C: States have general police power; Congress does not. D: Structural limits on congressional power apply even when no individual right is violated.
lb_rule_reference: Congress must identify an enumerated constitutional power; the federal government does not possess a general police power merely because a problem is national or important.
