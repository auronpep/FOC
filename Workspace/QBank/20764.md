Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: After an oral agreement to sell Isaac 60 carved chairs for $12,000, the seller signs a note to his warehouse manager stating, “Set aside 60 carved chairs for Isaac under our sale agreement; delivery next Friday.” The seller later refuses to deliver and argues that the note is not a formal contract. Can the note satisfy the Statute of Frauds memorandum requirement?
Answer A: No, because only a document titled “Contract” can satisfy the Statute of Frauds.
Answer B: Yes, because a signed informal writing can satisfy the statute if it indicates a contract and includes the required essential information.
Answer C: No, because the note was written to the warehouse manager rather than Isaac.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Isaac also signed the note.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A Statute of Frauds memorandum need not be a formal contract. A signed note can be sufficient if it indicates that a contract was made and contains the required information, such as the quantity in a goods contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because formal title is not required. C: C is wrong because the writing's addressee is not necessarily controlling. D: D is wrong because Isaac need not sign when he seeks enforcement against the seller who signed.
lb_rule_reference: SOF memorandum / informal signed writing
