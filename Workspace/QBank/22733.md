


BARMATRIX Q#: 22733
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A man from State A was injured when he was in a car accident with a woman from State B. The next day, the man saw the woman on the local news and discovered that she was a prominent State B politician. The following week, he went to a political rally the woman was holding in State B and told a television reporter at the scene that he was in a car accident with the woman and that the woman was “a drunk, crazy person who should not drive a car — let alone hold public office!” The woman filed suit against the man in State A federal court, claiming that she suffered $50,000 in damages in the car wreck and $50,000 in damages to her reputation due to the man's television interview. The man objected, arguing that the State A federal court did not have jurisdiction to hear the case. Does the State A federal court have jurisdiction?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the requirements for diversity jurisdiction are met.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the suit involves a defamation claim from a public figure.
Answer C: C: No, because the woman's two claims are not part of the same transaction or occurrence.
Answer D: D: No, because negligence and defamation claims arise out of state law.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A federal court has diversity jurisdiction over a case if the parties are citizens of different states and the amount in controversy is over $75,000. Importantly, even if the claims are unrelated, different claims asserted against the same defendant by the same plaintiff can be aggregated to meet the amount in controversy. Here, the man and the woman are citizens of different states and the two claims can be aggregated to meet the amount in controversy requirement. B is incorrect because there is no special jurisdictional rule regarding defamation claims brought by public figures. C is incorrect because all claims brought by the same plaintiff against the same defendant can be brought together. D is incorrect because the parties are from different states and the two claims meet the amount in controversy requirement when aggregated.
