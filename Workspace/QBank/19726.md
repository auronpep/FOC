Question: Solomon granted Abigail a recorded “exclusive easement to park two cars in the marked spaces at the rear of Solomon's lot.” The grant stated that Solomon could not use those spaces while the easement lasted. Abigail later claimed that the exclusive language gave her fee title to the parking spaces. Which result is most accurate?
Answer A: Abigail owns the parking spaces in fee because any exclusive easement is automatically a conveyance of title.
Answer B: Abigail has an exclusive use right within the easement's scope, but the exclusive easement does not by itself transfer fee title to the parking spaces.
Answer C: The easement is invalid because easements can never be exclusive.
Answer D: Solomon may revoke the easement at will because parking rights are always licenses.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement can be exclusive in the sense that the servient owner is excluded from the defined use area or use type. But exclusivity does not automatically convert the easement into fee title. Abigail has the defined parking use right, not ownership of the land in fee.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because exclusive use language does not automatically convey fee title. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because easements may be exclusive. D: D is wrong because a recorded parking easement can be a property interest rather than a revocable license.
lb_rule_reference: Exclusive easement / fee-title distinction
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is collapsing exclusive easement into fee ownership or, in the opposite direction, treating parking as merely permission.
