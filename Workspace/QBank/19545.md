Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Cause in fact (actual cause)
Question: A camper's tent caught fire after a spark landed on it. Three nearby campers had maintained campfires that evening. One camper violated posted rules by leaving a fire unattended. The other two used covered fire rings, kept water nearby, and fully complied with campground rules. Investigators can prove that the spark came from one of the three campfires, but they cannot identify which one. In the injured camper's negligence claim against all three campers, may the court use alternative liability to shift the burden of causation to them?
Answer A: Yes, because at least one of the three campfires must have caused the injury
Answer B: Yes, because campfire sparks are fungible like market-share products
Answer C: No, because not all defendants in the possible causal pool were negligent
Answer D: No, because negligence can never be proved by circumstantial evidence
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Alternative liability is limited to cases where all defendants in the relevant pool acted negligently. Because two campers used reasonable care, the burden should not shift to all three.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Alternative liability requires a negligent pool. The plaintiff cannot shift the burden to defendants who were not shown to have acted negligently. B: Market-share liability is narrow and does not apply merely because physical particles are hard to trace. This is not a fungible product market. D: Negligence can be proved circumstantially. The problem is not the type of evidence; it is the missing all-negligent condition for alternative liability.
lb_rule_reference: Alternative liability burden shifting is not available when the plaintiff cannot show that all defendants in the causal pool acted negligently.
