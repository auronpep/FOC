Question: Joseph offered to sell Benjamin 500 shares of investment stock for $15,000. Benjamin replied, “I accept if you also guarantee that the company will have no unpaid tax liability.” Joseph did not agree and later refused to transfer the shares. Benjamin argues that Article 2 makes his reply an acceptance with an additional term. Is Benjamin correct?
Answer A: Yes, because Article 2 treats every sale of personal property as a sale of goods.
Answer B: No, because investment securities are not goods under Article 2, and Benjamin's conditional reply was not a common-law acceptance.
Answer C: Yes, because any reply that includes the word accept forms a contract.
Answer D: No, because stock sale contracts are unenforceable unless both parties perform first.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Article 2 excludes investment securities from the definition of goods. Common-law formation rules therefore apply. Benjamin's reply added a condition and was not a mirror-image acceptance of Joseph's offer. Without Joseph's assent to that condition, no contract formed on Benjamin's terms.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Article 2 does not cover all personal property; investment securities are excluded. C: C is wrong because labels do not override the legal effect of a conditional reply. D: D is wrong because stock contracts can be enforceable, but this reply did not accept the offer.
