Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Anna sues a tour company after being left behind at a rest stop. The company claims Anna intentionally stayed behind to meet a friend. Anna offers a text message she sent her sister ten minutes before the bus departed: “I am exhausted and just want to get back on the bus and sleep.” The company objects that the text does not prove Anna actually tried to board the bus. Assuming no hearsay objection is before the court, how should the court rule on relevance?
Answer A: Exclude the text because it does not conclusively show Anna attempted to board.
Answer B: Admit the text because Anna's stated current desire to get back on the bus has some tendency to make intentional abandonment less probable.
Answer C: Exclude the text because text messages can never be relevant without a reply.
Answer D: Admit the text only if Anna's sister testifies that she believed it.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The text tends to show Anna's then-current desire and makes it less likely that she intentionally stayed behind. It need not prove she actually attempted to board. The limited strength of the inference is a weight issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevance does not require conclusive proof of the ultimate conduct. C: C is wrong because a text can be relevant without a reply if properly connected to the disputed fact. D: D is wrong because the sister's belief is not required for the text to have probative value as to Anna's state or plan.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Demanding ultimate-conduct proof from evidence of current state or plan.
