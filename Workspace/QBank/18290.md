Question: After Ruth received a demand letter threatening suit, her insurer assigned adjuster Mark to investigate for defense counsel. Mark prepared a report evaluating witness statements and liability exposure for the anticipated litigation. Is the report outside work-product protection merely because Mark is an adjuster rather than a lawyer?
Answer A: No, work product can include materials prepared in anticipation of litigation by or for a party or its representative, including an insurer or agent.
Answer B: Yes, work product protects only documents drafted by attorneys.
Answer C: Yes, because insurance investigations are always ordinary business records.
Answer D: No, but only if Mark's report is also an attorney-client communication.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 26(b)(3) protects litigation-preparation materials prepared by or for a party or its representative, including agents and insurers. The report was prepared after a demand letter for defense counsel and anticipated litigation. The decisive axis is representative preparation for litigation.
