MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A contractor was lawfully blasting rock at a remote quarry. A visitor arrived after blasting had stopped for the day. While walking across the office lobby to ask for directions, the visitor slipped on a puddle from a leaking water cooler. The visitor sues the contractor for strict liability based on blasting. Is the contractor strictly liable for the lobby injury?
Answer A: Yes, because the contractor engages in blasting somewhere on the property
Answer B: No, because the injury was outside the risk that makes blasting abnormally dangerous
Answer C: No, because blasting can never be abnormally dangerous at a remote quarry
Answer D: Yes, unless the contractor proves it used reasonable care to keep the lobby dry
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Strict liability is limited by risk scope. A water-cooler slip in the office lobby is not a blast-wave, vibration, debris, or explosive-risk injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The injury did not result from blasting's abnormal risk. It resulted from a lobby slip unrelated to explosive danger. C: The better answer is narrower: this harm is unrelated. Some blasting can still be abnormally dangerous depending on context. D: The call asks activity-based strict liability. Reasonable care about the lobby is a negligence issue, not abnormal-risk strict liability.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity is limited to harm resulting from the abnormal risk that makes the activity dangerous.
