Question: In a federal criminal trial, the prosecutor offers a typed transcript of a recorded phone call to prove the exact words spoken on the recording. The prosecutor does not offer the recording, does not claim it is lost, and calls no witness who heard the call live. Aaron objects under the original-writing rule. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because a transcript is always a duplicate of a recording.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because the case involves a phone call rather than a written document.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if Aaron proves the transcript is inaccurate.
Answer D: Sustain the objection because the prosecutor is trying to prove the contents of the recording without producing the recording or a proper substitute.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: When the proponent seeks to prove the contents of a recording, the original-writing rule requires the original recording or a recognized substitute. A transcript alone is not automatically enough when the recording is available and no adequate basis for secondary proof is shown.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a transcript is not automatically a duplicate of the recording for original-writing purposes. B: B is wrong because the original-writing rule applies to recordings as well as writings and photographs. C: C is wrong because Aaron need not first prove inaccuracy when the proponent has not satisfied the content-proof requirement.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 1002; FRE 1003
lb_student_diagnostic: False-negative original-writing trap: student fails to see that exact recorded words are contents.
