Outline: Mistake / Lack of Mutual Assent
Question: Peter contracts to buy “the eastern lot” from John. John owns two lots east of the road. Before signing, Peter points to the larger lot and says, “That is the one I am buying.” John silently knows that the written phrase could refer to either lot, but John intends the smaller lot. Which result is most likely?
Answer A: There is no contract because the phrase has two possible meanings.
Answer B: Peter's meaning controls because John knew or had reason to know Peter's meaning and Peter did not know John's different meaning.
Answer C: John's meaning controls because he owned the property.
Answer D: The contract is void for illegality.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When parties attach different meanings, the meaning of the party who does not know of the other meaning can control if the other party knows or has reason to know it. John knew Peter intended the larger lot, while Peter did not know John intended the smaller lot.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because ambiguity does not automatically defeat the contract when one party knows the other's meaning. C: C is wrong because ownership status does not control the interpretation when John knew Peter's meaning. D: D is wrong because no illegal bargain appears.
lb_rule_reference: Known ambiguity / innocent party meaning controls
