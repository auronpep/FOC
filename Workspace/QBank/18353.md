MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A laboratory received a delivery of highly toxic chemicals. The chemicals remained sealed and caused no leak, fumes, fire, or explosion. While the delivery truck was parked outside, an unrelated thief stole a visitor's bicycle from the sidewalk. The visitor sues the laboratory for strict liability based on chemical storage. Is the laboratory strictly liable for the bicycle theft?
Answer A: Yes, because the theft occurred while the chemicals were on site
Answer B: No, because the theft was outside the abnormal chemical-storage risk
Answer C: Yes, because strict liability applies to all losses once an activity is classified as abnormally dangerous
Answer D: No, but only because the chemicals were sealed with reasonable care
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Strict liability does not cover unrelated criminal theft merely because an abnormally dangerous activity was occurring nearby.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The theft did not result from the abnormal risk of toxic chemical storage. C: Even if chemical storage is abnormally dangerous, the plaintiff must show harm from that abnormal risk. D: The decisive reason is not due care; the bicycle theft is unrelated to the abnormal chemical risk.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity does not extend to harms that are merely temporally or geographically connected but outside the abnormal risk.
