Subtopic: Rights in Land
Outline: Creation
Question: Ruth signed and delivered a document stating, “I grant Boaz, as owner of Vineyard Lot, and Boaz's successors as owners of Vineyard Lot, a perpetual right to use the lane across my Orchard Lot for ingress and egress.” Boaz did not pay Ruth for the grant. Ruth later claimed the document created no easement because Boaz gave no consideration. Is Ruth correct?
Answer A: No. A signed and delivered grant can create an express easement even if the grantee paid no consideration.
Answer B: Yes. Consideration is required for every conveyance of a land-use right.
Answer C: Yes. The document created only a revocable license because Boaz did not buy part of Orchard Lot.
Answer D: No, but only because the right is a profit rather than an easement.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A deed or written grant can create an express easement if it is properly executed and delivered. Consideration is not required for validity of the conveyed property interest. The document grants a perpetual ingress-and-egress right tied to Vineyard Lot.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because consideration is not a universal requirement for a valid deeded easement. C: C is wrong because a written grant of a permanent use right is not merely a revocable license. D: D is wrong because the right is access, not removal of resources from land.
