Question: A bakery offered a chef a permanent full-time job as a pastry chef at a salary of $3,000 per month. The chef agreed to take the position and to begin work in two weeks. In her employment application, the chef had indicated that she was seeking a permanent job. One week after the chef was hired by the bakery, a hotel offered the chef a position as a restaurant manager at a salary of $3,500 a month. The chef accepted and promptly notified the bakery that she would not report for work at the bakery. Is the bakery likely to prevail in a lawsuit against the chef for breach of contract?
Answer A: A: No, because a contract for permanent employment would be interpreted to mean that the chef could leave at any time.
Answer B: B: No, because the position the chef took with the hotel was not substantially comparable to the one she had agreed to take with the bakery.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the chef's acceptance of a permanent position meant that she agreed to leave the bakery only after a reasonable time.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the chef's failure to give the bakery a chance to match the salary offered by the hotel breached the implied right of first refusal.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. The characterization of the pastry chef position as "permanent" indicates that it was an employment-at- will relationship, which may be terminated by either party, at any time, for any reason. B
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. The reason for not following through with the bakery position is irrelevant. The correct answer is based on the nature of the employent-at-will relationship, which may be terminated by either party at any time, and for ANY reason. C is incorrect. The word "permanent" in describing the position is dispositive here, because it indicates an employment-at-will relationship, as stated above. D is incorrect. The implied right of first refusal is not applicable here, where the nature of the employment relationship (at-will) governs the outcome. The right of refusal issue arises in the context of express agreements, not by implication.
