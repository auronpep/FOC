Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers reasonably suspect Tom of shoplifting after seeing him hide store merchandise under his coat. They stop him outside the store. Tom is calm, cooperative, makes no threatening movement, and has no visible bulge. Without more, an officer pats him down and finds pills in a pocket. Was the frisk lawful?
Answer A: No, because officers lacked reasonable suspicion that Tom was armed and dangerous
Answer B: Yes, because every valid Terry stop includes a frisk
Answer C: Yes, because shoplifting always involves weapons
Answer D: No, because officers needed a warrant before touching Tom's clothing
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The shoplifting stop may be valid, but a frisk requires a separate safety basis. The facts do not suggest Tom was armed or dangerous.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Stop and frisk are separate intrusions with separate justifications. C: A generalized safety concern is not enough. The officer needs articulable facts suggesting weapons or danger. D: Terry allows a limited frisk without a warrant when armed-and-dangerous suspicion exists. It was missing here.
lb_rule_reference: A Terry frisk is permitted only when officers reasonably suspect the stopped person is armed and dangerous; reasonable suspicion of crime alone is not enough.
