Question: Mary agreed in writing to buy all of Joseph's shares in a closely held corporation. Before signing, Joseph allegedly promised that the corporation had no undisclosed tax debts. The final writing contained a merger clause but no tax-debt representation. Mary seeks to introduce Joseph's prior statement to interpret the stock-sale agreement. Which source-law classification is correct before deciding admissibility?
Answer A: UCC Article 2 applies because stock certificates are pieces of paper that can be moved.
Answer B: Common law or securities-related contract principles apply because the transaction is a sale of corporate stock, not a sale of goods.
Answer C: Real-property law applies because corporations own property.
Answer D: Article 2 applies because any contract involving a certificate is a goods contract.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A sale of corporate stock is not a sale of goods under Article 2. The source-law lock is outside Article 2, so UCC course/usage and Article 2 parol-evidence rules should not be imported automatically.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and D are wrong because the paper certificate is not the transaction's real object; the shares are investment rights.
