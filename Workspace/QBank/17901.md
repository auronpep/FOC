Question: A repair shop did not follow the common practice of placing a warning cone beside every parked repair cart. A customer saw the cart, walked safely around it, and then slipped on a freshly waxed floor in a different aisle. The wax had been applied carefully and was not unusually slippery. The customer sues based only on the missing cone near the cart. Is the missing cone enough by itself to make the shop liable?
Answer A: Yes, because violating custom automatically establishes all negligence elements
Answer B: Yes, because the customer was injured somewhere on the shop premises
Answer C: No, because any breach evidence from the missing cone did not cause the wax-floor fall
Answer D: No, because industry custom is inadmissible against plaintiffs
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The missing cone may show a custom departure, but the customer safely avoided the cart. The fall was unrelated to that omission.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Custom departure may be breach evidence, but the customer must still prove the missing cone caused the injury. B: Premises status does not create automatic liability. The alleged breach must cause the harm. D: Custom can be relevant. The claim fails because causation is missing, not because custom evidence is barred.
lb_rule_reference: Departure from custom is evidence of breach, but negligence still requires breach plus causation and damages.
