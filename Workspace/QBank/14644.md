Question: A bartender is charged with the statutory offense of "knowingly violating a regulation of the State Alcoholic Beverage Control Board" and that he knowingly violated regulation number 345-90 issued by the State Alcoholic Beverage Control Board. That regulation prohibits the sale of alcoholic beverages to any person under the age of 18 and also prohibits the sale of any alcoholic beverage to a person over the age of 17 and under the age of 22 without the presentation of such person's driver's license or other identification showing the age of the purchaser to be 18 or older. The evidence showed that the bartender worked in a tavern and sold a bottle of beer to a person who was 17 years old and that the bartender did not ask for or see the purchaser's driver's license or any other identification. Which of the following, if found by the jury, would be of the most help to the bartender?
Answer A: A: The purchaser had a driver's license that falsely showed his age to be 21.
Answer B: B: The bartender had never been told he was supposed to check identification of persons over 17 and under 22 before selling them alcohol.
Answer C: C: The bartender did not know that the regulations classified beer as an alcoholic beverage.
Answer D: D: The bartender mistakenly believed the purchaser to be 24 years old.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. The regulation stating that bartenders must require people between the ages of 17 and 22 to show identification of their age carries with it the implication that people above the age of 22 are not required to show identification. The bartender's best argument is that, since he mistakenly believed the purchaser to be 24 years-old, he was allowed to sell them beer and was not required to obtain identification; in relying on that statute, he did not ascertain the minor's age. This may allow him to argue that the regulation contains a mens rea element requiring that he knowingly violate the law. If the bartender did not knowingly sell alcohol to the minor, he may be acquitted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The fact that the purchaser could have presented a fake identification does not excuse the bartender's not asking to see it; his only legitimate excuse would be that he believed the person to be over 22 and that the statute includes a mens rea requirement. B is incorrect. Ignorance of the law is generally not an excuse, and the bartender's not being told that he has to check identification does not affect the offense's mens rea requirement. When a crime requires that the defendant act knowingly, he must have been aware that his conduct was of a particular nature, or at least know that his conduct will necessarily or very likely cause a particular result. Here, he must have been aware of the fact that he was selling to a minor, or to someone between the ages of 17 and 22, not the fact that his conduct was violating that regulation. C is incorrect. As stated above, ignorance of the law is no excuse, and not knowing that beer is an alcoholic beverage does not excuse the bartender's actions. If the jury found that the bartender mistakenly believed the purchaser to be 24 years-old, the bartender could argue that he relied on the statute's requirement of identification from 17 to 22-year- olds, and thus did not meet the mens rea requirement of the regulation.
