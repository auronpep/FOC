Outline: Rational basis review classifications
Question: A state employer's disability insurance program provides income replacement for employees who are temporarily unable to work due to disability, including most physical conditions. The program expressly excludes temporary disability resulting from normal pregnancy. A state employee denied benefits for her pregnancy-related temporary disability challenges the exclusion under the Equal Protection Clause, arguing that excluding pregnancy from disability coverage is sex-based discrimination. Under the Equal Protection Clause, which standard governs the state's pregnancy exclusion from its disability program?
Answer A: Intermediate scrutiny, because the pregnancy exclusion is a sex-based classification that expressly burdens a characteristic unique to women
Answer B: Strict scrutiny, because denying benefits for a condition unique to women targets a suspect biological characteristic
Answer C: Rational basis review, under Geduldig v. Aiello, because the Supreme Court held that a pregnancy exclusion in a disability program classifies between pregnant and non-pregnant persons, not between men and women, and is therefore not a sex-based classification requiring intermediate scrutiny
Answer D: No Equal Protection violation exists because the disability program is a voluntary benefit, not a legal entitlement, and the state may shape discretionary benefit programs without constitutional constraint
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Geduldig v. Aiello held that a state disability insurance program that excludes pregnancy-related disabilities does not constitute sex-based discrimination under the Equal Protection Clause. The Court reasoned that the classification divides the world into pregnant and non-pregnant people; women appear in both categories (pregnant women vs. non-pregnant women and men). Without a sex-based classification, intermediate scrutiny does not apply; rational basis governs.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Supreme Court held in Geduldig v. Aiello that a pregnancy exclusion from a state disability program is not sex discrimination under the Equal Protection Clause. The Court reasoned that the classification is between pregnant and non-pregnant persons — not between men and women — because both pregnant persons (women) and non-pregnant persons (which includes both men and many women) are encompassed by each group. Constitutional EP analysis does not treat pregnancy as a sex-based classification. B: Neither strict scrutiny nor intermediate scrutiny applies. Geduldig specifically held that pregnancy-based classifications under the Equal Protection Clause are not sex-based. Rational basis governs. Strict scrutiny would be reserved for race or national origin classifications — pregnancy does not rise to that level under constitutional doctrine. D: The Equal Protection Clause constrains the state's exercise of discretion even in the design of benefit programs. While the state need not provide disability insurance at all, once it creates the program, it must do so consistently with Equal Protection. The question is what standard applies — which Geduldig says is rational basis, not that EP doesn't apply at all.
lb_rule_reference: Under Geduldig v. Aiello, a state law that classifies based on pregnancy is not a sex-based classification under the Equal Protection Clause; pregnancy classifications receive rational basis review, not intermediate scrutiny, under constitutional EP analysis.
