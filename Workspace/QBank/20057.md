Subtopic: Contract Content and Meaning
Outline: Interpretation
Question: Bethany agreed in a signed contract to sell Thomas “all premium jars” needed for a harvest festival. The writing contained a merger clause but did not define “premium jars.” Thomas offers negotiation evidence that both parties used “premium jars” to mean jars with sealed lids and painted labels. The trial judge asks only whether the negotiation evidence is admissible to interpret the phrase. Which answer best responds to that call?
Answer A: The evidence is admissible if the phrase is ambiguous and the evidence is offered to explain its meaning, not to add a new term.
Answer B: Thomas wins damages because sealed lids and painted labels were not delivered.
Answer C: The evidence is inadmissible because a merger clause always bars negotiation evidence.
Answer D: The contract is unenforceable because “premium jars” is ambiguous.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The call asks admissibility to interpret a term. If “premium jars” is ambiguous, negotiation evidence may be admitted to explain what that term meant to the parties. The question does not ask whether Thomas ultimately wins damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it answers the merits instead of the admissibility call. C: C is wrong because merger clauses do not automatically bar evidence used to explain ambiguity. D: D is wrong because ambiguity often calls for interpretation, not unenforceability.
