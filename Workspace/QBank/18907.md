Question: A state requires all jurors to swear an oath ending with "so help me God." A prospective juror asks to make a secular affirmation promising truthfulness instead. The court denies the request, stating that the religious wording is traditional and mandatory. What is the best constitutional answer?
Answer A: The requirement is valid because traditional oath language may always be made mandatory
Answer B: The state may require a truthfulness commitment but must allow a secular affirmation for jurors who object to religious wording
Answer C: The state may not require any oath or affirmation from jurors
Answer D: The requirement is valid if most jurors do not object
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The government can require jurors to promise truthfulness, but it cannot force religious wording as the only option.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Tradition does not allow the state to compel religious affirmation when a secular affirmation can serve the truthfulness function. C: The defect is mandatory religious wording, not the use of an oath or affirmation to secure truthful service. D: Constitutional protection does not depend on majority comfort with religious wording.
lb_rule_reference: Government may require truthful oaths or affirmations, but it may not require a religious oath without a secular alternative.
