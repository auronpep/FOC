Question: Miriam and Joshua are both merchants dealing in wool. After an oral agreement for Joshua to buy 700 bolts of wool, Miriam sends Joshua a signed written confirmation. Joshua calls Miriam two days later and says, “I object to your confirmation,” but he sends no written objection. Joshua refuses to buy. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Joshua's phone call prevents the confirmation from satisfying the Statute of Frauds because any objection within 10 days is enough.
Answer B: Joshua's phone call does not prevent the merchant-confirmation rule from applying, because the objection must be written.
Answer C: The confirmation fails because Joshua did not sign it.
Answer D: The confirmation fails unless Miriam proves Joshua actually read every word before objecting orally.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under §2-201(2), the merchant recipient must give written notice of objection within 10 days after receiving the confirmation. An oral objection does not satisfy that requirement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the statute requires written notice. C: C is wrong because recipient signature is not required under the merchant-confirmation rule. D: D is wrong because the rule asks whether the recipient had reason to know the contents, not whether he read every word.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(2)
