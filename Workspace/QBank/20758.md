Question: In a federal arson prosecution, the government offers evidence that Jonah insured his warehouse shortly before the fire. Jonah objects that insurance evidence should be excluded. The court overrules the objection and admits the evidence to show motive. What remains for the jury to decide?
Answer A: Nothing; admitting the insurance evidence establishes motive as a matter of law.
Answer B: Only whether Jonah owned the warehouse; motive is conclusively established by admissibility.
Answer C: Whether the insurance evidence is persuasive and whether the government has proved the charged offense beyond a reasonable doubt.
Answer D: Whether the judge should have admitted the insurance evidence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The judge decides admissibility and permitted purpose. The jury decides what weight to give the evidence and whether the party with the burden has proved the required facts and elements.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B confuse admissibility with conclusive proof. D is wrong because the jury does not review the judge's admissibility ruling. B: A and B confuse admissibility with conclusive proof. D is wrong because the jury does not review the judge's admissibility ruling. D: D is wrong because the jury does not review the judge's admissibility ruling.
