


BARMATRIX Q#: 22648
Outline_code: 44040400
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Privileges and Immunities of Citizens
Question: State A was a small state surrounded by three more prosperous states with better standards of living. Because of this, the majority of high- wage workers in State A were residents of the other states. In order to encourage more “high- wage” workers to live in the state, State A passed a law requiring that any business in State A classified as a “high- wage workplace” had to have at least 50 percent of its employees be State A residents. If the business employed less than 50 percent of State A residents, it was required to pay into the State A state beautification program, which was intended to build more parks and amenities to attract “high- wage” residents to State A. The business's payment was determined by how many out- of- state employees over 50 percent the company employed. A State B resident was fired from a “high- wage” State A business because the business wanted to avoid the beautification program payment. The man sued State A, arguing that the law was unconstitutional. Is the law constitutional?
Answer A: A: Yes, because State A has a compelling interest in the employment of its citizens.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the law only affects “highwage workplaces.”
Answer C: C: No, because it discriminates against out- of- state residents.
Answer D: D: No, because it is not rationally related to State A's unemployment crisis.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the Privileges and Immunities Clause, a state cannot engage in economic discrimination against out- of- state residents. Here, the law discriminates against out- of- state residents' right to pursue a livelihood. Such a law targeting out- of- state employment will be struck down unless the state can show that out- of- state residents are the cause of an employment problem that the law is designed to redress. Within the question, there is no indication that the state has an unemployment problem that the law is attempting to redress. Specifically, the facts show the law is actually trying to encourage state residents to move to the state by discriminating against their employment in the state. This is unlikely to pass constitutional muster under these circumstances. A is incorrect because the law is unlikely to pass constitutional muster under these circumstances. B is incorrect because the fact that this only affects “high- wage workplaces” would not make it constitutional. D is incorrect because a discriminatory law like this will only be upheld if the discrimination is closely related to a substantial governmental interest. Consequently, rational basis analysis would not be proper.
