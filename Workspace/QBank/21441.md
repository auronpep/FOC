Question: Paul retained scientist Martha only to help his lawyer prepare for trial. Paul does not plan to call Martha as a witness. The opposing party can hire its own scientist to inspect the same machine but wants Martha's opinions because they are cheaper to obtain. Should the court allow discovery of Martha's opinions?
Answer A: Yes, because all expert opinions are discoverable once litigation begins.
Answer B: No, absent exceptional circumstances making it impracticable to obtain facts or opinions on the same subject by other means.
Answer C: Yes, because Martha is not a lawyer and therefore cannot create protected work product.
Answer D: No, because consulting experts are protected by attorney-client privilege rather than trial-preparation rules.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Facts known or opinions held by a non-testifying expert retained in anticipation of litigation are ordinarily not discoverable except on exceptional circumstances. The ability to hire another expert cuts against discovery. The decisive axis is consulting expert protection.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This ignores the special rule for non-testifying trial-preparation experts. C: A is wrong because This ignores the special rule for non-testifying trial-preparation experts. B is wrong because Correct answer C is wrong because The protection does not depend on the expert being a lawyer. D is wrong because The protection is the trial-preparation expert rule, not attorney-client privilege. D: D is wrong because The protection is the trial-preparation expert rule, not attorney-client privilege.
