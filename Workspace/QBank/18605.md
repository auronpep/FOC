Question: Sarah contracted with Peter to sell Peter a wagon. In return, Peter promised Sarah that he would pay Sarah's son John $3,000 for school expenses. John knew nothing about the contract when it was made and gave no consideration to Peter. Sarah delivered the wagon, but Peter refused to pay John. If John sues Peter, what is Peter's best defense?
Answer A: Peter has a complete defense because John gave no consideration directly to Peter.
Answer B: Peter has a complete defense because John did not know of the contract when it was formed.
Answer C: Peter has a complete defense because family beneficiaries are always incidental beneficiaries.
Answer D: Peter has no defense on those facts because consideration between Sarah and Peter can support John's intended-beneficiary right.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A beneficiary need not give consideration to the promisor. If the contract between Sarah and Peter was supported by consideration and Peter promised direct payment to John, John may enforce as an intended beneficiary once the route is established.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because consideration from the beneficiary is not required. B: B is wrong because awareness at formation is not required for intended status. C: C is wrong because family status does not automatically make the benefit incidental; direct payment to John shows intended benefit.
