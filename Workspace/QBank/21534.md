MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Defamation
Question: The newspaper moved to dismiss the minister's action on the grounds that his complaint contained no allegation of damage. Which of the following additional facts or inferences, if it were the only one true, would be most helpful to the minister in opposing the motion to dismiss?
Answer A: A: Leprosy is a loathsome disease.
Answer B: B: The minister was so upset upon
Answer C: C: Editors of the newspaper disliked the minister.
Answer D: D: The minister is neither a public official nor a public figure.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: In general, the plaintiff in a defamation case must allege and prove special damages to his or her reputation that occurred because of the defendant's statements. However, if the defendant accuses the plaintiff of having a “loathsome” disease (such as leprosy, venereal diseases, or AIDS), the plaintiff is not required to show damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: Consequently, B, C, and D are incorrect.
