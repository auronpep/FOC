MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Larceny by trick
Question: Carla goes to an electronics store and selects a $1,500 laptop. She tells the sales clerk she will pay with a credit card, hands over a credit card she knows belongs to someone else, and signs the fraudulent receipt. The clerk bags the laptop and hands it to Carla. The transaction is processed as a completed sale. The store's standard policy is that goods are sold and title passes upon completion of the payment transaction at the register. The fraudulent charge is later reversed. Of which crime is Carla most likely guilty?
Answer A: Larceny by trick, because Carla used fraud to obtain the laptop from the store clerk
Answer B: Embezzlement, because Carla was entrusted with the laptop in a commercial transaction
Answer C: False pretenses, because Carla obtained title to the laptop by a false representation of present fact — her authorization to use the credit card
Answer D: Larceny, because Carla permanently deprived the store of the laptop without authorization
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: False pretenses requires obtaining title to property by a knowingly false representation of a present material fact with intent to defraud. Carla represented that she was authorized to use the credit card — a present fact about her authorization status — which was false. The store, intending to complete a sale, transferred title (not just possession) to Carla upon completion of the transaction. Because title passed as a result of Carla's fraudulent misrepresentation of present fact (her claimed authorization), all elements of false pretenses are satisfied. This is false pretenses, not larceny by trick, because title transferred.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Larceny by trick applies when the owner, induced by fraud, transfers only possession (not title) to the defendant. Here, the store's policy is that title passes upon completion of the purchase transaction. The clerk processed the sale as complete and transferred title — the store intended to sell the laptop outright, not merely lend or transfer it temporarily. When the victim intends to and does transfer title (even if induced by fraud), the correct charge is false pretenses. B: Embezzlement requires that the initial possession be genuinely lawful, typically through an employment or fiduciary relationship. Carla's possession was obtained by fraud from the outset. Fraud vitiates the apparent lawfulness of the delivery. Moreover, the store intended to complete a sale and transfer title, not to entrust the laptop to Carla for a limited custodial purpose. Embezzlement does not apply. D: The mechanism by which Carla obtained the laptop was fraud-induced transfer of title by the owner, not a trespassory taking. The store willingly (though mistakenly, due to fraud) transferred title. When property is obtained through fraudulent inducement of an ownership transfer, the specific crime is false pretenses (title passed) or larceny by trick (possession only passed). Generic "larceny" is imprecise and incorrect where the taking was not a classic trespassory snatch but rather a fraud-induced transfer by the victim.
lb_rule_reference: The distinction between false pretenses and larceny by trick turns on the owner's intent: if the owner intended to transfer title (even if induced by fraud), false pretenses applies. If the owner intended to pass only possession pending payment (such as delivering goods on credit before full payment), title does not pass and the crime is larceny by trick.
