Question: In federal court in the District of Bethany, Noah sues Adam over a collision near the city courthouse. Adam argues that Noah failed to prove that the courthouse sits inside Bethany City. The courthouse's location inside Bethany City is generally known throughout the district. May the court judicially notice that fact?
Answer A: No, because local geography can never be judicially noticed.
Answer B: No, because the fact must be proved by a surveyor.
Answer C: Yes, but only if both parties stipulate to the courthouse's location.
Answer D: Yes, because the fact is generally known within the trial court's territorial jurisdiction.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A court may judicially notice an adjudicative fact that is generally known within the trial court's territorial jurisdiction. The courthouse's location within the city is the type of local geographic fact that may qualify.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because some local geographic facts may be noticed. B: B is wrong because judicial notice can eliminate the need for a surveyor when the fact qualifies. C: C is wrong because party stipulation is not required for a qualifying noticed fact.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 201(b)(1) / generally known within territorial jurisdiction
