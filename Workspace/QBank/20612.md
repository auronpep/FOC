Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Commerce power
Question: Congress limits the amount of a crop that commercial growers may produce for their own use, after finding that home consumption by many growers reduces demand in the national market. A grower argues the law cannot apply to him because he consumes the crop in-state and sells none of it. What is the strongest argument for congressional power?
Answer A: Congress may aggregate intrastate economic production because the class of activity substantially affects the interstate market
Answer B: Congress may regulate only if this grower's individual crop alone changes national prices
Answer C: Congress lacks power because anything consumed at home is never economic
Answer D: Congress lacks power unless the crop crosses a state border after harvest
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The activity is economic production of a commodity, and aggregate home consumption can affect national market demand.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The substantial-effects analysis can consider the regulated class in the aggregate for economic activity. C: Growing a commodity can be economic production even if the grower consumes it. D: The substantial-effects category reaches some intrastate economic activity.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may regulate intrastate economic activity when, in the aggregate, that class of activity substantially affects interstate commerce.
