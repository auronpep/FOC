Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Martha contracted with Levi for a loan to build a small inn. Levi breached by refusing to fund the loan. Martha's expected profits from the inn are too speculative. Before the breach, Martha reasonably spent $12,000 on architectural plans and demolished an old storage shed worth $6,000 to prepare the site. The demolition increased the value of the land by $2,000. What reliance recovery best avoids overcompensation?
Answer A: $12,000, because architectural plans are the only cash expenditure.
Answer B: $18,000, because Martha spent $12,000 and lost a $6,000 shed.
Answer C: $16,000, because the $12,000 plans and $6,000 shed loss are reduced by the $2,000 land-value increase.
Answer D: The speculative profits the inn might have earned, because the breach caused the project to fail.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages may include reasonable expenditures and other losses incurred in reliance, but the award should avoid overcompensation. Martha's reliance loss is $12,000 for plans plus $6,000 for the shed, reduced by the $2,000 value increase she retained, for $16,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reliance can include noncash loss caused by preparing for the contract. B: B is wrong because it ignores the retained $2,000 value increase. D: D is wrong because speculative profits are not recoverable merely because the project failed.
