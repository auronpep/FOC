Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Joseph agreed in a signed writing to sell Esther “the black mare named Star” for a stated price. Before the writing was signed, Joseph orally told Esther that she could instead choose a white mare named Pearl. When Joseph delivers Star, Esther offers the prior oral statement to show she was entitled to Pearl. Is the prior oral statement admissible?
Answer A: Yes, because it was made by one of the contracting parties.
Answer B: Yes, because the writing does not say that Esther could not choose another horse.
Answer C: No, but only because horses are not goods.
Answer D: No, because the oral option contradicts the express written term identifying the horse to be sold.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A prior oral term cannot be used to contradict an express term in a final writing. The writing specifically identifies the black mare Star; an oral option to select Pearl would contradict that term.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because party source does not overcome the parol evidence rule. B: B is wrong because the written identification of Star is itself inconsistent with a right to choose Pearl. C: C is wrong because horses are goods; the issue is contradiction, not source-law exclusion.
