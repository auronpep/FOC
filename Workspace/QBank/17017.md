Question: A federal diversity action concerns whether Elijah, missing for nine years, was alive when a trust payment came due. State X law creates a rebuttable presumption of death after seven years of unexplained absence. The trustee offers proof of the absence. Opposing counsel offers a dated photograph and testimony that Elijah was seen alive five years after he disappeared. What is the effect of the opposing evidence?
Answer A: It shifts the burden of persuasion to the trustee to disprove the photograph.
Answer B: It creates a fact issue for the jury under the rebuttable presumption; the presumption does not conclusively establish death.
Answer C: It is admissible only to impeach the trustee's witness, not on whether Elijah was alive.
Answer D: It is inadmissible unless the entire photograph album is introduced under the rule of completeness.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A rebuttable presumption of death may be met with contrary evidence. When evidence suggests the missing person was alive during the period, the jury decides the fact under the applicable state-law presumption rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the contrary evidence does not create a special burden to disprove the photograph. C: C is wrong because the evidence goes directly to the presumed fact. D: D is wrong because completeness of an album is not required to use a relevant dated photograph.
