Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Abigail contracted to buy 50 identical pumps from Jonah for $1,000 each. Abigail wrongfully rejected them. Jonah privately resold the pumps for $930 each but gave Abigail no notice of his intent to resell. At the time and place for tender, the market price was $940 per pump. Jonah incurred $600 in reasonable incidental damages. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Jonah loses all damages because he failed to give notice of the private resale.
Answer B: Jonah may recover the full $50,000 contract price because Abigail wrongfully rejected the pumps.
Answer C: Jonah may not use the private-resale measure, but he may recover market damages if proved.
Answer D: Jonah must account to Abigail for any amount received on the resale.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Failure to give reasonable notice of a private resale prevents use of the resale-damages measure under the resale rule, but it does not necessarily eliminate all seller remedies. Jonah may still recover market damages under the seller market-damages provision: contract price minus market price, plus incidentals, less saved expenses if applicable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a defective resale route does not erase all other seller remedies. B: B is wrong because action for the price is limited and is not automatic upon wrongful rejection of resellable goods. D: D is wrong because the seller is not automatically accountable to the defaulting buyer for ordinary resale proceeds in this posture; the key issue is the proper remedy route.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-706; UCC §2-708
