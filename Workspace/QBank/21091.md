Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: Joseph and Miriam orally agree that Joseph will sell Miriam 900 measures of grain for $9,000. They shake hands and agree on all terms, but no writing is signed. Before delivery, grain prices rise and Joseph refuses to perform. Miriam sues for breach. Joseph asserts the Statute of Frauds. Which statement best describes the effect of the Statute of Frauds if no exception applies?
Answer A: The parties may have reached an oral agreement, but the agreement is unenforceable against Joseph in an action for breach because the goods Statute of Frauds is not satisfied.
Answer B: No contract could have been formed because contracts for goods over $500 can be formed only by signed writing.
Answer C: The agreement is enforceable because a handshake proves mutual assent.
Answer D: The agreement is void for illegality, so neither party may use it for any purpose.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Statute of Frauds is an enforceability defense. The parties may have assented to an oral goods contract, but absent a sufficient writing or UCC exception, the contract is not enforceable against the party asserting the defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the SOF does not mean assent can occur only through a writing. C: C is wrong because mutual assent alone does not defeat an applicable SOF defense. D: D is wrong because the problem is unenforceability under the SOF, not illegality or voidness.
