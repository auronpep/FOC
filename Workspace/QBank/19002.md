MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A doctor failed to disclose a serious, common risk of a nonemergency procedure. The risk occurred. The patient credibly testifies that she would have declined the procedure if told of the risk, and a reasonable patient would have considered the risk important. What is the strongest informed-consent analysis?
Answer A: The patient has a viable claim because the undisclosed risk was material and disclosure would have affected the decision
Answer B: The doctor wins because informed consent requires disclosure only of risks that are certain to occur
Answer C: The doctor wins because the technical procedure was performed skillfully
Answer D: The patient wins automatically whenever any undisclosed risk occurs
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The risk was serious and common enough to be material, and the facts supply causation through the decision to decline.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Material risks need not be certain; they must be important to the decision. C: Informed consent is a disclosure claim distinct from negligent treatment performance. D: The risk must be material and causally tied to the patient's decision; not every undisclosed risk qualifies.
lb_rule_reference: Informed consent focuses on undisclosed material risks and causation, including whether disclosure would have changed the patient's decision.
