Question: In a federal civil trial, Jacob testifies for the defendant. The plaintiff seeks to impeach Jacob with a fraud conviction. Jacob was convicted 14 years ago and released from confinement 12 years ago. The plaintiff gave no written notice before trial. Jacob objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the conviction because fraud is always admissible under Rule 609(a)(2).
Answer B: Admit the conviction if ordinary Rule 403 balancing favors admission.
Answer C: Exclude the conviction because more than 10 years have passed since conviction or release, and the special Rule 609(b) requirements, including written notice, are not satisfied.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction only if Jacob is a criminal defendant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 609(b) applies when more than 10 years have passed since the conviction or release from confinement, whichever is later. Such convictions are admissible only if probative value, supported by specific facts and circumstances, substantially outweighs prejudicial effect, and the proponent gives reasonable written notice. No notice was given.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the 10-year limit applies even to otherwise qualifying convictions. B: B is wrong because ordinary Rule 403 is not the Rule 609(b) standard. D: D is wrong because the 10-year limit is not limited to criminal defendants.
