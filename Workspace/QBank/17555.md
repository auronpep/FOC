Question: In a federal accounting-malpractice trial, Daniel asks that all witnesses be excluded. Leah, the defendant's forensic-accounting expert, will testify later. Defense counsel shows that Leah must hear the plaintiff's accounting witnesses to help counsel understand the calculations and prepare cross-examination. Daniel objects that Leah is still a witness. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude Leah because every witness must be excluded once a party requests sequestration.
Answer B: Permit Leah to remain if the court finds her presence essential to presenting the defense.
Answer C: Permit Leah to remain only if she promises not to testify.
Answer D: Exclude Leah unless Daniel first calls all of his witnesses.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The witness-exclusion rule does not authorize excluding a person whose presence a party shows to be essential to presenting the claim or defense. An expert needed to assist counsel may qualify if the necessary showing is made.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A ignores the essential-person exception. C is wrong because an essential person may still testify. D confuses sequestration with order of proof.
