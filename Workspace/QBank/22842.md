Outline_code: 95070101
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Complaint
Question: A tuna company sued a movie studio for copyright violations after the movie studio made a cartoon starring a shark that looked very much like the company's shark mascot. The company brought suit in federal court in State A. The company was incorporated in State B and had large factories in State C and D, although its corporate leadership all lived in State E. Most of the company workers were State E citizens working in State D. The movie studio was run as a general partnership incorporated in State A. The studio had three lead partners, who were all citizens of State E, and its principal place of business was in State B. The company claimed $75,000 in damages. The studio moved the State A federal court to dismiss for lack of subject- matter jurisdiction. Should the State A federal court grant the studio's motion to dismiss?
Answer A: A: Yes, because there is no diversity between the company and the studio.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the company did not claim damages in excess of $75,000.
Answer C: C: No, because the requirements for subject- matter jurisdiction have been met.
Answer D: D: No, because the studio's citizenship is based on the citizenships of the three lead partners.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Because copyright is a question of federal law, the State A federal court has subject- matter jurisdiction over the suit through federal question jurisdiction. Consequently, the citizenship of the parties and the amount of damages claimed are irrelevant. Thus, A, B, and D are incorrect.
