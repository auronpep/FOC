Question: The President enters an executive agreement with a foreign nation settling claims held by that nation's state-owned bank against American companies. The agreement directs that the claims be resolved in a federal claims process. A state statute, enacted earlier, allows the same bank to sue immediately in state court and prohibits transfer to any federal claims process. An American company argues that the executive agreement preempts the state statute. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The executive agreement can never preempt state law because it was not ratified by the Senate as a treaty
Answer B: The executive agreement may preempt conflicting state law if it is a valid exercise of federal foreign-affairs authority
Answer C: The executive agreement automatically has the same domestic status as any self-executing Article II treaty
Answer D: The state statute controls because claims filed in state court are always governed only by state law
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal foreign-affairs authority can support executive agreements that displace conflicting state law. The state cannot frustrate a valid federal settlement mechanism in this area.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Valid executive agreements can have preemptive effect over conflicting state law in foreign-affairs contexts. C: Executive agreements can preempt state law in proper circumstances, but they are not identical to Article II treaties for every domestic-law purpose. D: The Supremacy Clause can require state courts to yield to valid federal law, including qualifying executive agreements.
lb_rule_reference: Valid federal executive agreements can preempt conflicting state law, but they do not have the same status as Article II treaties for every purpose.
