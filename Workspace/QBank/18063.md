Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Burglary
Question: Three months before a planned burglary, Nora provided her co-conspirators with detailed architectural plans of the target building that she had obtained from her work. Two weeks before the planned burglary, Nora told her co-conspirators by phone that she was withdrawing and wanted nothing more to do with the plan. She did not attempt to retrieve the plans or to warn police. Her co-conspirators used Nora's architectural plans to execute the burglary successfully. Nora is charged as an accomplice to burglary. Is Nora's withdrawal effective to defeat accomplice liability?
Answer A: Yes, because Nora communicated her withdrawal to her co-conspirators before the burglary occurred
Answer B: No, because Nora did not neutralize her prior assistance before the crime was committed
Answer C: Yes, because Nora's role was completed before the crime occurred and she had no duty to act further
Answer D: No, but only because Nora failed to notify the police
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Effective withdrawal from complicity requires the accomplice to do all that is reasonably possible to neutralize previously furnished assistance — not merely to announce departure from the scheme. Nora provided the architectural plans, which were critical to the burglary. Her phone call repudiating involvement did nothing to recover the plans or render them useless. Because the plans remained in the co-conspirators' hands and were used in the burglary, Nora's prior assistance continued to operate. She retained accomplice liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: For accomplice withdrawal, communication alone is insufficient when prior assistance — here, the architectural plans — remains available to the principals. Nora's withdrawal must include neutralizing the effect of that aid. She did not retrieve the plans or render them useless, so her prior contribution continued to facilitate the crime. C: The whole point of the neutralization requirement is that a completed contribution can continue to assist the crime. Providing the plans is not a point of no return that relieves Nora of responsibility — it is precisely the kind of prior assistance that must be neutralized for withdrawal to succeed. D: While notifying police can sometimes satisfy the withdrawal requirement (as an alternative means of preventing the crime), the primary reason Nora's withdrawal fails is that she did not neutralize her prior aid. Police notification would be relevant only if it actually prevented the crime or was a substitute for recovering the plans. On these facts, the plans were used, and neither neutralization nor prevention occurred.
lb_rule_reference: To effectively withdraw from complicity, the accomplice must (1) repudiate prior aid, (2) do all that is reasonably possible to neutralize the effect of prior assistance, and (3) do so before the crime becomes unstoppable; notification to co-perpetrators alone is insufficient if prior assistance remains in effect
