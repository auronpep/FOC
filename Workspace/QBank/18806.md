


BARMATRIX Q#: 18806
Outline_code: 44040302
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Establishing discrimination
Question: The state enacts legislation providing that participation in certain elections is open only to persons who are able to pass a simple test of reading and writing in the English language. The constitutionality of the statute has been challenged by a litigant who objects to the literacy requirement. If that challenge is successful, it will probably be because the requirement violates
Answer A: A: the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment.
Answer B: B: the “race, color, or previous condition of servitude” clause of the Fifteenth Amendment.
Answer C: C: freedom of the press under the First Amendment.
Answer D: D: the Twenty-Sixth Amendment (granting the right to vote to persons 18 years of age and above).
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Equal Protection Clause prohibits invidious discrimination. Although a literacy requirement in a voting statute is not necessarily invidious, A is the only reason listed that could result in a judgment that the statute is unconstitutional.
Wrong Answer Explanations: Literacy tests have sometimes been found to be a tool of racial discrimination, but such a finding generally leads to the conclusion that there has been a denial of equal protection rather than a violation of the somewhat narrower prohibitions of the Fifteenth Amendment. B is therefore incorrect. C is incorrect because the literacy requirement does not prohibit or otherwise regulate the written use of foreign languages. D is incorrect because the Twenty-Sixth Amendment prohibits only discrimination in the franchise based on age.
