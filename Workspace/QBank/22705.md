


BARMATRIX Q#: 22705
Outline_code: 43020101
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Judicial Review
Outline: Constitutional basis
Question: A student passed his state's licensing examination and petitioned the state Board of Medical Licensing Examiners for a license to practice medicine. Solely because of an arrest by the city police, the Board refused to issue him a license. When the student was brought before a judge for arraignment after the arrest, the court dismissed the charge with the consent of the public prosecutor. Because of the Board's refusal to license him, the student sued the Board in state court and was unsuccessful. The student sued the Board in a federal court for an order directing the Board to license him. Should the federal court hear the student's claim?
Answer A: A: No, because a license to practice medicine is a privilege rather than a right.
Answer B: B: No, because the claim does not involve a federal question.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the Board's action was arbitrary and capricious.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the Board's decision deprived the student of property without due process.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Whether it is classified as a right or a privilege, a license to practice medicine is an entitlement of which a person cannot be deprived without due process. A is therefore incorrect. Since the denial of this entitlement was based on an arrest that did not involve a hearing, it has been accomplished without due process and thus violates the student's constitutional right. B is incorrect because the federal courts have jurisdiction to adjudicate federal questions, and an asserted violation of rights under the United States Constitution obviously raises a federal question. The deprivation of a property interest without a hearing violates the Due Process requirement whether or not the action was arbitrary or capricious. C is therefore incorrect.
