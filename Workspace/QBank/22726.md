Outline_code: 42010100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Legislative Branch
Question: A professor was tenured at a state university. Tenure guaranteed the professor lifetime employment. The professor went on a well- known radio show and stated some of his personal views, which many people found extremely offensive. The university president and board of trustees had an emergency meeting and voted to fire the professor. The next day, one of the trustees informed the professor that his tenure was revoked and he was fired for cause. The professor sued the university, arguing that the manner of his firing was unlawful. Should the court rule in the professor's favor?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the professor received no pre- termination hearing.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the professor's free speech rights have been violated.
Answer C: C: No, because the university had the right to determine the conditions of the professor's employment.
Answer D: D: No, because state employment is a privilege and not a right.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Here, because the state had given the professor tenure and promised him lifetime employment, the state had to give the professor a pre- termination hearing before taking that promise away. Not giving the professor a hearing to present evidence regarding the situation violated his due process rights. Thus, firing him without any hearing at all was unlawful. B is incorrect because the question says nothing about what the professor said. It's possible whatever it was did not implicate Free Speech at all. C and D are incorrect because the state would be required to give the professor a pre- termination hearing under the circumstances.
