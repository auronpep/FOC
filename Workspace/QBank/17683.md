Question: Adam privately consulted lawyer Ruth about potential civil liability from a business transaction. Adam died two months later. In a later federal investigation, the government subpoenas Ruth to disclose Adam's confidential communications, arguing that Adam can no longer be harmed by disclosure. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the privilege objection, because attorney-client privilege generally survives the client's death.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because death automatically terminates all testimonial privileges.
Answer C: Overrule the objection if the communications are important to the investigation.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Adam's estate is a party to the proceeding.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege generally survives the client's death. Importance or need alone does not defeat the privilege. The decisive axis is privilege survival, not witness availability or investigative need.
