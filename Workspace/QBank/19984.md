Question: After a bus crash, defense counsel interviewed three passengers and prepared memoranda summarizing their factual statements. The memoranda were prepared after litigation was reasonably anticipated. Before depositions could be taken, the passengers died in an unrelated accident. The plaintiff moves to compel the factual portions of the memoranda and does not seek counsel's mental impressions or legal theories. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny production entirely because anything prepared by counsel after an accident is absolutely protected.
Answer B: Order production of the entire memoranda, including counsel's legal theories, because the witnesses are unavailable.
Answer C: Deny production because work product and attorney-client privilege are identical protections.
Answer D: Order production of the factual portions if the plaintiff shows substantial need and cannot obtain their equivalent without undue hardship, while protecting opinion work product.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Ordinary work product prepared in anticipation of litigation may be discoverable on a showing of substantial need and inability to obtain the substantial equivalent without undue hardship. Opinion work product receives stronger protection. The decisive axis is ordinary factual work product versus opinion work product.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This overprotects ordinary work product by treating it as absolute. B: B is wrong because This underprotects opinion work product. Unavailable witnesses may justify factual production, not counsel's mental impressions. C: C is wrong because Attorney-client privilege and work product protect different objects and have different discovery standards.
lb_rule_reference: FRCP 26(b)(3) / ordinary work product
lb_student_diagnostic: Student collapses attorney-client privilege, ordinary work product, and opinion work product.
