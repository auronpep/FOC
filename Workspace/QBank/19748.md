MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Nuisance
Question: A contractor stored an uncovered irrigation pump on a vacant lot beside an apartment complex. Children had been seen climbing on the pump after school, and the contractor's supervisor knew it. The pump's belt was exposed, and a simple locked mesh cover would have cost little. A nine-year-old climbed on the pump, started it by pressing a large switch, and injured his hand in the belt. Is the child likely to prevail in a negligence claim against the contractor based on the attractive nuisance doctrine?
Answer A: No, because trespassers are owed no duty other than to avoid intentional injury
Answer B: No, because the pump was visible and therefore open and obvious
Answer C: Yes, because the contractor knew children were likely to encounter a dangerous artificial condition and failed to take a low-cost precaution
Answer D: Yes, but only because the child became an invitee when he entered from the apartment complex
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts satisfy the child-trespasser exception: foreseeable child trespass, artificial condition, serious risk, inadequate child appreciation, and a slight burden to guard the pump.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Child trespassers are a special category. The known artificial condition, foreseeable child presence, serious hidden mechanical danger, and cheap safeguard trigger the attractive nuisance analysis. B: Attractive nuisance asks whether the child appreciates the danger, not merely whether the object can be seen. A visible pump can conceal a serious mechanical risk to a child. D: Nothing made the child an invitee of the contractor. Liability, if any, rests on the child-trespasser attractive nuisance doctrine, not invitee status.
lb_rule_reference: A land possessor may owe a duty to child trespassers for an artificial condition when children are likely to trespass, the condition poses an unreasonable serious risk, the child does not appreciate it, and reasonable safeguards are slight compared with the risk.
