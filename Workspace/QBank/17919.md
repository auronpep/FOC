MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Custom
Question: A warehouse followed the local industry custom of leaving forklift charging bays without low guard rails. Evidence showed that a rail would cost little, would not interfere with work, and would prevent carts from rolling into the charging area. A visitor was injured when a cart rolled into the bay and knocked over a charger. The visitor sues the warehouse for negligence. What effect should the industry custom have on breach in the visitor's negligence claim?
Answer A: The custom conclusively proves the warehouse used reasonable care
Answer B: The custom is relevant evidence, but the factfinder may still find breach if reasonable care required guards
Answer C: The custom is inadmissible and must be ignored in every negligence case
Answer D: The custom automatically proves breach because the visitor was injured
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Industry practice informs breach but does not set the final legal standard. Cheap, effective guard rails could make the customary practice unreasonable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Custom is relevant but not conclusive. A factfinder may find the entire custom unreasonable when a cheap, effective precaution was available. C: Custom is often relevant breach evidence. The problem is treating it as controlling, not considering it at all. D: Injury alone does not prove breach. The custom, risk, and burden of precautions are evidence for the factfinder.
lb_rule_reference: Industry custom is relevant evidence of reasonable care but is not conclusive when the custom itself may be unreasonable.
