Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Commerce power
Question: A state requires every interstate freight train passing through the state to stop at each county line for a manual inspection. The rule applies to all trains, but it causes multi-hour national shipping delays. The state identifies only a minor recordkeeping benefit that could be achieved by electronic reporting. What is the likely DCC result?
Answer A: The law is automatically valid because it treats in-state and out-of-state trains the same
Answer B: The law likely fails because the interstate burden is clearly excessive compared with the slight benefit
Answer C: The law is facially discriminatory because trains cross state lines
Answer D: The law is saved because any recordkeeping benefit defeats DCC scrutiny
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Pike balancing can invalidate a nondiscriminatory law when heavy interstate burdens dwarf local benefits.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Evenhanded laws can fail if the interstate burden is clearly excessive. C: Crossing state lines is not the same as a law discriminating against out-of-state commerce. D: Pike weighs burden against benefit; a tiny benefit does not automatically save a severe burden.
lb_rule_reference: Evenhanded laws can still fail Pike balancing if their burdens on interstate commerce are clearly excessive compared with slight or speculative local benefits.
