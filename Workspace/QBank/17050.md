Outline: Privileges and Immunities Clause of Article IV, Section 2
Question: A state requires resident citizens to pay a normal filing fee to bring contract claims in state court but requires citizens of other states to pay a much larger surcharge for the same claims. The state says the surcharge raises revenue and offers no evidence that nonresident suits cost more. What is the strongest Article IV argument?
Answer A: Access to courts for important civil claims is a protected privilege, and the nonresident surcharge lacks a substantial reason and fit
Answer B: Court access is never protected by Article IV
Answer C: The surcharge is valid because revenue raising always justifies nonresident discrimination
Answer D: The surcharge is invalid only if the plaintiff is an out-of-state corporation
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule discriminates against nonresident citizens in court access without a supported justification.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Discriminatory court access burdens can implicate Article IV. C: A revenue goal alone does not explain why nonresident citizens should pay a discriminatory surcharge. D: Corporations do not invoke Article IV as citizens; natural citizens do.
lb_rule_reference: Access to state courts for important civil claims is a fundamental privilege protected by Article IV against discrimination against nonresident citizens absent substantial reason and close fit.
