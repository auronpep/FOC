Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Miriam sues Solomon's market after slipping near the entrance at noon. Miriam offers a certified weather report showing that heavy snow fell in the area between 10:30 and 11:30 that morning. No witness saw snow or slush inside the entrance before Miriam fell. Solomon objects that the weather report is too weak to prove the floor was wet. What should the court do?
Answer A: Exclude the report, because no witness saw snow inside the market before Miriam fell.
Answer B: Admit the report, because it has some tendency to make the presence of tracked-in moisture more probable.
Answer C: Exclude the report, because weather records are admissible only if the weather is the ultimate issue.
Answer D: Admit the report only if Miriam proves that a customer actually tracked snow into the entrance.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The weather report is circumstantial but probative. It does not prove the floor was wet by itself, but it makes tracked-in moisture more probable. The absence of a direct eyewitness affects weight, not admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because direct proof is not required. C: C is wrong because evidence can prove intermediate or evidentiary facts of consequence, not only ultimate issues. D: D is wrong because the report itself may contribute to the chain of proof; Miriam need not first prove the final inference.
