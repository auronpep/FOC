Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Levi hired Miriam to restore a mural for $20,000 in a signed writing. The writing was complete and exclusive. Two weeks after signing, Levi orally asked Miriam to add a second wall for an additional $5,000. Miriam completed the second wall and seeks the additional payment. Levi argues that the parol evidence rule bars evidence of the later oral request. What is the best response? What is the best response?
Answer A: The parol evidence rule does not bar evidence of a subsequent oral modification; the later request must be analyzed under modification and related rules.
Answer B: The parol evidence rule bars the later oral request because the writing was complete and exclusive.
Answer C: The parol evidence rule bars the evidence unless the second wall was mentioned during negotiations before signing.
Answer D: The parol evidence rule admits the later request automatically and proves that Levi owes the additional $5,000.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The parol evidence rule applies to prior or contemporaneous agreements offered to vary a final writing. A later oral request occurs after the writing and is not barred by PER, though it may face separate modification, consideration, waiver, or no-oral-modification issues.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because complete integration does not make PER apply to later modifications. C: C is wrong because pre-signing mention would create a PER problem, not solve it. D: D is wrong because escaping PER does not automatically prove enforceability of the later modification.
