Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Ezra contracted to finish Deborah's rental cottage by June 1 for $70,000. Ezra finished on July 1 without excuse. The fair rental value of the cottage for June was $3,000. Deborah also claims $7,000 for a special retreat she hoped to host there, but she never told Ezra about that planned retreat when they contracted. What amount is Deborah most clearly entitled to recover for the delay?
Answer A: $3,000, the ordinary loss-of-use value for the month of delay.
Answer B: $7,000, because Deborah actually hoped to earn that amount from the retreat.
Answer C: $10,000, because all losses following breach are recoverable.
Answer D: Nothing, because Ezra eventually completed the cottage.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A delayed construction completion can support ordinary expectation damages for loss of use, such as fair rental value during the delay. The special retreat loss is a consequential loss not recoverable on these facts because Ezra had no reason to know of it when contracting.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because actual hope or planned use is not enough for undisclosed special consequential damages. C: C is wrong because contract damages do not include every loss that follows breach. D: D is wrong because late performance can still cause compensable delay damages.
lb_rule_reference: Common law expectation damages; delay/loss-of-use measure
