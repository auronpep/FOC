Question: Esther hired a cleaning company to provide “all janitorial services required for Esther's two downtown office buildings during 2026,” with an estimate of 6,000 labor hours. Midyear, Esther acquired a suburban warehouse and demanded that the cleaning company provide another 5,500 hours for that warehouse at the contract rate. Which statement is least likely to be accepted?
Answer A: The stated estimate can help identify the expected scale of the service obligation.
Answer B: The phrase “two downtown office buildings” limits the scope of the requirements-like term.
Answer C: Esther may require the additional warehouse services merely because she honestly needs them.
Answer D: Article 2 is not the direct source of the rule because the transaction is for janitorial services.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An open requirements-style commitment is still limited by source law, stated scope, and good faith. The trigger facts are the services transaction, the named two downtown buildings, the 6,000-hour estimate, and the later suburban warehouse outside the named scope. The decisive lock is term object/scope: honest need does not expand the contract to a new property that the written requirements term did not cover.
