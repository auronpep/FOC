Outline: Offer
Question: A contractor sends Deborah a document titled “Preliminary Proposal” stating, “Estimated cost to remodel your kitchen: about $40,000. This proposal is not binding until I inspect the walls and approve final specifications.” Deborah signs the proposal the same day. Before inspection, the contractor declines the job. Did Deborah's signature form a contract?
Answer A: Yes, because signing any proposal forms a contract.
Answer B: Yes, because kitchen cabinets are goods and Article 2 always forms contracts despite open terms.
Answer C: No, because the proposal objectively stated that it was not binding until inspection and final approval.
Answer D: No, but only because the $40,000 price was probably inadequate.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Objective language controls. The proposal expressly said it was preliminary and not binding until inspection and approval of final specifications. Deborah's signature did not convert that nonbinding proposal into an offer capable of immediate acceptance.
