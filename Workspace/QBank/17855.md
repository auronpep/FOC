Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Self-defense
Question: Rhonda lives alone in her house. Her estranged ex-partner, Todd, breaks in through the back door. Todd advances toward Rhonda in her kitchen shouting that he will kill her. Rhonda could safely reach the front door and escape, but instead she grabs a kitchen knife and stabs Todd, killing him. The jurisdiction follows the minority retreat rule. Rhonda is charged with murder and claims self-defense. Under the minority retreat rule, is Rhonda's self-defense claim valid?
Answer A: No, because Rhonda had a safe avenue of retreat and was required to use it
Answer B: No, because Todd had not yet physically touched Rhonda
Answer C: Yes, because the castle doctrine exempts Rhonda from any duty to retreat in her own home
Answer D: Yes, because the majority rule applies, and there is no duty to retreat anywhere
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The minority retreat rule requires retreat when retreat is safely available — but only outside the home. The castle doctrine, recognized in virtually all retreat jurisdictions, excepts a person's own dwelling: a person is never required to retreat from her home before using deadly force in self-defense. Rhonda was in her own house when faced with an explicit death threat from an intruder. Todd's advance after announcing his intent satisfies imminence and the reasonable belief standard. The castle exception eliminates the retreat duty entirely, making Rhonda's self-defense claim valid.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Even in minority (retreat) jurisdictions, the castle doctrine provides that a person need not retreat from her own home before using deadly force in self-defense. Rhonda is in her own house. The castle exception applies, and she has no duty to retreat. B: Self-defense does not require the defendant to wait to be struck. Imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm is sufficient. Todd's explicit death threat combined with his advance toward Rhonda satisfies the imminence requirement. D: The question stipulates the minority retreat rule. Answering on majority-rule grounds ignores the binding jurisdiction. The correct basis for Rhonda's acquittal is the castle exception within the minority rule — not the inapplicable majority rule.
lb_rule_reference: Under the minority retreat rule, a defendant must retreat before using deadly force if retreat is possible with complete safety — but the castle doctrine excepts the defendant's own home; the majority rule imposes no duty to retreat anywhere
