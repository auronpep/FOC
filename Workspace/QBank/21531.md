Question: Solomon received an email from his lawyer analyzing legal exposure from a product failure. Solomon forwarded the email to Jonah, a public-relations consultant, and wrote, “Use this to draft a public statement that makes us look better.” Jonah was not retained to help the lawyer provide legal advice. What is the best privilege ruling?
Answer A: The email remains privileged because Jonah agreed to keep it confidential.
Answer B: The forwarding likely waived privilege, because Jonah's role was public-relations messaging rather than necessary assistance to legal advice.
Answer C: The email remains privileged because every consultant hired during litigation is within the attorney-client privilege.
Answer D: The email is not privileged even before forwarding because lawyers' legal-risk emails are never protected.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Disclosure to a consultant preserves privilege only when the consultant is reasonably necessary to obtain or provide legal advice. Jonah was used for publicity, not for the legal consultation. The decisive axis is legal-advice necessity, not business confidentiality.
