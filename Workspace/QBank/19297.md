Question: In a federal civil trial, Daniel testifies about a warehouse accident. On cross-examination, opposing counsel asks Daniel whether he filed a false federal tax return last year. The question is offered only to attack Daniel's character for truthfulness. Daniel invokes the privilege against self-incrimination. The opposing party argues that Daniel waived the privilege by taking the stand. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Compel Daniel to answer because any witness who testifies waives all privilege as to truthfulness questions.
Answer B: Compel Daniel to answer because false tax returns are probative of truthfulness.
Answer C: Permit Daniel to invoke the privilege if the answer could incriminate him; testifying on another matter does not waive the privilege for truthfulness-only testimony.
Answer D: Permit Daniel to invoke the privilege only if the question is asked on direct examination.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 608(b) expressly provides that by testifying on another matter, a witness does not waive any privilege against self-incrimination for testimony relating only to the witness's character for truthfulness. The question may be probative of truthfulness, but a valid Fifth Amendment privilege is not defeated by the fact that Daniel testified about the accident.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because testifying on the accident does not waive all self-incrimination protection. B: B is wrong because probative value for truthfulness does not override a valid privilege. D: D is wrong because the protection is not limited to direct examination.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(b) and Fifth Amendment / no waiver by testifying on another matter
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the interaction between truthfulness cross-examination and privilege against self-incrimination.
