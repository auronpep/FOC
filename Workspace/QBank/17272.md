Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Diversity Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff, a citizen of Ohio, sues defendant, a citizen of Michigan, in federal court under diversity jurisdiction for $100,000. Defendant impleads a third-party defendant under Rule 14. The third-party defendant is also a citizen of Ohio. Plaintiff then asserts a related state-law claim for $40,000 directly against the third-party defendant. May the court exercise supplemental jurisdiction over plaintiff's claim against the third-party defendant?
Answer A: Yes, because the claim is related to the original case
Answer B: Yes, because Rule 14 impleader automatically creates jurisdiction over all related claims
Answer C: No, because Section 1367(b) bars the plaintiff's nondiverse claim against a Rule 14 party in a diversity-only case
Answer D: No, because defendants can never implead third parties in diversity cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff's claim against the impleaded Ohio party would defeat complete diversity, and Section 1367(b) blocks it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1367(b) adds a plaintiff-side statutory bar in diversity-only cases. B: Joinder and jurisdiction must be tested separately. D: Rule 14 impleader can be procedurally proper; the plaintiff's later claim has the jurisdiction problem.
lb_rule_reference: Section 1367(b) bars claims by plaintiffs against parties joined under Rule 14 when exercising jurisdiction would be inconsistent with Section 1332.
