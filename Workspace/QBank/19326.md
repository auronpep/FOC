Question: Miriam owed Daniel $8,000 for grain. Miriam later sold a wagon to Esther. As part of the wagon sale, Esther promised Miriam, “I will pay Daniel the $8,000 you owe him.” Esther then refused to pay Daniel. Daniel sued Esther on the Miriam-Esther contract. Is Daniel likely to recover?
Answer A: No, because Daniel did not furnish consideration to Esther.
Answer B: No, because only Miriam may sue Esther for breach.
Answer C: Yes, because Esther's promised performance would satisfy Miriam's debt to Daniel, making Daniel an intended creditor beneficiary.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Miriam first assigns her rights under the wagon contract to Daniel.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Daniel may recover as an intended creditor beneficiary. Esther promised Miriam to pay Daniel directly, and that performance would satisfy Miriam's obligation to Daniel. The promise gives Daniel a direct enforcement route as an intended beneficiary.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the beneficiary need not supply consideration. B: B is wrong because intended creditor beneficiaries can enforce the promise. D: D is wrong because Daniel's route is beneficiary status, not assignment.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — creditor beneficiary
