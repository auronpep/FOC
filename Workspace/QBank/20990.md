Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Identifications
Question: Minutes after a purse snatching, police stop a suspect two blocks away carrying a purse matching the victim's description. They bring the victim to the curb, where she sees the suspect handcuffed beside a patrol car. The victim had looked directly at the thief in daylight from three feet away and immediately gives a certain identification. Should the identification be suppressed as a due process violation?
Answer A: No, because although the showup was suggestive, prompt necessity and strong reliability make suppression unwarranted
Answer B: Yes, because a one-person showup is always unconstitutional
Answer C: Yes, because police must always use a station-house lineup before any witness identification
Answer D: No, because witness certainty alone makes every identification admissible
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The procedure was suggestive, but prompt on-scene showups can serve legitimate needs, and the victim's close, daylight observation plus immediate certainty supports reliability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: There is no automatic exclusion rule. Courts assess necessity and reliability under the totality. C: The Constitution does not require the least suggestive possible procedure in every case, especially during a prompt field investigation. D: Certainty is only one reliability factor. The correct analysis considers the full totality, including opportunity to view and timing.
lb_rule_reference: Due process suppression of an identification requires unnecessary suggestiveness plus a substantial likelihood of irreparable misidentification under the totality of circumstances.
