Outline: Robbery
Question: After a party, Devin honestly believes that Alex is wearing Devin's own distinctive jacket by mistake. Devin shoves Alex, grabs the jacket, and leaves. The jacket in fact belongs to Alex, but Devin's belief is sincere. Is Devin guilty of robbery for taking the jacket?
Answer A: Yes, because force used to take property always creates robbery
Answer B: Yes, because Devin's belief was mistaken
Answer C: No, because Devin honestly believed he was taking his own specific jacket
Answer D: No, because force is irrelevant to all property crimes
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Devin may be liable for assault or battery, but robbery requires a larcenous taking. His honest claim of right to the specific jacket negates intent to steal.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Robbery includes larcenous intent. An honest claim of right to specific property can negate intent to steal. B: A sincere claim of right can negate larcenous intent even if mistaken, when it concerns specific property. D: Force is highly relevant to robbery and other offenses. It does not cure the missing larcenous intent here.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant who honestly believes specific property is his own may lack intent to steal that property, even if using force can create other offenses.
