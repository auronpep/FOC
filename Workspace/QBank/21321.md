Outline: Common objections to questions
Question: In a federal fraud trial, Joseph asks Miriam, “Did Caleb sign the invoice and tell you that the numbers were false before you mailed it?” Caleb objects to the form of the question. What is the best ground for the objection?
Answer A: The question is hearsay.
Answer B: The question calls for expert opinion.
Answer C: The question lacks relevance.
Answer D: The question is compound.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The question combines multiple factual inquiries—whether Caleb signed the invoice, whether he made a statement, when he made it, and whether Miriam mailed the invoice. A compound-question objection is the most precise objection to the form.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the defect in the question as framed is not primarily hearsay; it asks several things at once. B: B is wrong because no expert opinion is requested. C: C is wrong because the facts may be relevant; the issue is that the question is compound.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / compound question
