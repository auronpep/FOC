Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Moses is charged with burglary of a warehouse. The prosecutor offers a leather glove found just inside the broken window. The glove bears the initials “M.B.” stitched inside. A coworker testifies that Moses often wore monogrammed gloves at work. Moses objects that many people could have those initials and that the prosecutor has not proved the glove is his. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the glove if the evidence is sufficient for a reasonable juror to find that it is connected to Moses; the possibility that someone else had the same initials goes to weight.
Answer B: Exclude the glove unless the prosecutor proves by a preponderance of the evidence that Moses owned it.
Answer C: Exclude the glove because initials are never a sufficient basis for identity.
Answer D: Admit the glove only if Moses admits that it belongs to him.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When relevance depends on a linking fact, the proponent need only introduce evidence sufficient to support a finding of that fact. The initials and coworker testimony can permit a reasonable juror to connect the glove to Moses. Competing explanations affect weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because conditional relevance does not require the judge to find ownership by a preponderance. C: C is wrong because initials can be part of a circumstantial foundation when combined with other evidence. D: D is wrong because direct admission by the opponent is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / FRE 401 / FRE 901
lb_student_diagnostic: Demanding conclusive identification instead of recognizing the conditional-relevance threshold.
