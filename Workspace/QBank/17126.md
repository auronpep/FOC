Question: Sarah, a merchant seller of grain, emails David: “I will sell you 500 bushels of wheat for $8 per bushel, delivery on June 1. If you want this deal, reply by Friday.” David replies before Friday, “I accept.” Sarah refuses to deliver, arguing that her email was merely a price quote. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Sarah's email was not an offer because price quotations can never be offers.
Answer B: Sarah's email was an offer because it manifested present commitment with quantity, price, delivery, and an invited acceptance.
Answer C: David's reply was ineffective because an acceptance of a goods contract must always be made by shipment.
Answer D: No contract formed because Sarah did not receive consideration for keeping the offer open until Friday.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A communication that shows present commitment and invites assent can be an offer. Sarah's message was directed to David, stated the goods, quantity, price, delivery date, and acceptance deadline, and said “I will sell.” David's timely acceptance formed a contract.
