Question: A state law taking effect next month requires online sellers to display a government-approved warning or face daily fines. A seller must redesign its site now or risk enforcement immediately after the effective date. State officials have announced that enforcement will begin on day one. Is the seller's pre-enforcement challenge ripe?
Answer A: No, because a plaintiff must always violate the law and be prosecuted first
Answer B: No, because declaratory relief is never available before enforcement
Answer C: Yes, because the credible enforcement threat and immediate compliance costs make the dispute fit for review
Answer D: Yes, solely because the seller dislikes the law
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The legal issue is concrete, enforcement is credible and imminent, and waiting imposes hardship.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Credible threat plus present compliance hardship can make pre-enforcement review ripe. B: Declaratory relief still requires a ripe controversy, but it is not categorically barred before enforcement. D: Ripeness depends on credible threat and hardship, not mere disagreement.
lb_rule_reference: Pre-enforcement review can be ripe when the plaintiff faces a credible threat of enforcement and present hardship from compliance.
