Question: A seller contracted to sell 100 standard tables to Ezekiel for $300 each. Ezekiel wrongfully repudiated. The seller resold the tables in good faith and in a commercially reasonable resale for $320 each. The seller incurred no incidental damages and saved no expenses. Ezekiel argues that he is entitled to the $2,000 profit the seller made on resale. Is Ezekiel correct?
Answer A: Yes, because any profit on resale belongs to the breaching buyer.
Answer B: Yes, because resale creates a constructive trust for the buyer.
Answer C: No, because the seller is not accountable to the defaulting buyer for profit made on a proper resale.
Answer D: No, but only because the seller could instead recover the full contract price from Ezekiel.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: In a seller resale after buyer breach, the seller is not accountable to the buyer for profit made on the resale. The resale-profit fact prevents seller damages under the contract-resale differential but does not create a buyer claim to the seller's profit.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Article 2 does not give the defaulting buyer the seller's resale profit. B: B is wrong because ordinary resale profit does not create a constructive trust for the breaching buyer. D: D is wrong because the action for price is limited and does not justify the result on these facts.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-706(6)
