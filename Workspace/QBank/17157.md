Question: A state health program excludes pregnancy-related conditions from a general disability benefit. A plaintiff brings only a federal Equal Protection claim and offers no evidence that the exclusion was adopted because of sex stereotypes. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The exclusion automatically receives intermediate scrutiny because pregnancy always equals sex classification under Equal Protection
Answer B: The constitutional Equal Protection claim does not automatically trigger intermediate scrutiny on pregnancy status alone
Answer C: Strict scrutiny applies because pregnancy involves bodily autonomy
Answer D: The exclusion is invalid per se because disability benefits must cover every condition
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim is constitutional, not statutory, and no sex-stereotyping purpose is shown.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Pregnancy classifications are not automatically sex classifications for constitutional Equal Protection purposes. C: The stated Equal Protection classification does not automatically trigger strict scrutiny. D: Equal Protection does not require every benefit program to cover every condition.
lb_rule_reference: A pregnancy classification is not automatically a sex classification for Equal Protection purposes, though statutory law or proof of sex stereotyping can change the practical analysis.
