Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff, a State A citizen, sues two State B defendants in federal court. Plaintiff claims Defendant 1 owes $45,000 on one contract and Defendant 2 owes $40,000 on a separate, unrelated contract. Neither defendant is jointly liable for the other's debt. Plaintiff argues that the total case value is $85,000. Does the amount in controversy requirement appear satisfied?
Answer A: Yes, because the total case value across all defendants exceeds $75,000
Answer B: Yes, because Plaintiff is a single plaintiff
Answer C: No, because the claims are separate liabilities against different defendants and neither claim exceeds the threshold
Answer D: No, but only because both defendants are from State B
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The amount must be satisfied against a defendant unless joint liability or a common undivided right permits aggregation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Separate claims against separately liable defendants generally cannot be added together to reach the threshold. B: One plaintiff can aggregate claims against one defendant, but separate liabilities of different defendants are not automatically aggregated. D: Complete diversity exists on these facts; the problem is amount aggregation.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff generally may not aggregate separate claims against multiple defendants to satisfy the amount in controversy unless the defendants are jointly liable or the plaintiff asserts a common undivided right.
