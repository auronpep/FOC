Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: After receiving and waiving Miranda warnings, adult suspect Joel was questioned for forty minutes. Detectives falsely said they had his fingerprints on the cash drawer. They did not threaten him, deprive him of food or sleep, promise leniency, or use force. Joel confessed. Is Joel's confession involuntary solely because detectives lied about fingerprints?
Answer A: Yes, because any police lie during interrogation makes a confession involuntary
Answer B: Yes, because Miranda warnings cannot be waived after deception begins
Answer C: No, because deception alone did not overbear Joel's will under the totality of circumstances
Answer D: No, because a confession is admissible whenever it is true
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Joel was warned, waived, questioned briefly, and faced no threats, promises, deprivation, or force. The fingerprint lie alone does not make the confession involuntary.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Deception is one factor but does not automatically overbear the will. The remaining facts show a short, warned, noncoercive interview. B: The waiver was given before questioning, and ordinary deception does not automatically invalidate waiver or voluntariness. D: Voluntariness is not decided by truthfulness. The confession is admissible because police coercion did not overbear Joel's will.
lb_rule_reference: Voluntariness is assessed under the totality of circumstances; deception is relevant but does not by itself require suppression absent coercion that overbears the will.
