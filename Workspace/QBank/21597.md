MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Solicitation
Question: After losing a business dispute, Quinn tells a friend, "I wish someone would break my rival's windows so he finally learns a lesson." The friend says nothing and leaves. Quinn never asks the friend to do anything, never offers payment, and never gives instructions. Quinn is charged with solicitation to commit vandalism. What is Quinn's strongest argument?
Answer A: Quinn is not guilty because the statement was anger and fantasy, not a request that the friend commit a crime
Answer B: Quinn is guilty because the friend heard a statement about criminal damage
Answer C: Quinn is guilty because solicitation does not require agreement
Answer D: Quinn is not guilty only because vandalism is a misdemeanor
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Solicitation requires an intentional communication seeking another person's criminal action. Quinn expressed a wish but did not ask, command, encourage, or counsel the friend to vandalize anything.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Mentioning a desired bad outcome is not the same as intentionally soliciting another person to commit that crime. C: The missing element is the solicitation act itself. The no-agreement rule matters only after a request has been communicated. D: The defect is not offense grade. The defect is absence of a request or encouragement.
lb_rule_reference: Solicitation requires a communication that asks, commands, encourages, or counsels another person to commit a crime; mere fantasy, anger, or venting is not enough.
