Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: In a federal civil rights action, Joseph claims that a city official retaliated against him. Joseph invokes a State Y statute creating a presumption that any adverse action within 30 days of a complaint was retaliatory and shifts the burden of persuasion to the defendant. The claim arises under federal law. Which rule governs the presumption's burden effect?
Answer A: State Y law governs because the events occurred in State Y.
Answer B: State Y law governs because all state presumptions control in federal court.
Answer C: The federal rule governs unless state law supplies the rule of decision for the claim or defense.
Answer D: The jury decides which presumption rule is more persuasive.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: In a federal-question case, the federal rule generally controls the effect of evidentiary presumptions. State law governs the effect of a presumption only when state law supplies the rule of decision for the claim or defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the location of the events does not by itself make state presumption law controlling. B: B is wrong because state presumptions do not always control in federal court. D: D is wrong because the source of the presumption's legal effect is a question for the court.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 301; FRE 302
