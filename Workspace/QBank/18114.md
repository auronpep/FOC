Question: In a federal breach-of-contract action, the defendant requests emails between the plaintiff and the plaintiff's lawyer analyzing whether the contract was enforceable. The plaintiff withholds the emails as attorney-client privileged. The defendant argues that enforceability is the central issue in the case. What is the strongest basis for sustaining the objection?
Answer A: The emails are protected because privileged legal communications do not become discoverable merely because they concern a relevant issue
Answer B: The emails are protected only if they were created after the complaint was filed
Answer C: The emails must be produced because they discuss the same contract at issue in the lawsuit
Answer D: The emails must be produced if the defendant agrees not to use them at trial
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Discovery covers relevant nonprivileged matter; privilege remains a separate protection for confidential lawyer-client legal communications.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Attorney-client privilege is not limited to communications created after suit begins. C: Importance and relevance do not defeat attorney-client privilege. D: A use limitation does not eliminate privilege or require production of privileged communications.
lb_rule_reference: Privileged communications are protected from discovery even when the underlying topic is relevant.
