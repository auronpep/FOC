Question: During an argument, a tenant deliberately sprayed a scented cleaning mist into a landlord's face to make him leave the apartment. The mist touched the landlord's eyes and clothing. The landlord coughed for a minute but suffered no lasting injury. The landlord sues for battery. What is the best result?
Answer A: The landlord likely prevails because the tenant intentionally caused offensive indirect contact
Answer B: The landlord loses because mist is not a solid object
Answer C: The landlord loses unless he proves permanent medical injury
Answer D: The landlord wins only if he feared the mist before it touched him
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The mist was intentionally directed at the landlord and physically contacted him. Battery is not limited to hand-to-body touching.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Substances set in motion can supply the contact element. C: Offensive contact can establish battery without lasting injury. D: Apprehension matters for assault, not for completed battery contact.
lb_rule_reference: Battery may be based on indirect contact intentionally set in motion, including particles or substances directed at the plaintiff.
