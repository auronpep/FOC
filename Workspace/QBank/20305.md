Question: Sarah contracted with Daniel to pay “the winner of Miriam's annual writing contest” $2,000. At the time Sarah and Daniel signed, no entries had been submitted and the future winner was unknown. Deborah later won the contest. Daniel refused to pay, arguing that no beneficiary existed when the contract was formed. Is Deborah barred from enforcing solely because she was unidentified at formation?
Answer A: No, because a contract may intend to benefit a future or class beneficiary even if the specific person is identified later.
Answer B: Yes, because an intended beneficiary must be identified by name when the contract is made.
Answer C: Yes, because a beneficiary's rights must vest before a contract can be validly formed.
Answer D: No, but only if Miriam assigns the contest contract to Deborah.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Deborah is not barred merely because she was unidentified at formation. The contract can intend to benefit a future member of a defined class. Vesting and enforcement timing are separate from initial intended-beneficiary status.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because naming is not always required. C: C is wrong because vesting is not a prerequisite to contract formation or intended status. D: D is wrong because Deborah's route is class beneficiary status, not assignment.
