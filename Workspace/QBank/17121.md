MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Intentional infliction of emotional distress
Question: During a staff meeting, a supervisor angrily told an employee that the employee's presentation was "lazy and embarrassing." The supervisor used a sharp tone but made no threats, did not use slurs, did not know of any unusual vulnerability, and ended the meeting after a few minutes. The employee was upset for several days. The employee sues for intentional infliction of emotional distress. Is the employee likely to prevail?
Answer A: Yes, because the supervisor intentionally said hurtful words
Answer B: Yes, because the employee was upset for several days
Answer C: No, because ordinary workplace criticism and insults usually are not extreme and outrageous
Answer D: No, because supervisors are immune from emotional-distress claims
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The supervisor's conduct was rude, but the facts do not show threats, abuse of a known vulnerability, repeated harassment, or severe distress.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: IIED requires extreme and outrageous conduct, not merely hurtful criticism. B: Distress alone does not establish IIED without extreme and outrageous conduct and severe distress. D: Supervisors can commit IIED in extreme cases. This case fails because the conduct and distress facts are insufficient.
lb_rule_reference: Mere insults, indignities, or ordinary workplace rudeness generally do not satisfy the extreme-and-outrageous element of IIED.
