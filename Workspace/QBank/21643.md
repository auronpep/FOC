Question: A prisoner is transferred from one medium-security facility to another medium-security facility with similar rules and privileges. State law gives prison officials broad discretion over transfers. The prisoner received no hearing before transfer. Is a pre-transfer hearing constitutionally required?
Answer A: Yes, every prison transfer deprives liberty and requires a hearing
Answer B: No, not on these facts because the transfer is ordinary and discretionary
Answer C: Yes, because incarceration eliminates all state discretion
Answer D: Apply strict scrutiny because travel is a fundamental right
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts show no atypical significant hardship and no state-created entitlement limiting transfer discretion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ordinary transfers do not automatically impose the kind of hardship or entitlement that creates a liberty interest. C: Prison administration often involves broad discretion absent a protected interest. D: A prisoner transfer is not analyzed as ordinary interstate travel.
lb_rule_reference: An ordinary prison transfer or change in conditions does not create a protected liberty interest unless it imposes an atypical and significant hardship or state law creates a relevant entitlement.
