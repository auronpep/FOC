Question: Naomi hired Daniel to design a garden. The parties signed a detailed writing stating that it was “the complete and exclusive statement of all terms between the parties.” Before signing, Daniel orally promised to include a stone fountain at no extra charge. The writing describes paths, trees, benches, and price, but does not mention a fountain. Naomi offers the oral promise. Should the court admit it?
Answer A: Yes, because the fountain promise is consistent with the written terms.
Answer B: Yes, because oral promises are admissible whenever the writing is silent.
Answer C: No, because the writing was intended as complete and exclusive, so even a consistent additional prior term is barred.
Answer D: No, but only because garden-design contracts are governed by the UCC.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A complete and exclusive integration bars prior or contemporaneous consistent additional terms. Even if the fountain promise does not contradict the written terms, it adds an omitted term to a fully integrated agreement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because consistency is not enough when the writing is complete and exclusive. B: B is wrong because silence does not automatically allow supplementation. D: D is wrong because this is a services contract, not an Article 2 goods sale.
lb_rule_reference: Complete integration / consistent additional term barred
