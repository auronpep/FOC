Question: Moses owned Vineyard Lot, which benefited from a deeded easement over Ruth's private road “for access to Vineyard Lot.” Moses later bought adjoining Orchard Lot, which had no easement over Ruth's road. Moses began directing trucks to cross Ruth's road, pass through Vineyard Lot, and then continue into Orchard Lot. Ruth objects to the use of the road for Orchard Lot. What is the most accurate result?
Answer A: Moses may use the road for Orchard Lot because he owns both Vineyard Lot and Orchard Lot.
Answer B: Moses may use the road for Orchard Lot because the trucks first cross onto Vineyard Lot.
Answer C: Moses may use the road for Orchard Lot if Orchard Lot would be more valuable with access.
Answer D: Moses may not use the easement to serve Orchard Lot because the express easement benefits only Vineyard Lot.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement appurtenant benefits the dominant estate identified by the grant. Using it to serve additional land not included in the grant ordinarily overburdens the servient estate. The easement was for access to Vineyard Lot, not Orchard Lot.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because common ownership of the dominant parcel and a separate parcel does not expand the easement. B: B is wrong because routing traffic through the dominant parcel does not authorize service to non-dominant land. C: C is wrong because added value to Orchard Lot does not alter the grant. D is correct because the dominant estate limits scope.
lb_rule_reference: Dominant estate limitation / extra parcel overburden
