Question: Peter orally agrees to buy 900 shelves from John for $4,500. Peter signs a purchase order stating, “900 shelves from John, $4,500.” John never signs the purchase order. Peter later refuses to accept the shelves. John sues Peter. Does Peter have a good UCC Statute of Frauds defense?
Answer A: No, because the writing is signed by Peter, the party against whom enforcement is sought.
Answer B: Yes, because both buyer and seller must sign a UCC goods contract.
Answer C: Yes, because John did not sign the writing he seeks to enforce.
Answer D: No, but only because shelves are specially manufactured goods.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 2-201 requires a writing signed by the party against whom enforcement is sought, or that party's authorized agent or broker. John is suing Peter, and Peter signed a purchase order identifying the quantity and deal.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because both signatures are not required. C: C is wrong because the proponent's signature is not the SOF gate when enforcement is sought against the other party. D: D is wrong because the result follows from Peter's signed writing, not the specially manufactured goods exception.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(1)
