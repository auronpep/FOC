Question: A client privately told his lawyer where he had hidden assets from a business partner. The client later died. In a civil suit between the business partner and a third party, the third party subpoenas the lawyer to testify about the deceased client's statement. The client's estate asserts attorney-client privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Compel the testimony because attorney-client privilege ends when the client dies.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because attorney-client privilege generally survives the client's death and may be asserted by the estate.
Answer C: Compel the testimony because the deceased client cannot be cross-examined.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony only if the lawyer personally promised lifetime secrecy.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege generally survives the client's death. Death does not convert protected legal-advice communications into admissible evidence, absent an applicable exception not stated here. The decisive axis is privilege survival, not witness availability.
