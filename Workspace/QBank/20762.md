Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Lay opinion testimony
Question: Two weeks after a complaint is filed, defense counsel writes a memo to the case file evaluating which witnesses seem credible, which themes should be emphasized at trial, and whether the case should settle. The plaintiff moves to compel the memo, arguing that it is highly relevant and that no other document shows counsel's evaluation of the case. How should the court classify the memo?
Answer A: As discoverable ordinary work product because relevance and substantial need overcome all trial-preparation protection.
Answer B: As attorney-client privileged only if the memo quotes confidential client communications, and otherwise unprotected.
Answer C: As opinion work product containing counsel's mental impressions and legal theories, which receives heightened protection from disclosure.
Answer D: As unprotected because it was created after litigation began and therefore cannot have been prepared in anticipation of litigation.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A lawyer's litigation memo assessing credibility, settlement posture, trial themes, and legal strategy is opinion work product. Even when ordinary factual work product may be discovered on substantial need and undue hardship, the court must protect attorney mental impressions, conclusions, opinions, and legal theories.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because substantial need can reach ordinary factual work product, but it does not freely expose opinion work product. B: B is wrong because work product is distinct from attorney-client privilege and does not depend solely on whether the document repeats client communications. D: D is wrong because materials created after litigation begins are paradigmatic trial-preparation materials, not automatically unprotected.
lb_rule_reference: FRCP 26(b)(3) ordinary work product vs opinion work product
lb_student_diagnostic: Student collapses attorney-client privilege, ordinary work product, and opinion work product into a single protection.
