Question: Esther's house had a mountain view across Isaac's vacant lot for 18 years. Isaac then built a lawful house on his lot, blocking the view. Esther has no written easement, covenant, subdivision restriction, or statute protecting the view. Esther claims she has an easement preventing Isaac from blocking the view. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Esther has an implied negative easement because she enjoyed the view for many years.
Answer B: Esther has a prescriptive easement because Isaac left his lot vacant for more than the prescriptive period.
Answer C: Esther owns the airspace above Isaac's lot because the view increased her property value.
Answer D: Esther has no easement because a view restriction generally requires a recognized source such as a writing, covenant, statute, or other enforceable restriction.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A landowner generally does not acquire an implied negative easement for light or view merely because neighboring land remained open. The decisive axis is source of right. Esther has no writing, covenant, restriction, statute, or other recognized source of view protection.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because long enjoyment of a view does not itself create a negative easement. B: B is wrong because Isaac's vacancy was not adverse use by Esther. C: C is wrong because property value does not transfer ownership of a neighbor's airspace.
lb_rule_reference: No implied negative easement for view
