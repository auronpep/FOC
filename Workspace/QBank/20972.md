Question: A landowner sues a city seeking a declaration that a conservation easement is invalid. A land trust that holds enforcement rights under the easement promptly moves to intervene, arguing that a judgment invalidating the easement could impair its ability to protect the land. The city supports the easement but has limited resources and has declined to raise several defenses unique to the trust. What must the land trust show for intervention of right?
Answer A: Only that it has an economic or legal interest affected by the lawsuit
Answer B: Timeliness, a related interest, possible impairment of that interest, and inadequate representation by existing parties
Answer C: That no existing party agrees with its desired outcome
Answer D: That the court would have certified the trust as a class representative
Correct Answer: B
lb_rule_reference: Intervention of right requires a timely motion, an interest relating to the property or transaction, possible impairment of that interest, and inadequate representation by existing parties.
