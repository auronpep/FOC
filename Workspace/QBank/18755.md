Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff files one narrow federal record-access claim and ten related state-law business tort claims seeking extensive damages and complex state remedies. The state-law issues would dominate discovery, trial time, and remedies. Defendant asks the federal court to decline supplemental jurisdiction over the state claims. May the federal court decline supplemental jurisdiction on this basis?
Answer A: No, because a federal anchor requires the court to hear every related state claim
Answer B: No, because state-law claims can never predominate if any federal claim remains
Answer C: Yes, if the state-law claims substantially predominate over the federal claim
Answer D: Yes, but only if complete diversity is also absent
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Substantial predominance of state-law claims is a statutory basis for discretionary decline.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1367(c) allows decline when state claims substantially predominate. B: A remaining federal claim does not prevent the court from considering predominance. D: The discretion to decline supplemental claims does not depend on diversity citizenship.
lb_rule_reference: A federal court may decline supplemental jurisdiction when the state-law claims substantially predominate over the claim within original jurisdiction.
