


BARMATRIX Q#: 22867
Outline_code: 91090000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jury Trials
Outline: Trial
Question: A musician sued an advertiser in federal court claiming that the advertiser had violated federal copyright law when it used one of the musician's songs in a television commercial without the musician's permission. The case was tried in front of a jury, and the jury ultimately returned a verdict for the advertiser. A month after the trial, the musician ran into one of the jurors in a bar. The juror recognized the musician, walked over to him, and apologized for the verdict. The juror said that the jurors watched a copy of the commercial that was in evidence in the jury room, studied their own notes that they took during the case in the jury room, and that one juror had even done an experiment in the jury room where she ran the musician's song and the commercial's audio through a computer program that could test their similarity. However, even with all of this work, the jurors still felt that the musician had failed to prove his claim. Based on this conversation, the musician filed a motion for new trial based on jury misconduct. Which of the jurors' actions was juror misconduct sufficient for a new trial?
Answer A: A: Running the song and commercial audio through the computer program.
Answer B: B: Approaching the musician and telling him about juror deliberations.
Answer C: C: Watching a copy of the commercial in the jury room.
Answer D: D: Studying their own notes that they took during the case in the jury room.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 59, a court can grant a new trial based on juror misconduct. It is juror misconduct for a juror to conduct experiments inside the jury room. Here, the juror running the song and commercial through the computer program would be an example of the kind of action that would be juror misconduct. B is incorrect because the conversation took place after the trial was already over. While some jurisdictions have rules regarding communications after a trial, none of those rules are likely to result in a new trial. C and D are incorrect because jurors can take exhibits in evidence and their own notes into the jury room.
