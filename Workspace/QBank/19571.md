Question: In a federal breach-of-contract trial, Levi testifies for the defendant. The plaintiff seeks to impeach Levi with evidence that Levi was convicted four years ago of felony burglary, a crime punishable by more than one year in prison. The defendant objects that burglary does not necessarily involve a false statement. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the conviction because only crimes involving false statements may impeach any witness.
Answer B: Admit the conviction subject to Rule 403 because Levi is a nondefendant witness and the conviction was for a crime punishable by more than one year.
Answer C: Admit the conviction only if the plaintiff can prove that Levi lied during the burglary.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction unless Levi first denies being truthful on direct examination.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: For a crime punishable by death or imprisonment for more than one year, FRE 609(a)(1)(A) requires admission, subject to Rule 403, in a civil case or in a criminal case where the witness is not the defendant. The conviction need not involve a false statement when the felony route applies to a nondefendant witness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the felony route is separate from the dishonest-act route. C: C is wrong because the prosecution does not need to prove lying as an element when the felony route applies. D: D is wrong because conviction impeachment may attack credibility without prior self-bolstering.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(a)(1)(A) / felony conviction of nondefendant witness
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the separate felony route for nondefendant witnesses and the false-statement-only overread.
