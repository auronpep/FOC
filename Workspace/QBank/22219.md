


BARMATRIX Q#: 22219
Outline_code: 33040202
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Hearsay and Circumstances of Its Admissibility
Outline: Statement by opposing party
Question: A man and a woman were in a serious car accident on the freeway. The woman sued the man, alleging he caused the accident by negligently changing lanes without looking. The man sought to introduce the testimony of a police officer who arrived on the scene immediately after the accident. The police officer will testify that when he walked up to the woman's car immediately after the accident, she told him, “Oh my God! I didn't want to let that guy in my lane because he was driving too slow.” Is the officer's statement admissible?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the statement qualifies as a public record.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the woman is suing the man for negligence.
Answer C: C: No, because the statement is inadmissible hearsay.
Answer D: D: No, because the statement is not being used for impeachment.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 801(d), the statement of a party- opponent that is offered against that party is not hearsay. Because the woman is suing the man, that makes her a party opponent, and the statement is admissible. A is incorrect because the fact that the officer might have recorded it in a public record does not make the statement admissible. C is incorrect because 801(d) applies. D is incorrect because the party- opponent exception does not depend on the statement's use for impeachment.
