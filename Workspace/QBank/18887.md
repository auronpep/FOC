Outline: Offers of proof
Question: Before trial, Hannah asks the court to admit testimony from Samuel about later repairs. The court says, “I am not deciding this now. Ask again if the issue comes up during trial.” At trial, Hannah never offers Samuel's testimony and never asks for a ruling. After judgment, Hannah argues that Samuel's testimony was wrongly excluded. Is the claim preserved?
Answer A: Yes, because any discussion of evidence before trial preserves the issue.
Answer B: Yes, because motions in limine are always definitive rulings.
Answer C: No, because the court made only a tentative ruling and Hannah failed to offer the evidence or renew the request at trial.
Answer D: No, but only because evidence of repairs can never be relevant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 103(b) eliminates renewal only after a definitive ruling on the record. The court expressly reserved decision. Hannah needed to offer the evidence or obtain a ruling at trial to preserve the issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because discussion is not the same as a definitive ruling or preserved offer. B: B is wrong because not all in limine rulings are definitive. D: D is wrong because the preservation problem, not categorical irrelevance, controls.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(b) / tentative ruling renewal required
