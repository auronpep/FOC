Question: Two neighbors, both citizens of Iowa, sue a Missouri factory in federal court under state nuisance law. Neighbor 1 seeks $45,000 for damage to her garden. Neighbor 2 seeks $40,000 for damage to his driveway. Their claims are separate and distinct. Can the neighbors aggregate their claims to satisfy the amount in controversy?
Answer A: Yes, because their total alleged loss is $85,000
Answer B: Yes, because both claims arise from the same factory
Answer C: No, because each separate plaintiff's claim must independently satisfy the amount requirement unless a special rule applies
Answer D: No, because citizens of Iowa can never sue a Missouri defendant in federal court
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Neither separate claim exceeds $75,000, so ordinary diversity amount is not met for either plaintiff.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Separate plaintiffs generally cannot aggregate separate claims. B: Same source of harm does not automatically create a common undivided right. D: Complete diversity exists; the amount requirement is the problem.
lb_rule_reference: Multiple plaintiffs with separate and distinct claims generally cannot aggregate those claims to exceed $75,000.
