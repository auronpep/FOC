Question: A defendant answers without objecting to required-party joinder. Before trial, the defendant moves to dismiss under Rule 12(b)(7), arguing a required party cannot feasibly be joined. The plaintiff argues any omitted Rule 12 defense is waived forever. Who is correct about waiver?
Answer A: The plaintiff, because Rule 12(b)(7) is waived like personal jurisdiction
Answer B: The defendant, because failure to join a required party can be raised later under Rule 12(h)(2)
Answer C: The plaintiff, because Rule 19 never matters after an answer
Answer D: The defendant, because no Rule 12 defenses are ever waived
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The defense is not in the early-waiver group.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 12(b)(7) is preserved under Rule 12(h)(2). C: Rule 19 can matter later. D: Some Rule 12 defenses are waived if omitted early.
lb_rule_reference: The Rule 12(b)(7) defense can be raised in the Rule 12(h)(2) ways and is not waived like service or venue.
