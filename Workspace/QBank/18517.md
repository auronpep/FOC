Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Nonresponsive answers
Question: In a federal personal-injury trial, on cross-examination Joshua asks Hannah, “You arrived only after the collision, correct?” Hannah answers, “No. I was there before it happened and saw Caleb run the red light.” Caleb moves to strike the answer as nonresponsive because Hannah did not merely say “no.” How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant the motion because a witness on cross must answer only yes or no.
Answer B: Grant the motion because any explanation after the word “no” is inadmissible.
Answer C: Deny the motion because the explanation directly responds to the premise of the question.
Answer D: Deny the motion only if Hannah first qualifies as an expert on traffic signals.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Hannah's answer directly contradicts the question's premise and explains why it is wrong. A cross-examiner cannot force a misleading yes-or-no answer when the explanation is responsive to the question.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because cross-examination does not impose an absolute yes-or-no requirement. B: B is wrong because a responsive explanation may be allowed. D: D is wrong because she is testifying from personal observation, not expert opinion.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / responsive explanation on cross
