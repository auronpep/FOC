Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Roles of judge and jury
Question: In a federal civil trial, Moses offers a note written by Ruth shortly after a collision, stating, “The blue truck ran the red light.” Moses argues that the note is admissible as an excited utterance. To prove Ruth was still under the stress of the collision when she wrote the note, Moses offers the judge a sworn statement from an emergency medical technician who is not present at trial. Rebekah objects that the sworn statement is hearsay. Who should decide whether Ruth's note qualifies as an excited utterance, and may the sworn statement be considered for that purpose?
Answer A: The jury should decide the foundation issue, and it may consider the sworn statement.
Answer B: The jury should decide the foundation issue, but it may not consider the sworn statement.
Answer C: The judge should decide the foundation issue, and may consider the sworn statement.
Answer D: The judge should decide the foundation issue, but may not consider the sworn statement.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court decides preliminary questions about whether evidence is admissible, including whether a hearsay exception's foundation has been established. In making that preliminary ruling, the judge is not bound by the evidence rules except privilege, so the judge may consider the sworn statement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the jury evaluates weight after admissibility, but the judge decides the admissibility foundation. B: B is wrong for the same decisionmaker error and also because the judge may consider otherwise inadmissible material at this gate. D: D is wrong because it correctly identifies the judge but incorrectly bars consideration of the sworn statement.
