Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal contract action, Sarah sues Jacob for breach. Jacob testifies that he never agreed to buy Sarah's grain. Sarah then offers an authenticated email Jacob sent before the dispute, stating, “I agreed to buy Sarah's grain for $8 per bushel.” Sarah did not ask Jacob about the email while he was on the stand. Jacob objects that he had no opportunity to explain or deny it. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the email because Jacob was not first given an opportunity to explain or deny it.
Answer B: Admit the email only to impeach Jacob, not as proof of the agreement.
Answer C: Admit the email as an opposing party's statement; Rule 613(b)'s opportunity requirement does not apply to that use.
Answer D: Exclude the email unless Jacob wrote it under penalty of perjury.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 613(b)'s opportunity requirement for extrinsic prior inconsistent statements does not apply to an opposing party's statement under Rule 801(d)(2). Jacob's email is offered against him and is his own statement, so it is admissible substantively as an opposing party's statement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the 613(b) opportunity requirement does not apply to opposing-party statements. B: B is wrong because the email is substantive evidence against Jacob, not merely impeachment. D: D is wrong because opposing-party statements do not need to be sworn.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 613(b) and 801(d)(2)(A) / opposing party statement exception
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the exception to the Rule 613(b) opportunity requirement when the prior statement is independently admissible as an opposing party's statement.
