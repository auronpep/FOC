Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: In a federal assault trial, the prosecution offers a knife found in an alley twenty minutes after the assault. Ruth testifies that she saw Aaron run from the scene and throw a shiny knife into that same alley. Aaron objects that the prosecution has not proved the knife is the actual weapon. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the knife because the prosecution has not conclusively proved that it was the weapon.
Answer B: Admit the knife if the court finds enough evidence for a reasonable jury to find that it was connected to the assault.
Answer C: Exclude the knife unless Aaron admits that the knife was his.
Answer D: Admit the knife only if the judge personally finds, by a preponderance of the evidence, that it was the weapon.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When relevance depends on a conditional fact, the court asks whether there is evidence sufficient to support a finding that the fact exists. Ruth's testimony connects Aaron, the flight, the alley, and the knife closely enough for the jury to decide whether it is the weapon.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because foundation does not require conclusive proof. C: C is wrong because authentication or connection may be shown circumstantially; an admission is not required. D: D is wrong because this is a conditional-relevance threshold, not a judge-only merits finding.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / conditional relevance / physical object connection
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests foundation over-demand and the distinction between threshold admissibility and jury weight.
