Question: A state terminates disability benefits after sending written medical reports to the recipient and allowing a written response with additional records before termination. The decision turns mostly on medical documents rather than witness credibility. The recipient argues that a live oral hearing is always required. How should the court evaluate the process?
Answer A: Require a live oral hearing in every benefit-termination case
Answer B: Apply Mathews to decide whether written procedures are sufficient on these facts
Answer C: Deny all process because disability benefits are gifts
Answer D: Apply strict scrutiny because medical judgments are involved
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The test weighs the recipient's interest, error risk and value of more procedure, and administrative burden.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Mathews calibrates process; documentary medical decisions may need less than a full oral hearing. C: Statutory benefit entitlements can create protected property. D: The question asks what process is due, not whether a substantive classification is narrowly tailored.
lb_rule_reference: Due process does not require the same procedure for every benefit; Mathews balances private interest, risk of error and value of added safeguards, and government burden.
