Outline: Truthfulness
Question: In a federal fraud trial, Jacob testifies for the defendant. On rebuttal, the plaintiff calls Hannah and asks, “Based on your dealings with Jacob, what is your opinion of his character for truthfulness?” Hannah answers, “In my opinion, Jacob is untruthful.” The defendant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because a witness's character for untruthfulness may be attacked by opinion testimony.
Answer B: Overrule the objection only if Hannah first lists the transactions in which Jacob lied.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because only reputation, not opinion, may be used to attack truthfulness.
Answer D: Sustain the objection because Jacob's credibility may be attacked only by prior conviction.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 608(a) allows attack on a witness's character for untruthfulness through either reputation or opinion testimony. Hannah's opinion that Jacob is untruthful is an allowed form of credibility attack.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because direct opinion testimony does not require proof of specific instances. C: C is wrong because Rule 608(a) permits both reputation and opinion forms. D: D is wrong because conviction impeachment is only one credibility route; it is not exclusive.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(a) / opinion attack for untruthfulness
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests whether students recognize opinion as a proper direct form for attacking truthfulness.
