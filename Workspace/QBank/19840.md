Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Privileges
Question: In a federal negligence action, a defendant is asked in an interrogatory to identify who inspected a stairwell on the morning of the accident. The defendant refuses, saying the answer is privileged because the defendant told that information to its lawyer during a private meeting seeking legal advice. Is the identity of the inspector protected from discovery on that ground?
Answer A: Yes, because every fact discussed with a lawyer becomes privileged
Answer B: Yes, unless the lawyer repeats the fact in court
Answer C: No, because the opposing party may discover relevant underlying facts even if those facts were communicated to counsel
Answer D: No, because attorney-client privilege never applies in civil cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The communication to counsel may be protected, but the fact of who inspected the stairwell is discoverable if otherwise within scope.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Privilege protects confidential legal-advice communications, not the underlying facts. B: The issue is not waiver by courtroom disclosure; the underlying fact was not privileged in the first place. D: The privilege can apply in civil cases, but it does not cover the underlying facts.
lb_rule_reference: A party may not refuse to disclose relevant underlying facts merely because the party communicated those facts to counsel.
