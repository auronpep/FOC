Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Adam told Eve that he planned to give her Lot 9. A deed was prepared naming Adam as grantor and Eve as grantee, using words of present conveyance and describing Lot 9. Adam never signed the deed or authorized anyone to sign it for him. Eve signed an acceptance line at the bottom and recorded the document. Who owns Lot 9?
Answer A: Adam, because the deed lacked the signature of the grantor or an authorized signer.
Answer B: Eve, because the grantee's signature on the acceptance line satisfies the deed-signature requirement.
Answer C: Eve, because Adam orally said he planned to give her the lot.
Answer D: Eve, because recording cures any signature defect.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is the grantor signature. A deed must be signed by the grantor or someone authorized to sign for the grantor. Eve's signature as grantee, Adam's oral plan, and recording do not supply Adam's missing signature, so title remains with Adam.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a grantee's signature does not substitute for the grantor's execution. C: C is wrong because an oral plan to give land does not transfer title. D: D is wrong because recording cannot validate an unsigned deed.
lb_rule_reference: Grantor signature required
