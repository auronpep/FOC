Question: David signed a deed conveying Palaceacre to Solomon but placed it in his desk drawer because he had not decided whether to give it to Solomon. Solomon secretly took the deed from the drawer and recorded it. David immediately objected. What is the best result?
Answer A: Solomon owns Palaceacre because the deed was signed before he recorded it.
Answer B: Solomon owns Palaceacre because recording is conclusive delivery.
Answer C: David owns Palaceacre because Solomon's theft did not create delivery and recording cannot cure nondelivery.
Answer D: David owns Palaceacre only if he proves Solomon paid no consideration.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is the grantor's intent to part with the instrument as a present conveyance. David kept the deed in his drawer because he had not decided to give it. Solomon's theft and recording did not create delivery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because signature without delivery does not pass title. B: B is wrong because recording is not conclusive delivery and cannot cure a stolen deed. D: D is wrong because consideration is not the controlling lock.
lb_rule_reference: A stolen deed is not delivered and recording cannot make it valid
