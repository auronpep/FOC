Outline: Robbery
Question: Two armed men enter a convenience store and order the clerk, Marta, to lie on the floor in the back room. While Marta lies on the floor, the robbers take $1,200 from the cash register and $400 worth of merchandise from the shelves. Marta cannot see the cash register from the back room. Was the property taken "from the person or presence" of Marta for purposes of robbery?
Answer A: No, because Marta was in the back room and could not see the property being taken
Answer B: No, because the property belonged to the store, not to Marta personally
Answer C: Yes, because the property was within Marta's constructive presence — she would have been able to protect it but for the force used to remove her from the area
Answer D: Yes, but only as to the cash register, which was closer to Marta's work area
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: "From the person or presence" in robbery law is construed broadly. Property is within a victim's presence if the victim was close enough to have exercised control over it but for the defendant's use of force or intimidation. The robbers used force (ordering Marta to the back room at gunpoint) precisely to neutralize her ability to protect the store's cash and merchandise. The cash register and shelves were within the store she was staffing. Marta's constructive presence extended to all property under her care, and the force was used to overcome that protective presence. The "from the presence" element is satisfied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The "presence" element of robbery is not limited to the victim's line of sight. Property is taken from a victim's presence if the victim would have been able to prevent the taking but for the force or intimidation used against her. Marta was present in the store, had authority over the cash register and merchandise, and would have been able to protect the property but for being ordered to the back room at gunpoint. The entire store was within her constructive presence. B: Robbery can be committed by taking property in the possession or custody of another, not just their personally owned property. A store employee has constructive possession of store property in their care. The "from the person or presence" element and the victim's possessory interest in the property are both satisfied by Marta's role as the person responsible for the store's inventory and cash. D: The constructive-presence doctrine does not draw such fine geographic distinctions within a single premises. Marta, as the sole employee responsible for the entire store, had constructive presence over all store property. The robbers used force to neutralize that presence entirely, covering all property in the store. There is no basis to restrict the robbery finding to property physically closer to her work station.
lb_rule_reference: Robbery requires that property be taken from the person or immediate presence of the victim. Property is within a victim's "presence" if the victim was in close enough proximity that — but for the force or intimidation — the victim could have prevented the taking.
