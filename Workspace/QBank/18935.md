Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Offers of proof
Question: In a jury trial, Esther wants to preserve a claim that the court wrongly excluded testimony from Daniel about a prior accusation against the defendant. Esther asks to question Daniel in front of the jury “so the jury can hear what the court is keeping out.” The judge instead excuses the jury and directs Esther to make the offer of proof by questioning Daniel outside the jury's hearing. Was the judge's handling proper?
Answer A: No, because an offer of proof must always be made before the jury to show prejudice.
Answer B: No, because the court may never direct the form of an offer of proof.
Answer C: Yes, because the court may direct an offer of proof in question-and-answer form and should, as far as practicable, prevent the jury from hearing inadmissible evidence.
Answer D: Yes, but only because Daniel's proposed testimony involved impeachment rather than substantive proof.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may direct the form of an offer of proof, including question-and-answer form. In a jury trial, the court must, to the extent practicable, prevent inadmissible evidence from being suggested to the jury. Taking the proffer outside the jury's hearing was proper.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because offers of proof are made to preserve the record for the court, not to expose the jury to excluded matter. B: B is wrong because the court may control the character and form of the proffer. D: D is wrong because the procedure does not depend on whether the excluded testimony would be impeachment or substantive evidence.
