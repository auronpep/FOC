Question: John agreed to sell land to Esther for $200,000 at a closing on July 1. At the closing, Esther arrived with the money and asked for the deed. John said he had left the deed at home and would provide it the next week. Esther refused to pay and sued for breach. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: John breached because Esther was ready to perform and John failed to tender the deed at the concurrent closing.
Answer B: Esther breached because a buyer must pay before seeing a deed.
Answer C: Neither party could breach because duties at a closing are never concurrent.
Answer D: John did not breach unless Esther actually handed over the money before asking for the deed.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: In a land sale closing, the seller's tender of deed and the buyer's tender of payment are typically concurrent conditions. Esther was ready with the price, but John failed to tender the deed. Esther did not have to pay first without receiving the deed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the buyer's duty is concurrent with the seller's deed tender. C: C is wrong because closing obligations commonly operate concurrently. D: D is wrong because a ready and able tender can preserve the buyer's position when the seller fails to tender the deed.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law concurrent conditions at closing
