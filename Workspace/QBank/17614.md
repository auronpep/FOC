Question: Without telling Isaac, Rebecca secretly buried a small drainage pipe under Isaac's field to carry water from Rebecca's lot. The pipe left no visible surface marks, was not recorded, and Isaac had no reason to know it existed. Twelve years later, Isaac discovered the pipe and demanded its removal. The jurisdiction's prescriptive period is 10 years. Does Rebecca have a prescriptive easement for the pipe?
Answer A: Yes, because the pipe remained in place longer than the statutory period.
Answer B: Yes, because drainage is a useful land use and therefore presumed adverse.
Answer C: No, but only because underground utility easements can never be acquired by prescription.
Answer D: No, because the use was hidden and not open or notorious.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Prescription requires use that is open and notorious enough to put the owner on notice. The buried pipe had no visible indicators, was not recorded, and gave Isaac no reason to know of the use.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because time alone is insufficient without open and notorious use. B: B is wrong because usefulness does not create adversity or notice. C: C is wrong because underground easements are not categorically impossible, but the facts lack open notice. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Open and notorious requirement
lb_student_diagnostic: The recurring miss is counting years while ignoring whether the servient owner had notice-level facts.
