Question: Isaac granted Rebecca, as owner of Hill Lot, “a pedestrian walkway easement over the stone path to reach the public sidewalk.” Rebecca later used a wheelchair and occasionally a small mobility scooter on the stone path because she could no longer walk the distance. The use did not widen the path, damage the stones, or interfere with Isaac's use of the land. Isaac sues to stop the wheelchair and scooter use. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Isaac should prevail because the word pedestrian excludes every wheeled device.
Answer B: Rebecca should prevail because wheelchair or similar mobility use is reasonably incidental to pedestrian access and does not enlarge the burden.
Answer C: Isaac should prevail because any change in the manner of use terminates an easement.
Answer D: Rebecca should prevail because the walkway easement gives her ownership of the stone path.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Scope is read in light of the grant's purpose and reasonable use. A pedestrian access easement ordinarily includes reasonable mobility aids that serve the same access purpose and do not materially increase the burden. Rebecca's use stays on the path and causes no interference or damage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because pedestrian access is not read to exclude ordinary mobility assistance when the burden is unchanged. B is correct because the use is incidental to the access purpose. C: C is wrong because a change in manner does not automatically terminate an easement. D: D is wrong because an easement is not ownership of the path.
lb_rule_reference: Pedestrian access / reasonable incidental use
