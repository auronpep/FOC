


BARMATRIX Q#: 22543
Outline_code: 51060100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Contracts
Subtopic: Remedies
Outline: Measure of damages in major types of contract and breach
Question: An asbestos removal company entered into a written agreement with a university to remove the asbestos from all of the university's buildings. There were 20 buildings on campus, and the company agreed to remove the asbestos from the buildings for $10,000 per building. According to the terms of the contract, the company would be entitled to $200,000 when removal from all 20 buildings was completed. Due to cash flow problems, the company asked for $50,000 after it had completed removal from five of the buildings. The university refused to pay the company until the asbestos was removed from all 20 buildings. The company could not do anymore work without the $50,000 requested, so it stopped work and sued the university for the work it had completed. Is the company entitled to recovery?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the contract is divisible.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the company can't complete the contract without payment for the completed removal from the five buildings.
Answer C: C: No, because the company did not complete removal from all 20 buildings.
Answer D: D: No, because cash flow issues would have been foreseeable at the time of contracting.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Even if a party does not complete an entire contract, a party who performs some of the parts of a divisible contract is entitled to the agreed- upon price for the completed parts minus any damages resulting from the party's breach of contract. Here, it was a divisible contract because it was possible to apportion each of the parties' required performances. Specifically, removing the asbestos from each building was worth $10,000, so the company would be entitled to recover the amount agreed upon for five buildings minus any resulting damages. B is incorrect because the fact that the company can't complete the entire contract without payment is irrelevant. C is incorrect because the contract is easily divisible. D is incorrect because the foreseeability of a cash flow issue does not change the analysis.
