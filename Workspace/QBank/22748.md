


BARMATRIX Q#: 22748
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A State A woman sued a State B dog groomer in state court in State A after the woman's dog got a bad haircut. The dog was a highly valuable show dog, and the woman claimed $80,000 in damages. A week later, the woman added the dog grooming company that employed the groomer as a defendant. The company was incorporated and had its primary place of business in State A. Two weeks later, the groomer and the company agreed to remove the case to State A federal court. Two weeks after that, the woman challenged the removal based on lack of diversity, and the case was remanded back to state court in State A. The woman then settled with the company that day. Twenty- five days later, the groomer sought removal to federal court. May the groomer remove the case to federal court?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the groomer had 30 days to remove the case after the woman settled with the company.
Answer B: B: Yes, because claims for removal based on diversity can be made at any time before 180 days after the initial filing.
Answer C: C: No, because the groomer could only remove the claim within 30 days of the initial filing.
Answer D: D: No, because the groomer could only remove the claim within 30 days of the company being added.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Removal is timely if it is done within 30 days of the grounds for removal becoming apparent. Here, the State A woman settled with the State A company, leaving the State B groomer as the only defendant. At that point, there were sufficient grounds for diversity jurisdiction and for removal, and the woman removed 25 days later. B is incorrect because there is no 180- day deadline in removal proceedings. C and D are incorrect because the groomer had 30 days to remove once the woman settled with the company.
