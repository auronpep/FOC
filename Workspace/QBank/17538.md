Outline: Larceny
Question: Leo steals a neighbor's car keys from a table and drives the car away without permission, intending only to take a short joyride and return it. An hour later, while still driving the car, Leo decides to keep it and sells it to a chop shop. Is Leo guilty of larceny of the car?
Answer A: Yes, because the initial taking was trespassory and the later intent arose during a continuing trespass
Answer B: No, because intent to permanently deprive must exist at the first second of movement in every case
Answer C: No, because joyriding can never become larceny
Answer D: Yes, but only because selling the car is false pretenses
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Leo wrongfully took possession at the outset, so the later decision to keep the car can relate back through continuing trespass.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Continuing trespass is the exception when the original taking was wrongful. C: A wrongful taking that continues until permanent-deprivation intent forms can support larceny. D: The theft offense against the owner is larceny through continuing trespass, not false pretenses from the owner.
lb_rule_reference: If the original taking was trespassory, the continuing-trespass doctrine can connect later intent to permanently deprive with the original taking.
