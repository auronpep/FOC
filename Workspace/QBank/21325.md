Question: Sarah conveyed Blueacre to Esther by special warranty deed. The deed warranted title only against claims arising by, through, or under Sarah. After delivery, Esther discovered a title defect created by a prior owner before Sarah ever owned Blueacre. Which statement best describes the deed-type consequence?
Answer A: Sarah is liable as though she had given a general warranty against all title defects.
Answer B: The special warranty does not, merely by its deed type, warrant against defects created before Sarah owned Blueacre.
Answer C: The special warranty deed conveyed no title because the defect was old.
Answer D: The defect is irrelevant only if Esther paid no consideration for the deed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a special warranty deed, and the decisive deed-type lock is the scope of the warranty. A special warranty deed generally limits the grantor's warranty to claims arising by, through, or under that grantor. A prior-owner defect is not covered merely because the deed was a special warranty deed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it treats the special warranty as a general warranty. C: C is wrong because the existence of an old defect does not mean the deed conveyed no interest at all. D: D is wrong because consideration is not the lock that defines the warranty scope.
lb_rule_reference: Special warranty deed limits warranty to grantor's own acts
