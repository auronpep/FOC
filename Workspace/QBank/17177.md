Question: Abigail signed a deed stating, “I give my meadow to Hannah.” Abigail delivered the deed to Hannah and said, “This is yours now.” Hannah accepted. The deed was not notarized or acknowledged, and no consideration was paid. Abigail later argued that the missing acknowledgment and missing consideration made the deed ineffective between them. Who owns the meadow?
Answer A: Abigail, because a gift deed must be notarized to transfer title.
Answer B: Abigail, because lack of consideration and lack of acknowledgment together defeat the deed.
Answer C: Hannah only if the deed is first accepted for recording by the county recorder.
Answer D: Hannah, because the signed and delivered gift deed satisfied the validity locks despite no consideration or acknowledgment.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gates are execution, present transfer language, delivery, and acceptance. Acknowledgment may matter for recording, and consideration is not required for a deed. Abigail signed and delivered the gift deed with present intent, so title passed to Hannah between the parties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because notarization is not the baseline deed-validity requirement tested here. B: B is wrong because neither no consideration nor no acknowledgment defeats an otherwise valid delivered deed between the parties. C: C is wrong because recordation is not the title-passage event.
lb_rule_reference: Acknowledgment or notarization is not baseline validity between parties
