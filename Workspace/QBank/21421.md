Outline: Motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law
Question: At a jury trial, plaintiff rests. Defendant believes plaintiff has no legally sufficient evidence on causation. Defendant waits silently. The case is submitted to the jury, and the jury begins deliberating. Defendant then makes its first Rule 50(a) motion. Is the Rule 50(a) motion timely?
Answer A: Yes, because JMOL may first be made any time before verdict
Answer B: Yes, because defendant believed the evidence was weak
Answer C: No, because the case had already been submitted to the jury
Answer D: No, because JMOL is available only in bench trials
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The motion came after submission, so it missed Rule 50(a)'s timing window.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 50(a) must be made before submission to the jury. B: Legal sufficiency does not excuse missing the timing gate. D: JMOL is a jury-trial device.
lb_rule_reference: A Rule 50(a) motion may be made after a party has been fully heard and before the case is submitted to the jury.
