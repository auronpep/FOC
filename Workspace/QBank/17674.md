Question: Benjamin offers Rebecca $4,000 if she restores an old mural by the end of the month. The offer does not invite a promise. Rebecca begins the restoration with Benjamin's knowledge. Halfway through the work, Benjamin dies. Rebecca finishes the mural by the deadline and seeks payment from Benjamin's estate. What is the best analysis?
Answer A: Rebecca can recover because beginning the invited performance made the offer temporarily irrevocable, and she accepted by completing the work.
Answer B: Rebecca cannot recover because Benjamin's death automatically terminated the offer even after Rebecca began performance.
Answer C: Rebecca cannot recover because Benjamin's promise was a gift until the estate chose to honor it.
Answer D: Rebecca can recover only if her reliance expenses equal exactly $4,000.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Beginning invited performance under a unilateral offer can make the offer temporarily irrevocable. Rebecca began performance before Benjamin's death and completed by the deadline, so the estate cannot avoid payment merely because Benjamin died after protected performance began.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because death after beginning the invited performance does not automatically terminate the protected unilateral offer. C: C is wrong because the promise sought restoration in exchange. D: D is wrong because recovery is based on completing the requested performance, not matching reliance expenses to the price.
lb_rule_reference: common_law
lb_student_diagnostic: Student failed to separate death-before-performance from death-after-protected-performance.
