Question: Leah is served with a complaint by an improper method. Leah files a pre-answer motion raising only failure to state a claim. After that motion is denied, she files a second motion asserting insufficient service of process, a defect available from the start. What result?
Answer A: The service objection is waived because Leah omitted it from her first Rule 12 motion
Answer B: The service objection is never waived
Answer C: The service objection is preserved because failure to state a claim was raised first
Answer D: The court lacks subject-matter jurisdiction because service was defective
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Service objections are among the early-waivable Rule 12 defenses.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: That rule belongs to subject-matter jurisdiction, not service. C: The first Rule 12 motion was the time to include available service objections. D: Service defects and subject-matter jurisdiction are different.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 12(g) and 12(h) require certain defenses, including personal jurisdiction, venue, process, and service objections, to be raised early.
