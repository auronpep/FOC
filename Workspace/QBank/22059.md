


BARMATRIX Q#: 22059
Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: A tenant lived in an apartment in a state that was extremely hot during the summer. When the tenant moved into the apartment, the landlord told him that “[t] he air conditioner works great now, but sometimes it gets a little dicey.” Because the tenant liked sleeping in the cold, he left the air conditioner on all during the winter and spring. In the summer, just as temperatures were reaching above 90°F, the air conditioner broke. The tenant called the landlord, who said he'd come by and fix it as soon as he was done fixing problems in some of the other units that were caused by a freak tornado. The tenant waited for two weeks, but the landlord never fixed the air conditioner, and the apartment was too hot to stay in during the day. When his rent check became due, the tenant decided to withhold his rent to get the landlord's attention. May the tenant withhold his rent?
Answer A: A: No, because the tenant had not vacated the premises.
Answer B: B: No, because the landlord warned the tenant about the air conditioner.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the extreme heat resulted in a taking of the tenant's property rights.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the air conditioner was broken for two weeks.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the implied warranty of habitability, a landlord impliedly warrants to a tenant that the premises are in a good enough condition for someone to live there. In a state with extremely hot summers, a broken air conditioner would likely breach the warranty. If the implied warranty of habitability is breached, the tenant may withhold rent until the defect has been cured. A is incorrect because the tenant does not have to vacate the premises before he withholds rent. B is incorrect because a landlord's warning will not satisfy the warranty. C is incorrect because the broken air conditioner and the hot apartment would not amount to a taking.
