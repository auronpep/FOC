Question: Daniel signs a nondisclosure agreement with Martha before giving her business records. In later litigation, Daniel refuses to produce the records, arguing only that the NDA made them “privileged.” The records were not attorney-client communications and were not prepared for litigation. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because any contractual confidentiality promise creates an evidentiary privilege.
Answer B: Overrule the privilege objection, because an NDA may create contractual confidentiality obligations but does not itself create an evidence privilege.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Martha agrees to waive the NDA.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only because all business records are self-authenticating.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Confidentiality and evidentiary privilege are different. A nondisclosure agreement may support protective-order or contract arguments, but it does not create attorney-client privilege or work-product protection by itself. The decisive axis is confidentiality versus privilege.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is the confidentiality-equals-privilege trap. Private secrecy is not the same as an evidence privilege.
