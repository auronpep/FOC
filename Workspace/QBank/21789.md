Question: After Theo is indicted for murder, the court orders a psychiatric exam to determine competency. Without notifying defense counsel, the prosecutor also asks the psychiatrist to question Theo about future dangerousness for use at sentencing. The psychiatrist does so and the prosecution offers Theo's statements at sentencing. What is Theo's best Sixth Amendment argument?
Answer A: The use of the statements is problematic because counsel was not notified that the exam would be used to elicit sentencing evidence
Answer B: There is no issue because psychiatric examinations are never criminal questioning
Answer C: There is no issue because sentencing is not part of the criminal case
Answer D: Theo wins only if the psychiatrist was a uniformed police officer
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: After attachment, counsel must have notice and a chance to advise the defendant about a government-arranged examination used to gather incriminating sentencing evidence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A government-arranged exam can elicit incriminating information for prosecution purposes after attachment. C: Sentencing is a critical stage and can trigger counsel protections. D: The government can use non-police agents to elicit information for prosecution purposes.
lb_rule_reference: After the right to counsel attaches, the government may violate the Sixth Amendment by using a court-ordered psychiatric examination to elicit incriminating statements without notice to counsel about the examination's prosecutorial purpose.
