Question: A state requires taxpayers to pay a disputed sales tax assessment before suing for a refund. The refund procedure is prompt and allows full legal and factual review. A taxpayer argues that due process always requires a prepayment hearing. Is the taxpayer correct?
Answer A: Yes, a full hearing must always precede every tax collection
Answer B: No, due process can be satisfied by a meaningful refund action after payment
Answer C: No process is due because taxes are not property
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies because all taxes burden interstate commerce
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The state's revenue interest and the availability of prompt full refund review support the pay-first procedure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Tax collection is a context where meaningful post-payment review may satisfy due process. C: The taxpayer still has property interests; the point is that post-payment process may be adequate. D: The claim challenges procedure for tax assessment, not a suspect classification or DCC burden.
lb_rule_reference: Tax collection can use pay-first, litigate-later procedures when the taxpayer has a meaningful post-payment remedy and the government has a strong revenue-collection interest.
