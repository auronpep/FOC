Outline: Intermediate scrutiny (quasi-suspect) classifications
Question: A state administers a re-entry employment program that provides free vocational training, job placement assistance, and a $2,000 completion stipend to formerly incarcerated women. The program is not available to formerly incarcerated men. The state justifies the gender restriction on the ground that formerly incarcerated women face unique employment barriers because they are disproportionately caregivers and face compounded discrimination in hiring. A formerly incarcerated man who meets all eligibility criteria for the program (other than sex) challenges the male exclusion under the Equal Protection Clause. How should a court analyze the male challenger's Equal Protection claim?
Answer A: The male challenger's claim fails because the state's interest in remedying historically documented employment disadvantages faced by formerly incarcerated women is sufficient to sustain any sex-based classification designed to help women
Answer B: The male challenger's claim is governed by rational basis because he is a member of a majority group challenging a program designed to help a disadvantaged group
Answer C: The male exclusion must satisfy intermediate scrutiny, and the state must show the sex-based limitation is substantially related to the important interest it serves — general findings about employment disparities may be insufficient if excluding men is not carefully tied to the program's remedial goals
Answer D: The male exclusion is constitutional as a valid affirmative action program because it addresses documented employment discrimination against women, analogous to permissible race-conscious affirmative action programs
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Sex-based government benefit programs trigger intermediate scrutiny for the excluded sex, regardless of the benign intent behind the program. The state must show the sex-based limitation is substantially related to an important interest and must offer an exceedingly persuasive justification. General evidence of employment disparities affecting formerly incarcerated women may not be sufficient to justify a categorical male exclusion unless the state can connect the exclusion to the specific remedial goal.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Benign sex-based discrimination does not receive automatic dispensation. Sex-based programs must still satisfy intermediate scrutiny whether they burden women or men. The government must show the sex-based restriction is substantially related to an important interest. General findings about employment disparities are not sufficient without evidence that excluding men is substantially related to remediating those disparities. B: The Supreme Court has held that sex-based classifications trigger intermediate scrutiny regardless of which sex is burdened or benefited. A male challenger has the same claim to intermediate scrutiny protection as a female challenger. The standard of review does not shift based on the majority/minority status of the challenger. D: Sex-based affirmative action programs are analyzed under intermediate scrutiny, not strict scrutiny. More importantly, the race-based affirmative action framework (narrow tailoring, compelling interest) is a separate doctrine. Sex-based programs have their own intermediate scrutiny analysis. Drawing on race affirmative action doctrine does not exempt the sex-based program from intermediate scrutiny review.
lb_rule_reference: Sex-based government benefit programs favoring women are not automatically constitutional as "benign" discrimination; they receive intermediate scrutiny and must be substantially related to an important government interest.
