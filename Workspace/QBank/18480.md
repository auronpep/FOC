Question: In a federal civil trial, Deborah testifies for the plaintiff. The defendant seeks to impeach Deborah with a one-year-old misdemeanor conviction for making a false statement on a loan application. Deborah objects that the conviction was only a misdemeanor. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because misdemeanors are never admissible for impeachment.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless the court first finds that the conviction's probative value outweighs its prejudicial effect.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the conviction required proof of a false statement and must be admitted to attack truthfulness.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Deborah denies the conviction on cross-examination.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 609(a)(2), any conviction, regardless of punishment level, must be admitted if the court can readily determine that the offense required proving, or the witness admitted, a dishonest act or false statement. The misdemeanor label does not bar use.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because some misdemeanors involving false statements are admissible under Rule 609(a)(2). B: B is wrong because Rule 609(a)(2) does not use the felony balancing standard. D: D is wrong because the conviction may be used even before a denial; denial matters only to proof logistics, not admissibility.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(a)(2) / misdemeanor false-statement conviction
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the misdemeanor-dishonesty route and rejects felony-only reasoning.
