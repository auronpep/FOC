Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A car dealer hired an actor to imitate a local singer's distinctive voice and catchphrase in radio ads. Listeners recognized the singer's identity from the imitation. The singer had not consented and was not mentioned by name. Can the singer assert appropriation?
Answer A: Yes, because the ad used her recognizable identity for commercial advantage without consent
Answer B: No, because the ad did not use the singer's legal name or photograph
Answer C: No, because the imitation was complimentary
Answer D: Yes, but only if the ad stated a false fact about the singer
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Appropriation is not limited to exact name or photograph; distinctive identity use can suffice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Identity can be appropriated through voice or a recognizable imitation. C: Appropriation protects control over identity use, not only reputation. D: Appropriation does not require falsity.
lb_rule_reference: Appropriation can reach unauthorized use of a person's identity, including distinctive voice or recognizable imitation, for the defendant's commercial advantage.
