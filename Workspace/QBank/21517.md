Question: Deborah hired Joseph to provide “evening program support” for a youth retreat. The recitals stated that the retreat would include “music, lighting, and sound coordination.” The operative services paragraph required Joseph to provide “evening program support,” but did not define the phrase. Joseph provided lighting but no sound coordination, arguing that recitals are never relevant to duties. Which statement is least likely to be accepted?
Answer A: If “evening program support” is ambiguous, the recitals may help explain what the parties meant by that phrase.
Answer B: If the operative paragraph clearly excluded sound coordination, the recitals could not be used to add it back in.
Answer C: Recitals automatically override any inconsistent operative term because they appear before the signature page.
Answer D: The court may consider the whole writing before deciding whether the phrase is reasonably susceptible to Deborah's meaning.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that recitals can help interpret ambiguous operative language, but they do not automatically override clear and inconsistent operative terms. The trigger facts are the undefined operative phrase and recitals describing music, lighting, and sound coordination. The decisive lock is term status: the recitals are interpretive context, not supreme contract terms. C is least likely because it gives recitals automatic priority.
