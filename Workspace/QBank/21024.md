Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: Police arrested Omar for selling drugs under the street name "Northside O." At booking, before Miranda warnings, an officer asked for Omar's name, address, date of birth, and "the street name you used for sales on Maple Avenue." Omar answered, "Northside O." The prosecutor seeks to use that answer to connect Omar to undercover recordings using the same nickname. Is Omar's answer about the street name admissible under the booking exception?
Answer A: Yes, because all questions asked during booking are exempt from Miranda
Answer B: Yes, because aliases are always biographical information
Answer C: No, because the alias question was reasonably likely to elicit an incriminating response
Answer D: No, because Miranda prohibits police from asking any questions after arrest
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The officer's question went beyond routine booking. It connected Omar to a drug-sales nickname and location, so it was interrogation requiring Miranda warnings before use in the prosecution's case-in-chief.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The exception covers routine administrative data. A question tying Omar to drug sales was investigative and likely to elicit incriminating evidence. B: This question did not ask for neutral identity data. It asked for the sales name used at the charged location, making it incriminating interrogation. D: Routine booking questions remain permissible. The defect is that this particular question was investigative.
lb_rule_reference: The booking exception covers routine administrative identity questions, not questions police should know are reasonably likely to elicit incriminating responses.
