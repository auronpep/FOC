Question: After a bakery charged a cancellation fee disclosed in its order form, a disappointed customer posted, "That bakery is a pirate ship. Paying them feels like being robbed." The post did not accuse the bakery of stealing money, hiding charges, or committing any specific unlawful act. Is the bakery likely to prevail in a defamation claim against the customer?
Answer A: Yes, because the post harmed the bakery's business reputation
Answer B: No, because the statement is rhetorical hyperbole rather than a verifiable factual accusation
Answer C: No, because online statements are not publications for defamation purposes
Answer D: Yes, because any statement calling a business a pirate implies criminal conduct
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The post uses figurative language to criticize a disclosed fee and does not imply undisclosed facts capable of being proven true or false.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Harm does not replace the threshold need for a false factual assertion; the words are loose hyperbole about dissatisfaction. C: Online statements can be published; the bakery loses because the statement is not actionable fact. D: In context, the language is figurative consumer criticism, not an assertion that the bakery committed theft.
lb_rule_reference: Pure opinion and rhetorical hyperbole are not defamatory unless they imply undisclosed, verifiable facts.
