Subtopic: Remedies
Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Before making any contract, Naomi spent $7,000 researching neighborhoods and designing a logo for a possible tutoring center. Later, Samuel agreed to lease Naomi a particular classroom for six months but failed to make the classroom available. After that lease was signed, Naomi reasonably spent $11,000 on classroom-specific furniture and $4,000 on licensing fees required for that location. Naomi cannot prove future tutoring profits with reasonable certainty. What may Naomi recover as reliance damages?
Answer A: $22,000, because all expenditures relate to the tutoring-center plan.
Answer B: $7,000, because those were Naomi's earliest sunk costs.
Answer C: Nothing, because a new tutoring center cannot prove profits.
Answer D: $15,000, the expenditures made in reliance on Samuel's lease.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is reliance under common law for Samuel's nonperformance. The recoverable loss category is expenditure caused by reliance on the contract, not unilateral planning costs incurred before any promise. The formula is $11,000 classroom furniture + $4,000 location licensing = $15,000. The limiting gates are causation and certainty, and the result scope is limited. The answer does not decide lost profits because those profits are not reasonably certain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it stacks precontract planning costs with contract-caused reliance, a WAL-REM-008 overcompensation error. B: B is wrong because the earliest sunk costs were not caused by Samuel's promise. C: C is wrong because uncertain profits do not reduce the recovery to nominal damages when reliance expenditures are proven.
lb_rule_reference: Common law reliance damages — expenses must be caused by reliance on the contract
