Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Noah dictated to a clerk a paper stating, “I convey my pasture to Samuel.” The paper accurately described the pasture and named Samuel as grantee. Noah read the paper, handed it to Samuel intending an immediate gift, and Samuel accepted. Noah never signed, marked, initialed, or otherwise authenticated the paper, and no one signed Noah's name at his direction. Samuel signed the paper as grantee. Who owns the pasture?
Answer A: Noah, because the grantor did not sign or otherwise authenticate the deed.
Answer B: Samuel, because Noah delivered the paper with present donative intent.
Answer C: Samuel, because Samuel signed the paper as grantee.
Answer D: Samuel, because a written description of the pasture is enough without a grantor signature.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is the grantor's signature or authentication. Delivery intent alone cannot cure a deed that the grantor did not sign, mark, initial, or authorize someone to sign. Samuel's grantee signature does not substitute for Noah's required grantor signature, so title remains with Noah.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because delivery is a separate lock and cannot replace the required grantor signature. C: C is wrong because the grantee generally need not sign and the grantee's signature does not authenticate the grantor's conveyance. D: D is wrong because a property description is necessary but not sufficient without the grantor's signature.
lb_rule_reference: Grantor signature required
