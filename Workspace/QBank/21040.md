Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: A store security guard suspected Eli of shoplifting. Without any police involvement, request, or prior arrangement, the guard opened Eli's backpack in a back office and found stolen earbuds. The guard then called police and handed over the earbuds. Eli moves to suppress the earbuds under the Fourth Amendment. Should the earbuds be suppressed on Fourth Amendment grounds?
Answer A: Yes, because the guard searched a private backpack without a warrant
Answer B: Yes, because police later received the evidence
Answer C: No, because the search was purely private and not attributable to the government
Answer D: No, because backpacks have no reasonable expectation of privacy
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The guard acted on his own initiative with no police request, encouragement, or agency relationship. The Fourth Amendment exclusionary rule does not apply.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Fourth Amendment restricts government action. A private guard acting independently is not a government actor. B: Later receipt does not convert an already completed private search into government action. D: Backpacks can carry privacy interests. The reason suppression fails is lack of government action.
lb_rule_reference: Fourth Amendment protection requires government action; private actors are not bound unless acting as government agents.
