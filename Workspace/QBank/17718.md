Outline: Possibility of reverter
Question: David conveyed Redacre “to Solomon so long as the land is used as a vineyard.” Solomon later stopped growing grapes and converted the land into a private parking lot. Who owns Redacre after the conversion?
Answer A: David, because Solomon's estate automatically ended when the land stopped being used as a vineyard.
Answer B: Solomon, unless David brings an action to reenter.
Answer C: Solomon, because use language in a deed is always only a covenant.
Answer D: The parking-lot customers, because they are now using the land.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The phrase “so long as” is durational language. It creates a fee simple determinable in Solomon and leaves David with a possibility of reverter. When the land stopped being used as a vineyard, Solomon's estate automatically ended and ownership reverted to David.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because reentry is required for a fee simple subject to condition subsequent, not for a fee simple determinable. C: C is wrong because durational language creates a limitation, not merely a covenant. D: D is wrong because use or occupancy does not equal fee ownership.
lb_rule_reference: Fee simple determinable / possibility of reverter
