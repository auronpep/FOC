Question: After finding out that his former girlfriend, Lena, had destroyed his art portfolio, Roger was enraged. That night, Roger texted a friend: "I'm going to make Lena pay for this." The next morning, Roger took his kitchen knife and drove to Lena's apartment. When Lena opened the door, she laughed at Roger and said his portfolio "deserved to be destroyed." Roger stabbed her three times, killing her. Roger claims he snapped when she laughed. What is the most serious homicide offense for which Roger can properly be convicted?
Answer A: Voluntary manslaughter, because Lena's laughing and taunting at the door constituted adequate provocation that triggered a heat-of-passion response
Answer B: Second-degree murder, because Roger acted with intent to kill but the provocation at the door negates premeditation
Answer C: First-degree murder, because Roger's text message, knife retrieval, and deliberate trip to Lena's home establish premeditation and deliberation
Answer D: First-degree murder, but only because Roger used a knife, which is a deadly weapon per se
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: First-degree murder requires a willful, premeditated, and deliberate killing. Roger threatened to "make Lena pay" the night before, armed himself with a knife, and drove deliberately to her apartment — classic evidence of prior planning and cool reflection. The heat-of-passion defense requires both adequate provocation and an absence of prior planning. Here, the prior planning negates any heat-of-passion claim. Moreover, Lena's laughing constitutes words alone, which is independently insufficient provocation. Roger is guilty of first-degree murder.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The heat-of-passion claim is undermined by overwhelming premeditation evidence: Roger sent a text the night before threatening to "make her pay," retrieved a knife, and drove to her home. A defendant who plans a killing does not acquire a heat-of-passion defense simply because the victim provokes him upon arrival. The premeditation evidence defeats the heat-of-passion claim. B: Premeditation is established by the evening text message, the retrieval of a knife, and the deliberate trip to Lena's apartment. Taunting at the door — verbal mockery, which is words alone — does not constitute adequate provocation sufficient to undo premeditation. Words alone do not reduce murder, and they do not negate already-established premeditation. D: Weapon type does not determine the degree of murder at common law. The distinction between first- and second-degree murder turns on premeditation and deliberation, not the instrument used. D reaches the right result but for the legally incorrect reason.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant cannot simultaneously claim first-degree murder defenses and voluntary manslaughter mitigation; if planning evidence establishes premeditation, the heat-of-passion defense is unavailable because premeditation and heat of passion are mutually exclusive; but if re-encounter provocation occurs without prior planning, a fresh heat-of-passion claim may be viable
