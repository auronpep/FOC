Question: A farm owner emails Joseph: “I offer you the job of field manager starting May 1 at $4,000 per month. Reply by Monday if you accept.” Joseph replies on Sunday, “I accept.” The owner later argues that the email was not an offer because employment terms can never be definite. Was the email capable of acceptance as an offer?
Answer A: No, because employment offers are never definite enough unless they promise lifetime employment.
Answer B: Yes, because it stated a position, start date, compensation, and invited acceptance.
Answer C: No, because Joseph's reply was ineffective unless he also began working.
Answer D: No, because the court must decide whether $4,000 per month is adequate consideration.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A job offer can be definite enough to accept when it states the position, compensation, start date, and invites acceptance. Separate issues about employment duration or at-will status do not prevent the communication from being an offer.
