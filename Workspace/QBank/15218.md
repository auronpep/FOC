Question: The plaintiffs, a retired couple, had lived in their home in a residential neighborhood for 20 years when the defendants, a family of six, moved into the house next door and built a swimming pool in the back yard. The family's four young children frequently played in the pool after school. They often were joined by other neighborhood children. The plaintiffs were in the habit of reading and listening to classical music in the afternoons. Sometimes they took naps. The boisterous sounds of the children playing in the pool disturbed the plaintiffs' customary enjoyment of quiet afternoons. In the plaintiffs' nuisance action for damages against the defendants, the plaintiffs should
Answer A: A: prevail, because the children's noise constituted a substantial interference with the plaintiffs' use and enjoyment of their home.
Answer B: B: prevail, because the the plaintiffs' interest in the quiet enjoyment of their home takes precedence in time over the defendants' interests.
Answer C: C: not prevail, because the noise did not constitute a substantial and unreasonable disturbance to persons of normal sensibilities.
Answer D: D: not prevail, because the children's interest in healthy play has priority over the plaintiffs' interest in peace and quiet.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. Private nuisance is defined as a substantial, unreasonable interference with another private individual's use or enjoyment of their own property. Substantial interference is something that would be offensive, inconvenient or annoying to an average person of the same community. Unreasonable interference is determined by balancing the severity of the injury against the utility of the defendant's conduct.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. To prevail, the couple would need to not only establish that the children interfered with their enjoyment of their property, but also that this interference would constitute a substantial interference to the average person. Here, the children's interference does not appear to have been substantial to a reasonable person, even if the couple considered it to be substantial. Consequently, the couple would not recover. B is incorrect. A balancing test would likely weigh in favor of the children because their interference with the couple was not objectively substantial or unreasonable. Moreover, a balancing test is required because neither party's interests automatically trump those of the other. D is incorrect. Although a balancing test would likely weigh in favor of the children, because their interference with the couple was not objectively unreasonable, a balancing test would still need to be applied, as the children's interest in healthy play would not automatically trump the couple's interest in quiet enjoyment.
additional_info: A nuisance is a tortious harm that interferes with either private property rights or public rights. A private nuisance is a substantial, unreasonable interference with another private individual's use or enjoyment of property that is offensive, inconvenient, or annoying to an average person in the community. To prevail on a private nuisance claim, a plaintiff must demonstrate that the nuisance is an unreasonable interference by showing that the injury outweighs the utility of the defendant's conduct.
