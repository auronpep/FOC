Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: After arresting Priya, detectives read her Miranda rights. Priya said she understood. She sat silently for several minutes while detectives reviewed photographs. When a detective asked where she got the stolen credit cards, Priya answered and then spoke for twenty minutes. She never signed a waiver card or said, "I waive my rights." Were Priya's statements obtained after a valid Miranda waiver?
Answer A: No, because a Miranda waiver must be express and written
Answer B: No, because Priya's initial silence invoked the right to remain silent
Answer C: Yes, because Priya understood the warnings and voluntarily answered questions
Answer D: Yes, because waiver is presumed whenever police read Miranda warnings
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The totality supports implied waiver. Priya received complete warnings, acknowledged understanding, did not invoke, and voluntarily answered questions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: An express written waiver is not required. Understanding the warnings and then voluntarily answering can establish implied waiver. B: Invocation must be unambiguous. Priya said she understood and then voluntarily answered; she did not clearly say she wanted to stop or remain silent. D: Warnings alone do not establish waiver. The prosecution still must show knowing, voluntary, and intelligent waiver from the circumstances.
lb_rule_reference: Miranda waiver may be implied from receiving warnings, understanding them, and voluntarily responding; invocation must be unambiguous.
