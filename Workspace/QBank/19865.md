MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Consent
Question: Nexagen Software LLC, a California company, contracted with Brightpath Analytics Inc., a company incorporated and headquartered in the State of Duren. The written agreement contained the following clause: "Any dispute arising out of or relating to this Agreement shall be resolved exclusively in the federal or state courts of the State of Duren, and each party irrevocably submits to the personal jurisdiction of such courts." Nexagen performed software integration services for Brightpath over 18 months. A dispute arose, and Brightpath sued Nexagen in a Duren federal court for breach of contract. Nexagen has no employees, offices, or servers in Duren, and all of its work was performed remotely from California. Nexagen moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction, arguing it has insufficient contacts with Duren. How should the Duren federal court rule on Nexagen's motion?
Answer A: Grant the motion, because Nexagen performed all work from California and has no minimum contacts with Duren
Answer B: Grant the motion, because a forum-selection clause affects venue, not personal jurisdiction, and therefore cannot supply the missing contacts with Duren
Answer C: Deny the motion, because Nexagen expressly and irrevocably consented to personal jurisdiction in Duren courts as part of the written agreement, and that consent is binding for claims arising from the agreement
Answer D: Deny the motion, because Nexagen's 18 months of services for a Duren company satisfies purposeful availment of Duren even without physical presence there
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The contract's forum-selection clause contains an express "irrevocably submits to the personal jurisdiction" provision. This is contractual consent to PJ — an independent basis for jurisdiction that does not require minimum contacts. The claim (breach of contract) clearly arises from the agreement, so the clause covers it. The motion should be denied because Nexagen waived any due-process objection by contracting in advance to litigate in Duren.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The minimum-contacts analysis is irrelevant here because Nexagen consented to jurisdiction. A party who contractually agrees in advance to litigate in a specified forum waives any due-process objection to personal jurisdiction in that forum for claims covered by the clause. Nexagen irrevocably submitted to Duren courts in the written agreement; it cannot now invoke due process to undo that consent. B: Forum-selection clauses operate on two distinct doctrinal levels. As a matter of personal jurisdiction, a clause in which a party "irrevocably submits to the personal jurisdiction" of a court is a direct consent to PJ — it is not merely a venue designation. *Atlantic Marine* addressed forum-selection clauses primarily in the venue/§ 1404 context, but the contractual consent to PJ is a separate, independent basis for jurisdiction that pre-dates and supplements venue rules. D: Whether 18 months of remote services satisfies purposeful availment of Duren is contestable — courts split on whether remote-only performance directed at a forum-state client satisfies purposeful availment. More importantly, the correct and cleanest basis for jurisdiction is the explicit consent clause. An answer that denies the motion on a contested minimum-contacts theory when a clear consent basis exists is doctrinally incomplete and would mislead students.
lb_rule_reference: A forum-selection clause in a contract constitutes consent to personal jurisdiction in the designated forum. A defendant who agreed to such a clause cannot successfully challenge jurisdiction on minimum-contacts grounds for claims covered by the clause. *The Bremen v. Zapata Off-Shore Co.* (1972); *M/S Bremen* standard; *Atlantic Marine Construction Co. v. U.S. Dist. Court* (2013).
