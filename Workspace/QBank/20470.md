Outline: Lay opinion testimony
Question: In a federal fraud trial, Daniel denies signing a receipt. Lydia, who worked with Daniel for five years before the dispute and often saw him sign delivery forms, is asked whether the signature on the receipt appears to be Daniel's. Daniel objects that Lydia is not a handwriting expert. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because handwriting may be identified only by an expert.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because Lydia's opinion is based on familiarity with Daniel's handwriting acquired before the litigation.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Lydia personally saw Daniel sign this particular receipt.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the jury is not allowed to compare the signature for itself.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A lay witness with familiarity with a person's handwriting not acquired for the litigation may give an opinion identifying handwriting. Lydia's workplace familiarity supplies the foundation. She need not be a handwriting expert or have seen the specific receipt signed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because lay handwriting identification can be based on pre-litigation familiarity. C: C is wrong because Lydia need not have seen this exact receipt signed. D: D is wrong because lay opinion and jury comparison are not mutually exclusive proof methods.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 701 and 901 / lay handwriting familiarity
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests lay handwriting opinion and proof-channel confusion.
