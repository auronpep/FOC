Question: A hiker ignored posted no-entry signs and crossed a remote wooded parcel without the owner's knowledge. The hiker tripped over a naturally fallen branch on an unmaintained path and was injured. Under traditional entrant categories, what duty did the owner likely owe?
Answer A: The full invitee inspection duty for all hazards on the land
Answer B: A duty to warn of every natural condition on remote land
Answer C: Only limited duties, because the hiker was an unknown trespasser injured by a natural condition
Answer D: Strict liability because no-entry signs prove the owner expected injury
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The owner did not know of the trespasser, and the injury came from a natural condition on remote land.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Unknown trespassers receive limited duties, not the invitee inspection duty. B: Traditional rules do not impose broad duties to unknown trespassers for natural conditions. D: Warning signs do not create strict liability to trespassers.
lb_rule_reference: Traditional premises rules impose only limited duties to unknown trespassers, especially for natural conditions.
