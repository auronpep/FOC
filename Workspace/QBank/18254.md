Question: A written contract required Boaz to deliver “10,000 pounds of standard wool” to Deborah. The writing was complete and exclusive. Deborah offers two pieces of extrinsic evidence: first, a wool-trade usage that “standard wool” allows a 2% weight variance; second, a prior oral promise by Boaz to deliver 12,000 pounds for the same price. Which ruling is best under Article 2? Which ruling is best under Article 2?
Answer A: Admit both pieces of evidence because Article 2 freely admits all extrinsic evidence.
Answer B: Exclude both pieces of evidence because the writing is complete and exclusive.
Answer C: Admit the prior oral promise only, because specific negotiations control over trade usage.
Answer D: Admit the trade-usage evidence if reasonably consistent, but exclude the prior oral promise because it contradicts the express quantity term.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Article 2, usage of trade may explain or supplement a final written agreement if reasonably consistent with express terms. A usage allowing a 2% variance can qualify the meaning of “standard wool.” A prior oral promise to deliver 12,000 pounds contradicts the express 10,000-pound quantity and is barred.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Article 2 does not admit contradictory prior oral terms. B: B is wrong because complete integration does not automatically bar UCC usage evidence. C: C is wrong because a contradictory prior oral promise does not control over an express written quantity.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-202 / usage vs contradictory prior oral term
