MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Domicile
Question: A plaintiff files a diversity action in federal court. The complaint alleges that the plaintiff is a citizen of Missouri. At the time the complaint is filed, the plaintiff grew up in Missouri and has a Missouri driver's license. She moved to Colorado nine months ago for a two-year fellowship at a research institute. She rents an apartment in Colorado, pays Colorado income taxes, and registered her car in Colorado. She intends to return to Missouri when the fellowship ends. The defendant, a citizen of Missouri, moves to dismiss for lack of diversity jurisdiction. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Should the court dismiss for lack of diversity jurisdiction?
Answer A: No, because the plaintiff grew up in Missouri, holds a Missouri driver's license, and her legal domicile never changed
Answer B: Yes, because the plaintiff currently resides in Colorado and pays Colorado taxes, making her a Colorado citizen for diversity purposes
Answer C: Yes, because the plaintiff's domicile remains Missouri — she is on a fixed-term fellowship with intent to return — making her and the defendant both Missouri citizens and destroying diversity
Answer D: No, because intent to return to Missouri is irrelevant once the plaintiff has been physically present in Colorado for nine months
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An individual's citizenship for § 1332 purposes is determined by domicile: the state where the person is physically present with the intent to remain indefinitely. The plaintiff left Missouri for a defined two-year fellowship with the stated intent to return. A temporary stay with a fixed end date does not establish a new domicile. Her domicile at the time of filing remains Missouri. Because both parties are Missouri citizens, complete diversity is absent and the action must be dismissed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Domicile changes when a person physically moves to a new state with the intent to remain there indefinitely. That intent does not require permanent residence — "indefinitely" means no fixed end date for the current stay. Here the plaintiff is in Colorado under a two-year fellowship with a specific planned end date, indicating she does not intend to remain in Colorado indefinitely. Her domicile likely remained in Missouri. But the critical test is present intent, not origin. B: Residence alone does not determine citizenship for diversity purposes — domicile does. Domicile requires physical presence plus intent to remain indefinitely. The plaintiff is in Colorado on a fixed two-year fellowship and intends to return to Missouri when it ends. A temporary assignment with a defined end date does not establish a new domicile, even if the person acquires local indicia like tax registration. D: Federal diversity citizenship has no durational residency rule. Physical presence for any length of time does not establish domicile without the accompanying intent to remain indefinitely. Intent is always a required element and cannot be extinguished by duration alone.
lb_rule_reference: For § 1332 purposes, an individual's citizenship is the state of domicile: physical presence plus intent to make the state a permanent home.
