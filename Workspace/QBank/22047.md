


BARMATRIX Q#: 22047
Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: A tenant rented a house from a landlord. The lease did not contain any express covenants. The landlord began to build another house on the property. When the builders dug a hole for the new house's basement and foundation, the rented house shifted and its ceiling collapsed. The rented house was then uninhabitable, so the tenant moved out even though six months were left on the lease. Which of the following is true?
Answer A: A: The tenant may withhold rent because the tenant's actual eviction breached the covenant of quiet enjoyment.
Answer B: B: The tenant may withhold rent because the tenant's constructive eviction breached the covenant of quiet enjoyment.
Answer C: C: The tenant may only reduce rent payments before six months were left on the lease.
Answer D: D: The tenant is required to keep paying rent because there was no express covenant of quiet enjoyment in the lease.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A tenant is constructively evicted when a landlord unintentionally makes the property uninhabitable. If a tenant is constructively evicted and moves out, the tenant is no longer liable for rent. Importantly, every lease has a covenant of quiet enjoyment, whether that covenant is stated expressly or merely implied. Here, the tenant was constructively evicted because the roof collapsed accidentally. This breached the covenant of quiet enjoyment implied in the lease, so the tenant was no longer required to pay rent. A is incorrect because an actual eviction occurs when the landlord intentionally creates the situation. C is incorrect because the tenant has been completely evicted from the property because it is now uninhabitable. D is incorrect because the covenant of quiet enjoyment is contained in every lease even if it is not stated expressly.
