Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Naomi hired Daniel under a written agreement to paint the inside of her bakery. The painter would supply ordinary paint and brushes, but nearly all of the price was for labor. Before signing, Daniel allegedly promised to finish the work only if Naomi closed the bakery for three days. The final writing says nothing about closing the bakery. Which source-law route should be locked before analyzing Daniel's oral-condition evidence?
Answer A: UCC Article 2, because paint and brushes are involved.
Answer B: Common law, because the predominant purpose is a service contract.
Answer C: Real-property law, because the painting occurs inside a building.
Answer D: Article 2 automatically, because any contract involving movable items is a sale of goods.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The predominant purpose is painting services; the incidental paint and brushes do not make Article 2 the governing route. Because common law controls, the oral condition should be analyzed under common-law parol-evidence and condition-precedent principles.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because incidental materials do not control source law. C: C is wrong because painting a building does not make the agreement a land-sale contract. D: D is wrong because Article 2 is not triggered by the mere presence of movable items.
lb_student_diagnostic: Source-law lock error before condition analysis.
