Question: A public high school allows students to use empty classrooms during lunch for noncurricular clubs. A group of students meets voluntarily to pray. Teachers do not lead or attend the meetings. The school bans only the prayer group, citing establishment concerns. What is the prayer group's strongest argument?
Answer A: The meetings are private student speech, and equal access does not automatically establish religion
Answer B: The school must ban the meetings because religious speech in school is always government speech
Answer C: The group may meet only if teachers lead the prayer to keep order
Answer D: The group loses because religion is never a viewpoint on permitted student topics
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The school is not sponsoring the prayer by allowing equal access to a forum.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Student-initiated voluntary speech is not automatically school-sponsored speech. C: Teacher leadership would increase, not reduce, sponsorship concerns. D: Religious perspectives can be protected viewpoints in student forums.
lb_rule_reference: Private student religious expression is protected speech and does not become government establishment merely because it occurs in a public school, so long as the school does not sponsor or coerce it.
