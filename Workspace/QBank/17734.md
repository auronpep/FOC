Question: Boaz conveyed Millacre “to Ruth and her heirs.” In the same deed, Ruth promised not to build a factory on the land and agreed to pay Boaz $30,000 if she breached that promise. The deed stated that damages would be Boaz's sole remedy and did not reserve any right of entry, reverter, forfeiture, or gift-over. Ruth later built a factory. What retained future interest does Boaz have because of the factory use?
Answer A: A possibility of reverter, because the land is no longer being used as promised.
Answer B: None; Boaz has only the covenant remedy stated in the deed.
Answer C: A right of entry, because all deed restrictions are conditions subsequent.
Answer D: A reversion, because every grantor keeps one unless the deed says otherwise.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Boaz has no retained future interest because of the factory use. The deed creates a covenant with a damages remedy and expressly does not reserve reentry, reverter, forfeiture, or a gift-over. Ruth keeps title, and Boaz's remedy is the covenant remedy stated in the deed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a possibility of reverter requires durational limitation language. C: C is wrong because not every deed restriction is a condition subsequent; the deed makes damages the sole remedy. D: D is wrong because Boaz conveyed fee simple and did not retain a reversion.
lb_rule_reference: Covenant remedy only / no forfeiture interest
