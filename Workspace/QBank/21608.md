Question: A driver negligently bumped a jogger in a crosswalk. The jogger suffered bruising and soreness for two weeks but did not miss work and chose not to seek medical care. The driver admits negligence but argues the jogger cannot recover because there are no bills or lost wages. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The jogger cannot recover because pain and suffering require medical bills
Answer B: The jogger cannot recover because the injury was short-lived
Answer C: The jogger may recover proven pain-and-suffering damages caused by the collision
Answer D: The jogger may recover only nominal damages because there are no receipts
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The jogger suffered actual physical injury, and noneconomic damages can compensate tort-caused pain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Pain and suffering from physical injury can be compensable even without out-of-pocket economic loss. B: Duration affects amount, not whether tort-caused physical pain is compensable. D: Lack of receipts does not convert actual physical injury into nominal damages only.
lb_rule_reference: Pain and suffering from a tort-caused physical injury can be compensable even if the plaintiff has no medical bills or wage loss.
