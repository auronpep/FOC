Outline: Rule of completeness
Question: In a civil action, Esther offers a thirty-second excerpt from a recorded call in which David says, “I was at the warehouse at midnight.” David asks the court to play the next twenty seconds, in which he says, “only because Esther asked me to inspect the broken lock.” David does not ask to play the remaining two hours of the call. Which ruling is most appropriate?
Answer A: Allow the next twenty seconds if fairness requires that portion to be considered with Esther's excerpt.
Answer B: Exclude the next twenty seconds because David can explain the call through his own testimony later.
Answer C: Admit the entire two-hour call because Esther introduced part of the recording.
Answer D: Exclude the next twenty seconds unless David proves that the entire call is independently admissible under a hearsay exception.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule of completeness is targeted. If the next twenty seconds are necessary to put the excerpt in fair context, the court may require that portion to be played at the same time. The rule does not automatically admit the whole recording.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because delayed explanation may not cure the misleading impression caused by an excerpt. C: C is wrong because the completeness remedy is limited to the portion fairness requires. D: D is wrong because Rule 106 can supply the route for the completing portion when fairness requires it.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 106 / partial recording excerpt
