Outline: Nature and Type
Question: Rachel signed a document stating, “I give Leah an easement somewhere over my 100-acre farm if Leah later wants one.” The document did not identify a route, purpose, width, benefited parcel, existing path, or method for choosing the route. No road or path existed on the farm when Rachel signed the document. Leah later selected a route through Rachel's garden and recorded the document. What is the best result?
Answer A: Leah has a valid easement over any route she chooses because the document uses the word “easement.”
Answer B: Leah has fee title to the route she selected because she recorded the document.
Answer C: Leah has a valid easement because recording cures uncertainty in the description.
Answer D: The writing is likely too indefinite to create an enforceable express easement as written.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An express easement must be described with enough certainty to identify the interest being created, either directly or through facts that make the route or burden ascertainable. This document identifies no route, purpose, width, benefited land, existing way, or selection method. Recording does not cure that indefiniteness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because labels do not cure an indefinite property description. B: B is wrong because an easement is not fee title and recording does not create title to a chosen route. C: C is wrong because recording gives notice of an instrument; it does not supply missing essential terms.
lb_rule_reference: Indefinite description / no ascertainable route
