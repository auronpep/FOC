Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Freedom of speech and expression
Question: A public university creates a student-organization funding program for "debates about public policy." It funds groups advocating tax reform, criminal-justice reform, and environmental reform. It denies funding to a group that opposes tax increases because the university says the group's anti-tax viewpoint is "unconstructive." Is the denial likely constitutional?
Answer A: Yes, because a limited forum allows any restriction the university considers educational
Answer B: Yes, because public funding is always government speech
Answer C: No, because the university excluded a viewpoint within an allowed subject area
Answer D: No, because all limited forum restrictions receive strict scrutiny
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Public-policy debate is permitted; excluding the anti-tax side is viewpoint discrimination.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Limited forums still require viewpoint neutrality. B: A student funding forum for private groups is not automatically the government's own message. D: Reasonable subject limits may be allowed; viewpoint discrimination is the specific defect.
lb_rule_reference: In a limited public forum, restrictions must be reasonable and viewpoint neutral.
