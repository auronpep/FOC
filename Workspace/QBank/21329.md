Question: In a federal civil trial, Jonah offers a certified copy of a government inspection report. The copy bears the agency seal and a proper certification. The defendant objects only that no witness has authenticated the copy. What is the best ruling on that objection?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because every document requires live authentication.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because the certified copy may be self-authenticating; any hearsay objection would be a separate issue.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because public documents can never be self-authenticating.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the defendant agrees the report's contents are true.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Certain certified public documents are self-authenticating. Authentication does not necessarily resolve hearsay or truth issues, but the stated authentication objection fails.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and C contradict self-authentication rules. D confuses authenticity with truth. C: A and C contradict self-authentication rules. D confuses authenticity with truth. D: A and C contradict self-authentication rules. D confuses authenticity with truth.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 902; documentary exhibit and hearsay distinction
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests documentary self-authentication and separation of authentication from hearsay/truth.
