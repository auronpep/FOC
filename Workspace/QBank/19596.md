Outline: Homicide
Question: Dana slapped Miles once during an argument, leaving a minor red mark. Miles walked away unharmed. Ten minutes later, while standing alone in an open field, Miles was struck by lightning and died. The slap did not affect where Miles went or his ability to avoid danger. Is Dana guilty of homicide for Miles's death?
Answer A: Yes, because Dana committed a battery before Miles died
Answer B: Yes, because any unlawful touching followed by death is manslaughter
Answer C: No, because the lightning was an independent event unrelated to the risk Dana created
Answer D: No, because battery is never criminal
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Dana's slap did not cause Miles to be in the field, impair him, or create the lightning risk. The lightning was an independent superseding event. Homicide liability is not supported.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Chronology is not causation. The minor slap did not cause or increase the lightning risk. B: The unlawful act must actually and proximately cause death. It did not. D: Battery can be criminal. The defect is lack of causation between the battery and death.
lb_rule_reference: An independent, unforeseeable event that replaces the defendant's risk can supersede causation when the defendant's act did not contribute to death.
