Outline: Robbery
Question: After Lena is indicted for robbery, police conduct an uncounseled lineup. The witness identifies Lena there. Before the lineup, the witness had seen the robber only for one second from behind in a dim alley and had given no useful description. At trial, the prosecution asks the witness to identify Lena in court. Should the in-court identification be suppressed?
Answer A: Yes, because the uncounseled lineup was tainted and the witness lacks an independent source for identifying Lena
Answer B: No, because the witness may always identify the defendant in open court
Answer C: No, because suppression of the lineup ID is the only remedy for a counsel violation
Answer D: Yes, because any eyewitness identification is too unreliable to admit
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The lineup violated the Sixth Amendment, and the witness's original observation was too weak to supply an independent basis. The in-court ID is tainted by the lineup.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A courtroom identification can be suppressed when it derives from an unconstitutional lineup and lacks an independent source. C: A later in-court ID may also be suppressed if it is not independently sourced. D: The constitutional reason is specific: taint from an uncounseled lineup without independent source. Eyewitness evidence is not categorically inadmissible.
lb_rule_reference: If an unconstitutional lineup taints the witness and the government cannot show an independent source for the in-court identification, both the lineup identification and the in-court identification are suppressible.
