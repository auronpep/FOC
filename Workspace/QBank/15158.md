Question: While driving his pickup truck with a friend riding in the open bed, the driver swerved, throwing his friend to the pavement. The friend sustained severe injuries. The friend had often ridden in the open bed of the truck, and on some of those occasions the driver had swerved to frighten his friend. The friend sued the driver to recover both compensatory damages for his injuries and punitive damages. Which cause of action would NOT permit the friend to recover punitive damages?
Answer A: A: Assault.
Answer B: B: Battery.
Answer C: C: Negligence.
Answer D: D: Recklessness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. Punitive damages are generally not recoverable in negligence cases unless additional proof is offered that the defendant was wanton and willful, reckless, or malicious, which was not the case here.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Assault is an intentional tort. Punitive damages are typically allowed for intentional torts. B is incorrect. Battery is an intentional tort. Punitive damages are typically allowed for intentional torts. D is incorrect. Reckless conduct is usually sufficient to award punitive damages, even in some negligence actions.
additional_info: Once the judge or jury determines whether a defendant's conduct was intentional or negligent, assuming it is one or the other, the determination of damages available can be made. An intentional tort victim may recover punitive damages if the defendant's conduct was outrageous or malicious. Such damages may be very substantial and may even be awarded when little to no compensatory damages were awarded. In ordinary negligence cases, punitive damages are not recoverable. There are situations where punitive damages may be recoverable in negligence cases if the defendant's conduct was shown to be wanton and willful, reckless, or malicious. C is correct.
