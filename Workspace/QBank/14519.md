Question: A landowner entered into a single contract with a builder to have three different structures built on separate pieces of property owned by the landowner. Each structure was distinct from the other two, and the parties agreed on a specific price for each. After completing the first structure in accordance with the terms of the contract, the builder demanded payment of the specified price for that structure. At the same time, the builder told the landowner that the builder was “tired of the construction business” and would not even begin the other two structures. The landowner refused to pay anything to the builder. Is the builder likely to prevail in a suit for the agreed-upon price of the first structure?
Answer A: A: No, because substantial performance is a constructive condition to the landowner's duty to pay at the contract rate.
Answer B: B: No, because the builder's cessation of performance without legal excuse was a willful breach of the contract.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the contract is divisible, and the landowner will be required to bring a separate claim for the builder's failure to complete the other two structures.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the contract is divisible, but the landowner will be able to deduct any recoverable damages caused by the builder's failure to complete the contract.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. This contract is divisible because it contains an equal number of divisible and corresponding parts for each party, and by completing one entire (but separate) part, the builder has the right to recover for that performance, even before the contract is completed. However, the landowner will have the right to recover any damages in the event that the builder fails to complete the other two structures. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The builder did, in fact, substantially perform as to the first structure and is likely to recover the agreed price for it, less any recoverable damages to the landowner as a result of the builder's breach. B is incorrect. Willfulness is sometimes an important factor in breach of contract cases, although it does not impact the builder's right to recover here because he completed the first structure, which is divisible. C is incorrect. Though the contract has separable, divisible components, it is still a single contract for purposes of damages. To require a separate claim would go against judicial efficiency, especially regarding closely-related claims.
