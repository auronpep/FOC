Question: A negligent driver hit a utility pole, forcing police to close a downtown block for two hours. A restaurant on the block suffered no property damage and no customer was injured, but lunch traffic dropped during the closure. The restaurant sues the driver in ordinary negligence for lost sales only. What is the driver's best damages argument?
Answer A: The restaurant may recover because the lost sales were foreseeable
Answer B: The restaurant may recover because economic loss is always compensable in tort
Answer C: The restaurant's claim is likely barred as pure economic loss
Answer D: The restaurant's claim fails only because restaurants can never sue drivers
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The restaurant alleges only lost sales from a temporary closure, with no personal injury, property damage, or special relationship.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Foreseeability alone usually does not permit pure economic loss in ordinary negligence. B: Tort limits pure economic loss absent an anchoring injury, property damage, or recognized exception. D: The reason is the pure-economic-loss limit, not a categorical immunity for drivers against business plaintiffs.
lb_rule_reference: Ordinary negligence generally does not allow recovery for ripple pure economic loss unconnected to plaintiff's personal injury, property damage, or a recognized special duty.
