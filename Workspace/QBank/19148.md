Question: Sarah signed a written power authorizing Daniel “to lease my field for one year on such terms as he thinks best.” Daniel instead signed and delivered a deed purporting to convey the field in fee simple to Abigail. Abigail accepted the deed. Who owns the field?
Answer A: Abigail, because Daniel had written authority concerning the field.
Answer B: Sarah, because Daniel's written authority permitted a lease but not a deed conveying title.
Answer C: Abigail, because a deed is broader than a lease and therefore includes the power to lease.
Answer D: Sarah only if Abigail knew Daniel had exceeded his authority.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is an agent-executed deed, and the decisive validity gate is the scope of written authority. Daniel had written authority only to lease Sarah's field for one year. He had no written authority to convey fee-simple title, so the deed exceeded the authority lock and title remains with Sarah.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because written authority about the property is not enough if it does not authorize the deed conveyance made. C reverses the relationship; authority to lease does not include authority to convey the fee.
