Question: In a federal fraud trial, Isaiah testifies that he “might have been outside” when a contract was signed. On cross-examination, opposing counsel asks, “After you admitted you were inside the room when the contract was signed, what did you hear?” Isaiah's lawyer objects that the question misstates the testimony. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the question misstates the witness's testimony by treating uncertainty as an admission.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because cross-examination questions may freely misstate the evidence.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if the jury has already found that Isaiah was outside.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because any misstatement goes only to weight.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may control questioning to avoid misleading the witness and jury. The question treats Isaiah's uncertain testimony as an admission that he was inside. That misstates the testimony and is objectionable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B overstates cross-examination freedom. C incorrectly requires a jury finding before the court can control question form.
