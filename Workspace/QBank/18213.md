


BARMATRIX Q#: 18213
Outline_code: 62050401
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Defamation
Question: A minister ordained in the Church of Love, a religious organization that is not associated in any way with the Lovers, spoke at a local meeting. The following day, a local daily newspaper printed an article about the meeting. The article referred to the minister as “a minister of the Church of Love, better known as the Makers (Lovers of God).” The minister sued the newspaper, alleging that the reference to him as a minister of “the Makers (Lovers of God)” was defamatory. In his lawsuit, the minister must prove that the newspaper
Answer A: A: knew, or that the reasonable publisher would have known, that the minister was not affiliated with the Lovers of God.
Answer B: B: entertained serious doubts about whether or not the minister was affiliated with the Lovers of God.
Answer C: C: knew that “the Makers (Lovers of God)” were associated with shame or disgrace in the minds of some readers.
Answer D: D: made the statement, but the minister is not required to prove fault since the minister is not a public person.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: In a defamation action by a private person against a professional publisher (media defendant), the plaintiff must prove either actual malice or negligence. If the newspaper knew the statement to be false, it had actual malice. If the reasonable publisher would have known the statement to be false, the newspaper acted negligently.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B describes actual malice and is incorrect because a plaintiff who is not a public person is not required to prove actual malice. C is incorrect because no liability can be imposed unless the newspaper knew, or reasonably should have known, that the statement was false. D is incorrect since liability without fault cannot be imposed in an action for defamation against a professional (media defendant) publisher.
