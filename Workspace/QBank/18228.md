Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Right to Counsel
Question: Diana was charged by information with felony theft. At her arraignment, no attorney was present because the public defender's office was understaffed. The judge asked Diana whether she wanted to plead guilty or not guilty; Diana said she did not care and pled guilty. Diana was sentenced to two years. She later retained counsel, who discovered that the prosecution's evidence was entirely circumstantial and that Diana had a strong alibi defense. Counsel moves to vacate the conviction on the ground that Diana was denied counsel at arraignment. Is Diana's conviction subject to reversal?
Answer A: No, because Diana voluntarily entered the guilty plea without objecting to the absence of counsel
Answer B: No, because Diana's statement that she "did not care" about the proceedings constitutes an implied waiver of counsel
Answer C: Yes, because Diana was denied counsel at a critical stage, which is presumptively prejudicial structural error
Answer D: Yes, but only if Diana can demonstrate a reasonable probability that the outcome would have been different with counsel present
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Arraignment is a recognized critical stage under the Sixth Amendment. The complete denial of counsel at a critical stage is structural error — it does not require the defendant to demonstrate that the outcome would have been different. Because Diana's guilty plea was entered without counsel at a critical stage, the conviction is subject to reversal without any specific prejudice showing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Arraignment is a critical stage. The complete absence of counsel at a critical stage is a structural error that does not require a showing of specific prejudice. An uncounseled waiver at such a stage is not presumed to be knowing and intelligent. B: Waiver of the Sixth Amendment right to counsel must be knowing, voluntary, and intelligent. Indifference is not a knowing, intelligent waiver, especially when the defendant did not understand the rights being surrendered. D: Strickland* prejudice applies to claims of *ineffective* assistance, not complete denial of counsel at a critical stage. Complete denial is structural error and requires no individualized prejudice showing.
lb_rule_reference: Arraignment is a critical stage of criminal prosecution; the defendant is entitled to counsel, and absence of counsel is presumptively prejudicial
