Question: In a federal civil trial, Esther offers a certified copy of a recorded deed from the county recorder's office. The copy bears the recorder's seal and certification that it is a correct copy. The opponent objects that no employee from the recorder's office is present. How should the court rule on authentication?
Answer A: Exclude the deed because a public-record custodian must testify live.
Answer B: Admit the certified copy as self-authenticating for purposes of authentication.
Answer C: Exclude the deed unless Esther personally saw the original recorded.
Answer D: Admit the deed only if the opponent stipulates to authenticity.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Certified copies of public records are self-authenticating under Rule 902. That means no extrinsic evidence of authenticity, such as live custodian testimony, is required to authenticate the copy.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a live custodian is not required for a properly certified public record copy. C: C is wrong because personal observation of the original is unnecessary. D: D is wrong because a stipulation is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 902 / self-authenticating certified public record
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests self-authentication and the live-custodian false positive.
