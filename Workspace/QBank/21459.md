MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: In general
Question: A bakery driver was delivering bread to stores on an assigned route. Halfway through the route, she drove 12 miles away from the delivery area to pick up personal concert tickets before the box office closed. She turned off the delivery app, skipped two scheduled stops, and was still driving toward the box office when she negligently hit a cyclist. Is the bakery likely vicariously liable for the driver's negligence under respondeat superior?
Answer A: Yes, because the driver was still on a paid shift and was using the bakery's delivery van
Answer B: No, because the driver was on a substantial personal frolic when the negligent driving occurred
Answer C: Yes, because delivery driving foreseeably creates traffic risks
Answer D: No, because employers are never liable for negligent driving by employees
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The driver substantially abandoned the delivery route for a personal errand unrelated to the bakery's business. Because the accident occurred before she resumed the route, respondeat superior likely does not attach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: They are not conclusive. The driver substantially left the assigned route, disabled the delivery app, skipped stops, and was pursuing a personal errand when the accident occurred. C: Foreseeability of the general driving risk does not bring a substantial personal frolic back within the scope of employment. D: Employers can be liable for negligent driving within the scope of employment. The bakery wins here because the driver was on a frolic, not because driving torts are categorically outside respondeat superior.
lb_rule_reference: An employer is not vicariously liable when an employee substantially abandons the assigned work for a personal frolic before the tort occurs.
