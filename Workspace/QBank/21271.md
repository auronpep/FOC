Question: A treatment plant created a proven nuisance by emitting odor that affected three nearby parcels. Shutting the plant immediately would leave an entire town without sewage treatment for months. A filter system can reduce the odor but requires installation time. What is the best remedial analysis?
Answer A: The court must issue an immediate shutdown injunction once nuisance is proven
Answer B: The court may consider hardship and public interest when deciding whether to award damages, delay relief, or order abatement
Answer C: The court cannot award damages for nuisance
Answer D: The court must deny all relief because the plant serves the public
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Nuisance remedy selection can balance the equities.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Equitable relief is not automatic. C: Damages are an available nuisance remedy. D: Public utility affects remedy but does not erase the nuisance.
lb_rule_reference: Nuisance remedies can include damages or injunction, and equitable relief may turn on the relative hardship, adequacy of damages, and public consequences.
