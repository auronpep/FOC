Question: A contractor sues an owner for payment on a construction project. The owner has a related defective-work claim, but full relief on that claim requires joining the architect, who is outside personal jurisdiction and cannot be joined. Is the owner's related claim necessarily a compulsory counterclaim?
Answer A: Yes, because it arises from the same construction project
Answer B: No, if adjudicating the claim requires joining a party over whom the court cannot acquire jurisdiction
Answer C: Yes, because counterclaims never require personal jurisdiction over additional parties
Answer D: No, because all construction-defect claims are permissive counterclaims
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 13(a) does not make the counterclaim compulsory under that unavailable-party condition.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The rule contains an exception when adjudication requires adding a party outside the court's reach. C: This fact pattern requires an additional party, and the rule accounts for that problem. D: Related construction claims can be compulsory unless an exception applies.
lb_rule_reference: A counterclaim is not compulsory if it requires adding another party over whom the court cannot acquire jurisdiction.
