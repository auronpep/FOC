Question: A Pennsylvania plaintiff sues a Delaware defendant in federal court in Pennsylvania for a state-law negligence claim. Pennsylvania has a two-year statute of limitations for the claim. The complaint is filed three years after the accident. No federal statute supplies a limitations period. Which limitations rule should the federal court apply?
Answer A: The Pennsylvania limitations rule, because limitations are substantive for Erie purposes in diversity
Answer B: A federal common-law limitations period, because the case is in federal court
Answer C: No limitations period, because limitations are procedural
Answer D: The defendant's home-state limitations period automatically
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Applying a different limitations period in federal court would encourage forum shopping and inequitable administration of state-created rights.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: There is no general federal limitations rule for this state-law diversity claim. C: Erie treats state statutes of limitations as substantive in this setting. D: The federal court applies the forum state's Erie/choice framework, not the defendant's citizenship as a shortcut.
lb_rule_reference: State limitations rules are treated as substantive for Erie purposes in diversity.
