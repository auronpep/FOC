Subtopic: Law Applied by Federal Courts (Erie)
Outline: Erie Doctrine
Question: In a diversity personal-injury action, the plaintiff seeks damages for a permanent back injury. The defendant moves under Rule 35 for an independent medical examination. The forum state's court rule allows such exams only if the plaintiff consents, but the defendant shows the condition is genuinely in controversy and the exam is needed. May the federal court order the examination?
Answer A: No, because Erie always requires state discovery practice in diversity
Answer B: No, because a medical exam can never be ordered without consent
Answer C: Yes, because Rule 35 directly controls and its requirements are met
Answer D: Yes, but only if state substantive tort law also favors the defendant
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal rule governs the court-ordered examination issue in federal litigation when the rule applies and is valid.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The first question is whether a valid Federal Rule directly controls. Rule 35 answers this issue. B: Rule 35 uses an in-controversy and good-cause standard; consent is not the federal prerequisite on these facts. D: The court is deciding a discovery mechanism, not the underlying tort standard.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 35 permits a federal court to order a party whose condition is in controversy to submit to a physical or mental examination on a showing of good cause.
