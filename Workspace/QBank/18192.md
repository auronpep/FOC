Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: In a federal wage case, Jacob offers 900 time cards to prove a pattern of unpaid overtime. He also offers a properly supported summary chart that accurately condenses the same time-card data. The employer objects to reading each of the 900 time cards aloud to the jury. How should the court handle the objection?
Answer A: The court may limit needless presentation of the individual cards if the summary adequately presents the relevant information.
Answer B: The court must require every time card to be read because all relevant evidence must be presented in full.
Answer C: The court must exclude the summary because summaries are always less probative than the underlying documents.
Answer D: The court must exclude all evidence of the time cards because the volume of evidence makes it confusing.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 403 permits the court to manage needless cumulative evidence and waste of time. If a proper summary captures the relevant data, the court may avoid repetitive presentation of hundreds of records.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because relevant proof may be limited when its presentation is needlessly cumulative or wasteful. C: C is wrong because summaries can be an appropriate proof vehicle when properly supported. D: D is wrong because volume alone does not require excluding all proof; the court can manage the presentation.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats Rule 403 as all-or-nothing instead of presentation control.
