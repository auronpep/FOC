Question: A mother rushed her eight-year-old daughter to the emergency room at a local hospital after the child fell off her bicycle and hit her head on a sharp rock. The wound caused by the fall was extensive and bloody. The mother was permitted to remain in the treatment room, and held the child's hand while the emergency room physician cleaned and sutured the wound. During the procedure, the mother said that she was feeling faint and stood up to leave the room. While leaving the room, the mother fainted and, in falling, struck her head on a metal fixture that protruded from the emergency room wall. She sustained a serious injury as a consequence. If the mother sues the hospital to recover damages for her injury, will she prevail? If the mother sues the hospital to recover damages for her injury, will she prevail?
Answer A: Yes, because the mother was a public invitee of the hospital's.
Answer B: Yes, because the fixture was not an obvious, commonly used, and essential part of the hospital's equipment.
Answer C: No, because there is no evidence that the hospital's personnel failed to take reasonable steps to anticipate and prevent the mother's injury.
Answer D: No, because the hospital's personnel owed the mother no affirmative duty of care.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. The mother's status as an invitee imposed a duty on the hospital to warn of known dangers, inspect for others, and make safe any that are discovered. There is no evidence that hospital personnel failed to use reasonable care in connection with the fixture, which was not hidden and there was no indication that it posed a foreseeable danger of injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The mother's status as an invitee does not make the hospital automatically liable for any and all injuries she suffers while on the hospital premises. It may carry out its duty of care, as it did in this case, and not be liable even when an invitee is injured. B is incorrect. The fixture posed no unreasonable risk of danger to those around it, which means the hospital did not breach its duty to warn of such an unforeseeable danger or make it safer somehow. The mere fact that one could experience harm from a fixture in a non-obvious way will not render the hospital liable. D is incorrect. The hospital does owe an affirmative duty to protect hospital invitees against known and discoverable hazards. The hospital did not breach its duty, however, which is why the mother will not prevail.
additional_info: A general duty of care is imposed on all human activity. An invitee is a person who enters premises in response to an express or implied invitation of the landowner. The landowner owes an invitee reasonable and ordinary care in keeping the property reasonably safe, including warning of or making safe non-obvious dangerous conditions known to the landowner, using ordinary care in active operations, making reasonable inspections to discover dangerous conditions, and thereafter making them safe. The mother's claim will not prevail because the hospital did not breach its duty of care.
