Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Nora stayed overnight at her cousin's apartment with permission. She slept on the couch, kept a small bag beside it, and planned to leave the next morning. At dawn, police entered without a warrant or exception and searched the living room, finding narcotics in Nora's bag. Nora moves to suppress the narcotics. Does Nora have standing to challenge the warrantless entry and search?
Answer A: Yes, because an overnight guest has a reasonable expectation of privacy in the host's home and in her personal bag
Answer B: Yes, but only because she owned the narcotics
Answer C: No, because she did not rent or own the apartment
Answer D: No, because guests never have standing to challenge searches of another person's home
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Overnight guests receive Fourth Amendment protection in the host's home. Nora also had a direct privacy interest in her own bag. She may challenge the warrantless entry and search.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Ownership of contraband is not the standing test. Nora's status as an overnight guest and ownership of the searched bag supply the privacy interest. C: Fourth Amendment standing is not limited to owners and tenants. Overnight guests can have a recognized privacy interest. D: Overnight guests are a protected category. Brief commercial visitors are different.
lb_rule_reference: An overnight guest may challenge a search of the host's home because society recognizes that guest's privacy interest, even without ownership or lease rights.
