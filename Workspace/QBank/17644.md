Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: In a federal civil action, Esther claims that Jonah failed to deliver 500 crates of figs. Jonah admits non-delivery but argues that a flood made delivery impossible. Esther offers evidence that Jonah's warehouse office was painted blue. Jonah objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because any fact about Jonah's warehouse provides useful background.
Answer B: Admit the evidence because all details about a party's business are relevant in a contract dispute.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence only if Jonah proves the paint color would distract the jury.
Answer D: Exclude the evidence because the office color does not affect any disputed fact of consequence.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The color of Jonah's warehouse office has no shown connection to non-delivery, impossibility, damages, or any other consequential fact. It is not enough that the evidence concerns the setting of the dispute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because background facts still need a meaningful connection to the case. B: B is wrong because business-related facts are not automatically consequential. C: C is wrong because the basic problem is relevance, not merely distraction.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Treating contextual detail as probative without identifying a consequential proposition.
