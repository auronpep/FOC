Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: Executor, a citizen of State A, sues Driver, a citizen of State B, in federal court for wrongful death. The decedent whose estate Executor represents was domiciled in State B at death. Executor argues that diversity exists because Executor and Driver are citizens of different states. Is complete diversity present?
Answer A: Yes, because the executor's personal citizenship controls
Answer B: Yes, because wrongful-death claims always arise under federal law
Answer C: No, because the estate representative is deemed a citizen of the decedent's State B domicile, matching Driver
Answer D: No, but only because the amount in controversy is unknown
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The estate takes the decedent's citizenship, destroying complete diversity with the State B defendant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: For an estate representative, the decedent's citizenship controls. B: Wrongful-death claims are usually state-law claims unless a federal cause of action is pleaded. D: The facts already reveal a complete-diversity problem, which amount cannot cure.
lb_rule_reference: The legal representative of a decedent's estate is deemed a citizen of the same state as the decedent for diversity purposes.
