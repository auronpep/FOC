Question: Abraham signed a deed conveying Pastureacre to Isaac and handed it to Isaac, saying, “This farm is yours now, but you promised to care for me. If you do not, I will want the farm back.” The deed itself contained no condition or right of reentry. Isaac accepted. Abraham later argued that no delivery occurred because his oral statement made title conditional. What is the best result?
Answer A: Delivery occurred because Abraham used present-transfer words and handed the deed to Isaac; the oral side condition does not negate delivery on these facts.
Answer B: No delivery occurred because every oral condition stated during handoff prevents title from passing.
Answer C: No delivery occurred unless Isaac first proved that he cared for Abraham.
Answer D: Delivery occurred only if Isaac recorded the deed before any dispute arose.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is present intent. Abraham said the farm was Isaac's now and delivered the deed directly to Isaac, who accepted. The oral statement about future care may create another dispute, but it does not negate the present delivery shown by the grantor's words and conduct on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because an oral side condition does not automatically prevent delivery when present intent is clear. C: C is wrong because performance of the side promise is not a prerequisite to delivery under this stem. D: D is wrong because recording is not the delivery lock.
lb_rule_reference: Direct delivery with present-transfer intent is not defeated by an oral side condition about future conduct
