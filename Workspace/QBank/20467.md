Question: Deborah hired Caleb to renovate a shop, with payment due when Caleb finished. Caleb completed nearly all the work and needed two more days to install fixtures. Deborah then changed the locks and refused to let Caleb back onto the premises because she wanted to hire a cheaper contractor. Deborah later argued that Caleb could not recover because he did not complete the renovation. What is Caleb's best response?
Answer A: Deborah cannot rely on noncompletion that she wrongfully caused; her prevention can excuse the completion condition and make her liable.
Answer B: Deborah wins because completion language is always strictly enforced regardless of who prevents completion.
Answer C: Caleb wins only if the contract expressly said Deborah had to keep the premises unlocked.
Answer D: Caleb cannot recover because prevention is relevant only to UCC goods contracts.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A party who prevents or hinders occurrence of a condition generally cannot rely on that nonoccurrence to avoid its own duty. Deborah's lockout prevented Caleb from completing the final work. The completion condition may be excused, and Caleb may seek the appropriate contract recovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a party cannot benefit from its own prevention of a condition. C: C is wrong because the duty not to hinder performance can be implied from the contract relationship. D: D is wrong because prevention doctrine is not limited to goods contracts.
