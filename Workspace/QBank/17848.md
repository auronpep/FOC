Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Amos agreed to provide custom signs for Naomi's booth at a trade show and knew Naomi had to reserve booth space and install special wiring before the signs could be used. Amos breached. Naomi's expected profits are too speculative. Naomi reasonably spent $4,000 reserving the booth and $6,000 on the required wiring. She also spent $3,000 hosting an unrelated dinner for friends during the trade show week, which Amos did not know about and which was not needed for the booth. What reliance amount is most appropriate?
Answer A: $10,000, covering the booth reservation and required wiring.
Answer B: $13,000, covering all amounts Naomi spent during the trade show week.
Answer C: $3,000, because only the dinner was a cash expense unrelated to performance.
Answer D: Nothing, because Naomi's profits are too speculative.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages include reasonable expenditures made in reliance on the contract and within the scope of the contractual project. The booth reservation and required wiring were foreseeable preparation costs. The unrelated dinner was not a contract-reliance expense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it includes an unrelated personal expense. C: C is wrong because the dinner is the least connected expense. D: D is wrong because speculative profits do not bar recovery of proven reliance expenses.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance damages / foreseeability and scope of reliance
