Outline: Gap-Fillers
Question: Miriam hired a technology consultant to design a customer database. The signed agreement included a few tablet computers for testing, but most of the price was for design, migration, and training services. The agreement stated that all changes had to be in a signed writing. After work began, Miriam orally asked the consultant to build a separate analytics dashboard not listed in the specifications. The consultant built it, and Miriam used it for two months before refusing to pay the added charge. Which route should the court use to analyze the consultant's claim for the added dashboard charge?
Answer A: Article 2 controls because tablet computers were delivered, so the oral dashboard change is enforceable without further analysis.
Answer B: The parol evidence rule bars the oral dashboard request because it was not included in the signed agreement.
Answer C: Common-law modification, waiver, or reliance principles control because the predominant transaction was services and the requested dashboard was a later scope change.
Answer D: The court must apply a UCC gap-filler for price because every omitted technology term is filled by Article 2.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The source-law lock comes first. The transaction was predominantly a services contract even though testing tablets were incidental. The dashboard request occurred after signing and changed the scope of the consultant's services. The decisive locks are source law and timing: UCC Article 2's modification rule is not the direct source, and the parol evidence rule does not bar the later request. The court should analyze the claim through common-law modification, waiver, acceptance, or reliance principles.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because incidental tablets do not convert a services engagement into a goods transaction. B: B is wrong because the request was post-signing and therefore not barred as prior or contemporaneous parol evidence. D: D is wrong because the issue is a later scope change in a services contract, not a UCC omitted-term gap.
lb_rule_reference: Common law modification / reliance; UCC source-law contrast
