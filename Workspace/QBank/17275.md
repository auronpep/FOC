Outline: Power of termination
Question: Rachel conveyed Lotacre “to Leah and her heirs, but if the land is ever used for commercial parking, Rachel may enter and terminate the estate.” Leah later operated a paid parking lot on the land. Rachel promptly filed an action to terminate Leah's estate, and the court granted the requested relief. Who owns Lotacre after the court grants termination?
Answer A: Rachel, because she successfully exercised the retained right of entry.
Answer B: Leah, because a fee simple subject to condition subsequent can never be forfeited.
Answer C: Leah, because title could shift only if the deed used “so long as.”
Answer D: Rachel and Leah as tenants in common, because both have ownership-related interests.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rachel owns Lotacre after successfully exercising the right of entry. Before exercise, Leah retained title despite the breach. Once Rachel took the required action and obtained termination, Leah's defeasible estate ended and Rachel's retained power became possessory ownership.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a condition subsequent may be enforced when the power of termination is properly exercised. C: C is wrong because “so long as” would create automatic termination, but right-of-entry language can still support forfeiture after action. D: D is wrong because the deed does not create concurrent ownership.
lb_rule_reference: Power of termination / effect of successful exercise
