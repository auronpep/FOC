Question: Abraham contracted with David to sell a set of wagons. David promised Abraham that he would deliver one wagon to Rachel, Abraham's daughter. Rachel did not know about the contract and took no action in reliance on it. Before Rachel learned of the promise, Abraham and David signed a release eliminating the wagon-to-Rachel clause. Rachel later learned of the original promise and sued David. What is the likely result?
Answer A: Rachel can enforce because she was Abraham's daughter.
Answer B: Rachel can enforce because family beneficiaries vest immediately at contract formation.
Answer C: Rachel cannot enforce because the original parties released the beneficiary term before Rachel's rights vested.
Answer D: Rachel cannot enforce only if David proves Rachel expressly rejected the wagon.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rachel may have been an intended beneficiary, but her rights had not vested because she had not assented, relied, sued, or accepted performance. Before vesting, the promisor and promisee generally may release or modify the beneficiary promise.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because family relationship supports intent but does not create automatic vesting. B: B is wrong because vesting does not occur automatically for family beneficiaries. D: D is wrong because no rejection is required when the original parties modified before vesting.
