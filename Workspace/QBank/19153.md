Question: A woman signed up for a bowling class. Before allowing the woman to bowl, the instructor required her to sign a waiver explicitly stating that she assumed all risk of injuries that she might suffer in connection with the class, including injuries due to negligence or any other fault. After she signed the waiver, the woman was injured when the instructor negligently dropped a bowling ball on the woman's foot. The woman brought a negligence action against the instructor. The instructor has filed a motion for summary judgment based on the waiver. What is the woman's best argument in opposition to the instructor's motion? What is the woman's best argument in opposition to the instructor's motion?
Answer A: Bowling is an inherently dangerous activity.
Answer B: In circumstances like these, it is against public policy to enforce agreements that insulate people from the consequences of their own negligence.
Answer C: It was unreasonable to require the woman to sign the waiver before she was allowed to bowl.
Answer D: When she signed the form, the woman could not foresee that the instructor would drop a bowling ball on her foot.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. This applies an exception to the general enforceability of liability waivers based on public policy grounds. The woman should argue that it would be unfair to preclude her from raising any liability claims because such a practice reduces businesses' incentive to take reasonable precautions against harm if they face no risk of negligence liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: {"A": "A is incorrect. Bowling is not inherently dangerous. additional_info: A plaintiff is said to have assumed the risk of certain harm if she has voluntarily consented to take her chances that the harm will occur. If a plaintiff explicitly agrees, before any harm occurs, that she will not hold the defendant liable for certain harm, the plaintiff is said to have expressly assumed the risk of that harm. An express assumption of risk, also called a contractual limitation of liability, is generally enforceable, which will completely bar the plaintiff from recovery. However, there are three exceptions: intentional, reckless, or grossly negligent conduct; grossly unequal bargaining power, usually for essential goods or services; and overriding public interest. To enforce fine print waivers of liability, the defendant must show actual or reasonable awareness. B is correct because the most effective argument is that public policy should prevent enforcement of this broad waiver as applied to the instructor's own negligence. A is incorrect because bowling is not inherently dangerous and, if it were, that would tend to support assumption-of-risk reasoning. C is incorrect because the problem is not merely requiring a waiver before the class. D is incorrect because exact foresight of the specific injury is not dispositive.
