Question: Samuel conveyed Stoneacre “to Joshua so long as the land is used for worship.” Later, in a jurisdiction following the modern rule that retained future interests are transferable, Samuel validly conveyed “all future interests retained in Stoneacre” to Caleb. Joshua then stopped using the land for worship. Who owns Stoneacre after worship use stops?
Answer A: Caleb, because he acquired Samuel's possibility of reverter and the determinable fee ended automatically.
Answer B: Samuel, because a possibility of reverter can never be transferred during life.
Answer C: Joshua, unless Caleb first exercises a right of entry.
Answer D: Joshua in fee simple absolute, because Samuel's later deed did not repeat the worship limitation.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Caleb owns Stoneacre. Samuel retained a possibility of reverter when he created the fee simple determinable. The stem states that the jurisdiction follows the modern rule allowing transfer of retained future interests, so Caleb acquired that possibility of reverter. When worship use stopped, Joshua's estate ended automatically and title shifted to Caleb.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the stem supplies the modern transferability rule. C: C is wrong because a possibility of reverter does not require reentry. D: D is wrong because Samuel's later conveyance of the retained future interest did not erase the limitation on Joshua's estate.
lb_rule_reference: Modern transferability of possibility of reverter
