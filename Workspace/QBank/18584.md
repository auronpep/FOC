Question: Daniel is charged in federal court. The court orders Daniel to meet with Dr. Matthew for a competency evaluation and tells Daniel that Dr. Matthew will report his findings to the court. Daniel later objects that the report is protected by psychotherapist-patient privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because any conversation with a mental-health professional is privileged.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Daniel signed a written waiver.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if Dr. Matthew is not licensed.
Answer D: Overrule the objection, because the communication occurred in a court-ordered forensic evaluation, not a confidential treatment relationship.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Psychotherapist privilege protects confidential treatment communications. A court-ordered competency evaluation disclosed as reportable to the court is not the same as confidential therapy. The decisive axis is treatment confidentiality versus forensic evaluation purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is the relationship shortcut. A mental-health professional's involvement is not enough. B: B is wrong because A written waiver is unnecessary where the communication was never confidential treatment in the privilege sense. C: C is wrong because Licensure is not the defect. The issue is the nonconfidential forensic purpose.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / confidential treatment versus forensic evaluation
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is focusing on the professional title while missing the purpose and confidentiality of the encounter.
