Outline: Offers of proof
Question: After excluding testimony from Elijah, the court allows Elijah's lawyer to preserve the record. The lawyer begins a long narrative summary of what Elijah would say. The judge interrupts and states, “To make the record clear, ask Elijah the proposed questions and have him answer outside the jury's hearing.” Is the judge permitted to require that format?
Answer A: No, because only a lawyer's narrative summary can be an offer of proof.
Answer B: No, because a witness may never answer excluded questions even outside the jury's presence.
Answer C: Yes, because the court may direct that an offer of proof be made in question-and-answer form.
Answer D: Yes, but only if the opposing party agrees to the format.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may direct the character or form of the offer of proof and may require question-and-answer form. Doing so can make the record clearer and preserve the jury from hearing excluded evidence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a narrative offer is not the exclusive method. B: B is wrong because the witness may be examined outside the jury's hearing for the offer of proof. D: D is wrong because the court's authority to direct the form does not depend on the opponent's consent.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(c) / court may direct form of offer
