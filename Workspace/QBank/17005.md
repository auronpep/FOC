Outline: Erie Doctrine
Question: In a diversity tort case, the state's intermediate appellate court has squarely interpreted the relevant statute. The state's highest court has not addressed the issue. The losing party argues that the federal judge should disregard the intermediate decision because it is not the highest court. How should the federal court treat the intermediate appellate decision?
Answer A: Ignore it automatically because only state supreme court decisions count
Answer B: Follow it unless persuaded the state's highest court would decide differently
Answer C: Replace it with federal common law because federal courts are independent
Answer D: Certify every such case to the state high court before ruling
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Erie prediction uses available state-law authority; an on-point intermediate appellate decision is not discarded merely because the high court is silent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intermediate appellate decisions are strong evidence of state law and generally should be followed absent persuasive contrary evidence. C: The federal court's independence does not allow it to invent substantive state law in diversity. D: Certification may be available, but it is not required in every unsettled state-law case.
lb_rule_reference: A federal diversity court should generally follow a state's intermediate appellate decision unless there is persuasive evidence the state's highest court would decide differently.
