


BARMATRIX Q#: 22856
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: An art buyer, a citizen of State A, went to Paris, France to buy several paintings from an art dealer. The buyer bought three paintings. When the art buyer returned home to State A, he decided he did not like the paintings and refused to pay the art dealer the $150,000 due under the sales contract. In response, the art dealer, a French citizen, sued the art buyer in federal court in State A. The art buyer moved to dismiss the claim, arguing that the State A federal court did not have subject matter jurisdiction. How should the court rule?
Answer A: A: Grant the motion, because the art dealer is a French citizen.
Answer B: B: Grant the motion, because the sale took place in France.
Answer C: C: Deny the motion, because federal courts have jurisdiction over all claims filed by foreign nationals against United States citizens.
Answer D: D: Deny the motion, because diversity of citizenship exists and the amount in controversy is $150,000.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under 28 U.S.C. §1332(a)(2), federal district courts have subject matter jurisdiction over claims between United States citizens and citizens or subjects of foreign states if the amount in controversy is over $75,000. Here, the art buyer is a citizen of State A, the art dealer is a French citizen, and the amount in controversy is $150,000. Consequently, the federal district court would have subject matter jurisdiction over the claim. A and B are incorrect because the federal district court would have subject matter jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. §1332(a)(2). C is incorrect because federal courts do not automatically have jurisdiction over all claims filed by foreign nationals.
