Outline: Preliminary questions
Question: Sarah sues Peter for battery. Sarah offers testimony that Jacob, who is unavailable, said shortly after the incident, “Peter struck me first.” Peter objects that the statement does not qualify as a statement against interest. Sarah asks the court to let the jury decide whether Jacob's statement was sufficiently against Jacob's interest when made. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Submit the question to the jury, because the statement's reliability affects the weight of the evidence.
Answer B: Decide the preliminary admissibility question itself, including whether the statement fits the asserted hearsay exception.
Answer C: Admit the statement automatically because Sarah has offered it in a civil case rather than a criminal case.
Answer D: Require Peter to make an offer of proof before the court can decide the objection.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court decides preliminary questions about whether evidence is admissible, including whether a hearsay exception or other admissibility condition is satisfied. The jury may later weigh admitted evidence, but it does not decide the threshold admissibility of the statement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reliability and weight issues do not move the threshold admissibility decision from the judge to the jury. C: C is wrong because the civil posture does not automatically admit a hearsay statement. D: D is wrong because an offer of proof is used by the proponent to preserve excluded evidence; Peter is objecting to admission.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a) / judge decides preliminary admissibility facts
