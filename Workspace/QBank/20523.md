Question: Naomi orally promises a bank that she will pay her son Benjamin's business debt if Benjamin defaults. The next day, Naomi signs a letter to Benjamin stating, “I promised the bank I would guarantee your $30,000 business loan if you fail to pay.” Benjamin defaults, and the bank sues Naomi. Naomi argues that the letter cannot satisfy the Statute of Frauds because it was addressed to Benjamin, not to the bank. Is Naomi's letter potentially sufficient to satisfy the suretyship writing requirement?
Answer A: No, because a suretyship memorandum must be addressed to the creditor.
Answer B: No, because a guaranty must always be signed by both the debtor and the guarantor.
Answer C: Yes, but only if Benjamin signed the letter too.
Answer D: Yes, because a signed memorandum by the party to be charged can satisfy the writing requirement even if addressed to a third person, so long as it sufficiently identifies the promise and parties.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A writing satisfying the Statute of Frauds need not be addressed to the party seeking enforcement. The key is that the memorandum is signed by the party to be charged and sufficiently indicates the essential promise and parties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because audience is not necessarily fatal. B: B is wrong because the party to be charged is the guarantor, Naomi. C: C is wrong because Benjamin's signature is not required to enforce Naomi's promise against Naomi.
