Question: A state requires packaged snack advertisements to disclose the product's grams of added sugar in a small factual label. The rule does not ban any ad, require ideological language, or single out a viewpoint. A manufacturer argues the disclosure must satisfy strict scrutiny because it compels speech. What is the best response?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny automatically applies to every factual commercial disclosure
Answer B: The disclosure is reviewed more deferentially because it requires factual commercial information rather than banning speech
Answer C: No review applies because commercial speech has no protection
Answer D: The rule is invalid per se because the label might affect consumer choices
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule requires factual product information in commercial advertising and does not suppress a message.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Factual, uncontroversial commercial disclosures receive more deferential review than ideological compulsion. C: Commercial speech is protected, though disclosure rules use a distinct analysis. D: A factual disclosure is not invalid merely because consumers may react to the information.
lb_rule_reference: Required disclosure of factual, uncontroversial information in commercial advertising is generally reviewed more deferentially than a ban on truthful commercial speech.
