Question: Gideon is charged in federal court with knowingly possessing a stolen seal. The government offers evidence that Gideon hid the seal when officers approached. The judge proposes to instruct: “You may consider the hiding as evidence of consciousness of guilt. But you must find that Gideon knew the seal was stolen if you find that he hid it.” Gideon objects. Which ruling is correct?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because the hiding evidence is the proper proof channel for consciousness of guilt.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because Gideon's purpose in hiding the seal is a collateral issue.
Answer C: Sustain the objection to the must-find sentence, while allowing a properly framed permissive inference instruction if supported by the evidence.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if the hiding evidence is inadmissible for every purpose.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The jury may be allowed to consider hiding as circumstantial evidence, but the court may not require the jury to find the element of knowledge from that fact. The proper remedy is to remove the mandatory language while preserving any valid permissive inference.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a proper proof channel does not justify mandatory language. B: B is wrong because knowledge is an element, not merely collateral. D: D is wrong because evidence can support a limited permissive inference even though mandatory use is improper.
lb_rule_reference: Mandatory presumption / limited-use decoy
