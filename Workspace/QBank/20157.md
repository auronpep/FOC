Question: In a federal civil fraud case, Ruth calls Jacob, the defendant, during Ruth's case-in-chief. Ruth's lawyer asks Jacob, “You signed the invoice on March 3, correct?” Jacob's lawyer objects that Ruth is using leading questions on direct examination. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a party who calls a witness may never lead that witness.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because Ruth is calling an adverse party and leading questions are ordinarily permitted in that setting.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Jacob first refuses to answer.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Jacob has already contradicted a prior statement.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Although leading questions generally are disfavored on direct, they are ordinarily allowed when a party calls an adverse party or a witness identified with an adverse party. Jacob is the opposing party, so the court should allow leading questions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A ignores the adverse-party exception. C invents a refusal requirement. D confuses leading an adverse party with impeachment by prior inconsistent statement.
