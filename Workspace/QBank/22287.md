


BARMATRIX Q#: 22287
Outline_code: 75090400
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Right to Counsel
Question: A man moved to the United States from a foreign country when he was 10 years old. Although he never became a citizen, he lived in the United States for the next 30 years. After a sting operation, the man was arrested and charged with drug crimes. The case against the man was very strong, and the man's attorney advised him to plead guilty in exchange for a lesser sentence even though the man wanted to go to trial. The man asked his attorney if he could be deported based on the guilty plea, and the man's attorney assured him that he could not. Based on the attorney's advice, the man pled guilty. However, the man's plea resulted in a conviction for an aggravated felony, which was a deportable offense. The man appealed his conviction, arguing that he had received ineffective assistance of counsel. Does the man have a valid claim for ineffective assistance of counsel?
Answer A: A: No, because the man was not a United States citizen.
Answer B: B: No, because the case against the man was very strong.
Answer C: C: Yes, because there was a chance the man would have been acquitted if he went to trial.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the man would not have pled guilty except for the attorney's erroneous advice.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A person can make a valid claim of ineffective counsel under the Sixth Amendment by showing that the attorney's performance was deficient and that the proceeding would have had a different result except for the deficient performance. Lee v. United States, 137 S. Ct. 1958 (2017). Here, the attorney incorrectly told the man his plea could not result in deportation, and the man only pled guilty because of the attorney's assurance. A is incorrect because noncitizens are also protected by the Sixth Amendment. B is incorrect because the important point is that the man wouldn't have pled guilty except for the attorney's bad advice. This is true even if the government had a strong case against him. C is incorrect because the man's claim is not valid simply because things might have worked out for him.
