MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Three people entered an apartment for a two-hour cash-for-stolen-laptops transaction. They had never met the tenant before, brought no overnight items, and had no social relationship with the tenant. Police entered without a warrant and found the laptops on the kitchen table. One visitor moves to suppress the laptops based on the entry into the apartment. Does the visitor have standing to challenge the entry?
Answer A: Yes, because every person inside a home receives the same Fourth Amendment protection as the tenant
Answer B: Yes, because the visitor was charged with possessing the laptops found during the search
Answer C: No, because the visitor was present only briefly for a commercial transaction and had no recognized privacy interest in the apartment
Answer D: No, because people engaged in crimes lose all Fourth Amendment protection
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A short-term commercial visitor with no overnight stay, belongings, social relationship, or property connection generally lacks a reasonable expectation of privacy in the host's home. The visitor cannot suppress based on the tenant's rights.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The home's protection is not automatically transferable to all persons present. A brief commercial visitor with no connection to the premises may lack standing. B: Standing does not arise because evidence will be used against the defendant. It requires invasion of the defendant's own protected interest. D: The problem is not that the transaction was criminal. The problem is the visitor's limited connection to the premises. Criminal activity does not automatically erase Fourth Amendment rights.
lb_rule_reference: Presence in a residence does not automatically create a reasonable expectation of privacy; purpose, duration, and connection to the premises matter.
