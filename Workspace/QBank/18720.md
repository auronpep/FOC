Outline: Nature and Type
Question: Samuel owned a field. At a family meeting, Samuel signed a paper stating, “It is my wish that David receive this field someday.” The paper identified the field and David, but Samuel kept the paper in his desk and never signed any deed using words of present transfer. David later claimed that the paper conveyed the field to him. Who owns the field?
Answer A: David, because Samuel signed a writing identifying both the field and David.
Answer B: Samuel, because the paper expressed a wish for a future transfer and did not operate as a delivered present conveyance.
Answer C: David, because consideration is not required when the transfer is a family gift.
Answer D: Samuel only because David failed to record the paper.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The asserted title source is a signed writing, but the decisive validity gates are present transfer language and delivery as a deed. Samuel's paper expressed a future wish, not a present conveyance, and he kept it in his desk. The no-consideration rule does not cure the missing present-transfer and delivery locks, so Samuel retains title.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because identification of land and grantee is not enough without operative present transfer and delivery. C: C is wrong because a family gift still needs a valid delivered deed to transfer legal title. D: D is wrong because the defect is not failure to record; the paper did not pass title in the first place.
lb_rule_reference: Wish or recommendation is not a present conveyance
