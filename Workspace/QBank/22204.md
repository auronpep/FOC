


BARMATRIX Q#: 22204
Outline_code: 33040301
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Hearsay and Circumstances of Its Admissibility
Outline: Requiring unavailability of declarant
Question: A man's brother brought the man to the hospital after the brother found the man in his apartment suffering from a gunshot wound. The doctors only gave the man a few moments to live. The man pulled his brother to him and said, “I'm dying. I want you to know that I was shot trying to rob a drug dealer with my friend. My friend killed the drug dealer after the drug dealer shot me.” Soon thereafter, against all odds, the man recovered and fled to a small island off the coast of South America. The friend was later prosecuted for killing the drug dealer, and the prosecution called the man's brother to testify to the above statement by the man. The man's statement is
Answer A: A: admissible as a dying declaration.
Answer B: B: admissible as an excited utterance.
Answer C: C: inadmissible because the man did not die.
Answer D: D: inadmissible because it was hearsay not within any exception.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: If the declarant is unavailable as a witness, a statement made under the belief of impending death is admissible under an exception to the hearsay rule. To be admissible under this exception, the declarant must be unavailable, the declarant must have believed that his or her death was imminent, the statement must concern the cause or circumstances of the declarant's imminent death, and the statement must be offered in a civil proceeding or a criminal homicide prosecution. Importantly, while the declarant must be aware of his or her impending death, he or she does not have to actually die. Here, since the man has fled to a small island off the coast of South America and moved beyond the court's subpoena power, the exception applies. C and D are therefore incorrect. An excited utterance is an exception for certain statements made under the influence of a startling event. Here, the declarant has been brought to the hospital and the shooting took place at some time before. Thus, the statement was likely made too far after the time the man was shot. B is therefore not as strong an answer as A.
