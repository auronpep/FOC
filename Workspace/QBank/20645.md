Outline: Marital Communications Privilege
Question: Mark sent an email to his assistant James about altering inventory numbers and copied his wife Naomi because Naomi also worked in the business. The email was not personal and was sent in the ordinary business chain. In a later federal prosecution, Mark claims marital communications privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because Naomi was Mark's wife when she received the email.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because copying a spouse on a business email to a third-party assistant is not a confidential marital communication.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless James testifies first.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Naomi was paid for her work.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The communication was not confidentially made between spouses; it was a business email sent to a third-party assistant with Naomi copied in her business role. The decisive axis is confidentiality and communicative purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Marriage alone does not convert a business-chain email into a privileged marital communication. C: C is wrong because James's testimony order does not cure or create marital confidentiality. D: D is wrong because Naomi's compensation is not the controlling fact; the email was not confidential between spouses.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / marital purpose and confidentiality
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating spouse involvement as enough without checking whether the communication was confidentially marital.
