Outline: Intermediate scrutiny (quasi-suspect) classifications
Question: A state's military academy has offered an all-male rigorous military training program for 150 years. The state argues that a uniquely adversarial, physically demanding, and psychologically intense training environment produces a specific kind of officer character, and that including women would require modifying the program in ways that would alter its distinctive pedagogy. The state does not operate a comparable program for women. A female applicant who meets all physical and academic criteria is denied admission solely because of sex. Under Equal Protection intermediate scrutiny, what must the state demonstrate to uphold the sex-based exclusion?
Answer A: The state must show the exclusion is rationally related to a legitimate interest in operating a specialized military academy
Answer B: The state need only show that some evidence supports the pedagogical difference between single-sex and coeducational military training
Answer C: The state must demonstrate that the all-male program serves an important governmental interest and that the sex-based exclusion is substantially related to that interest — and the justification must be exceedingly persuasive, not merely plausible
Answer D: The state must show the exclusion is the least restrictive means of achieving a compelling state interest, because the all-male policy is the functional equivalent of a racial classification in its severity
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under intermediate scrutiny, the government bears the burden of showing the sex-based classification is substantially related to an important governmental interest, and the standard under United States v. Virginia requires an exceedingly persuasive justification. Generalizations about military pedagogy or historical tradition will not carry the burden. The state must offer actual evidence, not stereotypes or tradition.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Sex-based classifications trigger intermediate scrutiny, not rational basis. The state must demonstrate that the exclusion is substantially related to an important governmental interest, and under United States v. Virginia the justification must be exceedingly persuasive. Rational basis is too low a bar. B: While courts do extend some deference to military and educational judgments, intermediate scrutiny still requires the government to demonstrate a substantially important interest and a tight, substantive connection between the sex-based classification and that interest. The "exceedingly persuasive justification" standard is meaningful and demanding — generalized assertions about institutional tradition are not sufficient. D: The Supreme Court has established a distinct tier for sex-based classifications: intermediate scrutiny, not strict scrutiny. Sex is a quasi-suspect, not suspect, classification. Applying strict scrutiny to gender conflates the two tiers. United States v. Virginia applied intermediate scrutiny, not strict scrutiny, to strike down the VMI exclusion.
lb_rule_reference: Sex-based classifications receive intermediate scrutiny: the government must show the classification is substantially related to the achievement of an important governmental interest, and the justification must be exceedingly persuasive.
