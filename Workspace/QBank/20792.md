Question: Noah signed a written power authorizing Daniel to convey Noah's warehouse to Abigail. The deed stated, “Noah conveys the warehouse to Abigail by Daniel, his agent.” Daniel signed the deed “Daniel, agent for Noah,” delivered it to Abigail, and Abigail accepted. Noah later argued that title did not pass because Daniel signed Daniel's own name rather than writing Noah's signature. Who owns the warehouse?
Answer A: Noah, because an agent must write only the principal's exact name and may not sign the agent's own name.
Answer B: Noah, because the power of attorney had to be attached to the deed for the deed to be valid.
Answer C: Noah, because Abigail had to sign Daniel's power of attorney before accepting the deed.
Answer D: Abigail, because the deed identified Noah as principal and Daniel signed within written authority as Noah's agent.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed executed by an authorized agent, and the decisive validity gate is written authority plus an agency signature that identifies the principal. Daniel had written authority and signed as agent for Noah on a deed that identified Noah as the grantor. Exact reproduction of Noah's personal signature was not the controlling lock, so title passed to Abigail.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an authorized agent may sign in a representative form that identifies the principal. B: B is wrong because attachment or recording of the power is not required for baseline validity on these facts. C: C is wrong because the grantee need not sign the principal's authorization.
