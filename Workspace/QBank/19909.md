Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Rachel hired Jonah to inspect a factory and prepare a written safety report. The report would be printed and delivered in a binder, but nearly all of the price reflected Jonah's inspection time, judgment, and analysis. Jonah later argued that Article 2 governed because a physical report would be delivered. Which law most likely governs the contract?
Answer A: Article 2, because a physical report in a binder is movable.
Answer B: Common law, because the dominant purpose is Jonah's inspection and professional analysis, not the sale of a binder.
Answer C: Article 2, because any contract with a written deliverable is a goods contract.
Answer D: Neither Article 2 nor common law, because professional judgment cannot be part of a contract.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The source-law lock asks for the transaction's dominant purpose. The physical binder is incidental to Jonah's service of inspecting and analyzing the factory. Common law most likely controls the contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because incidental physical delivery does not make the transaction a goods sale. C: C is wrong because a written deliverable is not automatically a good that controls source law. D: D is wrong because professional services can be contracted for.
lb_rule_reference: common_law
lb_student_diagnostic: Student mistakes a tangible output for a goods-dominant transaction.
