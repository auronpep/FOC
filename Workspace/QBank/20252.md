Outline: Defamation
Question: A blogger saw an unverified post falsely accusing a local nurse of stealing patient medication. Without checking, the blogger reposted the accusation to 8,000 followers and added, "This looks true." The original poster later deleted the claim. The nurse sues the blogger for defamation. Can the blogger avoid liability merely because he did not create the original accusation?
Answer A: Yes, because only the original speaker can publish defamation
Answer B: Yes, because adding "looks true" made it opinion
Answer C: No, because the blogger republished a defamatory factual accusation to third parties
Answer D: No, but only because the original poster deleted the claim
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The blogger communicated the false theft accusation to thousands of followers. Republishing can satisfy publication.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Repeating defamatory matter to third parties is a new publication. B: The repost repeated a verifiable factual accusation of theft. D: The blogger's own publication occurred when he reposted the accusation.
lb_rule_reference: A person who repeats or republishes a defamatory statement to a third party can be liable as a publisher, even if someone else originated the statement.
