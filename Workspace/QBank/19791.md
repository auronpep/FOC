Outline: Freedom of religion
Question: A city contracts with nonprofit shelters to provide overnight beds during a freeze. A religious charity is selected under neutral criteria and must use city funds only for beds and meals, not worship. A challenger argues the contract is establishment solely because the provider is religious. What is the city's best response?
Answer A: A religious organization is not automatically excluded from a neutral secular-service program
Answer B: The contract is invalid because public money may never be paid to a religious charity
Answer C: The contract is valid only if the charity stops being religious during the contract period
Answer D: The contract is invalid unless all shelters are religious
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Equal participation in a neutral program for secular services does not automatically establish religion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Religious status alone does not bar participation in neutral public programs. C: Government cannot demand loss of religious identity as the price of equal access. D: Neutrality means religious and secular providers may compete on equal terms, not that all providers must share a religious status.
lb_rule_reference: Government may include religious organizations in neutral public programs or contracts on equal terms when funds support secular services and the government does not sponsor religious exercise.
