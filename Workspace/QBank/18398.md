MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: False imprisonment
Question: A private security guard at a museum wore a badge that looked like a police badge. After accusing a visitor of touching a painting, the guard said, "You are under arrest. Sit in this office until I release you." The visitor reasonably believed the guard had legal authority to arrest her and remained in the windowless office for thirty minutes. The guard had no such authority. The visitor sues for false imprisonment. Is the visitor likely to prevail?
Answer A: No, because the office door was not locked
Answer B: No, because a private guard can never falsely imprison someone without touching them
Answer C: Yes, because the guard intentionally confined the visitor by false asserted legal authority
Answer D: Yes, but only if the visitor proves the painting accusation was false
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The visitor was intentionally kept in a bounded office and reasonably submitted to the guard's false claim of arrest authority. Awareness was present.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Confinement can be created by threats or asserted legal authority. A locked door is not required. B: Physical force is not required if asserted authority reasonably causes the plaintiff to remain confined. D: False imprisonment turns on unlawful confinement and privilege, not the truth of the accusation by itself.
lb_rule_reference: False imprisonment can be accomplished by an asserted legal authority or threat that makes the plaintiff reasonably believe leaving is not permitted.
