Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Damages
Question: At a private club dinner, Guest falsely said to three people, "I think Nora is rude and unreliable about social plans." The statement did not accuse Nora of a crime, professional misconduct, loathsome disease, or sexual misconduct. Nora was upset but lost no money or business opportunity. Nora sues for slander. Is Nora likely to recover without proving special damages?
Answer A: Yes, because all false spoken statements presume damages
Answer B: Yes, because Nora was emotionally upset
Answer C: No, because this is ordinary slander and Nora has no special damages
Answer D: No, because a statement at a dinner is never publication
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The spoken statement did not fall within a per se category, and Nora cannot show pecuniary loss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ordinary slander generally requires special damages unless it falls in a per se category. B: Special damages mean pecuniary loss, not hurt feelings alone. D: Communication to three third parties is publication. The claim fails on damages, not publication.
lb_rule_reference: Ordinary slander outside a per se category generally requires proof of special damages.
