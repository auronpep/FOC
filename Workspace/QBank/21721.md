Question: Before taking office, a candidate signs a private campaign contract with a media consultant. During the presidency, the consultant sues the President personally for unpaid campaign fees. The President argues that any civil suit against a sitting President must be dismissed because the office requires complete immunity from private litigation. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The suit is barred because the President has absolute immunity from all civil litigation while in office
Answer B: The suit is not barred by official-act immunity because the alleged conduct was private campaign conduct outside presidential duties
Answer C: The suit is barred because the consultant waited until after inauguration to sue
Answer D: The suit is barred only if Congress approves the contract
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A private campaign contract is not an official presidential act. The President's official-act immunity does not shield that unofficial conduct from civil litigation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Absolute civil damages immunity protects official acts, not unofficial private or campaign conduct. C: Timing alone does not transform private campaign conduct into an official act. D: Congressional approval of the contract is irrelevant to whether the conduct was official or unofficial for immunity purposes.
lb_rule_reference: Presidential immunity for official acts does not shield unofficial conduct, including conduct undertaken in a private or campaign capacity.
