Question: A household cleaner's formula produced a toxic vapor when mixed with common bleach. Before sale, the manufacturer had customer reports and internal tests showing that consumers often used bleach nearby while cleaning bathrooms. The bottle label said only, "Use in a well-ventilated area," and did not mention bleach or toxic vapor. A consumer mixed the cleaner with bleach while cleaning a shower and suffered lung irritation. The consumer sues the manufacturer under strict products liability for failure to warn. Is the consumer likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim for failure to warn?
Answer A: No, because the label contained some warning about ventilation
Answer B: Yes, because the bleach-vapor risk was foreseeable, nonobvious, known before sale, and not adequately warned
Answer C: No, because consumers are expected to know that all cleaners are dangerous
Answer D: Yes, because every dangerous product must warn against every possible misuse
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The manufacturer knew or should have known of a nonobvious bleach-mixing hazard and gave only a vague warning. An adequate warning could have reduced the consumer's injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A warning must be adequate for the specific foreseeable nonobvious risk. A generic ventilation statement does not disclose the known bleach-mixing toxic vapor hazard. C: General awareness that cleaners can be dangerous does not make this specific toxic-vapor interaction obvious. The risk was hidden and known to the manufacturer. D: The duty is not to warn against every possible risk. It is to warn adequately about foreseeable nonobvious risks that warnings can reduce.
lb_rule_reference: A seller must give an adequate warning of foreseeable nonobvious risks known or knowable at sale when the warning could reduce the injury.
