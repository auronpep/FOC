Question: A state criminalizes illegal prizefighting between two fighters. Two fighters agree to hold an illegal match, and three promoters agree to arrange the venue, sell tickets, and collect proceeds. Prosecutors charge all five with conspiracy to conduct illegal prizefighting. The defendants invoke Wharton's Rule. What is the best response?
Answer A: Wharton's Rule bars the conspiracy charge because illegal prizefighting requires two fighters
Answer B: Wharton's Rule does not bar the charge because the agreement includes extra participants beyond the necessary fighters
Answer C: Wharton's Rule bars the charge only because the promoters expected payment
Answer D: Wharton's Rule always bars conspiracy whenever the target offense involves any agreement
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Wharton's Rule is a narrow limitation. It does not apply when additional conspirators beyond those necessary for the target offense join the agreement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The conspiracy includes three additional promoters beyond the minimum persons needed for the offense. That extra-party feature takes the case outside Wharton's Rule. C: The key fact is the presence of additional participants, not whether they expected payment. D: Wharton's Rule is narrow and subject to exceptions, including the extra-participant exception.
lb_rule_reference: Wharton's Rule does not bar conspiracy liability when the agreement includes more participants than are necessary to commit the target offense.
