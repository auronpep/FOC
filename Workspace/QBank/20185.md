Question: Rachel agreed to buy Isaac's farm. The contract stated, “Buyer's duty to close is conditioned on Buyer obtaining a farm loan by June 1.” The loan condition was included to protect Rachel. On June 1, Rachel told Isaac she would close using savings even though the loan had not been approved. Isaac refused to close, arguing that the financing condition failed. Who is most likely to prevail?
Answer A: Isaac, because any failed condition automatically excuses both parties.
Answer B: Isaac, because Rachel could waive the condition only with new consideration.
Answer C: Rachel, because the financing condition protected her and she could waive it.
Answer D: Rachel, but only if the lender later approved the loan.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A condition inserted for one party's protection may generally be waived by the protected party. The financing condition protected Rachel's ability to avoid closing without financing. She could waive that protection and proceed with savings; Isaac cannot invoke the nonoccurrence to avoid his duty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every condition protects both parties. B: B is wrong because waiver of a condition does not necessarily require new consideration. D: D is wrong because Rachel waived the condition; later loan approval is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Condition beneficiary may waive condition
