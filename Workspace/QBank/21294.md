Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Use of impeachment material
Question: In a federal slip-and-fall trial, Esther testifies that she saw water on the floor before Jacob fell. On cross-examination, Esther also says she entered the courthouse that morning through the north door. The defendant then offers courthouse security footage showing that Esther entered through the south door. The defendant offers the footage only to contradict Esther about which door she used that morning. Jacob objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the footage because any false statement by a witness may be contradicted extrinsically.
Answer B: Admit the footage because it proves Esther is generally untruthful.
Answer C: Exclude the footage because the door through which Esther entered court is collateral to the issues and is offered only for contradiction.
Answer D: Exclude the footage only if Esther is a criminal defendant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Extrinsic evidence offered solely to contradict a witness on a collateral matter may be excluded. The door Esther used to enter court has no independent relevance to the slip-and-fall or her perception of the floor; it is offered only to show a minor contradiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every contradiction may be proved extrinsically. B: B is wrong because a minor collateral contradiction does not become general truthfulness-character proof. D: D is wrong because the collateral-impeachment limit is not limited to criminal defendants.
lb_rule_reference: Collateral contradiction / extrinsic impeachment material
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the collateral-extrinsic bar and prevents students from treating all contradictions as admissible impeachment material.
