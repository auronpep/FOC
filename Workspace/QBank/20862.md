Question: Aaron held a recorded easement to use Naomi's road to reach Aaron's orchard. The easement did not permit parking. Aaron began parking trucks on the road overnight, blocking Naomi's use. Naomi sued, asking the court to terminate Aaron's access easement entirely. What is the most likely remedy?
Answer A: Automatic termination of the entire easement because any misuse destroys all easement rights.
Answer B: No remedy for Naomi because an easement holder may use the servient land for any convenient purpose.
Answer C: Transfer of title to the road to Aaron because he used the road for parking.
Answer D: An injunction or order stopping the parking misuse, while preserving Aaron's valid access use unless a termination doctrine is independently proven.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A use outside the easement's scope may be enjoined, but misuse does not automatically terminate the entire easement. Aaron's access right may remain valid while the improper parking is stopped unless Naomi proves a separate termination doctrine.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because misuse is not automatic total termination. B: B is wrong because the easement is limited by its access purpose. C: C is wrong because using a road for parking does not transfer title. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Misuse versus termination
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is selecting a remedy that is too broad after spotting a real scope violation.
