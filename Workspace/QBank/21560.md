MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Privileges
Question: A researcher told a participant that she would receive a harmless skin-temperature scan with a smooth sensor. In fact, the researcher planned to insert a small needle under the participant's skin to collect tissue for an unrelated study. The participant agreed to the scan but did not know about or agree to any needle insertion. The researcher inserted the needle. The participant sues for battery. Can the researcher rely on the participant's consent to the scan?
Answer A: Yes, because the participant signed a form agreeing to be touched
Answer B: Yes, because the researcher did not intend to injure the participant
Answer C: No, because fraud about the essential character of the contact can make the consent ineffective
Answer D: No, because consent is never a defense to an intentional tort
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The participant consented to a surface scan, not an invasive needle insertion. The deception went to the essential character and scope of the contact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Consent to a harmless scan is not consent to an undisclosed needle insertion for a different purpose. B: Battery can be based on intentional unauthorized contact even without intent to injure. D: Consent can be a defense when valid and within scope. The issue here is invalid scope and fraud.
lb_rule_reference: Consent may be ineffective when obtained by fraud as to the essential character of the contact.
