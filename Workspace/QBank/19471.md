Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Priya went to Lee's apartment for ten minutes to buy illegal pills. She had never been there before, brought no personal belongings, and planned to leave immediately after the sale. Police unlawfully entered the apartment and found pills on Lee's coffee table. Priya moves to suppress the pills. Does Priya have standing based on her presence in the apartment?
Answer A: Yes, because homes receive the highest Fourth Amendment protection
Answer B: Yes, because Priya was physically inside when police entered
Answer C: No, because Priya was only a short-term commercial visitor with no legitimate privacy interest in Lee's apartment
Answer D: No, because no one can challenge a search of someone else's apartment
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Priya was present briefly, solely for a commercial drug transaction, with no prior social connection, belongings, or overnight stay. She lacks standing to challenge the apartment search.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The protection belongs to people with their own legitimate privacy interest. Priya's brief commercial visit does not create the same standing as an overnight guest. B: Mere presence for a short commercial transaction is not enough for a reasonable expectation of privacy in the apartment. D: Some non-owners, such as overnight guests, may have standing. This answer states the right result too broadly.
lb_rule_reference: Overnight guests usually have standing in a home, but short-term commercial visitors normally do not.
