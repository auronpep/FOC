Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A homeowner sues a manufacturer in state court for negligence under state law. The manufacturer removes, asserting that federal safety regulations preempt the negligence claim. The complaint itself does not assert any federal cause of action, and no complete-preemption statute applies. Is removal proper based on federal-question jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because any federal preemption argument creates federal-question jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because the defendant raised federal law in the notice of removal
Answer C: No, because an ordinary federal preemption defense does not create Section 1331 jurisdiction
Answer D: No, but only because diversity jurisdiction is unavailable
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Without a federal claim, embedded substantial federal issue, or complete preemption, the federal defense does not support federal-question jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ordinary preemption is a defense and does not appear on the well-pleaded complaint. B: The federal hook must come from the plaintiff's claim or a recognized exception, not the defendant's removal argument. D: The question asks whether the federal defense creates federal-question jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant's ordinary federal preemption defense does not create federal-question jurisdiction under the well-pleaded complaint rule.
