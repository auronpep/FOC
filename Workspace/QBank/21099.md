Question: A store clerk saw a customer remove a watch from a display, slip it into a coat pocket, and walk toward the exit without paying. The clerk politely asked the customer to step into a nearby office, kept the door open, and called the manager. The manager checked the pocket within five minutes, found the watch, and released the customer after payment. The customer sues for false imprisonment. Can the store rely on the shopkeeper privilege?
Answer A: No, because only police officers can detain suspected thieves
Answer B: No, because the customer was embarrassed
Answer C: Yes, because the clerk had reasonable suspicion and the detention was brief and reasonable in manner
Answer D: Yes, but only because the customer actually had the watch
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The clerk directly observed facts supporting suspicion. The detention was brief, nearby, nonviolent, and limited to investigating the suspected theft.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A merchant has a limited privilege to reasonably detain suspected shoplifters for investigation. B: Embarrassment does not defeat a reasonable shopkeeper detention supported by reasonable suspicion. D: The privilege is usually tested by reasonable suspicion and reasonable detention, not solely by whether theft is later proven.
lb_rule_reference: A merchant may detain a suspected shoplifter for a reasonable investigation when there is reasonable suspicion and the detention is reasonable in time and manner.
