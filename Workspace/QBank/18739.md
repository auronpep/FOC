Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Trespass to land
Question: During a sudden wildfire, a driver left a blocked road and drove through a ranch gate into an open field to avoid smoke and flames. Entering the field was reasonably necessary to protect the driver from serious harm. The gate was broken, but the driver caused no other damage. The rancher sues the driver for trespass to land and gate damage. What is the likely result in the rancher's trespass to land claim against the driver?
Answer A: The driver is liable for trespass and all resulting damages because the rancher never consented to entry
Answer B: The driver is privileged to enter by private necessity but must pay for the actual gate damage
Answer C: The driver owes nothing because necessity erases all liability in every emergency
Answer D: The driver is liable only if a government official ordered the entry
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The wildfire emergency justified private-necessity entry, defeating trespass liability for the entry itself, but private necessity is incomplete and generally requires payment for actual damage caused.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Private necessity creates a privilege to enter in an emergency to protect private interests. C: Private necessity privileges the entry but does not eliminate responsibility for actual property damage caused. D: Private necessity can be invoked by private actors protecting private interests. Official action is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Private necessity privileges emergency entry onto land to protect private interests but usually requires payment for actual damage caused by the entry.
