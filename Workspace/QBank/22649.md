


BARMATRIX Q#: 22649
Outline_code: 42010100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Legislative Branch
Question: In order to address discrepancies in federal sentencing, Congress created a commission to create new sentencing guidelines for all federal offenses. Members were appointed by the President, and the commission was part of the judicial branch. Congress gave the commission specific directives that governed its authority and a classification hierarchy for federal crimes. However, the commission had discretion in setting the guidelines. A man who had been convicted of drug crimes sued. He claimed that the commission was unconstitutional because it had been given “excessive legislative powers.” Was the commission unconstitutional?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the commission violates the separation of powers.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the Constitution vests legislative authority in Congress.
Answer C: C: No, because Congress has absolute authority to delegate its legislative powers.
Answer D: D: No, because Congress outlined the goals and authority of the commission.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Congress may delegate its legislative authority to an agency or person so long as it provides an “intelligible principle” to control the agency or person by setting guidelines. Mistretta v. United States, 488 U.S. 361 (1989). Because Congress gave the commission specific directives to govern its authority, the delegation was constitutional. A is incorrect because such a delegation of legislative power would not violate the separation of powers. B is incorrect because although Article I, Section 1 vests legislative authority in Congress, Congress can delegate its legislative authority if it sets specific guidelines regarding the delegation. C is incorrect because under the Nondelegation Clause Congress does not have absolute authority to delegate its legislative powers.
