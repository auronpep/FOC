Question: Rex quietly takes a phone from an unattended cafe table and walks outside. Two minutes later, the owner sees Rex on the sidewalk and tries to grab the phone back. Rex shoves the owner and runs away. Under common-law robbery rules, is Rex guilty of robbery?
Answer A: Yes, because theft followed by any force is robbery
Answer B: Yes, because force used to escape always satisfies common-law robbery
Answer C: No, because the force occurred after Rex had completed the larceny
Answer D: No, because phones cannot be taken by robbery
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rex already had dominion and had left before using force; the later shove is not the robbery force element at common law.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common-law robbery requires force in the course of the taking, not after the taking is complete. B: The call specifies common-law robbery, where post-completion force is insufficient. D: Personal property such as a phone can be the object of robbery.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law robbery requires force or intimidation during the taking; force used only to escape after the larceny is complete is not enough.
