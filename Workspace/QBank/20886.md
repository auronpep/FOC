Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Leah sues Jacob's bakery after slipping near the front counter at 9:05 a.m. Leah offers testimony from a customer who entered the bakery at 9:08 a.m. and saw a fresh puddle of melted ice near the same counter. Jacob objects that the customer did not see the floor at the exact moment Leah fell. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because condition evidence is relevant only if observed at the exact time of the accident.
Answer B: Admit the testimony because the puddle observed minutes later has some tendency to show the floor condition at the time of the fall.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony unless Leah proves no employee cleaned the floor between 9:05 and 9:08.
Answer D: Admit the testimony only if the customer can identify who spilled the ice.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A condition observed shortly after an event can be probative of the condition at the relevant time, especially when it concerns the same place and condition. The three-minute gap and possible intervening events are weight issues.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because exact contemporaneity is not required for threshold relevance. C: C is wrong because Leah need not eliminate every possible intervening event before the testimony is relevant. D: D is wrong because identifying the source of the spill is not required for the testimony to bear on floor condition.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Demanding perfect temporal proof instead of recognizing a reasonable condition inference.
