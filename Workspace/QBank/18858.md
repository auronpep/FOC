Question: Daniel bought a “turnkey packaging line” from Ruth's company. The contract included conveyors, scanners, installation, and operator training, but most of the price was allocated to the equipment. The signed agreement said it was complete and exclusive. It did not define “turnkey.” Daniel offers two pieces of pre-signing evidence: first, industry evidence that a “turnkey packaging line” includes calibration of scanner settings; second, Ruth's oral statement that she would provide six months of free staffing support after installation. Which evidence should be admitted?
Answer A: Both the calibration evidence and the free-staffing statement.
Answer B: Only the calibration evidence.
Answer C: Only the free-staffing statement.
Answer D: Neither, because the writing is complete and exclusive.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that in a goods-predominant mixed transaction, UCC meaning sources may explain a technical or ambiguous goods term, but a complete and exclusive writing excludes separate additional prior terms. The trigger facts are the equipment-heavy bargain, the undefined “turnkey” phrase, and the two different offered purposes. The decisive lock is purpose. Calibration evidence explains what the included term “turnkey packaging line” means; free staffing support is a separate added service obligation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it admits a separate added staffing promise despite complete integration. C: C is wrong because staffing support does not explain “turnkey”; it adds an independent obligation. D: D is wrong because complete integration does not bar explanatory usage evidence for the included term.
