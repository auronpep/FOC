Question: The jury returns a verdict form finding that defendant was not negligent but awarding plaintiff $80,000 in negligence damages. The inconsistency is obvious when the verdict is read. Plaintiff and defendant both remain silent, and the court discharges the jury. Defendant later moves for a new trial based on the inconsistency. What is the likely preservation issue?
Answer A: The inconsistency objection may be waived because defendant failed to raise it before jury discharge
Answer B: Verdict inconsistencies can be raised for the first time years later
Answer C: The judge must ignore every inconsistent verdict
Answer D: The correct remedy is automatic default judgment
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Timely objection would have allowed the court to resubmit or clarify the verdict.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Apparent inconsistencies should be raised before discharge. C: Courts may address inconsistency when timely raised. D: Default has no role after a jury verdict.
lb_rule_reference: Failure to raise an apparent inconsistency before discharge may waive the objection because the court loses the opportunity to resubmit the verdict to the jury.
