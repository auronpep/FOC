MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police stopped a driver for an expired registration. The driver had a valid license, no outstanding warrants, was cooperative, and asked if a friend could come pick up the car. The car was parked legally on a public street, and the driver had committed only a minor civil infraction. Officers nonetheless impounded the vehicle — noting in the report they suspected (without articulable basis) drug activity — and conducted a full inventory search, finding heroin. The driver moves to suppress. Should the court grant the motion to suppress?
Answer A: No, because police have broad discretion to impound vehicles stopped for any traffic violation
Answer B: No, because the officers followed standardized inventory procedures, making the search constitutionally valid regardless of the impoundment's basis
Answer C: Yes, because the impoundment was pretextual; without a legitimate basis for impoundment, the inventory search is constitutionally invalid and the heroin must be suppressed
Answer D: Yes, because inventory searches of vehicles stopped for non-criminal traffic violations are always unconstitutional
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An inventory search is valid only when the predicate impoundment is lawful. Here, the impoundment lacked a genuine community-caretaking justification: the car was legally parked, the driver had a valid license, no outstanding warrants existed, a third party could retrieve the car, and the stated reason — drug suspicion — was an investigative motivation, not a caretaking one. The impoundment was pretextual, designed to create authority to search. Because the impoundment was unlawful, the inventory search that followed was an unconstitutional search. The heroin is suppressible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Although police have impoundment authority in many circumstances, that authority is not unlimited. An impoundment must serve a legitimate community-caretaking or public safety purpose. When a vehicle is legally parked, the driver has a valid license, a third party can retrieve the car, and the only stated motivation is suspicion of unrelated criminal activity, the impoundment is pretextual — not a legitimate exercise of caretaking authority. B: The validity of an inventory search depends on two sequential conditions: (1) a lawful impoundment and (2) a search conducted pursuant to standardized procedures. If the impoundment itself was unconstitutional, proper procedures during the search cannot remedy that foundational defect. The illegal impoundment is the poisonous tree; the inventory search is the fruit. D: The type of traffic violation does not categorically bar a lawful impoundment. If, for example, the driver had no license, had outstanding warrants, or the vehicle was illegally parked with no responsible party to retrieve it, impoundment and inventory would be lawful even after a minor traffic stop. The defect here is the absence of legitimate caretaking justification, not the nature of the infraction.
lb_rule_reference: An inventory search is valid only if the underlying impoundment was lawful; if the impoundment was pretextual or unjustified, the inventory search is unconstitutional regardless of how carefully procedures were followed
