Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: Vera enters a department store and places a $200 sweater inside her large tote bag while standing in the clothing aisle. Before she takes a single step, a loss-prevention officer who had been watching her on camera approaches and stops her. The sweater is still inside her bag, and Vera has not moved from the spot where she placed it. Which of the following best describes Vera's criminal liability?
Answer A: Vera is guilty of larceny because she exercised dominion and control over the sweater by placing it in her bag
Answer B: Vera is not guilty of any crime because she was caught before leaving the store
Answer C: Vera is guilty of attempted larceny because she completed caption but failed to achieve asportation
Answer D: Vera is guilty of larceny only if she concealed the sweater in a way not visible to others
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Common-law larceny requires both caption (gaining dominion and control over property) and asportation (carrying it away, even slightly). Vera achieved caption by placing the sweater in her tote bag, thereby asserting dominion and control over it with intent to permanently deprive the store. However, she did not move from her position — no asportation occurred. With one element missing, the completed crime of larceny is not established. Because she took a substantial step toward larceny with the requisite intent, she is guilty of attempted larceny.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Larceny at common law requires both a "taking" (caption — gaining dominion and control) and "carrying away" (asportation — movement, however slight). Vera gained dominion by placing the sweater in her bag (caption satisfied), but she never moved from her spot. Without any asportation, larceny is not complete. She is guilty only of attempted larceny. B: Larceny does not require leaving the premises. If asportation (any movement) had occurred, the crime would be complete within the store. Moreover, even without completed larceny, Vera committed attempted larceny — a crime — by placing the sweater in her bag with intent to steal, even if she never moved. D: Concealment is not an element of common-law larceny. The elements are trespassory taking (caption) and asportation, with intent to permanently deprive. Whether the item was hidden from view is irrelevant to whether the crime is complete. Asportation — movement — is what matters, not visibility.
lb_rule_reference: Larceny requires asportation — carrying away of the property — but even the slightest movement of the property is sufficient. If the defendant gains dominion and control but fails to move the property at all, asportation is not satisfied and only attempted larceny is committed.
