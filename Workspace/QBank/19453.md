Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: Naomi sues a repair company for failing to install a furnace. Naomi testifies that she called the repair company's publicly listed business number. The person who answered scheduled a furnace appointment, quoted the company's standard price, and later a technician arrived at Naomi's house. Naomi offers the call to show the company scheduled the work. The company objects that Naomi cannot identify the employee who answered. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because Naomi must identify the employee by name.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because only the employee who answered may authenticate the call.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if Naomi recorded the call.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because a call to a business number relating to business reasonably transacted by phone may be authenticated by those circumstances.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: For a business call, evidence that the call was made to the business's number and concerned business reasonably transacted over the phone may be enough to authenticate the conversation. Naomi need not identify the specific employee.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the rule does not require the caller to identify the employee by name in a routine business call. B: B is wrong because authentication may be circumstantial. C: C is wrong because recording is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(6)(B)
lb_student_diagnostic: Employee-name over-demand: student requires unnecessary speaker identity for routine business call authentication.
