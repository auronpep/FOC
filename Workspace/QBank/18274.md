Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: In a federal negligence trial, Ezra uses a simple diagram of an intersection while testifying about where the cars were. Ezra says the diagram is not to scale but accurately shows the relative positions he observed. The opponent objects that the diagram is not independently admissible evidence of the accident. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the diagram because a demonstrative aid must be drawn to scale.
Answer B: Exclude the diagram because only photographs may illustrate testimony.
Answer C: Allow the diagram to be used to illustrate Ezra's testimony, with an instruction if necessary that it is not independent proof of exact distances.
Answer D: Admit the diagram as conclusive proof of the cars' precise positions.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A demonstrative aid may be used to help illustrate testimony if a witness with knowledge explains that it fairly assists the testimony. It need not be independently substantive proof or perfectly to scale, but the court may limit its use.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every illustrative diagram must be to scale. B: B is wrong because demonstratives are not limited to photographs. D: D is wrong because illustrative use does not make the diagram conclusive proof.
