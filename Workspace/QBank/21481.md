Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff, a State A citizen, sues Defendant, a State B citizen, for $100,000 in federal diversity court. Defendant impleads Insurer, also a State B citizen, under Rule 14, alleging Insurer must indemnify Defendant for any liability to Plaintiff. The indemnity claim arises from the same accident and insurance policy. Does Section 1367(b) automatically bar Defendant's claim against Insurer?
Answer A: Yes, because Insurer and Defendant are citizens of the same state
Answer B: Yes, because Rule 14 never supports supplemental jurisdiction
Answer C: No, Section 1367(b) does not automatically bar this defendant impleader claim
Answer D: No, but only if Insurer is diverse from Plaintiff
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1367(b) limits specified plaintiff claims in diversity cases; it does not generally block a defendant's related Rule 14 indemnity claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The claim is asserted by Defendant, not by Plaintiff to evade diversity. B: Related Rule 14 claims can fall within supplemental jurisdiction. D: The supplemental claim is Defendant's indemnity claim; the key is the claim's relationship and Section 1367(b)'s plaintiff-side limit.
lb_rule_reference: In a diversity case, Section 1367(b) generally does not bar a defendant's related impleader claim against a nondiverse third-party defendant.
