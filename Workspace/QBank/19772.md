Question: In a federal civil trial, Stephen testifies that he was home all evening. The opposing party offers authenticated surveillance video showing Stephen walking into the warehouse at 9 p.m. Stephen objects that he was never confronted with the video as a prior inconsistent statement under Rule 613. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the video because all inconsistent impeachment evidence must first be shown to the witness under Rule 613.
Answer B: Exclude the video because nonverbal conduct can never contradict testimony.
Answer C: Admit the video if otherwise authenticated and relevant, because the video is not a prior statement governed by Rule 613's prior-statement procedure.
Answer D: Admit the video only if Stephen previously described the video under oath.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 613 governs a witness's prior statements. The surveillance video is not Stephen's prior statement merely because it contradicts his testimony. If authenticated and relevant, it may be used as contradiction evidence without satisfying the prior-statement procedure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 613 does not govern all contradiction evidence. B: B is wrong because conduct or video evidence may contradict testimony. D: D is wrong because the video's use does not depend on a prior sworn description by Stephen.
lb_rule_reference: Prior inconsistent conduct boundary / not a Rule 613 statement
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the boundary between prior inconsistent statements and other contradiction evidence.
