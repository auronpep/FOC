Question: Lydia is a merchant who regularly buys wool for resale to clothing makers. She sent Samuel, a wool supplier, a signed letter stating, “I offer to buy 2,000 pounds of wool at $4 per pound. I will hold this offer open for 60 days.” Samuel gave no consideration for the assurance. Thirty days later, Lydia tried to revoke. Samuel accepted on day 40. Is Lydia bound?
Answer A: No, because only a merchant seller can make an Article 2 firm offer.
Answer B: Yes, because a signed offer by a merchant to buy goods that gives assurance it will be held open is not revocable for lack of consideration during the stated period, within the statutory cap.
Answer C: No, because Article 2's three-month rule makes every firm offer lapse after 30 days unless consideration is paid.
Answer D: Yes, but only because Samuel's acceptance supplied retroactive consideration for Lydia's promise to hold the offer open.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The source-law gate points to Article 2 because the transaction is for goods. A merchant offer to buy or sell goods in a signed writing that assures the offer will be held open is not revocable for lack of consideration during the stated time, subject to the statutory cap. Lydia was a merchant buyer of wool, signed the assurance, and Samuel accepted within 60 days.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the firm-offer rule applies to a merchant offer to buy or sell goods, not only merchant sellers. C: C is wrong because the three-month limit caps irrevocability but does not create a 30-day lapse. D: D is wrong because the rule makes the offer irrevocable without consideration; Samuel's later acceptance did not retroactively supply consideration for the hold-open promise.
