internal_id: TR-305

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Defamation
Question: In a closed office with thick walls, a manager quietly told Employee, "I wrongly thought you altered the payroll report, but I now know you did not." Unknown to both of them, a technician had accidentally left a phone connected under the desk, and a third party heard the words remotely. Employee sues for defamation based on the third party's overhearing. Is the publication element likely satisfied?
Answer A: Yes, because any third-party hearing is automatically publication
Answer B: Yes, because the statement mentioned payroll alteration
Answer C: No, because the third-party overhearing was not intended or reasonably foreseeable
Answer D: No, because statements to an employee can never be defamatory
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The manager spoke privately in a closed office and had no reason to know about the connected phone. The third-party receipt was accidental and unforeseeable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Publication requires intentional or negligent communication to a third party. These facts make the overhearing unforeseeable. B: Defamatory meaning does not replace the publication requirement. D: Workplace statements can be defamatory if communicated to a third party; this one fails because third-party communication was unforeseeable.
lb_rule_reference: Defamation publication requires intentional or negligent communication to a third party; wholly unforeseeable overhearing may fail the publication element.
