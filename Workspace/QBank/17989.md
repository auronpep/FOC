Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Appointment and removal
Question: Congress creates a five-member trade commission with staggered terms, bipartisan membership limits, and authority to conduct adjudicatory hearings and issue industry reports. Commissioners may be removed by the President only for inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance. A regulated company argues that any for-cause removal limit on any federal officer is unconstitutional. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The removal limit is automatically invalid because all for-cause protections violate Article II
Answer B: The removal limit is more likely valid because it applies to members of a multimember expert commission with limited, mixed functions
Answer C: The removal limit is invalid because bipartisan membership limits are always unconstitutional
Answer D: The removal limit is valid only if commissioners are appointed by Congress
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Traditional for-cause protection is more defensible for a multimember commission with expert, adjudicatory, or quasi-legislative functions than for a single director exercising concentrated executive power.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The doctrine does not invalidate every for-cause limit. Structure, function, and degree of executive power matter. C: The question asks about removal protection. Bipartisan membership limits do not make every commission removal structure invalid. D: Congress cannot appoint executive officers, and congressional appointment is not a condition for removal protection.
lb_rule_reference: For-cause removal limits are more likely to be upheld for members of a multimember expert commission with quasi-legislative or quasi-judicial functions than for a single executive director exercising broad executive power.
