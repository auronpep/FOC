Outline: Scope of discovery
Question: After a building collapse, the defendant's non-testifying structural engineer inspected the site before the debris was removed. The plaintiff later discovers that the site has been demolished and no equivalent inspection is possible. The plaintiff seeks the engineer's observations and opinions. What is the plaintiff's best argument for discovery?
Answer A: The expert is discoverable because every retained expert must provide a report
Answer B: The expert is discoverable because the collapse is relevant to the claims
Answer C: Exceptional circumstances exist because the expert observed a condition that can no longer be inspected or replicated
Answer D: Discovery is unavailable because consulting experts are absolutely immune from discovery
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The destroyed site makes it impracticable for the plaintiff to obtain equivalent facts or opinions by other means.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A non-testifying consulting expert is treated differently. B: Relevance alone does not overcome consulting-expert protection. D: The rule allows discovery on exceptional circumstances.
lb_rule_reference: Facts known or opinions held by a non-testifying consulting expert are discoverable only on exceptional circumstances making it impracticable to obtain facts or opinions by other means.
