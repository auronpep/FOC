Question: An employee sues an employer in federal court, asserting a federal discrimination claim and a supplemental state-law negligent supervision claim. The employer seeks the same confidential counseling notes to defend both claims. State law would recognize a broader counseling privilege than federal common law. The parties ask the court to choose one privilege rule for the entire case. Which approach is most appropriate?
Answer A: Apply state privilege law to the entire case because one state-law claim is present.
Answer B: Apply federal privilege law to the entire case because the case was filed in federal court.
Answer C: Analyze the privilege source by claim or defense and manage any admitted evidence through limiting instructions or other controls if necessary.
Answer D: Admit the notes for both claims because mixed federal and state claims always favor admissibility.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Mixed claims require source-law discipline. Federal privilege law generally governs federal claims, while state law may govern privilege for state-law claims or defenses. The court should not collapse the entire case into one source rule without analyzing the use of the evidence. The decisive axis is claim-specific source law and permissible use.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This over-applies state privilege law by letting one supplemental state claim control the federal claim. B: B is wrong because This over-applies federal privilege law by ignoring that state privilege can govern a state-law claim or defense. D: A is wrong because This over-applies state privilege law by letting one supplemental state claim control the federal claim. B is wrong because This over-applies federal privilege law by ignoring that state privilege can govern a state-law claim or defense. C is wrong because Correct answer D is wrong because This is an admissibility shortcut. Mixed source law does not automatically eliminate privilege.
