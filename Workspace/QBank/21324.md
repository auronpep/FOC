Question: A company blocks a public boat ramp for two weeks while unloading equipment. Everyone in town loses access to the ramp. A restaurant owner sues for public nuisance, alleging that he lost more business than other merchants because many of his customers arrive by boat. Can the restaurant owner maintain a private action for public nuisance?
Answer A: Yes, because his economic loss is larger than the public's inconvenience
Answer B: Yes, because any member of the public may recover damages for a public nuisance
Answer C: No, unless he proves an injury different in kind from the harm suffered by the public
Answer D: No, because public nuisance never involves obstruction of public ways
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Special injury must differ in kind, not just amount.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A greater degree of the same public harm is not enough. B: Public nuisance is usually enforced by public officials unless a private plaintiff has special injury. D: Blocking public access can be a classic public nuisance.
lb_rule_reference: A private plaintiff may sue for public nuisance only by showing special injury different in kind, not merely greater in degree, from that suffered by the public.
