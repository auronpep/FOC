Question: Moses and David made a contract requiring David to pay Ruth $9,000. Ruth overheard Moses mention the promise and sent David a note saying, “I know about the contract.” Neither Moses nor David had asked Ruth to accept, sign, rely, or do anything in response. Ruth did not change position. Before Ruth sued, Moses and David reduced the payment to $3,000. May Ruth enforce the original $9,000 promise?
Answer A: Yes, because her note proved she knew of the contract.
Answer B: Yes, because any communication by a beneficiary is enough to vest rights.
Answer C: No, because Ruth's only possible route would be assignment.
Answer D: No, because mere knowledge or acknowledgment, without invited assent, reliance, suit, or acceptance of performance, did not vest her rights before modification.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Ruth was an intended beneficiary, but vesting requires more than mere awareness or an unrequested acknowledgment. Because she did not rely, sue, accept performance, or manifest assent in an invited manner before the modification, she cannot enforce the original amount.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because knowledge alone does not vest. B: B is wrong because not every beneficiary communication is legally sufficient assent. C: C is wrong because Ruth's possible route is beneficiary status, not assignment.
