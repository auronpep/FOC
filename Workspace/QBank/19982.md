Question: Congress offers states education grants. The statute says only that participating states must follow "all future conditions that the federal agency later considers appropriate." After a state accepts the grant, the agency imposes a costly school-construction mandate not mentioned in the statute. What is the state's strongest Spending Clause objection?
Answer A: Spending conditions must be clear enough for states to accept knowingly
Answer B: Congress may attach any undisclosed condition after funds are accepted
Answer C: Spending conditions are valid only if states draft them
Answer D: Clear notice matters only in criminal statutes
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Hidden or open-ended conditions undermine knowing acceptance of federal funds.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Acceptance must be knowing; conditions cannot be sprung later without clear notice. C: Congress may set conditions if constitutional limits are met. D: Spending Clause doctrine also requires clear notice to states.
lb_rule_reference: Spending conditions on federal funds must be stated unambiguously so states can knowingly decide whether to accept the funds and conditions.
