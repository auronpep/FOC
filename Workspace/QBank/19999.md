Question: In a federal civil trial, Paul calls Mark as a witness. Mark unexpectedly gives testimony favorable to the opposing party. Paul then asks Mark whether the opposing party recently hired Mark's daughter. The opposing party objects that Paul may not impeach his own witness. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a party vouches for any witness the party calls.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because any party, including the party that called the witness, may attack the witness's credibility by showing bias.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Mark first admits he intentionally lied.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the question concerns Mark's reputation for untruthfulness.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 607 permits any party, including the party that called the witness, to attack the witness's credibility. Evidence that the opposing party hired Mark's daughter may show Mark's possible bias or motive to favor that party.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the voucher rule is abolished. C: C is wrong because bias can be explored without an admission of lying. D: D is wrong because bias is distinct from reputation for untruthfulness.
lb_rule_reference: Bias impeachment / bias of one's own witness
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests impeachment of one's own witness and the bias route under Rule 607.
