


BARMATRIX Q#: 22265
Outline_code: 35030304
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Liability insurance
Question: A motorcyclist was seriously injured when he collided with a woman's car. The motorcyclist spent several weeks in the hospital, which was ultimately paid for by the woman's insurance company. The motorcyclist sued the woman for negligence. At trial, the motorcyclist testified that the woman's insurance company paid for his hospital stay. Is the motorcyclist's testimony admissible?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the testimony tends to show that the woman was at fault in the accident.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the testimony does not involve a communication.
Answer C: C: No, because the testimony involves the payment of the motorcyclist's hospital bill.
Answer D: D: No, because the testimony is inadmissible hearsay.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 409, offers to pay or actual payments of medical or hospital bills are inadmissible for the purposes of proving negligence, liability, or the value of a claim. Although there are purposes for which such evidence might be admissible, none are listed among the options. A is incorrect because the motorcyclist is testifying to the payment of his hospital bills. B is incorrect because the fact that the motorcyclist is not testifying to a communication does not change the analysis. D is incorrect because the payment of hospital bills would not qualify as a hearsay statement.
