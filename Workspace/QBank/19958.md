Question: A prisoner seeks a religious diet. The prison argues the court should deny the request because the prison's expert says the prisoner's interpretation of his faith is theologically mistaken, though the prisoner appears sincere. What is the best constitutional point?
Answer A: The court may reject the claim if it decides the prisoner's belief is theologically wrong
Answer B: The court may consider sincerity but should not decide whether the belief is true or orthodox
Answer C: The prisoner automatically loses because prison officials always decide religious doctrine
Answer D: The prisoner automatically wins once sincerity is shown
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The constitutional inquiry avoids adjudicating religious truth.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Courts do not decide religious truth or orthodoxy. C: Deference does not let officials or courts declare religious truth. D: Sincerity establishes the nature of the belief; the legal standard and government interests still matter.
lb_rule_reference: Courts may examine sincerity when necessary, but they may not decide whether a religious belief is true, orthodox, or reasonable.
