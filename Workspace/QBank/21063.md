Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: David sues a contractor and alleges in Count 1 that a written contract exists and was breached. In Count 2, pleaded in the alternative, David alleges that if no contract is found, the contractor was unjustly enriched by retaining the benefit of David's payment. Is the alternative pleading improper merely because the theories are inconsistent?
Answer A: Yes, because pleadings must choose one theory at filing
Answer B: Yes, unless David already knows which theory will win
Answer C: No, if both theories have a nonfrivolous basis under Rule 11
Answer D: No, because Rule 11 never applies to alternative claims
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Inconsistent alternatives are allowed when responsibly pleaded.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Federal rules permit alternative and inconsistent pleading. B: Alternative pleading exists because facts and legal characterization may be uncertain before discovery. D: Rule 11 still requires a proper factual and legal basis.
lb_rule_reference: Federal pleading permits alternative and inconsistent statements, subject to Rule 11.
