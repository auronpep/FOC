Question: Abraham granted Sarah, as owner of Lot 2, “a right to use the existing paved driveway on Lot 1 solely for ingress and egress to the residence on Lot 2.” Sarah began leaving a delivery van parked on the driveway overnight several days each week so that goods could be unloaded in the morning. The parked van blocked Abraham's use of part of the driveway. Abraham sues to stop the overnight parking. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Abraham should prevail because an easement limited to ingress and egress does not include recurring overnight parking that burdens the servient owner.
Answer B: Sarah should prevail because any use of the driveway by a vehicle is within an ingress-and-egress easement.
Answer C: Sarah should prevail because an easement holder owns the surface of the driveway while using it.
Answer D: Abraham should prevail only if Sarah completely abandoned all use of the driveway.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: An express easement's scope is measured by the grant's language and purpose. A right of ingress and egress allows passage to and from the dominant estate, but it does not ordinarily include recurring storage or parking that materially burdens the servient land. Sarah's overnight van parking exceeds the passage purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because not every vehicle-related use is ingress or egress. C: C is wrong because an easement is a nonpossessory use right, not ownership of the driveway surface. D: D is wrong because Abraham need not prove abandonment to stop a use outside the easement's scope.
lb_rule_reference: Ingress and egress / parking outside scope
