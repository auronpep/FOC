Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Truthfulness
Question: Lydia is sued for negligently leaving a delivery cart in a hallway. Before any witness has testified about Lydia's truthfulness, Lydia offers testimony from a neighbor that Lydia always tells the truth in personal conversations. Lydia argues that truthful people are careful people. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony, because truthfulness is always relevant in any case.
Answer B: Admit the testimony only if Lydia also testifies.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony only if the neighbor lacks personal knowledge of the hallway.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony, because Lydia's general truthfulness does not make hallway negligence more or less probable on these facts.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The offered truthfulness testimony does not logically bear on whether Lydia negligently left a cart in the hallway. No witness truthfulness issue has been opened, and the proposed inference from truthful to careful is not a relevance route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because truthfulness is not automatically relevant to every claim. B: B is wrong because Lydia's testimony would not by itself make truthful character relevant to hallway negligence. C: C is wrong because the defect is not the neighbor's knowledge of the hallway; it is the lack of a consequential link.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is confusing a positive character trait with relevance to the litigated event.
