


BARMATRIX Q#: 22841
Outline_code: 97100300
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: A man and woman were in a traffic accident at an intersection. After the accident, a state police officer on the scene gave the man a ticket for running the intersection's stop sign. The man paid the citation online. In paying the citation, the man admitted that he had run the stop sign. The woman sued the man in federal court for injuries she sustained in the accident. The woman asked the court to preclude the man from litigating the issue of whether he ran the stop sign based on the admission the man made when paying the ticket. Will the court preclude the man from litigating the stop sign issue?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the man admitted that he ran the stop sign to a judicial authority.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the man could have challenged the ticket in state court.
Answer C: C: No, because the stop sign issue had not been litigated yet.
Answer D: D: No, because the ticket was issued by a state authority.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Collateral estoppel (also known as issue preclusion) refers to the situation where a particular issue of fact or law has been determined in a prior proceeding, and a party seeks to use that determination in a subsequent proceeding. Importantly, issue preclusion only applies when the issue has been litigated. Here, although the man made an admission when he paid the ticket, the issue of whether or not he ran the stop sign has not been litigated yet, so the court will not preclude the man from litigating it. Therefore, A, B, and D are incorrect.
