Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A company staged a fireworks show. During the show, a pickpocket stole a spectator's wallet in the crowd. The spectator was not injured by any explosion, spark, smoke, or debris. The spectator sues the company under strict liability for conducting an abnormally dangerous fireworks display. What result?
Answer A: The spectator wins because the theft happened during an abnormally dangerous activity
Answer B: The spectator wins because large crowds are a foreseeable part of fireworks shows
Answer C: The company wins because the stolen wallet was not harm from the special dangers of the fireworks display
Answer D: The company wins because strict liability never covers property losses
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Strict liability does not extend to unrelated criminal theft in the crowd.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Temporal overlap is not enough; the harm must arise from the abnormal risk. B: Crowd theft is not the explosive or pyrotechnic risk that makes the activity abnormal. D: Strict liability can cover property harm when it results from the abnormal risk.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity does not make the actor liable for unrelated harms that merely occur near the activity, such as ordinary crowd theft.
