Question: In a federal employment trial, Joel asks Rebecca, “Were things bad in the office before you left?” The opposing party objects that the question is vague and ambiguous. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because the jury can decide what “bad” means.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the question is too vague to guide the witness or permit a meaningful answer.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because vague questions are permitted on direct but not on cross.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Rebecca gives an answer longer than one sentence.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may require questions to be clear enough to elicit useful testimony. “Were things bad” is vague and ambiguous; the court may require a more specific question.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A ignores the court's role in controlling effective examination. C invents a direct/cross distinction. D confuses vagueness with narrative length.
