Question: A signed contract required Miriam to buy 300 baskets from a merchant seller at $4 each. Later, the parties orally agreed to increase the quantity to 700 baskets at the same price. No new writing was signed. The seller delivered no baskets, and Miriam refused to perform at all. What is the best Statute of Frauds analysis?
Answer A: The oral modification is enforceable for all 700 baskets because UCC modifications need no consideration.
Answer B: The entire contract is unenforceable because the oral modification failed.
Answer C: The original signed 300-basket contract remains enforceable, but the added 400 baskets are not enforceable absent a writing or UCC §2-201 exception.
Answer D: The oral modification is enforceable only if Miriam is not a merchant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: UCC §2-209 provides that Article 2 modifications need no consideration, but a modification must still satisfy the Statute of Frauds if the contract as modified falls within UCC §2-201. The signed writing supports 300 baskets. The oral increase to 700 lacks a writing or exception, so the added quantity is not enforceable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because no-consideration modification does not eliminate the SOF requirement. B: B is wrong because the original signed contract remains supported by a sufficient writing. D: D is wrong because Miriam's merchant status is not the reason the oral quantity increase fails.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-209 + UCC §2-201
