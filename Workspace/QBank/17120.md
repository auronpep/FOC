Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: Rachel sues a building owner after a fire, claiming that insulation near a heater was dangerously wet and moldy before the fire. Rachel offers a piece of insulation taken from the site three weeks after the fire. The investigator who collected it admits that rain fell through the damaged roof for two weeks before collection and that he cannot say whether the insulation was wet or moldy before the fire. The owner objects that the sample is not connected to the condition Rachel must prove. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the sample because all physical evidence from the accident scene is relevant.
Answer B: Admit the sample because any change in condition always goes only to weight.
Answer C: Exclude the sample only if the owner proves the insulation was dry before the fire.
Answer D: Exclude the sample as proof of the pre-fire condition because Rachel has not laid a sufficient foundation connecting its later condition to the relevant earlier condition.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Physical evidence showing a condition at one time is relevant to a condition at another time only if the proponent supplies a reasonable connection. Here intervening rain and the investigator's inability to relate the later condition to the pre-fire condition make the foundation insufficient for the offered purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because scene origin alone does not establish relevance to the disputed pre-fire condition. B: B is wrong because a material change in condition can defeat the foundation for the offered purpose. C: C is wrong because Rachel, as proponent, must connect the evidence to the relevant condition.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(a)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student overuses the weight-not-admissibility rule despite a missing condition link.
