Outline: Burglary
Question: At noon, Maya breaks a side window of a closed retail store and enters intending to steal electronics. The question asks only about common-law burglary, not a modern burglary statute. Is Maya guilty of common-law burglary?
Answer A: Yes, because she broke and entered with intent to steal
Answer B: Yes, because intent to steal replaces the nighttime element
Answer C: No, because a daytime retail store entry is outside common-law burglary
Answer D: No, because breaking a window is not a breaking
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts fail both common-law dwelling and nighttime requirements.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common-law burglary requires a dwelling and nighttime entry. B: Intent is required in addition to, not instead of, dwelling and nighttime elements. D: Breaking a window is a breaking; the missing elements are dwelling and nighttime.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law burglary requires breaking and entering the dwelling of another at night with intent to commit a felony inside; a daytime store entry does not satisfy common-law burglary.
