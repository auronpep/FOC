Question: A shareholder files a federal derivative suit on behalf of a corporation, alleging directors wasted corporate assets. The complaint is not verified and says only, "Demand would have been pointless." What is the pleading problem?
Answer A: A derivative complaint must satisfy special requirements, including verification and particularized demand or demand-futility allegations
Answer B: The complaint is sufficient because derivative suits are governed only by ordinary notice pleading
Answer C: The complaint must be treated as a Rule 23(b)(3) class action
Answer D: No demand allegation is required if the shareholder seeks money damages
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 23.1 imposes special pleading requirements for derivative actions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Derivative actions have special pleading requirements. C: A derivative action is governed by Rule 23.1, not ordinary class certification. D: The demand pleading requirement does not disappear because the requested remedy is damages.
lb_rule_reference: A derivative complaint must be verified and state with particularity efforts to obtain desired action from directors or comparable authority, and reasons for failure to obtain action or for not making the effort.
