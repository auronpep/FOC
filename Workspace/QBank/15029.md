Question: A father owned two adjoining parcels known as Lot 1 and Lot 2. Both parcels fronted on Main Street and abutted a public alley in the rear. Lot 1 was improved with a commercial building that covered all of the Main Street frontage of Lot 1; there was a large parking lot on the rear of Lot 1 with access from the alley only. 15 years ago, the father leased Lot 1 to his son for 15 years. The son has continuously occupied Lot 1 since that time. 13 years ago, without his father's permission, the son began to use a driveway on Lot 2 as a better access between Main Street and the parking lot than the alley. Eight years ago, the father conveyed Lot 2 to his daughter and, five years ago, the father conveyed Lot 1 to his son by a deed that recited "together with all the appurtenances." Until last week, the son continuously used the driveway over Lot 2 to the son's parking lot in the rear of Lot 1. Last week the daughter commenced construction of a building on Lot 2 and blocked the driveway used by the son. The son has commenced an action against the daughter to restrain her from blocking the driveway from Main Street to the parking lot at the rear of Lot 1. The period of time to acquire rights by prescription in the jurisdiction is 10 years. If the son loses, it will be because
Answer A: A: the father owned both Lot 1 and Lot 2 until eight years ago.
Answer B: B: the son has access to the parking lot from the alley.
Answer C: C: mere use of an easement is not adverse possession.
Answer D: D: no easement was mentioned in the deed from the father to his daughter.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. The driveway between Lots 1 and 2 is an easement appurtenant. The father owned all of Lots 1 and 2 until he sold Lot 2 to his daughter eight years ago. Therefore, there has only been an easement appurtenant in existence for eight years (because there was no dominant or servant estate prior to that time).
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. Access only relates to easements by necessity. C is incorrect. This is an accurate general legal statement, but it does not explain why the son loses. D is incorrect. The easement appurtenant did not exist until after the daughter bought the land.
