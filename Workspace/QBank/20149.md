Question: Ruth leased a storefront from Naomi for a bakery. Ruth installed commercial ovens and display counters specially used for the bakery business. Some items were bolted to the floor, but they could be removed without substantial injury to the building if Ruth repaired the bolt holes. One week before the lease ended, Ruth removed the equipment and repaired the minor damage. Naomi claims the equipment became part of the real property. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Ruth may remove the equipment as trade fixtures because they were installed for her business and removed before the lease ended with repair of damage.
Answer B: Naomi owns the equipment because any bolted item is automatically a fixture.
Answer C: Naomi owns the equipment because trade fixtures can never be removed by a tenant.
Answer D: Ruth may remove the equipment only if the lease expressly used the words “trade fixture.”
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A tenant may generally remove trade fixtures installed for the tenant's business if removal occurs before the lease ends and the tenant repairs damage caused by removal. Ruth installed bakery equipment for her business, removed it before the lease ended, and repaired the minor damage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because annexation is only one fixture factor; bolting is not conclusive. C: C is wrong because trade fixtures are the major tenant-removal exception. D: D is wrong because the doctrine can apply without magic words unless the lease validly provides otherwise.
lb_rule_reference: Tenant trade fixtures / removal before lease end
