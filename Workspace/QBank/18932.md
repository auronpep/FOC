Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: Ramon tells Ellis, "I am coming to your shop tonight with my cousins, and we will hurt you." Ramon then walks away and gets into a taxi. Ellis believes the threat because Ramon has been violent before. Ten minutes later, while Ramon is still seated in the taxi at the curb and making no move toward Ellis, Ellis runs outside and shoots Ramon. If Ellis is charged with murder, is self-defense likely to justify the shooting?
Answer A: Yes, because Ellis honestly and reasonably believed Ramon would attack later that night
Answer B: Yes, because Ramon had a violent reputation
Answer C: No, because the threatened harm was future, not imminent, when Ellis used deadly force
Answer D: No, but only because Ellis used a gun instead of nondeadly force
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Common-law self-defense justifies force only when the defendant reasonably believes force is immediately necessary to prevent imminent unlawful force. Ramon threatened harm later and was moving away. Ellis had time to seek police help or avoid the confrontation. The facts may show fear, but they do not show imminent danger. Deadly force was therefore unjustified.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Self-defense requires imminent unlawful force at the moment defensive force is used. A threat to return later, while the threatener is leaving and not attacking, is future harm rather than imminent danger. B: Reputation may inform reasonableness when force is otherwise imminent, but it does not create imminence by itself. Ramon was leaving in a taxi, not using present force. D: The first failure is imminence. When there is no imminent threat at all, even lesser defensive force would not be justified as self-defense.
lb_rule_reference: Self-defense requires a reasonable belief in imminent unlawful force; a future threat, even if credible, does not justify preemptive deadly force now
