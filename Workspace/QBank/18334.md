Question: A local paper negligently but not recklessly reported that a private waste-hauling company had dumped oil near a public reservoir. The report was false and concerned public water safety. The company proved some customer confusion but sought only presumed damages and punitive damages without proving the paper knew the report was false or recklessly disregarded the truth. Is the company likely to recover presumed or punitive damages in its defamation claim against the paper?
Answer A: Yes, because negligence is always enough for all damages in a private-plaintiff defamation case
Answer B: No, because public-concern presumed and punitive damages require actual malice
Answer C: Yes, because a written newspaper report is libel and libel automatically carries presumed damages
Answer D: No, because a corporation can never sue for defamation
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The subject is public concern, and the plaintiff seeks presumed and punitive damages without proof of knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Presumed and punitive damages in public-concern defamation require actual malice, even for a private plaintiff. C: Constitutional limits override common-law presumed damages in public-concern cases absent actual malice. D: Businesses can bring defamation claims for reputational injury; the problem here is the requested damages category.
lb_rule_reference: In public-concern defamation, presumed or punitive damages generally require actual malice.
