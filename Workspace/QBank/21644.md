Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A manufacturer sold an industrial slicer with a fixed metal guard that prevented hands from reaching the blade. The guard complied with design standards and worked as intended. A buyer later removed the guard, rewired the interlock, and used the slicer that way for faster cleaning. An employee's hand entered the blade area and was injured. The employee sued the manufacturer for strict products liability. Is the manufacturer likely to defeat the strict products liability claim based on post-sale modification?
Answer A: Yes, because the buyer's substantial post-sale removal of the guard caused the injury
Answer B: No, because manufacturers are strictly liable for all injuries involving their products
Answer C: No, because any workplace removal of a guard is always foreseeable as a matter of law
Answer D: Yes, but only because the employee was negligent for using the machine
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The product left the manufacturer with a working safety guard. The buyer's substantial alteration and rewiring caused the blade-access injury, supporting a modification defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Strict products liability still requires a defect when the product left the seller and causation. A later substantial modification can break that chain. C: The facts do not make this substantial rewiring and guard removal automatically foreseeable. Foreseeability must be supported by product and use facts. D: The strongest defense is the buyer's substantial product modification causing the injury, not simply the employee's negligence.
lb_rule_reference: A substantial unforeseeable post-sale modification that causes the injury can defeat products liability, while foreseeable alterations or preexisting defects do not automatically do so.
