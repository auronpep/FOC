Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A pressure cooker has a defective valve that explodes during normal cooking and burns the user. After the explosion, the user's roommate removes a warning sticker while cleaning the broken cooker. The manufacturer argues that the post-accident removal of the sticker is product misuse barring the user's claim. What is the best response?
Answer A: The later sticker removal does not bar the claim because it did not cause the explosion injury
Answer B: Any alteration at any time automatically defeats strict products liability
Answer C: The user loses because warning stickers are always the only possible product safety device
Answer D: The case becomes negligence only because a roommate touched the product
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Product-defect defenses must connect to causation; a later alteration after the injury does not cause that injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Timing and causation matter. C: The defect was the exploding valve, and the later sticker fact does not answer that defect. D: The later contact does not change the pleaded strict-products theory.
lb_rule_reference: Misuse or alteration is a defense only when it causes the injury or breaks the causal chain; it does not defeat liability for harm already caused by a product defect.
