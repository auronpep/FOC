Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: Two weeks after answering, a defendant moves for summary judgment on a trade-secret claim. The plaintiff submits a declaration explaining that the key documents are in the defendant's possession and that no discovery responses have been served. The declaration identifies the categories of documents needed to oppose the motion. What may the court do in response?
Answer A: Grant summary judgment automatically because discovery has not yet produced evidence
Answer B: Deny all discovery because summary judgment motions always freeze discovery
Answer C: Defer or deny the motion, allow time for discovery, or issue another appropriate order
Answer D: Convert the summary judgment motion into a motion to dismiss
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The nonmovant identified why it cannot yet present essential facts and what discovery is needed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 56 gives the court tools to prevent a premature evidence-record ruling. B: A pending Rule 56 motion does not automatically bar discovery needed to oppose it. D: The procedural problem is premature Rule 56 adjudication, not a reason to transform the motion into Rule 12 review.
lb_rule_reference: If a nonmovant shows by affidavit or declaration that it cannot present facts essential to oppose summary judgment, the court may defer or deny the motion, allow discovery, or issue another appropriate order.
