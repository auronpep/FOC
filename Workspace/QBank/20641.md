Question: In a federal fraud trial, Daniel seeks to offer a letter he says proves his defense. The prosecutor objects that the letter is privileged attorney-client communication. Daniel argues that the jury should hear the letter and then decide whether it is privileged. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Permit the jury to hear the letter because privilege affects weight, not admissibility.
Answer B: Have the jury hear the letter only if Daniel promises not to argue its truth.
Answer C: Resolve the privilege issue before the jury hears the letter.
Answer D: Admit the letter temporarily and strike it later if the jury finds it privileged.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court decides privilege as a preliminary admissibility question. The jury should not hear allegedly privileged material before the court resolves the objection.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because privilege is an admissibility bar, not a weight issue. B: B is wrong because limiting purpose does not cure privilege. D: D is wrong because privilege is not submitted to the jury for a later finding.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a), 103(d)
