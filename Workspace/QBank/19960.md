Outline: Personal knowledge
Question: In a federal contract trial, Mary testifies that she saw Aaron sign a delivery receipt in the warehouse. The receipt itself is unavailable, and Mary is not asked to recite its terms. Aaron objects that the original receipt is required before Mary may testify that he signed it. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because Mary is testifying from personal knowledge of the signing event, not proving the contents of the receipt.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because any testimony involving a writing requires the original writing.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Mary can quote the exact contents of the receipt.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Aaron admits the receipt existed.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness may testify to matters she personally perceived. Mary is testifying that she saw Aaron sign, not proving the contents of the receipt. The original-writing rule is not triggered by testimony about the observed signing event.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the original-writing rule applies when contents are being proved, not every time a writing appears in the facts. C: C is wrong because the contents are not the target. D: D is wrong because personal knowledge does not depend on Aaron's admission.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 / personal knowledge and original-writing false positive
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests personal knowledge versus original-writing false positive.
