Question: Naomi contracted to sell a small store to Jacob. As part of the price, Jacob promised to pay Ruth $8,000 because Ruth had lent Naomi money to improve the store. Naomi delivered the store and all inventory on time, but she forgot to deliver one box of unused receipt paper worth $20. Jacob refused to pay Ruth, arguing that Naomi breached the sale contract. May Ruth enforce the payment promise?
Answer A: No, because any breach by Naomi defeats all beneficiary rights under the contract.
Answer B: No, because Ruth must first pay Jacob the value of the missing receipt paper.
Answer C: Yes, because Jacob's defense is based only on Naomi's trivial breach and does not excuse Jacob's payment duty to the intended beneficiary.
Answer D: Yes, but only because Ruth is treated as an assignee of Naomi's rights.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A promisor may assert real contract defenses against an intended beneficiary, but not every slight breach excuses performance. Naomi's omission of a small box of receipt paper is not a material breach that defeats Jacob's duty to pay Ruth under the beneficiary clause.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because only a defense sufficient to excuse or limit the promisor's duty defeats the beneficiary. B: B is wrong because Ruth does not become responsible for Naomi's minor nonperformance. D: D is wrong because Ruth's route is intended-beneficiary enforcement, not assignment.
