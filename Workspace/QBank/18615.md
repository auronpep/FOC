Question: John contracted with a builder to construct a porch for John's mother, Deborah. The contract required a simple wooden porch and stated that the builder would not be responsible for upgraded stonework. The builder built the wooden porch, but Deborah wanted a stone porch and sued the builder for the cost of stonework. What is the likely result?
Answer A: Deborah recovers the stonework cost because she was the person meant to benefit from the porch.
Answer B: Deborah recovers the stonework cost because nonparties are not limited by the contract's specifications.
Answer C: Deborah does not recover the stonework cost because her beneficiary right is limited to the promised wooden porch.
Answer D: Deborah does not recover only if John objects to her lawsuit.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Deborah may be an intended beneficiary of the porch promise, but she can enforce only the performance the contract promised. She cannot expand the builder's duty beyond the simple wooden porch that John and the builder agreed to.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because intended benefit does not enlarge the promised performance. B: B is wrong because beneficiaries are limited by contract specifications. D: D is wrong because John's objection is not the basis for limiting Deborah's claim.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — no better than promisee
