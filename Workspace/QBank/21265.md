Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Hannah sues a hotel after a metal bedframe collapsed. She offers the bedframe itself. The hotel argues that the bedframe was moved to a storage room after the accident and may have been bent further during storage. Hannah's maintenance expert testifies that the key fracture pattern is consistent with damage from the collapse and that later storage marks are distinguishable. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the bedframe because any possibility of later damage makes the exhibit irrelevant.
Answer B: Admit the bedframe if the evidence is sufficient to support a finding that it is the accident bedframe and still probative of the relevant damage.
Answer C: Exclude the bedframe unless Hannah proves no one touched it after the accident.
Answer D: Admit the bedframe only if the hotel stipulates that its condition is unchanged.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The proponent must provide enough evidence for a reasonable juror to find that the object is the relevant item and that its condition remains probative. Possible later changes affect weight unless they destroy the probative connection.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a possibility of change does not automatically defeat admissibility. C: C is wrong because a perfect preservation history is not required. D: D is wrong because an opponent's stipulation is not necessary.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / FRE 401
lb_student_diagnostic: Treating possible alteration as automatic exclusion rather than a foundation/weight issue.
