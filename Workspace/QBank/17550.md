Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A taxpayer sues a title company in federal court for state-law malpractice. The complaint alleges that the company mishandled a foreclosure notice and that the only disputed issue is whether a federal tax-lien notice statute required a different notice method. Both parties agree that liability turns on that federal notice issue. What is the strongest argument for federal-question jurisdiction?
Answer A: Federal jurisdiction exists whenever a state-law claim mentions a federal statute
Answer B: Federal jurisdiction exists because the state claim necessarily raises an actually disputed and substantial federal issue
Answer C: Federal jurisdiction is impossible because federal law did not create the malpractice cause of action
Answer D: Federal jurisdiction exists only if the damages exceed $75,000
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal notice issue is a necessary element of the malpractice theory, actually disputed, and substantial in the jurisdictional sense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Mentioning federal law is not enough; the issue must be necessary, disputed, substantial, and suitable for federal resolution. C: Federal creation is the main route, but embedded federal issue jurisdiction is a recognized narrow route. D: Section 1331 has no amount-in-controversy requirement.
lb_rule_reference: Embedded federal-question jurisdiction requires a necessarily raised, actually disputed, substantial federal issue that can be resolved in federal court without disrupting the federal-state balance.
