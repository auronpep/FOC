Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: An employee sued a company for race discrimination. The parties settled, and the case was dismissed with prejudice. The settlement agreement denied liability and did not admit or decide whether discrimination occurred. In a later suit by a different employee, the second employee argues the first settlement proves the company discriminated. Does the first settlement ordinarily establish the discrimination issue through issue preclusion?
Answer A: Yes, because dismissal with prejudice means every factual issue was actually litigated
Answer B: Yes, because settlements always admit liability
Answer C: No, because the discrimination issue was not actually litigated and determined in the settled case
Answer D: No, because issue preclusion never applies in employment cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Ordinary settlement without admissions does not supply the actual-litigation requirement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Dismissal with prejudice may support claim preclusion, but issue preclusion requires actual litigation and determination. B: This settlement expressly denied liability and made no issue determination. D: The subject matter is not the reason; the missing element is actual litigation.
lb_rule_reference: Issue preclusion generally requires that the issue was actually litigated and determined; an ordinary settlement with no admissions usually does not satisfy that requirement.
