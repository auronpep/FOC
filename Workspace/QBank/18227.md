Question: Sarah owed Paul $10,000, but Paul's claim against Sarah had been discharged in bankruptcy. Later, Sarah sold a truck to Abraham. As part of the truck sale price, Abraham promised Sarah in a signed contract that Abraham would pay Paul $6,000. Sarah delivered the truck. Paul sues Abraham for $6,000. What is Abraham's best defense?
Answer A: Paul cannot recover because the old debt was discharged before the truck sale.
Answer B: Paul cannot recover because creditor beneficiaries always need consideration from the creditor.
Answer C: Paul cannot recover because Sarah's bankruptcy discharge prevents anyone from making a new contract that benefits Paul.
Answer D: Abraham has no defense based solely on the old discharge because Paul is enforcing Abraham's new supported promise to pay him.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Paul is not enforcing the discharged debt against Sarah. He is enforcing Abraham's new promise, supported by Sarah's delivery of the truck, to pay Paul as an intended creditor beneficiary. The old debt's discharge is a decoy unless it defeats the new contract, which it does not on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the old debt status does not bar a new supported promise to pay Paul. B: B is wrong because beneficiary consideration is not required. C: C is wrong because bankruptcy discharge of Sarah's old debt does not prevent Sarah and Abraham from making a new contract for Paul's benefit.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — old debt / new promise
