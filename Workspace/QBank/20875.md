Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A repair shop sued a customer in federal court for $9,000 owed on a state-law services contract. The customer filed a counterclaim under a federal consumer-protection statute. The repair shop argues that the federal counterclaim confirms federal-question jurisdiction over the case. Was original federal-question jurisdiction present when the action was filed?
Answer A: Yes, because any federal counterclaim creates Section 1331 jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because the counterclaim and complaint involve the same transaction
Answer C: No, because the plaintiff's complaint pleaded only a state-law contract claim
Answer D: No, because federal courts can never hear counterclaims
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff's own claim did not arise under federal law. The later federal counterclaim does not create original jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Counterclaims do not supply original federal-question jurisdiction under the well-pleaded complaint rule. B: Same transaction may matter after a valid anchor exists, but it does not create original federal-question jurisdiction. D: Federal courts can hear counterclaims when jurisdictional requirements are satisfied.
lb_rule_reference: Section 1331 is measured from the plaintiff's well-pleaded complaint, not from counterclaims.
