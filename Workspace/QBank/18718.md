Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Exclusionary Rule
Question: An officer stopped Victor without reasonable suspicion. While the officer was asking questions, Victor punched the officer and tried to run away. The state charged Victor with assault on a police officer and seeks to introduce testimony about the punch. Victor moves to suppress all evidence of the punch as fruit of the unlawful stop. Should evidence of the punch be suppressed as fruit of the unlawful stop?
Answer A: Yes, because the punch happened during an unlawful stop
Answer B: Yes, because the exclusionary rule immunizes all conduct after police illegality
Answer C: No, because Victor's assault was a new voluntary crime sufficiently independent of the unlawful stop
Answer D: No, because unlawful stops are valid if the suspect later commits a crime
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The stop may have been unlawful, but Victor's punch was a new criminal act. Evidence of that act is generally admissible in the prosecution for the new crime.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A new voluntary crime against an officer is generally treated as an independent act not suppressed as fruit of the prior illegality. B: The exclusionary rule does not grant a license to commit new crimes. It suppresses evidence in appropriate settings, not independent criminal conduct. D: The later crime does not retroactively make the original stop lawful. It affects the remedy for the new offense.
lb_rule_reference: The exclusionary rule generally does not suppress evidence of a new crime committed in response to or after police illegality when the new act is sufficiently independent.
