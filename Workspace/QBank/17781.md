Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal civil case, Lydia testifies that she called a phone number listed for Abraham's household. A voice answered, “Hello,” and Lydia immediately asked, “Abraham, did you approve the shipment?” The voice replied, “Yes.” Lydia had never spoken with Abraham before, and several adults lived in the household. Lydia offers the call as Abraham's statement. Abraham objects that the speaker was not authenticated. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because the call was made to Abraham's household number.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the listed household number and Lydia's question do not, without more, sufficiently identify Abraham as the speaker.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because any answer to a question containing Abraham's name becomes Abraham's statement.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Abraham proves that another adult answered the phone.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A call to a number associated with a household may not, without additional circumstances, identify which person answered. Lydia did not recognize the voice, and multiple adults used the number. The foundation is insufficient.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a shared number does not automatically identify the speaker. C: C is wrong because the examiner cannot authenticate a speaker simply by addressing the caller by name. D: D is wrong because the proponent bears the authentication burden; Abraham need not prove who answered.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(6)
lb_student_diagnostic: Assigned-number overread: student treats a shared phone number as speaker identity.
