Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Commerce power
Question: A state requires every manufacturer that sells batteries in the state to certify that it will not sell the same batteries for a lower price in any other state. The law directly controls prices in transactions between out-of-state manufacturers and out-of-state buyers. What is the strongest constitutional objection?
Answer A: The law impermissibly regulates out-of-state transactions by tying them to the state's price rule
Answer B: The law is valid because the manufacturer chooses to sell inside the state
Answer C: The law is valid if it applies to in-state and out-of-state manufacturers equally
Answer D: The law violates only Article IV privileges and immunities because prices affect citizens
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The state is projecting its regulatory control into commerce occurring wholly outside its borders.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Selling in the state does not allow the state to control separate out-of-state transactions. C: Evenhanded wording does not cure direct regulation of out-of-state commerce. D: The primary issue is regulation of interstate commerce, not discrimination against nonresident citizens' fundamental privileges.
lb_rule_reference: A state may not regulate commerce occurring wholly outside its borders by controlling out-of-state prices or transactions.
