Outline: Duress
Question: Marisol receives a text from a former partner in another state: "If you do not steal medication from your clinic by next Friday, I will hurt you when I come back next month." Marisol believes the threat is serious. She steals medication the next day without contacting police, changing locks, or seeking help. Is duress likely to succeed?
Answer A: Yes, because Marisol genuinely believed the threat
Answer B: Yes, because the threat involved bodily harm rather than money
Answer C: No, because the threat was future and conditional and she had reasonable legal alternatives
Answer D: No, because duress is unavailable unless the defendant is physically touched
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The coercer was in another state, the threatened harm was next month, and Marisol had time to seek protection or avoid the threat. Duress fails for lack of imminence and because legal alternatives existed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Duress requires imminence and no reasonable legal alternative. The threat was delayed and conditional. B: The type of harm is only one element. Timing and alternatives also matter. D: Physical touching is not required. The problem is lack of imminence and alternatives, not absence of contact.
lb_rule_reference: Duress requires imminent threat and no reasonable legal alternative; a future conditional threat usually fails
