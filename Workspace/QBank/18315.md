Outline: Use of impeachment material
Question: In a federal fraud trial, James testifies for the plaintiff. On cross-examination, the defendant's lawyer asks, “Didn't you write to Ezra last month that the plaintiff had changed the invoice after signing?” James objects that the lawyer did not first show him the letter. The plaintiff's lawyer asks to see the letter before the questioning continues. How should the court rule?
Answer A: The lawyer may question James about the letter without first showing it to him, but must show or disclose its contents to the plaintiff's lawyer on request.
Answer B: The lawyer must show the letter to James before asking any question about it.
Answer C: The lawyer may question James about the letter and need not show it to anyone unless James denies making it.
Answer D: The lawyer may use the letter only if it is first admitted as a substantive exhibit.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 613(a) abolishes the old requirement that a cross-examiner first show a prior statement to the witness. But on request, the examiner must show or disclose the statement's contents to the adverse party's attorney.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the witness need not be shown the statement first. C: C is wrong because the adverse party's attorney is entitled to see or hear the statement on request. D: D is wrong because use of a prior statement for impeachment questioning does not require that the statement first be admitted substantively.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 613(a) / showing or disclosing prior statement during examination
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the presentation mechanics for using a prior statement during examination: witness-first disclosure is not required, but adverse-counsel disclosure is.
