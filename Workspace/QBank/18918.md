Question: A state legislature enacts a licensing statute requiring anyone who arranges travel itineraries for compensation to obtain a state travel-agent license. Obtaining the license requires passing a written exam and paying a $250 annual fee. A national internet-based travel booking company challenges the statute on Equal Protection grounds, arguing that the law irrationally treats travel-agent services differently from other internet-based booking platforms that charge transaction fees without arranging itineraries. The state offers no legislative findings justifying the distinction. At trial, the state's attorney argues that licensing prevents consumer fraud and ensures agents are familiar with booking procedures. The plaintiff responds that the legislature never articulated those goals. Under rational basis Equal Protection review, which of the following best explains how the court should evaluate the travel-agent licensing statute?
Answer A: The statute fails because the state did not prove that the licensing requirement actually reduces consumer fraud
Answer B: The statute fails because internet-based booking platforms are similarly situated to licensed travel agents and cannot be treated differently
Answer C: The statute survives because a court can hypothesize that reducing consumer fraud and ensuring agent competence are legitimate interests rationally served by a licensing requirement for itinerary arrangers
Answer D: The statute must be evaluated under intermediate scrutiny because it discriminates against a business that engages in interstate commerce
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under rational basis review, courts do not require the legislature to articulate its purpose. A reviewing court will uphold the statute if any conceivable legitimate interest exists and the classification is rationally related to it. Consumer protection and competence assurance are paradigmatic legitimate state interests, and requiring a license for those who actively arrange itineraries — as opposed to passive booking platforms — is a rational means to pursue those ends.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under rational basis review, the government does not bear the burden of proving efficacy. Courts ask only whether any conceivable set of facts supports the classification. The plaintiff bears the burden to negate every conceivable basis; the state's failure to submit findings is irrelevant. B: Even if two groups are functionally similar, a legislature may draw lines between them as long as the line is rationally related to a legitimate interest. Rational basis is highly deferential; the court will uphold a classification that has even a plausible, conceivable justification. The distinction between arranging itineraries and merely processing transactions is a rational line. D: Equal Protection heightened scrutiny applies to classifications based on suspect or quasi-suspect classifications (race, national origin, sex, etc.) or fundamental rights. Economic regulation affecting interstate commerce does not trigger intermediate or strict scrutiny under the Equal Protection Clause; it triggers rational basis. Commerce Clause challenges are a separate analytical track.
lb_rule_reference: Under rational basis review, a law survives Equal Protection challenge if it is rationally related to a legitimate government interest; courts will hypothesize any conceivable justification.
