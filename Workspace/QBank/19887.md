MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Negligence per se
Question: A city ordinance requires bicycle riders to use headlights at night to protect pedestrians and drivers from collisions. A cyclist rode at night without a headlight. A thief later stole the unlit bicycle from a rack and used it to damage a parked car. The car owner sues the cyclist and invokes negligence per se. Does the headlight ordinance establish negligence per se for the car damage?
Answer A: Yes, because any statutory violation is negligence per se for any later harm
Answer B: Yes, because negligence per se proves causation and damages too
Answer C: No, because the ordinance does not target this plaintiff or this type of harm
Answer D: No, because statutes can never supply negligence standards
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The ordinance protects against nighttime collision risks, not later theft damage by a third person.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The statute must match the protected class and harm type. Theft damage to a parked car is not the nighttime collision risk the ordinance targets. B: Even when applicable, negligence per se addresses duty and breach; causation and damages remain. D: Statutes can supply standards when class, risk, violation, and excuse requirements are met. They are not met here.
lb_rule_reference: Negligence per se applies only when the statute protects this class of plaintiff from this type of harm and the violation is unexcused.
