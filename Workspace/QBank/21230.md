Outline: Damages
Question: A courier negligently swung a heavy package into a shop's glass display case, leaving a visible scratch. The scratch did not stop the shop from opening, did not reduce sales, and cost only a modest amount to polish out. The courier admits breach but argues that the shop has no negligence claim because the loss was too small and no one was physically injured. Is the shop likely to satisfy the damages element in a negligence claim against the courier?
Answer A: Yes, because the scratched display case is actual property damage
Answer B: Yes, because nominal damages are always available when a defendant is careless
Answer C: No, because negligence damages require physical injury to a person
Answer D: No, because the shop did not prove lost profits
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Negligence requires actual harm, but the harm need not be large. Physical damage to the shop's property satisfies the damages element.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The shop does not need nominal damages because it has actual property damage. Nominal damages generally are not the negligence basis. C: Negligence can compensate property damage as well as personal injury when the other elements are met. D: Lost profits are not required when the plaintiff has physical property damage. Repair or restoration cost can be compensable.
lb_rule_reference: Even slight property damage can satisfy the actual harm element of negligence; the plaintiff need not show large economic loss.
