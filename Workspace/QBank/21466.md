Question: In a federal civil trial, Noah testifies for Sarah. On cross-examination, the defendant shows that Noah is Sarah's business partner and will benefit if Sarah wins. Sarah then calls Martha to testify that Noah has a reputation for truthfulness. The defendant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit Martha's testimony because any attack on credibility permits truthful-character rehabilitation.
Answer B: Admit Martha's testimony because bias and truthfulness are the same credibility trait.
Answer C: Exclude Martha's testimony because showing Noah's financial bias does not, by itself, attack Noah's character for truthfulness.
Answer D: Exclude Martha's testimony only if Noah has already admitted the partnership.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Truthful-character support under Rule 608(a) is permitted only after the witness's character for truthfulness has been attacked. Showing that Noah has a financial bias attacks motive or interest, not his character for truthfulness. That bias impeachment does not automatically open the door to reputation evidence for truthfulness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every credibility attack is a character-for-truthfulness attack. B: B is wrong because bias and truthfulness are different impeachment theories. D: D is wrong because the result does not turn on whether Noah admitted the partnership.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(a) / bias attack does not open truthful-character support
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the distinction between bias impeachment and character-for-truthfulness attack.
