Subtopic: Defenses to Enforceability
Outline: Illegality
Question: Rebecca signed a written agreement to pay Nathan $30,000 for “consulting services.” Nathan sues when Rebecca refuses to pay. Rebecca offers evidence that, before signing, Nathan told her the real purpose of the contract was to pay a city official for an unlawful permit approval. Nathan objects that the written agreement says only “consulting services.”
Answer A: Yes, because evidence offered to show an illegal purpose and avoid enforcement is not barred merely because the writing uses lawful words.
Answer B: No, because the written words “consulting services” control for every purpose.
Answer C: No, because illegality evidence is admissible only if the contract itself mentions the unlawful act.
Answer D: Yes, but only to prove the market value of consulting services.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Evidence of illegality may be admitted to show that a contract should not be enforced. Rebecca is not trying to add a consulting term; she is trying to prove that the transaction is unenforceable because of its unlawful purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because lawful wording does not bar extrinsic evidence of illegal purpose. C: C is wrong because illegality is often proved by evidence outside the writing. D: D is wrong because the evidence is offered to avoid enforcement, not value services.
