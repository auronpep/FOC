Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Identifications
Question: A witness saw a masked attacker for one second in a dim hallway. Two months later, police placed the defendant in a lineup with five fillers of a different race and much shorter height. Before the lineup, an officer told the witness, "We arrested the attacker." The witness identified the defendant. What is the strongest due process argument?
Answer A: The identification should be suppressed because police-arranged suggestiveness and weak reliability create a substantial likelihood of misidentification
Answer B: The identification is admissible because the witness selected someone
Answer C: The identification is admissible because confidence always cures suggestiveness
Answer D: The identification is suppressed only if counsel was absent
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The fillers did not match, police primed the witness, the original view was brief and poor, and two months passed. Suggestiveness plus weak reliability supports suppression.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The existence of an identification does not answer whether it is constitutionally reliable after police suggestiveness. C: Confidence is not a cure-all, especially after suggestive procedures and weak observation conditions. D: This objection is due process suggestiveness and reliability, not necessarily counsel absence.
lb_rule_reference: A police-arranged identification should be suppressed when unnecessary suggestiveness combines with weak reliability factors to create a substantial likelihood of misidentification.
