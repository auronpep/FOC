Outline: Clergy and penitent
Question: Two business partners, Thomas and Andrew, asked Rabbi Ezra to help mediate a lease dispute because Ezra knew both families. The meeting focused on rent numbers and property repairs, and Ezra did not offer religious counseling. Thomas later claims clergy-penitent privilege over his statements in the meeting. Which result is most proper?
Answer A: Sustain the claim, because any meeting with a rabbi is privileged.
Answer B: Overrule the claim, because Ezra was acting as a secular mediator in a business dispute rather than receiving confidential spiritual communications.
Answer C: Sustain the claim because lease disputes can affect family harmony.
Answer D: Overrule the claim only if Andrew also waives privilege.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The privilege depends on a communication to clergy in spiritual or professional religious capacity. A secular business mediation does not become privileged merely because clergy facilitated it. The decisive axis is function of the meeting.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is the spiritual-title shortcut. C: C is wrong because Family harmony is not the protected object of clergy-penitent privilege. D: D is wrong because Andrew's waiver is irrelevant because Thomas's statements do not qualify as clergy-penitent communications in the first place.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / spiritual versus secular mediation role
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is routing by title and relationship instead of the role and purpose of the communication.
