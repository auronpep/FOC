Question: Students wear black armbands to protest a foreign war. The school bans only the armbands because some parents object to the message. The school has no evidence that the armbands caused class interruptions, fights, or other disruption. Is the ban likely valid?
Answer A: Yes, because students have no speech rights in public school
Answer B: Yes, because discomfort with a political message is material disruption
Answer C: No, because the school targeted protected student expression without evidence of material disruption
Answer D: No, because the entire school is a traditional public forum
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A silent political armband is protected absent material disruption or a reasonable forecast of it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Students retain First Amendment rights, though schools have special authority. B: Mere discomfort or parent objection is not material and substantial disruption. D: Student-speech doctrine protects the armbands; the school is not treated as a full traditional public forum.
lb_rule_reference: A public school may not suppress student expression merely to avoid discomfort; it needs evidence or reasonable forecast of material and substantial disruption.
