Question: A defendant was charged with attempted murder. At the preliminary hearing, the presiding judge heard the testimony of four prosecution witnesses and found that the prosecution had failed to establish probable cause that the defendant had committed any offense. Accordingly, he dismissed the charge. The prosecutor then called the same four witnesses before a grand jury. The grand jury indicted the same defendant for attempted murder. The defendant has moved to quash the indictment on the ground of double jeopardy. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: A: Grant the motion, because the dismissal of the first charge on the merits, whether correct or incorrect, bars any further prosecution.
Answer B: B: Grant the motion, unless the prosecution has evidence that was not presented in the first case.
Answer C: C: Deny the motion, because the defendant has not yet been in jeopardy of conviction on the attempted murder charge.
Answer D: D: Deny the motion, because the protection of the double jeopardy clause does not come into play until there has been a conviction or an acquittal.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. The dismissal after a preliminary hearing had no double jeopardy consequences because jeopardy only attaches when the jury is empaneled and sworn in, or in a bench trial, the first witness is sworn in.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The dismissal of the first charge occurred during a preliminary hearing, and jeopardy had not yet attached because no jury or witness had been sworn in. Therefore, further prosecution is not barred. B is incorrect. The prosecution is not required to present new evidence to the grand jury once the charge had been dismissed during the preliminary hearing. Jeopardy had not attached, and the government can secure an indictment following the initial dismissal of the charges for the same offense. D is incorrect. While jeopardy does not attach until a trial begins, actions short of a conviction or acquittal may bar retrial on double jeopardy grounds.
