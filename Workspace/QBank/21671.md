Question: Samuel orally told David to sell Samuel's lot to Elizabeth. David then wrote and signed a memorandum stating, “Samuel has authorized me to convey his lot to Elizabeth.” Samuel did not sign the memorandum or any power of attorney. David signed Samuel's name to a deed, delivered it to Elizabeth, and Elizabeth accepted. Who owns the lot?
Answer A: Elizabeth, because there was a writing describing David's authority.
Answer B: Elizabeth, because David signed both the memorandum and the deed.
Answer C: Elizabeth, because Samuel orally authorized David before the deed was signed.
Answer D: Samuel, because the required written authority was not signed or adopted by Samuel.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is an agent-signed deed, and the decisive validity gate is written authority from the owner. David's own memorandum did not supply Samuel's written authorization because Samuel neither signed nor adopted it. The oral instruction and agent-signed memo do not satisfy the authority lock, so title remains with Samuel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the writing must evidence the principal's authorization, not merely the agent's assertion. B: B is wrong because the agent's signature cannot create the principal's deed authority. C: C is wrong because oral authority is insufficient for an agent to execute a land conveyance.
lb_rule_reference: Written authority must come from principal
