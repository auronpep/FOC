


BARMATRIX Q#: 22832
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A plane crashed in a foreign country, injuring many of the passengers. The passengers were all citizens of the foreign country. The passengers sued a company based in State A in State A federal district court, claiming that a part manufactured by the State A corporation failed and caused the crash. Each of the passengers claimed over $100,000 in damages. The attorney for the State A corporation did not want the case to be heard in federal district court. Which of the following is the attorney's best course of action?
Answer A: A: Move to dismiss based on the doctrine of forum non conveniens.
Answer B: B: Move to dismiss based on lack of personal jurisdiction over the foreign plaintiffs.
Answer C: C: Move to dismiss based on the lack of subject matter jurisdiction over the claim.
Answer D: D: File an answer requesting transfer to another federal district court.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The attorney's best chance to avoid federal district court is to ask the court to dismiss based on the doctrine of forum non conveniens. 28 U.S.C. §1404 allows a court to transfer an action for the convenience of the parties and witnesses. Here, a court in the foreign country would likely be a better forum, considering all of the plaintiffs are from that country and the accident occurred there. Consequently, the attorney should ask for the claim to be heard in the foreign country and not in federal district court. B is incorrect because the foreign plaintiffs consented to State A federal jurisdiction by filing a claim in State A federal court. C is incorrect because federal courts have subject matter jurisdiction over claims asserted by foreign nationals against United States citizens so long as the amount in controversy is over $75,000. Here, each one of the plaintiffs is claiming over $100,000 in damages. D is incorrect because the question specifically states that the attorney does not want the case to be heard in a federal district court.
