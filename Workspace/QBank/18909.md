Question: During an unlawful interrogation, a suspect told police where he hid a murder weapon in a public park. At that same time, a lawful grid search already underway had assigned officers to that exact park sector and was minutes from reaching the hiding place. The search protocol required officers to inspect every drainage pipe. Police found the weapon after using the suspect's statement. The suspect moves to suppress the weapon. What is the best reason the weapon may be admitted?
Answer A: The weapon may be admitted because the lawful grid search would inevitably have discovered it
Answer B: The weapon must be suppressed because all evidence following illegality is inadmissible
Answer C: The weapon may be admitted because physical evidence is never suppressed after an unlawful interrogation
Answer D: The weapon must be suppressed unless officers had a warrant for the park
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The government has a concrete lawful discovery path already in progress. The grid search would have reached the exact location and inspected the hiding place, satisfying inevitable discovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Fruit evidence can be admitted when a recognized exception such as inevitable discovery applies. C: Physical evidence can be suppressible depending on the constitutional defect. The correct path here is inevitable discovery. D: A public-park grid search can provide a lawful discovery path without a warrant on these facts.
lb_rule_reference: Evidence obtained unlawfully is admissible if the prosecution proves it would inevitably have been discovered through independent lawful procedures.
