Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Intentional infliction of emotional distress
Question: A debt collector, knowing that a debtor's young daughter was standing next to her, screamed false accusations that the mother would be taken to jail that night and deliberately shoved the mother against a wall. The daughter saw the entire event and suffered severe emotional distress. The daughter sues the debt collector for intentional infliction of emotional distress. Which result is most likely?
Answer A: The daughter may recover because she was present, was a close family member, the defendant knew she was present, and she suffered severe distress
Answer B: The daughter cannot recover because the conduct was directed at her mother, not at her
Answer C: The daughter can recover only if she was physically touched
Answer D: The daughter cannot recover unless the debt collector intended to harm her mother physically
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The bystander IIED gate is satisfied by presence, close relationship, defendant awareness, extreme conduct, and severe distress.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Bystander recovery can exist for close family members present for outrageous conduct. C: IIED does not require physical touching of the plaintiff. D: IIED can rest on intent or recklessness as to severe emotional distress.
lb_rule_reference: A bystander may recover for IIED when present for extreme and outrageous conduct directed at a close family member and suffers severe emotional distress, with the defendant aware of the bystander's presence.
