Question: A contractor agreed to build a storage shed for Ruth after Ruth selected one of three roof colors by June 1. Ruth selected the color on June 2. The contractor had not ordered materials yet, and the one-day delay caused no cost or schedule change. The contractor refused to build and claimed Ruth's late selection discharged all his duties. What is the best result?
Answer A: The contractor is discharged because any date in a contract is an express condition.
Answer B: The contractor is discharged only if Ruth selected a color the contractor disliked.
Answer C: The contract is void because roof color was an essential term left open until June 2.
Answer D: Ruth's one-day delay is at most a minor breach on these facts and does not discharge the contractor's duty to perform.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Where a timing requirement is not framed as a strict express condition and the minor delay causes no material harm, the delay generally does not discharge the other party's duty. The contractor may not treat a harmless one-day delay as a total discharge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a contract date is not automatically a strict express condition. B: B is wrong because the contractor's preference is not the legal test. C: C is wrong because the parties had an agreed method for selecting the color, and the slight delay did not void the contract.
lb_rule_reference: Constructive condition / minor delay
