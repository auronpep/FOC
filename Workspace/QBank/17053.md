Outline: Misrepresentation
Question: An accountant negligently prepares a financial report for a client to use in negotiations with one named lender. The client later posts the report online. An unrelated investor sees the post, relies on the report, and loses money buying the client's bonds. Is the investor's negligent misrepresentation claim against the accountant likely strong?
Answer A: Yes, because anyone who foreseeably reads a careless report may recover for economic loss
Answer B: Yes, because negligent misrepresentation never requires a duty to the plaintiff
Answer C: No, because the report was supplied for a named lender, not for an unlimited class of online investors
Answer D: No, because accountants are immune from tort liability for financial reports
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The investor was outside the limited class for whose benefit and guidance the report was supplied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Liability is not usually open-ended to every foreseeable reader. B: Duty and limited-purpose reliance are central limits. D: Accountants may be liable when the required duty and reliance limits are met.
lb_rule_reference: Negligent misrepresentation liability for economic loss is commonly limited to a person or limited class for whose benefit and guidance the information was supplied.
