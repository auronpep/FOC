Question: Reed drives a truck through a crowded alley at a reckless speed while making a delivery. A pedestrian jumps away just in time and is not hit. Reed did not want to injure or kill anyone, but he consciously disregarded a serious risk. Prosecutors charge Reed with attempted involuntary manslaughter. What is Reed's strongest argument?
Answer A: Attempted involuntary manslaughter is generally invalid because attempt requires purpose to commit the target crime, not merely reckless risk creation
Answer B: Reed is guilty because recklessness is enough for the completed manslaughter offense
Answer C: Reed is guilty because the pedestrian was physically present in the alley
Answer D: Reed is not guilty only because the pedestrian was not actually touched
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Attempt is a specific-intent crime. Involuntary manslaughter is based on an unintended killing caused by reckless or negligent conduct, so there is no intended killing for attempt liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The completed-offense mens rea does not replace attempt's specific-intent requirement. C: Physical danger does not supply the required intent to kill. D: Attempt does not require completion. The problem is the lack of specific intent for an attempted unintended killing.
lb_rule_reference: Attempt requires specific intent to commit the target offense; attempted involuntary manslaughter is generally not a valid charge because involuntary manslaughter is based on unintended killing.
