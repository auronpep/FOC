Question: In a federal civil fraud trial, Priscilla testifies for one party. The opposing party seeks to impeach her with a two-year-old conviction for perjury. Priscilla objects that the conviction is highly embarrassing and that the case is civil. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the conviction because conviction impeachment is allowed only in criminal cases.
Answer B: Admit the conviction because perjury required proof of a false statement and is admissible to attack truthfulness.
Answer C: Exclude the conviction unless Priscilla first denies that she was convicted.
Answer D: Admit the conviction only if the judge finds that its probative value substantially outweighs prejudice.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A conviction for a crime whose elements required proving a dishonest act or false statement must be admitted to attack the witness's character for truthfulness. Perjury is the classic false-statement conviction and Rule 609 applies in civil cases.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because conviction impeachment is not limited to criminal trials. C: C is wrong because denial is not a prerequisite. D: D is wrong because the special old-conviction standard is not triggered by a two-year-old conviction and the mandatory false-statement route applies.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(a)(2) / dishonest-act conviction
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the dishonest-act conviction route and civil-case false objection.
