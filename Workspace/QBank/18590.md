Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Abigail sues Peter for defamation. Abigail offers a screenshot of a social media post accusing her of theft. The account name is “PeterTruth,” but Abigail offers no evidence that Peter created or controlled the account, that the account used Peter's photograph, that the post contained information distinctive to Peter, or that Peter ever acknowledged the account. Peter objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the screenshot because an account name matching Peter is always enough to authenticate a social media post.
Answer B: Admit the screenshot because defamatory statements are automatically relevant.
Answer C: Exclude the screenshot only if Peter proves that someone else created the account.
Answer D: Exclude the screenshot because Abigail has not produced evidence sufficient to support a finding that Peter authored or adopted the post.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The screenshot may be relevant only if there is sufficient evidence to support a finding that it is connected to Peter. A username alone, without more, is not enough on these facts. Abigail bears the threshold burden of authentication.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because self-labeling or a similar account name alone is not automatically sufficient. B: B is wrong because relevance does not bypass authentication. C: C is wrong because Peter does not have the burden to disprove authorship before Abigail lays a foundation.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901
lb_student_diagnostic: Self-identification trap and burden reversal in digital authentication.
