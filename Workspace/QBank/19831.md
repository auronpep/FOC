Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A circular saw has a defective blade guard that sticks open. A user notices the guard sticking but carelessly continues cutting without slowing down. The defect and the user's carelessness both contribute to a hand injury. The jurisdiction applies comparative fault to strict products claims. What is the likely effect of the user's carelessness?
Answer A: It automatically bars recovery because strict products liability cannot coexist with plaintiff fault
Answer B: It is irrelevant because plaintiff conduct never matters in strict products liability
Answer C: It may reduce the user's recovery according to comparative fault
Answer D: It converts the defect claim into breach of warranty only
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The jurisdiction's comparative-fault rule makes the user's negligent conduct relevant to allocation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: In a comparative-fault jurisdiction, plaintiff fault may reduce recovery. B: Comparative responsibility can apply to strict products claims. D: Plaintiff conduct affects defense and apportionment, not the basic theory label.
lb_rule_reference: In a comparative-fault jurisdiction, a plaintiff's negligent use of a defective product may reduce recovery rather than automatically bar the claim.
