


BARMATRIX Q#: 18294
Outline_code: 95070104
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Crossclaims
Question: A computer manufacturer shipped 100 computers to a store through a special delivery service. When the computers arrived at the store, all of them had cracked screens, and the store refused to pay. The manufacturer then filed suit against both the store and the delivery company in federal district court. The manufacturer was incorporated and had its primary place of business in State A. The delivery company was incorporated in State B and had its primary place of business in State B. The store was incorporated in State C and had its primary place of business in State B. The store wanted to make a claim against the delivery service for the damaged computers, but it wanted to file its own claim in State B state court after the manufacturer's claim is over.&#10;&#10;May the store do so?
Answer A: A: No, because the issue of liability will already be fully litigated.
Answer B: B: No, because the claim arises from the same transaction or occurrence.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the store's claim is optional.
Answer D: D: Yes, because both the store and the delivery service are citizens of the same state.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A crossclaim is when a party makes a claim against a co-plaintiff or co-defendant. Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 13(g), a crossclaim must be part of the same transaction or occurrence. However, a crossclaim is always optional. Consequently, the store can file its own claim against the delivery service.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A and B are incorrect because the crossclaim is still optional even if it is fully litigated in the other action and even if it arises out of the same transaction or occurrence. D is incorrect because the citizenship of the parties does not change the analysis regarding the crossclaim.
