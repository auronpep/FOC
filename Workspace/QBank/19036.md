MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A clinic clerk angry about a billing dispute posted a patient's non-newsworthy lab results and mental-health intake form on a community forum with thousands of subscribers. The records were accurate and had never been public. Is the patient likely to prevail on a public disclosure of private facts claim against the clerk?
Answer A: No, because the posted information was true
Answer B: Yes, because the clerk publicized private, highly offensive medical facts with no public concern
Answer C: No, because the patient must prove the records created a false impression
Answer D: Yes, but only if the patient proves lost income from the disclosure
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The clerk broadly publicized private medical and mental-health information that was highly offensive to disclose and not newsworthy.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Public disclosure of private facts targets truthful but private, highly offensive, non-newsworthy publicity. C: False impression is relevant to false light; disclosure of private facts can involve true facts. D: Public disclosure of private facts does not require trade-libel-style special damages.
lb_rule_reference: Public disclosure liability requires widespread publicity of private, highly offensive facts that are not of legitimate public concern.
