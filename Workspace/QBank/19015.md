Outline: Defamation
Question: A columnist hated the mayor and wrote a harsh article falsely accusing the mayor of using city cars for personal errands. Before publication, the columnist checked mileage logs and interviewed two drivers, both of whom supported the accusation. The records later turned out to be inaccurate, but the columnist did not know that. Has the mayor shown actual malice merely from the columnist's hostility?
Answer A: No, because actual malice requires knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard for truth, not ill will alone
Answer B: Yes, because actual malice means spite toward the plaintiff
Answer C: Yes, because any false article about a mayor is automatically actionable
Answer D: No, because public officials can never sue for defamation
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Hostility is not enough when the publisher relied on seemingly supportive evidence without serious truth doubts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Constitutional actual malice is about the defendant's relationship to truth. C: A public official must prove falsity and actual malice. D: Public officials may recover when they prove actual malice.
lb_rule_reference: Actual malice means knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard for truth, not spite, hostility, or bad motive alone.
