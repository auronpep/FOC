Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Minimum contacts test
Question: Owner lives in State A but owns a cabin in State B. Neighbor sues Owner in State B to quiet title to a disputed strip of land along the cabin boundary and to enjoin Owner from building a fence on that strip. Owner has no other State B contacts. What is the best personal-jurisdiction analysis?
Answer A: Jurisdiction is supported because Owner's State B property is the subject of the dispute
Answer B: Jurisdiction fails because property can never count after modern minimum-contacts doctrine
Answer C: Jurisdiction exists because State B has subject-matter jurisdiction over land disputes
Answer D: Jurisdiction fails unless Owner personally entered State B to inspect the cabin
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim concerns rights in State B property. Ownership of that property supplies a claim-related forum contact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The property is not random collateral; it is the direct subject of the dispute. C: The correct PJ point is Owner's claim-related property contact, not subject-matter jurisdiction. D: Owning the forum property that is the subject of the suit can itself supply the relevant contact.
lb_rule_reference: Jurisdiction based on property must satisfy due process; ownership of forum property can supply the needed contact when the claim concerns rights in that property.
