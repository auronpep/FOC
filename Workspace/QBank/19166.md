Outline: Marital Communications Privilege
Question: After a divorce, a former wife is called in a civil action to testify that, during the marriage, she saw her husband carry a ledger into the garage and hide it under a floorboard. The husband objects under the marital communications privilege. No words or gestures intended as a confidential message are involved. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because the testimony concerns an observed act, not a confidential marital communication.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because any fact learned during marriage is privileged.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because the privilege survives divorce.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only because the case is civil rather than criminal.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The marital communications privilege protects confidential communications, not every observation one spouse makes during marriage. The former wife's testimony concerns conduct she observed. The decisive axis is communication versus observation.

C is wrong because The privilege can survive divorce, but only for protected communications. Survival does not convert observations into communications.
