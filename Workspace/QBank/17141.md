Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Solomon operated a large shop with an unlimited supply of identical tents and capacity to sell as many tents as customers ordered. Martha contracted to buy a tent for $1,000. Solomon's cost for each tent was $700. Martha repudiated. Solomon resold an identical tent to another customer for $1,000, but he would have made that sale even if Martha had performed. What damages should Solomon recover from Martha?
Answer A: Nothing, because Solomon resold an identical tent for the same price.
Answer B: $300, Solomon's lost profit on the additional sale he would have made to Martha.
Answer C: $1,000, the full contract price.
Answer D: $700, Solomon's cost of the tent.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A lost-volume seller is not made whole by resale when it would have made both sales. The proper expectation measure is the profit the seller would have made from the breached sale, plus any proper incidentals not stated here. $1,000 minus $700 equals $300.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because resale does not erase the loss for a lost-volume seller. C: C is wrong because action for the full price is not the ordinary measure when the seller resold and seeks profit. D: D is wrong because cost is not the seller's lost-profit expectation measure.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-708(2) / lost-volume seller profit
