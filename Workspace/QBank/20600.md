Outline: Defamation
Question: A radio host falsely said, "The drivers who work for Metro Taxi are all insurance scammers." Metro Taxi has 900 drivers. The host did not name any driver, route, shift, or small subgroup. One driver sued the host for defamation. Is the individual driver likely to prevail?
Answer A: Yes, because the statement was false and insulting
Answer B: Yes, because every member of a criticized group can sue
Answer C: No, because the statement did not reasonably identify the individual driver within the large group
Answer D: No, because radio statements can never be defamation
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Although the accusation was defamatory in tone, it targeted a 900-person group without facts pointing to this plaintiff.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The statement concerns a large group and does not reasonably identify the individual plaintiff. B: Large-group accusations generally are not of and concerning each member for individual defamation liability. D: Broadcast statements can be defamatory. The issue is of-and-concerning, not the medium.
lb_rule_reference: A defamatory statement about a large group usually is not of and concerning each member unless the statement reasonably identifies the plaintiff individually or a small group.
