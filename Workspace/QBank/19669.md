Question: Esther sues Peter for injuries from a fall in a store. Peter asks the court to take judicial notice of a complaint Esther filed two years earlier in another court. That complaint alleged that a different accident caused the same back injury. Esther objects. What is the most accurate ruling?
Answer A: The court may take judicial notice that the earlier complaint was filed and contained the allegation, but not that the allegation was true.
Answer B: The court must take judicial notice that the earlier allegation was true because it appeared in a court file.
Answer C: The court may not take judicial notice of court records for any purpose.
Answer D: The court must exclude the earlier complaint because pleadings in other cases are never relevant.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A court may take judicial notice of the existence and contents of court records, such as the fact that a complaint was filed and what it alleged. Judicial notice of the record does not establish the truth of disputed factual allegations in that record.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because court-record notice does not make disputed allegations true. C: C is wrong because courts may notice the existence and contents of court records. D: D is wrong because the complaint may be relevant for a nontruth or impeachment-related purpose, but judicial notice does not prove the allegation's truth.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 201 / court record existence versus truth
