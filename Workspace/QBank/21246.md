Question: In a federal contract trial, Sarah testifies for the plaintiff. The defendant seeks to impeach Sarah with a recent conviction for embezzlement. Sarah objects that her appeal from the conviction is still pending. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the conviction because a conviction on appeal is not final for impeachment purposes.
Answer B: Admit the conviction if it otherwise satisfies Rule 609, and also permit evidence that the appeal is pending.
Answer C: Admit the conviction only if the appellate court has affirmed it.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction because the pendency of appeal makes the conviction hearsay.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 609(e), a conviction that satisfies Rule 609 is admissible even if an appeal is pending, and evidence of the pendency of the appeal is also admissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and C are wrong because final appellate affirmance is not required under Rule 609(e). D is wrong because the issue is the effect of a pending appeal on conviction impeachment, not hearsay classification.
