Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Claudette runs a handmade jewelry business from her studio in the State of Parren. Each spring and fall, she travels to the State of Elwood to spend three weekends selling her jewelry at Elwood's licensed artisan market, which draws regional buyers. Over the past two years, Claudette has completed 320 Elwood transactions totaling $48,000 in Elwood sales. She collects Elwood sales tax, holds a temporary Elwood vendor permit, and has a dedicated Elwood-market PayPal account linked to an Elwood bank. An Elwood customer purchased a bracelet at the market that had a defective clasp; the clasp failed and the bracelet fell into the customer's soup, which the customer inhaled causing an allergic reaction. The customer filed a product-liability suit against Claudette in Elwood federal court. Claudette moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction, arguing she is not "established" in Elwood. Does the Elwood court have specific personal jurisdiction over Claudette?
Answer A: No, because Claudette's three-weekend visits to Elwood are temporary and do not establish the sustained presence required for specific personal jurisdiction
Answer B: No, because Claudette's Parren studio is her principal place of business, and a defendant's PPB state is its sole home state for personal jurisdiction purposes
Answer C: Yes, because Claudette deliberately and repeatedly sold goods in Elwood for commercial gain — holding an Elwood vendor permit, collecting Elwood sales tax, and completing 320 Elwood transactions — and the product-liability claim arises directly from one of those Elwood sales
Answer D: Yes, but only because Claudette's Elwood bank account creates a financial contact with Elwood that independently satisfies the minimum-contacts requirement
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Claudette purposefully availed herself of the Elwood market through deliberate, repeated commercial activity: traveling to Elwood, obtaining an Elwood vendor permit, paying Elwood sales tax, and completing 320 sales transactions totaling $48,000. This is not an inadvertent or unilateral contact — it is deliberate commercial exploitation of the Elwood market. The product-liability claim arises directly from an Elwood market sale. The long-arm statute (transacting business in state) is satisfied, and due process is satisfied by purposeful availment plus relatedness. The motion should be denied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Specific jurisdiction does not require sustained or continuous presence — it requires that the claim arise from the defendant's purposeful availment of the forum. Claudette deliberately traveled to Elwood to sell jewelry — 320 transactions, $48,000 in revenue over two years. She holds an Elwood vendor permit and Elwood sales-tax registration. This is deliberate commercial exploitation of the Elwood market. The product-liability claim arises directly from an Elwood sale at the Elwood market. Both the long-arm step and due-process purposeful-availment step are satisfied. B: The PPB-equals-home-state rule applies to general jurisdiction — it defines where a corporation (or, for individuals, where their domicile is) has general-PJ exposure. It does not limit specific jurisdiction. Specific jurisdiction may exist in any state where the defendant purposefully availed itself of that forum and the claim arises from those contacts. Claudette's Parren studio is her home base; that does not prevent Elwood from exercising specific jurisdiction for claims arising from her Elwood commercial activity. D: The bank account is confirmatory evidence of Claudette's Elwood commercial presence but is not the dispositive contact. The correct basis for specific jurisdiction is the 320 Elwood commercial transactions and the market vendor activity that gives rise to the product-liability claim. A bank account alone, without the underlying commercial activity, might not independently satisfy purposeful availment. The answer also states the bank account "independently" satisfies minimum contacts — a misstatement of how multiple contacts work together holistically.
lb_rule_reference: Specific personal jurisdiction may be based on the defendant's in-person commercial activity in the forum, even if periodic and temporary. A seller who travels to the forum to sell goods at a market or fair has purposefully availed itself of the forum for claims arising from those sales. The transient nature of the visit does not defeat jurisdiction when commercial exploitation of the forum is the purpose.
