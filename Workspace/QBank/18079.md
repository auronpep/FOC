Question: A privately owned shopping center invites the public to shop but prohibits political petitioning in its indoor walkways. Petitioners argue that because the mall is open to the public, it is a traditional public forum under the First Amendment. What is the best federal constitutional analysis?
Answer A: The mall is a traditional public forum because the public may enter
Answer B: The First Amendment generally does not require the private mall to allow petitioning absent state action or a specific state-law rule
Answer C: The mall must allow petitioning because political speech always overrides property rights
Answer D: The mall becomes government property because it resembles a town square
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal free-speech forum doctrine applies to government property and state action.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Public access to private property does not make it a federal public forum. C: Political speech is highly protected against government restriction, but not a general license to use private property. D: A shopping center is not performing a function traditionally and exclusively reserved to the state.
lb_rule_reference: The First Amendment generally constrains government actors; a privately owned shopping center is not a traditional public forum under the federal Constitution merely because the public is invited to shop.
