Question: In a federal negligence case, Leah testifies that she saw Abraham drive through a stop sign. On voir dire, Leah admits she was standing about half a block away and it was raining, but she says she was looking directly at the intersection. Abraham objects that Leah lacked personal knowledge. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude Leah's testimony because the rain and distance make her observation uncertain.
Answer B: Admit Leah's testimony if there is evidence sufficient to support a finding that she perceived the event; uncertainty goes to weight.
Answer C: Admit Leah's testimony only if another witness corroborates it.
Answer D: Exclude Leah's testimony because personal knowledge must be proved beyond dispute before the jury hears it.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court screens for evidence sufficient to support a finding that the witness has personal knowledge. Weaknesses in perception generally go to credibility and weight for the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and D demand too much at the admissibility stage. C is wrong because corroboration is not generally required for personal knowledge.
