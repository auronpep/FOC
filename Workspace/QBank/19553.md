Question: In a federal trial, Paul asks Timothy, “What did you see at the warehouse?” Timothy answers, “I saw Levi carry out the crates, and everyone in town knows Levi is a thief.” Levi's lawyer immediately says, “Move to strike the statement about what everyone in town knows.” What is the best preservation analysis?
Answer A: The motion can preserve the objection to the volunteered reputation statement because Levi moved to strike promptly and identified the challenged portion.
Answer B: The motion is ineffective because a party can object only before an answer is given.
Answer C: The motion is ineffective because Levi needed to make an offer of proof.
Answer D: The motion automatically strikes Timothy's entire answer, including what he saw at the warehouse.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When evidence has already come in through an answer, a timely motion to strike can preserve an objection. Levi targeted the volunteered reputation portion. The court need not strike the separate responsive testimony about what Timothy personally saw.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a motion to strike is the appropriate tool when the objectionable matter is revealed in the answer. C: C is wrong because an offer of proof is for excluded evidence, not admitted or volunteered evidence. D: D is wrong because the court may strike only the inadmissible or improper portion.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(1) / motion to strike after answer
