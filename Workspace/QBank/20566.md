Outline: Privileges and Immunities Clause of Article IV, Section 2
Question: A lawful permanent resident who is not a United States citizen lives in State A and applies for a professional license in State B. State B denies the license because the applicant is not a resident. The applicant brings only an Article IV Privileges and Immunities claim. What is the best response?
Answer A: Article IV applies because any nonresident person is a citizen of another state
Answer B: Article IV does not protect the applicant because the applicant is not a citizen of another state
Answer C: Article IV applies only to corporations
Answer D: Article IV automatically invalidates every residency rule
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim may need another doctrine, but Article IV is unavailable on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Article IV protects citizens of other states, not aliens as such. C: Article IV protects natural citizens, not corporations. D: Article IV has plaintiff and fundamental-privilege thresholds plus possible state justifications.
lb_rule_reference: Article IV Privileges and Immunities protects citizens of other states; aliens are not protected by Article IV as citizens, though other constitutional doctrines may apply.
