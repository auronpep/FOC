Question: Joshua owned land on the east side of a river, and Caleb owned land on the west side. The river was the boundary between their parcels. During one violent storm, the river suddenly cut a new channel across part of Joshua's land. What happens to the boundary between Joshua and Caleb under the usual avulsion rule?
Answer A: The boundary immediately moves to the new river channel because any river movement changes ownership.
Answer B: Caleb automatically owns all land west of the new channel, even though the shift was sudden.
Answer C: Joshua and Caleb become tenants in common of the land between the old and new channels.
Answer D: The boundary remains at the old channel because a sudden avulsive change generally does not move the legal boundary.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The boundary remains at the old channel. Avulsion is a sudden, perceptible change in a watercourse. Unlike gradual accretion or erosion, avulsion generally does not shift the legal boundary.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because only gradual changes generally move water boundaries. B: B is wrong because sudden avulsion does not transfer title merely because the water moved. C: C is wrong because avulsion does not create co-ownership between the riparian owners.
lb_rule_reference: Avulsion / sudden change in watercourse
