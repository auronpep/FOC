Question: Isaac purchased “field-ready irrigation pumps” from Miriam's farm-equipment company. The signed goods contract contained a merger clause and did not define “field-ready.” Isaac offered evidence that in four prior pump contracts between the parties, Miriam supplied intake screens with pumps described as field-ready. Miriam objected that the prior transactions were outside the final writing and that the general farm-equipment trade treats intake screens as separate accessories. Should the court admit Isaac's prior-transaction evidence to interpret the phrase “field-ready”?
Answer A: Yes, because under UCC meaning rules, a course of dealing may explain or supplement a final goods writing if reasonably consistent with the express terms.
Answer B: No, because the parol evidence rule bars every prior transaction once the contract has a merger clause.
Answer C: No, because general trade usage always controls over the parties' own prior course of dealing.
Answer D: No, because written purchase orders from earlier contracts are automatically excluded as prior writings.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that UCC goods contracts may be explained or supplemented by course of dealing even when the current writing is final, so long as the course is established and reasonably consistent with express terms. The trigger facts are the goods sale, the undefined technical phrase, and four prior contracts between the same parties. The decisive locks are source law and meaning source. Isaac's evidence is course-of-dealing evidence, not merely a barred prior writing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it applies PER absolutism to UCC course evidence. C: C is wrong because it reverses the hierarchy by making general trade usage automatically superior to private course of dealing. D: D is wrong because the fact that prior transactions were documented does not by itself bar their use as course-of-dealing evidence.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-202 / course of dealing
