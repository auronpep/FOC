Question: Noah conveyed a landlocked parcel to Esther. At the time of conveyance, the only access to a public road was over Noah's retained land, so Esther acquired an easement by necessity. Ten years later, the county built a public road that touched Esther's parcel and gave it legal access. Noah's successor now asks whether Esther may continue using the necessity easement over the retained land. What is the best result?
Answer A: Esther may continue forever because easements by necessity are permanent once created.
Answer B: Esther may continue only if the original deed expressly said the easement would end.
Answer C: Esther may continue because nonuse, not new access, is the only way to end an easement.
Answer D: The easement by necessity likely ends because the strict necessity that justified it no longer exists.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement by necessity lasts only as long as the necessity exists. Once a public road gives the formerly landlocked parcel legal access, the basis for the necessity easement disappears.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because necessity easements are tied to necessity and are not automatically perpetual. B: B is wrong because termination follows from the end of necessity, not only from express deed language. C: C is wrong because nonuse is not the only termination route. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Duration of easement by necessity
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating necessity like an ordinary express easement with no duration limit.
