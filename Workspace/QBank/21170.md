Question: David orally authorized Nathan to sign a deed conveying David's land to Caleb. Nathan signed David's name to a deed and delivered it to Caleb. Caleb paid fair value, had no reason to know that Nathan lacked written authority, and promptly recorded the deed. David had never signed a written authorization. Who owns the land?
Answer A: Caleb, because he paid value and recorded first.
Answer B: Caleb, because lack of written authority matters only if the grantee had notice of it.
Answer C: Caleb, because recording validates an otherwise defective deed against the grantor.
Answer D: David, because Nathan lacked written authority to execute the deed and recording/BFP status cannot cure that validity defect.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is an agent-executed deed, and the first decisive lock is validity, not recording priority. Nathan had only oral authority to sign David's deed. Caleb's value, lack of notice, and recording facts may matter after a valid instrument exists, but they cannot cure the missing written-authority gate. Title remains with David.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because BFP and first-recording facts do not matter until a valid conveyance exists. B: B is wrong because the authority defect is not merely a notice problem. C: C is wrong because recording gives notice and may affect priority, but it does not validate an invalidly executed deed.
lb_rule_reference: Authority validity before recording priority
