Question: Rachel orally agrees to buy 1,000 standard blue jars from Daniel for $4,000. Daniel has the same jars in ordinary inventory and regularly sells them to many buyers. After Rachel repudiates, Daniel sets aside 1,000 jars from inventory and places Rachel's name on the boxes. Rachel asserts the Statute of Frauds. Does Daniel satisfy the specially manufactured goods exception?
Answer A: Yes, because Daniel identified the goods to Rachel by placing her name on the boxes.
Answer B: Yes, because the contract involved more than $500 worth of goods.
Answer C: No, because the jars are standard inventory suitable for sale to others in Daniel's ordinary business.
Answer D: No, but only because Daniel set aside the jars after Rachel repudiated.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The specially manufactured goods exception requires goods made for the buyer and not suitable for sale to others in the seller's ordinary course. Standard inventory goods do not qualify merely because the seller sets them aside or labels boxes with the buyer's name.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because identification or labeling is not special manufacture. B: B is wrong because price over $500 triggers the writing requirement, not the exception.
