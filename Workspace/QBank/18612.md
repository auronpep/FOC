Question: Naomi held an easement across Ruth's land along an old path. Ruth asked Naomi whether Ruth could build a garage across the path. Naomi replied, “Build it; I will never use that path again.” Relying on Naomi's statement, Ruth spent substantial money building the garage. Years later, Naomi demanded that Ruth tear down the garage so Naomi could use the old path. What is Ruth's best defense?
Answer A: Naomi may be estopped from enforcing the easement route because Ruth substantially relied on Naomi's representation that she would not use it.
Answer B: Ruth has no defense because estoppel can never affect easement enforcement.
Answer C: The garage automatically gives Ruth ownership of Naomi's dominant estate.
Answer D: Naomi wins because only a recorded deed can ever affect any easement right, regardless of reliance.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement holder may be estopped from asserting an easement where the servient owner reasonably and substantially relies on the holder's representation or conduct. Ruth built an expensive garage after Naomi said she would never use the path again, making estoppel the strongest defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because estoppel can affect easement enforcement in reliance cases. C: C is wrong because reliance does not transfer ownership of the dominant estate. D: D is wrong because estoppel is distinct from formal release and may operate because of reliance.
lb_rule_reference: Termination or enforcement bar by estoppel
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is looking only for a formal release and ignoring reliance facts that support estoppel.
