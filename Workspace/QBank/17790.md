Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Res ipsa loquitur
Question: A hotel guest was walking on the sidewalk below the hotel when a heavy window sash fell from a guest room and struck her. The hotel controlled inspection and maintenance of the windows. No witness saw exactly why the sash fell. May the guest rely on res ipsa loquitur?
Answer A: Yes, because the accident supports an inference of negligence even without direct proof of the exact defect
Answer B: Yes, and the hotel is automatically liable once the doctrine applies
Answer C: No, because the guest must identify the precise screw or part that failed
Answer D: No, because falling objects are never evidence of negligence
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A properly maintained hotel window ordinarily should not fall, and maintenance was within the hotel's responsibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Res ipsa permits an inference; it does not compel liability in every case. C: The doctrine exists for cases where the exact mechanism is unavailable but the circumstances support negligence. D: Some falling-object cases are classic candidates for a negligence inference.
lb_rule_reference: Res ipsa loquitur permits an inference of negligence when the event ordinarily does not occur without negligence and the likely cause was within the defendant's responsibility.
