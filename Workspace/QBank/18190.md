MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Freedom of speech and expression
Question: A city requires groups using downtown streets for marches to obtain a permit. The ordinance sets a fixed $100 processing fee to cover traffic-control paperwork and requires the city to decide within five days under objective criteria. A protest group argues any permit fee for speech is unconstitutional. Is the permit system invalid on that basis alone?
Answer A: Yes, because permits for marches are always prior restraints and always invalid
Answer B: Yes, because the city may charge a higher fee when a hostile audience is expected
Answer C: No, because an objective permit system with a modest administrative fee and prompt deadline may be valid
Answer D: No, because city streets are nonpublic forums
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule manages competing street uses through objective standards rather than suppressing content.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Permit systems for managing streets can be valid if they use narrow, objective standards and prompt decisions. B: Fees cannot be increased because listeners may react badly to the message. D: Streets are traditional public forums, but content-neutral permit management may still be allowed.
lb_rule_reference: A parade permit system may use objective standards and charge fees tied to administrative costs, but it may not vest officials with unbridled discretion or vary fees based on anticipated hostility.
