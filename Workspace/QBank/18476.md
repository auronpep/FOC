Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Trespass to land
Question: A homeowner hired a crew to install a decorative fence. Relying on an old sketch, the homeowner directed the crew to place three posts two feet across the neighbor's boundary. The homeowner honestly believed the strip was his land. The posts caused no measurable damage. The neighbor sues the homeowner for trespass to land. Is the neighbor likely to prevail in a trespass to land claim against the homeowner?
Answer A: No, because the homeowner honestly believed he owned the strip
Answer B: Yes, because the homeowner intentionally caused posts to enter land that belonged to the neighbor
Answer C: No, because the neighbor cannot show actual damage to the land
Answer D: Yes, but only because the crew was negligent in reading the sketch
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The homeowner intentionally directed the placement of the posts. Because they physically invaded the neighbor's land, trespass is established despite the boundary mistake and lack of damage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Trespass requires intent to enter or place an object on the land, not intent to violate another's property rights. Mistake about the boundary is not a complete defense. C: Trespass to land is actionable upon intentional physical invasion, and actual damage is not required. D: The claim is trespass, and the controlling fact is intentional placement across the boundary. Negligence is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Trespass to land requires intentional entry or physical invasion of another's land; mistake about ownership or boundary does not defeat liability.
