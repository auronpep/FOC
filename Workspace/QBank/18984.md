Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Solomon signed and recorded a deed conveying Gardenacre to Abigail as a gift. When Abigail learned of the deed two days later, she immediately sent Solomon a signed letter stating, “I reject this deed and do not accept any title to Gardenacre.” Solomon argued that title had already vested because the deed was beneficial and recorded. Who owns Gardenacre?
Answer A: Solomon, because acceptance is required and Abigail's express rejection defeated the usual presumption of acceptance.
Answer B: Abigail, because acceptance of a beneficial deed is conclusive and cannot be rejected.
Answer C: Abigail, because recording substitutes for acceptance in every gift-deed case.
Answer D: Solomon only if Abigail rejected the deed before Solomon signed it.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive acceptance lock is whether the grantee accepted the conveyance. A beneficial deed ordinarily carries a presumption of acceptance, but Abigail expressly rejected the deed once she learned of it. That rejection defeated acceptance, so title did not vest in Abigail.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because presumed acceptance is not conclusive when the grantee expressly rejects. C: C is wrong because recording does not eliminate the acceptance requirement. D: D is wrong because rejection after learning of the deed can defeat presumed acceptance.
lb_rule_reference: Acceptance is presumed for a beneficial deed but can be defeated by express rejection
