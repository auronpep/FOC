Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: In a federal assault trial, Hannah is called to testify that she saw Peter swing a bottle at John. On voir dire, Hannah says she was across the street, wearing her glasses, and could see the two men clearly under a streetlight. Peter objects that Hannah was too far away to be certain. How should the court treat the objection?
Answer A: Sustain it because Hannah must be certain before she may testify.
Answer B: Overrule it if Hannah's testimony supports a finding that she perceived the event; distance affects weight.
Answer C: Sustain it unless John corroborates Hannah's testimony.
Answer D: Overrule it only if the judge personally believes Hannah's identification is accurate.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness may testify if evidence supports a finding that she has personal knowledge. Certainty is not required for admissibility. Weaknesses in distance or perception generally affect weight for the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because personal knowledge does not require certainty. C: C is wrong because corroboration is not required to establish personal knowledge. D: D is wrong because the judge screens for sufficient foundation; the jury evaluates accuracy.
