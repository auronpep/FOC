Question: Rachel planned to close her store and move to another town. Unknown to Rachel, Naomi contracted with Jacob to buy Jacob's building, and Jacob promised Naomi that he would rent the building to Rachel at a reduced rate. Before Rachel learned of the contract, she closed her store for unrelated reasons. Naomi and Jacob then deleted the reduced-rent clause. Rachel later learned of the original promise and sued Jacob. Is Rachel's store closing enough to vest her rights?
Answer A: Yes, because Rachel materially changed position before the deletion.
Answer B: Yes, because any major life decision by a beneficiary vests rights.
Answer C: No, because Rachel's change of position was not in reliance on the promise she did not know about.
Answer D: No, because no future tenant can ever be an intended beneficiary.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance vests beneficiary rights only when the beneficiary changes position in reasonable reliance on the promised performance. Rachel closed her store before she knew of the reduced-rent promise, so her action was not reliance on that promise.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because material change alone is not enough; it must be tied to the promise. B: B is wrong because vesting requires a legally relevant reliance, assent, suit, or similar vesting event. D: D is wrong because a future tenant may be an intended beneficiary if the contract shows direct intent.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — reliance must be on the promise
