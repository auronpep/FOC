Question: Moses conveyed Orchardacre to Miriam by special warranty deed, warranting title against claims arising by, through, or under Moses. While Moses owned Orchardacre, he had granted Aaron a valid access easement across it. After the deed to Miriam was delivered, Aaron enforced the easement. Which statement best states the deed-type consequence?
Answer A: The easement is void because a special warranty deed conveys land free of all interests not repeated in the deed.
Answer B: Miriam has no possible warranty claim because special warranty deeds never cover easements.
Answer C: The special warranty may apply because Aaron's easement arose through Moses while Moses owned Orchardacre, although the deed type does not itself extinguish Aaron's easement.
Answer D: Miriam owns nothing because the existence of the easement prevented any title from passing.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a special warranty deed, and the decisive deed-type lock is whether the claim arose by, through, or under the grantor. Aaron's easement came from Moses during Moses's ownership. That can place the claim within the special warranty, but the warranty route creates covenant consequences; it does not automatically erase the easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because warranty language does not invalidate a valid easement. B: B is wrong because an easement can be the kind of claim covered if it arose through the grantor. D: D is wrong because a burdened title can still pass.
lb_rule_reference: Special warranty covers claims arising by, through, or under the grantor but does not erase the interest
