Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: In a federal breach-of-contract trial, Abigail claims that Boaz failed to deliver 200 crates of oranges. Boaz admits he did not deliver the oranges but argues that Abigail waived timely delivery. Abigail offers testimony that Boaz's favorite food is fish. Boaz objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony because any fact about Boaz helps the jury evaluate him.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because Boaz's favorite food does not make waiver, delivery, breach, or damages more or less probable.
Answer C: Admit the testimony because relevance requires only that the evidence be interesting background.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony only if Boaz proves that he does not like fish.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Relevance requires a tendency to prove a fact of consequence. Boaz's favorite food does not affect any issue in the delivery dispute, including waiver, breach, or damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because facts about a party are not automatically consequential. C: C is wrong because background evidence must still aid a consequential issue. D: D is wrong because the factual truth of the fish preference is not the legal problem.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / no fact of consequence
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the fact-of-consequence requirement and rejects mere personal-detail evidence.
