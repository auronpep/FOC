Outline: Offers of proof
Question: Martha sues John for breach of contract. Martha calls Mark and asks, “Did you personally see John sign the March 3 contract at your office?” John objects that Mark lacks foundation. During argument, the court and both lawyers discuss on the record that Mark would answer yes and would identify the signing. The court still excludes the testimony. Martha makes no additional formal offer of proof. Is the substance of the excluded evidence sufficiently preserved?
Answer A: Yes, because the substance of Mark's testimony was apparent from the question and the on-record discussion.
Answer B: Yes, but only because every excluded witness is presumed to support the proponent.
Answer C: No, because an offer of proof must always use a sworn question-and-answer format.
Answer D: No, because only documentary evidence can be preserved without a formal offer.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: An offer of proof is unnecessary when the substance of the excluded evidence is apparent from the context. Here, the question and the on-record discussion made clear that Mark would testify he saw John sign the contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because there is no presumption that excluded testimony favors the proponent. C: C is wrong because the court may direct question-and-answer form, but that form is not always required. D: D is wrong because the context exception applies to excluded evidence generally, not just documents.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(2) / offer of proof after exclusion
