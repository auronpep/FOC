Subtopic: Remedies
Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Hannah contracted with John to provide a specialized data-processing module for Hannah's new subscription site. John repudiated before delivery. Hannah's expected subscription profits cannot be proven with reasonable certainty. In preparation after the contract, Hannah reasonably spent $21,000 adapting her database and $4,000 testing compatibility. John received no money or property from Hannah. What may Hannah recover as reliance damages?
Answer A: $25,000, because those were reasonable expenditures made in reliance on John's promised performance.
Answer B: Nothing, because John received no benefit from Hannah.
Answer C: Hannah's projected subscription profits, because John knew the module would be used in the site.
Answer D: $21,000 only, because compatibility testing was not paid to John.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is reliance under common law. Reliance protects reasonable expenditures made because of the contract; it does not require that the breaching party received a benefit. The formula is $21,000 database adaptation + $4,000 compatibility testing = $25,000. The limiting gates are certainty and causation, and the result scope is limited. The answer does not decide restitution because restitution would require a benefit conferred on John.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it swaps restitution for reliance, a WAL-REM-006 label error. C: C is wrong because projected subscription profits fail the certainty gate. D: D is wrong because reliance expenditures need not be payments to the breaching party if they were reasonably caused by the contract.
lb_rule_reference: Common law reliance damages — plaintiff's reasonable expenditures, not defendant's benefit
