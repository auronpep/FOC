Question: Solomon granted Hannah an easement over Roadacre “for as long as Hannah operates a plant nursery on Gardenacre.” Hannah operated a nursery for several years, then permanently closed it and converted Gardenacre into an accounting office. Hannah continued to use Roadacre for customer access to the accounting office. May Hannah continue using the easement?
Answer A: No, because the easement ended by its express terms when Hannah stopped operating the nursery.
Answer B: No, because any change in use of the dominant estate automatically transfers the easement to the servient owner.
Answer C: Yes, because an easement appurtenant always lasts forever regardless of its stated terms.
Answer D: Yes, because the accounting office also benefits Gardenacre.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Hannah may not continue using the easement. The grant expressly limited the easement to the period during which Hannah operated a plant nursery on Gardenacre. Once that use permanently ended, the easement terminated by its own terms.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because termination of the easement does not transfer a new property right to the servient owner; it simply removes the burden. C: C is wrong because easements can be limited by express terms. D: D is wrong because the relevant question is the stated limitation, not whether the new use also benefits the dominant estate.
lb_rule_reference: Easement limited by express duration or purpose
