Question: Esther signed a deed conveying her garden to Ruth. Esther then orally told Luke, “Take this signed deed to Ruth today; I want her to own the garden now.” Luke delivered the deed to Ruth, and Ruth accepted. Esther later argued that Luke needed written authority to deliver the deed. Who owns the garden?
Answer A: Esther, because any agent involved in a land transfer must have written authority.
Answer B: Ruth, because Esther herself signed the deed and Luke merely delivered the owner-executed instrument with Esther's present intent.
Answer C: Esther, because Luke did not sign the deed as an agent.
Answer D: Ruth only if Luke had a written power of attorney from Esther.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is an owner-signed deed, and the decisive locks are grantor execution and delivery intent. Esther personally signed the deed, then used Luke as a messenger to deliver the signed instrument with present transfer intent. The written-authority rule for an agent executing a deed was not triggered, so title passed to Ruth upon delivery and acceptance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every helper in a land transfer must have written authority; the deed was executed by Esther. C: C is wrong because Luke did not need to sign the deed to deliver it as a messenger. D: D is wrong because the power-of-attorney requirement applies to an agent signing the deed, not to this owner-signed delivery.
