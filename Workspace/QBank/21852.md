Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Kai and Lila agree to buy what they believe are stolen passports and resell them. The passports are fake documents created by police for a sting and were never stolen. Kai and Lila are charged with conspiracy to receive and resell stolen property. Is the fact that the passports were not actually stolen a defense?
Answer A: Yes, because the target property was not truly stolen
Answer B: No, because they agreed to commit conduct they believed would be receiving and reselling stolen property
Answer C: Yes, because conspiracy requires the completed target offense
Answer D: No, but only because passports are government documents
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The agreement had a criminal objective under the facts the conspirators believed. The police-created nature of the passports is factual impossibility, not a defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Factual impossibility generally does not defeat conspiracy. If the facts were as Kai and Lila believed, their agreed conduct would be criminal. C: Conspiracy does not require completion of the target offense. D: The same impossibility principle would apply to other property if the conspirators believed the criminal facts existed.
lb_rule_reference: Factual impossibility is generally not a defense to conspiracy when the conspirators agree to commit conduct that would be criminal if the facts were as they believed.
