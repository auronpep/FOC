Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Miriam hired Aaron to build cabinets. The parties signed a final written contract for basic cabinets. Two weeks after signing, Miriam orally asked Aaron to add glass fronts, and Aaron agreed. When Aaron later seeks payment for the glass fronts, Miriam argues that the parol evidence rule bars evidence of the later oral agreement because the signed writing was final. How should the court analyze the objection?
Answer A: The parol evidence rule bars all oral agreements related to a signed contract, whether made before or after signing.
Answer B: The parol evidence rule does not bar the evidence merely because the later agreement was oral; the issue is modification or waiver, not prior/contemporaneous parol evidence.
Answer C: The parol evidence rule admits the evidence only if the written contract was for goods.
Answer D: The later oral agreement automatically replaces the entire written contract.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The parol evidence rule addresses prior or contemporaneous agreements offered to vary or supplement a final writing. An agreement made after signing is a later modification or waiver issue, not a PER issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores timing. C: C is wrong because the timing distinction applies outside Article 2 as well. D: D is wrong because later oral agreement evidence may matter, but it does not automatically replace the entire contract.
lb_rule_reference: PER timing gate / subsequent modification not PER
lb_student_diagnostic: Student uses PER as a universal bar against all oral evidence.
