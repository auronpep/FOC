Question: In a federal employment trial, Esther's lawyer asks Daniel, “What happened during the meeting with Leah?” Daniel describes the meeting in detail, including Leah's statements, Daniel's responses, and who left the room first. Leah moves to strike the answer as nonresponsive because Daniel gave more than one fact. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant the motion because a witness may state only one fact in response to any question.
Answer B: Grant the motion because all meeting testimony must be presented through minutes.
Answer C: Grant the motion unless Daniel's answer is favorable to Esther.
Answer D: Deny the motion because the broad question invited a narrative description of the meeting, though the court may require narrower questions going forward.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The question asked what happened during the meeting, inviting a narrative. The court can control the mode of questioning, but the answer is not nonresponsive merely because it contains several details.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A invents a one-fact rule. B is an original-writing false positive; a witness may testify from personal knowledge about a meeting.
