Outline: Personal knowledge
Question: In a federal extortion trial, Abigail testifies that she answered a telephone call and recognized the caller's voice as Eli's because she had spoken with Eli many times over several years. The call was recorded, but the recording was accidentally erased before trial. Eli objects that Abigail cannot identify the voice without the recording. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a voice heard over a telephone can be identified only by producing the recording.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because Abigail heard the voice through a telephone rather than in person.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if an expert first compares Eli's voice with a known sample.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because Abigail's prior familiarity with Eli's voice gives her personal knowledge to identify the caller.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness may identify a voice based on hearing the voice at any time under circumstances connecting it with the speaker. Abigail's prior familiarity with Eli's voice supplies a personal basis. The missing recording may matter for other issues, but it does not bar her testimony from personal perception.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the recording is not required when the witness testifies from her own auditory perception. B: B is wrong because voice identification may be based on a voice heard by telephone. C: C is wrong because expert comparison is not required for a familiar voice identification.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 and 901 / voice identification by perception
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests voice-identification foundation and rejects the recording/expert-only false positive.
