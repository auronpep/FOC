Outline: Bias
Question: In a federal fraud trial, the defendant cross-examines John for twenty minutes about John's plea agreement, expected sentence reduction, payments for relocation, and meetings with the prosecutor. The judge allows those questions. When defense counsel begins asking the same questions again in slightly different words, the judge stops the repetition. The defendant objects that bias cross-examination can never be limited. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a party has an unlimited right to repeat bias questions until satisfied.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because stopping repetitive bias questions violates the hearsay rule.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if John has admitted that he lied on direct examination.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because the court may reasonably control cumulative or repetitive examination after allowing meaningful inquiry into bias.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Bias is important impeachment, but the court retains reasonable control over the mode and order of examining witnesses and may avoid wasting time, harassment, or cumulative questioning. After allowing meaningful inquiry into John's bias, the judge may stop repetitive questioning.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because bias cross is important but not unlimited. B: B is wrong because the issue is examination control, not hearsay. C: C is wrong because the court's power to limit cumulative questioning does not depend on an admission of lying.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611 and 403 / court may reasonably limit cumulative bias proof
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the boundary between meaningful bias inquiry and the court's control over cumulative examination.
