Question: Naomi hired Daniel to design a courtyard. The parties signed a detailed contract stating the design fee, drawings to be delivered, deadline, and payment schedule. The contract also said, “This writing is the complete and exclusive statement of the parties' agreement.” Naomi later offers evidence that, before signing, Daniel orally promised to include a fountain design at no additional charge. The writing is silent about fountains. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the oral promise because it does not contradict any express written term.
Answer B: Admit the oral promise because merger clauses affect only sales of goods.
Answer C: Exclude the oral promise because the writing was intended as a complete and exclusive statement of the agreement.
Answer D: Exclude the written contract because it omitted the fountain term.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A complete and exclusive integration bars prior or contemporaneous consistent additional terms as well as contradictory terms. The merger clause is strong evidence that the writing was intended to be complete and exclusive.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a consistent additional term may be barred by a complete integration. B: B is wrong because common-law contracts can have complete integrations and merger clauses. D: D is wrong because omission of the oral term does not void the writing.
lb_rule_reference: Complete integration / merger clause / consistent additional term barred
lb_student_diagnostic: Student sees consistency and forgets integration level.
