Question: In a federal civil trial against a charity, Miriam testifies for the charity. On cross-examination, the plaintiff asks Miriam whether she is a paid officer of the same religious organization that controls the charity. The charity objects under the rule barring religious-belief credibility evidence. The plaintiff offers the question to show Miriam's bias, not to show that her beliefs make her truthful or untruthful. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because any reference to religious affiliation is inadmissible.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because bias may be proved only by reputation evidence.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the question is offered to show bias from organizational affiliation, not to attack or support credibility through religious belief.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Miriam's character for truthfulness has first been attacked.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 610 bars using religious beliefs or opinions to attack or support credibility, but it does not bar evidence of affiliation when offered for another proper credibility purpose such as bias. The question targets Miriam's relationship to a party-controlling organization, not her religious belief as a truthfulness trait.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 610 is not an absolute ban on all religious-affiliation evidence. B: B is wrong because bias is not limited to reputation evidence. D: D is wrong because bias impeachment does not depend on a prior attack on character for truthfulness.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(a) and FRE 610 / affiliation for bias not character for truthfulness
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the boundary between opinion/reputation credibility proof and bias-purpose evidence.
