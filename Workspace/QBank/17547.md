Question: Esther hired a caterer for a banquet under a signed agreement that listed the menu and stated that the writing was complete. Two weeks after signing, Esther orally asked the caterer to add a vegan dessert station for an additional charge. The caterer hired extra staff, bought the ingredients, and served the desserts at the banquet. Esther later refused to pay the added charge. Which argument is least likely to be accepted?
Answer A: The parol evidence rule bars the caterer from proving the dessert-station request because the signed menu was complete.
Answer B: The dessert-station request occurred after signing, so it is analyzed as a later modification or waiver issue rather than as parol evidence.
Answer C: The added service and the caterer's performance support treating the request as a new or modified obligation.
Answer D: Esther's acceptance of the desserts at the banquet supports the caterer's argument that the added service became part of the parties' deal.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The least likely argument is the parol-evidence argument. PER bars certain prior or contemporaneous terms offered against a final writing; it does not bar proof of a later request for added service. The trigger facts are the post-signing timing, extra dessert station, extra staff and ingredients, and Esther's acceptance of the service. The decisive lock is timing. The issue is whether the later change became part of the parties' deal, not whether an earlier oral term varies the complete menu.
