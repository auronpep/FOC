


BARMATRIX Q#: 21353
Outline_code: 92080205
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Remittitur/additur
Question: After a jury trial in federal district&#10;court based on diversity jurisdiction, the&#10;court believed the jury's compensatory&#10;damage award met the state standard for excessiveness. Consequently, the court offered the plaintiff the choice of either accepting a lower award than that&#10;given by the jury or submitting to a new&#10;trial.&#10;&#10;May the court do so?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the court believed the award was excessive under the state standard.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the court could simply lower the jury's award if it chose to do so.
Answer C: C: No, because there was no indication the jury's award was so excessive as to “shock the conscience.”
Answer D: D: No, because forcing the plaintiff to accept a potentially lower award violates the Seventh Amendment.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When a court believes a jury award is excessive, remittitur allows a court to offer a plaintiff the choice of a reduced award or a new trial. If the suit is in federal court based on diversity jurisdiction, the court can look at whether the award meets the state standard for excessiveness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect because a court cannot simply reduce a jury award without giving a plaintiff the option of a new trial. See Hetzel v. Prince William County, 523 U.S. 208 (1998). C is incorrect because the award does not necessarily have to “shock the conscience.” If the federal court is sitting in diversity, the court can look to the state standard. D is incorrect because remittitur does not violate the Seventh Amendment (however, additur, or offering a defendant the choice between a higher award or a new trial when damages are inadequate, does).
