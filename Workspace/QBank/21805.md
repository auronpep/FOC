Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: A witness to a hit-and-run says the fleeing car was blue and had a cracked left taillight. The next day, police found a blue car owned by Isaac with a cracked left taillight. Isaac objects that many blue cars have cracked taillights and that the evidence does not prove he was the driver. Assuming proper authentication of the car, how should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence, because the matching features are not unique.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence unless the witness can identify Isaac as the driver.
Answer C: Admit the evidence only if police prove that no other blue car in the city had a cracked taillight.
Answer D: Admit the evidence, because the matching features have some tendency to connect Isaac's car to the hit-and-run; the weakness goes to weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The matching description makes it somewhat more probable that Isaac's car was involved. The match need not be unique or independently prove driver identity to be relevant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevance does not require uniqueness. B: B is wrong because object or vehicle connection can be relevant even if the driver is disputed. C: C is wrong because eliminating all other possible cars is not required for threshold relevance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is demanding a perfect match instead of a logical link.
