Outline: Attorney-client
Question: Jacob met with his lawyer, Mary, to discuss a subpoena. Mary's paralegal, Peter, attended the meeting to organize documents and take notes for Mary. No outsider was present. Does Peter's presence defeat attorney-client privilege?
Answer A: Yes, because only the lawyer and client may be present.
Answer B: Yes, unless Peter later becomes a witness for Jacob.
Answer C: No, but only if Peter personally gives legal advice.
Answer D: No, because Peter was acting as the lawyer's representative in facilitating the legal consultation.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Privilege generally extends to communications made through or in the presence of agents who assist the lawyer in providing legal services, such as a paralegal. Peter's presence did not destroy confidentiality. The decisive axis is lawyer-agent assistance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This overstates the third-party rule and ignores lawyer representatives. B: B is wrong because This misroutes privilege to Peter's later witness status. C: C is wrong because Peter need not personally give legal advice; he was assisting the lawyer's legal work.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / lawyer representative presence
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating all nonlawyers alike instead of separating necessary legal-service agents from outsiders.
