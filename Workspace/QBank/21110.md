Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: In a federal civil trial, Ruth offers a relevant repair invoice that she forgot to list in a pretrial exhibit summary. Boaz objects under Rule 403 only, arguing that he is surprised and unprepared to respond. The invoice is not inflammatory, confusing, misleading, cumulative, or time-consuming. Under Rule 403, what is the best answer?
Answer A: Exclude the invoice because surprise is an enumerated Rule 403 ground.
Answer B: Exclude the invoice because all late-disclosed evidence is unfairly prejudicial.
Answer C: Do not exclude the invoice under Rule 403 on surprise alone; another remedy may be available under procedural rules.
Answer D: Admit the invoice only if Ruth proves that her failure to disclose was accidental.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 403 lists unfair prejudice, confusion, misleading the jury, undue delay, wasting time, and cumulative proof. Surprise alone is not the standard Rule 403 counterweight. Procedural remedies such as continuance or sanctions may be considered separately, but the objection as framed does not satisfy Rule 403.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because surprise is not an enumerated Rule 403 counterweight. B: B is wrong because late disclosure is not automatically unfair prejudice under Rule 403. D: D is wrong because accidental nondisclosure is not the Rule 403 admissibility test.
