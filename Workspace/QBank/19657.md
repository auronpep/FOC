Question: A city rezones a neighborhood to block a community center. The ordinance is facially neutral, but meeting transcripts show council members repeatedly said they wanted to keep a racial minority group out, and the city departed from its usual zoning process. What is the strongest Equal Protection argument?
Answer A: The law is immune from heightened scrutiny because it does not classify by race on its face
Answer B: The evidence of discriminatory purpose may trigger strict scrutiny despite facial neutrality
Answer C: Disparate impact alone always triggers strict scrutiny
Answer D: Rational basis applies because zoning is local economic regulation
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Official statements and procedural departures support a finding of discriminatory purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Discriminatory purpose can be shown through context and official statements. C: Purpose, not impact alone, is required for heightened scrutiny of a facially neutral law. D: Discriminatory racial purpose changes the Equal Protection tier.
lb_rule_reference: A facially neutral law can trigger heightened scrutiny if discriminatory purpose is shown through evidence such as official statements, sequence of events, procedural departures, or historical background.
