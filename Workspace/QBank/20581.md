Question: A defendant intentionally deletes relevant text messages after receiving a litigation hold, telling a manager that the messages would "look bad in court." The messages cannot be restored or replaced. The plaintiff asks for an adverse-inference instruction. What fact most strongly supports the requested severe sanction?
Answer A: The deleted material was electronic rather than paper
Answer B: The deletion happened after a duty to preserve arose and was intended to deprive the plaintiff of the messages in litigation
Answer C: The plaintiff would prefer to have the messages
Answer D: The defendant has more resources than the plaintiff
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Intent to deprive is the key gate for severe Rule 37(e) measures such as an adverse inference.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: ESI status alone does not justify an adverse inference. C: Preference for evidence does not establish intent to deprive. D: Resources are not the Rule 37(e) trigger for severe sanctions.
lb_rule_reference: For lost ESI, adverse-inference, presumption, dismissal, or default sanctions require a finding that the party acted with intent to deprive another party of the information's use in the litigation.
