Outline: Liability insurance
Question: After a collision, an insurance investigator recorded a substantially verbatim statement from Hannah, a pedestrian witness. The recording was made after litigation was anticipated. Months later, Hannah asks for a copy of her own recorded statement about the collision. The defendant refuses, arguing that the statement is protected work product and that Hannah has not shown substantial need or undue hardship. Must Hannah make the ordinary substantial-need and undue-hardship showing to obtain her own previous statement?
Answer A: Yes. Any statement recorded after litigation is anticipated is work product and can be obtained only on substantial need and undue hardship.
Answer B: No. A person may obtain that person's own previous statement about the action or its subject matter without the ordinary required showing.
Answer C: Yes, because the previous-statement rule applies only to parties and Hannah is a nonparty witness.
Answer D: No, because work-product protection never applies to statements taken by an insurance investigator.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A recorded witness statement taken in anticipation of litigation may be trial-preparation material, but Rule 26(b)(3)(C) creates a specific route for a person to obtain that person's own previous statement about the action or subject matter without the ordinary substantial-need showing. Hannah is requesting her own substantially verbatim recorded statement. The decisive axis is the previous-statement carveout.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it states the ordinary work-product rule but ignores the specific previous-statement access provision. C: C is wrong because the rule is not limited to parties. It covers any party or other person requesting the person's own previous statement. D: D is wrong because statements gathered by an insurer or representative can be work product when prepared in anticipation of litigation. The reason Hannah can obtain the statement is the specific carveout, not absence of protection.
lb_rule_reference: FRCP 26(b)(3)(C) previous statement route
lb_student_diagnostic: Student knows ordinary work-product protection but misses the previous-statement exception built into the discovery rule.
