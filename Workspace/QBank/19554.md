Question: A cashier is authorized to receive customer payments and place the money in the store register. During a shift, the cashier removes $200 from the register and puts it in his pocket intending to keep it. What is the best common-law classification?
Answer A: Larceny, because the cashier had custody of the cash while the store retained possession
Answer B: Embezzlement, because all employee theft from an employer is embezzlement
Answer C: False pretenses, because customers paid the cashier voluntarily
Answer D: No theft offense, because the cashier was allowed to touch the money
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A cashier handling register funds for the store often has custody rather than independent possession.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Employee status does not automatically mean lawful possession. C: The customers intended to pay the store, not transfer title to the cashier personally by deception. D: Permission to handle register money does not authorize personal appropriation.
lb_rule_reference: An employee who merely has custody of employer cash and secretly pockets it can commit larceny because possession remains with the employer.
