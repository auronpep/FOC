Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A manufacturer sold a press with an interlocked safety guard that stopped the machine when lifted. The buyer's employer later rewired the press to bypass the interlock so production could continue with the guard open. The bypass caused a worker's hand injury. The original interlock would have prevented the accident. What is the manufacturer's strongest defense?
Answer A: The employer's substantial post-sale modification caused the injury
Answer B: Post-sale changes never matter in strict products liability
Answer C: The manufacturer is liable because all workplace injuries involving products are design defects
Answer D: The manufacturer is liable unless it inspected the press at the employer's plant every month
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The product left the manufacturer with a functioning safety guard. The later bypass defeated the safety system and caused the harm.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Product condition when it left defendant and later substantial modifications are central causation issues. C: The facts show a safety feature that would have worked absent later alteration. D: The strict-products defense turns on substantial modification and causation, not a monthly inspection duty.
lb_rule_reference: A substantial unforeseeable post-sale modification that causes the injury can defeat products-liability causation.
