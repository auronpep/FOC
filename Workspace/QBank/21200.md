Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A nurse finished her shift, clocked out, and drove her own car home. She was not on call, was not transporting supplies, and was free to choose her route. Ten minutes later, she negligently hit a cyclist. Is the hospital likely vicariously liable under respondeat superior?
Answer A: Yes, because the nurse was employed by the hospital that day
Answer B: Yes, because all travel to and from work benefits the employer
Answer C: No, because she was engaged in an ordinary commute outside assigned work duties
Answer D: No, because employers are never liable for employee negligence
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: She had clocked out and was not performing a work mission.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Employment status alone is not enough. B: Ordinary commuting is usually outside the scope of employment. D: Employers can be vicariously liable for negligence within scope.
lb_rule_reference: Ordinary commuting to or from work is usually outside the scope of employment for respondeat superior.
