Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Miriam hired Caleb to install handmade blue tiles in a small entryway for $9,000. Caleb instead installed white tiles of equal quality. The tile color was expressly chosen to match a mural in the entryway. Replacing the tiles with the promised blue tiles will cost $5,000 and will not require disturbing other work. The market value of the house is the same either way. What is Miriam's proper expectation recovery?
Answer A: Nothing, because the house has the same market value.
Answer B: $5,000, because the reasonable cost to provide the promised aesthetic feature is the appropriate measure.
Answer C: $9,000, because any deviation from an express specification forfeits the entire price.
Answer D: The reasonable value of the white tiles, because Caleb conferred a benefit.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Expectation damages protect the promised performance. Where an express aesthetic specification can be corrected at a reasonable cost without economic waste, the owner may recover the cost to obtain that promised feature even if market value is unchanged.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because market value is not the sole measure when correction is reasonable and the term was expressly bargained for. C: C is wrong because the defect does not justify refund of the entire contract price. D: D is wrong because reasonable value of the substitute tiles is a restitution idea, not the owner's expectation measure.
lb_rule_reference: Common law expectation damages; reasonable repair cost
