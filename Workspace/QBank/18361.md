Question: After a warehouse fire, the defendant's lawyer sent an investigator to interview the only night guard, who was critically ill and died before suit was filed. The investigator prepared a signed factual statement from the guard. The plaintiff seeks the statement and shows there is no other way to obtain the guard's account. Is the statement discoverable?
Answer A: No, because all materials prepared by a lawyer's investigator are absolutely protected
Answer B: Yes, because the plaintiff has shown substantial need and inability to obtain the substantial equivalent without undue hardship
Answer C: Yes, because any relevant statement by a witness must be produced automatically
Answer D: No, unless the defendant plans to call the investigator as a testifying expert
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A factual witness statement prepared in anticipation of litigation is ordinary work product, and the deceased witness creates the required hardship.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ordinary fact work product can be discovered on the required showing. C: Relevance alone does not overcome work-product protection. D: The discoverability of ordinary work product does not depend on whether the investigator testifies as an expert.
lb_rule_reference: Documents prepared in anticipation of litigation are work product, but ordinary fact work product may be discovered on substantial need and inability to obtain the substantial equivalent without undue hardship.
