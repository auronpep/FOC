Outline: Joinder of parties
Question: Two customers sue the same grocery chain in one federal action. One customer slipped in a Texas store in March. The other was injured by a falling shelf in an Ohio store in September. The incidents involved different employees, different locations, and different maintenance records. Is Rule 20 permissive joinder proper merely because both claims are against the same chain?
Answer A: Yes, because any plaintiffs suing the same defendant may always join
Answer B: Yes, because all negligence claims share a common legal label
Answer C: No, because the claims do not arise from the same transaction, occurrence, or series
Answer D: No, because Rule 20 requires all plaintiffs to be citizens of the same state
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Different accidents in different stores months apart do not satisfy Rule 20's transactional relatedness requirement merely because the same corporate defendant is named.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 20 also requires the same transaction, occurrence, or series. B: A common legal label is not enough when the operative facts are unrelated. D: Rule 20 has no same-citizenship requirement.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 20 requires both transactional relatedness and at least one common question; separate unrelated events against the same defendant usually fail the transaction requirement.
