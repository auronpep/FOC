Question: Milo starts an argument by shoving Perry in the chest. Perry immediately pulls a knife and lunges at Milo's abdomen. Milo grabs a metal stool and swings once at Perry's arm, knocking the knife away and breaking Perry's wrist. Milo is charged with battery. Which statement best describes Milo's self-defense claim?
Answer A: Milo has no defense because he was the initial aggressor
Answer B: Milo has no defense because he did not verbally withdraw before using the stool
Answer C: Milo may claim self-defense because Perry escalated a nondeadly shove into a deadly knife attack
Answer D: Milo may claim self-defense only if Perry actually intended to kill him
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Milo began as the aggressor by shoving Perry, but that was nondeadly force. Perry escalated the conflict to deadly force by lunging with a knife. Milo's single swing at Perry's arm was directed at stopping the deadly attack and was proportionate. Under the escalation exception, self-defense is available.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The rule is not absolute. A nondeadly aggressor may defend when the other party escalates to deadly force. Perry's knife lunge changed the force level. B: Withdrawal is one route. Another route is the victim's escalation from nondeadly to deadly force. Milo had no time to withdraw before Perry lunged with a knife. D: The issue is whether Milo reasonably believed he faced imminent death or serious bodily harm, not whether Perry subjectively intended murder.
lb_rule_reference: An initial aggressor generally loses self-defense, but may regain it if the victim escalates a nondeadly fight to deadly force and the aggressor responds proportionately
