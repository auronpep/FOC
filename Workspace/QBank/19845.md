Outline: Fee simple absolute
Question: Solomon contracted to sell Hillacre to Mary by a deed purporting to convey “fee simple absolute.” Before closing, Mary discovered that Solomon's own recorded deed conveyed Hillacre “to Solomon so long as the land is used only for residential purposes.” The land is currently used as a residence. What estate can Solomon convey to Mary?
Answer A: Fee simple determinable, subject to the original grantor's possibility of reverter.
Answer B: Fee simple absolute, because the residential-use limitation has not been violated.
Answer C: Fee simple subject to condition subsequent, because the original grantor must reenter.
Answer D: A life estate, because residential use is tied to personal occupancy.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Solomon owns a fee simple determinable because his deed uses durational language: “so long as.” He can convey that estate to Mary, but he cannot convey greater title than he owns. Current compliance means the estate has not ended, but it remains defeasible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because compliance with the limitation does not make the estate absolute. C: C is wrong because the language creates automatic termination, not a right of entry. D: D is wrong because a use restriction is not a life estate.
lb_rule_reference: Defeasible estate / seller cannot convey greater title
