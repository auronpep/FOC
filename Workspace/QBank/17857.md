Question: A software vendor expected a retailer to renew an annual subscription, but no renewal contract had been signed. A competing vendor truthfully offered a better price and won the retailer's business. The first vendor sues for interference with prospective advantage. Is the first vendor likely to prevail?
Answer A: Yes, because any lost expected deal is tortious interference
Answer B: Yes, because competition privilege applies only to existing contracts
Answer C: No, because truthful price competition without improper means is generally privileged
Answer D: No, because business expectancies are never protected
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts show lawful competition, not wrongful interference.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Prospective advantage requires more than losing to lawful competition. B: The privilege is especially relevant to prospective business. D: They can be protected when interference is improper.
lb_rule_reference: A competitor generally may seek prospective business through lawful means, and liability usually requires improper means or interference with an existing contract.
