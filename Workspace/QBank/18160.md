Outline: Presumptions and inferences
Question: John is charged with knowingly passing a counterfeit coin. The evidence shows that John passed three similar counterfeit coins in different shops on the same morning. The prosecutor asks for an instruction that the jury may consider the other passes in deciding whether John knew the coin in the charged transaction was counterfeit. John objects that the instruction should not be given unless the jury is told it must find knowledge if it believes the other passes occurred. What should the court do?
Answer A: Give the must-find instruction because repeated conduct conclusively proves knowledge.
Answer B: Give only a permissive instruction if the evidence supports the inference; the jury may but need not infer knowledge.
Answer C: Refuse any instruction because John's objection should have been made as a motion to strike a nonresponsive answer.
Answer D: Give the must-find instruction in a civil case but not a criminal case, regardless of the facts.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may permit the jury to draw a rational inference from repeated similar conduct, but it cannot require the jury to find knowledge. The prosecution retains the burden of proof in the criminal case.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the inference is not conclusive. C: C is wrong because the issue is jury instruction language, not a nonresponsive answer. D: D is wrong because the question is this criminal case and the instruction must preserve the jury's freedom to accept or reject the inference.
