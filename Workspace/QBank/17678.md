Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: Rachel alleges that on March 3 at a named meeting, Jacob falsely stated that a warehouse had passed a safety inspection, identifies who heard the statement, and attaches the inspection failure notice. The complaint states generally that Jacob knew the statement was false. Jacob moves to dismiss because knowledge was not pleaded with detailed facts. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Dismiss, because knowledge must be pleaded with the same particularity as the statement itself
Answer B: Dismiss, because Rule 9(b) never permits fraud claims
Answer C: Deny dismissal on this ground because the circumstances are particularized and knowledge may be alleged generally
Answer D: Deny dismissal because fraud uses only Rule 8
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The complaint states the who, what, when, and where, and Rule 9(b) allows general mental-state allegations.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Conditions of mind may be alleged generally. B: Rule 9(b) regulates pleading; it does not ban fraud claims. D: Fraud is subject to Rule 9(b) particularity for circumstances.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 9(b) requires particularity for fraud or mistake circumstances while allowing malice, intent, knowledge, and other conditions of mind to be alleged generally.
