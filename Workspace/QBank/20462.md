Question: In a federal civil trial, Saul cross-examines Martha and asks, “You expect this jury to believe that ridiculous story, don't you?” Martha's counsel objects that the question is argumentative. Saul responds that he is entitled to challenge credibility on cross-examination. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because any question about credibility is proper on cross-examination.
Answer B: Sustain the objection and require Saul to ask questions rather than argue with the witness.
Answer C: Overrule the objection unless Martha has already been impeached by a conviction.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if the question calls for hearsay.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may control the mode of examining witnesses and prevent argumentative questioning. Cross-examination may challenge credibility, but counsel must do so through proper questions rather than argument.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because credibility cross-examination is still subject to court control. C: C is wrong because a conviction is not required before the court can control argumentative questions. D: D is wrong because the issue is form and courtroom control, not hearsay.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / argumentative questions
