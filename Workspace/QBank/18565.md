Question: During a cave-in, five trapped miners could survive only if a heavy support beam was moved. Moving it would crush one pinned miner who was still alive. Rowan intentionally moved the beam, killing the pinned miner, because he believed it was the only way to save the five others. Rowan was charged with murder. Is necessity a complete defense to the intentional homicide at common law?
Answer A: Yes, because Rowan saved more lives than he took
Answer B: Yes, because natural forces rather than a human threat created the emergency
Answer C: No, because necessity is generally not a complete defense to intentional homicide
Answer D: No, because necessity is available only when a human threatens the defendant
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rowan intentionally killed an innocent person. Although the emergency was severe and his motive was to save others, necessity generally does not provide a complete common-law defense to intentional murder.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common law generally does not allow necessity as a complete defense to intentional homicide, even under tragic pressure. B: Classification is only the first step. Necessity still generally fails as a complete defense to intentional homicide. D: Necessity involves natural or circumstantial pressures; duress involves human threats. The defense fails here because of the intentional-homicide limit.
lb_rule_reference: Necessity generally is not a complete defense to intentional homicide at common law, even when the defendant acts to avoid a greater harm.
