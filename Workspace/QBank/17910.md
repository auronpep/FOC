Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Ezra agreed in a signed writing to sell Miriam 500 jars of oil each month for six months. The writing was silent about whether Miriam could return sealed jars that were not defective. During the first three months, Ezra accepted returns of sealed, undefective jars without objection. In month four, Ezra refuses a similar return. Miriam offers the prior three months of conduct to interpret the agreement. Should the evidence be admitted?
Answer A: Yes, because course of performance under the same goods contract may explain or supplement the agreement if reasonably consistent with the writing.
Answer B: No, because the signed writing is final and therefore all performance evidence is barred.
Answer C: No, because only negotiations before signing can be used to interpret a contract.
Answer D: Yes, but only if the writing expressly says course of performance is admissible.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Article 2, a final written expression may be explained or supplemented by course of performance, course of dealing, and usage of trade when reasonably consistent. Ezra's repeated acceptance of returns under the same contract is course of performance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because UCC 2-202 allows course evidence to explain or supplement a final writing. C: C is wrong because post-signing conduct under the same contract is an important meaning source. D: D is wrong because the UCC makes course evidence relevant without requiring an express admissibility clause.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-202 / course of performance
