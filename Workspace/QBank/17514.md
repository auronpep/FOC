Question: In a federal civil fraud case, Esther must prove fraud by clear and convincing evidence. She offers testimony from Daniel that he saw Jacob alter a receipt. Jacob objects that Daniel's testimony is inadmissible unless the judge finds it clear and convincing. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because evidence in a fraud case must itself be clear and convincing before admission.
Answer B: Overrule the objection if Daniel has personal knowledge and the testimony is otherwise admissible; the clear-and-convincing standard governs the jury's ultimate persuasion, not the admissibility of each item.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Daniel's testimony is corroborated by a document.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Jacob first fails to produce contrary evidence.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A heightened burden of persuasion governs the factfinder's ultimate decision on the claim. It does not mean each individual item of evidence must independently meet that standard before admission. If Daniel has personal knowledge and the testimony is otherwise admissible, the jury decides its weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it confuses the merits burden with the admissibility threshold. C: C is wrong because corroboration is not categorically required for admissibility. D: D is wrong because the opponent's failure to produce contrary evidence is not a condition of admissibility.
