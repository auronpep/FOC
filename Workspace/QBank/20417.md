Question: Two hunters negligently fired toward a hiker at the same time. One pellet struck the hiker, but the pellet cannot be matched to either gun. Both hunters are before the court, and both breached the standard of care by firing toward the hiker. What is the likely causation consequence?
Answer A: The hiker automatically loses because he cannot identify which hunter's pellet hit him
Answer B: The burden may shift to the hunters to prove who did not cause the injury
Answer C: Market-share liability applies because all bullets are fungible products
Answer D: Both hunters are liable only if they acted with intent to hit the hiker
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: This is the classic alternative-liability setting: all defendants acted negligently, one caused the harm, uncertainty remains, and all are before the court.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Alternative liability may shift the burden to negligent defendants in this narrow setting. C: This is not a fungible-product market-share case. Alternative liability is the better doctrine. D: Alternative liability can apply to negligent conduct; intent is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Alternative liability may shift the burden when all defendants acted negligently and uncertainty remains over which caused one indivisible injury.
