Question: Elizabeth owned Hill House, which benefited from a recorded easement over John's lane “for ingress and egress to Hill House.” John allowed Elizabeth to use the lane personally but objected when Elizabeth's guests, mail carrier, and repair workers used the lane to reach Hill House. Their use was ordinary in frequency and did not damage the lane. May John exclude those users?
Answer A: Yes, because only the named owner of the dominant estate may use an ingress-and-egress easement.
Answer B: No, because ordinary guests, mail carriers, and repair workers may use the access easement to reach the dominant estate when their use is reasonable.
Answer C: Yes, because every non-owner must obtain a separate written easement from John.
Answer D: No, because the easement gives the general public an unrestricted right to use John's lane.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An access easement for a residence ordinarily includes reasonable use by the dominant owner's guests, invitees, and service providers when they are going to or from the dominant estate. This incidental use serves the easement's access purpose and does not materially enlarge the burden.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because access easements are not usually limited to the owner's personal footsteps or vehicle. B is correct because the users are reasonably connected to Hill House. C: C is wrong because separate easements are not required for ordinary invitee use. D: D is wrong because incidental invitee use is not a public easement.
lb_rule_reference: Access easement / ordinary invitee use
