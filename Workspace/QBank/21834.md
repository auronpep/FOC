Question: A state charges higher annual registration fees for luxury boats than for smaller recreational boats. Owners of luxury boats challenge the fee schedule, arguing that wealth classifications are suspect and must be justified by a compelling interest. What scrutiny applies?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny, because wealth is a suspect classification
Answer B: Intermediate scrutiny, because fees burden property ownership
Answer C: Rational basis, because this is an ordinary economic classification without a separate fundamental-right burden
Answer D: No review, because taxes and fees are never subject to Equal Protection
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The state may draw economic lines if they are rationally related to legitimate revenue or regulatory interests.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Wealth is not a suspect classification for ordinary Equal Protection review. B: Ordinary fee schedules do not receive intermediate scrutiny on that basis. D: Deference is not immunity; rational basis still applies.
lb_rule_reference: Wealth is not a suspect classification, and ordinary economic fees generally receive rational basis review absent a separate fundamental-right burden.
