


BARMATRIX Q#: 14068
Outline_code: 91090100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jury Trials
Outline: Right to Jury Trial
Question: A farmer sued a botanist in federal court, alleging that pollutants from the botanist's property were traveling to the farmer's property in the water stream that they shared, polluting the farmer's land and reducing the market value of the farmer's land. The farmer sought an injunction against the botanist's continuing pollution of the stream and money damages for the diminution in value of the farmer's property that had already resulted. The suit was properly based on diversity jurisdiction. The botanist made a timely request for a jury trial. Does the botanist have a Seventh Amendment right to a jury trial on his claims?
Answer A: A: No, the botanist does not have a right to a jury trial as to any claim because the case is essentially equitable because of the request for injunctive relief.
Answer B: B: No, the botanist does not have a right to a jury trial as to any claim because none of them bear exclusively upon equitable relief.
Answer C: C: Yes, the botanist has a right to a jury trial as to every claim, regardless of whether they are equitable in nature.
Answer D: D: Yes, the botanist has a right to a jury trial as to every claim because the issues are common to both the legal and equitable claims.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. The U.S. Supreme Court has declared that an issue common to both legal and equitable claims in the same proceeding must be tried by a jury when a jury has been timely and properly requested. Here, the issues going to liability for damages to the farmer's land and whether the farmer should be awarded an injunction on the same facts should go to the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The Supreme Court has declared that an issue common to both legal and equitable claims in the same proceeding must be tried by a jury when a jury has been timely and properly requested. B is incorrect. On the contrary, purely equitable relief is a basis for NOT allowing a jury trial. When an issue is common to both legal and equitable claims, as is the case here, the Court has held that the Seventh Amendment right to a jury trial will apply. C is incorrect. The botanist would not have a right to a jury trial as to facts that would bear only on whether the court should grant the requested injunction, as injunctions are a form of equitable relief. There is no Seventh Amendment right to a jury trial for matters that are relevant only to equitable relief. However, the issues go to both legal and equitable forms of relief, which is why the botanist does have the right to a jury trial.
