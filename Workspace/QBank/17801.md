Question: A plaintiff requests emails stored only on obsolete backup tapes. The defendant identifies the tapes as not reasonably accessible because restoring them would cost hundreds of thousands of dollars. The plaintiff shows the tapes likely contain unique messages from the only month when the disputed negotiations occurred. How should the court analyze the request?
Answer A: The defendant must produce all requested ESI because relevant electronic documents are always discoverable
Answer B: The defendant never has to produce the tapes once it identifies them as not reasonably accessible
Answer C: The court should consider whether good cause justifies discovery despite burden and may impose conditions such as sampling or cost allocation
Answer D: The plaintiff can obtain the tapes only by proving the defendant destroyed accessible copies intentionally
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule balances inaccessibility against good cause and permits conditioned production.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The rules recognize undue burden or cost for inaccessible ESI sources. B: The court may order discovery for good cause, often with conditions. D: Not-reasonably-accessible ESI discovery does not require proof of intentional destruction.
lb_rule_reference: A party need not provide discovery of ESI from sources identified as not reasonably accessible because of undue burden or cost, but the court may order discovery for good cause and set conditions.
