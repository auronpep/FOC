Question: A buyer and seller orally agree to a sale of 1,000 tablets. The seller later signs a memo stating, “Confirmed: sale of 300 tablets to Buyer for delivery next week.” The buyer sues to enforce the alleged agreement for 1,000 tablets. Assuming no exception applies, how much of the alleged goods contract is enforceable under the signed memo?
Answer A: None, because the memo incorrectly states the quantity.
Answer B: All 1,000 tablets, because the oral agreement controls if the memo shows a deal existed.
Answer C: Only 300 tablets, because the contract is not enforceable beyond the quantity shown in the writing.
Answer D: All 1,000 tablets, but only if the buyer proves the oral quantity by clear and convincing evidence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC §2-201, a writing is not insufficient merely because it omits or incorrectly states a term, but the contract is not enforceable beyond the quantity of goods shown in the writing. The signed memo supports enforcement only for 300 tablets.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an incorrect quantity does not make the writing wholly ineffective. B: B is wrong because the oral quantity cannot expand SOF enforceability beyond the written quantity. D: D is wrong because a higher proof standard does not bypass the quantity cap.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 — quantity term caps enforcement
