Outline: Joinder of claims
Question: Two siblings dispute ownership of a painting in federal court. A third sibling is not joined but claims a one-third ownership interest in the same painting and cannot practically protect that interest if the court declares the painting owned entirely by one existing party. What is the best Rule 19(a) analysis?
Answer A: The third sibling may be a required party because the action may impair the sibling's claimed ownership interest
Answer B: The third sibling cannot be required because complete relief can be awarded between the two existing siblings
Answer C: The third sibling is automatically indispensable, so the case must be dismissed immediately
Answer D: Rule 19 applies only to tort cases, not property disputes
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 19(a) includes absentee interest impairment as a required-party ground.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The test also considers absentee interest impairment and inconsistent-obligation risk. C: Rule 19 first asks required-party status and feasibility; dismissal comes only after the Rule 19(b) analysis if joinder is not feasible. D: Rule 19 applies in federal civil actions generally.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 19(a) asks whether complete relief can be accorded among existing parties, whether the absentee's interest may be impaired, or whether existing parties face substantial risk of multiple or inconsistent obligations.
