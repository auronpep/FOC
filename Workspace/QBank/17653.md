Question: A tenant, Lydia, leased a storefront from Nathan for one year. Nathan promised to repaint the front door within two weeks after move-in. Nathan repainted the door a week late. Lydia continued operating the store but refused to pay the next three months of rent, claiming Nathan breached first. Which result is most likely?
Answer A: Lydia may withhold all rent because any landlord breach discharges rent duties.
Answer B: Lydia may cancel the lease because a late repainting is always a material breach.
Answer C: Nathan's late repainting is at most a covenant breach giving damages if proven, but it does not discharge Lydia's rent duty.
Answer D: Nathan cannot collect rent unless Lydia expressly waived the repainting promise.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A minor breach of a collateral covenant does not automatically discharge the other party's continuing payment duty. Lydia continued to use the storefront, and the late repainting did not materially defeat the lease. Her remedy, if any, is damages, not refusal to pay rent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every breach discharges all remaining duties. B: B is wrong because late repainting is not automatically material. D: D is wrong because Nathan does not need a waiver to enforce matured rent when his breach was nonmaterial.
lb_rule_reference: Independent covenant / damages not rent forfeiture
