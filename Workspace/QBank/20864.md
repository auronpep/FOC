Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal robbery trial, John testifies that he cannot identify the robber in court. John also testifies that two days after the robbery he viewed a lineup and identified Peter as the robber. John is subject to cross-examination about the lineup. The prosecution offers testimony from the officer who conducted the lineup, confirming John's identification of Peter. Peter objects that the statement is a prior inconsistent statement not given under oath. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the lineup testimony because John's prior identification was not under oath.
Answer B: Admit the lineup testimony as substantive evidence of identification because John testifies and is subject to cross-examination about the prior identification.
Answer C: Admit the lineup testimony only to impeach John's failure to identify Peter in court.
Answer D: Exclude the lineup testimony because prior identifications are admissible only if repeated in court.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A prior statement identifying a person as someone the declarant perceived earlier is not hearsay if the declarant testifies and is subject to cross-examination about it. John's prior lineup identification has its own substantive route under Rule 801(d)(1)(C); it is not governed by the stricter sworn-prior-inconsistent-statement rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because prior identifications do not need the Rule 801(d)(1)(A) oath/proceeding requirement. C: C is wrong because the prior identification can be substantive, not merely impeachment. D: D is wrong because the prior identification route exists precisely even when the witness cannot or does not repeat the identification in court.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 801(d)(1)(C) / prior identification versus prior inconsistent statement
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the boundary between prior inconsistent statements and prior identifications.
