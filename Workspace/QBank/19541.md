Question: A state bans all online art and literature containing sexual themes because minors might see it. The law applies to adults and minors alike and does not require that the material satisfy the constitutional obscenity test. What is the strongest First Amendment objection?
Answer A: The law is valid because all sexual content is obscenity
Answer B: The law is valid because protecting minors erases adult speech rights
Answer C: The law is suspect because it suppresses protected adult speech without limiting itself to obscenity or a tailored minor-protection rule
Answer D: The law receives no review because the internet has no First Amendment protection
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Nonobscene adult expression remains protected, and a broad ban on sexual themes sweeps far beyond unprotected obscenity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Sexual content is not automatically obscene; the constitutional obscenity test must be met. B: The state may protect minors, but it cannot broadly suppress protected adult speech without proper tailoring. D: Protected speech does not lose First Amendment protection simply because it appears online.
lb_rule_reference: Obscenity is unprotected only if the constitutional obscenity test is satisfied; nonobscene adult sexual expression remains protected from overbroad suppression.
