Question: Rebekah signed and delivered a deed conveying her house to Jacob. In exchange, Jacob orally promised to support Rebekah for the rest of her life. The deed used present words of transfer and did not state that title would end if Jacob failed to provide support. Jacob accepted the deed but later stopped supporting Rebekah. Rebekah seeks to recover the house solely on the theory that the promised consideration failed. What is the best title result?
Answer A: Rebekah automatically owns the house again because Jacob failed to provide the promised support.
Answer B: Rebekah owns the house because oral support promises can never accompany a deed.
Answer C: Jacob keeps title under the deed, although Rebekah may have a separate remedy if the support promise is enforceable.
Answer D: Jacob keeps title only if he records the deed before Rebekah sues.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive title lock is whether the deed itself made title conditional. Rebekah signed and delivered a present conveyance, and Jacob accepted. Because the deed did not provide that title would end on failure to support, Jacob's later nonperformance does not automatically undo the deed; any support claim is a separate remedy issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because failure of consideration does not automatically revest title after an unconditional deed. B: B is wrong because the problem is not the mere existence of an oral support promise; the title question turns on the delivered deed and absence of a title-ending condition. D: D is wrong because recording is not what preserves title between these parties.
