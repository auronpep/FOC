Outline: Truthfulness
Question: In a federal trial, Mark testifies for the plaintiff. To attack Mark's truthfulness, the defendant calls Joanna. Joanna says she has never heard Mark's general reputation for truthfulness discussed, but she heard one neighbor accuse Mark of lying in a land dispute. The defendant offers Joanna's testimony as reputation evidence that Mark is untruthful. Mark objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because one accusation is enough to prove community reputation.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because any statement about lying is reputation evidence.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because an isolated accusation is not testimony about Mark's general reputation for truthfulness.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if the neighbor who made the accusation is unavailable.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 608(a) permits reputation testimony about a witness's character for truthfulness or untruthfulness. An isolated accusation by one person is not the same as testimony about general reputation in a relevant community.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because one accusation does not establish general reputation. B: B is wrong because a statement about lying may be a specific accusation, not reputation evidence. D: D is wrong because the neighbor's availability does not convert the accusation into reputation testimony.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(a) / reputation must be general reputation, not isolated rumors
