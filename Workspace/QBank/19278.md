Question: Aaron owed Paul $5,000 on an old debt that Paul could no longer enforce against Aaron because the limitations period had expired. Aaron later sold tools to Joshua. As part of the sale price, Joshua promised Aaron in a signed agreement, “I will pay Paul $5,000.” Joshua refused to pay Paul, arguing that Paul's old claim against Aaron was time-barred. May Paul enforce Joshua's promise?
Answer A: No, because Paul could no longer sue Aaron on the old debt.
Answer B: No, because Paul did not sign the Aaron-Joshua agreement.
Answer C: Yes, because Joshua made a new supported promise to Aaron to pay Paul, making Paul an intended creditor beneficiary of that new contract.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Paul first revives the original debt against Aaron.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Paul may enforce as an intended beneficiary of the new contract. The enforceability of the old debt against Aaron does not defeat a new supported promise by Joshua to pay Paul directly as part of the Aaron-Joshua bargain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the old debt's status does not eliminate rights under the new contract. B: B is wrong because a beneficiary need not sign. D: D is wrong because Paul's route is the new promise, not revival of the original debt.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — new promise to pay creditor
