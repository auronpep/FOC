Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Self-defense
Question: Tara sees a stranger, Owen, pinning Maya against a wall in an alley. Maya is yelling, "Let go of me!" Tara reasonably believes Owen is assaulting Maya. Tara pushes Owen away, causing him to fall and bruise his elbow. In fact, Owen was an undercover officer lawfully arresting Maya, but Tara did not know that and had no reason to know it. If Tara is charged with battery, what is her strongest defense?
Answer A: No defense, because Owen was actually making a lawful arrest
Answer B: Defense of others, because Tara reasonably believed Maya faced imminent unlawful force and used nondeadly force
Answer C: No defense, because defense of others is available only for family members
Answer D: Defense of property, because Tara was protecting Maya's bodily autonomy
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Tara used proportionate nondeadly force after reasonably perceiving that Maya was being unlawfully restrained. Under the modern majority rule, defense of others turns on Tara's reasonable belief, not on perfect accuracy. Because her mistake was reasonable and her response was limited, the defense applies.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The modern rule does not always require the defended person's actual right to self-defense. It protects a reasonable defense-of-others belief. Tara reasonably perceived an assault. C: The modern rule allows defense of strangers as well as family members when the defendant reasonably believes defensive force is necessary. D: This is defense of another person, not defense of property. Using the wrong doctrine risks importing the wrong force limits.
lb_rule_reference: Under the modern majority rule, a defendant may use proportionate force to defend another if the defendant reasonably believes the other person faces imminent unlawful force
