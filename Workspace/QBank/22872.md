


BARMATRIX Q#: 22872
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A limited partnership brought a breach of contract suit against a man for $1 million in federal district court. The man was a citizen of State A. The limited partnership consisted of three general partners and four limited partners. All three general partners and three of the limited partners were citizens of State B. One of the limited partners was a citizen of State A. The partnership conducted a good deal of business in State A. The man moved to dismiss, arguing that the federal court did not have subject matter jurisdiction over the claim. How should the court rule?
Answer A: A: Grant the motion, because one of the limited partners is a citizen of State A.
Answer B: B: Grant the motion, because the partnership conducted business in State A.
Answer C: C: Deny the motion, because all of the general partners are citizens of State B.
Answer D: D: Deny the motion, because the majority of the partners were citizens of State B.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A court has subject matter jurisdiction based on diversity if the amount in controversy is over $75,000 and the plaintiff and defendant are citizens of different states. In determining the citizenship of a limited partnership, the court considers the citizenship of all partners whether those partners are general or limited. Here, since one of the limited partners is a citizen of State A, the court would not have subject matter jurisdiction based on diversity. Carden v. Arkoma Associates, 494 U.S. 185 (1990). B is incorrect because the citizenship of the limited partnership would not be based on where it conducts business. C and D are incorrect because the court would consider the citizenship of all of the partners, whether they were general or limited.
