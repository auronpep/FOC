


BARMATRIX Q#: 22267
Outline_code: 31010502
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: A woman got into a fight with a waitress at a local sports bar. The police were called. When the police arrived, the woman was long gone, but the waitress told one of the officers that the woman had gotten mad that her team was losing and hit the waitress with a beer mug. The woman was charged with criminal battery. The waitress chose not to testify at trial, so the prosecutor sought to have the police officer testify as to what the waitress told him. Is the police officer's testimony regarding the waitress's statement admissible?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the statement qualifies as an excited utterance.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the statement qualifies as a public record.
Answer C: C: No, because the prosecutor must introduce the officer's entire report into evidence.
Answer D: D: No, because the statement is testimonial in nature.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the Confrontation Clause of the Sixth Amendment, statements that are testimonial in nature are inadmissible in criminal cases. Importantly, victim statements made to police after the emergency is over qualify as testimonial. Here, the woman is charged with a crime, and the waitress made her statement to police after the emergency was over. A is incorrect because an excited utterance requires the declarant is under the stress of a startling event. Here, the woman is long gone by the time the police arrive, so the timing of the statement is unlikely to qualify as an excited utterance. B and D incorrect because the public records exception specifically excludes statements taken by law enforcement personnel in criminal cases.
