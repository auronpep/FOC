Outline: Contribution and indemnity
Question: A bicyclist was injured when a city van and a courier car negligently entered the same intersection. The jury found the city 30 percent responsible and the courier 70 percent responsible for one indivisible injury. Because the city had available insurance, it paid the entire judgment. The city now seeks reimbursement from the courier. What recovery is the city most likely entitled to in its contribution claim against the courier?
Answer A: Contribution for the courier's equitable share of the judgment
Answer B: Complete indemnity for the entire judgment, because the courier was more at fault
Answer C: No recovery, because the city voluntarily paid more than its share
Answer D: Reimbursement from the bicyclist, because the bicyclist collected more from the city than the city's percentage share
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The city and courier were joint tortfeasors for an indivisible injury. After paying more than its 30 percent share, the city may seek contribution for the courier's equitable share.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Greater fault supports contribution by share, not complete indemnity. Indemnity generally requires vicarious liability, derivative liability, or a similar complete-shift basis. C: Joint and several liability may require one defendant to pay the full judgment. Contribution exists precisely to correct overpayment among joint tortfeasors. D: The bicyclist received one judgment satisfaction. The allocation correction is between joint tortfeasors, not a refund from the plaintiff.
lb_rule_reference: A joint tortfeasor who pays more than its equitable share of an indivisible injury may seek contribution from another jointly liable tortfeasor.
