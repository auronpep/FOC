Outline: Conviction of crime
Question: In a federal civil trial, Peter testifies for the plaintiff. The defendant seeks to impeach Peter with a two-year-old misdemeanor conviction for disorderly conduct, punishable by 30 days in jail. The conviction did not require proof of deceit, fraud, or a false statement. Peter objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the conviction because any criminal conviction may be used to attack credibility.
Answer B: Admit the conviction because it occurred less than ten years ago.
Answer C: Exclude the conviction because it is neither a qualifying felony nor a conviction involving a dishonest act or false statement.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction only if Peter is a party rather than a nonparty witness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A conviction that is not punishable by more than one year must fit FRE 609(a)(2) to be used for impeachment. Disorderly conduct punishable by 30 days and not requiring dishonesty or false statement fits neither route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every conviction impeaches under Rule 609. B: B is wrong because being recent does not make an otherwise nonqualifying misdemeanor admissible. D: D is wrong because the route fails regardless of whether Peter is a party.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(a) / misdemeanor not involving dishonesty or false statement
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests offense-type classification and prevents treating any criminal record as credibility impeachment.
