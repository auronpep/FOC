Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: In a federal contract trial, Naomi wants to impeach a witness by proving that the witness has hated Naomi for years. Naomi offers to call seven witnesses to describe a separate business dispute between Naomi and the witness, including accounting records, emails, and testimony from two accountants. The judge allows Naomi to ask the witness directly about hostility but excludes the seven-witness side presentation under Rule 403. Was the exclusion proper?
Answer A: No, because bias evidence can never be limited.
Answer B: No, because once evidence is relevant to credibility, Rule 403 does not apply.
Answer C: Yes, because the court may allow targeted bias inquiry while excluding a collateral mini-trial whose time and confusion substantially outweigh added probative value.
Answer D: Yes, but only because bias evidence is always inadmissible character evidence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Bias is important, but Rule 403 still permits the court to control proof that would consume excessive time or confuse the issues. The court can allow a direct bias inquiry while limiting collateral proof that adds little additional value.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because bias evidence is not immune from reasonable Rule 403 control. B: B is wrong because credibility evidence remains subject to Rule 403. D: D is wrong because bias is not inadmissible character evidence; the problem is excessive collateral proof.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403
