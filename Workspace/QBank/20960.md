Question: A state requires all apples grown in the state to be packed and labeled inside the state before they may be shipped anywhere. The law applies to in-state and out-of-state buyers alike, but it forces packing jobs to remain in the state. What is the best DCC characterization?
Answer A: It is neutral because every buyer must use in-state packing
Answer B: It is protectionist because it forces local processing as a condition of commerce
Answer C: It is automatically valid because states own all goods grown inside their borders
Answer D: It raises only Article IV privileges and immunities, not DCC
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The law burdens interstate flow by requiring a local economic step before goods can move.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Formal equality does not hide that the law reserves processing activity for in-state businesses. C: Private goods do not become state-owned merely because they were grown locally. D: A local-processing rule burdening interstate goods is a classic DCC problem.
lb_rule_reference: A state rule requiring goods to be processed in-state before sale or export is usually discriminatory protectionism even if it applies to all sellers in identical words.
