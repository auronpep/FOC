Question: A defendant was charged with using a forged prescription from a doctor to obtain Percodan from a drugstore on May 1. At trial, the drugstore owner identified the defendant as the customer, but the defendant testified that he had not been in the store. In rebuttal, the prosecutor calls two additional drug store owners to testify that, on May 1, a man they identified as the defendant had presented prescriptions for Percodan from a doctor at their drug stores. The additional drug store owners' testimony is
Answer A: A: admissible, to prove a pertinent trait of the defendant's character and the defendant's action in conformity therewith.
Answer B: B: admissible, to identify the man who presented the prescription at the original drugstore.
Answer C: C: inadmissible, because it proves specific acts rather than reputation or opinion.
Answer D: D: inadmissible, because other crimes may not be used to show propensity.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. Evidence of other crimes, wrongs, or acts is not admissible to prove the character of a person in order to show action in conformity therewith. However, other crimes evidence can be admitted to show identity, intent, preparation, or plan. The evidence the prosecutor seeks to admit is that the exact same man, the defendant, had presented a forged prescription for the exact same drug, on the exact same day. That evidence would be admissible to prove the identity, which the defendant called into question by his testimony, of the person who obtained the Percodan at the drugstore.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Evidence of other crimes, wrongs, or acts is not admissible to prove the character of a person in order to show action in conformity therewith. C is incorrect. The additional drug store owners' testimony is not being used to prove character; it is being used to prove identity. Used for this purpose, the testimony is admissible even though it covers specific acts. D is incorrect. Although evidence of other crimes is inadmissible to prove propensity, evidence of the other crimes in this case is admissible to prove identity.
