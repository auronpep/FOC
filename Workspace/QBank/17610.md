MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Trial judge’s discretion to control order and presentation of evidence
Question: In a federal employment trial, Esther asks to use a timeline while questioning Daniel. The timeline summarizes dates Daniel will testify about. The judge allows Esther to use the timeline as an aid during testimony but tells the jury that the timeline itself is not evidence unless separately admitted. The defendant objects that allowing the timeline gives Esther too much control over presentation. How should the objection be resolved?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because the judge may allow a demonstrative aid to make testimony effective while controlling its status before the jury.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because demonstrative aids are never allowed during testimony.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless the timeline is admitted as substantive evidence before Daniel testifies.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because anything displayed to the jury automatically becomes admitted evidence.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may manage the presentation of evidence, including the use of demonstrative aids, so long as the jury is not misled about the aid's evidentiary status.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because demonstrative aids may be allowed under court control. C: C is wrong because an aid may sometimes be used to explain testimony without being admitted substantively. D: D is wrong because display to the jury does not automatically make an item admitted evidence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / demonstrative timeline as aid
