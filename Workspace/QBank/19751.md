Question: After indictment, police place Carla in a live lineup without notifying counsel or obtaining a waiver. The witness identifies Carla. At trial, the witness also wants to identify Carla in court based on having seen the robber clearly for several minutes during the crime. What is the best statement of the remedy?
Answer A: The lineup identification is vulnerable, but the in-court identification may be admitted if the prosecution proves an independent source
Answer B: Every later identification is automatically excluded once a lineup counsel violation occurs
Answer C: The lineup identification is admissible because in-court identification might have an independent source
Answer D: The lineup violation is cured because the witness was certain
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The uncounseled lineup violates the Sixth Amendment. The later in-court identification is not automatically barred if it rests on observations independent of the unlawful lineup.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The independent-source doctrine can permit a later in-court identification. C: Independent source can save a later in-court ID; it does not retroactively make the uncounseled lineup lawful. D: Certainty does not cure the absence of counsel or waiver at a critical lineup.
lb_rule_reference: A post-attachment lineup conducted without counsel or waiver can require suppression of the lineup identification, but a later in-court identification may still be admitted if supported by an independent source.
