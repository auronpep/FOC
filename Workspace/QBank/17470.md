Question: Naomi bought an industrial oven from Paul's company. The agreement included delivery and basic installation, but most of the price was allocated to the oven. The signed writing promised a “premium installation package” without defining that package. Naomi claims the package included operator training; Paul claims it included only connecting the oven to power and gas. Which additional fact most strengthens Naomi's Content/Meaning argument?
Answer A: Naomi lost several catering jobs after her employees misused the oven.
Answer B: During negotiations, Paul orally said, “We usually help buyers learn the equipment,” but the final writing did not mention training.
Answer C: The signed agreement incorporated Paul's attached premium-installation checklist, and the checklist listed a two-hour operator-training session.
Answer D: Paul's installer arrived one day later than the scheduled installation date.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that an incorporated checklist or specification can become part of the agreement and define a disputed term. The trigger facts are the undefined “premium installation package” and the incorporated checklist. The decisive locks are source law, term status, and offered purpose: because the deal is goods-dominant with installation incidental, Article 2 may apply, but the key meaning fact is that the writing itself incorporates the checklist. C directly defines the package.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because lost jobs may affect damages, not the meaning of the installation package.
