Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A building owner hired an independent demolition contractor to remove a brick facade over a busy public sidewalk. City rules required the owner to keep the sidewalk reasonably safe during facade removal. The contractor controlled the demolition method, failed to install required debris netting, and a falling brick injured a pedestrian. The pedestrian sues the building owner for vicarious liability based on the contractor's negligence. Is the pedestrian likely to prevail on a vicarious liability claim against the building owner for the contractor's negligent demolition work?
Answer A: No, because the contractor controlled the manner and means of the demolition work
Answer B: Yes, because the owner's sidewalk-safety duty during dangerous facade removal could not be delegated away
Answer C: Yes, because hiring an independent contractor always makes the principal the contractor's insurer
Answer D: No, because nondelegable duties apply only when the owner personally directs the negligent act
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The owner had a duty to keep the public sidewalk reasonably safe during the dangerous demolition work. That duty is nondelegable, so the owner may be vicariously liable for the contractor's negligence in performing it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Even when the contractor controls the work, a principal may remain liable for nondelegable duties or inherently dangerous work. Removing a facade over a public sidewalk with a safety duty imposed on the owner fits that exception. C: The exception is not unlimited. Liability turns on the nondelegable duty or inherently dangerous nature of the work, not on a rule that principals insure every contractor tort. D: A nondelegable duty can create vicarious liability even without personal direction of the negligent act. The duty remains with the principal despite delegation.
lb_rule_reference: A principal may be vicariously liable for an independent contractor's negligence when the work involves a nondelegable duty or inherently dangerous activity.
