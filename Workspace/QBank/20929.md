Question: In a federal civil trial, during closing argument, Sarah's lawyer displays a slide listing the admitted exhibits that support Sarah's damages calculation. The slide itself was not admitted. The opponent asks the court to tell the jury that the slide is argument, not evidence. What should the court do?
Answer A: Refuse because any visual shown to the jury becomes evidence.
Answer B: Give the instruction because the slide is a demonstrative aid or argument aid, not substantive evidence unless admitted.
Answer C: Exclude all admitted exhibits mentioned on the slide.
Answer D: Tell the jury the slide is conclusive if it accurately lists exhibit numbers.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A lawyer's visual aid in argument can be permitted to help organize argument, but it is not itself evidence unless admitted. A clarifying instruction is appropriate.
