Question: A state charges resident plumbers a modest license application fee but charges citizens of other states a much higher fee to apply for the same plumbing license. The state offers no evidence that nonresident plumbers create special administrative costs. What is the strongest Article IV argument?
Answer A: The rule burdens nonresident citizens in pursuing a common calling without a substantial reason closely fitted to the discrimination
Answer B: The rule is valid because states may always charge nonresidents more for jobs
Answer C: Only the Dormant Commerce Clause can apply because plumbing is economic
Answer D: Article IV applies only if the plaintiff is a corporation
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Occupational livelihood is a fundamental privilege protected by Article IV.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Nonresident citizens cannot be disadvantaged in common callings without a substantial reason and fit. C: The plaintiff is a nonresident citizen seeking a common calling, a direct Article IV pattern. D: Corporations are not Article IV citizens; natural citizens are.
lb_rule_reference: A state burden on nonresident citizens pursuing a common calling violates Article IV unless the state has a substantial reason and the discrimination closely relates to that reason.
