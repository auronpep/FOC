Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Consent
Question: A Delaware software company licensed its platform to an Arizona retailer. The contract stated: "The parties consent to exclusive jurisdiction in the state and federal courts located in Delaware for disputes arising from this agreement." After a payment dispute, the software company sued the retailer in Delaware federal court. The retailer moved to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction, arguing that it had no offices, employees, or property in Delaware. What is the strongest response to the retailer's motion?
Answer A: The clause can constitute consent to personal jurisdiction in Delaware for contract disputes
Answer B: The clause affects venue only and can never affect personal jurisdiction
Answer C: The clause matters only if the retailer is incorporated in Delaware
Answer D: The clause is irrelevant because subject-matter jurisdiction is always enough in federal court
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A valid forum-selection clause expressly consenting to exclusive Delaware courts is a consent basis for PJ, so the retailer's lack of ordinary contacts is not the dispositive issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Contractual consent can waive or establish PJ objections as well as shape venue analysis. C: Consent is independent of incorporation. D: SMJ does not supply PJ; the clause matters precisely because PJ is separate.
lb_rule_reference: A valid, mandatory forum-selection clause can operate as consent to personal jurisdiction in the selected forum.
