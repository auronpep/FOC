Question: Sarah and Rachel jointly hired lawyer Mary to draft a partnership agreement. During the joint representation, both discussed the same partnership tax risks with Mary in a joint meeting. Years later, Sarah and Rachel sue each other over the partnership and Rachel seeks Mary's notes from that joint meeting. Can Sarah use attorney-client privilege to block Rachel from obtaining the joint-meeting communications?
Answer A: Yes, because all communications with Mary remain privileged against every person forever.
Answer B: Yes, because Rachel is now Sarah's litigation opponent.
Answer C: No, because communications made in a joint representation generally are not privileged as between the former joint clients in a later dispute between them.
Answer D: No, because attorney-client privilege never applies to partnership matters.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Joint clients may assert privilege against outsiders, but communications made during the joint representation generally are not privileged as between those joint clients when they later become adverse to each other. The decisive axis is who is asserting privilege against whom.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is privilege absolutism and ignores the joint-client boundary. B: B is wrong because Later adversity does not transform joint-client communications into secrets from the former joint client. D: A is wrong because This is privilege absolutism and ignores the joint-client boundary. B is wrong because Later adversity does not transform joint-client communications into secrets from the former joint client. C is wrong because Correct answer D is wrong because This underprotects partnership legal advice against outsiders; the issue is the later dispute between joint clients.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / joint-client boundary
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating privilege as absolute and failing to identify the relationship between the privilege claimants.
