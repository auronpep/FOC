Outline: Defeasible fees
Question: Peter conveyed Brickacre “to John for the purpose of operating a carpenter's shop.” The deed contained no words of duration, no right of entry, no reverter language, and no gift-over to a third party. John operated a carpenter's shop for two years, then converted the property into his home. What estate does John hold after the conversion?
Answer A: A fee simple absolute.
Answer B: A fee simple determinable.
Answer C: A fee simple subject to condition subsequent.
Answer D: A life estate.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A statement of purpose, standing alone, does not usually create a defeasible fee. Because the deed lacks durational language, conditional language, a right of entry, reverter language, or gift-over language, John holds a fee simple absolute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because there is no durational language such as “so long as,” “while,” or “until.” C is wrong because there is no right of entry or termination power. C: B is wrong because there is no durational language such as “so long as,” “while,” or “until.” C is wrong because there is no right of entry or termination power. D is wrong because the deed is not measured by John's life. D: D is wrong because the deed is not measured by John's life.
lb_rule_reference: Purpose language / no defeasible fee
