Question: A supplier sued a buyer for breach of contract. While the contract suit was still pending, the buyer filed a new tort claim for malicious prosecution, alleging that the supplier had filed the contract suit out of spite and without probable cause. What is the buyer's strongest problem with the malicious prosecution claim?
Answer A: The prior proceeding has not terminated favorably for the buyer
Answer B: Malicious prosecution never applies to civil proceedings
Answer C: The supplier's spite automatically proves the claim
Answer D: The buyer should label the claim nuisance instead
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Favorable termination is an element of malicious prosecution.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: It can apply in appropriate civil settings depending on jurisdiction, but the missing element controls here. C: Malice alone is not enough. D: The facts concern litigation conduct, not interference with land use.
lb_rule_reference: A malicious prosecution claim is premature or unavailable unless the prior proceeding ended favorably to the current plaintiff.
