Question: Jonathan is charged with stealing silver from his employer. The prosecutor offers evidence that Jonathan was behind on rent and had recently received a collection letter. Jonathan objects that many people have debts and do not steal. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because financial pressure has some tendency to show motive; the fact that many debtors do not steal affects weight.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because debt never has any relevance to theft.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence unless the prosecutor proves Jonathan had no lawful way to pay rent.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if the collection letter is itself the stolen property.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Financial pressure may be weak motive evidence, but it has some tendency to make theft more probable. The jury may decide how much significance to give it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because debt can be relevant to motive depending on context. C: C is wrong because the prosecutor need not eliminate all lawful payment options before offering motive evidence. D: D is wrong because the letter is not offered as the object of theft; it is offered to show financial pressure.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401; FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats common motive evidence as irrelevant unless exclusive or conclusive.
