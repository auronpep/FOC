Question: A city ordinance makes it a misdemeanor to "act in a way that annoys any police officer or disrupts the officer's sense of order." It provides no further definitions or objective standards. Officers use it to arrest people for criticizing police, filming police, and standing silently near police. What is the strongest due process objection?
Answer A: The ordinance is void for vagueness because it lacks fair notice and invites arbitrary enforcement
Answer B: The ordinance is valid because any city may punish annoying behavior
Answer C: The ordinance is valid unless every possible application is unclear
Answer D: The ordinance is invalid only if it also violates the Takings Clause
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The standard depends on an officer's subjective annoyance and gives no objective guide.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Regulation still must give fair notice and avoid standardless enforcement. C: A law can be vague if it lacks fair notice or invites arbitrary enforcement, especially in speech-adjacent settings. D: Vagueness is a due process doctrine independent of takings.
lb_rule_reference: A law is void for vagueness if it fails to give ordinary people fair notice of what is prohibited or is so standardless that it invites arbitrary enforcement.
