Question: Noah owned a small coastal lot. After Noah bought the lot, the state adopted a regulation permanently prohibiting any building, camping, farming, leasing, or other economically productive use of the lot. The state did not show that Noah's proposed uses were already barred by background nuisance or property principles. Noah seeks compensation. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Noah is likely entitled to compensation because the regulation deprived the parcel of all economically beneficial use.
Answer B: Noah is not entitled to compensation because environmental regulations can never be takings.
Answer C: Noah is entitled to compensation only if the state physically occupies the parcel.
Answer D: Noah is not entitled to compensation unless he proves the regulation reduced value by exactly 50 percent or more.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A regulation that deprives land of all economically beneficial use is generally a categorical taking unless the prohibited use was already barred by background principles of property or nuisance law. The facts state a permanent prohibition of all economically productive use and no background-principles defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because environmental regulations may still require compensation if they cross the takings line. C: C is wrong because physical occupation is one takings route, not the only route. D: D is wrong because there is no fixed 50 percent threshold, and Lucas requires total economic wipeout.
lb_rule_reference: Lucas total regulatory taking
