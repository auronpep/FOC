Question: In a federal criminal prosecution for drug trafficking, the defendant's spouse agrees to testify that she saw the defendant load packages into a hidden compartment. The defendant objects, invoking spousal privilege. The spouse is willing to testify. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because either spouse may prevent adverse testimony during marriage.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because the federal adverse spousal testimonial privilege belongs to the witness-spouse, who may choose to testify.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because observations during marriage are confidential marital communications.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only because drug trafficking is never covered by any privilege.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the federal adverse spousal testimonial privilege, the witness-spouse holds the privilege to refuse adverse testimony in a criminal case. A defendant spouse cannot prevent a willing spouse from testifying. The observed loading is also not a confidential communication.
