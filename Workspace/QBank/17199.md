Question: Paul conveyed Harboracre “to Timothy and his heirs.” In the same deed, Timothy promised that Harboracre would never be used as an inn and agreed to pay Paul $20,000 if the promise was breached. The deed did not provide for reentry, forfeiture, automatic termination, or transfer to anyone else. Timothy later opened an inn. What is the effect of Timothy's breach?
Answer A: Title automatically reverts to Paul.
Answer B: Timothy keeps title, but Paul may seek the deed's covenant remedy.
Answer C: Title automatically shifts to Paul's heirs.
Answer D: Timothy's estate becomes a tenancy at will.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The deed creates a covenant, not a defeasible fee. It provides a damages remedy and does not include forfeiture, reentry, automatic termination, or gift-over language. Timothy keeps title, but Paul may pursue the stated covenant remedy.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because automatic reverter requires fee simple determinable language. C: C is wrong because no third-party gift-over or executory limitation appears. D: D is wrong because breach of a covenant in a deed does not create a tenancy at will.
lb_rule_reference: Covenant remedy / no forfeiture
