


BARMATRIX Q#: 22749
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A State A man wanted to sue a State B doctor in State A because State B courts were notoriously protective of State B doctors. The State A man knew that there was a big medical conference in State C that weekend, and the doctor would likely drive through State A if he chose to go to the conference. The man called the doctor's home. The doctor's minor son answered. The man said that he was a friend of the doctor and was wondering if the doctor might like to go fishing that weekend. The man asked the son if the doctor would be around. The son said that the doctor was going to the medical conference so he couldn't go fishing. The man immediately found a process server and described the doctor's car to him, which was an extremely recognizable purple sportscar. The process server drove to the doctor's house and then followed the doctor as he drove to the conference. When the doctor stopped at a rest stop in State A, the process server handed the doctor a copy of the complaint and told him he had been served. Is the doctor subject to State A jurisdiction?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the doctor was served in State A.
Answer B: B: Yes, because there was unfair prejudice in State B due to the state's protection of doctors.
Answer C: C: No, because the man lied to get the information regarding the doctor's travel plans.
Answer D: D: No, because the man was only passing through State A.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A party is subject to personal jurisdiction if he or she is served within that jurisdiction. While a party cannot be lured into the jurisdiction in order to be served, there is nothing stopping parties from using questionable means to discover when that party will be in a subject jurisdiction on their own accord. Here, the doctor was served in State A, so the State A court has jurisdiction. B is incorrect because the perceived prejudice in State B would not give the State A court jurisdiction. C is incorrect because a party can use tricky means to discover another party's travel plans. D is incorrect because it didn't matter that the doctor was only passing through.
