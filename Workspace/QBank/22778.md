


BARMATRIX Q#: 22778
Outline_code: 94050100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Venue
Question: A woman sued a painter and a framer for copying the woman's framed painting in violation of copyright laws. The framer was from the Northern District of State A, the painter was from the Southern District of State B, the woman was from Western District of State C, and the copied paintings were created and sold in the Northern District of State B. Where would venue be proper for the woman's claim?
Answer A: A: Northern District of State B.
Answer B: B: Northern District of State A or Southern District of State B.
Answer C: C: Northern District of State A, Southern District of State B. or Northern District of State B.
Answer D: D: Northern District of State A, Southern District of State B, or Western District of State C.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under 28 U.S.C. §1391, venue is appropriate in “a judicial district in which any defendant resides, if all defendants are residents of the State in which the district is located; a judicial district in which a substantial part of the events or omissions giving rise to the claim occurred, or a substantial part of property that is the subject of the action is situated; or if there is no district in which an action may otherwise be brought as provided in this section, any judicial district in which any defendant is subject to the court's personal jurisdiction with respect to such action.” Here, because the defendants are citizens of different states, venue is only appropriate in the district where a substantial portion of the events occurred. Thus, venue is only appropriate in the Northern District of State B, because that's where the copied paintings were created and sold. Therefore, B, C, and D are incorrect.
