Outline: Appointment and removal
Question: Congress creates a five-member trade commission. No more than three members may belong to the same political party. The commission conducts studies, adjudicates licensing disputes, and issues industry guidance. Commissioners serve staggered seven-year terms and may be removed by the President only for inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance. The President removes a commissioner solely because the commissioner disagrees with the President's preferred trade policy. What is the commissioner's strongest argument?
Answer A: The removal protection is likely valid because Congress may protect members of certain multimember expert commissions from at-will policy removal
Answer B: The removal protection is invalid because the President must be able to remove every federal officer at will
Answer C: The removal protection is invalid because commissions with any adjudicative function must be Article III courts
Answer D: The removal protection is valid only because the President personally appointed the commissioner
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts resemble the traditional multimember independent-commission setting, with staggered terms, bipartisan structure, and adjudicative or quasi-legislative functions. For-cause removal protection is more likely valid in that context.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Removal doctrine recognizes some valid for-cause limits, especially for certain multimember commissions. C: Administrative adjudication does not automatically require Article III status, and this question turns on removal. D: The validity of removal limits turns on structure and function, not merely on who appointed the officer.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may more readily provide for-cause removal protection for members of certain multimember bodies that perform quasi-legislative or quasi-adjudicative functions.
