Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Boaz owned a small house near Shiloh. He signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey the house to the pastor of Shiloh Church on this date.” On that date, Eli was the only pastor of Shiloh Church. Eli accepted the deed. Who owns the house?
Answer A: Boaz, because a deed must state the grantee's personal name.
Answer B: Eli, because the office description identified a specific person when the deed was delivered.
Answer C: Shiloh Church, because the office title automatically makes the church the grantee.
Answer D: Boaz, because Eli did not sign the deed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantee ascertainability. A grantee may be identified by a role or office if the person fitting that description is reasonably certain at delivery. Eli was the only pastor of Shiloh Church on that date, so the deed identifies him and title passes to Eli.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a personal name is not always required. C: C is wrong because the deed describes the current office holder, not the church as an entity. D: D is wrong because the grantee generally need not sign the deed to take title.
