Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: Paula, a citizen of Kansas, sues Driver, a citizen of Missouri, in federal court for $100,000 after a crash. Driver impleads Mechanic, a citizen of Kansas, for indemnity. Paula then asserts a related negligence claim directly against Mechanic arising from the same crash. Can Paula use supplemental jurisdiction for her claim against Mechanic?
Answer A: Yes, because the claim arises from the same crash
Answer B: Yes, because Rule 14 allows impleader
Answer C: No, because Paula is a plaintiff asserting a claim against a Rule 14 party in a diversity-only case and the claim would destroy diversity
Answer D: No, because defendants may never implead third parties in diversity cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1367(b) blocks this plaintiff claim against the nondiverse impleaded party.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1367(a) relatedness is not the end of the analysis in diversity-only cases. B: Rule 14 joinder does not itself create SMJ over Paula's claim. D: The impleader may be proper; Paula's direct claim is the jurisdictional problem.
lb_rule_reference: When original jurisdiction is based solely on diversity, supplemental jurisdiction does not extend to certain claims by plaintiffs against parties joined under Rules 14, 19, 20, or 24 if inconsistent with Section 1332.
