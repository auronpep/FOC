


BARMATRIX Q#: 21821
Outline_code: 64010101
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Assault
Question: The seller and the buyer entered into a valid written contract for the sale of the seller's home to the buyer. Subsequently, the seller's neighbor, the defendant, telephoned the seller and said, “If you don't back out of your contract with the buyer, there's going to be an accident and one of your children is going to be seriously hurt. Understand?” Before the seller had a chance to answer, the defendant hung up. The seller became so frightened by the defendant's threat that he suffered an immediate heart attack. If the seller asserts a claim against the defendant for assault, which of the following would be the defendant's LEAST effective argument in defense against that claim?
Answer A: A: The defendant's statement did not justify apprehension of immediate harm.
Answer B: B: The defendant told the seller that he could avoid harm by complying with a specified condition.
Answer C: C: The defendant's threat was not directed against the person of the seller.
Answer D: D: The defendant committed no physical act.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Since it was obvious from the defendant's statement that the threatened harm would not occur until sometime in the future, A would be an effective defense and is therefore incorrect. Since the threat was of harm to the seller's children and not to the seller, C would be an effective defense and is therefore incorrect. Courts have generally argued (although this argument is not always credible) that apprehension induced by the defendant's mere use of words is not reasonable. This has led to a rule that there can be no assault liability unless the defendant has performed some physical act. D is therefore an effective defense and is therefore incorrect.
