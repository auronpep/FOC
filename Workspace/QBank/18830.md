Question: Leah ordered custom display cases from Benjamin's cabinet shop for her retail store. The agreement allocated most of the price to the finished cases and a smaller amount to design consultation. It required “museum-grade acrylic fronts” but did not define that phrase. Benjamin delivered cases with scratch-resistant acrylic that was not UV-filtered. Leah refused to pay, arguing that “museum-grade” necessarily meant UV-filtered. Benjamin proved that, in the retail-display trade, “museum-grade acrylic” means scratch-resistant acrylic suitable for public display and does not ordinarily require UV filtering. If the usage is established and not otherwise negated by the writing, is Benjamin likely to recover the price?
Answer A: No, because any design consultation makes the transaction a services contract and bars UCC usage evidence.
Answer B: No, because a merger clause prevents all evidence about the meaning of “museum-grade.”
Answer C: Yes, because the predominant goods transaction permits established trade usage to explain the technical quality term, and Benjamin supplied goods matching that meaning.
Answer D: Yes, but only because Leah failed to prove damages from the missing UV filtering.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a predominantly goods transaction may use Article 2 meaning sources, including trade usage, to define technical quality terms. The trigger facts are the price allocation to the finished display cases, the undefined technical phrase “museum-grade acrylic,” and the established retail-display usage. The decisive locks are source law and term status: the usage defines the quality term, and under that meaning Benjamin performed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because incidental design consultation does not defeat the predominant goods route. B: B is wrong because integration does not bar UCC usage evidence used to explain a term. D: D is wrong because the question turns on contract meaning and performance under that meaning, not damages proof.
lb_rule_reference: UCC technical term / usage-defined quality
