Question: In a federal fraud case, Paul offers a screenshot of text messages from Peter. Paul testifies that the messages came from the number Peter had used for years, referred to facts only Peter and Paul knew, and were followed by Peter's delivery of the requested documents. Peter objects that only the phone company can authenticate the screenshot. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because screenshots can be authenticated only by a phone-company custodian.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because electronic messages are never documentary evidence.
Answer C: Overrule the objection if the circumstances are sufficient to support a finding that the messages are what Paul claims.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Peter admits sending the messages.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Electronic messages can be authenticated by circumstantial evidence such as phone number, distinctive contents, and subsequent conduct. A phone-company custodian or sender admission is not the exclusive method.
