Question: In a federal contract case, the court grants summary judgment for the plaintiff on the plaintiff's contract claim. The defendant's counterclaim for fraud from the same deal remains pending. The court does not enter Rule 54(b) certification. The defendant immediately files a notice of appeal. Is there a final appealable judgment?
Answer A: Yes, because any summary-judgment order is immediately appealable
Answer B: Yes, because counterclaims never affect appealability
Answer C: No, because the counterclaim remains unresolved and there is no Rule 54(b) certification or exception
Answer D: No, because contract cases cannot be appealed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The litigation has not ended as to all claims and parties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Summary judgment can be final only when it resolves the whole case or fits an exception. B: An unresolved counterclaim generally means claims remain pending. D: Contract cases can be appealed after a final judgment or proper exception.
lb_rule_reference: A final decision generally ends the litigation on the merits as to all claims and parties; unresolved counterclaims usually prevent immediate appeal absent Rule 54(b) certification or another exception.
