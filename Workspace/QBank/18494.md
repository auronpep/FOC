Question: Aaron signed a written contract to sell a house to Rebekah for $300,000. The writing identifies the parties, property, and price. Rebekah later claims Aaron also made a prior oral promise to include a separate tractor with the sale. Aaron argues that the oral promise is barred by the Statute of Frauds because the deal involves land. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The land Statute of Frauds makes the entire signed land contract void.
Answer B: The oral tractor promise is automatically enforceable because the land contract was signed.
Answer C: The Statute of Frauds is the wrong reason to reject the land sale itself because the land writing is sufficient; the oral tractor promise raises a parol-evidence/content question and possibly a separate goods/term issue.
Answer D: The oral tractor promise is enforceable only if Rebekah made improvements to the house.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The signed land contract satisfies the Statute of Frauds for the land sale. A prior oral promise about a separate tractor is not rejected simply because the land deal is within the SOF; the real issue is whether the oral term can be proved as part of the agreement under parol-evidence/content rules or another applicable route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the signed writing satisfies, rather than violates, the land SOF. B: B is wrong because a sufficient land writing does not automatically incorporate every prior oral side promise. D: D is wrong because improvements relate to land part performance, not enforcement of a separate oral tractor term.
