Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Elijah signed a deed describing his field and using present words of conveyance, but the grantee line was blank. Elijah handed the paper to Daniel only for safekeeping and gave Daniel no authority to fill in a name. After Elijah died, Daniel wrote his own name in the blank and recorded the paper. Who owns the field?
Answer A: Elijah's estate, because the deed did not identify a grantee when delivered and Daniel lacked authority to complete it.
Answer B: Daniel, because possession of a signed deed gives the holder authority to complete blanks.
Answer C: Daniel, because recording cured the blank grantee line.
Answer D: Daniel, because consideration was not required.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantee identity at delivery. A deed must identify an ascertainable grantee, or the completion must be authorized. Daniel had no authority to insert his name, and recording did not cure the missing grantee. Title remains in Elijah's estate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because mere possession of the paper does not create authority to complete it. C: C is wrong because recording does not validate an instrument lacking an authorized grantee. D: D is wrong because the no-consideration rule does not cure an unauthorized completion.
