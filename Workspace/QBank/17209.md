Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Foreseeable plaintiffs
Question: A train employee negligently bumped a passenger carrying a small, ordinary-looking package while helping him board. The package unexpectedly contained fireworks and exploded. A spectator standing far down the platform behind several barriers was startled by the sound, ran away, and tripped over her own bag. Under the common Palsgraf duty framing, is the spectator likely owed a negligence duty by the employee?
Answer A: Yes, because the spectator was injured after the employee acted negligently
Answer B: Yes, because negligence creates a duty to the whole world
Answer C: No, because the remote spectator was outside the foreseeable zone of risk before the package exploded
Answer D: No, because proximate cause is never relevant in Palsgraf-style questions
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The ordinary-looking package did not make a far spectator behind barriers a foreseeable plaintiff before the accident.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Duty is assessed before the accident by identifying foreseeable plaintiffs in the risk zone, not everyone later injured. B: Palsgraf rejects duty to unforeseeable remote plaintiffs under the Cardozo-style framing. D: Foreseeability can arise in duty or proximate cause. This question asks duty, so the reason must be the plaintiff's zone of risk.
lb_rule_reference: Under the exam-default Palsgraf framing, duty is owed to plaintiffs foreseeably within the zone of risk before the accident.
