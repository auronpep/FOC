Question: Peter signed and recorded a deed granting John “a perpetual easement to place and maintain one billboard on the west edge of Peter's land.” John owns no parcel that is benefited by the billboard location. What is the best classification of John's right?
Answer A: An easement appurtenant because all recorded easements must benefit land.
Answer B: An easement in gross because the right benefits John personally or commercially without a dominant parcel.
Answer C: A revocable license because John owns no neighboring parcel.
Answer D: A profit because the billboard will generate advertising revenue.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A recorded nonpossessory right to place and maintain a structure on another's land can be classified as an easement in gross when it benefits the holder rather than a dominant parcel. The commercial value of the billboard does not make it a profit.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because easements in gross need not benefit land. C: C is wrong because no dominant parcel does not automatically mean the right is revocable. D: D is wrong because a profit concerns removal of land resources, not revenue earned from a sign.
