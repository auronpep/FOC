Question: After a federal bench trial, the district judge issues findings that a witness delivered goods on a specific date and concludes that the delivery satisfied the contract. On appeal, the buyer challenges both the date finding and the legal conclusion about contract performance. Which appellate review framework is correct?
Answer A: Both the date finding and the legal conclusion are reviewed only for abuse of discretion
Answer B: Both the date finding and the legal conclusion are reviewed for clear error
Answer C: Both the date finding and the legal conclusion are reviewed de novo
Answer D: The date finding is reviewed for clear error, and the legal conclusion is reviewed de novo
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 52 fact findings receive clear-error review, while legal conclusions receive fresh appellate review.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Abuse of discretion is not the default framework for these bench-trial fact and law determinations. B: Clear-error review applies to factual findings, not legal conclusions. C: De novo review applies to legal conclusions, not ordinary fact findings.
lb_rule_reference: In a bench trial, the court must find facts and state conclusions of law; factual findings are reviewed for clear error, while legal conclusions are reviewed de novo.
