Question: Solomon signed a paper stating, “I agree to convey my farm to Nathan next month for fifty thousand dollars.” Nathan signed the paper too. No deed was delivered, and the paper did not purport to transfer title immediately. Before the closing date, who holds legal title to the farm?
Answer A: Nathan, because the signed paper satisfies the Statute of Frauds for a land transaction.
Answer B: Solomon, because the paper is a promise to convey later, not a deed making a present transfer of legal title.
Answer C: Nathan, because both parties signed the paper.
Answer D: Solomon only if he returns any deposit Nathan paid.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source asserted is a signed land-sale writing, but the decisive lock is whether the writing operates as a deed of present transfer. The paper promised a future conveyance next month and no deed was delivered. Nathan may have contract rights, but legal title remains with Solomon before the deed transfer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because satisfying the Statute of Frauds for a contract does not itself pass legal title. C: C is wrong because both signatures on a contract do not convert it into a deed. D: D is wrong because refund rights are separate from the title-transfer lock.
