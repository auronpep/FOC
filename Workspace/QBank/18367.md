Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Affirmative duties to act
Question: A delivery driver negligently backed into a bicyclist, knocking the bicyclist down a grassy slope into a drainage canal. The driver saw the bicyclist struggling in the water but drove away without calling emergency services or throwing the nearby rescue ring. The bicyclist sues the driver for negligence. Is the bicyclist likely to establish that the driver owed an affirmative duty in the negligence claim?
Answer A: No, because there is never a duty to rescue another person from danger
Answer B: Yes, because the driver created the peril and then failed to take reasonable steps to mitigate it
Answer C: Yes, because every driver owes a duty to rescue any person seen near water
Answer D: No, because the bicyclist's claim must be limited to the initial collision and cannot include the failure to help
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Risk creation is a recognized exception to the no-duty-to-rescue rule. Once the driver caused the bicyclist to enter the canal, the driver owed reasonable aid or mitigation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The driver was not a mere bystander. By negligently knocking the bicyclist into the canal, the driver created the peril and had a duty to use reasonable care to help mitigate it. C: The duty arises from the driver's creation of this danger, not from a universal duty of drivers to rescue people near water. D: The later omission matters because risk creation imposed a continuing duty to take reasonable mitigation steps after the bicyclist was in peril.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant who creates a risk of physical harm has a duty to use reasonable care to prevent or mitigate that harm.
