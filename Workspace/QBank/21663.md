Question: Abigail hired Joseph to provide “all event-staffing services reasonably required” for a summer fundraiser series. The signed services agreement listed the monthly fee, the event dates, and the categories of staff, but did not state the exact number of workers for each event. When the largest fundraiser required more servers than Joseph expected, Joseph argued that the agreement was too indefinite to enforce and that no outside planning materials could be considered. Which statement is least likely to be accepted?
Answer A: Because the agreement is for services, common-law interpretation rules rather than Article 2 gap-fillers generally control.
Answer B: Planning calendars and staffing charts may help explain the scope of “reasonably required” staffing if the term is ambiguous and the evidence is otherwise reliable.
Answer C: The lack of an exact worker count automatically makes the promise illusory, so the court must apply a UCC quantity gap-filler or refuse enforcement.
Answer D: The court should identify the meaning of the scope term before deciding whether Joseph failed to perform it.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a services agreement with an open or flexible scope term is not automatically illusory merely because it lacks a precise number. The trigger facts are the services source law, listed event dates and categories, and the phrase “reasonably required.” The decisive locks are source law and term status. C is least likely because it both overstates indefiniteness and rushes to UCC gap-filler reasoning in a services contract.
