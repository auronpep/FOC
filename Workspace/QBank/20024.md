Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Damages
Question: An accountant prepared a report for a company to use in internal budgeting. The report was not prepared for investors or a specific financing transaction. Months later, the company posted the report online. An investor unknown to the accountant read it, relied on an erroneous inventory number, and lost money buying the company's stock. Is the accountant likely liable to the investor for negligent misrepresentation?
Answer A: Yes, because it was foreseeable that someone might read the online report
Answer B: Yes, because accountants are strictly liable for inaccurate reports
Answer C: No, because the investor was not within a known or limited class for a specific transaction
Answer D: No, because pure economic loss is never recoverable for negligent misrepresentation
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The accountant prepared the report for internal budgeting, not for this investor or a limited group in a known transaction. Duty does not extend to every later reader.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The duty for professional information-supply economic loss is limited. The report was not prepared for this investor or a limited transaction class. B: The claim is negligence-based, and duty scope remains limited. D: Negligent misrepresentation can allow economic recovery in a limited information-supply relationship. This claim fails because the class is too open-ended.
lb_rule_reference: A professional information supplier's duty for economic loss is usually limited to a known or limited class and transaction, not all foreseeable investors who later read the information.
