Question: A contractor agreed to build shelves after Joshua selected one of three wood stains. Joshua was supposed to choose by Monday. He selected the stain on Tuesday morning. The one-day delay did not affect the contractor's schedule because the wood would not arrive until Friday. The contractor refused to perform, claiming Joshua's late selection discharged the contractor's duty. What is the likely result?
Answer A: The contractor is discharged because any missed date in a contract is automatically an express condition.
Answer B: The contractor is not discharged because the delay was nonmaterial and did not defeat the performance sequence.
Answer C: The contractor is discharged unless Joshua proves substantial performance of the selection duty by clear and convincing evidence.
Answer D: The contractor is discharged because selection of a stain is always a condition precedent to formation.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A minor delay in a cooperative step does not automatically discharge the other party's performance duty unless the timing was an express condition or the delay was material. Joshua's one-day delay had no effect on the contractor's schedule, so it was not a total discharge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because missed dates are not automatically strict express conditions. C: C is wrong because no clear-and-convincing burden applies. D: D is wrong because the contract was already formed; the issue is performance sequence.
lb_rule_reference: Constructive condition / nonmaterial delay
