MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A hiker, although acting with reasonable care, fell while attempting to climb a mountain and lay unconscious and critically injured on a ledge that was difficult to reach. The plaintiff, an experienced mountain climber, was himself seriously injured while trying to rescue the hiker. The plaintiff's rescue attempt failed, and the hiker died of his injuries before he could be reached. The plaintiff brought an action against the hiker's estate for compensation for his injuries. In this jurisdiction, the traditional common-law rules relating to contributory negligence and assumption of risk remain in effect. Will the plaintiff prevail in his action against the hiker's estate? Will the plaintiff prevail in his action against the hiker's estate?
Answer A: Yes, because his rescue attempt was reasonable.
Answer B: Yes, because the law should not discourage attempts to assist persons in helpless peril.
Answer C: No, because the hiker's peril did not arise from his own failure to exercise reasonable care.
Answer D: No, because the plaintiff's rescue attempt failed and therefore did not benefit the hiker.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. One who acts negligently and endangers himself is also liable for the resulting injuries of anyone who undertakes to rescue him. Here, however, the hiker was not negligent in creating his peril, and the plaintiff will not prevail.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. An action ending with a need to be rescued must have been negligent for the injured rescuer to have a claim against the estate. The negligence of a rescuer is generally a foreseeable result of the original negligent act, and the rescuer need not show that the rescue was reasonable. B is incorrect. This is the general policy reason behind statutes that grant immunity to doctors or nurses who render medical assistance at the scene of an accident. The statutes do not, as a matter of majority rules, create an affirmative duty to help emergency victims at the scene of an accident. They only absolve the medical professionals from liability for ordinary negligence. D is incorrect. One who acts negligently and endangers only himself is also liable for the resulting injuries of anyone who undertakes to rescue him. A failed rescue attempt would not cut short liability if the hiker had been negligent.
additional_info: The issue is the negligence doctrine for rescuers. A rescuer is a foreseeable plaintiff as long as the rescue is not reckless. A defendant is liable when he negligently puts himself or another in peril and the plaintiff is injured attempting a rescue. Here, the hiker is stated to have been acting with reasonable care, so he breached no duty to the plaintiff. The failed rescue fact does not change the result; if the hiker had negligently created the peril, failed rescue alone would not cut off liability.
