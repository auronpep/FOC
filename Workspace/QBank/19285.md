Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A homeowner sells an old lawn mower at a neighborhood garage sale. The homeowner does not sell mowers or other tools as a business. The mower has a hidden blade-guard defect, and the buyer is injured while using it normally. The buyer sues the homeowner for strict products liability. What is the homeowner's strongest defense?
Answer A: The homeowner is not a commercial product supplier
Answer B: The homeowner wins only if the buyer had no contract with the homeowner
Answer C: The homeowner is strictly liable because any seller of a defective product is covered
Answer D: The homeowner is strictly liable because the injury was physical rather than economic
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Strict products liability generally targets sellers and distributors engaged in the business of supplying the product, not casual sellers.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Contract privity is not the relevant gate; commercial supplier status is. C: A casual one-time sale is outside the usual strict-products chain. D: Physical injury matters, but the defendant must still be in the covered product-supply chain.
lb_rule_reference: Strict products liability generally applies to commercial suppliers in the distribution chain, not casual one-time sellers.
