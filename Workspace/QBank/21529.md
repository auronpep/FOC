Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Yolanda spent 14 years living and working in the State of Creston, where she owned a home. She recently accepted a two-year research contract at a university in the State of Palvern, subletting her Creston home. Yolanda moved her personal belongings to a Palvern apartment and updated her driver's license and voter registration to Palvern. She has stated to friends that she plans to return to Creston when her contract ends, though she has not made definitive plans. A creditor holding a judgment against Yolanda filed suit against her in Creston state court. Yolanda objects that Creston no longer has personal jurisdiction over her because she is now "based in Palvern." The creditor argues that Creston has general jurisdiction based on Yolanda's former domicile and connections to Creston. Does Creston have general personal jurisdiction over Yolanda?
Answer A: No, because Yolanda is physically present in Palvern and courts exercise jurisdiction over individuals where they are currently located
Answer B: Yes, but only because Yolanda's home in Creston gives her sufficient property contacts with the forum for in personam jurisdiction
Answer C: Yes, because Yolanda's domicile remains Creston — she has not formed the intent to remain in Palvern indefinitely, and her prior domicile continues until a new one is established with both presence and intent
Answer D: Yes, because Yolanda has been domiciled in both Creston and Palvern simultaneously, giving both states general jurisdiction over her
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Creston has general jurisdiction over Yolanda based on domicile. Although Yolanda is physically present in Palvern, domicile requires both physical presence and the intent to remain indefinitely. Yolanda has stated she intends to return to Creston when her two-year contract ends. This expressed intent prevents Palvern from becoming her new domicile. Her prior domicile in Creston continues in effect. Creston therefore retains general personal jurisdiction over Yolanda for any claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Domicile is not the same as current residence. Although physical presence is necessary to establish a new domicile, it must be accompanied by intent to remain indefinitely. Yolanda has stated she plans to return to Creston when her two-year contract ends — that expression of intent to return prevents Palvern from becoming her new domicile. Her domicile remains Creston until she forms the intent to make Palvern her indefinite home. Creston retains general jurisdiction based on domicile. B: General in personam jurisdiction over Yolanda in Creston is based on her domicile there, not her property ownership. Property ownership alone is not the basis for in personam general jurisdiction over an individual; it is relevant to in rem or quasi-in-rem jurisdiction over the property itself. The correct basis is domicile, and the property is merely confirming evidence of that domicile, not the jurisdictional hook. D: A person can have only one domicile at a time. Courts do not recognize simultaneous domicile in multiple states. The question of which state holds domicile — and therefore which has general jurisdiction — must be resolved by determining where the individual has physical presence plus intent to remain indefinitely. Here, that is Creston.
lb_rule_reference: A state has general personal jurisdiction over an individual who is domiciled there. Domicile requires physical presence in the state plus the intent to make it one's permanent or indefinite home. A person can have only one domicile at a time. Prior domicile continues until a new one is established. *Milliken v. Meyer* (1940).
