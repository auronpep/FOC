Question: Congress validly creates a federal cause of action that can be heard in state courts with ordinary civil jurisdiction. A state supreme court announces that hearing federal claims would commandeer state courts and that state judges may refuse to apply the federal law. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The state court may refuse because anti-commandeering lets every state institution ignore federal law
Answer B: State courts with jurisdiction must apply valid federal law; that is not barred by anti-commandeering
Answer C: State courts must apply federal law only when the governor consents
Answer D: State courts may hear federal claims only if Congress pays each judge directly
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The Supremacy Clause binds state judges to valid federal law.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: State courts are bound by valid federal law under the Supremacy Clause. C: Valid federal law does not depend on gubernatorial approval. D: The Supremacy Clause imposes the duty without a judge-specific payment condition.
lb_rule_reference: The anti-commandeering doctrine does not allow state courts to refuse to apply valid federal law; state courts are bound by the Supremacy Clause when they have jurisdiction.
