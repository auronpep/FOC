Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Damages
Question: A cyclist negligently clipped a parked food cart. No one was hurt, but the collision bent the cart's wheel and cracked a display panel. The cart owner paid for repair and sues the cyclist for negligence. The cyclist argues negligence damages require personal injury. Is the cyclist's argument correct?
Answer A: Yes, because negligence requires physical injury to a person
Answer B: Yes, because minor repair damage is treated as nominal
Answer C: No, because the cracked panel and bent wheel are compensable property damage
Answer D: No, because negligence does not require damages at all
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Negligence requires actual harm, and property damage satisfies that requirement even without bodily injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Property damage is actual harm and can support a negligence claim. B: Small actual loss is still actual loss; amount affects recovery size, not liability existence. D: The owner has damages here, but negligence still requires them.
lb_rule_reference: Small property damage can satisfy negligence's actual-harm element; the plaintiff need not prove bodily injury.
