Outline: Misrepresentation
Question: An accountant prepared a financial report for a company, knowing the company would give it to one named lender to secure a loan. The accountant carelessly overstated assets. The named lender relied on the report and made the loan, which defaulted. Is the lender likely within the class that can sue for negligent misrepresentation?
Answer A: Yes, because the accountant supplied information for this known lender's transaction
Answer B: Yes, because accountants owe negligence duties to the entire investing public
Answer C: No, because negligent misrepresentation never allows economic damages
Answer D: No, because only intentional fraud can involve financial statements
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The duty fits a limited, foreseeable user in a specific business context.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The duty is not unlimited. C: The tort specifically addresses certain economic reliance losses. D: Careless professional information can support negligent misrepresentation.
lb_rule_reference: A professional who supplies information for a known transaction may owe a duty to a limited class of intended users, but not to every remote market participant.
