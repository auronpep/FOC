Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Rebekah signed a complete and exclusive writing to buy 200 bronze lamps from Aaron at $30 per lamp. Before signing, Aaron orally promised to include free lamp oil with every lamp. The writing did not mention oil. Aaron delivered the lamps but no oil. Rebekah sues for damages for failure to deliver oil. If Aaron objects to the oral-oil promise under the parol evidence rule, what is Rebekah's likely result on the oil claim?
Answer A: Rebekah recovers because the oral promise is consistent with the lamp sale.
Answer B: Rebekah recovers because the oral promise was made by the seller.
Answer C: Rebekah recovers only if she proves the oil was worth more than the lamps.
Answer D: Rebekah does not recover on the oil promise because the complete and exclusive writing bars the prior consistent additional term.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The call asks the legal result on a claim based on an oral promise. A complete and exclusive integration bars a prior consistent additional term, so the oil promise cannot supply a contractual duty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because consistency is insufficient when the writing is complete and exclusive. B: B is wrong because a party's own promise can still be barred by the parol evidence rule. C: C is wrong because the value of the oil does not solve the integration problem.
