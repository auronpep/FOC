Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: Paul sues Lydia for property damage from a car crash and obtains a final judgment. Before judgment, Paul knew he also had medical bills from the same crash, but he chose not to include them. Paul later files a second suit against Lydia seeking those medical expenses. What is the best result?
Answer A: The second suit is allowed because medical expenses are a different category of damages
Answer B: The second suit is barred because the medical-expense claim could have been raised in the first action
Answer C: The second suit is allowed unless the medical bills were actually litigated
Answer D: The second suit is barred only if Paul and Lydia are citizens of different states
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Claim preclusion prevents claim splitting after a valid final judgment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Different damages from the same transaction generally must be brought together. C: Actual litigation is an issue-preclusion element, not a claim-preclusion requirement. D: Preclusion does not turn on diversity citizenship.
lb_rule_reference: Claim preclusion bars matters arising out of the same transaction that were or could have been raised before judgment.
