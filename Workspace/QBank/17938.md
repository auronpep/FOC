Question: A negligent truck driver struck a bicyclist at an intersection. A nearby pedestrian, who did not know the bicyclist and was not physically endangered, watched the collision and suffered severe emotional distress with sleeplessness. The pedestrian sues the driver for bystander NIED. Is the pedestrian likely to recover as a bystander?
Answer A: Yes, because the pedestrian personally witnessed a serious injury
Answer B: Yes, because physical symptoms followed the distress
Answer C: No, because the pedestrian was unrelated and outside the recognized bystander category
Answer D: No, because emotional distress is never foreseeable to negligent drivers
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The pedestrian was neither physically endangered nor closely related to the injured bicyclist. The bystander NIED gate is not met.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Bystander NIED generally requires a close family relationship with the injured person. The pedestrian was unrelated. B: Physical symptoms do not substitute for the close-family relationship requirement. D: Emotional distress may be foreseeable and recoverable in recognized categories; this plaintiff loses because the category limits are missing.
lb_rule_reference: An unrelated bystander who witnesses another person's injury generally cannot recover under the bystander NIED rule merely because the scene was traumatic.
