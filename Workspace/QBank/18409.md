Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: An employee files a state-law benefits claim in state court. A federal statute completely preempts that category of benefits claim and provides the exclusive federal civil enforcement action. The employer removes to federal court, arguing that the claim is necessarily federal despite the state-law label. Does the federal court have federal-question jurisdiction?
Answer A: No, because the complaint uses only state-law labels
Answer B: No, because preemption is always only a defense
Answer C: Yes, if the federal statute completely preempts the field and supplies the exclusive federal cause of action
Answer D: Yes, but only if the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Complete preemption transforms the state-labeled claim into a federal claim for Section 1331 purposes.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Complete preemption is a narrow exception that can recharacterize the claim as federal despite state-law labels. B: Ordinary preemption is defensive, but complete preemption is jurisdictional when the federal statute supplies the exclusive cause of action. D: The amount threshold belongs to diversity jurisdiction, not federal-question jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: If federal law completely preempts a field and supplies the exclusive federal cause of action, a state-law complaint within that field may arise under federal law for jurisdictional purposes.
