Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: In a federal fraud trial, the prosecutor offers a hotel receipt bearing Gabriel's name to show that Gabriel was in the city on the night of the fraud. The prosecutor says a later witness will testify that Gabriel personally checked into that hotel. Gabriel objects that the receipt is irrelevant unless that later connection is made. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Admit the receipt subject to later evidence sufficient to support the connection, and strike or limit it if the connection is not made.
Answer B: Exclude the receipt permanently because the connection has not yet been proved.
Answer C: Require the prosecutor to prove the connection beyond a reasonable doubt before the jury sees the receipt.
Answer D: Admit the receipt unconditionally because any document bearing a party's name is automatically relevant.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When relevance depends on a conditional fact, the court may admit evidence subject to later proof of the connection. If the proponent fails to supply evidence sufficient for the jury to find the condition, the court can strike or limit the evidence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because conditional evidence may be admitted subject to connection. C: C is wrong because the judge does not require proof beyond a reasonable doubt at this gate. D: D is wrong because the document still needs a relevant connection to the party and event.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b)
