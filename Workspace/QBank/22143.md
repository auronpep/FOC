Outline_code: 31010400
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Witnesses
Question: A chef stabbed another man, claiming that the other man had sexually assaulted his wife. During the chef's trial, the prosecutors played a tape for the jury because the wife was too scared to appear in court. The tape was a recording of the wife's statement to the police describing what had happened before the stabbing, including her recollection of statements made by the chef. The wife's statement contradicted the chef's argument that he had stabbed the man in defense of his wife. Because it was pre- recorded, the chef could not cross- examine his wife's statement. The jury convicted the chef of assault. The chef appealed, arguing that the playing of the tape recording violated his constitutional rights. Were the chef's constitutional rights violated?
Answer A: A: Yes, because he could not cross- examine the recorded statement.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the tape included the wife's recollections of statements made by the chef.
Answer C: C: No, because the tape recording was made by the police.
Answer D: D: No, because the wife was too scared to appear in court.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Playing out- of- court testimony to a jury, with no chance for cross- examination, violates a defendant's Sixth Amendment right to confront the witnesses against him or her. See Crawford v. Washington, 541 U.S. 36 (2004). Consequently, the playing of the wife's recorded statement was unconstitutional. B is incorrect because the playing of the tape was not unconstitutional based on its contents. C and D are incorrect because the playing of the tape violated the chef's constitutional rights.
