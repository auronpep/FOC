Question: Officer Chen lawfully arrests Mateo for shoplifting. Mateo does not resist. After handcuffing Mateo, Chen repeatedly strikes Mateo with a baton. Mateo kicks Chen once in the shin to stop the blows and then stops. Mateo is charged with battery on an officer. What is Mateo's best argument?
Answer A: No defense, because a suspect may never use force against an officer during arrest
Answer B: No defense, because Chen had probable cause for shoplifting
Answer C: Self-defense, because Mateo used limited force to stop excessive force after he was handcuffed
Answer D: Self-defense to the shoplifting charge as well as the battery charge
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Mateo was already handcuffed and nonresistant when Chen repeatedly struck him. Mateo's single kick was limited and aimed at stopping excessive force. Self-defense can apply to that battery charge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A lawful arrest does not authorize excessive force. A suspect may use reasonable force to defend against excessive force. B: The legality of the arrest does not answer whether Chen used excessive force after Mateo was handcuffed. D: Excessive force may justify Mateo's responsive kick, but it does not erase the separate shoplifting offense.
lb_rule_reference: A suspect generally may not use force to resist a lawful arrest, but may use reasonable force to defend against excessive force
