Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: A public high school assistant principal receives a reliable teacher report that a student is selling prescription pills from a backpack during school hours. The assistant principal searches only the student's backpack and a small pill bottle inside it, finding pills. Police were not directing the search. Was the school search reasonable under the Fourth Amendment?
Answer A: Yes, because the search was justified at inception and reasonably limited to places where pills could be found
Answer B: No, because public school officials always need a warrant before searching student property
Answer C: Yes, because schools may search any student anywhere for any reason
Answer D: No, because the teacher report had to establish probable cause
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: In the school context, officials need reasonableness, not a warrant and probable cause. The reliable report justified the search, and the backpack and pill bottle fit the suspected item.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: School searches by school officials are governed by a reasonableness standard, not the ordinary warrant requirement. C: The search still must be justified at inception and reasonable in scope. D: The public school reasonableness standard does not require probable cause for a school official's search.
lb_rule_reference: A public school search by school officials is valid when justified at inception and reasonably related in scope to the school's legitimate objectives.
