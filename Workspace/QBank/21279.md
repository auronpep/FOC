Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: In a federal fraud trial, the plaintiff offers a letter allegedly written by Jonah. The plaintiff calls Rachel, who had never seen Jonah's handwriting before the lawsuit. Rachel testifies that counsel showed her ten authenticated samples of Jonah's writing last week and that she now recognizes the letter as Jonah's. Jonah objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the letter because Rachel compared it with authenticated samples.
Answer B: Exclude Rachel's nonexpert opinion because her familiarity with Jonah's handwriting was acquired for the litigation.
Answer C: Admit the letter because any person may authenticate handwriting after studying examples.
Answer D: Exclude the letter only if Jonah denies writing it.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A nonexpert handwriting opinion must be based on familiarity that was not acquired for the current litigation. Rachel's familiarity was created by counsel's litigation preparation, so her nonexpert opinion is not a proper foundation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a trier or expert may compare authenticated samples, but Rachel's nonexpert opinion is based only on litigation-acquired familiarity. C: C is wrong because Rule 901 does not permit any lay witness to create familiarity for litigation. D: D is wrong because the foundation problem exists regardless of Jonah's denial.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(2) / familiarity acquired for litigation insufficient
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the litigation-acquired familiarity defect in handwriting authentication.
