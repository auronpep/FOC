Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: While serving a sentence, Felix agreed to meet with two officers in a prison conference room about an unrelated theft. He was told the meeting was voluntary, was not restrained beyond ordinary prison security, and could return to his unit whenever he wanted. After twenty minutes, Felix admitted selling stolen tools. No Miranda warnings were given. Is Felix's statement suppressible solely because he was already in prison?
Answer A: Yes, because a prisoner is always in Miranda custody for every police question
Answer B: Yes, because the officers asked about a new crime
Answer C: No, because ordinary incarceration without added restraint does not automatically create Miranda custody for the voluntary interview
Answer D: No, because incarcerated people have no Fifth Amendment rights
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Felix was already incarcerated, but the interview was voluntary and did not impose additional arrest-like pressure. Miranda warnings were not required solely due to prison status.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The custody inquiry for prisoners asks whether the interview added restraints beyond ordinary incarceration. The facts show a voluntary, noncoercive meeting. B: Interrogation alone is not enough. Miranda requires custody for the new interrogation setting. D: Prisoners retain Fifth Amendment rights. The specific custody threshold is not met here.
lb_rule_reference: For a prisoner, Miranda custody requires restraints beyond ordinary incarceration that create interrogation pressure comparable to formal arrest for the new matter.
