MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Foreseeable plaintiffs
Question: A science teacher negligently left a portable burner on after class in a laboratory next to a busy school hallway. A small stack of paper towels began smoking. A parent visiting the office walked past the lab door, inhaled smoke, and fell while coughing. The parent sues the teacher for negligence. Is the parent likely to establish that the teacher owed a duty in the negligence claim?
Answer A: No, because the burner was left in a classroom and the parent was not a student
Answer B: Yes, because a hallway visitor was foreseeably endangered by smoke from the unattended burner
Answer C: No, because duty depends on whether the exact coughing fall was foreseeable
Answer D: Yes, because every negligent act in a school creates a duty to all visitors anywhere on campus
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Before the accident, it was foreseeable that smoke or fire from a burner left on beside a school hallway could harm persons passing nearby, including a visiting parent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Smoke from an unattended burner next to a busy hallway foreseeably endangers people walking nearby, including visitors. The duty field is not limited to the intended users of the room. C: Duty asks whether the plaintiff or class was foreseeably endangered, not whether the exact mechanism of injury was predicted. The parent was in the risk field. D: The parent wins on duty because she was near the smoke hazard, not because the teacher owed a duty to every visitor anywhere on campus for every negligent act.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff need not be the closest target of the risk; duty extends to persons foreseeably endangered by the negligent conduct before the accident.
