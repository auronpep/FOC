


BARMATRIX Q#: 22590
Outline_code: 43020201
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Judicial Review
Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: A city enacted a “Clean Truck” program that required trucking companies to place a placard on each truck providing a phone number to call with safety or environmental complaints. An association of trucking companies from across the country sued the city in federal court, arguing that the new rules were unfair and unconstitutional. The city did not enforce the rules during the association's litigation, but the rules were ultimately upheld. After the rules were upheld, the association filed a suit to prevent the city from penalizing trucking companies for not having the placards on their trucks while the litigation was pending. The city moved to dismiss the claim. How should the court rule?
Answer A: A: Deny the motion, because Article I, Section 10 prohibits ex post facto laws.
Answer B: B: Deny the motion, because the city's rule affects interstate commerce.
Answer C: C: Grant the motion, because the claim is not ripe for decision.
Answer D: D: Grant the motion, because no state court has yet made a decision on the city's new rules.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal courts avoid issuing advisory opinions. Consequently, before issuing a decision, courts require a claim to be ripe. If a claim is based on events that have not occurred yet, the claim is not ripe and the court will not issue a decision. American Trucking Association, Inc. v. Los Angeles, 569 U.S. 641 (2013). Here, since the city has not yet enforced its new rules and may never decide to do so retroactively, the court should grant the motion because the association's claim is not ripe for decision. A and B are incorrect because the association's claim is not yet ripe for decision. D is incorrect because the federal courts are not required to wait until the state courts have acted before determining the constitutionality of a state statute.
