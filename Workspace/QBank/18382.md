Question: Congress creates a new optional environmental program. A state may join the new program only if it adopts federal monitoring rules. Congress also provides that any state refusing the new program will lose all of its long-standing federal healthcare funding, a large share of the state budget. What is the state's strongest constitutional argument?
Answer A: The condition may be coercive because Congress threatens a massive existing funding stream to force acceptance of a new program
Answer B: The condition is valid because states always remain free to reject federal funds
Answer C: The condition is invalid because Congress may never fund state programs
Answer D: The condition is valid only if it is enacted under the Commerce Clause
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Conditional spending can cross from inducement into coercion when the financial threat leaves states no real choice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A formally optional choice can become coercive when the financial pressure is extreme. C: Congress may spend for the general welfare and attach valid conditions. D: The relevant power is spending, and spending has its own coercion limit.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may encourage state action through conditional spending, but financial pressure can become unconstitutional coercion when states have no real choice.
