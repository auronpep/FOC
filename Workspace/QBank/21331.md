Question: A bus driver negligently slammed the doors on a passenger's arm. The passenger suffered a sprain and later experienced anxiety about riding buses. The bus company argues the anxiety is not recoverable unless the passenger satisfies the jurisdiction's standalone negligent-infliction-of-emotional-distress test. What is the best response?
Answer A: The anxiety is automatically barred because emotional distress is never recoverable in negligence
Answer B: The anxiety may be considered as parasitic damages if caused by the physical injury
Answer C: The anxiety is recoverable only if the passenger witnessed a close relative being injured
Answer D: The anxiety is recoverable only if the driver intended to cause distress
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The passenger has a physical injury, so related emotional distress can be part of compensatory damages without using the standalone NIED gate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Emotional distress may be recovered as part of damages for a physical tort injury. C: This plaintiff was directly physically injured, not merely a bystander. D: This is a negligence damages issue attached to physical injury, not an IIED claim.
lb_rule_reference: Emotional distress damages may be recovered as part of damages for a physical tort injury without satisfying standalone NIED limits.
