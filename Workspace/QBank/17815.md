Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff sues Officer in federal court under a federal civil-rights statute for an arrest. Plaintiff also asserts a state-law battery claim against Officer and City arising from the same arrest. City and Plaintiff are citizens of the same state. Defendants argue that Section 1367(b) bars the state-law claim against City. Is Section 1367(b) the correct bar?
Answer A: Yes, because Section 1367(b) bars every claim against a nondiverse additional party
Answer B: Yes, because supplemental jurisdiction never reaches claims against additional parties
Answer C: No, because the federal civil-rights claim supplies a federal-question anchor and the state battery claim arises from the same arrest
Answer D: No, but only because City consents to federal jurisdiction
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: With a federal-question anchor, Section 1367(b) does not impose the diversity plaintiff-side bar.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1367(b)'s listed limits apply to cases founded solely on diversity, not federal-question anchors. B: Supplemental jurisdiction can include related claims involving additional parties. D: Parties cannot create subject-matter jurisdiction by consent.
lb_rule_reference: When original jurisdiction is based on a federal question, related state-law claims against additional parties may fall within supplemental jurisdiction if they form part of the same case or controversy.
