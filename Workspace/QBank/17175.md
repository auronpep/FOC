Outline: Freedom of speech and expression
Question: A public school opens classrooms after hours for community groups teaching "character development for children." It allows secular groups to discuss honesty and kindness but denies a church group's application because the group teaches the same topics from a religious perspective. Is the denial likely constitutional?
Answer A: Yes, because limited forums allow any religious exclusion
Answer B: Yes, because religion is always a subject the government may exclude from any forum
Answer C: No, because the school excluded a religious viewpoint on an otherwise permitted topic
Answer D: No, because the school must open classrooms for every topic once it opens them for one topic
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The school opened the forum to character-development speech and then excluded a perspective because it was religious.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Limited forums still prohibit viewpoint discrimination. B: When the permitted subject is character development, excluding the religious perspective on that subject is viewpoint discrimination. D: A limited forum may impose reasonable subject limits; the defect is viewpoint exclusion within the allowed subject.
lb_rule_reference: Viewpoint discrimination is presumptively unconstitutional even in a limited forum; religious perspective on an allowed topic is not automatically excludable.
