Question: Congress makes it a federal offense to intentionally damage aircraft used in interstate passenger service, even when the damage occurs while the aircraft is parked overnight at a local airport. A defendant argues the conduct is local vandalism. Why is the statute likely within Congress's commerce power?
Answer A: Aircraft used in interstate passenger service are instrumentalities of interstate commerce that Congress may protect
Answer B: Congress may regulate any local crime that could be committed near an airport
Answer C: The statute is valid only if the defendant intended to cross state lines
Answer D: The statute is invalid because vandalism is always noneconomic local activity
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Congress can protect interstate instrumentalities from local threats.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The valid category is protection of the aircraft as an instrumentality, not a general federal criminal power near airports. C: Congress may protect instrumentalities from intrastate threats without proving the defendant intended interstate travel. D: The instrumentality category does not require the defendant's act to be economic.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may regulate and protect the instrumentalities of interstate commerce, and persons or things in interstate commerce, even when the threat comes from intrastate activity.
