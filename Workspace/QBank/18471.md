Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Right to Counsel
Question: Patel was arrested and charged by information with felony assault. A magistrate scheduled a preliminary hearing to determine whether probable cause existed to proceed to trial. Patel told the magistrate she could not afford a lawyer and asked for appointed counsel. The magistrate denied the request, reasoning that the preliminary hearing was not a formal trial proceeding and that the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had not yet attached. Patel proceeded through the preliminary hearing without a lawyer; the magistrate found probable cause, and Patel was bound over for trial. Was Patel entitled to appointed counsel at the preliminary hearing?
Answer A: No, because the Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches only at arraignment
Answer B: No, because the preliminary hearing is equivalent to a grand jury proceeding, and no right to counsel exists there
Answer C: Yes, because a preliminary hearing is a critical stage of the prosecution at which the Sixth Amendment right to counsel applies
Answer D: Yes, but only if the prosecution actually used evidence from the preliminary hearing at trial
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under *Coleman v. Alabama* (1970), a preliminary hearing constitutes a critical stage because the presence of skilled counsel could, inter alia, expose weaknesses in the prosecution's case through cross-examination, preserve testimony, and otherwise substantially affect the outcome. An indigent defendant therefore has a right to appointed counsel. The magistrate's denial was constitutional error.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Supreme Court has held that the right to counsel attaches at all critical stages of the prosecution. A preliminary hearing — where the magistrate determines probable cause and where the opportunity exists to cross-examine witnesses and shape the record — is a critical stage under *Coleman v. Alabama* (1970), regardless of whether it occurs before or after arraignment. B: A preliminary hearing is fundamentally different from a grand jury. The preliminary hearing is an adversarial proceeding before a neutral magistrate where the defendant may cross-examine witnesses; the grand jury is an ex parte body with no right to counsel inside the room. *Coleman v. Alabama* specifically recognized preliminary hearings as critical stages. D: The right to counsel at a critical stage does not depend on whether evidence from that stage was later used at trial. The constitutional protection exists at the time of the proceeding, and denial of counsel at a critical stage is structural error requiring reversal without a showing of specific prejudice.
lb_rule_reference: A preliminary hearing to determine probable cause is a critical stage of the prosecution at which the defendant has a Sixth Amendment right to counsel; the right to counsel does not extend to grand jury proceedings
