Question: In a federal products case, Sarah retained Daniel as a testifying engineering expert. Before serving Daniel's final expert report, Sarah's lawyer exchanged marked-up draft reports with Daniel. The opposing party requests all drafts solely to see how Daniel's wording changed. Are the draft reports discoverable merely because Daniel will testify?
Answer A: No, draft reports or disclosures are protected trial-preparation materials regardless of the form of the draft.
Answer B: Yes, every draft of a testifying expert's report is discoverable.
Answer C: Yes, but only drafts written by Daniel personally are discoverable.
Answer D: No, but only because expert reports are attorney-client communications.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 26(b)(4)(B) protects drafts of required expert reports or disclosures regardless of form. A testifying expert's final opinions and bases may be discovered through the required report and deposition, but drafts are protected. The decisive axis is expert draft protection.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A is wrong because Correct answer B is wrong because This ignores the specific draft-report protection. C is wrong because The protection is not limited by who typed the draft. D is wrong because The rule is trial-preparation protection, not attorney-client privilege.
