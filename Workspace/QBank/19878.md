Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Trial judge’s discretion to control order and presentation of evidence
Question: In a federal civil trial, Lydia calls Peter to testify that he saw a truck run a red light. After Peter is excused, Lydia asks to recall him only so he can repeat the same testimony because she thinks the jury “did not seem impressed.” The defendant objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: The court must allow Peter to be recalled because Lydia has the burden of persuasion.
Answer B: The court must refuse because a witness may never be recalled after being excused.
Answer C: The court must allow the recall unless the defendant proves Peter will change his testimony.
Answer D: The court may refuse the recall as needless repetition and waste of time.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The trial judge may control the presentation of evidence and avoid wasting time. A request to recall a witness only to repeat the same testimony may be denied as cumulative.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because carrying the burden of persuasion does not entitle a party to needless repetition. B: B is wrong because recall is not categorically forbidden. C: C is wrong because the issue is not whether the testimony will change, but whether the repeat testimony is necessary or wasteful.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / recall and repetition
