Question: Deborah offers to pay Samuel $3,000 to design a garden. The offer states, “You may accept only by sending me a written promise by Friday.” Samuel does not send a promise. Instead, on Thursday he spends two hours sketching a design and mails Deborah the sketch. Deborah refuses to proceed. Was Deborah's offer accepted?
Answer A: Yes, because Samuel conferred a benefit by beginning design work.
Answer B: No, because the offer unambiguously required acceptance by a written promise, and Samuel did not provide that promise.
Answer C: Yes, because any reliance on an offer is enough to accept it.
Answer D: No, but only because Samuel's sketch was not detailed enough.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An offeror may prescribe the method of acceptance. Deborah's offer allowed acceptance only by a written promise by Friday. Samuel's performance did not satisfy that prescribed method.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because benefit from preparatory work does not override the offer's exclusive acceptance method. C: C is wrong because reliance is not the same as acceptance, and promissory estoppel would require a separate showing. D: D is wrong because the decisive point is not the quality of the sketch but failure to use the required acceptance method.
lb_rule_reference: common_law
lb_student_diagnostic: Student ignored the offeror's power to prescribe the acceptance method.
