Question: John bought a “winterized trailer package” from a manufacturer under a signed goods contract. The contract did not define “winterized.” The court admits established trade-usage evidence that, in the trailer industry, “winterized trailer package” means insulated water lines and underfloor heat tape. The manufacturer delivered a trailer with a portable space heater but without insulated water lines or heat tape. Assuming the court adopts the trade meaning, what is the likely legal effect?
Answer A: The manufacturer performed because the word “winterized” appears in the writing and the trailer included some heating device.
Answer B: The trade usage affects only admissibility and cannot define whether the delivered trailer conformed.
Answer C: The manufacturer likely failed to provide the agreed winterized package because the adopted trade meaning required insulated water lines and heat tape.
Answer D: The buyer must lose unless the Statute of Frauds separately lists every winterizing component.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that established UCC trade usage can give meaning to a technical goods-contract term, and once adopted, that meaning becomes the benchmark for conformity. The trigger facts are the undefined “winterized trailer package,” the admitted trade meaning, and the delivery without the required components. The decisive lock is call type: the question asks legal effect after meaning is adopted. Under that meaning, a portable heater does not satisfy the agreed package.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the mere presence of the word “winterized” and a different heating device does not satisfy the adopted trade meaning. B: B is wrong because usage evidence can define the agreement's content, not merely pass through an evidentiary gate. D: D is wrong because the issue is meaning and conformity, not Statute of Frauds writing sufficiency.
