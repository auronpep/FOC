Question: Noah started a fistfight with Sam outside a bar. After exchanging punches, Noah stepped back, raised his hands, and said, "I'm done. I'm leaving." Sam then pulled a knife and rushed toward Noah. Noah drew a lawfully carried pistol and fired once, killing Sam. May Noah claim self-defense?
Answer A: No, because Noah started the fight and an initial aggressor can never use self-defense
Answer B: No, because Noah used a gun in response to a fistfight
Answer C: Yes, because Noah withdrew, communicated withdrawal, and then faced an imminent deadly threat
Answer D: Yes, but only because Sam was the person who ultimately died
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Noah initially lost the privilege by starting the fight, but he stepped back and clearly said he was leaving. Sam then escalated by rushing with a knife. Noah may claim deadly self-defense against that imminent threat.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: An initial aggressor can regain self-defense by withdrawing and communicating withdrawal. Noah did both before Sam escalated with a knife. B: The relevant threat changed when Sam drew a knife and rushed Noah after withdrawal. Deadly force may be proportionate to an imminent knife attack. D: Self-defense depends on threat, necessity, proportionality, and aggressor status at the time of force. The identity of the person who died does not itself create the defense.
lb_rule_reference: An initial aggressor generally loses self-defense, but may regain it by withdrawing from the conflict and clearly communicating that withdrawal; if the other person then threatens deadly force, defensive force may be justified.
