MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff, a citizen of Georgia, files a diversity action against a defendant, a citizen of Florida, claiming $90,000 in damages arising from a car accident. The defendant files an answer and a compulsory counterclaim for $20,000, asserting that the plaintiff was actually at fault in the same accident and owes the defendant damages for injuries the defendant sustained. The plaintiff moves to dismiss the counterclaim, arguing that $20,000 does not independently satisfy the $75,000 AIC requirement. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. Should the court dismiss the defendant's counterclaim?
Answer A: Yes, because every claim in a federal court action must independently satisfy the amount-in-controversy threshold for the court to exercise jurisdiction over it
Answer B: No, because the defendant's compulsory counterclaim arises from the same car accident as the main claim, satisfying § 1367(a), and the § 1367(b) limitation does not bar defendant counterclaims
Answer C: Yes, because even with supplemental jurisdiction, the counterclaim must at least partially satisfy the AIC by meeting a proportionate floor — here $20,000 is less than 50% of the $75,000 threshold and cannot qualify
Answer D: No, but only because the parties are completely diverse (Georgia vs. Florida) and the counterclaim is between the same parties, so § 1332 independently provides jurisdiction over the counterclaim
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1367(a) grants supplemental jurisdiction over all claims that share a common nucleus of operative fact with the anchor claim. The defendant's counterclaim arises from the same accident that gave rise to the plaintiff's claim — the same nucleus of operative facts. Section 1367(b) limits claims by plaintiffs in diversity cases; it does not restrict defendant counterclaims. The court has supplemental jurisdiction over the counterclaim without independent AIC, and the motion to dismiss should be denied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1367(a) provides supplemental jurisdiction over related claims, including compulsory counterclaims, that share a common nucleus of operative fact with the anchor claim. The defendant's compulsory counterclaim arises from the same car accident as the main claim — it shares a common nucleus. Supplemental jurisdiction under § 1367(a) applies; independent AIC is not required. C: There is no proportionality floor for supplemental claims. Section 1367(a) contains no minimum dollar amount. A compulsory counterclaim for $1 shares a common nucleus with the anchor claim and qualifies for supplemental jurisdiction as fully as one for $20,000. D: While it is true that the parties are diverse, the counterclaim for $20,000 does not independently satisfy § 1332's $75,000 threshold. Section 1332 does not support the counterclaim independently. The correct basis is § 1367(a) supplemental jurisdiction — the common-nucleus relationship to the anchor claim. Using § 1332 as the stated basis would require the counterclaim to meet $75,000, which it does not.
lb_rule_reference: A Rule 13(a) compulsory counterclaim that arises from the same transaction as the main claim falls within § 1367(a) supplemental jurisdiction; § 1367(b) does not bar defendant counterclaims.
