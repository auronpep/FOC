Outline: Opinion or reputation
Question: In a federal trial, Timothy testifies for the plaintiff. The defendant calls Silas, who testifies that Timothy has a reputation for being untruthful. The plaintiff then calls Lydia to testify that, in her opinion, Timothy is truthful. The defendant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude Lydia's opinion because only reputation evidence may support truthfulness.
Answer B: Exclude Lydia's opinion because Timothy himself must deny Silas's testimony first.
Answer C: Admit Lydia's opinion because Timothy's character for truthfulness has been attacked and opinion testimony is a permitted form of support.
Answer D: Admit Lydia's opinion only if she also describes specific truthful acts by Timothy.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: After a witness's character for truthfulness has been attacked, FRE 608(a) permits support by reputation or opinion evidence. Lydia's opinion testimony is a proper form of rehabilitation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because opinion as well as reputation is allowed. B: B is wrong because a rehabilitating character witness may respond after the attack. D: D is wrong because specific truthful acts are not the proper form of direct support under Rule 608(a).
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(a) / opinion or reputation after attack
