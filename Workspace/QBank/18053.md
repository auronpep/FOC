Question: A public school allows student clubs to meet after school to discuss community service. It denies a religious student club access because the club wants to discuss community service from a faith-based perspective. What is the strongest First Amendment argument for the club?
Answer A: Excluding the club because of its religious perspective on an allowed subject is viewpoint discrimination
Answer B: The school may exclude all religious speech from any limited public forum
Answer C: The club must be excluded to avoid an Establishment Clause violation
Answer D: The club wins only if it can prove every student shares the same faith
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Religion can be a viewpoint on a permitted subject; excluding it on that basis is impermissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Subject limits do not permit viewpoint discrimination against religious perspectives on allowed topics. C: Equal access for private student speech does not automatically establish religion. D: First Amendment protection does not depend on religious majority status.
lb_rule_reference: When government opens a limited public forum for a subject, it may impose reasonable subject limits but may not exclude religious viewpoints on an otherwise permitted subject.
