Outline: Presumptions and inferences
Question: Abigail sues Ezra in federal court for a warehouse fire. Abigail proves that Ezra removed his tools from the warehouse the night before the fire and that he gave inconsistent explanations for doing so. Abigail requests an instruction that the jury may infer Ezra anticipated the fire. Ezra objects that the instruction is improper unless Abigail proves by clear and convincing evidence that he actually anticipated the fire. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because any inference from Ezra's conduct is an overbroad objection slogan.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the court must apply California inference law in every Evidence question.
Answer C: Overrule the objection if the evidence rationally supports the permissive inference; the jury decides what weight to give it.
Answer D: Sustain the objection unless the judge personally finds that Ezra anticipated the fire.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A permissive inference does not require the judge to find the inferred fact by clear and convincing evidence. If the evidence rationally supports the inference, the jury may consider it and decide weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the objection should be assessed on the inference's rational support, not rejected by slogan. B: B is wrong because the federal default controls this batch unless the question says otherwise. D: D is wrong because the judge does not decide the ultimate inferred fact.
