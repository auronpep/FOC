Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: A city awards construction contracts under a program that a resident believes uses unconstitutional racial preferences. The resident does not own a construction business, has never applied for a city contract, and does not allege that the program denied him any opportunity. He sues, alleging that the existence of the program stigmatizes all city residents. What is the strongest standing argument for dismissal?
Answer A: The resident lacks standing because he has not been personally denied equal treatment by the challenged program
Answer B: The resident has standing because any race-conscious government program stigmatizes every resident
Answer C: The resident has standing because Equal Protection claims are exempt from Article III
Answer D: The resident lacks standing only because municipalities are immune from federal suits
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Stigmatic injury is not enough when asserted as a generalized objection. Standing requires a personal denial of equal treatment or another concrete and particularized injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The plaintiff must connect the alleged discrimination to his own treatment, not merely to his disagreement with the program. C: Serious merits questions do not eliminate standing requirements. D: Municipalities are not treated like states for Eleventh Amendment immunity; the issue here is the plaintiff's injury.
lb_rule_reference: Stigmatic injury from allegedly discriminatory government action generally supports standing only for persons personally denied equal treatment by the challenged conduct.
