Question: Moses owned Lot 1, which was benefited by an express easement over Leah's Lot 2 “for access from Lot 1 to the public road.” Moses later subdivided Lot 1 into Lot 1A and Lot 1B and sold both parcels. The owners of both new parcels claim that the easement remains appurtenant to the land originally known as Lot 1. Which statement best describes the classification issue?
Answer A: The easement remains appurtenant to the dominant land and may benefit successors to subdivided portions, subject to scope and overburden limits.
Answer B: The easement automatically becomes in gross because more than one owner now claims the benefit.
Answer C: The easement automatically terminates because subdivision destroys appurtenant rights.
Answer D: The easement becomes a license because later deeds did not personally name Leah.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: An appurtenant easement benefits land and generally follows the dominant estate, including subdivided portions of that estate, unless use exceeds the easement's scope or unreasonably overburdens the servient land. The classification remains appurtenant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because multiple successor owners do not convert a land-benefiting easement into one in gross. C: C is wrong because subdivision does not automatically terminate an appurtenant easement. D: D is wrong because appurtenant status does not depend on later personal naming of the servient owner.
lb_rule_reference: Appurtenant easement / subdivided dominant land
