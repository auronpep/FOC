Question: During a federal bench trial, Deborah's lawyer draws a timeline on a whiteboard while questioning Daniel. The timeline lists dates already testified to and helps Daniel explain the sequence. The opponent objects that the whiteboard is not an exhibit. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a lawyer may never use a visual aid unless it is admitted as an exhibit.
Answer B: Overrule the objection if the court treats the timeline as an illustrative aid rather than substantive evidence, subject to control for accuracy and confusion.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the timeline is self-authenticating documentary evidence.
Answer D: Sustain the objection unless each date on the timeline is proved by an original writing.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may allow demonstrative aids to organize or illustrate evidence if they accurately reflect admitted proof and do not mislead. The aid need not itself be a substantive exhibit.
