MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Damages
Question: A warehouse operator repeatedly parked loading rigs across the only bridge into a business district during rush hour. Delivery drivers, commuters, and pedestrians were delayed for weeks. A cafe owner on the far side of the bridge lost lunch customers because traffic backed up. The cafe owner suffered no physical injury, no property damage, and no loss of access different from other businesses and residents beyond having fewer customers. Is the cafe owner likely to recover damages in a public nuisance claim against the warehouse operator?
Answer A: Yes, because the blocked bridge interfered with a right common to the public
Answer B: Yes, because lost profits are foreseeable whenever public access to a business district is blocked
Answer C: No, because the cafe owner alleges only the same access obstruction and delay suffered by the public, plus derivative lost sales
Answer D: No, because only government entities may sue to abate a public nuisance
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The operator may have created a public nuisance, but the cafe owner's lost sales are derivative economic consequences of the same bridge obstruction suffered by everyone. Without a different-kind injury, private damages are unavailable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A public nuisance may exist, but a private damages plaintiff must show special injury different in kind from the public harm. Lost customer traffic is a degree of the same access delay suffered by the community. B: Foreseeability alone does not overcome the public nuisance special-injury requirement or the limits on remote economic loss. D: A private plaintiff can sue for public nuisance if the plaintiff has special injury. The cafe loses because that showing is absent, not because private suits are categorically barred.
lb_rule_reference: A private plaintiff may recover for public nuisance only by showing harm different in kind, not merely degree, from the public's harm; pure lost profits from a public obstruction usually are not enough.
