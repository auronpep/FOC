Outline: Creation
Question: Abraham owned Lot 1 and Lot 2. For many years, while he owned both lots, Abraham used a visible gravel driveway across Lot 2 to reach a barn on Lot 1. The driveway was the only practical route for farm vehicles to reach the barn, although a narrow footpath from a public road also touched Lot 1. Abraham sold Lot 1 to Sarah by a deed that did not mention the driveway. Sarah continued using the driveway. Abraham later sold Lot 2 to Moses, who blocked the driveway. Sarah sues to establish a right to continue using the driveway. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Sarah has an easement by necessity because any practical inconvenience in reaching land is enough.
Answer B: Sarah has no easement because implied easements require the parcel to be completely landlocked.
Answer C: Sarah has an implied easement by prior use because the driveway was apparent, continuous, and reasonably necessary when the lots were severed.
Answer D: Sarah owns the driveway by adverse possession because Abraham used it openly for many years.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An implied easement by prior use may arise when commonly owned land is severed and, before severance, one part was used for another in an apparent, continuous, and reasonably necessary way. The driveway existed before Abraham sold Lot 1, was visible, and was reasonably necessary for vehicle access to the barn.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it uses the stricter necessity route and overstates necessity; practical inconvenience alone does not create an easement by necessity. B: B is wrong because complete landlock is not required for an implied easement by prior use. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because Abraham's use of his own land before severance was not adverse possession and Sarah is claiming a use right, not title.
lb_rule_reference: Implied easement by prior use
lb_student_diagnostic: The recurring trap is swapping prior-use implication with necessity and then demanding complete landlock.
