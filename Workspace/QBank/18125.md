Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers stopped a car for a broken taillight. During the stop, a drug-detection dog alerted to the passenger compartment. Officers arrested the driver for an outstanding warrant for check fraud. They secured the driver in the patrol car and then searched the passenger compartment. They found cocaine. The driver argues the search was invalid because SITA-Gant does not permit a search incident to an arrest for check fraud, which generates no vehicle evidence. Was the search of the passenger compartment constitutional?
Answer A: No, because SITA-Gant bars the search and no other exception permits it
Answer B: No, because the automobile exception requires exigency, and the driver was already in custody
Answer C: Yes, because the dog alert provided probable cause to believe the vehicle contained contraband, and the automobile exception authorizes the search independently of SITA-Gant
Answer D: Yes, because the driver's outstanding warrant for check fraud authorized a full search of his person and property
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The driver is correct that SITA-Gant does not authorize this search: he was secured in the patrol car and check fraud is not an offense for which vehicle evidence is anticipated. However, the automobile exception independently authorizes the search. A trained drug-detection dog's alert to the passenger compartment constitutes probable cause that contraband is present (Florida v. Harris). The automobile exception then permits a warrantless search of the vehicle and any containers that could hold the contraband. The cocaine is admissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: SITA-Gant and the automobile exception are independent. Even if SITA-Gant fails, the automobile exception may independently authorize the search. The dog alert provided probable cause to believe the vehicle contained contraband — which is exactly what the automobile exception requires. The analysis does not end with SITA-Gant. B: The automobile exception does not require exigency. Probable cause alone suffices under Carroll and its progeny. The driver's custody has no bearing on the automobile exception analysis. D: An arrest warrant authorizes arrest of the person. It does not independently authorize a full search of a vehicle. The vehicle search must be independently justified — here, by the automobile exception via the dog alert. The warrant alone is not the correct basis for the vehicle search.
lb_rule_reference: SITA-Gant and the automobile exception are independent doctrines; SITA-Gant requires either an unsecured arrestee or reason to believe the vehicle contains evidence of the offense of arrest; the automobile exception requires probable cause to believe the vehicle contains contraband, regardless of whether an arrest occurred
