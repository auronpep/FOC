Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Assault
Question: A nightclub hires a bouncer to remove unruly patrons. While ejecting a patron who refuses to leave, the bouncer angrily shoves the patron harder than necessary and causes injury. The club argues that intentional torts are always outside the scope of employment. Is the club's argument correct?
Answer A: Yes, because respondeat superior never covers intentional torts
Answer B: Yes, because anger automatically makes the employee's act purely personal
Answer C: No, because use of force in ejecting patrons was closely connected to the bouncer's assigned work
Answer D: No, because an employer is liable only if it expressly ordered the exact shove
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The risk of excessive force can be foreseeable job-related friction for a bouncer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Some intentional torts can fall within scope when closely tied to the work. B: Mixed motives do not automatically defeat scope when the conduct arises from assigned duties. D: Vicarious liability does not require a command to commit the tort.
lb_rule_reference: An employer may be vicariously liable for an employee's intentional tort when the act is closely connected to assigned work or arises from foreseeable job-related friction.
