Outline: Marital Communications Privilege
Question: Isaac and Rebekah divorced last year. During their marriage, while alone in their bedroom, Isaac privately told Rebekah, “I hid the money from the auditors.” In a later federal fraud prosecution against Isaac, the prosecutor calls Rebekah to testify about that private statement. Isaac objects on spousal-privilege grounds. What is the best ruling on Isaac's objection?
Answer A: Overrule it because divorce ends every spousal privilege for statements made during the marriage.
Answer B: Sustain it under the adverse spousal testimonial privilege because Rebekah is being asked to testify adversely against Isaac.
Answer C: Overrule it because incriminating statements between spouses are never privileged in a criminal case.
Answer D: Sustain it if the statement was a confidential marital communication made during the marriage; divorce ends the adverse testimonial privilege but does not itself eliminate the marital communications privilege for prior confidential communications.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal law distinguishes the adverse spousal testimonial privilege from the confidential marital communications privilege. The testimonial privilege depends on a current marriage, but the communications privilege can protect confidential communications made during the marriage even after divorce. Isaac's statement was made privately during the marriage, so the communication privilege—not the testimonial privilege—is the controlling route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it transfers the current-marriage limit from testimonial privilege to the separate marital communications privilege. B: B is wrong because the adverse spousal testimonial privilege generally depends on current marriage. Isaac and Rebekah are divorced, so that privilege is not the correct basis. C: C is wrong because incriminating content does not by itself destroy marital communications privilege. The rule turns on confidentiality, timing, and any exception or waiver.
lb_rule_reference: Confidential marital communications privilege can survive divorce; adverse spousal testimonial privilege requires current marriage
lb_student_diagnostic: Student collapses adverse spousal testimony and confidential marital communications into one current-marriage rule.
