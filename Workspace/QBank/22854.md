


BARMATRIX Q#: 22854
Outline_code: 95070600
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Pretrial Conferences and Orders
Question: A woman sued a hospital for malpractice in federal court. The parties scheduled a Rule 26(f) conference 25 days before the scheduling conference. Although the woman told her attorney that she was interested in settling her claim with the hospital, the woman's attorney told her that they shouldn't mention any possibility of settlement during the Rule 26(f) conference because they didn't have to discuss settlement options and mentioning a possible settlement would hurt the woman's arguments during trial. The woman agreed and neither she nor her attorney mentioned a possible settlement. The woman's claim went to trial, and the jury found in favor of the hospital and awarded the woman nothing in damages. Was the woman's attorney correct in telling the woman that they shouldn't mention any possibility of settlement at the Rule 26(f) conference?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the woman's attorney was in charge of any litigation tactics, including any litigation tactics used at the Rule 26(f) conference.
Answer B: B: Yes, because a Rule 26(f) conference only deals with evidentiary and disclosure issues.
Answer C: C: No, because the parties must consider settlement possibilities during a Rule 26(f) conference.
Answer D: D: No, because the woman would have likely received some compensation if her attorney had mentioned that she was interested in settling her claim during the Rule 26(f) conference.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(f)(2) states that parties holding a Rule 26(f) conference “must consider the nature and basis of their claims and defenses and the possibilities for promptly settling or resolving the case.” Consequently, the woman's attorney was incorrect when he told the woman that they did not have to discuss the possibility of a settlement at the Rule 26(f) conference. A and B are incorrect because the parties must discuss settlement possibilities under Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(f)(2). D is incorrect because the issue here is not whether the woman might have received some compensation if the issue of a settlement had been brought up.
