Outline: Nonresponsive answers
Question: In a federal premises-liability case, Abigail asks Solomon, “Did you see water on the floor?” Solomon answers, “Yes, and the store manager later offered to pay Abigail $5,000 if she would drop the claim.” The store immediately moves to strike the statement about the offer. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the motion because settlement offers are always admissible once a witness mentions them.
Answer B: Grant the motion because the settlement-offer statement was volunteered beyond the question and is inadmissible for liability.
Answer C: Deny the motion because the question was not compound.
Answer D: Grant the motion and strike Solomon's testimony that he saw water on the floor.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Solomon's observation of water responds to the question. The statement about a settlement offer was volunteered and is inadmissible when offered to prove liability. The proper remedy is to strike the volunteered settlement portion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because settlement evidence is not freely admissible for liability simply because a witness blurts it out. C: C is wrong because the objection is not about a compound question. D: D is wrong because the responsive observation need not be stricken.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(1); FRE 408 decoy / volunteered compromise statement
