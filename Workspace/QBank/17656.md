Question: Naomi hired Daniel to design a garden. The parties signed a detailed written agreement listing the drawings to be delivered, the fee, the deadline, and the payment schedule. It also stated, “This writing is the complete and exclusive statement of the parties' agreement.” Naomi later offered evidence that, before signing, Daniel orally promised to include a fountain design at no added cost. The writing says nothing about fountains. How should the court rule? How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the oral promise because it is consistent with the writing.
Answer B: Admit the oral promise because it was made before signing.
Answer C: Exclude the oral promise because the writing was intended as a complete and exclusive integration.
Answer D: Exclude the written contract because it omitted the fountain term.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A complete and exclusive integration bars prior or contemporaneous consistent additional terms as well as contradictory terms. The merger clause and detailed writing support treating the writing as complete and exclusive.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a consistent term can still be barred by a complete integration. B: B is wrong because pre-signing timing makes the statement parol evidence; it does not make the statement admissible. D: D is wrong because omission of the oral term does not invalidate the written contract.
lb_rule_reference: Complete integration / consistent additional term barred
