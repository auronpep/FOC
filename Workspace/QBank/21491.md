Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Miriam owned a townhouse. She signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey the townhouse to my daughter Abigail.” Miriam had only one daughter, whose full legal name was Abigail Naomi. No other Abigail was connected with Miriam or the townhouse. Abigail Naomi accepted the deed. Who owns the townhouse?
Answer A: Miriam, because the deed did not state Abigail Naomi's full legal name.
Answer B: Naomi alone, because the deed omitted Abigail's second name.
Answer C: Miriam, until a court reforms the deed to add the full legal name.
Answer D: Abigail Naomi, because the deed identifies the grantee with reasonable certainty.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantee identity. Exact full legal names are not required when the deed and surrounding identifying facts make the grantee reasonably certain. “My daughter Abigail” identifies Abigail Naomi, so title passes to her.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a full legal name is not mandatory when identity is clear. B: B is wrong because Naomi is part of Abigail Naomi's full name, not a separate named grantee. C: C is wrong because reformation is unnecessary when the grantee is already ascertainable.
lb_rule_reference: Grantee identity / name variance
