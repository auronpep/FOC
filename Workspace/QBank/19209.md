Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: David owned a two-acre parcel. He signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey the parcel to Jonathan and Samuel as tenants in common, one-half each.” Jonathan was alive and accepted the deed. Samuel had died before delivery. The deed did not say that Jonathan should receive Samuel's share if Samuel could not take. What is the result?
Answer A: The entire parcel remains with David because one named grantee was dead.
Answer B: Jonathan takes an undivided one-half interest, and David retains the one-half interest attempted for Samuel.
Answer C: Jonathan takes the entire parcel because he was the only living named grantee.
Answer D: Samuel's devisees take Samuel's one-half interest because the deed named Samuel.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantee capacity for each stated share. Jonathan was a living, ascertainable grantee and can take the one-half interest conveyed to him. Samuel was dead at delivery and took nothing, so David retains the attempted one-half share for Samuel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the invalid share does not necessarily void the valid conveyance to Jonathan. C: C is wrong because the deed fixed one-half shares and did not give Jonathan Samuel's failed share. D: D is wrong because Samuel could not devise an interest that never vested in him.
