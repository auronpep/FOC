Question: In a federal tax prosecution, the government subpoenas a defense lawyer to testify only to the dates and amounts of cash fees the defendant paid the lawyer. The subpoena does not ask for legal advice, litigation strategy, or the substance of any client communication. Should attorney-client privilege bar the testimony?
Answer A: Yes, because all information learned by a lawyer during representation is privileged.
Answer B: Yes, because fee information necessarily reveals a confidential communication.
Answer C: No, because fee amounts and payment dates are ordinarily not protected legal-advice communications.
Answer D: No, because a criminal defendant has no attorney-client privilege against the government.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications for legal advice, not every fact connected to representation. Fee amounts and payment dates are ordinarily outside the protected object, absent special facts revealing privileged substance. The decisive axis is protected communication versus representation fact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is lawyer-possession absolutism. Not all information known by counsel is privileged. B: B is wrong because Fee records can be privileged in unusual circumstances, but the stem gives no fact showing legal advice or privileged substance would be revealed. D: A is wrong because This is lawyer-possession absolutism. Not all information known by counsel is privileged. B is wrong because Fee records can be privileged in unusual circumstances, but the stem gives no fact showing legal advice or privileged substance would be revealed. C is wrong because Correct answer D is wrong because Criminal defendants do have attorney-client privilege. The subpoena wins because the object requested is ordinarily nonprivileged.
