Question: Caleb owned North Lot and South Lot. Caleb conveyed South Lot to Aaron by deed, reserving “for the owner of North Lot a right to cross the stone lane on South Lot.” Caleb later sold North Lot to Miriam. Miriam's deed did not mention the stone-lane right. Aaron then blocked the lane. Who has the better claim?
Answer A: Aaron, because a reserved easement is always personal to the grantor who created it.
Answer B: Aaron, because Miriam's deed did not repeat the easement language.
Answer C: Miriam, because the easement was appurtenant to North Lot and passed with the dominant estate.
Answer D: Miriam, but only if North Lot was completely landlocked when Caleb sold it.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement appurtenant benefits land and generally passes automatically with the dominant estate. The reservation benefited “the owner of North Lot,” so North Lot is the dominant estate. Miriam received that benefit when she bought North Lot even though her deed omitted the language.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the reservation is tied to ownership of North Lot, not Caleb personally. B: B is wrong because appurtenant easements generally pass with the land. D: D is wrong because necessity is not required when an express reserved easement already exists.
lb_rule_reference: Reserved appurtenant easement / later transfer of dominant estate
