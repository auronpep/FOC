Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Rebecca agreed in a written contract to buy 1,000 baskets from Gideon, with payment “on delivery.” The contract did not define that phrase. On the first four deliveries under the same contract, Gideon accepted payment within ten days after delivery without objection. On the fifth delivery, Gideon demanded payment the same day and claimed Rebecca was in breach. Rebecca offers the first four accepted payments to interpret “on delivery.” Should the court admit the evidence? Should the court admit the evidence?
Answer A: No, because conduct after signing is barred by the parol evidence rule.
Answer B: Yes, because course of performance under the same contract may help interpret the agreement.
Answer C: No, because only prior contracts can be used to interpret a written agreement.
Answer D: Yes, but only to prove that Gideon waived all future payment deadlines forever.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Course of performance is conduct under the same contract and can be relevant to interpret the parties' agreement. It is not barred merely because it occurred after signing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because post-signing performance is not prior or contemporaneous parol evidence in the same way. C: C is wrong because course of performance, not only course of dealing, is a recognized meaning source. D: D is wrong because interpretation and waiver are distinct routes; the question asks whether the evidence may interpret the term.
lb_rule_reference: UCC course of performance / current contract
