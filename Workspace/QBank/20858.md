MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Custom
Question: At a business reception, a vendor wanted a guest to stop criticizing his product. Without warning, the vendor grabbed the guest's lapel, pulled the guest close, and spoke into his face. The guest was not physically injured but found the contact humiliating and offensive. The guest sues the vendor for battery. Is the vendor likely liable for battery?
Answer A: No, because the guest suffered no physical injury
Answer B: No, because business receptions involve some ordinary touching
Answer C: Yes, because the vendor intentionally caused offensive contact beyond ordinary social consent
Answer D: Yes, but only if the vendor intended to cause emotional distress
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The vendor deliberately grabbed the guest's clothing attached to his person and pulled him close. That contact can offend a reasonable sense of personal dignity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Offensive contact can be enough for battery. Physical injury is not required. B: Grabbing a lapel and pulling someone close during an argument exceeds ordinary social contact. D: Battery intent focuses on harmful or offensive contact, not intent to cause severe emotional distress.
lb_rule_reference: Offensive contact can satisfy battery when it would offend a reasonable sense of personal dignity and is not justified by consent, privilege, or ordinary social custom.
