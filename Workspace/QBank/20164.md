Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Ruth contracted with Boaz to lease a storefront for a new bakery for one year. Boaz wrongfully refused to deliver the premises. Ruth had already signed catering contracts with three customers for the first month, had fixed ingredient and labor costs, and had financial records from a similar bakery she operated in another town. Ruth seeks the net profits she would have earned during the first month. May Ruth recover those lost profits?
Answer A: No, because lost profits of a new business are never recoverable.
Answer B: No, because profits can be recovered only when the contract itself states a profit figure.
Answer C: Yes, if Ruth proves the lost profits with reasonable certainty rather than speculation.
Answer D: Yes, but only as restitution measured by Boaz's benefit from withholding the storefront.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Expectation damages may include lost profits if they are caused by the breach, foreseeable, and proven with reasonable certainty. A new-business label is not an automatic bar when concrete contracts, known costs, and comparable operating history provide a reliable basis for net-profit proof.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the modern certainty inquiry does not categorically bar all new-business profits. B: B is wrong because the contract need not state the profit figure if other evidence proves it with reasonable certainty. D: D is wrong because Ruth seeks her expected performance position, not Boaz's unjust enrichment.
