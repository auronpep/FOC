Outline_code: 97100000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Judgments and Verdicts
Question: A plaintiff filed a suit for breach of contract in state court. After the plaintiff failed to prosecute the action, the defendant moved to dismiss. The court dismissed the action with prejudice. The plaintiff then filed the same action in federal court. The defendant asserted res judicata (claim preclusion) in her answer. Will the federal court give preclusive effect to the state court's decision?
Answer A: A: Yes, because a judgment based on failure to prosecute acts as a judgment on the merits.
Answer B: B: Yes, because a dismissal with prejudice acts as a judgment on the merits.
Answer C: C: No, because the state court did not make a final decision on the merits.
Answer D: D: No, because the judgment was entered by a state court.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 41(a)(2), a court may dismiss a claim. Importantly, unless the court states otherwise, a dismissal under Fed. R. Civ. P. 41(a)(2) is without prejudice. Here, the court stated that the dismissal was “with prejudice,” and a dismissal “with prejudice” means the plaintiff cannot file the lawsuit again. A is incorrect because the answer choice states that the court's decision was a “judgment” instead of a “dismissal.” C is incorrect because the dismissal was “with prejudice,” meaning that the plaintiff cannot file his claim again. D is incorrect because a dismissal with prejudice by a state court has the same effect as a dismissal with prejudice by a federal court.
