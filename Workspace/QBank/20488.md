Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Damages
Question: A cyclist was negligently struck by a delivery van and suffered a shoulder injury. Doctors offered an experimental surgery that might reduce future pain but carried a significant risk of paralysis. The cyclist refused the surgery and chose standard therapy instead. The van company argues that all future pain damages should be denied for failure to mitigate. Is the cyclist required to undergo the surgery to preserve damages?
Answer A: Yes, because a plaintiff must choose whatever treatment might reduce damages
Answer B: Yes, because refusal of treatment is always comparative negligence
Answer C: No, because mitigation does not require unreasonable or risky treatment
Answer D: No, because a tort plaintiff never has a duty to mitigate damages
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The proposed surgery carried serious risk. Choosing standard therapy was not an unreasonable failure to mitigate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The duty is to take reasonable steps. A risky experimental surgery is not required. B: Post-injury mitigation is not automatically breach-stage comparative fault, and unreasonable refusal is not shown when treatment is risky. D: Plaintiffs do have a reasonable mitigation duty; this particular surgery exceeds it.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff must take reasonable steps to reduce damages but need not undergo risky, experimental, or unusually burdensome medical procedures.
