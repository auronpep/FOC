Question: In a federal civil trial, Noah calls Daniel, the defendant, as an adverse witness and questions him briefly. Daniel's own lawyer then begins “cross-examination” and asks, “You carefully inspected the machine before selling it, didn't you?” Noah objects that Daniel's lawyer is using leading questions with his own friendly client. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because leading questions are always allowed whenever the examination is labeled cross-examination.
Answer B: Sustain the objection or require nonleading questions because the examination is cross in form only and Daniel is friendly to his own lawyer.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if Daniel previously refused to answer Noah's questions.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because an adverse witness may always be led by every lawyer in the case.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Leading questions are ordinarily allowed on cross-examination, but the court retains control over the mode of examination. When a party's own lawyer examines that party after the opponent called him, the examination may be cross in form only; the court may require nonleading questions.
