


BARMATRIX Q#: 22852
Outline_code: 95070101
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Complaint
Question: A patient filed a complaint against a doctor in federal court claiming the doctor negligently removed one of her organs. Twenty days after filing the complaint, the woman filed an amended complaint alleging breach of contract and battery. The breach of contract claim was completely unrelated to the negligence claim, although the battery claim was related to the same incident as the negligence claim. The doctor had not yet filed any response. May the patient amend her complaint?
Answer A: A: No, because the breach of contract claim was unrelated to the negligence claim.
Answer B: B: No, because over 14 days had passed since the filing of the complaint.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the battery claim arose from the same incident as the negligence claim.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the patient had not amended her complaint yet.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(a)(1) allows a party to amend a pleading once as a matter of course if the party does so within 21 days after serving it or if the party does so within 21 days after service of a responsive pleading if a responsive pleading is required. Thereafter, a pleading can be amended only by leave of the court or with the opposing party's written consent. Here, since this is the first time the patient has amended her complaint and only 20 days have passed, she may amend it. A and C are incorrect because there does not need to be any connection between the claims and a plaintiff may bring all of his or her claims against a defendant whether they are related or not. B is incorrect because 21 days is the deadline, not 14.
