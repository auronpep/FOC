Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: A buyer, Miriam, sends a purchase order to a seller, Caleb, for 400 identical lamps at a stated price. The order does not specify a required method of acceptance. Caleb immediately emails Miriam, “I accept and will ship the lamps tomorrow.” Before Caleb ships, Miriam tries to cancel. Under Article 2, was a contract formed before shipment?
Answer A: Yes, because the order invited acceptance by a prompt promise to ship unless the offer unambiguously required shipment only.
Answer B: No, because an order for goods can be accepted only by actual shipment.
Answer C: No, because an email acceptance must match every formal term in a purchase order under the common-law mirror-image rule.
Answer D: No, because Caleb did not sign a paper acceptance.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC Article 2, an offer to buy goods generally invites acceptance in any reasonable manner, including a prompt promise to ship, unless the offer unambiguously provides otherwise. Caleb's prompt promise formed a contract before shipment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because Article 2 allows acceptance by a prompt promise to ship as well as by shipment. C: C is wrong because the source law is Article 2, not common-law mirror-image formalism. D: D is wrong because the facts do not require a signed paper acceptance.
