Question: A state redistricting committee draws a congressional district that snakes across the state to connect neighborhoods with the same racial majority. Internal emails say race was the "nonnegotiable criterion," and traditional districting principles were repeatedly subordinated to hit a racial target. What level of review applies to the district?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny, because race predominated over traditional districting principles
Answer B: Rational basis, because all districting is political
Answer C: Intermediate scrutiny, because voting is involved
Answer D: No review, because the district lines do not name race in the statute
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When race predominates in districting, Equal Protection strict scrutiny applies even if the plan is not worded as a racial statute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Racial gerrymandering is a justiciable Equal Protection problem when race predominates. C: Racial classification or racial predominance triggers strict scrutiny. D: Predominant racial sorting can be proven from evidence beyond statutory labels.
lb_rule_reference: If race is the predominant factor in drawing electoral districts, the districting plan is subject to strict scrutiny.
