Question: Lydia hired Timothy to install a heating system in a workshop for $25,000. Timothy installed it incorrectly, and the reasonable cost to repair the system is $4,000. Lydia also claims $15,000 in lost profits from a special holiday sale she planned to run in the workshop, but she did not tell Timothy about the sale when they contracted and the lost profits are uncertain. What is Lydia's most secure expectation recovery?
Answer A: $4,000, the reasonable cost to repair the heating system.
Answer B: $15,000, because lost profits are always recoverable when a contractor breaches.
Answer C: $19,000, because Lydia may stack every loss she claims after breach.
Answer D: Nothing, because lost profits are uncertain.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The repair cost is the direct cost of obtaining the promised heating system. The special lost profits require separate consequential-damages gates—foreseeability, certainty, causation, and mitigation. On these facts, those profits were undisclosed and uncertain, so Lydia's secure recovery is the $4,000 repair cost.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because lost profits are not automatically recoverable after breach. C: C is wrong because damages cannot be stacked without satisfying the legal gates for each category. D: D is wrong because uncertainty about consequential profits does not defeat the direct repair-cost recovery.
