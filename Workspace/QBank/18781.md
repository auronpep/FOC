Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A homeowner hires a licensed plumber as an independent contractor to replace a sink. The homeowner does not control the details of the plumber's work. The plumber negligently drops a wrench from a second-floor window, injuring a passerby. Is the homeowner vicariously liable under the usual rule?
Answer A: Yes, because anyone who hires another person is liable for that person's negligence
Answer B: Yes, because the work occurred on the homeowner's property
Answer C: No, because the plumber was an independent contractor and no exception is stated
Answer D: No, because negligence by a contractor is not actionable by injured third parties
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Without control or an exception, the hirer is generally not vicariously liable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The default rule differs for independent contractors. B: Location alone does not create vicarious liability for an independent contractor's negligence. D: The passerby may sue the plumber directly.
lb_rule_reference: A person who hires an independent contractor is generally not vicariously liable for the contractor's negligence unless an exception applies.
