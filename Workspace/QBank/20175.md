Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: Aaron is charged with violating a court order that barred him from driving. To prove that Aaron had no valid license on the day in question, the government offers a certified statement from the state records office that a diligent search found no license issued to Aaron. Aaron argues that the government must produce every license file in the state before the jury may consider absence of a license. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the certificate because absence of a record can be proved only by producing all records.
Answer B: Admit the certificate if the rule's foundation is satisfied; the jury may consider it, and the prosecution retains the burden of persuasion.
Answer C: Admit the certificate only if Aaron first testifies that he had no license.
Answer D: Exclude the certificate because negative evidence is never probative.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A properly supported absence-of-record certification can satisfy the prosecution's production burden for that evidentiary point. It does not relieve the prosecution of its burden of persuasion on the charged offense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the rule does not require producing every record. C: C is wrong because the government need not obtain Aaron's admission before using an absence-of-record route. D: D is wrong because absence evidence can be probative.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 803(10); burden of production
