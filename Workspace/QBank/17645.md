Question: In a federal criminal burglary trial, David testifies in his own defense. The prosecutor seeks to impeach David with a six-year-old felony conviction for assault. The conviction does not involve a dishonest act or false statement. David objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the conviction automatically because any felony conviction may be used to impeach a witness.
Answer B: Admit the conviction if its probative value outweighs its prejudicial effect to David.
Answer C: Admit the conviction subject only to ordinary Rule 403 balancing.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction because only crimes involving dishonesty or false statement may ever be used against a criminal defendant who testifies.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When the witness is the accused in a criminal case and the conviction is a felony that does not qualify under Rule 609(a)(2), FRE 609(a)(1)(B) permits admission only if the probative value of the conviction outweighs its prejudicial effect to that defendant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because criminal-defendant felony convictions are not automatic. C: C is wrong because Rule 609(a)(1)(B) imposes a defendant-specific balancing standard, not ordinary Rule 403. D: D is wrong because some non-dishonesty felony convictions may be admitted against a criminal defendant if the Rule 609(a)(1)(B) standard is met.
