Question: Naomi signed a paper stating, “I plan to give my orchard to Samuel after the harvest.” Naomi handed the paper to Samuel, who thanked her. The paper accurately described the orchard but contained no words of present transfer. Before the harvest, Naomi changed her mind. Who holds legal title to the orchard?
Answer A: Samuel, because the paper was signed and described the land.
Answer B: Naomi, because the paper expressed a future plan rather than a present deed conveyance.
Answer C: Samuel, because no consideration is required for a gift deed.
Answer D: Naomi only if Samuel failed to record the paper.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The asserted title source is a signed writing, but the decisive validity gate is whether the writing operates as a present deed. Naomi's paper expressed a future plan to give the orchard after the harvest; it did not presently convey title. The no-consideration rule does not cure the missing present-transfer lock, so legal title remains with Naomi.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a signed land description is insufficient without present words of conveyance. C: C is wrong because a gift deed still must operate as a present transfer when delivered. D: D is wrong because recording does not convert a future intention into a present deed.
lb_rule_reference: Future promise does not presently convey legal title
