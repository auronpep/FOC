Outline: Possibility of reverter
Question: Elijah conveyed Clinicacre “to Elisha while the land is used as a clinic.” Elijah later died, leaving all of his property to Hannah. Elisha then stopped operating a clinic and converted the building into apartments. Who owns Clinicacre after the conversion?
Answer A: Elisha, until Hannah files an action to reenter.
Answer B: Hannah, because the fee simple determinable ended automatically and Elijah's possibility of reverter passed to her.
Answer C: Elijah's estate, but only after a court enters a forfeiture decree.
Answer D: No one, because the deed did not say who would take if clinic use ended.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Elisha held a fee simple determinable because the deed used durational language: “while.” Elijah retained a possibility of reverter. That retained interest passed to Hannah under Elijah's will, and title automatically shifted to Hannah when clinic use ceased.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because no reentry is required for a fee simple determinable. C: C is wrong because automatic termination does not depend on a forfeiture decree. D: D is wrong because the grantor retains a possibility of reverter even if the deed does not expressly name the taker on breach.
lb_rule_reference: Possibility of reverter / automatic vesting in successor
