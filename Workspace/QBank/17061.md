Question: A public high school invites a local clergy member to deliver an invocation selected by administrators at graduation. Attendance is not legally required, but most students attend to receive their diplomas publicly. What is the strongest Establishment Clause concern?
Answer A: The school has sponsored a religious exercise in a setting where students face practical pressure to attend and conform
Answer B: There is no issue because graduation attendance is technically voluntary
Answer C: The prayer is private speech because the clergy member is not a school employee
Answer D: The prayer is valid because all ceremonial prayer is historically permitted
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Graduation is a school-controlled event with coercion concerns even absent formal penalties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Practical and social pressure in school ceremonies can be coercive. C: The school selected and placed the prayer in its official ceremony. D: Public-school ceremonies involving students have distinct coercion concerns.
lb_rule_reference: School-sponsored prayer at graduation can violate the Establishment Clause because students face coercive pressure to participate in a school-controlled ceremony.
