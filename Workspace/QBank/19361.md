Question: Police suspect a homeowner of drug trafficking. On trash collection morning, officers take opaque garbage bags that the homeowner placed at the curb for pickup. They open the bags and find drug packaging residue. The homeowner moves to suppress the residue. How should the court rule on the Fourth Amendment challenge?
Answer A: Suppress the residue because opaque bags always preserve privacy
Answer B: Admit the residue because the homeowner lacked a reasonable expectation of privacy in curbside garbage
Answer C: Suppress the residue because police needed an arrest warrant before inspecting trash
Answer D: Admit the residue only if the garbage truck driver consented first
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Once the bags were placed at the curb for collection, the homeowner generally lost Fourth Amendment privacy protection in them.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Garbage left at the curb for collection is generally exposed to public access and collection workers. C: The threshold problem is that the curb trash was not protected by a reasonable expectation of privacy. D: The analysis does not require consent from the trash collector; it turns on abandonment of privacy at the curb.
lb_rule_reference: A person generally has no reasonable expectation of privacy in garbage left at the curb for collection.
