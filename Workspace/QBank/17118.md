MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: In general
Question: A warehouse owner fenced a vacant side yard but did not lock a gap in the fence. An adult pedestrian used the yard as a shortcut at night without the owner's knowledge. The pedestrian crossed an open drainage ditch that was visible in daylight, slipped on loose gravel, and was injured. The pedestrian sues the warehouse owner for negligence. Is the pedestrian likely to establish that the owner owed an invitee-level duty in the negligence claim?
Answer A: Yes, because anyone physically entering land is owed reasonable inspection and repair
Answer B: No, because an undiscovered adult trespasser ordinarily receives only limited protection from willful or wanton injury
Answer C: Yes, because the unlocked fence gap made the pedestrian a licensee
Answer D: No, because trespassers can never recover for any land condition
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The owner did not know of this shortcut user, and the facts do not show willful or wanton conduct. The broad invitee duty does not apply.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The pedestrian was an undiscovered adult trespasser, not an invitee. The possessor did not owe a general duty to inspect and repair for this person. C: A failure to lock a gap is not permission to enter. Without permission or known tolerated use, the pedestrian remains a trespasser. D: The conclusion is too broad. Known, anticipated, or child trespasser rules can create duties; this case is narrower because the plaintiff was an undiscovered adult trespasser.
lb_rule_reference: A land possessor generally owes an undiscovered adult trespasser only a limited duty to refrain from willful or wanton injury.
