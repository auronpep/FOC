Question: Peter bought a packaging machine from Deborah's company under a written agreement that also required Deborah to install and test the machine. The agreement stated, “Seller may assign accounts receivable; neither party may delegate performance duties without written consent.” Deborah later assigned the right to receive Peter's final payment to a bank, but Deborah kept all installation duties and completed the installation. Peter refused to pay the bank, arguing that the contract barred any transfer without his consent. What is the likely legal effect of the transfer clause?
Answer A: The bank cannot collect because any transfer involving a contract with installation duties is a prohibited delegation.
Answer B: The assignment is likely permitted because the clause allows assignment of accounts receivable while separately restricting delegation of performance duties.
Answer C: The bank can collect only if it becomes an intended third-party beneficiary at the time the original contract was signed.
Answer D: The transfer clause is irrelevant because Article 2 automatically bars assignments in mixed goods/services contracts.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that the court must interpret the transfer language before applying assignment or delegation consequences. The trigger facts are the clause separately allowing assignment of receivables and barring delegation of performance duties without consent. The decisive lock is term object: the transfer was of the payment right, while Deborah retained the installation duties. The clause therefore likely permits the assignment to the bank and does not treat it as a forbidden delegation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it collapses assignment of a payment right into delegation of duties. C: C is wrong because a valid assignee need not be an original intended third-party beneficiary. D: D is wrong because Article 2 does not automatically bar assignment of receivables and does not override the clause's distinct assignment/delegation language.
