Question: Quentin wanted to stop Jules from testifying against him. He stabbed Jules deep in the thigh with a hunting knife, saying he meant to "cripple, not kill." The knife severed an artery, and Jules died from blood loss. Is murder supported?
Answer A: No, because Quentin did not intend to kill
Answer B: No, because the thigh is not a vital organ
Answer C: Yes, because Quentin intended serious bodily injury and Jules died from that injury
Answer D: Involuntary manslaughter only, because the fatal artery injury was accidental
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Quentin used a hunting knife to inflict a disabling wound. His statement confirms intent to cause serious injury. Death from that injury supports murder through grievous-bodily-harm malice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Malice includes intent to cause grievous bodily harm. A deep knife wound meant to cripple is serious-injury intent. B: The issue is the seriousness of the intended injury and resulting death. A deep knife wound severing an artery can reflect grievous-bodily-harm intent. D: The exact fatal mechanism need not be intended. The intended serious wound caused death.
lb_rule_reference: Intent to inflict serious bodily injury can establish malice for murder when death results, even if the defendant did not aim at a traditionally vital area.
