Question: Holly decided to empty an old gasoline can by pouring its contents down a storm drain behind her house. She did not know or think about where the drain led. The drain connected to a sewer line adjacent to an occupied building; gasoline vapors accumulated and ignited, causing a fire that injured two workers inside. A neighbor who saw Holly pouring the gasoline immediately recognized it as extremely dangerous and shouted a warning that Holly ignored. Holly later told police she "just didn't think anything bad would happen." Under the Model Penal Code, which mental state most accurately characterizes Holly's culpability regarding the risk of fire?
Answer A: Purpose, because Holly deliberately performed the act of pouring gasoline down the drain
Answer B: Knowledge, because a reasonable person would have known that pouring gasoline near a drain creates a risk of fire
Answer C: Recklessness, because Holly consciously disregarded a substantial and unjustifiable risk after being warned
Answer D: Negligence, because Holly failed to recognize a risk that a reasonable person would have perceived
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: MPC recklessness requires conscious disregard of a substantial and unjustifiable risk. Holly's neighbor warned her that what she was doing was extremely dangerous. By continuing after hearing and ignoring this warning, Holly became consciously aware of the risk and chose to disregard it. This is the paradigm of MPC recklessness: subjective awareness of the risk followed by a deliberate decision to proceed anyway. The risk (gasoline vapors igniting) was substantial and unjustifiable. Holly's statement that she "didn't think anything bad would happen" is inconsistent with this — the jury instruction would ask whether Holly in fact was aware of the risk after being warned, and the warning makes conscious disregard the most accurate characterization.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: MPC purpose means the defendant consciously desired to cause the specific result (the fire or injury). Holly's purpose was to empty the can, not to cause a fire. Purpose goes to the result element, not merely to the act. A defendant can purposely perform an act while being only reckless or negligent as to the resulting harm. B: MPC knowledge requires that the defendant was aware that her conduct was practically certain to cause the result — not that a reasonable person would have known. The objective standard ("a reasonable person would have known") describes MPC negligence, not knowledge. Knowledge is subjective and requires actual awareness. Holly stated she didn't think anything bad would happen, which is more consistent with recklessness (consciously disregarded the warning) or negligence (failed to perceive what she should have), not knowledge. D: This would be correct if Holly had never received the warning, because failing to perceive what a reasonable person would perceive is MPC negligence. But Holly received an explicit warning from a neighbor and continued. Once warned, the risk became subjectively known to her. Continuing after subjective awareness of the risk constitutes conscious disregard, elevating the culpability from negligence to recklessness. The warning is the critical fact that distinguishes recklessness from negligence here.
lb_rule_reference: Under the MPC, recklessness is conscious disregard of a substantial and unjustifiable risk; negligence is failure to be aware of a substantial and unjustifiable risk that a reasonable person would have perceived
