Question: A crane operator negligently swung a load of pipes over a marked pedestrian walkway. The load slipped and crashed inches from a pedestrian, showering dust on her but not striking her. The pedestrian immediately experienced chest tightness, shaking, and sleeplessness that her doctor attributed to the fright. She sues the crane company for negligent infliction of emotional distress. Is the pedestrian likely to recover on a negligent infliction of emotional distress claim?
Answer A: Yes, because she was in the zone of physical danger and suffered physical symptoms from the fright
Answer B: Yes, because any emotional distress caused by negligence is compensable
Answer C: No, because emotional distress is never recoverable in negligence
Answer D: No, because she must prove the operator acted intentionally or outrageously
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The pedestrian was directly endangered by the falling pipes and suffered physical symptoms. Those facts fit a recognized NIED gate under the zone-of-danger approach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Standalone NIED usually requires a recognized limiting category. Recovery is not available for every upset caused by negligence. C: Emotional distress can be recoverable in negligence when a recognized NIED category applies, such as zone of danger with physical symptoms. D: The claim is NIED, not IIED. NIED does not require intentional or outrageous conduct when its negligence-based gate is satisfied.
lb_rule_reference: Standalone negligent infliction of emotional distress may be recoverable when the plaintiff was in the zone of danger and suffered physical symptoms from the fright.
