Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Daniel is charged with stealing $20,000 from a synagogue account in March. The prosecutor offers evidence that Daniel bought an expensive necklace in December of the previous year, three months before the money disappeared. The prosecutor offers no evidence that Daniel lacked money in December or that the purchase was connected to the charged theft. Daniel objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because expensive purchases are always relevant in theft cases.
Answer B: Admit the evidence because a defendant's wealth always shows motive.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence because, without a timing or source connection, the December purchase does not make the March theft more probable.
Answer D: Exclude the evidence only if Daniel proves the necklace was a gift.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Unexplained wealth can be probative when tied to the timing and source of missing funds. Here the purchase occurred before the alleged theft and lacks a connection to the charged event. The evidence does not make the consequential fact more probable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because purchases are not automatically relevant in theft cases. B: B is wrong because wealth does not always prove motive or involvement. D: D is wrong because Daniel need not explain the necklace when the proponent has not shown a probative connection.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Using the correct category—wealth evidence—without the required timing/source link.
