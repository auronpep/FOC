


BARMATRIX Q#: 22864
Outline_code: 94010200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: After a couple was injured in a railway accident, the railroad settled the couple's claims by signing a contract giving them a lifetime pass for free transportation. Several years later, Congress passed an act that forbid interstate railroads from giving out free transportation passes. Based on that act, the railroad refused to honor the contract and renew the couple's free travel pass. The couple sued the railroad company in federal district court, arguing that the railroad's refusal to honor the contract was based on an erroneous understanding of the new act. In response, the railroad argued that the federal district court did not have jurisdiction over the couple's claim. Does the federal district court have jurisdiction over the couple's claim?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the federal court has federal question jurisdiction over the claim.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the federal court would have jurisdiction over the interstate railroad under the Commerce Clause.
Answer C: C: No, because the federal issue is merely an anticipation of the railroad's defense.
Answer D: D: No, because the federal court would not have supplemental jurisdiction over the contractual issue.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A federal court will not have federal question jurisdiction over a claim if the complaint only alleges federal issues that might be raised by the defendant in defense. Louisville & Nashville Railroad v. Mottley, 211 U.S. 149 (1908). A is incorrect because the federal court would only have federal question jurisdiction if the couples' complaint arose under federal law. Here, the complaint is in contract, and the new act is only a possible defense for why the railroad company refused to honor the contract. B is incorrect because the federal court would not have jurisdiction over the claim simply because the railroad was an interstate company. D is incorrect because the issue here is not supplemental jurisdiction. Importantly, the couple's entire claim is based in contract.
