Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: In an employment case, the employer moves for summary judgment. The employee responds with a declaration stating, "Everyone knows the supervisor fired me because of my age, and a coworker told me the supervisor was biased." The declaration gives no facts showing personal knowledge and identifies no admissible evidence supporting the coworker's statement. Can this declaration by itself create a genuine dispute?
Answer A: Yes, because any signed declaration automatically defeats summary judgment
Answer B: Yes, because hearsay is always considered at summary judgment
Answer C: No, because unsupported speculation and nonadmissible hearsay do not create a genuine dispute
Answer D: No, but only because employment cases can never survive summary judgment
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The declaration lacks personal-knowledge facts and admissible support.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 56 requires more than a signed paper; the facts must be admissible or capable of admissible presentation. B: Summary judgment evidence must be reducible to admissible form. D: Employment cases can survive with proper evidence.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 56 materials must be capable of being presented in admissible form and cannot rest on unsupported speculation or hearsay not reducible to admissible evidence.
