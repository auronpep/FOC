Question: Rachel is charged in federal court with robbery. She moves to suppress an alleged confession and asks to testify at the suppression hearing only about the circumstances of the interrogation. Rachel also asks that the hearing be held outside the jury's hearing. The prosecutor says that if Rachel testifies, she may be cross-examined generally about the robbery. What should the court do?
Answer A: Deny Rachel's request because any defendant who testifies at a preliminary hearing must testify before the jury.
Answer B: Hold the hearing outside the jury's hearing and limit cross-examination to the preliminary issue Rachel raises.
Answer C: Permit the prosecutor to cross-examine Rachel generally about the robbery because any testimony waives all protections.
Answer D: Refuse to hold a hearing because suppression issues are always for the jury.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When a defendant in a criminal case testifies on a preliminary question and so requests, the hearing must be conducted so the jury cannot hear it. By testifying on a preliminary question, the defendant does not become subject to cross-examination on other issues in the case.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 104 allows such a hearing outside the jury's hearing on request. C: C is wrong because testifying on a preliminary question does not open the defendant to general cross-examination on the merits. D: D is wrong because the court decides preliminary admissibility issues such as suppression.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(c), (d) / defendant testimony at preliminary hearing
