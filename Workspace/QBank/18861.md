Outline: Duress
Question: Evan's supervisor tells him that unless he falsifies customer refund records by Friday, Evan will be fired and blacklisted in the industry. Evan believes he will lose his apartment if fired. He falsifies the records and is charged with fraud. Is Evan likely to succeed on a duress defense?
Answer A: Yes, because losing housing is a serious consequence
Answer B: Yes, because the deadline left him no realistic business alternative
Answer C: No, because economic pressure is not the type of imminent physical threat required for duress
Answer D: No, because duress is available only when the defendant is charged with homicide
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Criminal-law duress is narrow. It requires a human threat of imminent death or serious bodily harm and no reasonable legal alternative. Evan faced serious economic pressure, but not an immediate physical threat. Duress fails.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Duress requires an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm. Economic harm, even severe economic harm, is not enough. B: No reasonable alternative matters only after a qualifying threat exists. The threat here is firing and blacklisting, not death or serious bodily harm. D: Duress is generally unavailable for intentional homicide and available for many nonhomicide crimes. Evan loses because the threat is economic, not because fraud is outside the doctrine.
lb_rule_reference: Duress requires an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm, not merely economic pressure, job loss, embarrassment, or financial ruin
