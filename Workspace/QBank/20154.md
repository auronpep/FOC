Question: Timothy sues Lydia for fraud. Lydia objects to Timothy's proposed testimony about a conversation Lydia had with her lawyer. Timothy argues that Lydia later repeated the same legal advice to her accountant, who was not helping the lawyer give legal advice. The judge says, “The jury can hear the conversation first and then decide whether Lydia waived any privilege.” How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Allow the jury to hear the conversation first because waiver is always a jury question.
Answer B: Decide the privilege and waiver issue before admitting the testimony.
Answer C: Admit the testimony automatically because any statement repeated to an accountant is always admissible for every purpose.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony automatically because the word lawyer appears in the fact pattern.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Privilege and waiver are preliminary admissibility questions for the court. The jury should not hear the allegedly privileged communication before the court determines whether the privilege applies or has been waived.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because privilege is a gatekeeping issue for the court, not a merits issue for the jury. C: C is wrong because disclosure to a third party may affect confidentiality, but the court still decides the privilege issue. D: D is wrong because lawyer involvement alone does not automatically establish privilege.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a) / privilege fact and preservation
