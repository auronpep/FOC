Question: Riley unlawfully enters a closed warehouse to sleep. A guard discovers Riley and, without warning, swings a metal pipe at Riley's head. Riley blocks the blow and pushes the guard away, causing the guard to fall and suffer a bruise. Riley is charged with battery and criminal trespass. What is the best analysis of Riley's defenses?
Answer A: Riley has self-defense to both charges because the guard attacked first
Answer B: Riley has no self-defense because Riley was trespassing
Answer C: Riley may assert self-defense to battery, but the defense does not automatically defeat trespass
Answer D: Riley can defeat trespass only by proving legal insanity
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The guard's pipe swing created an imminent threat of serious harm, and Riley's push was a proportionate response. That supports self-defense to the battery charge. But Riley's unlawful entry was complete before the guard attacked, and self-defense does not retroactively justify trespass.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Self-defense justifies responsive force; it does not erase the earlier unlawful entry. Riley may have a battery defense but still be guilty of trespass. B: Trespassing does not permit another person to use unjustified deadly or seriously dangerous force. Riley may defend against the pipe attack with proportionate force. D: Nothing suggests mental disease. The issue is not insanity; it is that self-defense is charge-specific and does not excuse the completed entry.
lb_rule_reference: Self-defense may justify the defendant's responsive force, but it does not automatically excuse separate offenses such as trespass, weapons possession, or burglary
