Question: A state public university system caps eligibility for its subsidized graduate-housing program at applicants under age 30. A 34-year-old doctoral student who otherwise qualifies for the housing benefit is denied participation solely because of his age. He sues under the Equal Protection Clause, arguing that no legitimate purpose justifies excluding older graduate students from affordable housing. The university defends the age cap on the ground that younger graduate students statistically earn lower wages before entering academia and are therefore more financially vulnerable than those who have had a decade in the workforce. Which of the following correctly identifies the applicable Equal Protection standard of review and likely outcome?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny, because the age cap affects the doctoral student's right to pursue graduate education, which is a fundamental right
Answer B: Intermediate scrutiny, because age classifications disproportionately burden older individuals who have less ability to adapt their life circumstances
Answer C: Rational basis review, and the age cap likely survives because targeting younger students as a financially vulnerable group is a conceivable legitimate government interest
Answer D: Rational basis review, and the age cap is unconstitutional because the university's statistical rationale is overinclusive — some students over 30 are also financially vulnerable
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Age is not a suspect or quasi-suspect classification, so rational basis review applies. Under rational basis, the age cap survives if it is rationally related to a legitimate interest. Allocating limited housing subsidies to those statistically least financially established is a rational proxy for need, and government subsidy programs may use age as a rough proxy for such conditions. The university's stated rationale — that younger students have had less time to accumulate income and savings — is at least plausible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Supreme Court has not recognized education as a fundamental right under the Equal Protection Clause. Access to subsidized housing is also not a fundamental right. A restriction on a subsidy does not trigger strict scrutiny simply because the subsidy enables valued activity. The applicable standard for an age classification is rational basis. B: The Supreme Court has expressly held that age is not a quasi-suspect classification. Massachusetts Board of Retirement v. Murgia confirmed that age-based classifications receive rational basis review. Intermediate scrutiny is reserved for sex and legitimacy classifications. Practical impact on a group does not elevate the standard of review. D: Under rational basis, over- and underinclusiveness are not fatal. Legislatures may address problems broadly and imperfectly, drawing lines that do not perfectly match the underlying concern. An age-based proxy for financial vulnerability is not required to be perfectly calibrated; it need only be rationally related to a legitimate interest.
lb_rule_reference: Age is not a suspect or quasi-suspect classification. Age-based distinctions in government programs receive rational basis review under the Equal Protection Clause.
