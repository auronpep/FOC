Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Settlement negotiations and offers of compromise
Question: After a tenant sends a written demand claiming that a warehouse spill caused property damage, the warehouse owner denies fault. At a settlement meeting, the owner's manager says, “The spill came from our broken valve, but we will pay only $30,000 if the tenant releases all claims.” At trial, the tenant offers the manager's statement that the spill came from the owner's broken valve to prove liability. How should the court rule on the owner's Rule 408 objection?
Answer A: Admit the statement because it is a factual admission by the owner's manager, separate from the dollar offer.
Answer B: Exclude the statement because it was made during compromise negotiations about a disputed claim and is offered to prove liability.
Answer C: Admit the statement because Rule 408 excludes only offers and completed settlements, not factual statements.
Answer D: Exclude the statement only if the tenant offers it to prove the amount of damages rather than liability.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 408 bars both compromise offers and statements made during compromise negotiations about a disputed claim when offered to prove the validity, invalidity, or amount of that claim. The manager's factual statement was made inside the settlement negotiation and is offered to prove liability, so the policy exclusion controls despite the party-opponent route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because nonhearsay or party-opponent status does not override an independent policy exclusion. C: C is wrong because modern Rule 408 protects statements made during compromise negotiations, not just settlement amounts. D: D is wrong because Rule 408 bars use to prove validity or amount; liability-validity use is within the prohibited purpose.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 408 statements during compromise negotiations
lb_student_diagnostic: Student stops at “party admission” and fails to ask whether a policy container independently excludes the statement.
