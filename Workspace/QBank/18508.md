Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Trial judge’s discretion to control order and presentation of evidence
Question: In a federal civil battery trial, Caleb cross-examines Ruth, who says she saw the fight. After Ruth answers that she was standing twenty feet away, Caleb repeats the same question four times and then asks, “You are just trying to make yourself important, aren't you?” Ruth becomes visibly upset. Naomi objects to the manner of questioning. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because cross-examination may never be limited when credibility is at issue.
Answer B: Sustain the objection and require Caleb to stop repetitive or harassing questioning.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if Ruth is a party rather than a nonparty witness.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because questions about credibility are not subject to courtroom control.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court should exercise reasonable control over interrogation to make the proceedings effective, avoid wasting time, and protect witnesses from harassment or undue embarrassment. Repetitive and insulting questioning may be controlled even on cross-examination.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because credibility cross-examination is important but not unlimited. C: C is wrong because nonparty witnesses may also be protected from harassment. D: D is wrong because credibility questioning remains subject to reasonable trial control.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / harassment and undue embarrassment
