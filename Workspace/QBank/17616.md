Question: A state tax exemption applies only to religious organizations that are part of a denomination with at least 100 congregations nationwide. Independent churches and smaller faith communities are excluded, even if they perform the same charitable work. The state says the threshold makes administration easier. What constitutional standard most likely applies?
Answer A: Rational basis, because tax exemptions are economic regulations
Answer B: Strict scrutiny, because the exemption favors larger denominational structures over smaller or independent religious groups
Answer C: No scrutiny, because religious charities have no First Amendment protection in tax programs
Answer D: The exemption is valid if the state can show the 100-congregation line is easy to apply
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The law classifies among religious organizations based on denominational structure and size. Such denominational preferences are highly suspect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A preference among religious denominations or structures triggers strict scrutiny. C: Government may not distribute benefits through denominational preferences without constitutional scrutiny. D: Administrative convenience alone will not justify denominational preference under strict scrutiny.
lb_rule_reference: Government classifications that prefer some religious denominations or theological structures over others trigger strict scrutiny.
