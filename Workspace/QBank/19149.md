Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal civil trial, Deborah testifies that a traffic light was green. On cross-examination, Deborah admits that she told an investigator the day after the accident, “The light was red.” The prior statement was not made under oath. The proponent offers it to prove the light was red. The opponent objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the statement to impeach Deborah, but not as substantive proof that the light was red.
Answer B: Admit the statement as substantive proof because every prior inconsistent statement is nonhearsay.
Answer C: Exclude the statement entirely because Deborah admitted making it.
Answer D: Exclude the statement unless Deborah is unavailable.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A prior inconsistent statement may be used for impeachment. Under the federal substantive route, a prior inconsistent statement is nonhearsay only if made under oath at a trial, hearing, deposition, or other proceeding. This unsworn investigator statement is impeachment-only absent another route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because not every prior inconsistent statement is substantive evidence under the federal rule. C: C is wrong because Deborah's admission does not eliminate its impeachment use. D: D is wrong because witness unavailability is not required for impeachment use.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 801(d)(1)(A) and 613 / unsworn PIS impeachment-only
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests federal sworn-route requirement and impeachment/substantive split.
