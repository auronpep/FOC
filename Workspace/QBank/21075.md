Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Solomon is charged with stealing copper wire from a construction site. The prosecutor offers a receipt for work gloves and wire cutters found in Solomon's jacket pocket the morning after the theft. A site worker testifies that similar tools were used to cut the missing wire. Solomon objects that the receipt does not prove he used the tools at the site. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the receipt because it has some tendency to connect Solomon to tools that could have been used in the theft.
Answer B: Exclude the receipt because it does not prove Solomon used the tools.
Answer C: Exclude the receipt unless the store clerk identifies Solomon as the buyer.
Answer D: Admit the receipt only if the prosecutor also produces the exact gloves and wire cutters.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The receipt need not prove the ultimate fact by itself. It has some tendency to connect Solomon to tools similar to those used in the theft, and the possession of the receipt provides a threshold link. Weaknesses go to weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because relevance and foundation do not require conclusive proof. C: C is wrong because the receipt can be connected through possession and surrounding facts. D: D is wrong because the exact tools are not required for the receipt to have probative value.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / FRE 401
lb_student_diagnostic: Treating a circumstantial link as irrelevant because it is not conclusive.
