Outline: Receipt of stolen property
Question: Two scenarios: **Scenario 1:** Rita, knowing a television was stolen, agrees to store it in her garage for the thief for two weeks. She keeps the TV in her garage, has the key, and can access it at will. **Scenario 2:** Tom, knowing a ring was stolen, is handed the ring briefly by a friend who says "hold this for a second" while the friend ties his shoe. Tom holds the ring for approximately five seconds and hands it back. Tom does not put it in his pocket or exercise any control over it. In which scenario has the defendant committed receipt of stolen property?
Answer A: Both, because both defendants physically handled stolen property with knowledge of its stolen nature
Answer B: Scenario 1 only, because only Rita exercised dominion and control over the stolen property
Answer C: Scenario 2 only, because Tom physically received the property from the thief's associate
Answer D: Neither, because the original thief retained ownership and control of the stolen property in both scenarios
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Receipt of stolen property requires (1) receiving stolen property, (2) with knowledge it is stolen, (3) with intent to permanently deprive the true owner. "Receiving" means gaining dominion and control. Rita stored the TV in her locked garage for two weeks with sole access — she exercised full dominion and control over the stolen property while knowing it was stolen. Tom, by contrast, held a ring for five seconds as a transient handler with no intent to control it and no assertion of dominion. Tom's momentary handling does not satisfy the dominion-and-control requirement. Only Rita is guilty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Receipt requires gaining dominion and control, not mere physical touching. Tom's five-second handling of the ring with no intent to exercise control and no assertion of dominion is not receipt. He was a momentary conduit with no opportunity to exercise possessory control. Dominion and control require something more than brief physical contact. C: Receipt requires dominion and control, not merely physical contact. Rita, not Tom, is the one who exercised dominion and control. Tom's five-second hold with immediate return is the paradigmatic example of insufficient handling for receipt purposes. Rita's two-week storage in her controlled space is exactly the kind of dominion and control the offense targets. D: The receipt-of-stolen-property offense does not require that the defendant permanently displace the thief's control. It requires that the defendant gain dominion and control with knowledge of stolen status. Rita clearly exercised such dominion in Scenario 1. The thief's ultimate claim to the goods is irrelevant to Rita's liability.
lb_rule_reference: Receipt of stolen property requires that the defendant gain dominion and control over the stolen property with knowledge of its stolen nature. A fleeting, momentary handling of an object without exercising dominion over it does not constitute receipt.
