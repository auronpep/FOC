Question: Shop Owner saw a customer leave with unpaid merchandise and gave a police officer a statement identifying the customer. Owner honestly and reasonably believed the statement was true, gave it only to the officer, and did not repeat it publicly. The customer later showed a receipt and sued Owner for defamation. Is Owner likely protected by privilege?
Answer A: No, because the statement turned out to be false
Answer B: No, because police reports are never privileged
Answer C: Yes, because Owner made a good-faith limited report to police for a proper purpose
Answer D: Yes, but only because the customer was in fact guilty
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Owner had reasonable grounds, spoke only to the officer, and did not abuse the privilege through knowing falsity or excessive publication.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Qualified privilege can protect good-faith proper-purpose reports even if the statement is later shown false. B: Reports made for a proper law-enforcement purpose can be qualifiedly privileged. D: The customer was not guilty. The defense rests on qualified privilege for a reasonable good-faith report.
lb_rule_reference: A good-faith report to police about suspected wrongdoing may be protected by qualified privilege if made for a proper purpose and not abused.
