Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Misrepresentation
Question: An accountant prepared a financial statement for a client, knowing the client would give it to one named bank to obtain a loan. The accountant carelessly omitted a major liability. The named bank reasonably relied on the statement, made the loan, and suffered a loss when the client defaulted. Is the bank likely to prevail on a negligent misrepresentation claim against the accountant?
Answer A: No, because the bank was not in contract with the accountant
Answer B: Yes, because the accountant supplied false information for a known bank's reliance in a business transaction
Answer C: Yes, because negligent misrepresentation reaches every foreseeable investor who might see the statement
Answer D: No, because negligent misrepresentation requires knowledge of falsity
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The accountant owed a duty in a professional information-supply context to the specifically known bank and carelessly supplied false information that caused reliance loss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Liability can extend to a known limited class, or a specifically known recipient, expected to rely on professional information. C: Negligent misrepresentation liability is limited; it does not extend to every remote foreseeable user. D: Negligent misrepresentation is based on careless false information in a duty setting, not knowledge of falsity.
lb_rule_reference: Negligent misrepresentation may impose liability on a professional information supplier to a known limited class expected to rely on the information.
