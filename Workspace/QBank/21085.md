Outline: Refreshing recollection
Question: In a federal tort trial, Ezekiel cannot remember the speed of a truck he saw two years ago. His lawyer shows him a note Ezekiel wrote that evening. Ezekiel reads it and says, “I still do not remember the speed, but the note says 55 miles per hour.” The lawyer asks Ezekiel to tell the jury the speed from the note. The opponent objects. The proponent has not yet shown that the note accurately reflected Ezekiel's knowledge when made. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because any writing used to refresh memory may be read to the jury if the refresh fails.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because the note is automatically admitted as a recorded recollection once Ezekiel cannot remember.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because the refresh failed and the proponent has not established the recorded-recollection foundation.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if the note was not an original.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: If a writing refreshes memory, the witness testifies from present recollection. If the refresh fails, the proponent may need another route, such as recorded recollection under Rule 803(5). That route requires foundation that the record concerned a matter once known, was made or adopted when fresh, and accurately reflected the witness's knowledge. Those facts have not yet been shown.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 612 does not itself permit reading a failed-refresh writing to the jury. B: B is wrong because recorded recollection is not automatic. D: D is wrong because the issue is hearsay/recorded-recollection foundation, not original-document status.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 612 and FRE 803(5) / failed refresh versus recorded recollection
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the boundary between refreshing recollection and recorded recollection.
