Question: After leaving office, a former President is sued for civil damages based on an order he issued while in office directing a federal agency to prioritize enforcement against certain regulated entities. The complaint alleges that the order was politically motivated and harmed the plaintiff's business. The former President asserts immunity. Which statement best states the immunity analysis?
Answer A: The former President has no immunity because he is no longer in office
Answer B: The former President is likely immune from civil damages for official acts within the outer perimeter of presidential responsibility
Answer C: The former President loses immunity if the plaintiff plausibly alleges a political motive
Answer D: The former President is immune only if Congress expressly authorized the enforcement order
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Directing executive enforcement priorities is an official presidential function. Civil damages claims based on official acts within the outer perimeter of presidential responsibility are barred by presidential immunity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Immunity for official acts can protect conduct taken while in office even after the President leaves office. C: Official-act immunity for civil damages does not turn on motive allegations. Courts classify the act, not the President's alleged subjective motive. D: Immunity for civil damages asks whether the act was official, not whether the act would survive a separate merits challenge under Youngstown.
lb_rule_reference: Presidential immunity analysis turns on whether the conduct is core official, other official, or unofficial; officeholder status alone does not classify every act.
