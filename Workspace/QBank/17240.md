Question: Rachel signed a written listing agreement allowing Mark to find a buyer for Rachel's ranch and negotiate a sale price. The listing agreement did not authorize Mark to sign deeds. Mark found John, signed Rachel's name to a deed conveying the ranch to John, and delivered the deed. John accepted. Who owns the ranch?
Answer A: John, because Mark had a written agreement concerning the sale of the ranch.
Answer B: John, because a broker who can negotiate a sale can always sign the deed closing that sale.
Answer C: Rachel, because the listing agreement did not give Mark written authority to execute a deed.
Answer D: Rachel only if John knew that the listing agreement did not authorize Mark to sign deeds.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is an agent-signed deed, and the decisive validity gate is written authority to execute the conveyance. Rachel's listing agreement authorized Mark to find and negotiate with a buyer, but it did not authorize him to sign a deed. The deed-signing authority lock failed, so title remains with Rachel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a writing about negotiation is not the same as written authority to execute a deed. B: B is wrong because negotiating authority does not automatically include deed-execution authority. D: D is wrong because John's notice does not cure Mark's lack of authority.
