Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: At her deposition, plaintiff testified three times that she never saw who spilled water on the floor before her fall. After defendant moved for summary judgment, plaintiff submitted an affidavit stating, "I personally saw defendant's employee spill the water." The affidavit gives no explanation for the contradiction. What may the court do with the affidavit?
Answer A: Treat the affidavit as automatically creating a genuine dispute
Answer B: Disregard the affidavit if it is an unexplained contradiction designed to create a fact issue
Answer C: Grant summary judgment solely because deposition testimony always beats affidavits
Answer D: Convert the motion into a Rule 50 motion
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 56 does not require a court to accept a sham affidavit that flatly contradicts prior deposition testimony without explanation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A direct unexplained contradiction of prior sworn testimony may be disregarded as a sham. C: The issue is the unexplained contradiction, not a universal hierarchy. D: Rule 50 applies during trial, not on a pretrial Rule 56 motion.
lb_rule_reference: Courts may disregard a sham affidavit when it directly contradicts prior deposition testimony and no adequate explanation is offered.
