Question: Mary owned a hillside home with a view across John's vacant lot. For 12 years, no structure blocked the view. John then built a lawful home on his lot, and the home blocked Mary's view. Mary has no written easement, covenant, subdivision restriction, or statute protecting the view. Mary claims an easement preventing John from blocking the view. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Mary has an implied negative easement because she enjoyed the view for many years.
Answer B: Mary has a prescriptive easement because John's lot remained vacant for more than the statutory period.
Answer C: Mary owns the airspace above John's lot because the view increased her home's value.
Answer D: Mary has no easement because a negative view restriction generally requires a recognized source such as a writing, covenant, or statute.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A landowner generally does not obtain an implied negative easement for light or view merely because a neighbor's land remained open for many years. Mary has no writing, covenant, restriction, statute, or other recognized source of a view-protection right.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because long enjoyment of a view does not by itself create an implied negative easement. B: B is wrong because vacancy is not adverse use by Mary. C: C is wrong because property value does not transfer ownership of a neighbor's airspace.
lb_rule_reference: Negative easement limits / no implied view right
