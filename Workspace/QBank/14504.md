Question: A seller and a buyer, standing on Greenacre, orally agreed to its sale and purchase for $5,000 and orally marked its bounds as "that line of trees down there, the ditch that intersects them, the fence on the other side, and that street on the fourth side." In which of the following is the remedy of reformation most appropriate?
Answer A: A: As later reduced to writing, the agreement by clerical mistake included two acres that are actually beyond the fence.
Answer B: B: The buyer reasonably thought that two acres beyond the fence were included in the oral agreement but the seller did not. As later reduced to writing, the agreement included the two acres.
Answer C: C: The buyer reasonably thought that the price orally agreed upon was $4,500, but the seller did not. As later reduced to writing, the agreement said $5,000.
Answer D: D: The buyer reasonably thought that a dilapidated shed backed up against the fence was to be torn down and removed as part of the agreement, but the seller did not. As later reduced to writing, the agreement said nothing about the shed.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. Reformation is only proper when the parties' original agreement is improperly documented in a way that does not reflect their shared understanding. A clerical error about an agreed-upon boundary line falls within this category and reformation would be proper. B
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. Reformation is not available to correct a mistake unless the parties actually reached an agreement prior to reducing the agreement to writing. C is incorrect. If the parties were confused about the price before agreeing to a contract, reformation is not an appropriate remedy, for the reasons stated above. D is incorrect. Again, a misunderstanding of terms before an agreement has been made cannot be reformed.
