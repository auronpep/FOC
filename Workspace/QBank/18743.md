Question: Peter posts a reward offering $700 to anyone who finds and returns his missing goat. Lydia reads the notice but does not begin searching. Peter dies that night. The next day, Lydia finds and returns the goat to Peter's family and seeks the reward. Is Peter's estate bound to pay the reward?
Answer A: Yes, because the reward notice was a moral obligation that survived death.
Answer B: No, because Peter's revocable unilateral offer terminated when he died before Lydia began or completed the requested performance.
Answer C: Yes, because Lydia relied by deciding that she might search later.
Answer D: No, but only because Lydia failed to notify Peter personally after returning the goat.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A revocable offer terminates on the offeror's death before acceptance. Lydia had not begun protected performance or completed the reward performance before Peter died, so the later return did not accept Peter's terminated offer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because moral obligation is not the formation route here. C: C is wrong because merely deciding to search is not acceptance or protected part performance. D: D is wrong because the decisive issue is Peter's death before acceptance, not later notice.
lb_rule_reference: common_law
lb_student_diagnostic: Student ignored death-before-acceptance timing in a unilateral reward offer.
