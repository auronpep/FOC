Question: Noah granted an electric company an easement “to install, operate, and maintain overhead electric lines across the rear ten feet of Noah's land.” Branches from Noah's trees grew into the lines and created a fire risk. After giving notice, the company proposed to trim only the branches touching or threatening the lines. Noah objects that the grant says lines, not tree trimming. Which is the best result?
Answer A: The company may reasonably trim branches that interfere with safe operation and maintenance of the lines.
Answer B: The company may cut down every tree on Noah's land because it holds a utility easement.
Answer C: The company may not trim any branch unless the deed separately grants a timber profit.
Answer D: Noah may terminate the easement because vegetation now makes the lines harder to maintain.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A utility easement to operate and maintain overhead lines includes reasonable incidental acts necessary to keep the lines safe and functional. Limited trimming of interfering branches after notice fits that purpose and does not convert the easement into ownership or a profit.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the easement does not authorize unnecessary destruction of trees outside the maintenance need. C: C is wrong because trimming interfering branches is not the same as holding a profit to remove timber. D: D is wrong because maintenance difficulty does not terminate the easement.
lb_rule_reference: Utility easement / incidental vegetation control
