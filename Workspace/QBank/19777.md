Outline: Personal knowledge
Question: In a federal hit-and-run trial, Isaiah testifies that he stood next to Benjamin's truck in a parking lot and read the license plate aloud before the truck drove away. The prosecutor asks Isaiah to state the plate number he read. Benjamin objects that the government has not produced a photograph of the plate or a certified vehicle record. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a license plate number may be proved only by a certified vehicle record.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the original-writing rule requires a photograph or registration document.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if the jury first decides Isaiah accurately remembered the number.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because Isaiah's testimony that he personally read the plate is sufficient foundation; corroborating documents are not required.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Isaiah's testimony that he personally stood next to the truck and read the plate supplies a sufficient foundation for personal knowledge. A photograph or certified record may corroborate or contradict him, but it is not required before he may testify from perception.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a certified record is not the exclusive proof method for a perceived license plate. B: B is wrong because the proponent is not proving the contents of a writing or photograph; Isaiah is testifying to what he saw. C: C is wrong because the judge screens foundation and the jury later weighs accuracy.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 / witness's own testimony establishes personal knowledge
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests that a witness's own testimony can establish personal knowledge and rejects corroboration/original-writing overdemands.
