Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: John agreed in a signed writing to sell Mary 500 clay lamps. The writing set quantity, price, and delivery date but said nothing about packaging. It did not contain a merger clause. Before signing, John orally promised to pack the lamps in straw-lined boxes. Mary offers evidence of that promise after several lamps arrive broken. Under UCC Article 2, what is the best analysis?
Answer A: The promise is automatically excluded because it was oral and made before signing.
Answer B: The promise may be admitted as a consistent additional term unless the court finds the writing was intended as a complete and exclusive statement of the agreement.
Answer C: The promise is automatically admitted even if it contradicts the writing.
Answer D: The promise is excluded unless Mary first proves fraud.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC 2-202, a final writing may be supplemented by consistent additional terms unless the writing was intended as a complete and exclusive statement. The packaging promise may supplement the silent writing if consistent and if the writing was not complete and exclusive.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because prior oral consistent additional terms are not automatically barred under UCC 2-202. C: C is wrong because contradictory terms are not admitted merely because Article 2 applies. D: D is wrong because fraud is not required for a consistent additional term.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-202 / consistent additional term unless complete and exclusive
lb_student_diagnostic: Student uses PER absolutism instead of the UCC 2-202 completion test.
