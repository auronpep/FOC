Question: Moses is charged in federal court with stealing tablets from a warehouse. The judge proposes to instruct the jury: “If you find that Moses possessed the tablets the day after they were stolen, you must presume that he stole them unless Moses explains his possession.” Moses objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because the instruction would shift the burden on an element of the crime to Moses.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because unexplained possession of recently stolen property always proves theft.
Answer C: Overrule the objection, because Moses can rebut the presumption by testifying.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Moses first proves that someone else stole the tablets.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The prosecution must prove every element beyond a reasonable doubt. A mandatory presumption requiring the jury to presume an element unless the defendant explains shifts the burden improperly. A permissive inference may be allowed in proper circumstances, but this instruction is mandatory.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the evidence may support an inference, but it does not compel a finding. C: C is wrong because making Moses rebut the presumption is the burden-shift problem. D: D is wrong because Moses need not prove an alternative thief before objecting to an improper burden-shifting instruction.
