


BARMATRIX Q#: 18282
Outline_code: 91090200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jury Trials
Outline: Demand for Jury Trial
Question: A man sued a company in federal court for claims based on the federal National Labor Relations Act. Thirty days after the service of the last pleading, the man's lawyer filed and served a demand for a jury trial. The court granted the demand, finding juries normally tried such claims and there was no compelling reason not to do so.&#10;&#10;Is the court's decision correct?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the court has the discretion to allow an untimely request for a jury trial.
Answer B: B: Yes, because there is no indication the man intended to waive his right to a jury trial.
Answer C: C: No, the right to a jury trial is waived unless demand is made within 14 days after service of the last pleading.
Answer D: D: No, a jury trial demand must be made in the initial complaint.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 38 states that failure to demand a jury trial within 14 days after service of the last pleading directed to the issue on which a jury is sought results in a waiver of the right to a jury trial. However, Rule 39 gives a court the discretion to grant a belated motion. Importantly, a court should do so in the absence of compelling reasons to the contrary. See Cox v. Masland & Sons, Inc., 607 F.2d 138 (5th Cir. 1979). Therefore, C is incorrect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect because although there is no indication the man intended to waive his right, the decision to allow a jury trial is still within the discretion of the court. D is incorrect because the demand for a jury trial does not need to be made in the initial complaint.
