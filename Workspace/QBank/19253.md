Question: Aquila agreed in writing to sell Peter a wagon “fully equipped.” The contract contained a clause stating that the writing was the parties' complete agreement. In the wagon trade, “fully equipped” could mean either with basic wheels and axles or with a cover and lanterns. Peter offers evidence of the parties' negotiations showing that both used the phrase to mean a wagon with a cover and lanterns. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Exclude the negotiation evidence because a complete agreement can never be explained by outside evidence.
Answer B: Admit the evidence to explain the ambiguous phrase “fully equipped.”
Answer C: Admit the evidence only if Peter proves Aquila committed fraud.
Answer D: Exclude the written contract because the phrase is unclear.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A merger or complete-agreement clause may bar additional terms, but it does not bar evidence used to explain an ambiguous term already in the writing. The evidence goes to meaning, not supplementation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because complete integration does not eliminate the ambiguity route. C: C is wrong because fraud is not the only basis for considering extrinsic evidence. D: D is wrong because ambiguity calls for interpretation, not automatic exclusion of the contract.
lb_rule_reference: Merger clause does not bar ambiguity explanation
lb_student_diagnostic: Student mistakes explanation for supplementation.
