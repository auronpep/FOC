Question: Abraham promised Sarah that he would pay Mary $1,000 each month for one year if Sarah sold Abraham her delivery cart. Their written contract stated, “Abraham shall pay Mary directly on the first day of each month.” Mary was not present when Abraham and Sarah signed the contract and gave no consideration. If Abraham refuses to make the first payment, may Mary enforce the promise?
Answer A: Yes, because the contract expressly required direct payment to Mary and made her an intended beneficiary.
Answer B: No, because Mary was not a party to the contract and gave no consideration.
Answer C: No, because Mary did not know of the contract when it was made.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Sarah first assigns her contract rights to Mary.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Mary may enforce as an intended third-party beneficiary. The contract specifically directs performance to Mary by name, and recognition of Mary's enforcement right gives effect to Abraham and Sarah's expressed intent. Mary does not need to be a contracting party or provide consideration.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because an intended beneficiary may enforce despite not being a party and despite giving no consideration. C: C is wrong because awareness at formation is not required for intended-beneficiary status. D: D is wrong because Mary's route is beneficiary status, not assignment.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — intended beneficiary
