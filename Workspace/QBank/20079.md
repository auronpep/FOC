Question: A store negligently left clear packing straps stretched across a customer walkway. A shopper tripped, fell, and fractured her hip. The shopper had a rare bone condition that made the fracture much worse than the fracture an average person would have suffered. Medical evidence shows the fall aggravated the condition and caused unusually extensive treatment needs. In the shopper's negligence claim against the store, may she recover for the full aggravation caused by the fall?
Answer A: Yes, because physical injury from a walkway fall was foreseeable and the store takes the shopper as it finds her
Answer B: Yes, because the store is liable for all effects of the shopper's preexisting disease, even those not aggravated by the fall
Answer C: No, because the precise severity of the hip fracture was not foreseeable
Answer D: No, because preexisting conditions always break causation
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A trip-and-fall physical injury is the foreseeable type of harm. The eggshell-plaintiff rule makes the store liable for the full extent of aggravation caused by that injury, even if the severity was unusual.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The defendant is liable for aggravation caused by the tort, not for the entire preexisting condition independent of the fall. C: The eggshell rule requires foreseeability of the type of harm, not the exact extent or severity once that type of harm occurs. D: A preexisting condition does not break causation when the defendant's tort aggravates it. The defendant remains liable for the aggravation.
lb_rule_reference: Once a foreseeable type of physical injury occurs, the defendant takes the plaintiff as found and is liable for the full extent of aggravation caused by the injury, even if unusually severe.
