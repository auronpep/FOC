Subtopic: Contract Content and Meaning
Outline: Gap-Fillers
Question: A produce buyer's signed goods contract with a grower required delivery to the buyer's north dock. During construction at that dock, the buyer's manager told the grower to use the south dock. The grower made three south-dock deliveries under the contract, and the buyer accepted and paid without objection. The grower then made a fourth identical south-dock delivery. The buyer rejected the shipment solely because the written contract said north dock. May the buyer reject the fourth shipment solely on that ground?
Answer A: Yes, because an express delivery term always defeats any later course of performance.
Answer B: No, if the repeated accepted south-dock deliveries created a waiver or modification of the delivery-place requirement and the buyer did not effectively retract it.
Answer C: Yes, because when a delivery place is disputed, the court must ignore the parties' conduct and apply the default place-of-delivery gap-filler.
Answer D: No, but only because the grower substantially performed by delivering produce somewhere on the buyer's property.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that repeated performance under the same goods contract can be relevant to waiver or modification of a term, especially when one party accepts deviations without objection. The trigger facts are the manager's instruction, three accepted south-dock deliveries, and the identical fourth delivery. The decisive locks are timing and stage crossover: this is a performance dispute, but the content question is whether the north-dock requirement remained strict after later conduct. If the waiver or modification was not effectively retracted, rejection solely for south-dock delivery is not justified.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it overreads the express term and ignores later waiver or modification. C: C is wrong because a default gap-filler is not used before checking the parties' agreement and conduct. D: D is wrong because substantial performance is not the primary route; the decisive issue is the meaning or waiver of the delivery-place term.
