Question: A merchant buyer sent a purchase order to a merchant seller for 500 tablet computers. The purchase order said, “Acceptance is limited to the terms of this order,” and included the buyer's ordinary warranty terms. The seller sent an acknowledgment accepting the order but adding: “Seller's sole obligation for defects is repair or replacement; all consequential damages are excluded.” The buyer did not expressly agree to the added limitation. The seller shipped the tablets, and the buyer accepted them. After a defect caused lost resale contracts, the seller invoked the limitation clause. Can the seller enforce the limitation clause as part of the parties' agreement?
Answer A: Yes, because the seller's acknowledgment was the last written form before shipment and therefore became the complete expression of the agreement.
Answer B: Yes, because trade usage always supplies warranty limitations in merchant-to-merchant goods transactions.
Answer C: No, because the additional limitation term did not become part of the agreement when the offer limited acceptance to its terms and the buyer did not assent to the added clause.
Answer D: No, because no contract formed once the acknowledgment included a different remedy term.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that UCC battle-of-forms analysis can determine what terms are in a goods contract after formation. The trigger facts are the buyer's express limitation of acceptance to its own terms, the seller's added warranty limitation, and no buyer assent to that added term. The decisive lock is doctrine route: this is a content-term problem under Article 2, not a common-law mirror-image or parol-evidence problem. The added remedy limitation is not enforceable on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the last-form idea is not the governing Article 2 term route. B: B is wrong because trade usage does not automatically supply a remedy limitation and cannot override the term-analysis problem. D: D is wrong because Article 2 can recognize contract formation despite nonmatching forms; the issue is whether the additional term became part of the contract.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-207 content crossover / material additional term
