Subtopic: Rights in Land
Outline: Scope
Question: Moses owned Lot 1, which benefited from an express easement across Rachel's Lot 2 to reach the road. Moses later bought adjoining Lot 3, which had never been part of Lot 1 and had no easement over Lot 2. Moses opened a gate between Lot 1 and Lot 3 so that visitors to Lot 3 could cross Lot 2 using the easement route. Which result is most accurate?
Answer A: Moses may use the easement for Lot 3 because he owns both Lot 1 and Lot 3.
Answer B: Moses may use the easement for Lot 3 because the physical route across Lot 2 has not changed.
Answer C: Rachel may stop use of the easement to benefit Lot 3 because Lot 3 is outside the original dominant estate.
Answer D: The entire easement automatically terminates because one improper use destroys all valid access rights.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An appurtenant easement benefits the dominant estate identified by the grant. The holder may not use the easement to serve additional land outside the dominant estate merely because the holder later acquired that land. Rachel may stop the Lot 3 overburdening use while the valid Lot 1 access may remain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because common ownership of the dominant parcel and an additional parcel does not expand the easement. B: B is wrong because the benefited land matters, not merely the physical route. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because misuse usually supports limiting or enjoining the excessive use, not automatic destruction of the entire easement.
lb_rule_reference: Dominant estate boundary / extra land overburden
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is checking only who owns the parcels or where the road lies, while missing which land the easement is allowed to benefit.
