Question: Naomi agreed to sell a small office building to Jacob. The written contract stated, “Seller promises to provide all roof-repair records by May 1.” It did not state that Jacob's duty to close was conditioned on receiving those records. Naomi provided the records three days late, and Jacob suffered no delay or loss from the late records. Jacob refused to close, arguing that Naomi's late delivery of the records excused his duty entirely. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Jacob's duty to close is not automatically excused; Naomi's late records are at most a covenant breach unless material.
Answer B: Jacob's duty is excused because every promise in a contract is automatically a condition to the other party's performance.
Answer C: Jacob may refuse to close because any delay in land contracts is material as a matter of law.
Answer D: Jacob may refuse to close only if Naomi acted in bad faith, because bad faith is required for every contract breach.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A promise is not automatically an express condition. The contract language says Naomi “promises” to provide records and does not make Jacob's duty to close conditional on timely receipt. A minor, harmless delay is at most a covenant breach and does not automatically discharge Jacob's duty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it collapses covenants into conditions. C: C is wrong because not every delay in a land contract automatically excuses performance. D: D is wrong because breach does not always require bad faith; the problem is that this breach is not shown to be material or a condition failure.
