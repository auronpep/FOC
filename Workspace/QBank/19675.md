Outline: Preliminary questions
Question: Mary sues John for breach of contract. John objects to Mary's proposed testimony about a conversation John had with his lawyer, arguing that the conversation was privileged. Mary responds that John's neighbor Moses sat in the room during the conversation and was not helping the lawyer provide legal advice. Who should decide whether the privilege applies?
Answer A: The judge should decide the preliminary privilege question before the testimony is admitted.
Answer B: The jury should decide privilege because it will also decide whether John breached the contract.
Answer C: Mary should decide because she is the party offering the evidence.
Answer D: The lawyer should decide because the privilege belongs to the lawyer alone.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The court decides preliminary questions about whether a privilege exists. The jury does not first hear the disputed privileged communication and decide privilege as part of its merits deliberations.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because privilege is a preliminary admissibility matter for the court. C: C is wrong because the proponent of evidence does not decide admissibility. D: D is wrong because attorney-client privilege is not a personal veto held by the lawyer alone, and the court decides the admissibility dispute.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a) / privilege preliminary fact
