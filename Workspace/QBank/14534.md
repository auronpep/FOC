Question: On January 5, a creditor loaned $1,000 to a debtor under a contract calling for the debtor to repay the loan at the rate of $100 per month payable on the first day of each month. On February 1, at the debtor's request, the creditor agreed to permit payment on February 5. On March 1, the debtor requested a similar time extension and the creditor replied, “Don't bother me each month. Just change the date of payment to the fifth of the month. But you must now make the payments by cashier's check.” The debtor said, “Okay,” and made payments on March 5 and April 5 by cashier's check. On April 6, the creditor sold the loan contract to a bank but did not tell the bank about the agreement permitting payments on the fifth of the month. On April 6, the bank wrote to the debtor: “Your debt to [the creditor] has been assigned to us. We hereby inform you that all payments must be made on the first day of the month.” Can the debtor justifiably insist that the payment date for the rest of the installments is the fifth of each month?
Answer A: A: No, because a contract modification is not binding on an assignee who had no knowledge of the modification.
Answer B: B: No, because although the creditor waived the condition of payment on the first of the month, the bank reinstated it.
Answer C: C: Yes, because although the creditor waived the condition of payment on the first of the month, the creditor could not assign to the bank his right to reinstate that condition.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the creditor could assign to the bank only those rights the creditor had in the contract at the time of the assignment.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. The parties modified the contract, which was supported by consideration; going forward, the debtor benefitted from later due dates and the creditor benefitted from receiving cashier's checks. Because the bank was the assignee, it will be subject to any claims that were valid against the creditor. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. No rule exists that requires an assignee to have notice of any contract modifications for it to be effective against the assignee's rights; rather, the assignee becomes subject to all claims or defenses, known and unknown, upon effective assignment. B is incorrect. This case did not involve the waiver of a condition. The agreement between the creditor and the debtor to change the due date to the fifth and to use cashier's checks going forward constituted consideration in support of a modification of the existing contractual terms, which the bank is now subject to after assignment. C is incorrect. As stated above, this was not a waived condition but a mutually agreed-upon modification of the terms, supported by consideration. Moreover, if this had simply been a waiver of the condition of the due date by the creditor, the bank-assignee would have been able to reinstate the payment due date as a condition.
