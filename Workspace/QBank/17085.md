Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Trespass to land
Question: A photographer intentionally flew a camera drone five feet above a homeowner's fenced backyard for ten minutes to film a private patio dinner. The drone did not touch the ground but hovered low enough to blow napkins off the table and force the guests inside. The homeowner sues for trespass to land. Is the photographer likely liable for trespass to land?
Answer A: No, because the photographer never physically stepped onto the land
Answer B: No, because airspace can never be part of possessory land interests
Answer C: Yes, because the photographer intentionally caused a physical invasion of the homeowner's low possessory airspace
Answer D: Yes, but only if the homeowner proves the photographer intended to damage the patio
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The drone intentionally entered the low space above the backyard and interfered with use of the patio. That can qualify as a physical invasion for trespass to land.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Trespass can be committed by causing a physical object to invade the possessory space of land. B: Low airspace immediately connected to use and enjoyment of the land can support trespass when invaded by a physical object. D: Trespass intent is intent to enter or cause entry, not intent to damage.
lb_rule_reference: Trespass to land can occur through intentional physical invasion of the plaintiff's possessory space, including low airspace entry that interferes with possession.
