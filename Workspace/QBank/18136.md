Question: Hannah is a merchant who sells grain. Noah is a homeowner buying grain for his private animals and is not a merchant. They orally agree that Noah will buy $2,000 worth of grain. Hannah sends Noah a signed confirmation stating the quantity and price. Noah receives it and does not object. Noah later refuses to buy. Can Hannah use the merchant-confirmation rule to satisfy the Statute of Frauds against Noah?
Answer A: No, because the merchant-confirmation rule applies only between merchants.
Answer B: Yes, because Noah did not object within 10 days.
Answer C: Yes, because the sender was a merchant and signed the confirmation.
Answer D: No, because confirmations are never sufficient unless signed by both parties.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The special confirmation rule applies between merchants. Noah is not a merchant in this transaction. Without Noah's signature or another exception, Hannah cannot use §2-201(2) merely from Noah's silence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the 10-day silence rule matters only between merchants. C: C is wrong because sender merchant status alone is insufficient. D: D is wrong because confirmations can satisfy the statute between merchants in the proper case.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(2)
