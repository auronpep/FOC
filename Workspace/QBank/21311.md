Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: PineCo, a corporation incorporated and headquartered in Eastland, negotiated a large supply contract in Eastland with a buyer from Westmont. PineCo never entered Westmont, never shipped goods to Westmont, and never directed advertising there. The buyer sued PineCo in Westmont federal court for breach of contract. Westmont's long-arm statute reaches only defendants who commit a tort in Westmont or own property in Westmont. The buyer argues that due process would allow jurisdiction because PineCo knew the buyer was from Westmont. How should the court analyze personal jurisdiction?
Answer A: Deny dismissal because due process is the only personal-jurisdiction limit in federal court
Answer B: Grant dismissal if Westmont's long-arm statute does not reach PineCo, even before the due-process analysis
Answer C: Deny dismissal because PineCo could foresee litigation anywhere its counterparty resided
Answer D: Grant dismissal only if Westmont also lacks subject-matter jurisdiction
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 4(k)(1)(A) usually makes federal PJ depend first on the forum state's long-arm reach. Westmont's statute is narrower than due process and does not cover this contract dispute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Federal courts usually borrow the forum state's long-arm statute. If the statute does not authorize jurisdiction, the court need not reach due process. C: Foreseeability alone is not a statutory hook and is not enough for defendant-created forum contacts. D: Personal jurisdiction and subject-matter jurisdiction are independent. A court can have SMJ while lacking PJ.
lb_rule_reference: A federal court ordinarily applies the forum state's long-arm statute under Rule 4(k)(1)(A), then asks whether exercising jurisdiction satisfies due process.
