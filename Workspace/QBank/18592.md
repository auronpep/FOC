Question: A family bought a home next to a quarry that had operated for years. After the purchase, the quarry doubled its blasting schedule, creating vibrations that cracked plaster and made the home difficult to occupy during afternoons. The quarry argues that the family came to the nuisance. How should the court treat that argument?
Answer A: It is a complete defense because the quarry was there first
Answer B: It is irrelevant in every nuisance case
Answer C: It is a factor in the nuisance and remedy analysis, but it does not automatically defeat the claim
Answer D: It converts the claim from nuisance to strict liability only
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The expanded blasting and serious interference still must be evaluated.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Priority in time is not an automatic bar. B: The timing can matter to reasonableness and remedy. D: The timing does not change the tort category that way.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff's move into an area where the activity already exists may affect reasonableness and remedy, but it is not a complete defense by itself.
