Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Solicitation
Question: Owen writes a letter asking Pat to burn down a warehouse. Owen places the letter in a stamped envelope and drops it in a mailbox. Before the postal service collects the mail, police seize the envelope pursuant to a warrant. Pat never receives or learns about the letter. Owen is charged with completed solicitation to commit arson. What is Owen's best argument?
Answer A: He is not guilty of completed solicitation because the request was never communicated to Pat
Answer B: He is guilty because writing the letter proves his criminal intent
Answer C: He is guilty because mailing the letter is an overt act
Answer D: He is not guilty only because arson is a property crime
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Solicitation is the communicated request or encouragement that another commit a crime. Owen's intent is clear, but the request never reached the person solicited.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Intent alone does not complete solicitation when the communication never reaches another person. C: The charge is completed solicitation, which turns on communication of the request. D: The issue is communication, not whether the target crime is property-related.
lb_rule_reference: Completed solicitation generally requires communication of the request to another person; an unreceived message may show intent but does not complete solicitation under that formulation.
