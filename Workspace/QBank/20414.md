Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Offers of proof
Question: Caleb offers a photograph through Ruth, promising that a later witness will connect the photograph to the disputed warehouse. Over Joshua's foundation objection, the court admits the photograph subject to that later connection. Caleb rests without calling the connecting witness. Joshua says nothing further. On appeal, Joshua argues that the photograph should not have remained in evidence because Caleb never supplied the promised foundation. Is the issue preserved?
Answer A: Yes, because Joshua objected before the photograph was conditionally admitted and had no duty to act later.
Answer B: No, because after the promised foundation failed to appear, Joshua needed to call the failure to the court's attention, such as by a motion to strike.
Answer C: Yes, because the judge must sua sponte remove all conditionally admitted evidence if the condition is not met.
Answer D: No, but only because photographs are always self-authenticating.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When evidence is admitted subject to later foundation, the opponent must alert the court if the promised foundation never appears. A timely motion to strike or similar motion preserves the complaint that the condition was not satisfied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the initial objection does not necessarily preserve later failure of foundation when the court admitted the evidence conditionally. C: C is wrong because the court is not required to police the missing foundation sua sponte. D: D is wrong because the issue is preservation after conditional admission, not a categorical rule about photographs.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(1) / conditionally admitted evidence / motion to strike
