internal_id: TR-100

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A construction company used explosives to break rock for a tunnel in a residential area. The company obtained permits, hired certified blasters, used standard mats, and followed all safety rules. Even so, the blast sent a pressure wave through a nearby house, shattering windows and injuring the homeowner. The homeowner sues the construction company for strict liability based on an abnormally dangerous activity. Is the homeowner likely to prevail on a strict liability claim for abnormally dangerous blasting?
Answer A: Yes, because blasting in a residential area creates strict liability for blast-wave harm despite reasonable care
Answer B: No, because the company obtained permits and used certified blasters
Answer C: No, because the homeowner must prove the company negligently placed the explosive charges
Answer D: Yes, because strict liability covers every injury occurring near a blasting site
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Blasting is a classic abnormally dangerous activity in this context, and the shattered windows resulted from the blast risk that makes the activity abnormal. Reasonable care does not defeat strict liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Due care and permits do not eliminate strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity when the injury flows from the abnormal risk. C: Strict liability for abnormally dangerous activity does not require negligent placement. It requires an abnormally dangerous activity and harm within the abnormal risk. D: Strict liability is limited to harms from the abnormal risk. This fact pattern qualifies because blast shock caused the harm, but unrelated injuries near the site would not be covered.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity covers injuries caused by the very abnormal risk that makes the activity subject to strict liability.
