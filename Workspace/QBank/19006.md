Question: A jury in a federal diversity negligence case awards the plaintiff $5,000. The judge believes the award is far too low. The forum state's practice allows additur, letting a judge raise the award unless the defendant accepts a new trial. The plaintiff asks the federal judge to use additur. May the federal court use additur?
Answer A: Yes, because state damages practice always controls in diversity
Answer B: Yes, because additur is identical to remittitur
Answer C: No, because federal law bars additur in federal court
Answer D: No, because federal courts can never order a new trial on damages
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal jury-trial rule controls this adjudicative function despite contrary state practice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Additur conflicts with the federal jury-trial function and is not available in federal court. B: Federal law treats additur differently because it increases the jury award without a jury determination. D: The court may have other remedies, including a new trial where proper; the specific problem is additur.
lb_rule_reference: A federal court may not use additur to increase a jury's damages award over a defendant's objection, even if state practice permits it.
