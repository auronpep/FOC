Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A licensed zoo kept a wolf in a reinforced enclosure that exceeded all industry standards. During a supervised feeding, a latch failed despite careful inspection, and the wolf bit a visitor standing in the viewing area. The visitor sues the zoo for strict liability. What is the zoo's strongest defense?
Answer A: The zoo is not strictly liable because it exercised reasonable care in housing and inspecting the wolf
Answer B: The zoo is strictly liable because the bite was a characteristic risk of keeping a wolf
Answer C: The zoo is liable only if the visitor proves the wolf had bitten someone before
Answer D: The zoo is not liable because its public license authorized possession of the wolf
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The injury flowed from the dangerous propensity that makes the wild animal risky, so strict liability applies despite careful precautions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Reasonable care does not defeat strict liability for harm caused by a wild animal's characteristic dangerous propensity. C: Prior incidents are not required for wild animals; dangerous propensities are treated as characteristic of the species. D: A permit may allow possession, but it does not eliminate strict tort liability for characteristic animal harm.
lb_rule_reference: A possessor of a wild animal is strictly liable for physical harm caused by a dangerous propensity characteristic of that species, even if the possessor used great care.
