Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Conversion
Question: A repair shop finished minor work on Owner's car and was paid in full. Because of a bookkeeping error, the shop insisted Owner still owed money and refused to release the car for three months. The shop kept the car locked in its lot and used it once to move equipment. Owner repeatedly demanded return. Owner sues the shop for conversion. Is Owner likely to prevail?
Answer A: No, because the shop initially received the car lawfully
Answer B: No, because conversion requires destroying or selling the chattel
Answer C: Yes, because the shop seriously interfered with Owner's dominion by withholding the car for months after payment and demand
Answer D: Yes, but only for trespass to land because the car sat in the shop's lot
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The shop's prolonged refusal to return the paid-for car, plus exclusive control and use, is serious dominion inconsistent with Owner's rights.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Lawful initial possession can become conversion when the possessor later seriously interferes with the owner's dominion after demand. B: Extended wrongful withholding can also be serious dominion supporting conversion. D: Owner's invaded interest is dominion over the car, a chattel. Conversion is the better route.
lb_rule_reference: Serious intentional dominion over another's chattel, such as withholding it for an extended period inconsistent with the owner's rights, can constitute conversion.
