Question: A seller agreed to sell 50 wool cloaks to John for cash. The contract did not provide credit. The seller brought conforming cloaks to John's shop on the due date and asked for the price before unloading them. John said he would pay after inspecting and selling the cloaks the next week. The seller refused to leave the cloaks. Who is most likely in breach?
Answer A: John, because the seller's tender of conforming goods entitled the seller to demand payment as a concurrent condition of delivery.
Answer B: The seller, because unloading goods must always occur before payment can be demanded.
Answer C: The seller, because buyers always receive a reasonable time after delivery to pay.
Answer D: Neither, because a sale of goods is unenforceable unless credit terms are stated.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: In a cash sale of goods, the buyer's payment duty is generally concurrent with the seller's tender of conforming goods. The seller need not leave goods with John on an unsecured promise to pay next week. John's refusal to pay at tender was the breach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the seller's delivery obligation may be conditioned on concurrent payment. C: C is wrong because a reasonable-time payment rule does not override cash-on-tender terms or defaults. D: D is wrong because the absence of credit terms does not make the sale unenforceable.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-507 / delivery on condition
