Question: Plaintiff sues ForeignCo in federal court on a federal antitrust claim. Plaintiff invokes Rule 4(k)(2). ForeignCo responds that it is subject to personal jurisdiction in State C for this claim because it directly negotiated the challenged agreements there and would accept suit there. Plaintiff prefers State A and argues that Rule 4(k)(2) still applies because ForeignCo is not subject to jurisdiction in State A. How should the court analyze Rule 4(k)(2)?
Answer A: Apply Rule 4(k)(2) because ForeignCo lacks State A contacts
Answer B: Decline Rule 4(k)(2) if ForeignCo is subject to jurisdiction in State C for this claim
Answer C: Apply Rule 4(k)(2) only if Plaintiff also proves complete diversity
Answer D: Decline Rule 4(k)(2) because foreign corporations can never be sued in federal court
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 4(k)(2) is unavailable when a state forum can exercise jurisdiction over the defendant for the federal claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 4(k)(2) asks whether the defendant is subject to jurisdiction in any state's courts for the claim, not just the chosen forum. C: Rule 4(k)(2) applies to federal-law claims and does not require diversity. D: Foreign defendants can be subject to federal personal jurisdiction when the rule's requirements and due process are satisfied.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 4(k)(2) applies only if the claim arises under federal law, the defendant is not subject to jurisdiction in any state's courts of general jurisdiction, and exercising jurisdiction is constitutional.
