MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Apex Global Holdings Ltd. ("Apex") is incorporated in England with its global headquarters in London. Apex wholly owns Apex North America Inc. ("ANA"), a Delaware corporation with its principal place of business in the State of Caldmore. ANA operates independently of Apex — it has its own board of directors, officers, and separate accounting records. ANA sets its own prices, manages its own employees, and makes its own operational decisions. Apex reviews ANA's annual financial performance at a year-end meeting but does not supervise day-to-day operations. ANA's Caldmore operations are extensive: 1,100 employees, 3 facilities, and $200 million in annual Caldmore revenue. A Caldmore employee of ANA brought a lawsuit against Apex (not ANA) in Caldmore federal court, alleging that Apex's company-wide policy on executive compensation violated federal wage law. Apex moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. Does the Caldmore court have personal jurisdiction over Apex?
Answer A: Yes, because Apex's wholly owned subsidiary ANA has extensive contacts with Caldmore — 1,100 employees, 3 facilities, and $200 million in revenue — and those contacts are attributed to Apex as the parent
Answer B: Yes, because Apex has general jurisdiction in Caldmore as the corporate parent of a company that is incorporated and based in Caldmore
Answer C: No, because ANA and Apex are separate legal entities, ANA operates independently, and the plaintiff has not shown the alter-ego or agency control necessary to attribute ANA's Caldmore contacts to Apex
Answer D: No, because Apex's company-wide compensation policy is not specifically directed at Caldmore — a policy applied nationwide cannot supply specific jurisdiction in any single state
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Corporate separateness is the default rule: a parent's contacts are its own; a subsidiary's contacts are its own. Attribution of a subsidiary's contacts to the parent requires a showing that the parent exerts such pervasive day-to-day control as to make the subsidiary its alter ego or mere agent. Here, ANA has its own board, officers, accounting, operational decisions, and pricing authority. Apex's involvement is limited to annual financial review — classic arm's-length parent oversight, not alter-ego control. ANA's Caldmore contacts cannot be attributed to Apex. Apex has no independent Caldmore contacts. The motion to dismiss should be granted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Daimler* expressly addressed — and rejected — the automatic attribution of a subsidiary's contacts to its parent. ANA operates independently with its own board, officers, accounting, pricing decisions, and employment management. Apex's oversight is limited to annual financial review. Without an alter-ego or agency showing (day-to-day control rendering the subsidiary a mere instrumentality), ANA's Caldmore contacts cannot be attributed to Apex. Corporate separateness is respected. B: Being the parent of a company whose home state is Caldmore does not make Caldmore Apex's home state. *Daimler* and *Goodyear* define the at-home inquiry by the defendant's own characteristics — its own incorporation and PPB. Apex is incorporated in England and headquartered in London; ANA's Caldmore connections are ANA's, not Apex's. Ownership of a Caldmore-based subsidiary does not place Apex at home in Caldmore. D: This answer reaches the correct outcome but applies a different theory — specific-jurisdiction targeting — rather than the corporate-separateness attribution analysis. The correct basis for dismissal is that ANA's contacts cannot be attributed to Apex, not that the policy failed to target Caldmore specifically. Additionally, the "nationwide policy never supplies specific PJ in any state" statement is overbroad — a company that implements a policy affecting all states could have specific-jurisdiction exposure in each state where affected employees are located, if the company has its own contacts there.
lb_rule_reference: The separate corporate form is respected for personal jurisdiction purposes. A parent corporation is not subject to jurisdiction in a state merely because its subsidiary operates there. To attribute a subsidiary's contacts to the parent, the plaintiff must show that the parent exerts extensive day-to-day control over the subsidiary, making the subsidiary a mere agent or instrumentality of the parent (alter ego). *Daimler AG v. Bauman* (2014) (rejecting attribution of subsidiary's contacts without agency showing).
