Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A helmet seller's website states, "This helmet meets Safety Standard X for cycling impacts." The helmet does not meet that standard, and it fails in a cycling crash. The buyer relied on the standard statement in choosing the helmet and sues on a warranty theory. Which statement best describes the warranty issue?
Answer A: The standard statement can support an express-warranty theory because it is a factual affirmation about the product
Answer B: The statement is nonactionable puffery because every website statement is opinion
Answer C: Warranty theory is impossible in any products case
Answer D: The buyer must prove the seller was careless in writing the website copy
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A specific factual statement about compliance with a safety standard differs from vague sales praise.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: "Meets Safety Standard X" is a factual representation. C: Products questions can test strict liability, negligence, and warranty theories separately. D: Express warranty focuses on the affirmation and bargain, not necessarily negligent drafting.
lb_rule_reference: A product-warranty theory depends on warranty concepts such as factual affirmations or descriptions that become part of the bargain; vague puffery is different.
