Question: Isaac openly and adversely used a lane across Leah's land for six years to reach Lot 1. Isaac then conveyed Lot 1 to Rachel by deed, and Rachel continued the same open adverse use of the lane for five more years. The jurisdiction's prescriptive period is 10 years. May Rachel tack Isaac's use to her own to claim a prescriptive easement?
Answer A: No, because prescriptive easement periods can never be tacked.
Answer B: No, because tacking applies only when the claimant seeks fee title.
Answer C: Yes, because Rachel is in privity with Isaac through the deed and continued the same use.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Leah signed Rachel's deed.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Successive periods of adverse use may generally be tacked when there is privity between users and continuity of the same claimed use. Isaac deeded Lot 1 to Rachel, and Rachel continued the same lane use.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because tacking is not categorically barred in prescription. B: B is wrong because tacking can apply to prescriptive easement claims as well as adverse possession. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because Leah's signature is not required to establish privity between Isaac and Rachel.
lb_rule_reference: Tacking prescriptive use with privity
lb_student_diagnostic: The repair signal is missing transfer privity and restarting the prescriptive clock unnecessarily.
