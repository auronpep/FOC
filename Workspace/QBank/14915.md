Question: Three months ago, a buyer agreed in writing to buy a property owner's single-family residence, Liveacre, for $110,000. The buyer paid the owner a $5,000 deposit to be applied to the purchase price. The contract stated that the owner had the right at his option to retain the deposit as liquidated damages in the event of the buyer's default. The closing was to have taken place last week. Six weeks ago, the buyer was notified by his employer that he was to be transferred to another job 1,000 miles away. The buyer immediately notified the owner that he could not close, and therefore he demanded the return of his $5,000. The owner refused, waited until after the contract closing date, listed with a broker, and then conveyed Liveacre for $108,000 to a subsequent purchaser found by the real estate broker. The subsequent purchaser paid the full purchase price and immediately recorded his deed. The subsequent purchaser knew of the prior contract with the original buyer. In an appropriate action, the original buyer seeks to recover the $5,000 deposit from the owner. The most probable result will be that the owner
Answer A: A: must return the $5,000 to the original buyer, because the owner can no longer carry out his contract with the original buyer.
Answer B: B: must return the $5,000 to the original buyer, because he was legally justified in not completing the contract.
Answer C: C: must return $3,000 to the original buyer, because the owner's damages were only $2,000.
Answer D: D: may keep the $5,000 deposit, because the original buyer breached the contract.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. The buyer breached the contract and the parties' agreed-upon liquidated damages clause that allowed the owner to keep the $5,000 will be upheld by the court as it is less than 10% of the purchase price and not a penalty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The buyer will not have an impossibility defense because his job was transferred, which does not amount to the type of unforeseen event that will discharge the buyer's duty of performance. B is incorrect. Whether or not the buyer was justified in not completing the contract is irrelevant to the issue of liquidated damages. C is incorrect. Although the owner only lost $2,000 on the eventual sale, the original buyer breached the sales contract and the owner may, therefore, keep the $5,000 deposit as damages.
