Outline: Conditions
Question: David hired Leah to renovate a courtyard. The contract stated, “Final payment is due only after the architect issues a completion certificate.” Leah substantially finished the work and asked for inspection. David, unhappy about market conditions, repeatedly locked the gate and refused to allow the architect to inspect. The architect never issued the certificate. What is the most likely result?
Answer A: David owes nothing because the certificate was an express condition and did not occur.
Answer B: David cannot rely on the absence of the certificate because he prevented the inspection needed for the condition to occur.
Answer C: Leah can recover only in restitution because prevention destroys the contract.
Answer D: Leah must prove that the architect certainly would have issued the certificate.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The certificate requirement was an express condition to final payment, but a party who prevents or hinders the condition cannot rely on its nonoccurrence. David's refusal to allow inspection excuses the certificate condition.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because strict occurrence does not protect the party who prevented occurrence. C: C is wrong because the contract remains the framework; prevention can excuse the condition rather than destroy the contract. D: D is wrong because Leah need not prove certainty of the certificate when David blocked the inspection process.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law express condition / prevention
