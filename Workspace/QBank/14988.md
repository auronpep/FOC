Question: A landowner conveyed his land by quitclaim deed to his daughter and son “as joint tenants in fee simple.” The language of the deed was sufficient to create a common law joint tenancy with right of survivorship, which is unmodified by statute. The daughter then duly executed a will devising her interest in the land to a friend. Then the son duly executed a will devising his interest in the land to a cousin. The son died, and later the daughter died. Neither had ever married. The daughter's friend and the cousin survived. After both wills have been duly probated, who owns what interest in the land?
Answer A: A: The cousin owns the fee simple.
Answer B: B: The daughter's friend and the cousin own equal shares as joint tenants with right of survivorship.
Answer C: C: The daughter's friend and the cousin own equal shares as tenants in common.
Answer D: D: The daughter's friend owns the fee simple.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. Because a joint tenancy with right of survivorship existed, on the death of the son, the interest of the daughter increased, and she then owned the land alone and in fee simple. She had the right to devise that entire interest via her will to her friend, and the cousin receives nothing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The son had no separate interest in the land to convey by will to the cousin because the right of survivorship automatically increased the daughter's interest in the land to 100%. The cousin will receive nothing and the friend will own the land in fee simple. B is incorrect. Because a joint tenancy with right of survivorship is not devisable or inheritable and cannot be severed by a will, the son's interest terminated on his death and he had no interest to convey to the cousin. C is incorrect. As explained above, the son's interest terminated on his death and he had no interest to convey to the cousin.
