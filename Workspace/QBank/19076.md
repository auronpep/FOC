Question: For three years, Hannah bought jars from Caleb under annual contracts, and each year Caleb allowed Hannah a 10% discount for payment within 15 days. Their new signed goods contract uses the same form, is silent about discounts, and contains a merger clause. Hannah pays within 15 days and claims the 10% discount. Caleb objects that the merger clause bars all evidence of the prior discount practice. How should the court rule on the course-of-dealing evidence? How should the court rule on the course-of-dealing evidence?
Answer A: Admit it, because course of dealing may explain or supplement a goods contract even when the writing is final and contains a merger clause, if reasonably consistent.
Answer B: Exclude it, because merger clauses always erase the parties' prior dealings.
Answer C: Exclude it, because only usage of trade can supplement a goods contract.
Answer D: Admit it only if Caleb expressly repeated the discount in the new writing.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Article 2 and UCC 1-303, course of dealing between the parties may explain or supplement the agreement when reasonably consistent with the express terms. A merger clause is not an automatic bar to this UCC meaning source.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because merger-clause absolutism is too rigid under the UCC course-evidence framework. C: C is wrong because course of dealing is an independent meaning source. D: D is wrong because course of dealing can supply meaning even when the current writing is silent.
