Subtopic: Rights in Land
Outline: Scope
Question: A deed granted Miriam, as owner of Lot 1, “a right to use the driveway over Lot 2 for ingress and egress to the residence on Lot 1.” Miriam lives on Lot 1. Her family members, dinner guests, and ordinary delivery drivers use the driveway to reach the residence. Nathan, the owner of Lot 2, argues that only Miriam personally may use the driveway because the deed names Miriam. May Nathan bar Miriam's guests and ordinary delivery drivers from using the driveway?
Answer A: No, because reasonable use by household members, guests, and ordinary delivery drivers is incidental to ingress and egress to the residence.
Answer B: Yes, because an easement may be used only by the named grantee personally unless the deed lists invitees.
Answer C: Yes, because any use by delivery drivers converts a residential easement into a commercial easement.
Answer D: No, because the easement permits any use of Lot 2 that benefits Miriam economically.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The scope of an express easement is measured by the grant's language and purpose. A residential ingress-and-egress easement ordinarily includes reasonable incidental use by residents, guests, and ordinary delivery drivers reaching the residence. Those users are not expanding the easement beyond Lot 1 or changing its basic purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because an appurtenant access easement is not limited to the named owner's personal footsteps. C: C is wrong because ordinary residential deliveries do not automatically convert the use into an impermissible commercial use. D: D is wrong because the easement remains limited by its residential access purpose.
lb_rule_reference: Express easement scope / reasonable incidental use
lb_student_diagnostic: The recurring trap is reading the named grantee too literally and excluding ordinary incidental users.
