Question: In a federal injury trial, Hannah testifies that she saw bruises on Isaac's arm the day after the incident. A nurse also took photographs of Isaac that day, but the photographs are unavailable. The defendant objects that Hannah cannot testify without the photographs. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because Hannah may testify from personal knowledge about what she saw.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the photographs are the best evidence of the bruises.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because only medical experts may describe bruises.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Hannah can authenticate the missing photographs.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Hannah is proving her own observation of Isaac's bruises, not the contents of the photographs. A witness with personal knowledge may testify to the observed condition. The original-writing rule does not bar testimony about an event or condition independently perceived.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the original-writing rule applies when proving contents, not when a witness proves an observed condition. C: C is wrong because ordinary bruise observation does not require expert testimony. D: D is wrong because the missing photographs are not the proof channel.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 and FRE 1002 / observed event versus contents
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests original-writing false positive and personal-knowledge foundation.
