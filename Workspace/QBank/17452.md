Subtopic: Performance, Breach, and Discharge
Outline: Covenants
Question: Mary agreed to perform at John's theater “provided that the city issues a public-event permit at least ten days before the performance.” The city denied the permit. Mary had already rehearsed and was willing to perform if John found another venue. John refused to pay Mary. If Mary sues for the performance fee, what is the likely result?
Answer A: Mary wins because she was ready and willing to perform.
Answer B: Mary wins because denial of a permit is John's breach as a matter of law.
Answer C: John wins because the permit was a condition precedent to his duty to pay for the performance.
Answer D: John wins only if the city's permit denial was unforeseeable.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The contract made the city permit a condition precedent to the performance/payment obligation. Because the permit did not issue, John's duty to pay the performance fee did not mature unless the condition was excused. Readiness to perform does not itself satisfy the express condition.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Mary's willingness does not cause the permit condition to occur. B: B is wrong because no facts show John caused the denial. D: D is wrong because foreseeability is not the condition route; the duty never matured because the condition failed.
lb_rule_reference: Condition precedent to duty
