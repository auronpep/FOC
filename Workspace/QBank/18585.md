Question: Daniel contracted to sell Esther 50,000 jars of sauce. The signed contract required “red allergen-warning labels.” Daniel delivered jars with blue warning labels. Daniel offered evidence that, in the specialty-food trade, “allergen-warning labels” may be any bright color. Esther objected that the contract did not merely require warning labels; it required red labels. Which statement is least likely to be accepted?
Answer A: The trade usage controls even if it cannot reasonably be reconciled with the express red-label requirement.
Answer B: The usage might explain what counts as an allergen-warning label, but it cannot eliminate an irreconcilable express color requirement.
Answer C: The merger clause does not automatically bar all trade-usage evidence in a goods contract.
Answer D: If a consistent trade usage were offered, the court would still require sufficient notice to prevent unfair surprise.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that express terms and usage should be construed consistently when reasonable, but express terms prevail if reconciliation is unreasonable. The trigger facts are an express “red” label requirement and a usage that would permit blue labels. The decisive lock is hierarchy. A is least likely because it treats trade usage as supreme over a direct express conflict.
