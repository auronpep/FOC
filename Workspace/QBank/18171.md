Question: Tariq robbed a jewelry store at gunpoint. A security guard fired at Tariq during the robbery, missed, and killed a shopper. The jurisdiction expressly follows the proximate-cause theory of felony murder. Tariq was charged with felony murder for the shopper's death. Is Tariq guilty of felony murder?
Answer A: No, because the guard, not Tariq or a co-felon, fired the fatal shot
Answer B: No, because the shopper was not the intended victim of the robbery
Answer C: Yes, because the armed robbery proximately caused the guard's responsive shot and the shopper's death
Answer D: Yes, but only because Tariq personally fired a gun
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The jurisdiction uses proximate-cause theory. Tariq's armed robbery foreseeably provoked a guard's defensive gunfire, which killed a shopper. The death was proximately caused by the felony.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The question expressly supplies proximate-cause theory. Under that theory, liability can attach when the felony proximately causes the death. B: Felony murder does not require the death of an intended target. The issue is proximate causation from the felony. D: Tariq did not fire the fatal shot. Liability arises from the supplied proximate-cause theory, not from personal firing.
lb_rule_reference: If the jurisdiction supplies the proximate-cause theory, felony murder can attach to deaths proximately caused by the felony, even when the immediate shooter is a resisting non-felon.
