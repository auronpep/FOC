Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Isaiah hired Jonah to repair a stone wall. The parties signed a detailed writing stating that it was the complete agreement and that Jonah would repair the north wall for $4,000. Before signing, Jonah orally promised also to repair the south gate for no extra charge. Isaiah offers the oral promise after Jonah repairs only the north wall. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The oral promise is admissible because the UCC always allows consistent additional terms.
Answer B: The oral promise is admissible because all repair contracts involve goods.
Answer C: The oral promise is barred because this services contract has a complete and exclusive writing, and the oral promise would add an additional duty.
Answer D: The oral promise is barred only if Jonah proves Isaiah suffered no damages.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: This is a services contract governed by common-law parol evidence principles, not Article 2's goods rules. A complete and exclusive writing bars a prior oral promise adding another repair obligation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Article 2 does not govern the wall-repair services contract. B: B is wrong because repair services do not become a goods contract merely because stone is involved. D: D is wrong because damages do not control the admissibility or existence of the added oral duty.
