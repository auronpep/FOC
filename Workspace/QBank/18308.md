Outline: Embezzlement
Question: Harmon was charged with embezzlement from her employer. The prosecution did not separately produce the company's internal audit report, which showed irregularities but was inconclusive as to who caused them. Harmon's own attorney had commissioned the same audit report through a civil subpoena in a parallel civil suit months before the criminal trial, reviewed it, and used portions of it in the civil proceeding. Harmon moved post-conviction to vacate on Brady grounds, arguing the prosecution had a duty to produce the audit report. Did the prosecution's failure to separately disclose the audit report to defense counsel violate Brady?
Answer A: Yes, because the prosecution's duty to disclose is absolute and cannot be excused by the defense's independent access to the material
Answer B: Yes, because the report was in the prosecution's possession, regardless of what the defense knew
Answer C: No, because there is no Brady violation when defense counsel already had access to the information through independent means
Answer D: No, because the audit report was not sufficiently exculpatory to trigger the Brady obligation
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Brady's suppression element requires that the evidence was withheld from the defense. Where the defendant or counsel already possessed the evidence or could have discovered it through the exercise of due diligence — as Harmon's attorney did by obtaining the audit report through a civil subpoena and actually using it — the prosecution's failure to separately produce it does not constitute Brady suppression. There is no constitutional obligation to hand the defense what the defense already has.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Courts have consistently held that there is no Brady violation when the defendant or defense counsel possessed or had access to the exculpatory information and could have used it with reasonable diligence. The "suppression" element of Brady requires that the prosecution actually withheld information the defense lacked. B: Brady violations require prejudice to the defendant. If the defense already had independent access to the same information and used it in parallel litigation, there is no suppression of material the defense lacked. The prosecution's mere possession of a document the defense also had does not automatically constitute a Brady violation. D: The correct answer turns on the independent-access doctrine, not on whether the audit was exculpatory. If the report were exculpatory and the defense had not independently obtained it, Brady would require disclosure. Choosing this answer avoids the real doctrinal point.
lb_rule_reference: Brady is not violated when the defendant or defense counsel was aware of the exculpatory evidence or could have discovered it through reasonable diligence; suppression implies the evidence was withheld from the defense, which requires the defense to have lacked the information
