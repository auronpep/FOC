Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Caleb owned a single 100-acre tract in Jordan County. He signed and delivered to Deborah a deed stating, “I convey the western 20 acres of my 100-acre tract in Jordan County to Deborah.” The western boundary of the tract was fixed and undisputed. The deed had no seal, no survey attached, and was not recorded. Who owns the western 20 acres?
Answer A: Caleb, because the deed lacked metes and bounds.
Answer B: Caleb, because the deed lacked a seal.
Answer C: Caleb, because the deed was not recorded.
Answer D: Deborah, because the description reasonably identifies the land conveyed and the missing seal, survey, and recording do not defeat validity between the parties.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is property description. The deed describes an identifiable portion of Caleb's only 100-acre tract: the western 20 acres. Because the land can be located with reasonable certainty, and no seal, attached survey, or recording is required on these facts, title to that portion passes to Deborah.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because metes and bounds are not always required. B: B is wrong because a seal is not an ordinary deed-validity requirement here. C: C is wrong because recording is not required for validity between Caleb and Deborah.
lb_rule_reference: Property description / identifiable portion
