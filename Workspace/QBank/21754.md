Question: Rina and Cole robbed a store. The store owner pulled a gun and fired at Cole, but the bullet missed Cole and killed a customer. The jurisdiction follows the common-law agency theory of felony murder. Prosecutors charged Rina with felony murder for the customer's death. Is Rina guilty of felony murder under the agency theory?
Answer A: Yes, because the death would not have occurred but for the robbery
Answer B: Yes, because the customer was innocent rather than a co-felon
Answer C: No, because a non-felon victim fired the fatal shot in resistance to the robbery
Answer D: No, because robbery is not an inherently dangerous felony
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Agency theory limits felony murder to killings by felons or their agents in furtherance of the felony. The store owner's defensive shot caused the death. Rina is not liable under this specified theory.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under the agency theory, but-for causation is not enough. The fatal shot was fired by a resisting victim, not a felon acting in furtherance of the robbery. B: The key is who killed, not only who died. The shooter was a non-felon resisting the felony. D: Robbery is a qualifying predicate. The failure is the agency-theory limit on who caused the death.
lb_rule_reference: Under the agency theory, felony murder generally requires the killing to be committed by a felon or co-felon acting in furtherance of the felony, not by a victim or other non-felon resisting it.
