Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: After discovery closes, a customer sues a store for negligence. The store moves for summary judgment and points out that the customer has no witness, video, inspection record, or expert evidence showing the store caused or knew about the spill. The customer's opposition repeats the complaint's allegation that the store "must have known." What is the strongest reason to grant summary judgment?
Answer A: The complaint failed to use detailed enough language at the pleading stage
Answer B: The store showed a lack of evidence on an essential element, and the customer responded only with allegations
Answer C: Negligence claims are never proper for summary judgment
Answer D: The customer automatically loses because she has the burden of proof at trial
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Once the record gap is identified, the nonmovant must cite evidence from which a reasonable jury could find notice or causation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: This is a Rule 56 motion after discovery, so the question is evidence in the record, not pleading detail. C: Negligence claims can be resolved on summary judgment when the Rule 56 record leaves no genuine material dispute. D: Having the trial burden does not automatically defeat a claim; the problem is the failure to produce evidence after the movant identified the gap.
lb_rule_reference: A movant may obtain summary judgment by showing the nonmovant lacks evidence on an essential element, after which the nonmovant must cite record evidence creating a genuine dispute.
