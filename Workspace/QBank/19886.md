Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police receive a detailed victim statement and matching surveillance footage showing Paul committing a felony assault. Officers find Paul walking on a public street and arrest him without an arrest warrant. They do not enter any home or private premises. Was the warrantless arrest unconstitutional?
Answer A: No, because police had probable cause and arrested Paul in a public place
Answer B: Yes, because every arrest requires a warrant unless the officer sees the crime occur
Answer C: Yes, because surveillance footage can never establish probable cause
Answer D: No, because arrests in public are never Fourth Amendment seizures
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A public felony arrest can be made without a warrant when supported by probable cause. The home-entry warrant rule is not implicated.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Public felony arrests do not require an arrest warrant when officers have probable cause. C: Probable cause can rest on reliable victim statements and corroborating video. D: An arrest is a seizure. It is valid here because probable cause supported a public arrest.
lb_rule_reference: Police may make a warrantless arrest in a public place when they have probable cause to believe the suspect committed a felony.
