Question: In a federal fraud trial, Abigail testifies on direct that she mailed an invoice. On cross-examination, the defendant shows Abigail a prior email that seems to suggest she was unsure whether she mailed it. On redirect, Abigail's lawyer asks her to explain the email. The defendant objects that redirect may not revisit the mailing issue. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because redirect may never address matters already covered on direct.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because redirect may address matters raised on cross and permit the witness to explain the apparent inconsistency.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Abigail's prior email is first admitted substantively.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the defendant consents to reopening direct examination.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court controls the mode and order of examination. Redirect may properly address matters raised on cross-examination, including explaining or rehabilitating testimony affected by cross-examination.
