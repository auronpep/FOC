Outline: Truthfulness
Question: In a federal negligence trial, Abigail testifies that she saw Samson speeding. On cross-examination, Samson's lawyer asks Abigail, “Isn't it true that you got into a fistfight outside a market two years ago?” The lawyer offers the question only to attack Abigail's character for truthfulness. Abigail objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the prior fistfight is not probative of Abigail's character for truthfulness.
Answer B: Sustain the objection only if Abigail has a reputation for peacefulness.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because any prior misconduct may be used to attack credibility.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because specific acts are always allowed on cross-examination.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A specific act may be inquired into under Rule 608(b) only if it is probative of the witness's character for truthfulness or untruthfulness. A fistfight may suggest violence, but the facts do not make it probative of truthfulness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the issue is not Abigail's peacefulness. C: C is wrong because not all misconduct attacks truthfulness. D: D is wrong because cross-examination about specific acts is limited and discretionary, not automatic.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(b) / act not probative of truthfulness
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the common trap of treating every bad act as truthfulness impeachment.
