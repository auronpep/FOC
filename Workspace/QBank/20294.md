Outline: Gap-Fillers
Question: Rachel hired a consultant to provide “launch support” for a new nonprofit program. The signed services agreement listed the fee and launch date, but it did not define “launch support.” Before signing, both sides used a written planning chart that described launch support as volunteer training, press scheduling, and donor-call scripts. When Rachel sought to introduce the planning chart to explain the phrase, the consultant objected that the final writing did not include the chart. Should the court admit the planning chart for this purpose?
Answer A: Yes, if the chart is offered to explain the ambiguous services phrase rather than to add an inconsistent new duty.
Answer B: No, because any prior written planning document is automatically barred by the parol evidence rule.
Answer C: No, because the phrase “launch support” makes the services contract illusory unless a UCC quantity gap-filler supplies exact tasks.
Answer D: No, because the chart is collateral and therefore can never be used to interpret a final writing.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that extrinsic evidence may be admitted to explain an ambiguous term in a services contract. The trigger facts are the undefined phrase “launch support” and the offered planning chart used to define that phrase. The decisive locks are source law and offered purpose: this is common-law services interpretation, and the chart is offered to explain meaning, not to contradict the writing or invoke a UCC gap-filler.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a prior writing is not automatically excluded when it is offered to explain ambiguity. C: C is wrong because this services contract is not governed by UCC gap-fillers and a flexible scope phrase is not automatically illusory. D: D is wrong because the label “collateral” does not bar evidence whose function is to explain an ambiguous term.
