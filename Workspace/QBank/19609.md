Question: Near the end of a federal civil trial, Leah asks the court to take judicial notice from an official government website that a bridge was closed on the date alleged in the complaint. Rebekah objects that it is too late because all witnesses have finished testifying. Assuming the fact is otherwise proper for judicial notice, is the request too late under Rule 201?
Answer A: Yes, because judicial notice must be requested before the first witness testifies.
Answer B: No, because the court may take judicial notice at any stage of the proceeding.
Answer C: Yes, because judicial notice is available only before trial.
Answer D: No, but only if both parties consent to reopening the evidence.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 201 allows a court to take judicial notice at any stage of the proceeding. If the fact otherwise qualifies, the request is not barred merely because it comes after testimony has closed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and C are wrong because Rule 201 does not impose those timing cutoffs. D is wrong because the court's power to take judicial notice does not depend on both parties consenting to reopen evidence, though the parties may be entitled to be heard.
