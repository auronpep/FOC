Question: At a rally, a speaker says, "Someday people should refuse to obey this tax law." The crowd listens quietly and leaves. The state prosecutes the speaker for incitement, arguing that the speech encouraged lawbreaking in general. Is the prosecution likely constitutional?
Answer A: Yes, because advocacy of unlawful conduct is always unprotected
Answer B: Yes, because the speaker criticized a tax law
Answer C: No, because the statement was not directed to likely imminent lawless action
Answer D: No, because incitement can never be punished
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The speech urged possible future disobedience in abstract terms and produced no imminent lawless action.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Abstract advocacy is protected unless the incitement standard is met. B: Criticism of law is core protected speech absent an unprotected category. D: Incitement can be punished when the strict imminence and likelihood requirements are met.
lb_rule_reference: Incitement requires advocacy directed to producing imminent lawless action and likely to produce that action; abstract advocacy is protected.
