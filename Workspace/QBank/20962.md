Question: A defendant never identifies a key employee in initial disclosures or discovery responses. On the first day of trial, the defendant tries to call the employee as a surprise liability witness. The plaintiff objects. The defendant offers no substantial justification for the omission. What is the likely consequence?
Answer A: The witness may be excluded unless the failure was substantially justified or harmless
Answer B: The witness must testify because exclusion is never available for disclosure failures
Answer C: The case must be dismissed for lack of subject matter jurisdiction
Answer D: The witness may testify only if the plaintiff also failed to disclose someone
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 37(c)(1) provides an automatic-use sanction for unjustified, harmful disclosure failures.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Exclusion is a standard consequence unless the failure was justified or harmless. C: Failure to disclose a witness is not a jurisdictional defect. D: The sanction analysis focuses on the party's failure and whether it was justified or harmless.
lb_rule_reference: If a party fails to identify a witness or provide required information, the party generally may not use that witness or information unless the failure was substantially justified or harmless.
