Outline: Mistake / Lack of Mutual Assent
Question: Rachel and Joseph orally agree that Joseph will sell Rachel 50 wool blankets for $5,000. Their written contract, prepared by a clerk, mistakenly states the price as $50,000. Both parties sign without noticing the extra zero. When Joseph demands $50,000, Rachel asks the court to change the writing to match the actual agreement. What is the proper remedy?
Answer A: No remedy is available because parol evidence can never alter a signed writing.
Answer B: Rachel may rescind only if Joseph intentionally inserted the extra zero.
Answer C: Rachel must pay $50,000 because signed writings are conclusive.
Answer D: The court may reform the writing to reflect the parties' actual agreement if the mistake in the writing is proven.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Where the parties actually agreed on one term but the writing mistakenly records another, reformation may correct the writing to reflect the true agreement. The issue is not adding a new bargain but correcting a mutual mistake in the memorial.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reformation is an equitable remedy for a mistaken writing and is not barred by a parol-evidence slogan. B: B is wrong because reformation for mutual drafting mistake does not require intentional fraud. C: C is wrong because the signed writing may be corrected when it fails to express the actual agreement.
lb_rule_reference: Scrivener's error / reformation
