Outline: Scope
Question: A deed granted Ruth an easement across Boaz's field “over a route reasonably designated by the owner of the field from time to time, so long as the route provides safe and convenient access to Ruth's parcel.” Boaz later moved the route 40 feet east to plant crops. The new route is equally safe, equally convenient, and does not increase Ruth's travel distance. May Ruth insist on the original route despite the deed language?
Answer A: Yes, because the servient owner can never relocate an easement under any circumstances.
Answer B: Yes, because relocation always requires a new deed signed after every route change.
Answer C: No, because any relocation clause converts an easement into a revocable license.
Answer D: No, because the grant itself permits reasonable route designation by the servient owner and the substitute route satisfies that condition.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The parties may define the easement's scope in the grant. Here, the deed expressly allowed the field owner to designate the route from time to time if the route remains safe and convenient. Boaz's relocation fits that express limitation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the no-unilateral-relocation rule yields to valid grant language permitting relocation. B: B is wrong because the original deed supplied the relocation authority. C: C is wrong because a relocation clause does not make the easement merely revocable. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Relocation permitted by express grant language
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is applying the fixed-location rule mechanically without reading the grant's relocation language.
