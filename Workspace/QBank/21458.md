Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Samson signed a deed conveying Hillacre to Delilah but placed it in his desk and told Delilah, “I am not ready to give this to you.” Delilah secretly took the deed from the desk and recorded it. Delilah then claimed she owned Hillacre because the deed appeared in the public records. Who owns Hillacre?
Answer A: Delilah, because the recorded deed gave constructive notice to the world.
Answer B: Samson, because Samson never delivered the deed with present intent and Delilah's wrongful recording could not cure that defect.
Answer C: Delilah, because Samson signed the deed before she took it.
Answer D: Samson only if Delilah failed to pay fair market value.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity lock is delivery. Samson expressly withheld delivery and Delilah obtained the instrument wrongfully. Recording gives notice only to an instrument capable of operating; it does not make an undelivered deed effective, so title stayed with Samson.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because constructive notice does not validate a stolen or undelivered deed. C: C is wrong because signature alone is not delivery. D: D is wrong because value is a protected-party issue, not the validity lock for a stolen deed.
lb_rule_reference: A stolen deed is not delivered, and wrongful recording cannot cure the missing delivery
