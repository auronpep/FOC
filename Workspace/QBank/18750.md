Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: A buyer sues a seller in federal court for fraud. The complaint alleges that the seller "lied many times during negotiations" and "intended to deceive the buyer," but it does not identify the speaker, date, place, content, or transaction in which any false statement was made. The seller moves to dismiss for failure to plead fraud with particularity. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the motion because intent to deceive may be alleged generally
Answer B: Deny the motion because all civil claims use only Rule 8 notice pleading
Answer C: Grant the motion because the complaint does not plead the circumstances of fraud with particularity
Answer D: Grant the motion for lack of personal jurisdiction because fraud is intentional
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The complaint asserts fraud in general terms but omits the circumstances that Rule 9(b) requires. General allegations of intent do not cure missing details about the fraudulent statements.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The circumstances of fraud still must be pleaded with particularity. The complaint lacks the who, what, when, where, and how of the alleged misrepresentation. B: Fraud and mistake are special matters. Rule 9(b) adds a particularity requirement for the circumstances of fraud. D: The motion concerns pleading particularity, not minimum contacts. The defendant's intentional conduct does not supply the missing Rule 9(b) details.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 9(b) requires a party alleging fraud or mistake to state the circumstances with particularity; malice, intent, knowledge, and other conditions of mind may be alleged generally.
