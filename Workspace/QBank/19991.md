Outline: Nature and Type
Question: Samuel signed and delivered a deed to Rachel stating, “Samuel grants Rachel a right of way, twenty feet wide, over the eastern edge of Samuel's lot for access to Rachel's lot.” The deed did not say that Samuel conveyed the twenty-foot strip itself. Rachel later claimed that she owned the twenty-foot strip in fee simple and could fence Samuel out of it entirely. Samuel argued that Rachel received only a right of way. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Rachel owns the strip in fee simple because any written right over land transfers ownership of the land itself.
Answer B: Rachel has only a revocable license because the deed did not transfer the entire lot.
Answer C: Rachel has a profit because she may travel across Samuel's land.
Answer D: Rachel has an express easement right of way, not fee title to the twenty-foot strip, because the deed grants a use right for access.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A right of way is ordinarily an easement: a nonpossessory right to use another's land for access. The deed grants Rachel a defined access right over Samuel's lot but does not convey fee title to the strip.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a use right is not the same as ownership of the land. B: B is wrong because a signed and delivered deed granting a right of way creates a property interest, not revocable permission. C: C is wrong because a profit is a right to remove resources from land, not to travel across it.
lb_rule_reference: Right-of-way grant / easement not fee title
