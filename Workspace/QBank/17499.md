Question: Naomi sold a delivery van to Jacob. As part of the sale price, Jacob promised Naomi that he would pay Rachel $5,000. Rachel did not know about the promise. Before Rachel learned of it, Jacob and Naomi signed a new agreement deleting the payment-to-Rachel term and reducing the purchase price. Rachel later learned of the original contract and sued Jacob for $5,000. Is Rachel likely to recover?
Answer A: Yes, because she was an intended beneficiary when the original contract was made.
Answer B: No, because Jacob and Naomi modified the promise before Rachel's rights vested.
Answer C: Yes, because a beneficiary's rights vest immediately at contract formation.
Answer D: No, because third-party beneficiaries can never enforce payment promises.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rachel may have been an intended beneficiary under the original contract, but her rights had not vested because she had not learned of the promise, assented, sued, or relied on it. The original parties could modify or rescind the beneficiary term before vesting.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because intended-beneficiary status and vesting are separate. C: C is wrong because beneficiary rights do not automatically vest at formation. D: D is wrong because intended beneficiaries may enforce when their route and rights are established.
