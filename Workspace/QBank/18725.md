Question: David granted Esther an easement “to maintain the existing drainage ditch carrying ordinary stormwater from Esther's orchard across David's field.” Esther later paved most of the orchard and built a large market. The paving dramatically increased runoff into the ditch, causing repeated flooding on David's field. David sues to limit the drainage. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Esther may discharge unlimited runoff because the grant used the word drainage.
Answer B: Esther may discharge the increased runoff because any development of the dominant land is automatically within scope.
Answer C: David may limit the use because the grant covered ordinary orchard stormwater, not a materially increased flow from later paving and commercial development.
Answer D: David may terminate the easement only if Esther stops using the ditch for a full statutory period.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A drainage easement's scope is controlled by the grant and expected burden at creation. A right to carry ordinary stormwater from an orchard does not automatically include a dramatically increased flow caused by paving and commercial redevelopment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the word drainage does not authorize unlimited burden. B: B is wrong because normal development has limits when the use materially changes the servient burden. C is correct because the later runoff differs in character and burden. D: D is wrong because the issue is excessive scope, not abandonment or prescription.
lb_rule_reference: Drainage easement / materially increased burden
