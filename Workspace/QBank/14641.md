Question: A girl told a man she would like to have sexual intercourse with him and that he should come to her apartment that night at 7 p.m. After the man arrived, he and the girl went into the bedroom. As the man started to remove the girl's blouse, the girl said she had changed her mind. The man tried to convince her to have intercourse with him but after ten minutes of her sustained refusals, the man left the apartment. Unknown to the man, the girl was 15 years old. Because she appeared to be older, the man believed her to be about 18 years old. A statute in the jurisdiction provides: "A person commits rape in the second degree if he has sexual intercourse with a girl, not his wife, who is under the age of 16 years." If the man is charged with attempting to violate this statute, he is
Answer A: A: guilty, because no mental state is required as to the element of age.
Answer B: B: guilty, because he persisted after she told him she had changed her mind.
Answer C: C: not guilty, because he reasonably believed she had consented and voluntarily withdrew after she told him she had changed her mind.
Answer D: D: not guilty, because he did not intend to have intercourse with a girl under the age of 16.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. The man is being charged with attempt, not with statutory rape. Therefore, the man must have had the specific intent to have sex with a girl under 16. The man believed that the girl was 18 and therefore did not have the specific intent to have sex with a girl under 16.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Although statutory rape is considered a strict liability crime, the man here is being charged with attempt, requiring specific intent, which the man did not have. B is incorrect. This answer ignores the specific intent requirement for a charge of attempted statutory rape and instead addresses the issue of whether or not the man committed a "substantial step" toward the commission of the offense. However, he cannot be found guilty even if he committed a substantial step toward the commission of the offense because he lacked the requisite mental state for intent. C is incorrect. The man is being charged with attempted statutory rape, which requires a substantial step and the specific intent to commit the crime. Consent would not be a defense. If the man had already taken a substantial step towards the commission of the crime, he would not be able to withdraw from the attempt.
