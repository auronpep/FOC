Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Joseph manufactured standard mixers and contracted to sell one batch to Hannah for $48,000. Hannah wrongfully rejected the conforming mixers before accepting them. The mixers were readily resellable, and Joseph had no lost-volume proof. After proper notice, Joseph made a commercially reasonable resale for $43,000. Which amount is Joseph entitled to recover as direct seller damages?
Answer A: $48,000, because Hannah wrongfully rejected conforming goods.
Answer B: $5,000, the difference between the contract price and the proper resale price.
Answer C: Nothing, because Joseph resold the mixers.
Answer D: Joseph's expected profit on an additional sale, even though the resale replaced Hannah's sale.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under UCC Article 2 for a seller after wrongful rejection of goods. Because the goods were not accepted, lost, damaged after risk passed, or unresellable, Joseph cannot use an action for the price. The proper resale formula is contract price minus commercially reasonable resale price: $48,000 − $43,000 = $5,000. The result scope is limited resale damages, and the answer does not decide lost volume because the facts negate that route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it uses WAL-REM-012, action-for-price overuse. C: C is wrong because resale does not erase all seller damages. D: D is wrong because lost-volume profit is unavailable without capacity/inventory facts showing an additional sale was lost.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §§2-706 and 2-709 — resale damages; action for price limited to accepted, lost, damaged, or unresellable goods
