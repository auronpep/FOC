Question: Moses agreed to buy Sarah's land “only if the county issues a building permit by June 1.” Sarah did everything required of her, but the county did not issue the permit by June 1. Moses refused to close. Sarah sued Moses for breach, arguing that she had substantially performed all of her obligations. Who is most likely to prevail?
Answer A: Sarah, because substantial performance of her obligations satisfies the express condition.
Answer B: Sarah, because Moses must close unless he proves the permit denial was Sarah's fault.
Answer C: Moses, but only if the permit denial made performance impossible.
Answer D: Moses, because the permit was an express condition to his duty to buy and the condition did not occur.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The phrase “only if the county issues a building permit by June 1” creates an express condition precedent to Moses's duty to buy. Express conditions generally require strict occurrence unless excused. Because the permit did not issue by the stated date and no excuse is given, Moses's duty did not mature.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because substantial performance does not ordinarily satisfy a strict express condition. B: B is wrong because Sarah's performance does not itself cause Moses's condition to occur. C: C is wrong because this is a condition-precedent issue, not an impossibility issue.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law express condition precedent
