Question: An armed robber presses a gun into Omar's ribs and orders him to unlock the jewelry-store display case immediately or be shot. Omar has no chance to call police or escape. Omar unlocks the case, and the robber takes the jewelry. Omar is charged as an accomplice to theft. Is Omar likely to have a duress defense?
Answer A: Yes, because he faced an imminent threat of death and had no reasonable legal alternative
Answer B: No, because duress is never available for property crimes
Answer C: No, because Omar intentionally unlocked the case
Answer D: Yes, but only because Omar was legally insane during the robbery
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Duress applies when a human threat of death or serious bodily harm is imminent and leaves no reasonable opportunity to escape or seek help. Omar was under a gun at the moment of the act and the charged offense is nonhomicide. On these facts, duress is available.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Duress can apply to many nonhomicide offenses, including property crimes, when its elements are met. C: Duress assumes intentional action under coercion. The issue is whether coercion excuses the act, not whether the muscles moved intentionally. D: The facts show coercion by a human threat, not mental disease. The correct doctrine is duress.
lb_rule_reference: Duress can excuse many nonhomicide crimes when the defendant faces an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm, has no reasonable legal alternative, and did not create the situation
