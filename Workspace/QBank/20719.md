Question: The recorded declaration for a residential community allowed the homeowners' association board to adopt “reasonable rules governing landscaping and exterior appearance.” The board adopted a rule requiring every owner to replace all front-yard landscaping with a specific imported stone sold only by Samuel, the board president, at three times the market price. The board gave no aesthetic or safety reason for requiring that particular stone. Mary refused to comply. The association sued to enforce the rule. Which is the best result?
Answer A: The rule is enforceable because HOA boards have unreviewable discretion over aesthetics.
Answer B: The rule is likely unenforceable because it is unreasonable and appears to exceed the board's granted authority.
Answer C: The rule is enforceable because zoning law does not prohibit the stone.
Answer D: The rule is enforceable if any board member honestly believed it was attractive.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An HOA rule must be authorized by the governing documents and generally must be reasonable. The declaration allowed reasonable landscaping and appearance rules, but the board adopted a self-dealing rule with no aesthetic or safety justification and a required purchase from the board president at an inflated price.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because HOA authority is not absolute. C: C is wrong because public zoning permission does not validate an unreasonable private association rule. D: D is wrong because subjective preference alone does not make a self-dealing rule reasonable.
lb_rule_reference: HOA authority / reasonableness
