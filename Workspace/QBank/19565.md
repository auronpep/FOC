Question: A printer hoped to renew a customer relationship but had no signed contract. A competing printer learned of the opportunity and offered the customer a lower price and faster delivery, truthfully describing its services. The customer chose the competitor, and the first printer lost the expected renewal. Is the first printer likely to prevail on an interference with prospective economic advantage claim against the competitor?
Answer A: Yes, because the competitor intentionally caused the printer to lose expected business
Answer B: No, because the competitor used truthful lawful competition to win a prospective customer
Answer C: Yes, because prospective advantage is easier to prove than interference with contract
Answer D: No, because at-will and prospective relationships can never support interference claims
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: There was only a prospective expectancy, and the competitor used lawful price and service competition without wrongful means.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Lawful competition for prospective business is privileged absent improper means. C: Prospective advantage is weaker and often requires independently wrongful or improper means. D: Such relationships can sometimes support claims if improper means are used; here, the means were lawful.
lb_rule_reference: Lawful competition for prospective business generally is privileged absent independently wrongful or improper means.
