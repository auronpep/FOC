Question: A small trust has a fixed fund that cannot satisfy all beneficiaries' claims. Several beneficiaries sue separately, and early judgments could exhaust the fund before absent beneficiaries are heard. Which certification theory most directly addresses this risk?
Answer A: Rule 23(b)(1), because separate adjudications may impair absent claimants' interests in a limited fund
Answer B: Rule 23(b)(2), because any trust case seeks uniform equitable relief
Answer C: Rule 23(b)(3), because limited funds always require opt-out damages classes
Answer D: No class may be certified because the fund is too small
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Limited-fund pressure is a classic Rule 23(b)(1) concern.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The tested risk is depletion or impairment of absent claimants' interests, not indivisible injunctive relief. C: A limited fund can support Rule 23(b)(1) treatment without defaulting to opt-out damages analysis. D: Scarcity is the reason a limited-fund class may be appropriate.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 23(b)(1) may support certification when individual adjudications would, as a practical matter, dispose of or impair other members' ability to protect their interests, including limited-fund situations.
