Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A Florida citizen sued a Georgia estate attorney in federal court, alleging malpractice in drafting estate documents and seeking $250,000 in damages from the attorney personally. The plaintiff does not ask the federal court to probate a will, distribute estate property, or control property held by a probate court. Complete diversity exists. Does the probate exception automatically bar the federal action?
Answer A: Yes, because every dispute involving an estate is outside federal jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because malpractice claims can never satisfy diversity jurisdiction
Answer C: No, because the plaintiff seeks ordinary in personam damages and does not ask the court to probate or administer an estate
Answer D: No, but only because the attorney is a federal official
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim is estate-adjacent, but it is not asking the federal court to perform probate functions or control probate property.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The probate exception is narrow and does not bar all estate-related damages suits. B: Malpractice claims may be heard in diversity when requirements are met and no exception applies. D: The facts do not make the attorney a federal official; diversity and the narrow exception control.
lb_rule_reference: Federal courts lack jurisdiction to probate a will, administer an estate, or assume control over property in probate custody, but may hear ordinary in personam damages claims when diversity otherwise exists.
