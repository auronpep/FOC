Question: A seller orally tells a buyer that a delivery truck has never been in a crash. Before buying, the buyer receives the truck's title documents, which plainly state "salvage after collision" on the first page. The buyer does not read the documents and later sues for fraud. What is the seller's strongest defense?
Answer A: The seller made no factual statement because vehicle history is always opinion
Answer B: The buyer may lack justifiable reliance because the documents plainly contradicted the oral statement
Answer C: Fraud does not require reliance if the statement was intentional
Answer D: The buyer automatically wins because oral statements always override written documents in tort
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Ignoring an obvious contradiction can defeat justifiable reliance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Crash history is factual. C: Reliance is an element of fraud. D: The written contradiction is central to reliance.
lb_rule_reference: Fraud requires justifiable reliance; reliance may be unjustified when the plaintiff ignores an obvious contradiction in materials provided before the transaction.
