internal_id: TR-059

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Res ipsa loquitur
Question: After abdominal surgery, a patient developed pain. Imaging showed a surgical sponge left inside the abdomen. The patient was unconscious during the procedure and had no access to the surgical site. The surgical team argues that the patient has no direct evidence identifying the exact person who failed to count the sponge. The patient sues the surgical team for negligence. May the patient rely on res ipsa loquitur in the negligence claim?
Answer A: No, because the patient lacks direct evidence of the exact negligent act
Answer B: Yes, because a retained surgical sponge supports a permissible inference of negligence
Answer C: Yes, because res ipsa makes the surgical team strictly liable
Answer D: No, because exclusive control is impossible whenever more than one medical worker was present
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A sponge left inside a patient ordinarily suggests negligence, the surgical team had responsibility for the instrumentality and procedure, and the unconscious patient did not contribute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Res ipsa exists for circumstantial proof when the event ordinarily does not occur without negligence and the defendant side likely had responsibility. C: Res ipsa usually permits an inference of negligence; it does not automatically impose strict liability or compel judgment in every case. D: Modern res ipsa focuses on probable responsibility or control by the defendant side. Multiple team members do not automatically defeat the inference.
lb_rule_reference: Res ipsa loquitur permits an inference of negligence when the event ordinarily does not occur absent negligence, the defendant probably had responsibility, and the plaintiff did not contribute.
