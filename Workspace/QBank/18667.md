Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Isaac contracted to sell rare stone to Hannah for $70,000. Isaac breached. Hannah proved with certainty that the market value of the stone at breach was $90,000, so her expectation loss was $20,000. Hannah also spent $3,000 traveling to inspect the stone before signing the contract and asks to add that amount to the $20,000 award. Assuming the travel cost was part of ordinary bargaining expense and not otherwise recoverable as incidental damages, what should the court award?
Answer A: $23,000, because reliance costs are always added to expectation damages.
Answer B: $20,000, because the expectation award already puts Hannah in the performance position and the travel cost cannot be stacked on top merely as reliance.
Answer C: $3,000, because reliance damages replace all expectation damages whenever the buyer spent money before contract formation.
Answer D: Nothing, because Hannah chose to measure damages by market value.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A plaintiff generally may not stack reliance expenditures on top of a full expectation recovery when doing so overcompensates. The $20,000 market differential gives Hannah the benefit of the bargain. The ordinary precontract travel cost is not automatically added as reliance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it stacks remedy interests. C: C is wrong because reliance does not automatically displace a provable expectation measure. D: D is wrong because Hannah is entitled to the expectation differential.
lb_rule_reference: No stacking reliance on full expectation
