Question: A state denies unemployment benefits to a worker who refused a job requiring work on her Sabbath. The benefits agency has discretion to decide whether a refusal is for "good cause" and often grants benefits for secular scheduling conflicts. What is the best Free Exercise argument?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny may apply because the state used an individualized benefits system to deny a religious reason while accepting comparable secular reasons
Answer B: Free Exercise never applies to government benefits programs
Answer C: Rational basis automatically applies because unemployment law is economic regulation
Answer D: The worker wins only if her religion is shared by a majority of state residents
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Individualized exemptions and discretionary good-cause standards trigger heightened Free Exercise concern.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Government may not condition benefits in a way that penalizes religious exercise without satisfying the applicable constitutional test. C: The individualized religious denial changes the Free Exercise analysis. D: Free Exercise does not depend on religious popularity.
lb_rule_reference: Denying unemployment benefits through an individualized assessment because a claimant refused work for sincere religious reasons can trigger strict scrutiny.
