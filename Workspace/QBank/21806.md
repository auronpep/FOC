Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Daniel contracted to build Esther a deck for $50,000. Daniel completed the deck, but the support beams were installed in a way that made the deck unsafe for ordinary use. Repairing the deck will cost $22,000. The defect reduces the home's market value by only $8,000 because the defect is not obvious to buyers. What is Esther's proper expectation recovery?
Answer A: $8,000, because diminution in market value always caps construction damages.
Answer B: $22,000, because reasonable repair cost is appropriate to make the deck safe and usable.
Answer C: $50,000, because an unsafe defect defeats the entire contract price.
Answer D: Nothing, because the market value loss is less than half the repair cost.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Cost to repair is generally proper when repair is necessary to give the owner the promised safe and usable performance and is not merely wasteful replacement. The lower market-value reduction does not cap damages where the defect affects safety and function.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because diminution is not an automatic cap when repair is needed for safety or function. C: C is wrong because Esther is entitled to repair cost, not refund of the entire contract price. D: D is wrong because the comparison to half the repair cost is not the legal test.
lb_rule_reference: Common law construction damages; safety/function defect
