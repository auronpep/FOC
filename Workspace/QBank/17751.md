Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Hannah is called to testify about a machine she saw malfunction. Hannah cannot speak because of a recent injury, but she can read, write, and communicate by pointing to written answers. The court also has a qualified interpreter available to translate Hannah's hand signs, and Hannah gives an affirmation to tell the truth. The manufacturer objects that Hannah is incompetent because she cannot speak aloud. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a competent witness must speak orally in court.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Hannah can testify without any interpreter or communication aid.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Hannah can communicate through reliable means and has affirmed a duty to testify truthfully.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the parties stipulate to every answer Hannah gives.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Competency does not require oral speech. If Hannah can communicate through reliable means, understands and affirms the duty to testify truthfully, and has relevant perception, she is not incompetent merely because she cannot speak aloud. Interpreter or communication-channel issues can be handled through qualification and oath safeguards.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because speech aloud is not a competency requirement. B: B is wrong because a qualified interpreter or communication aid can permit testimony. D: D is wrong because party stipulation is not required for a competent witness to testify.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 601, 603, and 604 / communication assistance and competence
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests disability/communication-channel confusion and routes the issue to truth-duty and interpreter safeguards rather than automatic incompetency.
