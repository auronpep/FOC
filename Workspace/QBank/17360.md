Question: Solomon sold Lot 2 to Ruth by deed stating, “Lot 2 shall be used only for residential purposes, and this restriction shall bind Ruth and her successors for the benefit of Solomon's retained Lot 1.” The deed did not give Solomon any right to enter or use Lot 2. What is the best classification of the residential-use restriction?
Answer A: A restrictive covenant or equitable servitude, because it limits use of Lot 2 for the benefit of another parcel.
Answer B: An affirmative easement, because Solomon benefits from the restriction.
Answer C: A profit, because Solomon receives value from Ruth's land.
Answer D: A license, because Solomon has no right to enter Lot 2.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A land-use promise restricting how a parcel may be used, especially one intended to bind successors for the benefit of another parcel, is routed as a covenant or equitable servitude. It is not an easement merely because it benefits neighboring land.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because an affirmative easement authorizes use of another's land; Solomon has no entry or use right. C: C is wrong because value from a restriction is not removal of resources. D: D is wrong because lack of entry does not make the restriction a license.
