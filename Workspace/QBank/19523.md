Question: In a federal burglary trial, Paul hands Sarah an unmarked photograph and asks, “Tell the jury what this photograph proves about Timothy's guilt.” No witness has testified who took the photograph, what it depicts, or when it was taken. Timothy objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because photographs are always self-explanatory.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because Paul may ask any question after showing a photograph.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because no foundation has been laid identifying or authenticating the photograph.
Answer D: Sustain the objection because photographs are never admissible in criminal cases.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Before a witness may testify about what a photograph shows or proves, the proponent must lay a foundation sufficient to identify and authenticate it. The objection is lack of foundation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because photographs are not automatically self-authenticating simply because they are shown to a witness. B: B is wrong because a displayed exhibit does not eliminate foundation requirements. D: D is wrong because photographs can be admissible in criminal cases if properly supported.
