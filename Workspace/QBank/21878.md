MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Vantex Corp., a large, well-resourced multinational company incorporated in Delaware with its global headquarters in New York, maintains substantial operations in the State of Forden — including three regional offices and 900 employees there. Vantex sells software to Forden-based clients and has generated $140 million in Forden revenue over three years. A Forden resident purchased Vantex software, which contained a defect that damaged her business systems. She sues Vantex in Forden federal court. Vantex does not dispute that minimum contacts exist but argues that exercising jurisdiction would be unreasonable because its legal department is centralized in New York and would have to coordinate Forden litigation remotely, which it claims is inconvenient. Is Vantex's reasonableness objection sufficient to defeat personal jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because Vantex's legal department is based in New York and would face genuine logistical inconvenience litigating in Forden, satisfying the burden factor
Answer B: Yes, because minimum contacts and reasonableness are independently necessary, and the defendant can always defeat jurisdiction by showing any one reasonableness factor weighs in its favor
Answer C: No, because once minimum contacts are established, the exercise of jurisdiction is presumed reasonable, and Vantex's ordinary litigation inconvenience does not overcome that presumption for a large corporation with extensive Forden operations
Answer D: No, because reasonableness is only considered when minimum contacts are borderline — once clear minimum contacts are established, the reasonableness analysis is skipped entirely
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Minimum contacts having been conceded, the burden shifts to Vantex to demonstrate that exercising jurisdiction would be unreasonable. Vantex must show it would be placed at a "severe disadvantage" — not mere inconvenience. A large multinational with three Forden offices, 900 Forden employees, and substantial Forden revenue faces no such disadvantage from Forden litigation. The forum state has a strong interest in protecting its residents from defective software sold there. The plaintiff has an interest in her home forum. Vantex's New York legal department logistical argument does not come close to meeting the heavy burden required to defeat jurisdiction on reasonableness grounds.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The burden on the defendant must be "so gravely difficult and inconvenient" that the defendant would be placed at a "severe disadvantage" in the litigation. *Burger King*. Coordinating remote litigation for a large multinational corporation with three Forden offices, 900 Forden employees, and $140 million in Forden revenue is ordinary litigation inconvenience, not a severe disadvantage. Large corporations routinely litigate outside their legal departments' home state. The burden factor is not met. B: The reasonableness factors are considered holistically and the defendant bears a heavy burden. No single factor automatically defeats jurisdiction; the court weighs all five. Moreover, the stronger the minimum contacts, the harder it is for the defendant to demonstrate that jurisdiction is unreasonable. With Vantex's extensive Forden contacts, the reasonableness hurdle is even higher. D: The reasonableness analysis is not skipped when contacts are strong — it is structurally required in every specific-jurisdiction analysis. *Burger King* and its progeny apply the reasonableness factors as a standard element of the PJ inquiry. What changes with stronger contacts is the difficulty of defeating jurisdiction on reasonableness grounds, not whether the analysis is conducted.
lb_rule_reference: After establishing minimum contacts, the court evaluates five reasonableness/fairness factors: (1) burden on the defendant, (2) the forum state's interest in adjudicating the dispute, (3) the plaintiff's interest in obtaining convenient relief, (4) the interstate judicial system's interest in efficient resolution, and (5) the shared interest of the states in furthering fundamental substantive social policies. *World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson* (1980); *Asahi Metal Industry Co. v. Superior Court* (1987). The defendant must demonstrate that the exercise of jurisdiction would be "so gravely difficult and inconvenient" as to put it at a severe disadvantage. *Burger King*.
