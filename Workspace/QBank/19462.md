Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: An officer checked a court database that incorrectly showed an active arrest warrant for Iris. The database error was caused by a court clerk's isolated failure to delete a recalled warrant. The officer had no reason to doubt the database, arrested Iris, and found pills during a search incident to arrest. Iris moves to suppress. What is the best result?
Answer A: Suppress the pills because the warrant was not actually valid
Answer B: Admit the pills because the officer objectively relied on a database showing an active warrant and the error was isolated clerical negligence
Answer C: Suppress the pills because good faith applies only to search warrants
Answer D: Admit the pills because any computer record is conclusive for Fourth Amendment purposes
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The officer's reliance was objectively reasonable, and the recordkeeping mistake was isolated and not shown to be reckless or systemic police misconduct. Suppression is not required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Exclusion is not automatic. When officers reasonably rely on an isolated clerical error, suppression may not serve deterrence. C: Objective good faith can also apply in other reliance settings, including certain recordkeeping errors. D: Database reliance is not absolute. Systemic or reckless errors may require a different result.
lb_rule_reference: The exclusionary rule targets deterrable police misconduct; objectively reasonable reliance on isolated negligent recordkeeping may not justify suppression.
