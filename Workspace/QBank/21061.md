MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A research company stored a highly toxic experimental chemical in tanks near a residential well field. The company used state-of-the-art tanks and complied with all regulations. A tiny undetectable fissure allowed vapor to seep into a neighbor's basement, causing respiratory injury. The neighbor sues for strict liability based on an abnormally dangerous activity. Is the company likely strictly liable?
Answer A: Yes, if the storage is abnormally dangerous and the injury came from the toxic-seep risk
Answer B: No, because the company used state-of-the-art tanks and complied with regulations
Answer C: No, because strict liability covers only explosives and animals
Answer D: Yes, because strict liability covers every illness on land near the tanks
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Highly toxic chemical storage near homes may be abnormally dangerous, and respiratory injury from seepage is within the risk that makes the activity abnormal.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Due care and regulatory compliance do not defeat strict liability when an abnormally dangerous activity causes harm within its abnormal risk. C: Abnormally dangerous activity doctrine can include other activities, such as highly toxic chemical storage in the right context. D: The injury must result from the abnormal risk. This vapor injury may qualify, but unrelated illnesses would not.
lb_rule_reference: Storing or using highly toxic chemicals may be abnormally dangerous when the activity creates a serious unavoidable risk uncommon to the area, and liability is limited to harms from that risk.
