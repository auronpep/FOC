Question: Sarah contracted to buy land from Eli. Eli breached. Sarah had paid Eli a $5,000 deposit and separately spent $4,000 on inspections and surveys in preparation for the purchase. The land was never transferred. Sarah's expectation damages are unavailable because market value cannot be proven. Which statement best separates Sarah's remedy interests?
Answer A: The $5,000 deposit and $4,000 inspection costs are both restitution because Sarah spent them.
Answer B: The $5,000 deposit is restitutionary because it was a benefit conferred on Eli; the $4,000 inspection and survey costs are reliance expenditures.
Answer C: The $4,000 inspection costs are expectation damages because Sarah expected to buy the land.
Answer D: Sarah has no claim because expectation damages cannot be proven.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A deposit paid to the breaching seller is a benefit conferred on that seller and is restitutionary in character. Inspection and survey costs are out-of-pocket expenditures Sarah incurred in reliance on the contract. Distinguishing remedy interests matters even when both categories may be recoverable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every expenditure is restitution; restitution focuses on the defendant's benefit. C: C is wrong because inspection costs are reliance expenditures, not the value of the promised performance. D: D is wrong because absence of expectation proof does not eliminate restitution or reliance claims.
