Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Naomi signed and delivered to Ruth a deed conveying Naomi's cottage. The deed named the parties, described the cottage, used present words of transfer, and Ruth accepted. Naomi's signature was never acknowledged before a notary, and Ruth did not record the deed. As between Naomi and Ruth, who owns the cottage?
Answer A: Ruth, because acknowledgment and recording are not required for validity between grantor and grantee.
Answer B: Naomi, because an unacknowledged deed cannot transfer title.
Answer C: Naomi, because an unrecorded deed is void between the original parties.
Answer D: Ruth only if no later purchaser appears.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is execution and delivery between the parties. Naomi signed and delivered an otherwise valid deed, and Ruth accepted. Acknowledgment and recording may matter for recordability, proof, notice, or priority, but they are not required for validity as between Naomi and Ruth.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because acknowledgment is not the baseline validity lock between the parties. C: C is wrong because recording is a priority and notice device, not a condition to validity between grantor and grantee. D: D is wrong because the question asks only the title result between Naomi and Ruth, not priority against a later claimant.
lb_rule_reference: Acknowledgment and recording not baseline validity
