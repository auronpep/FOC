Question: David is charged with robbing a jewelry shop. The prosecutor offers cell-site evidence showing David's phone connected to a tower that serves the block containing the shop at the approximate time of the robbery. David objects that the same tower also serves several restaurants and apartments. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence because it does not place David inside the jewelry shop.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence unless the prosecutor proves no other location in the tower area was possible.
Answer C: Admit the evidence only if a witness saw David holding the phone.
Answer D: Admit the evidence because it tends to place David near the shop; alternative locations within the tower area affect weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The tower connection does not prove David was in the shop, but it tends to make his presence near the shop more probable. The other possible locations weaken the evidence and are proper weight arguments.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because evidence can be relevant without placing David inside the exact location. B: B is wrong because the proponent need not eliminate every alternative location. C: C is wrong because direct proof that David held the phone would strengthen the proof but is not required for relevance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401; FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Student requires exact-location proof instead of recognizing circumstantial proximity value.
