Question: Wade and Priya agree to steal a museum sculpture. They carry out the plan and remove the sculpture from the museum. Both are charged with conspiracy to commit larceny and the completed larceny. They argue that the conspiracy merged into the completed offense. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The conspiracy merges because all inchoate crimes merge into completed crimes
Answer B: The conspiracy does not merge, so convictions for both conspiracy and larceny may stand if both are proved
Answer C: The conspiracy merges only because the target offense was a property crime
Answer D: The conspiracy merges unless the museum was federally owned
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Conspiracy remains a separate offense even when the target crime is completed. The agreement creates a distinct social danger.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Conspiracy is different. It punishes the agreement and does not merge into the completed target crime. C: The non-merger rule for conspiracy does not turn on the target offense being property-related. D: Merger of conspiracy is not controlled by federal ownership of the victim property.
lb_rule_reference: Conspiracy does not merge into the completed target offense, so a defendant may be convicted of both conspiracy and the completed crime when both are proved.
