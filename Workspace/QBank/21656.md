Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Procedural due process
Question: A state warehouse employee violates state policy and intentionally destroys a vendor's stored equipment without warning. The state has a meaningful post-deprivation claims process that can award compensation. The vendor argues due process required the state to provide a hearing before the rogue employee acted. What is the state's best procedural due process response?
Answer A: A pre-deprivation hearing was required because the state must predict and stop every unauthorized employee act
Answer B: An adequate post-deprivation remedy can satisfy due process when the loss resulted from a random unauthorized act
Answer C: No process is needed because intentional property destruction is never state action
Answer D: Due process is satisfied only if the employee is criminally prosecuted
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The state cannot realistically provide a pre-deprivation hearing before unpredictable unauthorized misconduct.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Pre-deprivation process may be impracticable for random unauthorized conduct. C: The better analysis is the adequacy of post-deprivation process, not a categorical no-state-action rule. D: Procedural due process can be satisfied by civil compensation remedies; criminal prosecution is not the due process measure.
lb_rule_reference: When a state employee's random unauthorized act causes a property loss and pre-deprivation process is impracticable, an adequate post-deprivation remedy can satisfy procedural due process.
