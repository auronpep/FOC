Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers arrested Elena in her home without a warrant, consent, or exigent circumstances. They immediately drove her to the station, read Miranda warnings, and questioned her within fifteen minutes. She confessed. No intervening warrant, release, counsel consultation, or time lapse occurred. Elena moves to suppress the confession as fruit of the illegal arrest. Should the confession be suppressed?
Answer A: No, because Miranda warnings always purge the taint of an illegal arrest
Answer B: No, because a confession is never fruit of a Fourth Amendment violation
Answer C: Yes, because the confession closely followed the illegal arrest and Miranda alone did not attenuate the taint
Answer D: Yes, because Miranda warnings make all statements involuntary
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The illegal arrest led directly to stationhouse questioning minutes later. With no meaningful intervening circumstances, Miranda warnings alone do not dissipate the taint.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Miranda warnings are relevant but not automatically sufficient to attenuate an illegal arrest. The confession followed immediately with no intervening circumstances. B: A confession can be fruit of an illegal arrest if causally connected. D: Warnings usually support voluntariness. Suppression here rests on Fourth Amendment fruit, not involuntariness caused by warnings.
lb_rule_reference: A confession after an illegal arrest may be suppressible despite Miranda warnings when the causal connection remains close and no meaningful intervening circumstances exist.
