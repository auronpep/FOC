Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Manslaughter
Question: In a crowded parking lot, Blair and Omar began shoving each other after a minor collision. Omar punched Blair hard in the face, breaking Blair's nose. Seconds later, while still bleeding and enraged, Blair grabbed a tire iron from beside his truck and struck Omar once in the head, killing him. What is the best homicide classification?
Answer A: Murder, because Blair intentionally used a deadly weapon
Answer B: Voluntary manslaughter, because Omar's serious assault caused Blair to kill in the heat of passion before cooling
Answer C: Involuntary manslaughter, because Blair only struck Omar once
Answer D: No homicide liability, because Omar started the physical fight
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A serious physical assault can be adequate provocation. Blair was actually provoked, acted seconds later while still bleeding and enraged, and had no meaningful cooling period. The facts support voluntary manslaughter rather than murder.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intent and dangerous force establish malice unless mitigation applies. Here, the sudden serious punch and immediate response support adequate provocation and no cooling, reducing the offense to voluntary manslaughter. C: A single intentional blow with a tire iron to the head is deadly force. The issue is mitigation from murder to voluntary manslaughter, not reduction to negligent homicide. D: Provocation mitigates; it is not a complete defense. Blair intentionally used deadly force in response to a non-deadly punch.
lb_rule_reference: Voluntary manslaughter requires adequate provocation, actual heat of passion, no adequate cooling period, and a causal connection between provocation and killing.
