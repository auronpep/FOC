Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Solomon agreed to build an addition for Abigail for $120,000. Abigail paid Solomon $50,000 before Solomon wrongfully abandoned the job. The unpaid balance under the contract was $70,000. Abigail reasonably paid a replacement builder $88,000 to finish the same work. What is Abigail's direct expectation damage from Solomon's abandonment?
Answer A: $88,000, because that is what Abigail paid the replacement builder.
Answer B: $70,000, because that was the unpaid balance on the original contract.
Answer C: $18,000, because the cost to complete exceeded the unpaid contract balance by that amount.
Answer D: $120,000, because Solomon abandoned the entire contract.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The owner's expectation loss is the additional amount required to obtain the promised performance, not the full substitute cost. Abigail would have owed Solomon $70,000 if he completed the job. She instead had to pay $88,000 to finish it, so her loss is $18,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it fails to subtract the unpaid balance Abigail would have paid anyway. B: B is wrong because the unpaid balance is not her loss; it is part of the bargain price. D: D is wrong because awarding the contract price would ignore what Abigail already paid and what performance she still needed.
lb_rule_reference: Common law construction damages; cost to complete offset
