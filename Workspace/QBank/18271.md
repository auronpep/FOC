Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Noah contracted to build a fence for Abraham for $40,000. Before Abraham repudiated, Noah reasonably spent $18,000 on labor and materials. At trial, Abraham proves that if the contract had been fully performed, Noah would have spent $50,000 total to earn the $40,000 price, creating a $10,000 loss. What is Noah's reliance recovery?
Answer A: $40,000, because reliance damages always equal the contract price.
Answer B: $18,000, because all prebreach expenditures are automatically recoverable.
Answer C: $0, because the contract would have been losing.
Answer D: $8,000, Noah's $18,000 reliance reduced by the $10,000 loss Abraham proved Noah would have suffered.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages are reduced to avoid putting the plaintiff in a better position than performance would have. If the defendant proves the contract would have been losing, the reliance award is reduced by that proven loss. $18,000 minus $10,000 equals $8,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reliance does not automatically equal the contract price. B: B is wrong because reliance must be adjusted to prevent overcompensation. C: C is wrong because the proved loss reduces the reliance claim; it does not erase all recovery where some reliance remains after the offset.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance damages reduced by proved losing contract
