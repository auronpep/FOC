Question: A store employee saw a customer faint near the entrance. The employee told other customers not to call emergency services because the store would handle it, moved the customer to a side room, and then forgot to call for help. The delay worsened the customer's condition. The store argues it had no duty because it did not cause the fainting. What is the customer's best duty argument?
Answer A: The store had no duty because it did not create the original medical condition
Answer B: The store had no duty because omissions can never be negligent
Answer C: The store owed a duty because it undertook the rescue and discouraged others from seeking help
Answer D: The store is liable only if it promised a perfect medical outcome
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: By taking charge and inducing reliance, the store had to use reasonable care in the undertaking.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A duty may also arise from a voluntary undertaking that induces reliance or increases risk. B: Omissions can be actionable when a duty to act arises from an undertaking or reliance. D: The duty is reasonable care in the undertaking, not a promise of a perfect result.
lb_rule_reference: A voluntary undertaking can create a duty when the actor increases risk or induces reliance, and the actor must perform the undertaking with reasonable care.
