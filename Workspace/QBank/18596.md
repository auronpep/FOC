Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: Mary claims that a bottle of cleaning fluid she bought from a store lacked a required warning label. Mary discarded her bottle before trial. To prove the missing warning, she buys a different bottle of the same brand from a different store two years later and offers it to show what her bottle looked like. The manufacturer objects that the later bottle is not sufficiently connected to Mary's bottle. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the later bottle automatically because it is the same brand.
Answer B: Exclude the later bottle for this purpose unless Mary supplies evidence connecting the later bottle's label design to the label on her bottle at the relevant time.
Answer C: Admit the later bottle because all weaknesses in similarity go only to weight.
Answer D: Exclude the later bottle only if the manufacturer proves the labels changed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A later bottle from a different store can be relevant only if the proponent connects it to the product condition at the relevant time. Without evidence that the label design was the same, the sample lacks sufficient connection for the offered purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because same brand alone does not automatically establish the same label condition. C: C is wrong because similarity problems can defeat relevance when the offered item is not connected to the relevant time or object. D: D is wrong because Mary bears the threshold burden of connecting her substitute sample to her bottle.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401; FRE 104(b)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats same-brand similarity as enough without source/time connection.
