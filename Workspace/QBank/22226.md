


BARMATRIX Q#: 22226
Outline_code: 31010200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Objections and Motions to Strike
Question: A woman was shot by a security guard while trespassing in a restaurant she had recently been fired from. She claimed she was in the back room of the restaurant looking for a sweater she had left there. While she was being treated by the doctor for her gunshot wound, the doctor asked how she had been hit so high on her back. The woman told the doctor that she was actually shot while crouched down and trying to open the restaurant safe, but no one had figured that out yet. She pleaded with the doctor to not tell anyone about what she was doing in the restaurant. The doctor agreed to keep it quiet. At a later civil trial between the restaurant and woman, the owner wanted to call the doctor to testify to the woman's statement. The defense attorney objected. Should the objection be sustained?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the statement was made for the purpose of medical treatment.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the woman voluntarily told the doctor that she was robbing the safe.
Answer C: C: No, because the physician- patient privilege does not apply to information dealing with non- medical matters.
Answer D: D: No, because the woman was not asked a question likely to elicit criminal information.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The physician- patient privilege only protects information acquired by the physician that the physician can use while treating the patient. Importantly, non- medical matters are not protected by the privilege. Here, the fact that the woman was crouched down to rob the safe is not a medical matter protected by privilege. A is incorrect because the statement was not made for the purpose of medical treatment. B is incorrect because the fact that the woman made the statement voluntarily would not cause it to be protected by privilege. D is incorrect because the issue here is not whether the woman was somehow being subjected to interrogation.
