Outline: Nature and Type
Question: Aaron signed a writing that accurately described his farm and stated, “I acknowledge receiving ten thousand dollars from Timothy for this farm.” The writing did not say “convey,” “grant,” “transfer,” or otherwise state that Aaron was presently passing title. Aaron handed the writing to Timothy. Timothy claimed that the receipt itself transferred the farm. Who owns the farm?
Answer A: Timothy, because the signed writing described the farm and acknowledged payment.
Answer B: Timothy, because payment of the price completes the land transfer.
Answer C: Timothy only if he records the signed receipt before Aaron objects.
Answer D: Aaron, because a receipt of consideration without operative words of present transfer is not a deed conveying title.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is operative words of transfer. Aaron's writing acknowledged payment and described the farm, but it did not state a present conveyance of title. Consideration language alone does not supply the missing transfer lock, so title remains with Aaron.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a signed land description plus payment receipt is not enough without present conveyance language. B: B is wrong because payment does not itself transfer legal title. C: C is wrong because recording cannot turn a nonconveyancing receipt into a deed.
lb_rule_reference: Consideration receipt alone is not operative conveyance language
