Question: In the most recent deed in the chain of title to a tract of land, a man conveyed the land as follows: "To my niece and her heirs and assigns in fee simple until my niece's daughter marries, and then to my niece's daughter and her heirs and assigns in fee simple." There is no applicable statute, and the common law Rule Against Perpetuities has not been modified in the jurisdiction. Which of the following is the most accurate statement concerning the title to the land?
Answer A: A: The niece has a life estate and the daughter has a contingent remainder.
Answer B: B: The niece has a fee simple and the daughter has no interest, because after the grant of a fee simple there can be no gift over.
Answer C: C: The niece has a fee simple and the daughter has no interest, because she might not marry within 21 years after the date of the deed.
Answer D: D: The niece has a defeasible fee simple determinable and the daughter has an executory interest.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. The niece's daughter has an executory interest that does not violate the Rule Against Perpetuities, because it will vest within the lifetime of the niece and her daughter, the validating lives of this scenario.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. A defeasible fee estate was created by the gift to the niece "and her heirs and assigns," rather than a life estate. B is incorrect. A grantee who holds a future interest following a defeasible estate is an executory interest. C is incorrect. The niece and the niece's daughter are both validating lives and the condition of the marriage either will or will not occur during their lifetimes.
