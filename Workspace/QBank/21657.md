Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: During an argument at a bar, Vincent shoved Pablo, who stumbled and knocked over Vincent's drink. Vincent immediately grabbed a beer bottle from the bar and struck Pablo twice over the head with it. Pablo collapsed and died from the blunt force trauma. The entire altercation lasted less than thirty seconds. There was no evidence Vincent had planned or expected a confrontation with Pablo before that evening. What is the most serious homicide offense for which Vincent can properly be convicted?
Answer A: First-degree murder, because Vincent used a deadly weapon with intent to kill
Answer B: Voluntary manslaughter, because Vincent was provoked by Pablo knocking over his drink
Answer C: Second-degree murder, because Vincent intended to kill but did not premeditate or deliberate
Answer D: Involuntary manslaughter, because Vincent did not plan to kill Pablo
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Second-degree murder at common law covers intentional killings that lack the premeditation and deliberation required for first-degree murder. Vincent intended to strike Pablo with a deadly weapon, satisfying the malice aforethought element. But the killing was instantaneous and unplanned — the opposite of cool reflection. The provocation (spilled drink, shove) is legally insufficient to reduce to voluntary manslaughter. Second-degree murder is the correct charge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intent to kill is a necessary but not sufficient element of first-degree murder. First-degree also requires premeditation and deliberation — cool reflection before forming the intent. Here, Vincent acted instantly in the heat of a bar fight with no prior planning. There is no basis for premeditation. B: Adequate provocation for voluntary manslaughter requires provocation sufficient to inflame the passions of a reasonable person to the point of losing self-control. A spilled drink and a shove fall far short of the adequate provocation threshold — a reasonable person would not be provoked to kill by such minor conduct. D: Involuntary manslaughter involves killings without intent to kill, resulting from criminal negligence or an unlawful act. Vincent intentionally struck Pablo with a bottle with apparent intent to cause serious harm or death. An intentional killing does not become involuntary manslaughter simply because it was unplanned.
lb_rule_reference: Second-degree murder covers intentional killings that are not premeditated and deliberate; intent to kill without prior cool reflection produces second-degree, not first-degree, murder
