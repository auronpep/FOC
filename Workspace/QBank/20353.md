Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: In a diversity action, plaintiff sues defendant for $100,000 arising from a car crash. Defendant counterclaims against plaintiff for $20,000 for damage to defendant's car from the same crash. The counterclaim lacks an independent amount in controversy. May the federal court hear the counterclaim?
Answer A: No, because every claim in diversity must independently exceed $75,000
Answer B: No, because Section 1367(b) bars all nondiverse or low-value defendant claims
Answer C: Yes, because the counterclaim arises from the same crash and fits the same Article III case
Answer D: Yes, but only because the counterclaim raises federal law
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The counterclaim shares a common nucleus with the diversity anchor claim and is not barred by the plaintiff-side limits.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Supplemental jurisdiction may cover related claims without independent amount. B: The plaintiff-side limitations do not categorically bar defendant counterclaims. D: The facts support supplemental jurisdiction, not federal-question jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant's related counterclaim may fall within supplemental jurisdiction when it shares a common nucleus with the claim supporting original jurisdiction.
