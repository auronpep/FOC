Question: Karl stabbed Rita in the leg with a knife, inflicting a serious but non-life-threatening wound. Rita was taken to the hospital and admitted for treatment. While Rita was in the hospital recovering and clearly improving, a third party, Max — who had a separate personal grievance against Rita — entered her hospital room and injected a fatal dose of poison into her IV line. Rita died within minutes. Max was apprehended and charged separately. Karl is charged with Rita's murder. Is Karl the proximate cause of Rita's death?
Answer A: Yes, because Karl's stabbing was the but-for cause of Rita being in the hospital where Max could reach her
Answer B: Yes, because Karl created the conditions that made Rita vulnerable
Answer C: No, because Max's deliberate, unforeseeable poisoning was a superseding cause that broke the causal chain between Karl's stabbing and Rita's death
Answer D: No, because Rita was recovering and would have survived Karl's stabbing absent Max's intervention
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Karl's stabbing was an actual (but-for) cause of Rita being in the hospital. However, proximate causation also requires foreseeability. Max's deliberate, independent decision to poison Rita — motivated by a wholly separate grievance unrelated to Karl — was not a foreseeable consequence of Karl's stabbing. This is the paradigm case of a superseding intervening cause: an independent, intentional, unforeseeable criminal act by a third party that breaks the causal chain. Karl remains liable for the stab wound and any consequences flowing from it, but not for Rita's death by poisoning. Karl is not the proximate cause of her death.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: But-for (actual) causation and proximate causation are distinct elements. A defendant is not the proximate cause of every result that traces back to his act in some factual chain. Max's deliberate, independent, unforeseeable act of poisoning Rita is a superseding intervening cause. It was not a foreseeable response to Karl's stabbing; it was a wholly independent criminal act by a third party pursuing a separate grievance. This breaks the causal chain between Karl's stab and Rita's death. B: "Creating conditions of vulnerability" is not the proximate-cause standard. Proximate cause requires that the ultimate result be a foreseeable consequence of the defendant's act. An independent criminal act by a third party pursuing an unrelated grievance is not a foreseeable consequence of a stabbing, even if that stabbing placed the victim in a hospital. The foreseeability test fails here. D: The correct reason Karl lacks proximate cause is the superseding cause doctrine, not the medical prognosis. A defendant who inflicts a fatal wound can still be the proximate cause even if the victim lingered for weeks. Conversely, a defendant whose wound was non-lethal is not automatically exonerated from all charges. The analysis must focus on whether the intervening act was foreseeable, not on whether the original wound was eventually survivable.
lb_rule_reference: An independent intervening cause that is unforeseeable breaks the causal chain and becomes the superseding cause; the original defendant is no longer the proximate cause of the ultimate result
