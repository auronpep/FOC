Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Exclusionary Rule
Question: Police unlawfully opened the locked console of a car after arresting its driver and found a pistol. Department policy required impounding the car because it blocked a fire lane and required officers to inventory all compartments, including locked consoles, before towing. The tow truck had already been called. The driver moves to suppress the pistol. What is the best argument for admitting the pistol?
Answer A: The pistol is admissible because standardized inventory procedures would inevitably have found it
Answer B: The pistol must be suppressed because officers first found it unlawfully
Answer C: The pistol is admissible because all cars may be searched after any arrest
Answer D: The pistol must be suppressed unless the driver consented to inventory
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The car was going to be lawfully impounded, the tow had been called, and written policy required inventory of locked consoles. That concrete lawful path supports inevitable discovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Fruit evidence can still be admitted if a lawful inevitable discovery path is proven. C: The search-incident theory is not the supplied clean path. Inevitable discovery through standardized inventory is. D: Standardized inventory searches do not require consent when lawfully conducted for caretaking purposes.
lb_rule_reference: Evidence unlawfully found may be admitted if it would inevitably have been discovered through standardized lawful procedures.
