Question: A state insurance rule excludes coverage for a particular elective procedure for all minors, regardless of sex or gender identity. An adult patient is covered for the same procedure when medically indicated. A challenger argues strict scrutiny applies because the procedure is sometimes sought by transgender minors. What is the best first step in the Equal Protection analysis?
Answer A: Automatically apply strict scrutiny because the facts mention transgender minors
Answer B: Identify the classification actually drawn before selecting the scrutiny tier
Answer C: Apply rational basis automatically because all medical coverage rules are economic
Answer D: Apply intermediate scrutiny only if the patient proves bad motive by every legislator
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule may be framed by age, medical use, sex, or another category; tier selection depends on that classification and proof.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The first step is identifying the classification actually drawn. C: Some medical rules may classify by protected status or burden constitutional rights; classification still must be identified. D: The required proof depends on how the law classifies and the claim asserted; unanimity of bad motive is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Do not assume every transgender-related law automatically triggers strict scrutiny; identify whether the law classifies by sex, transgender status, medical use, age, or another category, then apply the governing tier.
