Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Freedom of speech and expression
Question: A school district lets parent-teacher groups place flyers in teacher mailboxes about school events, tutoring, and school-sponsored programs. It denies a local business's flyer advertising weekend furniture sales because the flyer is not school-related. The district would reject all non-school commercial flyers regardless of viewpoint. Is the denial likely unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination?
Answer A: Yes, because once any outside flyer is allowed, all outside flyers must be allowed
Answer B: Yes, because commercial speech always receives strict scrutiny
Answer C: No, because excluding non-school commercial flyers is a viewpoint-neutral subject limit consistent with the forum's purpose
Answer D: No, because teachers have no First Amendment rights in school buildings
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The district is limiting the forum to school-related messages, not favoring one viewpoint on a permitted topic.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A limited forum can be confined to specified subjects or speakers. B: Commercial speech has its own doctrine; this question turns on forum scope. D: The analysis is forum scope, not a categorical absence of speech rights.
lb_rule_reference: A limited public forum may be restricted to specified speakers or subjects if the restriction is reasonable and viewpoint neutral.
