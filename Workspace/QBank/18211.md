Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal criminal trial, the prosecution calls Rebecca, who begins direct examination by saying, “I did not see the defendant near the bank.” Before cross-examination begins, Rebecca invokes a valid privilege and is excused. The prosecution offers Rebecca's prior deposition testimony, under oath, in which she said she saw the defendant outside the bank. The prosecution offers it as a federal prior inconsistent statement under Rule 801(d)(1)(A). The defendant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the deposition as a prior inconsistent statement because it was under oath.
Answer B: Admit the deposition because Rebecca started to testify at trial.
Answer C: Exclude the deposition under Rule 801(d)(1)(A) because Rebecca is not subject to cross-examination about the prior statement at the current trial.
Answer D: Exclude the deposition only if Rebecca denies giving it.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 801(d)(1)(A) requires that the declarant testify and be subject to cross-examination about the prior statement. Although Rebecca's deposition was under oath, she was excused before cross-examination and is not subject to cross about the prior statement at trial. That defeats the federal prior-inconsistent-statement nonhearsay route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because oath alone is insufficient. B: B is wrong because merely beginning direct examination is not the same as being subject to cross-examination about the prior statement. D: D is wrong because denial is not the issue; the cross-examination requirement is.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 801(d)(1)(A) / declarant must testify and be subject to cross-examination
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the subject-to-cross requirement for substantive prior inconsistent statements.
