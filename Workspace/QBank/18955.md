Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A tire manufacturer designed a tire with two internal steel belts. One tire accidentally left the factory with only one belt because a machine skipped a step. Thousands of other tires made from the same design contained two belts and performed safely. The one-belt tire failed during normal highway driving and caused a driver to crash. The driver sues the manufacturer under strict products liability for a manufacturing defect. Is the driver likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim for a manufacturing defect?
Answer A: No, because the tire design used for the product line was generally safe
Answer B: Yes, because the tire differed from intended specifications by missing a steel belt and failed during normal use
Answer C: Yes, because any crash involving a tire proves the manufacturer's design was defective
Answer D: No, because strict products liability requires proof that the manufacturer failed to maintain the machine
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The tire was supposed to have two belts and left the factory with one. That departure from specifications is a classic manufacturing defect that caused the crash.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A safe design does not defeat a manufacturing-defect claim when the injury-causing unit departed from that design by missing a steel belt. C: Injury alone does not prove a design defect. The facts point to a rogue unit, not a linewide unsafe design. D: Strict liability does not require proof of negligent maintenance. The manufacturing defect is the defective condition of the tire when it left the manufacturer.
lb_rule_reference: A single product that differs from its intended specifications can be defective in manufacture even when the product line's design is sound.
