Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: A citizen of Vermont sues a citizen of New Hampshire in federal diversity for $100,000 over a failed equipment sale. The defendant counterclaims for $12,000 arising from an unrelated apartment lease between the same parties two years earlier. Can the unrelated counterclaim rely on supplemental jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because Rule 13 permits counterclaims
Answer B: Yes, because the parties are already in federal court
Answer C: No, because the lease counterclaim is unrelated and lacks an independent jurisdictional basis
Answer D: No, because defendants can never file counterclaims in federal court
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The counterclaim does not form part of the same case or controversy and is below the diversity amount.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Procedural permission does not supply subject matter jurisdiction. B: Same parties do not create a common nucleus of operative fact. D: Defendants may file counterclaims, but SMJ must still exist.
lb_rule_reference: Unrelated counterclaims that do not share a common nucleus of operative fact need an independent jurisdictional basis.
