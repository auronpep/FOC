Question: A congressional committee subpoenas a narrow set of presidential briefing notes about a completed covert operation while investigating whether to amend the statute governing that program. The President refuses all production, stating that any reference to national security makes the materials completely immune from any legal process. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The privilege claim deserves special weight because national security is involved, but the label does not create automatic blanket immunity
Answer B: The materials are automatically immune because the President used the words "national security."
Answer C: The privilege is unavailable because Congress is investigating possible legislation
Answer D: The privilege is unavailable unless the President is a party to a criminal trial
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: National security concerns strengthen the executive interest in confidentiality. But the court still analyzes the privilege claim and the requesting body's need; the phrase "national security" is not a magic shield from all process.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Sensitivity matters, but the doctrine does not reduce to magic words. The claim must be evaluated in context. C: A legislative purpose supports Congress's request, but it does not erase executive privilege concerns, especially where national security is implicated. D: Executive privilege can arise outside criminal trials. The analysis changes with context, but the privilege is not limited to criminal cases.
lb_rule_reference: National security and diplomatic secrets give executive privilege special weight, but the privilege still must be analyzed as a constitutional claim rather than as a blanket immunity from judicial process.
