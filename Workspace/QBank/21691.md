Question: Leah contracted to renovate Joseph's storefront. The signed contract required written change orders. After signing, Joseph orally directed Leah to replace the specified brick with imported stone and later refused to pay for the stone, citing the written-change-order clause. Which additional fact would most strengthen Leah's argument that Joseph cannot rely on the written-change-order clause?
Answer A: Before signing, Joseph once mentioned that imported stone looked attractive.
Answer B: After Joseph approved a stone sample and said to proceed, Leah canceled the brick order and bought nonreturnable imported stone.
Answer C: The signed contract contained a merger clause as well as a written-change-order clause.
Answer D: Leah completed the original storefront work by the date stated in the contract.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a no-oral-modification or written-change-order clause may be overcome by waiver or estoppel when a party materially changes position in reliance on the later oral direction. The trigger fact in B is Joseph's post-signing approval followed by Leah's nonreturnable purchase. The decisive lock is reliance: it explains why strict reliance on the written-change-order clause may be unjust. The fact most directly supports waiver/estoppel, not merely contract preference or performance quality.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a pre-signing preference is weaker and risks a parol-evidence route. C: C is wrong because an additional merger clause strengthens the written-integration argument rather than Leah's reliance argument. D: D is wrong because timely original performance does not show reliance on the later stone change.
