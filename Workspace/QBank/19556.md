Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: Moses orally agrees to sell a small field to Leah for $80,000. Leah pays a refundable $1,000 deposit but does not take possession, make improvements, or receive any signed writing from Moses. Moses later refuses to convey the field. Leah sues for specific performance. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The oral agreement is enforceable because Leah paid some money.
Answer B: The oral agreement is enforceable because the price and property were clearly stated.
Answer C: The oral agreement is void, so neither party may ever rely on it for any purpose.
Answer D: Moses has a Statute of Frauds defense because a land sale contract generally requires a sufficient signed writing absent an applicable exception.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A contract for the sale of an interest in land is within the Statute of Frauds. A small refundable deposit alone, without possession, improvements, or a signed writing, does not supply the usual part-performance route to specific enforcement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because partial payment alone is generally not enough for land-sale part performance. B: B is wrong because clarity of oral terms does not replace the writing requirement. C: C is wrong because the SOF generally makes the contract unenforceable, not necessarily void for all purposes.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law Statute of Frauds — land interest
