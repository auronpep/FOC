Outline: Personal knowledge
Question: In a federal robbery trial, Caleb testifies that he saw the robber run under a streetlight for two seconds from about forty feet away and recognized the robber as David, whom he had known for years. David objects that two seconds was too short for Caleb to have personal knowledge. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because two seconds is never enough time to identify a person.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless another eyewitness independently identifies David first.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Caleb's claimed perception and prior familiarity are sufficient foundation; the short viewing time goes to weight.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the judge personally finds by a preponderance that David was the robber.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Caleb's testimony that he saw the robber and recognized David from prior familiarity is sufficient to support a finding of personal knowledge. The brief viewing time, distance, and lighting are credibility and weight issues for cross-examination.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because there is no categorical minimum viewing time. B: B is wrong because corroboration is not required. D: D is wrong because the judge screens for sufficient foundation; the judge does not decide the ultimate identity question before admission.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 / identification basis and weakness as weight
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests foundation over-demand and the distinction between perception opportunity and ultimate accuracy.
