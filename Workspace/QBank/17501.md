Question: A neighbor installed motion lights over a driveway. The lights briefly shine toward a homeowner's bedroom window when a car enters at night, but curtains block the light and other residents describe the effect as ordinary for a residential street. The homeowner sues for private nuisance. Is the homeowner likely to establish private nuisance?
Answer A: Yes, because any unwanted light from neighboring land is a nuisance
Answer B: Yes, because the homeowner subjectively finds the light irritating
Answer C: No, because the facts show only ordinary neighborhood light that is not a substantial interference
Answer D: No, because nuisance is limited to smoke or odors
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Brief, manageable light on these facts does not meet the nuisance threshold.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Nuisance requires more than any unwanted effect. B: The interference must be substantial and unreasonable under an objective standard. D: Nuisance can involve intangible invasions, but only if the interference is substantial and unreasonable.
lb_rule_reference: A private nuisance claim requires substantial and unreasonable interference with use and enjoyment of land, judged objectively.
