Question: A former employee intentionally changed the password on a company tablet without permission. The company could not use the tablet for two days until a technician restored access. The tablet was not physically damaged and was returned to normal function. The company sues for trespass to chattels and conversion. Which claim best fits?
Answer A: Trespass to chattels, because the employee intentionally interfered with use and caused temporary loss of use
Answer B: Conversion, because any intentional interference with a device is automatically full-value liability
Answer C: No tort, because there was no physical damage to the tablet
Answer D: Battery, because the employee used an electronic device
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The interference caused dispossession or loss of use but was not serious enough to require forced-sale damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Conversion requires serious dominion or interference; temporary lockout points to trespass to chattels. C: Loss of use or dispossession can satisfy trespass to chattels. D: Battery protects persons, not the company's possessory interest in a tablet.
lb_rule_reference: Trespass to chattels can arise from intentional interference with use or possession that causes loss of use or impairment, even if the chattel is later restored.
