Outline: Liquidated damages
Question: Ruth contracted to buy Daniel's small commercial lot for $300,000. The contract provided that Ruth's $15,000 deposit would be retained as liquidated damages if she wrongfully failed to close. When the contract was signed, the parties reasonably expected that resale delay, market movement, carrying costs, and lost opportunities would be difficult to measure, and the 5% deposit was a reasonable forecast of that loss. Ruth wrongfully repudiated. Daniel resold the lot two months later for the same price and proved no net actual loss. What is Daniel's best recovery?
Answer A: Nothing, because Daniel ultimately resold the lot for the same price.
Answer B: Nominal damages only, because no compensatory market loss was proved.
Answer C: Only any carrying costs that Daniel can prove with certainty, because the deposit clause is displaced by later actual damages.
Answer D: $15,000, because the liquidated-deposit term was reasonable when made and the damages were difficult to estimate.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation for breach of a land-sale contract, measured by a valid liquidated-damages deposit. The breach posture is Ruth's buyer repudiation. The limiting gate is penalty: the clause is enforceable because the anticipated resale and delay losses were hard to estimate when the contract was made and the 5% deposit was a reasonable forecast. The formula is the stipulated amount, $15,000. The result scope is limited to the valid clause; the answer does not award specific performance or a separate market-differential measure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it measures enforceability only by hindsight actual loss and ignores the contracting-time reasonableness inquiry. B: B is wrong because nominal damages are not the fallback when a valid liquidated-damages clause supplies the compensatory measure. C: C is wrong because it displaces the stipulated remedy with a later actual-damages formula, a timing and penalty-analysis error under WAL-REM-004 and WAL-REM-015.
lb_rule_reference: Common law land contract liquidated damages — later zero actual loss does not automatically defeat a reasonable clause
