


BARMATRIX Q#: 22281
Outline_code: 75070000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: A college student was growing marijuana in his dorm room closet. The dorm was owned by the college, which was a private school. The dorms were set up like motels, with individual doors leading outside to the street. One day, when the student had his door open while he was carrying in groceries, a police officer and his drug- sniffing dog walked by. Because of recent robberies in the area, the police officer approached the student and asked if he could ask him a few questions. While the officer was trying to speak with the student, the officer's dog stepped into the dorm room. The dog immediately alerted and dragged the officer over to the closet. The officer opened the closet, discovered the student's marijuana operation, and arrested the student. At trial, the student asked the court to suppress the marijuana. Should the court grant the student's motion?
Answer A: A: No, because the student did not own the dorm room.
Answer B: B: No, because the dog's sniff did not count as a search.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the officer did not have a search warrant.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the officer was investigating robberies unrelated to the marijuana.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: If a person has a reasonable expectation of privacy in a place, a search of that place requires a search warrant under the Fourth Amendment. Here, the student would clearly have a reasonable expectation of privacy in his dorm room, so the officer could not search it without a valid warrant. A is incorrect because the student would still have a reasonable expectation in the dorm room even though he did not own it. B is incorrect. While a dog sniff of something in public is lawful, the sniff here is an unlawful search because the dog entered the student's home. D is incorrect because the officer's reason for being in the area was irrelevant.
