Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: In a federal civil trial, Ruth offers a transcript of prior testimony by Nathan, who is now unavailable. Ruth argues that the transcript is former testimony because Samuel, the party against whom it is offered, cross-examined Nathan in the earlier case. Samuel objects that the earlier case involved a different issue. Who bears the burden on the admissibility foundation for the transcript?
Answer A: Ruth must establish the foundation for the former-testimony route, including the required opportunity and similar motive.
Answer B: Samuel must disprove similar motive because he objects to the evidence.
Answer C: The jury must decide admissibility because the transcript may affect the merits.
Answer D: The transcript is automatically admissible because the witness is unavailable.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The proponent of hearsay evidence bears the burden of establishing the exception or exclusion that permits admission. Ruth must establish the former-testimony foundation. If admitted, the jury evaluates the testimony's weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the objecting party does not bear the burden of disproving the proponent's chosen hearsay route. C: C is wrong because the court decides admissibility; the jury weighs admitted proof. D: D is wrong because unavailability alone is not enough for former testimony.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 804(b)(1); preliminary foundation
