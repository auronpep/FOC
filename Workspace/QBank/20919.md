Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Ruth signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey my field to Boaz.” The deed described the field accurately and used present words of transfer. Boaz accepted the deed but did not sign it. Ruth later argued that the deed was ineffective because only she signed the instrument. Who owns the field?
Answer A: Ruth, because a deed must be signed by both grantor and grantee.
Answer B: Ruth, because Boaz did not promise to pay consideration in the deed.
Answer C: Boaz only after he signs and records the deed.
Answer D: Boaz, because the grantor's signature is required but the grantee's signature generally is not.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is the grantor's execution of the conveyance. Ruth signed and delivered a deed with sufficient parties, property description, and present transfer words. Boaz's signature was not required to receive title because he accepted the conveyance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the grantee's signature is not a baseline deed-validity requirement. B: B is wrong because consideration is not required for a delivered deed. C: C is wrong because neither grantee signature nor recording is required for validity between Ruth and Boaz.
lb_rule_reference: Grantee signature not required / acceptance presumed or shown
