Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Larceny
Question: Norbert is a cashier at a hardware store. His manager asks him to take the day's cash receipts to the bank for deposit. While walking to the bank, Norbert decides to pocket $400 from the deposit bag and deposit only the remainder. Norbert was entrusted with the full deposit bag as part of his job duties. Of which crime is Norbert most likely guilty?
Answer A: Larceny, because Norbert took the $400 without the owner's consent
Answer B: Embezzlement, because Norbert fraudulently converted property entrusted to him
Answer C: False pretenses, because Norbert intended to deceive his employer about the amount deposited
Answer D: Larceny by trick, because Norbert obtained the money through a scheme
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Embezzlement is the fraudulent conversion of property of another by a person in lawful possession of that property, typically in a fiduciary or employment relationship. All elements are met: (1) Norbert was entrusted with the deposit bag in his capacity as an employee — a relationship giving rise to lawful possession; (2) he converted $400 by pocketing it; (3) the conversion was of property belonging to his employer; and (4) it was done with fraudulent intent. Because initial possession was lawful and arose from an entrustment relationship, embezzlement — not larceny — is the correct charge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Larceny requires a trespassory taking — one in which the defendant had no lawful right to possession at the moment of taking. Here, Norbert's initial possession of the entire deposit bag was lawful; the manager entrusted it to him for the specific purpose of deposit. Because initial possession was lawful, the crime is embezzlement, not larceny. The conversion occurred from a position of lawful possession. C: False pretenses requires that the defendant obtain title to property by a false representation of a present material fact. Norbert did not acquire the $400 by deceiving his employer into transferring title to him. He already had lawful possession and simply converted it. The deception here is incidental to the conversion, not the mechanism by which property was obtained. False pretenses does not apply. D: Larceny by trick applies where the defendant uses fraud to induce the owner to hand over possession of property. Here, Norbert did not use fraud to obtain possession — he was straightforwardly entrusted with the bag for a legitimate purpose. His fraudulent intent formed after lawful possession was already obtained. Lawful initial possession followed by conversion equals embezzlement.
lb_rule_reference: Embezzlement requires that the defendant's initial possession of the property be lawful — typically through entrustment by the owner in a fiduciary or employment relationship. If the initial taking was trespassory, the crime is larceny, not embezzlement.
