Question: Sarah hired Jacob to repair four separate cottages on her land. The written contract listed a separate price of $8,000 for each cottage, payable when that cottage was completed. Jacob fully repaired the first two cottages, but before he began the third, Sarah refused to let him continue and refused to pay anything. What is Jacob's strongest contract claim?
Answer A: He cannot recover until all four cottages are repaired because the contract concerns one project.
Answer B: Sarah owes the contract price for the two completed cottages, and Jacob may also seek appropriate damages for Sarah's refusal to allow further performance.
Answer C: Jacob may recover only the reasonable value of his labor because divisible contracts are never enforced at the stated price.
Answer D: Jacob may recover the full price for all four cottages because Sarah refused to let him finish.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The contract is divisible by cottage: each unit had a separate price and payment became due when that cottage was completed. Jacob fully performed the first two units, so Sarah's payment duty matured for those units. Her refusal to allow further work may create additional damages, but it does not eliminate the matured unit payments.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the separate unit prices and unit-payment language make the contract divisible. C: C is wrong because completed divisible units can be compensated at the contract rate. D: D is wrong because Jacob did not complete all four units, so the full price is not automatically due.
