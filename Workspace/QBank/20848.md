Question: Samuel is charged with arson. The prosecutor offers testimony that Samuel confessed while in custody. Samuel objects and says the confession was involuntary. The judge proposes to let the officer repeat the confession in front of the jury and then instruct the jury to disregard it if the jury thinks it was involuntary. Is that procedure proper?
Answer A: Yes, because the jury always decides voluntariness after hearing the confession.
Answer B: Yes, because a limiting instruction is always an adequate substitute for a preliminary hearing.
Answer C: No, because the court must conduct the confession-admissibility hearing so that the jury cannot hear it.
Answer D: No, but only because confessions must be written to be admissible.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A hearing on a preliminary question involving the admissibility of a confession must be conducted outside the jury's hearing. The court cannot simply expose the jury to the confession and then ask the jury to decide admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the jury does not decide threshold admissibility of the confession. B: B is wrong because the required outside-jury hearing cannot be replaced by a general instruction. D: D is wrong because oral confessions are not categorically inadmissible.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(c) / confession hearing cannot be replaced by jury instruction
