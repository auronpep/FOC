Question: A city charges food trucks a higher parking permit fee than brick-and-mortar restaurants. Food truck owners sue under Equal Protection. No suspect class or fundamental right is involved. What is the correct rational-basis burden?
Answer A: The city must prove with evidence that the higher fee actually reduces congestion
Answer B: The owners must negate every conceivable legitimate basis for the fee classification
Answer C: Strict scrutiny applies because the fee affects the right to work
Answer D: Intermediate scrutiny applies because small businesses are politically weak
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Economic classifications without suspect class or fundamental right receive deferential rational basis review.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under rational basis, the challenger bears the burden and conceivable justifications can suffice. C: Ordinary economic work regulations generally do not trigger strict scrutiny. D: Economic status is not a quasi-suspect class.
lb_rule_reference: Under rational basis review, the challenger bears the burden to negate every conceivable legitimate basis for the classification.
