Outline: Diversity Jurisdiction
Question: Lena is a U.S. citizen who has permanently moved to Portugal with no intent to return to any U.S. state. She sues Mateo, a citizen of Texas, in federal court for $100,000 under state law. Is ordinary diversity jurisdiction available?
Answer A: Yes, because Lena is diverse from Texas by living abroad
Answer B: Yes, because all U.S. citizens are citizens of their birth states forever
Answer C: No, because Lena is not a citizen of any U.S. state for diversity purposes
Answer D: No, because the amount in controversy is too high
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: She is a U.S. citizen, but her domicile abroad leaves her stateless for ordinary diversity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A U.S. citizen domiciled abroad is not a state citizen for Section 1332(a)(1). B: Domicile, not birthplace alone, controls. D: The amount exceeds the threshold; citizenship is the issue.
lb_rule_reference: A U.S. citizen must be a citizen of a state to invoke ordinary diversity jurisdiction as a state citizen.
