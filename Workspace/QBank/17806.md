Outline: Assumption of risk
Question: A user discovers that an electric drill sparks from a cracked cord. The manual instructs users to stop using the drill if the cord is cracked. The user understands the shock risk but continues drilling because he wants to finish quickly. He is shocked and injured. What is the manufacturer's strongest plaintiff-conduct argument?
Answer A: The user knowingly and voluntarily encountered the specific defect risk
Answer B: The user assumed all possible risks merely by buying a drill
Answer C: Plaintiff conduct is always irrelevant in strict products liability
Answer D: The claim fails only if the drill was not a product
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Awareness of the cracked cord and shock risk supports an assumption-of-risk or comparative-responsibility defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The defense depends on knowing a specific risk, not owning a tool. C: Known-risk conduct can matter. D: The drill is a product; the issue is the user's knowing encounter with the defect.
lb_rule_reference: Knowing and voluntary encounter with a specific known product defect can support assumption of risk or comparative responsibility in a products case.
