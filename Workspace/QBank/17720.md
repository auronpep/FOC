Question: After a final trial judgment, the losing party files a timely appeal. While the appeal is pending, the winning party invokes the judgment for preclusion in a second case. The losing party argues that a pending appeal always destroys finality for preclusion everywhere. What is the best response?
Answer A: The losing party is correct because appeal always prevents preclusion
Answer B: The court should identify the governing preclusion law and determine whether that law treats the judgment as final during appeal
Answer C: Preclusion applies only after the United States Supreme Court denies review
Answer D: Appealability and preclusion are the same question
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Finality during appeal is a source-law question.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Many systems treat a final judgment as preclusive unless and until reversed, but governing law must be checked. C: That is not the general finality threshold. D: Whether an order may be appealed and whether a judgment is preclusive are different inquiries.
lb_rule_reference: Whether a judgment remains final for preclusion during appeal depends on the governing preclusion law; do not assume appeal always destroys finality.
