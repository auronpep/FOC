Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: A defendant was sued in federal court. Before answering, the defendant filed a Rule 12(b)(6) motion arguing that the complaint failed to state a claim. The motion did not mention personal jurisdiction, service, or venue, even though the facts supporting a PJ objection were known at the time. After the court denied the 12(b)(6) motion, the defendant filed a Rule 12(b)(2) motion to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. How should the court rule on the later personal-jurisdiction motion?
Answer A: Grant it because personal jurisdiction can be challenged at any time
Answer B: Grant it because a defendant may file one Rule 12 motion for each defense
Answer C: Deny it because the defendant waived the PJ defense by omitting it from the first Rule 12 motion
Answer D: Deny it only if the plaintiff also proves complete diversity
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The PJ objection was available when the defendant filed the first pre-answer Rule 12 motion. Omission waives the Rule 12(b)(2) defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Personal jurisdiction is waivable and must be included in the first Rule 12 response when available. B: Rule 12(g) and 12(h) require consolidation of certain defenses and impose waiver for omissions. D: Diversity is SMJ. The waiver question turns on Rule 12 timing, not citizenship.
lb_rule_reference: A party waives a Rule 12(b)(2) personal-jurisdiction defense by omitting it from the first Rule 12 motion when the defense was available.
