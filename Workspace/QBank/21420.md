Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: After Miranda warnings, Keon said, "I want a lawyer." Detectives stopped questioning and left. An hour later, Keon called a detective back and asked, "If I tell you where the car is, can we talk about the charges?" The detective reread Miranda warnings. Keon said he understood, waived, and confessed. Is Keon's confession barred solely because he earlier requested counsel?
Answer A: Yes, because once counsel is requested, no later waiver is possible without a lawyer present
Answer B: Yes, because Keon's question about charges was not a full confession
Answer C: No, because Keon reinitiated discussion and then waived after fresh warnings
Answer D: No, because police could have resumed questioning after one hour even if Keon had remained silent
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Police honored the counsel request. Keon later initiated a conversation about the case, received fresh warnings, and waived. The confession is not barred on Edwards grounds.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A suspect can reinitiate communication and then validly waive after fresh warnings. B: Reinitiation requires opening further communication about the investigation; it need not itself be a confession. D: After counsel invocation, police cannot reinitiate merely because an hour passed. The key is Keon's reinitiation.
lb_rule_reference: Police may not reinitiate after a clear counsel invocation, but if the suspect initiates further communication and then knowingly and voluntarily waives, questioning may resume.
