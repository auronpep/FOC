Outline: Duty to mitigate
Question: Sarah contracted to teach advanced mathematics at a private school for a school-year salary of $60,000. Before the school year began, the school wrongfully canceled the contract. Sarah made a diligent search, but no comparable teaching or academic position was available. The only job offered to her was an overnight desk-clerk position at a motel for $25,000, with lower status and different duties. Sarah declined that job. What is Sarah's best damages recovery?
Answer A: $60,000.
Answer B: $35,000, the contract salary minus the motel wages she could have earned.
Answer C: Nothing, because Sarah declined available employment.
Answer D: Nominal damages only, because damages after a declined job offer are too uncertain.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under employment/common-law rules. The breach posture is anticipatory breach of an employment contract, and the loss category is lost wages. The mitigation gate asks whether substantially comparable employment was available. Sarah had no duty to accept inferior or different work, so the motel wages are not offset. The formula is $60,000 unpaid salary − $0 comparable-employment offset = $60,000. The result scope is full wage recovery for the contract period only.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it treats inferior and different work as comparable employment, triggering WAL-REM-032. C: C is wrong because declining unsuitable work is not mitigation forfeiture under WAL-REM-009. D: D is wrong because the unpaid salary is certain; nominal damages are not a shortcut when a compensatory wage formula applies.
lb_rule_reference: Common law employment mitigation — employee need not accept inferior or different employment
