Outline: Identifications
Question: Police conduct a suggestive stationhouse identification that the court suppresses. The witness had also spent fifteen minutes face-to-face with the robber in a bright office, gave a detailed description before the stationhouse procedure, and recognized the defendant in court based on that original encounter. May the in-court identification be admitted?
Answer A: Yes, if the prosecution proves the in-court identification rests on an independent source from the witness's original observations
Answer B: No, because any earlier defective identification permanently bars all later identifications
Answer C: Yes, because in-court identifications are never subject to suppression
Answer D: No, because the witness spent too much time with the robber
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A suppressed pretrial procedure does not automatically bar every later identification. Strong original observations can provide an independent source.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Independent-source analysis can permit the later in-court identification. C: In-court identifications can be excluded if they are tainted and lack an independent source. D: A longer, well-lit observation supports independent source; it does not defeat it.
lb_rule_reference: A later in-court identification may be admitted despite an earlier defective identification procedure if the prosecution shows an independent source based on the witness's observations at the crime.
