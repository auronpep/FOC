Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Samuel is charged with stealing rare coins from a shop. The prosecutor offers a message sent to the shop owner the night before the theft: “I will take the Roman coin from the locked green case.” The message was sent from an account using Samuel's nickname. A witness testifies that only Samuel and the owner knew the Roman coin was in the locked green case. Samuel objects that the account name could have been faked. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the message because account names can always be faked.
Answer B: Admit the message if the nickname and distinctive knowledge are sufficient to support a finding that Samuel sent it.
Answer C: Exclude the message unless the prosecutor proves Samuel's password was used.
Answer D: Admit the message only if a computer expert traces the account to Samuel's device.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Distinctive contents and surrounding circumstances may authenticate a message. The proponent need not eliminate all possibility of fabrication at the threshold stage. The jury decides whether Samuel actually sent it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because possible fabrication affects weight, not necessarily admissibility. C: C is wrong because password proof is not required. D: D is wrong because expert tracing is not the only authentication route.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(4)
lb_student_diagnostic: Digital-authentication over-demand; ignoring distinctive contents.
