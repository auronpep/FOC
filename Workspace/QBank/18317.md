MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Mistake
Question: In a jurisdiction that follows the minority alter-ego rule, Sam sees Officer Lee wrestling Alex to the ground in plain clothes. Sam reasonably but mistakenly believes Lee is a mugger and punches Lee to free Alex. In fact, Lee is lawfully arresting Alex, and Alex has no right to resist. Under the alter-ego rule, does Sam have a defense-of-others claim?
Answer A: Yes, because Sam's belief was reasonable
Answer B: Yes, because defense of others always applies to strangers
Answer C: No, because Alex had no right to resist the lawful arrest and Sam stands in Alex's shoes
Answer D: No, because defense of others is never recognized in criminal law
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the alter-ego rule, a defender of another is privileged only if the defended person actually had a self-defense right. Lee was making a lawful arrest, so Alex had no right to resist. Sam's reasonable mistake would matter under the modern rule, but not under this supplied minority rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The question specifies the minority alter-ego rule. Under that rule, Sam gets only the defense Alex actually had, and Alex had none. B: The problem is not Sam's relationship to Alex. It is that Alex had no actual right to resist in an alter-ego jurisdiction. D: Defense of others is recognized. Sam loses because of the alter-ego limitation, not because the doctrine does not exist.
lb_rule_reference: Under the minority alter-ego rule, a defender of another steps into the defended person's shoes and loses the defense if that person had no right to self-defense
