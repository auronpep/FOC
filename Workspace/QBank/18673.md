Outline: Marital Communications Privilege
Question: Samuel sent a group message to his wife Hannah and his brother Aaron saying, “I altered the bid before submitting it.” In a federal contract-fraud case, the government calls Hannah to identify the message. Samuel asserts marital communications privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because Hannah was one of the recipients.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because family members are treated as part of the marital unit.
Answer C: Overrule the objection, because Samuel did not make the communication confidentially to Hannah alone.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Aaron is unavailable to testify.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The marital communications privilege requires confidentiality between spouses. Samuel intentionally included Aaron, so the message was not a confidential communication between spouses alone. The decisive axis is voluntary third-party disclosure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Including a spouse as one recipient does not make a group message privileged. B: B is wrong because The privilege does not extend automatically to siblings or other family members. D: D is wrong because Aaron's availability is not the issue; the communication was nonconfidential when sent.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / communication not confidential if shared beyond spouse
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is letting the marital relationship override the confidentiality requirement.
