Outline: Nonresponsive answers
Question: In a federal fraud trial, Isaac calls Rebekah to testify. Isaac's lawyer asks, “Were you at the warehouse at noon?” Rebekah answers, “Yes, and I saw David hand Isaac a sealed envelope.” David moves to strike the envelope statement solely because Rebekah did not stop after saying “yes.” David does not claim hearsay, lack of personal knowledge, prejudice, or any other substantive inadmissibility. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant the motion because any answer that says more than yes or no must be struck.
Answer B: Grant the motion because only Isaac's lawyer may use the envelope testimony.
Answer C: Deny the motion because nonresponsiveness alone, without an independent inadmissibility ground, does not require striking otherwise relevant firsthand testimony at the opposing party's request.
Answer D: Deny the motion because David failed to object before Rebekah answered.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A court may control the mode of examination, but a nonexamining party does not obtain automatic exclusion merely because a witness gave a fuller answer than expected. The statement is based on Rebekah's firsthand observation and David has asserted no substantive reason for exclusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because answers need not be limited mechanically to yes or no. B: B is wrong because relevant testimony is not reserved to the party who called the witness. D: D is wrong because the objection is to the answer after it was given; the problem is that the stated ground is insufficient.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / nonexaminer standing and admissible volunteered matter
