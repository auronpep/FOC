Question: Hannah hired Isaac to prepare a custom set of design drawings for a fixed fee, payable “upon delivery of the completed drawings.” Isaac completed rough sketches and demanded half the fee before preparing final drawings. The contract contained no progress-payment term. Hannah refused. Who is most likely to prevail on Isaac's claim for half the fee?
Answer A: Hannah, because Isaac's delivery of completed drawings is a constructive condition to Hannah's payment duty.
Answer B: Isaac, because any partial performance creates a right to half the contract price.
Answer C: Isaac, because custom design contracts are always divisible by hours worked.
Answer D: Hannah, but only because Isaac acted in bad faith.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Where a contract for work over time provides payment upon delivery of completed work and contains no progress-payment provision, completion and delivery are constructive conditions to payment. Isaac's rough sketches do not make Hannah's payment duty mature.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because partial performance does not automatically trigger half the contract price. C: C is wrong because the contract is not stated as hourly or divisible. D: D is wrong because the lack of bad faith does not make the payment duty mature early.
