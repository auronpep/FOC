Question: In a federal criminal trial of a former official, the prosecutor subpoenas recordings of Oval Office conversations that directly concern the charged obstruction scheme. The President asserts executive privilege over all presidential communications and argues that courts may never require disclosure of confidential presidential discussions. Which statement best describes the privilege?
Answer A: The privilege is absolute whenever presidential communications are confidential
Answer B: The privilege may yield because the subpoena seeks specific evidence needed in a criminal trial
Answer C: The privilege belongs only to former Presidents, not sitting Presidents
Answer D: The privilege is unavailable because the subpoena comes from the executive branch
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A generalized interest in confidentiality can be overcome by a demonstrated, specific need for evidence in a criminal case. The subpoena targets directly relevant recordings, not a broad fishing expedition.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Executive privilege is qualified, not absolute, especially when specific evidence is needed for a criminal proceeding. C: The privilege protects presidential communications, but its qualified character applies regardless of that status point. D: Courts still balance the privilege against the need for evidence in a criminal proceeding. Same-branch origin does not eliminate the privilege or make it absolute.
lb_rule_reference: Executive privilege for presidential communications is qualified; it may yield to a demonstrated, specific need for evidence in a criminal proceeding.
