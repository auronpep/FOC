Question: Joseph is charged with damaging his employer's delivery van. The prosecutor offers evidence that Joseph had been told the day before that he would be fired. Joseph objects that many people are fired without damaging company property. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because the firing notice has some tendency to show motive; the fact that many fired employees do not retaliate goes to weight.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because motive evidence is relevant only if it proves the defendant acted.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence because employment disputes are unfairly prejudicial as a matter of law.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if the prosecutor proves Joseph said he wanted revenge.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Being told of an impending firing can make retaliatory motive more probable. The evidence need not prove that Joseph acted or that every similarly situated person would act. The defense argument weakens the inference but does not defeat relevance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because motive evidence need not independently prove conduct. C: C is wrong because this is not a categorical unfair-prejudice rule. D: D is wrong because a direct revenge statement is not required for motive relevance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Over-demanding causal certainty from motive evidence.
