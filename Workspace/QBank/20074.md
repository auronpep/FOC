Subtopic: Defenses to Enforceability
Outline: Mistake / Lack of Mutual Assent
Question: Esther agrees to buy a unique handmade harp from Miriam and pays a $2,000 deposit. Unknown to both parties, the harp had been destroyed in a storage fire the day before the contract was signed. Miriam had no reason to know of the fire before signing. What is the best result?
Answer A: The contract is avoidable because both parties were mistaken about the existence of the subject matter, and Esther may recover the deposit.
Answer B: Miriam must deliver a substitute harp of equal value.
Answer C: Esther must pay the full price because she assumed all fire risk by paying a deposit.
Answer D: The contract is enforceable because Miriam did not cause the fire.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The existence of the unique subject matter at the time of contract was a basic assumption. Because the harp had already been destroyed without the parties' knowledge, the contract is avoidable and restitution of the deposit is appropriate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the bargain was for a unique harp, not any substitute. C: C is wrong because paying a deposit does not itself allocate preexisting destruction risk to Esther. D: D is wrong because fault is not required for mutual mistake.
lb_rule_reference: Preexisting destruction / mutual mistake
