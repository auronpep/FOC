Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: When Iris found out that her business partner, Greg, had been secretly skimming money from their joint account, she was furious. She confronted Greg that day in a heated argument but did not harm him. That night, Iris spent three hours reading financial records confirming the theft, then decided she wanted Greg dead. The following morning, Iris parked near Greg's jogging route, waited for two hours, and shot him as he ran past. Iris told police she was still "angry about the money" when she shot him. What is the most serious homicide offense for which Iris can properly be convicted?
Answer A: Voluntary manslaughter, because the embezzlement constituted adequate provocation and Iris was still angry when she shot Greg
Answer B: Second-degree murder, because Iris's anger over the embezzlement negates the cool deliberation required for first-degree murder
Answer C: First-degree murder, because Iris cooled off, formed a deliberate plan, and lay in wait — establishing premeditation and deliberation
Answer D: First-degree murder, because embezzlement by a business partner can never constitute adequate provocation at common law
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: First-degree murder requires premeditation and deliberation. Iris's initial provocation may have been real, but she did not act on it immediately — she confronted Greg without violence and then spent the evening reviewing records and forming a plan. By the following morning, any heat of passion had cooled. Iris then planned the killing in advance, drove to the jogging route, waited two hours, and executed the plan methodically. This is classic premeditated and deliberate murder. The original provocation does not preserve a heat-of-passion claim once the defendant has cooled and planned.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Two elements of voluntary manslaughter fail here. First, the cooling-off period: a full day elapsed between the provocation and the killing, during which Iris read records for three hours and made a deliberate decision to kill — more than enough time for a reasonable person to cool. Second, and independently, Iris planned the killing, waited two hours in ambush, and executed it methodically — the hallmarks of premeditation. Heat of passion and premeditation are mutually exclusive. B: Deliberation does not require absence of emotion. A defendant who makes a calculated decision to kill — even while emotionally motivated — satisfies deliberation if the decision was formed through planning and reflection rather than in the heat of an impulse. Iris spent hours reviewing records, made a deliberate decision the night before, arrived early, and waited in ambush. This is the definition of cool deliberation. D: Whether embezzlement constitutes adequate provocation is a separate question from whether Iris cooled off before killing. Even if the initial provocation was adequate (or even if it was not), first-degree murder is established because Iris cooled and then premeditated. D's stated rationale is at best incomplete and arguably incorrect — financial betrayal might constitute adequate provocation, but the correct reason for first-degree murder here is the cooling and subsequent planning, not the adequacy of the provocation.
lb_rule_reference: If a defendant cools off after initial provocation and then plans a killing, the heat-of-passion defense is lost; premeditation formed after cooling satisfies the deliberation requirement for first-degree murder even though the original motivation was a prior provocation
