Question: A restaurant manager blocked the front door and told a customer she could not leave until she paid a disputed bill. The customer knew that the unlocked side door two steps away opened directly to a safe public sidewalk. She remained inside for ten minutes because using the side door would have been embarrassing. The customer sues for false imprisonment. Is the manager likely liable?
Answer A: No, because the customer had a known, reasonable, safe means of exit
Answer B: Yes, because blocking any door is always confinement
Answer C: Yes, because embarrassment is enough to make an exit unreasonable
Answer D: No, but only because the confinement lasted ten minutes
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The blocked front door was not bounded confinement when a safe, known side exit was immediately available.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The analysis checks the whole boundary and available exits, not one blocked route. C: Mere embarrassment does not make a safe known exit unreasonable. D: Brief confinement can be actionable; the key fact is the reasonable safe exit.
lb_rule_reference: False imprisonment requires confinement within a bounded area; a reasonable, known, safe exit usually defeats confinement.
