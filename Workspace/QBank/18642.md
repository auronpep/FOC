Question: Daniel signed a deed conveying Lakeacre to Abigail as a gift. Without telling Abigail, Daniel took the deed to the recorder's office and had it recorded. The deed was beneficial to Abigail, and Abigail did not reject it when she later learned of it. Daniel later claimed the deed failed because Abigail did not physically receive it before recordation. What is the best result?
Answer A: Abigail owns Lakeacre because Daniel's act of recording the beneficial deed supports delivery, and acceptance is presumed absent rejection.
Answer B: Daniel owns Lakeacre because a grantee must physically receive the original deed before title can pass.
Answer C: Daniel owns Lakeacre because a gift deed requires the grantee's signature.
Answer D: Abigail owns Lakeacre only if she paid value before the deed was recorded.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery/acceptance locks are Daniel's objective act of recording and the beneficial character of the conveyance. A grantor's recording can evidence delivery, and acceptance of a beneficial deed is presumed unless the grantee rejects. Physical receipt before recordation was not required on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because physical possession by the grantee is not always required. C: C is wrong because the grantee's signature is not a deed-validity requirement. D: D is wrong because value is not required for delivery or acceptance of a gift deed.
