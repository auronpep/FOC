MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Battery
Question: At a rehearsal, an actor angrily threw a cup of water intending to splash a stagehand in the face. The stagehand ducked, and the water instead hit a musician seated nearby. The musician was not injured but was offended and had not joined the argument. The musician sues the actor for battery. Is the musician likely to prevail in a battery claim against the actor?
Answer A: No, because the actor did not intend to contact the musician
Answer B: Yes, because the actor's intent to make offensive contact with the stagehand transfers to the musician
Answer C: No, because the musician suffered no physical injury
Answer D: Yes, but only if the actor was negligent as to where the water would land
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Battery is within the traditional transferred-intent group. The actor intended offensive contact with one person and caused offensive contact with another.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intent can transfer from the intended victim to the actual victim for battery. C: Offensive contact can support battery without physical injury, and transferred intent supplies the intent element. D: The claim is battery, and transferred intent supplies intentional-tort liability without proving negligence toward the actual victim.
lb_rule_reference: Intent transfers among the traditional intentional torts, including battery, when the defendant intends offensive contact with one person but causes offensive contact with another.
