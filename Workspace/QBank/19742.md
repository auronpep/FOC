Question: Esther signed a deed conveying Oliveacre to Ruth and handed it to Ruth, saying, “Hold this for me in your desk. I have not decided whether to give you Oliveacre, and you are not to treat it as yours.” Ruth later recorded the deed while Esther was still alive. Who owns Oliveacre?
Answer A: Esther, because the handoff was for safekeeping and Esther expressed no present intent to transfer title.
Answer B: Ruth, because delivery always occurs when the named grantee physically receives the deed.
Answer C: Ruth, because recording before Esther's death cured any uncertainty about delivery.
Answer D: Ruth, because a grantee's private decision to keep land always supplies delivery intent.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is Esther's present intent. Esther told Ruth that the deed was only being held for safekeeping and that Ruth should not treat the land as hers. Recording could not supply the missing delivery intent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because physical handoff to the grantee is not conclusive when the grantor negates present intent. C: C is wrong because recording does not cure nondelivery. D: D is wrong because the grantor's delivery intent, not the grantee's private wish, controls.
lb_rule_reference: Direct possession of the paper does not create delivery when grantor states the grantee is only holding it for safekeeping
