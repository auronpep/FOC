internal_id: TR-320
Outline_code: 62050402
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A yoga instructor signed a release allowing Studio to use her photo for one month on flyers advertising a charity class. Six months later, Studio used the same photo on paid ads for a commercial membership campaign. The instructor did not consent to that later use. The instructor sues Studio for appropriation. Can Studio rely on the original release?
Answer A: Yes, because any signed release allows all future commercial uses
Answer B: Yes, because the same studio took the photo
Answer C: No, because the later paid membership ads exceeded the release's purpose and time limit
Answer D: No, but only because appropriation requires reputational harm
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The instructor consented to one month of charity-class flyers, not later commercial membership advertising. The new use was outside the agreed scope.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Consent is limited by scope, purpose, and duration. The later commercial campaign exceeded the release. B: Control over the photo does not automatically authorize every use of the instructor's identity. D: Appropriation protects control over identity use. The issue is consent scope, not reputational injury.
lb_rule_reference: Consent can defeat appropriation only within its scope; a use outside the agreed purpose or duration can be unauthorized identity use.
