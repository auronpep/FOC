MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Law Applied by Federal Courts (Erie)
Outline: State Law Applied in Federal Courts
Question: A designer sued an independent contractor in federal court. The claim turned on the scope of work covered by the independent contractor's contract. The court held a bench trial. The designer and the contractor were the only witnesses, and they strongly disagreed on the scope of work under the contract. At the end of the trial, the judge stated findings of fact on the record and neither party objected to the findings. Ultimately, the judge ruled in favor of the contractor. The designer appealed, arguing that the findings should be overturned. Is the appellate court likely to overturn the findings?
Answer A: A: Yes, because there were disputed issues of fact at trial.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the decision was made in a bench trial.
Answer C: C: No, because the designer did not object to the findings before the judge's ruling.
Answer D: D: No, because the appellate court must give due regard to the trial judge's opportunity to determine witness credibility.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 52(a)(6), “findings of fact, whether based on oral or other evidence, must not be set aside unless clearly erroneous, and the reviewing court must give due regard to the trial court's opportunity to judge the witnesses' credibility.” Here, there's no indication that the findings of fact were clearly erroneous, so the appellate court should not overturn them. A is incorrect because the fact that there were disputed issues would not change the analysis. B is incorrect because the fact that it was a bench trial would not change the analysis. C is incorrect because the designer was not required to object before the judge's ruling.
