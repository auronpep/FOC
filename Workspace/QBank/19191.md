Question: Congress labels an exaction a "tax," but it applies only after a person violates a federal licensing rule, is set at many times the value of the transaction, and is enforced through criminal-style penalties. The government defends the measure under the taxing power based on the label alone. What is the strongest response?
Answer A: The label "tax" conclusively validates the exaction under the taxing power
Answer B: Punitive features can show the exaction functions as a penalty rather than a tax
Answer C: The exaction is valid because all penalties are taxes
Answer D: The exaction is invalid only if states also tax the same conduct
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The exaction is tied to unlawful conduct, severe, and punitive rather than a normal revenue measure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Courts examine function, not only label. C: A payment obligation can be a penalty rather than a tax. D: The tax-versus-penalty question does not turn on state taxation.
lb_rule_reference: An exaction labeled a tax may fail as a tax when it is punitive, triggered by unlawful conduct, and functions more like a regulatory penalty than a revenue measure.
