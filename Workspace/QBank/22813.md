


BARMATRIX Q#: 22813
Outline_code: 95070103
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Counterclaims
Question: A man, a citizen of State A, was in a car accident in State B with a woman who was a citizen of a foreign country. Both the man and the woman suffered serious injuries. The man filed a negligence action against the woman in federal district court in State B for $100,000. State B was a contributory negligence state. The woman believed that the man ran a stop sign and was therefore responsible for the accident. What is the best way for the woman to respond to the action?
Answer A: A: Move to dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction, because the woman is not a United States citizen.
Answer B: B: Move to dismiss for personal jurisdiction, because the woman is not a United States citizen.
Answer C: C: File an answer raising the affirmative defense of contributory negligence and move for judgment on the pleadings.
Answer D: D: File an answer raising the affirmative defense of contributory negligence and assert a counterclaim for negligence.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Contributory negligence is an affirmative defense to a negligence claim. In a contributory negligence state, if the defendant can show that the plaintiff was negligent, the plaintiff is barred from recovery. While this affirmative defense would protect the woman from liability, she would also need to file a counterclaim based on the man's negligence if she wanted to recover for the damages that she suffered. A is incorrect because the court has subject matter jurisdiction over the matter because the controversy exceeds $75,000 and the parties are citizens of different states or countries. 28 U.S.C. §1332. B is incorrect because the court would have personal jurisdiction over the matter since the accident occurred in State B. C is incorrect because it doesn't address the damages suffered by the woman.
