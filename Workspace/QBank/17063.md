Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: An anonymous caller says a woman named Rina will leave 15 Oak Street at 8:10 p.m., get into a blue sedan with a cracked windshield, drive to a specific motel, and deliver cocaine. Officers verify each predicted movement before stopping the sedan shortly before it reaches the motel. Did officers have reasonable suspicion for the stop?
Answer A: Yes, because corroboration of predictive details gave the anonymous tip sufficient reliability
Answer B: No, because anonymous tips can never justify a stop
Answer C: No, because officers needed probable cause before stopping the car
Answer D: Yes, because officers verified Rina's name only
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The caller accurately predicted future movements, suggesting inside knowledge. That corroboration supported reasonable suspicion for a Terry stop.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Corroborated predictive details can supply reliability even when the tipster is anonymous. C: A brief investigative stop requires reasonable suspicion, not probable cause. D: The reliability comes from corroborated predictive behavior, not just confirming an innocent identity detail.
lb_rule_reference: An anonymous tip may support a Terry stop when police corroborate predictive details showing the tipster had inside knowledge of criminal activity.
