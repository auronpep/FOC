Question: After Julian is indicted for bank robbery and counsel is appointed, police arrange an in-person lineup at the station. They do not notify counsel. The teller identifies Julian in the lineup. Julian moves to suppress the lineup identification. Should the lineup identification be suppressed?
Answer A: Yes, because the post-indictment in-person lineup was a critical stage conducted without counsel
Answer B: No, because identification procedures are governed only by due process reliability
Answer C: No, because the teller could have identified Julian in court later
Answer D: Yes, because counsel is required at every photo and in-person identification after arrest
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The indictment attached the Sixth Amendment right, and an in-person lineup after attachment is a critical stage requiring counsel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Due process is not the only doctrine. A post-indictment corporeal lineup also triggers the Sixth Amendment counsel rule. C: Possible later in-court identification does not save the uncounseled lineup identification itself. D: The correct rule depends on post-attachment corporeal confrontation. Counsel is not required for every post-arrest or photo identification.
lb_rule_reference: After formal adversary proceedings begin, an in-person lineup or showup is a critical stage at which the accused has a Sixth Amendment right to counsel.
