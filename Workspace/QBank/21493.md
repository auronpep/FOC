Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Radiant Stone Inc. is a gemstone dealer incorporated in Arizona with its principal place of business in Scottsdale. Radiant Stone maintains a website that displays photographs of its gemstone inventory, describes each stone's characteristics and approximate pricing, and provides contact information for its Scottsdale showroom. The website has no shopping cart, no account creation, no payment processing, and no interactive features beyond a static contact form that sends an email to the Scottsdale office. Forum-state customers who wish to purchase gems must travel to Scottsdale or call to arrange a transaction. A citizen of the State of Brenhaven viewed Radiant Stone's website and, based on a photograph and description of a ruby, arranged by phone to purchase it during a future Scottsdale visit. The ruby turned out to be misdescribed on the website, and the Brenhaven resident sued Radiant Stone in Brenhaven federal court for fraudulent misrepresentation. Radiant Stone has no other Brenhaven contacts. Does the Brenhaven court have specific personal jurisdiction over Radiant Stone?
Answer A: Yes, because Radiant Stone's website is accessible to Brenhaven residents and the plaintiff's purchase decision was induced by website content viewed in Brenhaven
Answer B: Yes, because the plaintiff's phone call to Scottsdale — initiated from Brenhaven — constitutes a contact between Radiant Stone and Brenhaven sufficient for specific jurisdiction
Answer C: No, because Radiant Stone's website is passive — purely informational with no transactional capability — and is accessible to the entire internet without deliberate Brenhaven targeting; accessibility alone does not constitute purposeful availment of the Brenhaven forum
Answer D: No, because the plaintiff purchased the ruby in Scottsdale, and the cause of action (fraudulent misrepresentation) arose in Scottsdale, where the transaction concluded
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The *Zippo* sliding scale places Radiant Stone's website at the passive end: photographs, descriptions, pricing, and a static contact form with no shopping cart, no accounts, no payment processing. A passive, informational website accessible from anywhere does not supply purposeful availment of any specific state. Radiant Stone took no deliberate action directed at Brenhaven — no targeted advertising, no Brenhaven-specific features, no Brenhaven transactions. The plaintiff's phone call to Scottsdale and subsequent Scottsdale visit are the plaintiff's contacts with Arizona, not Radiant Stone's contacts with Brenhaven. The Brenhaven court lacks specific personal jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under the *Zippo* framework, a passive, informational website — one that merely displays information without enabling transactions — does not supply purposeful availment. The fact that a Brenhaven resident could view the site does not make Radiant Stone subject to Brenhaven's jurisdiction. The plaintiff could have viewed any website from any state; a defendant with a viewable-but-passive site cannot be subject to jurisdiction everywhere. The relevant question is whether Radiant Stone deliberately directed interactive commercial activity at Brenhaven — it did not. B: The plaintiff's phone call is the plaintiff's act, not Radiant Stone's act. Under *World-Wide Volkswagen* and general purposeful-availment doctrine, the defendant must have directed its own activities at the forum. A plaintiff calling a defendant's Scottsdale office from Brenhaven is the plaintiff reaching into Arizona, not Radiant Stone reaching into Brenhaven. Unilateral acts of the plaintiff cannot supply the defendant's forum contacts. D: This answer reaches the right outcome but focuses on where the transaction occurred rather than on Radiant Stone's Brenhaven contacts (or lack thereof). The correct analysis is that Radiant Stone has no deliberate Brenhaven contacts — its website is passive, it took no Brenhaven-directed action, and the plaintiff's forum contacts do not supply the defendant's contacts. The transaction-completion location is a separate issue (and might actually support specific jurisdiction in Arizona, where the transaction did occur), not the operative reason Brenhaven lacks jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: Under the *Zippo* sliding scale: (1) highly interactive commercial websites — where the defendant knowingly transacts business with forum residents — support PJ; (2) middle-ground sites — where users can exchange information but not complete transactions — are analyzed case-by-case; (3) passive sites that merely post information are not sufficient for PJ. Most federal courts accept some version of this framework. A passive, informational website accessible from the forum does not by itself supply purposeful availment.
