Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers ask Henry if they may "look around the living room." Henry says yes. On a shelf, officers see a small locked metal box. Without asking for more permission, they pry it open and find drugs inside. Did Henry's consent authorize opening the locked box?
Answer A: No, because consent to look around the room did not reasonably include prying open a locked container
Answer B: Yes, because any consent to enter a room authorizes every container search inside it
Answer C: Yes, because locked boxes never carry privacy interests
Answer D: No, because consent searches are never valid without a warrant
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Consent scope is judged objectively. A limited permission to look around a room does not authorize forced opening of a locked box.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Consent is not all-or-nothing. Scope depends on what a reasonable person would understand from the permission given. C: Visibility of a container does not equal permission to force it open. D: Valid consent is a warrant exception. The defect here is scope, not the consent doctrine itself.
lb_rule_reference: A consent search is valid only within the scope of the consent given; permission to look around a room does not automatically permit forced entry into locked containers.
