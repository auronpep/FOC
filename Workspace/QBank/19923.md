Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Nexo Hospitality Group Inc. ("Nexo") is a franchise system incorporated and headquartered in the State of Ralford. Nexo licenses its branded hotel concept to franchisees nationwide through standardized franchise agreements. Bartholomew, a Michigan entrepreneur, entered a 20-year franchise agreement with Nexo to operate a Nexo-branded hotel in Michigan. The agreement was negotiated with Nexo's Ralford headquarters team, is governed by Ralford law, requires Bartholomew to make monthly royalty payments to Nexo in Ralford, obligates Bartholomew to follow Nexo's Ralford-developed operational manuals, and authorizes Nexo to audit Bartholomew's Michigan operations from Ralford. After two years, a dispute arose and Nexo filed suit against Bartholomew in Ralford federal court for breach of the franchise agreement. Bartholomew moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction, arguing that all his operations are in Michigan and he has never been to Ralford. Should the Ralford federal court exercise personal jurisdiction over Bartholomew?
Answer A: No, because Bartholomew's hotel is located in Michigan and all his work is performed there — his activities occurred in Michigan, not Ralford
Answer B: No, because Bartholomew is a Michigan resident and Michigan is the natural forum for a Michigan resident defending a suit
Answer C: Yes, because Bartholomew deliberately entered a long-term franchise relationship centered in Ralford — with Ralford-based negotiations, Ralford choice-of-law, Ralford royalty payments, and Ralford supervisory oversight — constituting purposeful availment of the Ralford forum for franchise-related claims
Answer D: Yes, but only because Ralford's law governs the agreement — the choice-of-law clause is independently sufficient to establish personal jurisdiction in Ralford
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under *Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz*, a franchisee who knowingly and voluntarily enters a comprehensive, long-term franchise agreement with a forum-based franchisor — negotiated in the forum, governed by forum law, requiring ongoing payments to the forum, and subject to the forum franchisor's supervisory oversight — has purposefully availed itself of the forum for claims arising from the franchise relationship. The breach-of-franchise claim arises directly from that relationship. Nexo's Ralford long-arm statute (transacting business) is satisfied by the ongoing royalty payment and supervisory relationship. Jurisdiction is proper.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Under *Burger King*, the relevant question is not just where the franchisee's physical operations occur but whether he purposefully availed himself of the franchisor's forum by entering a long-term, heavily regulated contractual relationship centered on that forum. Bartholomew negotiated with Ralford, agreed to pay royalties there, accepted governance by Ralford law, submitted to ongoing Ralford-based supervision, and entered a 20-year relationship deliberately centered in Ralford. This is precisely the *Burger King* fact pattern. Michigan is where Bartholomew's physical operations are; Ralford is where the relationship's legal and supervisory center of gravity lies. B: Personal jurisdiction is determined by the defendant's forum contacts and the reasonableness factors — not simply by where the defendant lives. The due-process reasonableness analysis does consider burden on the defendant, but that factor is outweighed by other factors when the defendant deliberately entered a long-term relationship with the forum-state entity. *Burger King* itself upheld jurisdiction over a Michigan franchisee in Florida (analogous to these facts) on these grounds. Convenience is relevant but not determinative. D: Burger King* itself explicitly held that a choice-of-law clause, standing alone, is not sufficient to establish personal jurisdiction in the named state. It is one relevant factor in the purposeful-availment analysis but cannot independently supply jurisdiction. Jurisdiction in Ralford is proper here because of the totality of contacts, of which the choice-of-law clause is one part — not because of the clause alone.
lb_rule_reference: Under *Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz* (1985), a franchisee who knowingly enters a long-term, heavily regulated franchise relationship with a forum-state company, under a contract governed by the forum state's law and requiring ongoing interaction with the forum-state franchisor, may be subject to specific jurisdiction in the forum state for breach-of-franchise claims — even if the franchisee's own operations are in another state.
