Question: A private contractor sues a federal agency in federal district court. Final judgment is entered against the contractor. Forty-five days after judgment, the contractor files a notice of appeal. No tolling motion was filed. Is the notice untimely solely because it was filed more than thirty days after judgment?
Answer A: Yes, because every federal civil appeal has a thirty-day deadline
Answer B: Yes, because contractors can never appeal against federal agencies
Answer C: No, because a case involving a federal agency generally uses the sixty-day notice-of-appeal period
Answer D: No, because there is no deadline when the government is a party
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Forty-five days is within the ordinary sixty-day period for cases involving the United States or covered federal parties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The deadline changes when the United States or a covered federal party is in the case. B: Contractors may appeal if appellate jurisdiction and timing requirements are met. D: There is still a deadline; it is generally sixty days rather than thirty.
lb_rule_reference: In a federal civil case, the usual notice-of-appeal period is extended to sixty days when the United States, a federal agency, or covered federal officer or employee is a party.
