Question: A subdivision's recorded declaration limited each lot to “one single-family residence.” Years later, the city rezoned the area to permit duplexes. Deborah bought a lot in the subdivision with record notice of the declaration and began building a duplex. Daniel owns another lot in the subdivision and sues to enforce the single-family restriction. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Deborah may build the duplex because zoning permission automatically overrides private restrictions.
Answer B: Daniel may enforce the private restriction if it is otherwise valid, because zoning permission does not eliminate a more restrictive private covenant or servitude.
Answer C: Deborah may build the duplex because the city's rezoning took Daniel's private enforcement rights.
Answer D: Daniel may enforce the restriction only if Deborah personally signed the original declaration.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Public zoning and private land-use restrictions can coexist. Zoning permission means the government allows the use; it does not necessarily eliminate a valid, more restrictive private covenant or equitable servitude. Deborah had record notice of the recorded declaration.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because zoning generally sets public-law limits and does not automatically erase private restrictions. C: C is wrong because the rezoning is not described as condemning or taking Daniel's private enforcement rights. D: D is wrong because successors may be bound by recorded land-use restrictions even if they did not personally sign the original declaration.
lb_rule_reference: Private covenant or servitude coexisting with zoning
