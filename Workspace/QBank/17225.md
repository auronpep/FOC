Question: At midnight, Cole knocks on a homeowner's door and falsely says he is from the gas company and needs to check a leak. The homeowner lets him in. Cole entered intending to steal jewelry from the bedroom. Can the breaking element of common-law burglary be satisfied?
Answer A: Yes, because entry obtained by fraud can be constructive breaking
Answer B: No, because breaking always requires physical damage
Answer C: No, because the homeowner opened the door voluntarily
Answer D: No, because burglary requires theft after entry, not intent at entry
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Cole procured entry by deception and had felonious intent at entry.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Opening a door, or entry by fraud, can satisfy breaking without damage. C: Consent procured by fraud can be constructive breaking. D: Common-law burglary requires intent to commit a felony at entry, not completed theft.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law burglary's breaking element may be satisfied by constructive breaking, such as gaining entry through fraud, if the other elements exist.
