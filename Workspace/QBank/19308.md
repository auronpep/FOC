Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Roles of judge and jury
Question: In a federal assault trial, the prosecution calls Miriam, age seven, who says she saw David strike Peter. Before she testifies, defense counsel asks the court to determine whether Miriam understands the duty to tell the truth and can describe what she saw. The court questions Miriam outside the jury's hearing and finds she can answer simple questions and understands that lying is wrong. Should Miriam be permitted to testify?
Answer A: No, because children under ten are incompetent as a matter of law.
Answer B: No, because the jury, not the judge, must decide witness competency.
Answer C: Yes, if the judge finds she has sufficient capacity and personal knowledge; the jury may then evaluate her credibility.
Answer D: Yes, but only if her testimony is corroborated by an adult witness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court determines witness competency and personal-knowledge foundation. Once the threshold is satisfied, the jury evaluates credibility and weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because there is no automatic federal age bar. B: B is wrong because competency is a preliminary matter for the court. D: D is wrong because corroboration is not required merely because the witness is a child.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a), 601, 602
