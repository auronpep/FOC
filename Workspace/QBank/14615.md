Question: A grand jury returned an indictment charging the defendant with bank robbery, and when he could not make bond he was jailed pending trial. He had received Miranda warnings when arrested and had made no statement at that time. The prosecutor arranged to have an informant placed as the defendant's cellmate and instructed the informant to find out about the bank robbery without asking any direct questions about it. The informant, once in the cell, constantly boasted about the crimes that he had committed. Not to be outdone, the defendant finally declared that he had committed the bank robbery with which he was charged. At the defendant's trial, his attorney moved to exclude any testimony from the informant concerning the defendant's boast. The motion should be
Answer A: A: granted, because the defendant's privilege against self-incrimination was violated.
Answer B: B: granted, because the defendant's right to counsel was violated.
Answer C: C: denied, because the defendant had received Miranda warnings.
Answer D: D: denied, because the defendant was not interrogated by the informant.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. The defendant had the right to counsel during the period of arrest and any attempt by the police to directly elicit incriminating statements, including through the use of an informant while he was in his cell, violates his right to have counsel present during interrogation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The defendant's right against self-incrimination was not violated because he was given Miranda warnings and subsequently made voluntary incriminating statements. C is incorrect. Although the defendant received his Miranda warnings, his right against self-incrimination is not the issue. Rather, the defendant's statements were elicited in violation of his right to counsel and for that reason, they should be suppressed. D is incorrect. By placing the informant in the cell with the defendant, the prosecution created a situation likely to induce the defendant to incriminate himself without counsel present, which amounts to a violation of his constitutional rights.
