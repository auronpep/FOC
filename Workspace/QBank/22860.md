


BARMATRIX Q#: 22860
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A woman, a citizen of State A, sued a corporation in federal district court for $1 million in damages after she was burned by one of the corporation's haircare products. The corporation was incorporated in State B, and had its primary place of business in State C. The woman's husband, also a citizen of State A, joined in the woman's action. He sought $10,000 in damages for loss of consortium. The corporation moved to dismiss the husband's claim, arguing that the federal district court did not have subject matter jurisdiction over the husband's claim. Does the federal district court have subject matter jurisdiction over the husband's claim?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the woman and the husband are married.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the husband's claim is based on the same event as the woman's claim.
Answer C: C: No, because the husband's claim is only for $10,000.
Answer D: D: No, because the husband is a citizen of State A.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Fed. R. Civ. P. 20(a)(1) allows permissive joinder of any party when that party is seeking relief based on the same transaction or occurrence. When parties join through permissive joinder, the federal court has subject matter jurisdiction over the joined claim so long as the court had subject matter jurisdiction over the first claim. Here, the federal court would have subject matter jurisdiction over the woman's claim based on diversity because the parties are citizens of different states and the amount in controversy is over $75,000. Because the federal court has subject matter over the woman's claim, it would also have subject matter jurisdiction over the husband's joined claim since it arose from the same transaction or occurrence as the woman's claim. A is incorrect because there are no rules regarding subject matter jurisdiction based on marriage. C is incorrect because the court would still have jurisdiction over the husband's claim even if was only for $10,000. D is incorrect because the husband's citizenship would not defeat diversity since he is also a citizen of State A, and the corporation is a citizen of State B and State C.
