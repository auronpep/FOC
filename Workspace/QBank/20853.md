MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A parking garage had repeated nighttime robberies in its stairwell. The owner knew the stairwell lock was broken and ignored requests to fix it. A robber entered through the broken door and assaulted a customer in the stairwell. The owner argues that the robber's intentional crime supersedes any negligence. Is the robber's act likely superseding?
Answer A: Yes, because criminal acts always cut off negligence liability
Answer B: Yes, because the owner did not personally assault the customer
Answer C: No, because the assault was within the foreseeable risk created by ignoring the broken stairwell lock
Answer D: No, because criminal acts never matter in proximate cause
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The prior robberies and broken lock made stairwell crime foreseeable. The criminal act is not superseding on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Criminal acts can be foreseeable, especially when the breached duty was aimed at preventing that type of crime. B: The owner can still be a proximate cause if the assault was within the foreseeable risk of the broken lock. D: Criminal acts can matter when extraordinary and outside the risk. This one does not supersede because it was foreseeable.
lb_rule_reference: A criminal act does not supersede when the defendant's negligence made that kind of criminal act foreseeable.
