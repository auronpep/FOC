Question: In a federal contract trial, Isaac testifies for Rebekah that he saw Jacob sign the agreement. On cross-examination, Jacob's lawyer asks Isaac, “You were standing across the room when the signature was made, correct?” Rebekah objects that the question is leading. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because leading questions are improper whenever the question concerns a disputed fact.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because leading questions are ordinarily permitted on cross-examination.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Isaac has first been declared hostile.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Jacob proves that Isaac is lying.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Leading questions are ordinarily allowed on cross-examination. The question is leading, but that is normally proper in this procedural posture.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a disputed fact does not bar leading on cross. C: C is wrong because a witness need not be declared hostile before the cross-examiner may use leading questions. D: D is wrong because leading-question permission does not depend on proving the witness dishonest.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(c) / leading on cross
