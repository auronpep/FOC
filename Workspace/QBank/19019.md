Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police walk across a large rural field located half a mile behind a farmhouse. The field is fenced and marked "No Trespassing," but it is not near the home and is used for crop storage. Officers see illegal plants growing there. Did the officers conduct a Fourth Amendment search by entering the field?
Answer A: Yes, because private ownership and no-trespassing signs always create Fourth Amendment protection
Answer B: No, because the field is an open field outside the home's curtilage
Answer C: Yes, because all land behind a house is curtilage
Answer D: No, but only because police found contraband before touching anything
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The field is far from the home and used for crop storage, not intimate home life. It falls under the open-fields doctrine.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Fourth Amendment protection does not extend to open fields merely because they are privately owned or posted. C: Curtilage depends on proximity, enclosure, use, and steps taken to shield from observation; not all land behind a house qualifies. D: The threshold issue is place protection, not whether officers touched the plants.
lb_rule_reference: Open fields are not protected like the home or curtilage, even if privately owned and posted with no-trespassing signs.
