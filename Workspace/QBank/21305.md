Question: An accountant prepared a financial report for a business owner, knowing it would be shown to a named bank for a specific loan decision. The accountant negligently overstated inventory. The bank relied on the report and made the loan, then suffered financial loss when the borrower defaulted. The bank sues for negligent misrepresentation. What is the accountant's best argument under the economic-loss rule?
Answer A: The bank is barred because all pure financial loss is barred in every tort
Answer B: The bank is barred because it had no contract with the accountant
Answer C: The bank may recover if the accountant owed a limited information-supply duty for this transaction
Answer D: The bank may recover because all foreseeable lenders may rely on any report
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The accountant allegedly knew the report would guide a named bank's specific loan decision, fitting a limited-reliance negligent-misrepresentation theory.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Negligent misrepresentation can be a recognized exception for a known limited class and transaction. B: Liability may extend to a known limited class intended to rely, even without strict privity. D: The exception is limited; it does not extend liability to an open-ended universe of foreseeable users.
lb_rule_reference: Pure economic loss may be recoverable in negligent misrepresentation when a professional supplies information for a known limited class and transaction.
