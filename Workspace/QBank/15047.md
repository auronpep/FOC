Question: A creditor received a valid judgment against a debtor and promptly filed the judgment in the county. Two years later, the debtor purchased land in the county and promptly recorded the warranty deed to the land. Subsequently, the debtor borrowed $30,000 from his aunt, signed a promissory note for that amount, and secured the note with a mortgage on the land. The mortgage was promptly recorded. The aunt failed to make a title search before making the loan. The debtor made no payment to the creditor and defaulted on the mortgage loan from his aunt. A valid judicial foreclosure proceeding was held, in which the creditor, the aunt, and the debtor were named parties. A dispute arose as to which lien had priority. A statute of the jurisdiction provides: “Any judgment properly filed shall, for 10 years from filing, be a lien on the real property then owned or subsequently acquired by any person against whom the judgment is rendered.” A second statute of the jurisdiction provides: “No unrecorded conveyance or mortgage of real property shall be good against subsequent purchasers for value without notice, who shall first record.” Who has the prior lien?
Answer A: A: The aunt, because a judgment lien is subordinate to a mortgage lien.
Answer B: B: The aunt, because she is a mortgagee under a purchase-money mortgage.
Answer C: C: The creditor, because its judgment was filed first.
Answer D: D: The creditor, because the aunt had a duty to make a title search of the property.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. In this race-notice jurisdiction, a bona fide purchaser for value without notice of who records first will be protected. The creditor filed first, giving the aunt constructive notice of the judgment lien.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The judgment lien has priority because it was filed first, before the aunt loaned the money to the debtor, giving her constructive notice. B is incorrect. By the time the debtor borrowed money from the aunt, signed the note, and signed the mortgage, he already owned the property, so the aunt is not a mortgagee under a purchase-money mortgage. D is incorrect. Because the judgment lien was filed in a race-notice jurisdiction, the creditor has priority.
