Question: A recorded easement allowed Leah to cross “the stone bridge at the north edge of Deborah's land.” Deborah later built a wider and safer bridge at the south edge of her land and asked Leah to use the new bridge instead. The new bridge would be equally convenient, but Leah refused to agree to the change. Under the traditional common-law rule, may Deborah unilaterally relocate the easement to the south bridge?
Answer A: No, because a fixed easement location generally cannot be unilaterally relocated by the servient owner without the easement holder's consent or applicable legal authority.
Answer B: Yes, because any safer route automatically replaces the original route.
Answer C: Yes, because the servient owner always controls where an easement crosses the servient land.
Answer D: No, because relocation always terminates the easement and gives Leah fee title to both bridges.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the traditional common-law rule, once an easement location is fixed, the servient owner generally may not unilaterally relocate it, even to an equally convenient route, without the holder's consent or applicable legal authority. The recorded easement fixed the route at the north bridge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because safety or convenience alone does not create unilateral relocation power under the traditional rule. C: C is wrong because servient ownership is burdened by the fixed easement. D: D is wrong because refusing relocation does not transfer fee title to the easement holder.
lb_rule_reference: Fixed easement location / traditional relocation rule
lb_student_diagnostic: The recurring trap is allowing the servient owner to rewrite a fixed location because the substitute seems reasonable.
