Question: Ruth signed and delivered a deed stating, “For one thousand dollars paid, I convey my field to Boaz.” Boaz accepted the deed, but no money was ever paid. Ruth later claimed that the deed was void because the recited consideration was false. Who owns the field?
Answer A: Ruth, because a deed is void whenever the stated consideration is not actually paid.
Answer B: Ruth, because Boaz did not sign a separate contract promising to pay.
Answer C: Boaz, because consideration is not required for a valid delivered deed, even if the recital of payment was inaccurate.
Answer D: Boaz only if he later records the deed before Ruth objects.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is not consideration. Ruth signed and delivered a deed with present words of transfer, and Boaz accepted. Nonpayment of the recited consideration may create a separate claim, but it does not defeat the deed-validity lock, so title passed to Boaz.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a false or unpaid consideration recital does not automatically void a delivered deed. B: B is wrong because the grantee's contract promise is not a deed-validity requirement. D: D is wrong because recording affects notice or priority, not whether the delivered deed passed title between Ruth and Boaz.
