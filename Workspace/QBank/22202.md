


BARMATRIX Q#: 22202
Outline_code: 33040101
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Hearsay and Circumstances of Its Admissibility
Outline: Hearsay rule
Question: The principal of a private high school met with parents who were concerned that male students were being sent to detention more often than female students. During the meeting, one of the parents told the principal of her intention of filing a lawsuit against the school. The next day, the principal asked a secretary to start keeping records of the sex and gender of students sent to detention, although the school had never done so in the past. Ultimately, the parent sued the school for constitutional violations. At trial, the school moved to introduce the detention records into evidence. The records showed that male students were three times more likely to be sent to detention than female students. Are the records admissible?
Answer A: A: No, because the records are inadmissible hearsay.
Answer B: B: No, because the records fail to support the school's defense.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the records are business records.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the records are a past recollection recorded.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The records are hearsay because they are out- of- court statements used for the truth of the matter asserted. B is incorrect because whether the records support the school's defense is irrelevant to the records' admissibility. C is incorrect because the records were prepared in anticipation of litigation and not in the ordinary course of business, so they do not count as business records. D is incorrect because a past recollection recorded is used as a substitute to a witness's lost memory. Here, the records are being used to prove the truth of the matter asserted, not to aid a witness's forgotten testimony.
