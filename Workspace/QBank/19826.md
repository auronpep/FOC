Question: A state allows in-state wineries to ship directly to consumers but prohibits out-of-state wineries from doing the same. The state points only to protecting local wine retailers and invokes the Twenty-first Amendment. What is the best DCC analysis?
Answer A: The Twenty-first Amendment automatically validates all alcohol discrimination
Answer B: The law is suspect because it favors in-state wineries and the stated purpose is local economic protection
Answer C: The law is valid because direct shipping is not commerce
Answer D: The law must be analyzed only under Article IV because wineries have citizenship
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule discriminates against out-of-state wineries and lacks a nonprotectionist justification.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The amendment does not give states blanket power to engage in economic protectionism. C: Direct shipment to consumers is commerce. D: Business entities do not invoke Article IV as citizens; the commerce discrimination is the DCC lane.
lb_rule_reference: The Twenty-first Amendment gives states regulatory authority over alcohol, but it does not create blanket immunity for economic protectionism against out-of-state alcohol interests.
