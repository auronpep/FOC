Question: A public high school journalism class produces a newspaper as part of the curriculum. The faculty adviser reviews articles before publication. The principal removes an article containing private details about students because it is unsuitable for the school-sponsored paper's educational standards. What is the best First Amendment framework?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny applies because any student newspaper is a traditional public forum
Answer B: The school may regulate school-sponsored speech for legitimate pedagogical reasons if the paper is not opened as a public forum
Answer C: No First Amendment rights exist because students are minors
Answer D: The article can be removed only if it is obscene
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Curricular, school-sponsored speech receives a school-control framework rather than ordinary public forum analysis.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A school-sponsored curricular newspaper is not automatically a traditional or designated public forum. C: Students retain speech rights, but the school setting changes the standard. D: School-sponsored speech can be regulated for pedagogical concerns beyond unprotected categories.
lb_rule_reference: A school may exercise editorial control over school-sponsored student speech in a nonpublic curricular setting if its actions are reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns.
