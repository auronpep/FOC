Question: A newsletter reported that a landlord had been cited "five times last month for unsafe fire exits." In fact, the city had issued four citations last month for blocked exits and one warning the prior week for a missing exit sign. The article accurately conveyed that the landlord had serious fire-exit compliance problems. The landlord sues for defamation. What is the landlord's likely result?
Answer A: The landlord wins because one detail in the statement was technically inaccurate
Answer B: The landlord wins because fire-safety statements are defamatory per se
Answer C: The landlord loses because the statement was substantially true in its gist
Answer D: The landlord loses only because the newsletter had no ill will
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The core accusation was serious fire-exit noncompliance. The minor count and timing differences did not materially change the sting.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Substantial truth defeats defamation when the gist or sting is true despite minor inaccuracies. B: Even a defamatory category fails if the statement is substantially true. D: The stronger defense is substantial truth. Ill will is not needed to resolve this fact-gate issue.
lb_rule_reference: Truth or substantial truth is a defense to defamation; minor inaccuracies do not create liability if the gist is true.
