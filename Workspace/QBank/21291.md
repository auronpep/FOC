Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Esther sues Pharaoh Manufacturing for firing her after she reported unsafe machinery. Pharaoh denies knowing about any safety concern before the firing. Esther offers an email from the company's safety office sent to all supervisors two weeks earlier warning that the same machine had been reported as unsafe. Pharaoh objects that the email does not prove the machine was actually unsafe. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the email, because it is not conclusive proof that the machine was unsafe.
Answer B: Admit the email as evidence that Pharaoh's supervisors had notice of safety concerns before the firing.
Answer C: Exclude the email because background evidence is admissible only if every fact in it is disputed.
Answer D: Admit the email only if Esther first proves the machine was defective.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The email is probative of notice, a fact of consequence to Esther's retaliation theory. It need not prove that the machine was actually unsafe to be relevant for that purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the email is offered to show notice, not conclusively prove danger. C: C is wrong because evidence can provide relevant context even if it does not prove an ultimate disputed fact by itself. D: D is wrong because proof of actual defect is not a condition for the notice purpose.
