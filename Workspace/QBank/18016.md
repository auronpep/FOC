Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Joseph owned a store. He signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey the store to Martha of Nazareth.” Joseph knew only one Martha, and she was the person to whom he handed the deed. Martha had once lived in Nazareth but lived in Bethany when the deed was delivered. No other Martha claimed the store. Who owns the store?
Answer A: Martha, because the erroneous residence description does not defeat a grantee otherwise reasonably identified.
Answer B: Joseph, because every descriptive detail in a grantee line must be exactly correct.
Answer C: Joseph, unless a court first reforms the deed to state Bethany.
Answer D: Martha only if she records the deed before Joseph objects.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantee identity. A false or outdated descriptive detail does not defeat the deed when the grantee is otherwise reasonably identified and no competing person fits the description. Martha is the identifiable grantee, so title passes to her.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because exact accuracy in every descriptive detail is not required if identity is reasonably certain. C: C is wrong because reformation is unnecessary when the grantee can already be identified. D: D is wrong because recording is not required for validity between Joseph and Martha.
