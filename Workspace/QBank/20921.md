Question: Deborah granted a utility company a recorded easement in gross to maintain underground cables across Deborah's land. The instrument did not prohibit assignment. The utility company later transferred its cable business and the easement to another utility. The new utility used the same cable route for the same purpose. Deborah argues that an easement in gross can never be transferred. Which is the best answer?
Answer A: Deborah is correct because all easements in gross are personal and nontransferable.
Answer B: Deborah is correct unless the new utility owns a neighboring dominant parcel.
Answer C: Deborah is correct because transfer automatically converts the easement into a revocable license.
Answer D: Deborah is likely incorrect because a commercial easement in gross is generally transferable when the transfer does not exceed the granted scope.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Commercial easements in gross, such as utility easements, are generally transferable unless the grant provides otherwise or the transfer unreasonably expands the burden. The new utility uses the same route for the same cable purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not all in-gross easements are nontransferable; commercial ones commonly are transferable. B: B is wrong because an easement in gross does not require a dominant parcel. C: C is wrong because assignment does not automatically reduce a property interest to a license.
