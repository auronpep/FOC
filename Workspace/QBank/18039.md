Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police illegally searched Troy's backpack and found a stolen passport. At the suppression hearing, the prosecutor argues the passport would inevitably have been discovered because officers might have later obtained a warrant if they had continued investigating. No warrant application was being prepared, no inventory search was scheduled, and no independent search team was looking for the passport. Should inevitable discovery apply?
Answer A: Yes, because officers could have sought a warrant later
Answer B: Yes, because the evidence is reliable and important
Answer C: No, because there was no concrete lawful process that would have discovered the passport
Answer D: No, because inevitable discovery applies only to bodies, not documents
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The government's theory is speculative. Without an active inventory, warrant process, search protocol, or other independent lawful path, inevitable discovery does not apply.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Inevitable discovery requires more than a possible lawful path. The government must show discovery would have occurred through concrete lawful procedures. B: Reliability does not create an inevitable-discovery exception. The doctrine turns on lawful discovery, not evidentiary value. D: The doctrine is not limited to one type of evidence. It fails here because the lawful path is missing.
lb_rule_reference: The government must show that lawful discovery would have occurred, not merely that it could have occurred.
