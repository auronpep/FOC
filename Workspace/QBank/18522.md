Question: Caleb owned a field crossed by a path to a market. For years, Caleb kept a sign near the path stating, “Neighbors may cross during daylight as a courtesy.” Joshua used the path with many other neighbors for 12 years and never told Caleb that he claimed a legal right. The jurisdiction's prescriptive period is 10 years. Does Joshua have an individual prescriptive easement?
Answer A: Yes, because use by many people makes the prescriptive claim stronger.
Answer B: Yes, because the sign proves Caleb knew people were crossing.
Answer C: Yes, because public use automatically becomes a private right after the statutory period.
Answer D: No, because the use was permissive and Joshua did not make an adverse individual claim.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Adverse use is required for prescription. Caleb's sign invited neighbor use as a courtesy, making the use permissive. Joshua also did not communicate any adverse individual claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because widespread use does not cure permission. B: B is wrong because knowledge of permissive use is not knowledge of an adverse claim. C: C is wrong because public or neighborly use does not automatically become an individual property right. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Public permissive use does not create private prescription
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating owner awareness of use as equivalent to owner notice of adverse use.
