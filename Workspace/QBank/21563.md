Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Samuel agreed to prepare a training manual for Naomi for $18,000. Naomi paid $4,000 at signing. Samuel completed and delivered a conforming manual. The reasonable value of comparable work was $25,000, but Naomi refused to pay the remaining balance. In Samuel's contract action, how much may Samuel recover?
Answer A: $14,000.
Answer B: $21,000.
Answer C: $18,000.
Answer D: $25,000.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under common law for nonpayment after full performance. The direct formula is the contract price minus payments already made: $18,000 − $4,000 = $14,000. The limiting gate is offset for amounts already paid, and the result scope is partial because only the unpaid balance remains. The answer does not decide restitution or consequential losses because Samuel has a valid express contract and a completed conforming performance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it uses reasonable value minus the payment, a WAL-REM-007 reasonable-value default that ignores the contract-price measure. C: C is wrong because it ignores the $4,000 payment already made, a wrong-offset formula error. D: D is wrong because it awards reasonable value rather than expectation under the express contract.
lb_rule_reference: Common law expectation damages — contract price minus payments and offsets
