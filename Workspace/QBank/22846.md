Outline_code: 92080204
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motion for new trial
Question: An employee of a corporation sued to recover money allegedly due under the corporation's incentive compensation plan. After the jury returned a special verdict for the employee, the federal district judge granted the corporation's motion for a new trial, finding that the verdict was against the weight of the evidence and contrary to law. The employee appealed, arguing that there was sufficient evidence to support the jury's verdict and that a new trial was not appropriate under the circumstances. Should the court grant the employee's appeal?
Answer A: A: Yes, because a new trial is inappropriate without a finding of fraud.
Answer B: B: Yes, because a special verdict is immediately appealable.
Answer C: C: No, because the court granted a new trial.
Answer D: D: No, because there was no indication of clear error.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: In the federal system, an order for a new trial is not appealable because it is not a final judgment under 28 U.S.C. §1291. If a party wants to appeal a new trial order, he or she must wait until the new trial has been completed. At that point, he or she can appeal the new trial's final judgment and raise the new trial order as an issue. A is incorrect because a new trial can be granted even if there is no finding of fraud. B is incorrect because a special verdict simply refers to a verdict that requires an answer to a specific question. D is incorrect because the issue has to do with the granting of the new trial, not anything involving error.
