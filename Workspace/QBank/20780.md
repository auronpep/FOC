Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police arrest a suspect just inside his front door. They have no information that anyone else is inside. After handcuffing him, officers walk upstairs, open a closed closet in a bedroom, and find stolen electronics. Was the upstairs closet search valid as a protective sweep?
Answer A: Yes, because any in-home arrest permits a search of every room for officer safety
Answer B: No, because officers lacked articulable facts that another dangerous person was upstairs
Answer C: Yes, because closets may contain weapons
Answer D: No, because police may never look in closets during a protective sweep
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The search went beyond immediately adjoining spaces and was unsupported by facts suggesting another person was present.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A broader sweep requires articulable facts suggesting a dangerous person is present. C: Protective sweeps are for people who may pose danger, not general weapons searches. D: Officers may check spaces where a person could hide when sweep requirements are met.
lb_rule_reference: A protective sweep beyond immediately adjoining spaces requires articulable facts supporting reasonable belief that a dangerous person may be present.
