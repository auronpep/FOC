Question: Peter promised Naomi that he would pay Naomi's son John $15,000 as part of Peter's purchase of Naomi's store. Peter refused to pay. John filed suit against Peter. After the suit was filed, Peter and Naomi agreed that Peter would instead pay John $1,000. What amount may John pursue?
Answer A: Only $1,000, because Peter and Naomi are the only contracting parties.
Answer B: $15,000, because John's rights vested when he filed suit before the attempted reduction.
Answer C: Nothing, because John gave no consideration.
Answer D: Only $1,000, unless Naomi assigned her contract rights to John.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: John was an intended beneficiary of Peter's payment promise. His rights vested when he filed suit before Peter and Naomi attempted to reduce the benefit. The later reduction cannot cut off the vested right without John's consent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because contracting parties cannot modify vested beneficiary rights without the beneficiary's consent. C: C is wrong because intended beneficiaries do not need to provide consideration. D: D is wrong because John is not relying on assignment.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — vested rights after suit
