Question: In a federal fraud trial, Miriam is called to testify that she saw Daniel sign a paper in a conference room. The prosecution offers the testimony only to prove that Daniel was present and signed a document that day, not to prove the paper's terms. Daniel objects that the paper itself has not been produced. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because the original paper is required whenever a writing is mentioned.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because a witness may never testify about signing unless the signed writing is admitted.
Answer C: Admit the testimony only if Miriam can recite the exact terms of the paper.
Answer D: Admit the testimony because Miriam is testifying from personal observation of the signing, not to prove the writing's contents.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Miriam may testify from personal observation that Daniel was present and signed a paper. The original-writing rule applies when a party seeks to prove the contents of a writing. Here the offered fact is the event of signing and Daniel's presence, not the document's terms.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because merely mentioning a writing does not trigger the original-writing rule. B: B is wrong because eyewitness testimony can prove an observed act. C: C is wrong because the proponent is not offering Miriam to prove the terms of the paper.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 and 1002 / observed event versus contents of writing
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the original-writing false positive and separates perceived event from writing contents.
