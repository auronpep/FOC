Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: An Illinois blogger wrote a post criticizing a consultant's work on a project in Illinois. The post was written in Illinois, concerned Illinois events, and was aimed at the blogger's local industry readers. The consultant happened to live in Maine and claimed the post damaged his reputation there. The blogger had no Maine readers, advertisers, or contacts beyond knowing the consultant's home address. Does Maine have specific jurisdiction over the blogger?
Answer A: Yes, because the blogger knew the consultant lived in Maine and would feel harm there
Answer B: Yes, because internet publication creates jurisdiction in every state
Answer C: No, because the facts show Illinois-focused conduct and harm merely felt by a Maine resident
Answer D: No, because defamation claims are exempt from personal-jurisdiction rules
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The defendant's conduct, subject matter, and audience were Illinois-focused. Maine appears only through the plaintiff's residence and claimed harm.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Knowledge of residence and felt harm are not enough when the conduct was not expressly aimed at Maine. B: Accessibility alone is not forum targeting. D: Defamation claims can support PJ when the defendant expressly aims defamatory conduct at the forum.
lb_rule_reference: The forum must be the focal point of the defendant's conduct or targeting; harm suffered in the forum alone is insufficient.
