Question: Derek knowingly joins a gang crew planning armed robberies. During a robbery he agreed to help commit, the leader points a gun at Derek and orders him to hold the door closed while others flee. Derek complies and later claims duress to the robbery charge. What is the best analysis?
Answer A: Duress succeeds because the leader threatened Derek with a gun
Answer B: Duress succeeds because Derek did not plan to hold the door
Answer C: Duress likely fails because Derek recklessly placed himself in the coercive criminal situation
Answer D: Duress fails because threats by accomplices never count as human threats
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Derek knowingly joined a violent criminal enterprise. A defendant who recklessly creates or enters a situation where coercion is likely cannot later use duress to escape liability for resulting crimes.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Duress is limited when the defendant voluntarily or recklessly entered a criminal setting where coercion was foreseeable. B: He voluntarily joined an armed robbery crew, making violent coercion foreseeable. The created-risk limitation can defeat duress. D: Threats by accomplices are human threats. The limiting fact is Derek's voluntary participation in the dangerous criminal plan.
lb_rule_reference: Duress is unavailable when the defendant recklessly or intentionally placed himself in a situation where coercion was likely
