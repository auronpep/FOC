Question: A gym salesperson orally told a customer that a membership could be canceled at any time. The one-page contract, printed in large type above the signature line, stated "noncancelable twelve-month membership." The customer signed without reading and later sued for fraud based only on the oral statement. What is the gym's strongest defense?
Answer A: The customer may not have justifiably relied on a statement plainly contradicted by the obvious written term
Answer B: Fraud never applies to consumer contracts
Answer C: The oral statement was automatically incorporated into the writing
Answer D: The customer wins because reliance is presumed whenever a salesperson speaks
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Justifiable reliance is a separate fraud element.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Fraud can apply to contracts when the elements are met. C: The issue is tort reliance, not automatic incorporation. D: Reliance must be shown and must be justifiable.
lb_rule_reference: Fraud requires justifiable reliance, and reliance is usually not justified when the alleged oral statement is plainly contradicted by obvious written terms.
