Question: Martha sues a grocery store owned by Amos after slipping in an aisle. Martha offers a written warning that an employee allegedly handed to Amos two hours before the fall, stating, “Water is leaking near the produce.” Martha offers the writing only to prove that Amos had notice of a possible hazard. Amos denies receiving it. Martha presents testimony from Leah that she saw the employee place the warning on Amos's desk. How should the court handle the evidence?
Answer A: Exclude the warning because Amos denies receiving it.
Answer B: Admit the warning if the evidence is sufficient for the jury to find that Amos received it, and limit its use to notice if properly requested.
Answer C: Admit the warning as conclusive proof that water was actually leaking.
Answer D: Exclude the warning unless Martha proves receipt beyond a reasonable doubt.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The evidence is relevant to notice only if Amos received the warning. The judge screens for sufficient evidence to support that finding; the jury decides whether Amos received it. Because the writing is offered for notice rather than the truth of the leak, the court can limit its use to that purpose on proper request.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because denial of receipt does not automatically bar conditionally relevant evidence. C: C is wrong because the offered purpose is notice, not proof that water was actually leaking. D: D is wrong because the preliminary threshold is not proof beyond a reasonable doubt.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b), 105 / conditional relevance and limited purpose
