Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A commercial dough mixer was designed with an exposed pinch point near the bowl rim. The manufacturer knew that bakers commonly used a rubber scraper to clear dough from the rim while the mixer was running, even though the manual said to stop the machine first. A fixed guard would have blocked fingers while still allowing scraping and would have added minimal cost. A baker used a scraper as the manufacturer knew bakers commonly did, caught a hand in the pinch point, and was injured. The baker sues the manufacturer under strict products liability for a design defect. Is the baker likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim for a design defect?
Answer A: No, because the baker did not follow the manual's instruction to stop the mixer first
Answer B: Yes, because the linewide design exposed users to a foreseeable pinch-point risk that a feasible guard could reduce
Answer C: No, because design defect requires that this particular mixer differ from other mixers in the same line
Answer D: Yes, because every warning violation by a product user proves the design was defective
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The hazard was built into every unit, the misuse was known and foreseeable, and a practical guard could reduce the risk without destroying utility. That supports design defect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Foreseeable misuse is part of design-defect analysis. The manufacturer knew bakers commonly scraped during operation, and a feasible guard could reduce that foreseeable risk. C: That comparison belongs to manufacturing defect. Design defect challenges the intended design shared by the whole line. D: A warning violation does not automatically prove a design defect. The claim is strong because the risk was foreseeable and guardable, not because warnings are irrelevant in every case.
lb_rule_reference: Foreseeable misuse may be part of design-defect analysis; it does not automatically bar a claim when a reasonable design could reduce the foreseeable risk.
