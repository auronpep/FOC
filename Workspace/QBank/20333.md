Question: Leah orally agrees to sell Rachel 400 copper cups. Rachel signs a note stating, “I will buy 400 copper cups from Leah.” The note does not state the price, delivery date, or payment terms. Rachel later refuses to buy, and Leah sues to enforce the contract for 400 cups at a reasonable price. What is the best Statute of Frauds analysis?
Answer A: The writing is insufficient because it omits price, delivery, and payment terms.
Answer B: The writing is insufficient unless Rachel also signed a formal purchase order.
Answer C: The writing may satisfy the UCC Statute of Frauds because it indicates a sales contract and states the quantity, even though other terms are omitted.
Answer D: The writing is sufficient only if Leah also signed it.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under §2-201, a writing is not insufficient because it omits or incorrectly states a term, but the contract is not enforceable beyond the quantity shown. Rachel's signed note indicates a sales contract and shows 400 cups.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the UCC does not demand all terms. B: B is wrong because a formal purchase order is unnecessary if the memorandum is otherwise sufficient. D: D is wrong because the signature of the party against whom enforcement is sought—Rachel—is enough for Leah's action against Rachel.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(1)
