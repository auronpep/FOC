Question: Moses contracted with Ruth to buy a flock. Ruth promised Moses that she would deliver one lamb from the flock directly to Moses's niece, Abigail, as part of the price Moses negotiated. Ruth later refused to deliver the lamb. Abigail sued Ruth. Ruth argued that Abigail could not recover because Moses never assigned Abigail any rights under the contract. What is the best response?
Answer A: Ruth is correct because a nonparty can enforce a contract only through assignment.
Answer B: Ruth is correct because Abigail was merely aware of the contract.
Answer C: Ruth is incorrect if the contract was intended to give Abigail direct performance as a third-party beneficiary.
Answer D: Ruth is incorrect only if Abigail paid Moses for the assignment.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Abigail's likely route is intended-beneficiary status, not assignment. If Moses and Ruth intended direct delivery to Abigail as part of their bargain, Abigail can enforce even though Moses did not assign his rights.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because assignment is not the only nonparty enforcement route. B: B is wrong because the key fact is direct promised performance, not mere awareness. D: D is wrong because assignment consideration is not required for the beneficiary route.
