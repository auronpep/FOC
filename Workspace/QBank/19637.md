Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A delivery van's defective wheel assembly breaks while the van is being driven normally. The van swerves onto a sidewalk and injures a pedestrian who never bought, used, or touched the van. The pedestrian sues the commercial manufacturer in strict products liability. What is the best response to the manufacturer's argument that the pedestrian lacks privity?
Answer A: The pedestrian had no contractual privity with the manufacturer
Answer B: The pedestrian was not the van's buyer
Answer C: Strict products liability can extend to a foreseeable bystander injured by the defect
Answer D: The manufacturer may contest whether the wheel assembly was actually defective
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The pedestrian's lack of purchase or use does not bar a claim if the defect caused physical injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Lack of privity is not a strong strict-products defense for a foreseeable bystander. B: Foreseeable bystanders can be protected. D: as the weakest defense. Contesting defect is legitimate, unlike relying on privity alone.
lb_rule_reference: Strict products liability can protect foreseeable users and bystanders injured by a defective product; privity is not required.
