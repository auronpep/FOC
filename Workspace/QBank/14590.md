Question: A store owner whose jewelry store had recently been robbed was shown by a police detective a photograph of the defendant, who previously had committed other similar crimes. The store owner examined the photograph and then asked the detective whether the police believed that the man pictured was the robber. After the detective said, “We're pretty sure,” the store owner stated that the man in the photograph was the one who had robbed her. The defendant was indicted for the robbery. His counsel moved to suppress any trial testimony by the store owner identifying the defendant as the robber. Should the court grant the motion and suppress the store owner's trial testimony identifying the defendant as the robber?
Answer A: A: No, because suppression of in-court testimony is not a proper remedy, even though the out-of- court identification was improper.
Answer B: B: No, because the out-of-court identification was not improper.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the improper out-of-court identification has necessarily tainted any in- court identification.
Answer D: D: Yes, unless the prosecution demonstrates that the in-court identification is reliable.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. An out-of-court identification procedure is improper if it is unnecessarily suggestive, which this procedure (involving just one photograph and the detective's leading statement) plainly was. However, the prosecution has the opportunity to demonstrate the reliability of the in-court identification and still succeed in getting the testimony admitted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. This out-of-court identification was in fact unnecessarily suggestive, and the proper remedy for such a violation is suppression of the in-court testimony, which renders this answer incorrect. However, suppression of in- court testimony would not be required if the eyewitness's identification were shown to be reliable. B is incorrect. As stated above, the out-of-court identification was improper because it was unduly suggestive for the police to use only one photograph and indicate that they believed this was the correct suspect. C is incorrect. It may be possible to establish the reliability of the eyewitness's testimony without regard to the tainted out-of-court identification if the prosecution can establish the reliability of the in-court testimony.
