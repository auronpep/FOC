Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Burglary
Question: An officer arrested Iris for burglary and, while walking her to the patrol car, asked without warnings, "Do you know why we are here?" Iris said, "Because of the break-in." At the station, a different detective gave full Miranda warnings. Iris signed a waiver and gave a detailed confession. The trial court finds the first question was an inadvertent mistake, not a deliberate two-step strategy. Must the later warned confession be suppressed because of the earlier unwarned statement?
Answer A: Yes, because any unwarned first statement automatically taints all later statements
Answer B: Yes, because the two-step rule always applies whenever police question first and warn later
Answer C: No, because the first Miranda failure was inadvertent and the later confession followed valid warnings and waiver
Answer D: No, because the first unwarned statement was admissible in the case-in-chief
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The first statement was voluntary and the violation was not deliberate. The later full warnings, written waiver, and detailed station confession support admissibility of the second statement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A voluntary unwarned statement does not automatically taint a later warned and waived confession when the first violation was inadvertent. B: The stricter two-step rule targets deliberate tactics designed to undermine Miranda. The court found an inadvertent mistake. D: The unwarned custodial statement itself may be excluded from the case-in-chief. The question is whether it taints the later warned confession.
lb_rule_reference: A voluntary unwarned statement caused by an inadvertent Miranda failure does not automatically require suppression of a later properly warned and waived confession.
