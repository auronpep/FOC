Question: A state supreme court affirms a conviction. Its opinion says the defendant's federal search claim fails and also says a state evidence rule independently bars relief because the defendant did not object at trial. The state procedural rule is firmly established and regularly followed. The defendant petitions the U.S. Supreme Court, focusing on the federal search issue. What is the strongest obstacle to U.S. Supreme Court review?
Answer A: The Court may not review because the judgment rests on an adequate and independent state procedural ground
Answer B: The Court may review because the opinion discusses a federal constitutional issue
Answer C: The Court may review because state procedural rules can never block federal constitutional review
Answer D: The Court may not review only if the state court expressly says the federal claim is frivolous
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The state procedural default independently supports the judgment and is adequate because it is firmly established and regularly followed. The federal issue does not create review when the state ground is sufficient.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A federal issue is not enough if an adequate and independent state ground supports the judgment. C: Adequate and independent state procedural grounds can bar Supreme Court review of a state judgment. D: The review bar turns on the independent and adequate state ground, not whether the federal claim was labeled frivolous.
lb_rule_reference: The U.S. Supreme Court cannot review a state judgment resting on an adequate and independent state ground; when independence is unclear, the Court may look to whether the state judgment fairly appears to rest on federal law.
