Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Rebecca sues a restaurant after slipping on a wet floor at noon. She offers a photograph of a wet restaurant floor. The photograph was found online, and Rebecca offers no testimony about who took it, when it was taken, where in the restaurant it was taken, or whether it depicts the floor at issue. The restaurant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the photograph because it shows a wet floor and Rebecca claims she slipped on a wet floor.
Answer B: Admit the photograph because internet photographs are self-authenticating.
Answer C: Exclude the photograph only if the restaurant proves it was staged.
Answer D: Exclude the photograph because Rebecca has not provided enough evidence that it depicts the relevant place or condition.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A photograph must be connected to what the proponent claims it depicts. Without evidence of time, place, source, or relation to the accident area, the photograph lacks a foundation making it probative of the disputed condition.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because generic similarity is not enough. B: B is wrong because an online image is not automatically authenticated or connected to the case. C: C is wrong because the restaurant need not prove staging before Rebecca lays a foundation.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 901
lb_student_diagnostic: Generic visual similarity treated as case-specific foundation.
