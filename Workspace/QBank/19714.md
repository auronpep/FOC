Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: In a federal robbery trial, Miriam identifies Caleb as the robber. Caleb offers evidence that Miriam was looking down at her phone when the robber first entered the store. The prosecutor objects that the phone evidence does not prove Caleb was not the robber. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because it tends to make Miriam's opportunity to perceive the robber less reliable.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because only facts proving Caleb's innocence are relevant.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence because credibility facts are never facts of consequence.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if Miriam admits she did not see the robber enter.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Evidence that a witness was distracted can be relevant to perception and therefore to credibility. It need not prove the defendant's innocence by itself.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because evidence may be relevant to credibility without directly proving innocence. C: C is wrong because witness perception and credibility may affect consequential factfinding. D: D is wrong because the evidence may be relevant even if Miriam disputes its significance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / perception credibility
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests relevance through witness perception rather than direct merits proof.
