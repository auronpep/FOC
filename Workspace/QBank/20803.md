Question: Jeremiah told his nephew Eliab, “If you finish your school term, I will help you buy a modest used cart for work.” Eliab immediately quit school, spent $12,000 on luxury cart accessories, and then asked Jeremiah to pay. Jeremiah refused. If Eliab sues under promissory estoppel, what is the best result?
Answer A: Eliab recovers $12,000 because any spending after a promise is reliance.
Answer B: Eliab recovers the value of a modest used cart because that was the promised item.
Answer C: Eliab likely recovers nothing for the accessories because the reliance was not reasonable or consistent with the promise's terms.
Answer D: Eliab recovers expectation damages plus the accessory costs.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Promissory estoppel requires reasonable and foreseeable reliance, and the remedy is limited as justice requires. Eliab's quitting school and buying luxury accessories was not the reliance Jeremiah invited; it was inconsistent with the condition and scope of the promise.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reliance must be reasonable and induced by the promise. B: B is wrong because Eliab did not satisfy the condition of finishing school. D: D is wrong because it stacks expectation and unreasonable reliance.
lb_rule_reference: Unreasonable reliance limitation
