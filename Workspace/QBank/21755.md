Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: Voters sue in federal court alleging that a state legislative map favors one political party too heavily. They ask the court to set a constitutional limit on how much partisan advantage is permissible, but they do not allege racial discrimination or one-person-one-vote defects. What is the likely federal justiciability result?
Answer A: The claim is likely nonjusticiable because federal courts lack manageable standards for partisan-gerrymandering claims
Answer B: The claim must receive strict scrutiny because voting is fundamental
Answer C: The claim is moot because maps are political
Answer D: The claim is automatically justiciable because all election disputes are reviewable
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal partisan-gerrymandering claims are treated as political questions because courts lack a manageable constitutional standard for the degree of permissible partisanship.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The threshold issue is justiciability of partisan-gerrymandering claims, not ordinary scrutiny. C: The issue is political question, not a live controversy that ended. D: Some election claims are justiciable, but federal partisan-gerrymandering claims face the no-manageable-standards bar.
lb_rule_reference: Federal partisan-gerrymandering claims are treated as nonjusticiable political questions because there are no judicially manageable standards for deciding how much partisanship is too much.
