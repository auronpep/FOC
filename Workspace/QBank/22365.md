Outline_code: 75120000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Other Considerations
Question: During an argument, a husband hit his wife in the head with a frying pan. The wife picked up a knife and stabbed him through the heart, killing him instantly. The wife was charged with aggravated murder and assigned a defense attorney. The woman pleaded self- defense. Under state law, the wife had the burden of proving by a preponderance of the evidence that she acted in self- defense. The wife argued that the state law violated her due process rights. Did the state law violate her due process rights?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the state had to prove every element of the crime beyond a reasonable doubt.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the woman had to prove her defense by a preponderance of the evidence.
Answer C: C: No, because the state could place the burden of proof on the defendant for an affirmative defense.
Answer D: D: No, because the woman was not representing herself pro se.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Although the Due Process Clause requires that states prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt in criminal cases, a state may require a defendant to have the burden of proof for an affirmative defense. Martin v. Ohio, 480 U.S. 228 (1987). Because self- defense is an affirmative defense, the state could impose the burden of proof on defendants without violating the Due Process Clause. A is incorrect because self- defense is an affirmative defense. B is incorrect because the state could require the wife to prove her affirmative defense by a preponderance of the evidence. D is incorrect because the burden of proof does not depend on whether a defendant is represented or not.
