


BARMATRIX Q#: 22127
Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: A landlord signed a written lease agreement with a professor to lease a home for four years at $500 per month. The lease expressly allowed the tenant to sublet and assign the lease. After one year of paying rent every month, the professor won a twoyear writing retreat in a foreign county. He moved out and sublet the home to a dean for two years for $500 per month. The dean moved in for a year but never paid any rent. After the year was up, the dean abandoned the home and left it vacant. When the professor returned, he lived in the home for the final year of the term but did not pay any rent. The landlord sued both the professor and the dean for $18,000, or three years of unpaid rent. Who is liable for the unpaid rent?
Answer A: A: The dean is liable for $18,000 in rent.
Answer B: B: The professor is liable for $6,000 in rent, the dean for $12,000 in rent.
Answer C: C: The professor is liable for $12,000 in rent, the dean for $6,000 in rent.
Answer D: D: The professor is liable for $18,000 in rent.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: If a tenant transfers less than all of his or her rights in a leased property, a sublease occurs. Importantly, unless there is an express assumption of the duty to pay rent, the landlord cannot sue the subtenant directly for any unpaid rent because the subtenant is not in privity of estate or privity of contract with the landlord. Here, the professor subleased two years of the remaining three years of the lease, so he transferred less than all of his rights in the property. Consequently, the professor, who signed the original lease, is liable for all of the unpaid rent and the landlord cannot recover from the dean. Consequently, A, B, and C are incorrect.
