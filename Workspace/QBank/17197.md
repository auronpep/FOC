Question: A city ordinance makes it unlawful to "say anything annoying, disrespectful, or inappropriate" within 200 feet of city hall. Police officers are instructed to decide case by case what speech crosses the line. What is the strongest constitutional objection?
Answer A: Vagueness, because the ordinance lacks fair notice and gives officials broad discretion
Answer B: No objection, because all disrespectful speech near city hall is unprotected
Answer C: Rational basis, because city hall security is a legitimate interest
Answer D: The law is valid because police discretion is needed for speech cases
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Terms like annoying and inappropriate give little guidance and invite arbitrary enforcement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Offensive or disrespectful speech is not categorically unprotected. C: Speech restrictions need clear standards and appropriate scrutiny. D: Unbounded discretion is a vagueness problem, especially for speech.
lb_rule_reference: A speech regulation is void for vagueness if people of ordinary intelligence cannot tell what is prohibited or if it authorizes arbitrary enforcement.
