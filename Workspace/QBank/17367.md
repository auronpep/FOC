MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Detectives arrested Cole in his home without a warrant, consent, exigency, or probable cause. Thirty minutes later at the station, they gave proper Miranda warnings. Cole waived and confessed. No intervening evidence, warrant, or voluntary return occurred between the arrest and confession. Cole moves to suppress the confession. Should the confession be suppressed?
Answer A: Yes, because the confession was the direct fruit of an unlawful arrest and no attenuation fact broke the causal chain
Answer B: Yes, because any statement made after arrest is inadmissible unless counsel is present
Answer C: No, because Miranda warnings always cure the taint of an illegal arrest
Answer D: No, because unlawful arrests never affect later statements
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Proper warnings matter, but they do not automatically purge the taint of an illegal arrest. The confession followed soon after the arrest, at the station, with no intervening event. Suppression is appropriate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Counsel presence is not required for every post-arrest statement. The problem here is the Fourth Amendment taint, not a universal counsel rule. C: Warnings are one factor, not an automatic attenuation rule. The causal chain from illegal arrest to confession still matters. D: A statement can be fruit of an unlawful arrest when the arrest caused the statement and no exception applies.
lb_rule_reference: A confession following an illegal arrest is suppressible unless the causal taint is attenuated; warnings alone do not necessarily break the chain.
