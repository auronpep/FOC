Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Miriam is charged with stabbing Leah. The medical examiner testifies that the wound could have been made by a narrow kitchen knife. The prosecutor offers a narrow kitchen knife found in Miriam's kitchen. Miriam objects that many knives could have made the wound and that no blood was found on this knife. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the knife because the prosecutor must prove it was the actual weapon before it can be relevant.
Answer B: Admit the knife because its consistency with the wound gives it some probative value; the absence of blood and existence of other knives affect weight.
Answer C: Exclude the knife because evidence is relevant only if it is more likely than not the murder weapon.
Answer D: Admit the knife only if a witness saw Miriam use that exact knife.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The knife's consistency with the wound gives it some tendency to connect Miriam to the stabbing. The fact that many knives could have caused the wound weakens the evidence, but does not make it irrelevant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevance does not require conclusive identification as the weapon. C: C is wrong because Rule 401 does not require a more-likely-than-not showing for each item of evidence. D: D is wrong because direct eyewitness proof is not required for relevance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401; FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Student demands weapon certainty instead of recognizing weak probative value.
