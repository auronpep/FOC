Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal fraud trial, Esther testifies for the plaintiff. On cross-examination, the defendant's lawyer asks, “Didn't you tell Ezra last month that the plaintiff altered the invoice?” Esther objects that the lawyer has not shown her the text message. The plaintiff's lawyer requests to see the message before questioning continues. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Require the defendant's lawyer to show the message to Esther before asking about it.
Answer B: Allow the question without first showing the message to Esther, but require the lawyer to show or disclose its contents to the plaintiff's lawyer on request.
Answer C: Allow the question and refuse the plaintiff's lawyer's request unless Esther denies sending the message.
Answer D: Exclude all questions about the message unless the message is first admitted as an exhibit.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When examining a witness about a prior statement, a party need not show the statement to the witness or disclose its contents to the witness first. But on request, the party must show it or disclose its contents to an adverse party's attorney.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 613(a) does not require witness-first disclosure. C: C is wrong because adverse counsel is entitled to see or hear the statement's contents on request. D: D is wrong because a prior statement can be used in examination for impeachment without first being admitted as a substantive exhibit.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 613(a) / no witness-first disclosure requirement
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the disclosure mechanics for prior-statement examination.
