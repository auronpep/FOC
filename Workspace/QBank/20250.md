Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Burglary
Question: After valid Miranda warnings and waiver, police questioned Carla for forty minutes about a burglary. They falsely said that a neighbor's camera showed Carla entering the house. They made no threats or promises and allowed breaks. Carla then confessed. The camera statement was false. Does the false statement about the camera automatically make Carla's confession involuntary?
Answer A: Yes, because any police lie during interrogation violates due process
Answer B: Yes, because Miranda waivers are invalid whenever police later use deception
Answer C: No, because deception alone did not overbear Carla's will under the totality of circumstances
Answer D: No, because police deception can never be considered in voluntariness analysis
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The questioning was limited, warnings and waiver were valid, no threats or promises were used, and breaks were allowed. The false evidence claim alone does not automatically establish involuntariness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Deception is a factor, but not every lie overbears the will. A false statement about evidence, without threats, promises, or extreme pressure, does not automatically make a confession involuntary. B: A valid waiver is not automatically undone by ordinary evidence deception. Suppression requires coercion sufficient to overbear the will. D: Deception is relevant to the totality. It simply is not dispositive on these facts.
lb_rule_reference: Deception is one factor in the voluntariness totality, but a noncoercive lie about evidence does not itself overbear the suspect's will.
