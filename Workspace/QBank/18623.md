Outline: Duty to mitigate
Question: Joseph had a one-year contract to work as a sales manager. When his employer wrongfully discharged him, $90,000 in salary remained unpaid. Joseph diligently searched for comparable management work, spent $1,200 in reasonable job-search expenses, and earned $30,000 from comparable interim management jobs during the remaining contract period. What is Joseph's recovery?
Answer A: $61,200.
Answer B: $90,000.
Answer C: $60,000.
Answer D: $91,200.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under employment/common-law rules. The breach posture is wrongful discharge, and the loss category is unpaid wages reduced by actual comparable earnings, with reasonable mitigation expenses added. The formula is $90,000 remaining salary − $30,000 actual comparable earnings + $1,200 reasonable search expenses = $61,200. The limiting gate is mitigation/offset, and the result scope is offset recovery. The answer does not include punitive or emotional-distress damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it ignores actual comparable earnings. C: C is wrong because it omits reasonable search expenses incurred in mitigation. D: D is wrong because it stacks the search expense onto the full salary while ignoring the earnings offset, a WAL-REM-008 overcompensation error.
lb_rule_reference: Common law employment damages — actual comparable earnings offset lost wages, with reasonable search expenses added
