Outline: Mode and order of witnesses
Question: In a federal negligence trial, Moses completes his testimony and is excused. After a later exhibit is admitted, the plaintiff asks to recall Moses briefly to identify where he was standing on a diagram. The defendant argues that once a witness is excused, recall is never permitted. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny recall because an excused witness can never testify again in the same trial.
Answer B: Permit recall only if both parties consent.
Answer C: Permit or deny recall in the court's discretion as part of controlling the order and mode of proof.
Answer D: Permit recall only if Moses's earlier testimony was false.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court has reasonable control over the order and mode of presenting witnesses. Brief recall to clarify an exhibit is within the court's discretion, subject to fairness and efficiency.
