Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A portable heater can overheat if placed within two feet of curtains. The only warning appears in tiny print under a glued package flap that consumers do not open before use. The front of the box and heater have no warning. A consumer places the heater near curtains, and a fire results. What is the best failure-to-warn analysis?
Answer A: The warning is automatically adequate because some warning existed somewhere on the package
Answer B: The warning may be inadequate because it was hidden and unlikely to reach users before use
Answer C: The claim fails because fire risk is always obvious for every heater placement
Answer D: The case must be treated only as express warranty because the warning was printed on a box
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A warning buried under a glued flap may fail to communicate a nonobvious fire risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Adequacy includes whether the warning is reasonably visible and likely to communicate the risk. C: The specific clearance risk may require an adequate warning. D: Printed package warnings can be part of a failure-to-warn products claim.
lb_rule_reference: A warning must be reasonably adequate in content and presentation; a hidden or inconspicuous warning may be inadequate.
