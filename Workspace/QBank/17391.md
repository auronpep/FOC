Subtopic: Rights in Land
Outline: Scope
Question: A deed granted Caleb, as owner of Farm Lot, “a right of way over Leah's lane for access to Farm Lot.” Caleb later lawfully divided Farm Lot into two residential parcels and sold one to Hannah. Both new parcels are entirely within the original Farm Lot. The two homes now use the same lane, but the increase in traffic is modest and does not interfere with Leah's use. May Leah bar Hannah from using the lane solely because Hannah owns only part of the original dominant estate?
Answer A: Yes, because an easement appurtenant disappears whenever the dominant estate is subdivided.
Answer B: Yes, because an appurtenant easement may be used only by the first grantee named in the original deed.
Answer C: No, because an appurtenant easement may generally benefit subdivided portions of the dominant estate if the use does not unreasonably increase the burden.
Answer D: No, because subdivision allows the new owners to use the easement to reach any other land they later acquire.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An appurtenant easement generally passes with and may be apportioned among subdivided parts of the dominant estate, so long as the use remains tied to the original dominant land and does not unreasonably burden the servient estate. Hannah's parcel is within the original Farm Lot, and the increase is modest.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because subdivision does not automatically extinguish an appurtenant easement. B: B is wrong because the easement benefits the dominant land, not only the first named grantee. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because the easement cannot be extended to land outside the original dominant estate.
lb_rule_reference: Apportionment of appurtenant easement after subdivision
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating subdivision as either automatic termination or unlimited expansion.
