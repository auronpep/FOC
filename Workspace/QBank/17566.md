Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers arrested a driver for driving with a suspended license — a non-evidence-generating offense. After handcuffing the driver and placing him in the patrol car, officers searched the passenger compartment of his car. In the back seat they found a bag containing cocaine. The driver moves to suppress. Should the court suppress the cocaine found in the passenger compartment?
Answer A: No, because SITA has always authorized a full search of the passenger compartment incident to any lawful arrest of the driver
Answer B: No, because the automobile exception gives officers probable cause to search any vehicle they lawfully stop
Answer C: Yes, because under Gant, vehicle SITA requires either an unsecured arrestee within reach of the compartment or reason to believe the vehicle contains evidence of the offense of arrest; neither condition is met
Answer D: Yes, because once a suspect is in custody, any search of his possessions requires a warrant
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Arizona v. Gant holds that the passenger compartment search incident to arrest is lawful only when (1) the arrestee is unsecured and within reaching distance at the time of the search, or (2) there is reason to believe the vehicle contains evidence of the offense of arrest. Here, the driver was handcuffed and secured in the patrol car — he posed no threat to reach the compartment. Driving with a suspended license is a status offense that generates no physical evidence in the vehicle. Neither Gant prong is satisfied. The search was warrantless, no valid exception applies, and the cocaine must be suppressed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Arizona v. Gant narrowed Belton. Post-Gant, SITA of a vehicle is permitted only if the arrestee is unsecured and within reaching distance of the passenger compartment at the time of the search, or if it is reasonable to believe the vehicle contains evidence of the offense of arrest. Neither condition is met here: the driver was handcuffed in a patrol car, and driving with a suspended license does not typically generate evidence found in the vehicle. B: The automobile exception requires probable cause to believe the vehicle contains contraband or evidence. A traffic stop for a suspended license does not, without more, supply probable cause to believe contraband is in the car. The automobile exception is inapplicable on these facts. D: This overstates the rule. Even after arrest, SITA can validate a vehicle search if either Gant prong is met. The automobile exception can also justify a warrantless search post-arrest if probable cause exists. The correct analysis is Gant-specific; a blanket warrant-always rule for any post-arrest search is wrong.
lb_rule_reference: Police may search the passenger compartment of a vehicle incident to arrest only if (1) the arrestee is unsecured and within reaching distance, or (2) it is reasonable to believe the vehicle contains evidence of the offense of arrest (Arizona v. Gant)
