Question: Hannah owned Greenacre, which had an appurtenant access easement over Isaac's lane. Hannah divided Greenacre into two smaller lots and conveyed one lot to Joseph. Joseph's use of Isaac's lane would be ordinary residential access and would not materially increase the burden beyond the use reasonably contemplated when the easement was created. May Joseph use the easement?
Answer A: No, because subdividing the dominant estate automatically terminates all appurtenant easements.
Answer B: No, because Joseph's deed must obtain a new signature from Isaac.
Answer C: Yes, because a subdivided portion of the dominant estate may use the appurtenant easement if the use is within the easement's scope and does not impermissibly overburden the servient estate.
Answer D: Yes, because Joseph may use the lane for any purpose, including serving land outside Greenacre.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Joseph may use the easement. When the dominant estate is subdivided, an appurtenant easement may generally benefit the subdivided parcels, so long as the use remains within the easement's scope and does not unreasonably or materially increase the burden on the servient estate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because subdivision does not automatically terminate an appurtenant easement. B: B is wrong because an existing appurtenant easement can pass with the subdivided dominant parcel without a new servient-owner signature. D: D is wrong because the easement may not be expanded to serve land outside the dominant estate or uses beyond its scope.
lb_rule_reference: Appurtenant easement / subdivision of dominant estate
