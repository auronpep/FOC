Question: Noah stayed overnight at his cousin's apartment, sleeping on the living-room couch. Police entered unlawfully at dawn and opened Noah's zipped duffel bag next to the couch. They found a stolen watch. Noah moves to suppress the watch. What is the best standing analysis?
Answer A: Noah lacks standing because he was not on the lease
Answer B: Noah has standing because he was an overnight guest and the police searched his own closed duffel
Answer C: Noah lacks standing because the watch was stolen
Answer D: Noah has standing only if his cousin also moves to suppress
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Noah had a legitimate privacy interest as an overnight guest and an additional personal interest in his zipped bag. He may challenge the unlawful entry and bag search.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Overnight guest status can create a reasonable expectation of privacy even without lease ownership. C: Standing turns on the searched apartment area and duffel, not lawful ownership of the watch. D: Fourth Amendment rights are personal. Noah can assert his own privacy interest.
lb_rule_reference: An overnight guest generally has a reasonable expectation of privacy in the host's home for the stay, and may also challenge searches of his own closed containers.
