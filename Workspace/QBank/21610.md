Question: A state requires all employers to provide a weekend shift schedule. The statute allows exemptions for secular morale events, annual inventory, and hardship caused by sports tournaments, but the agency categorically refuses exemptions for religious Sabbath observance. A religious employer challenges the denial. What is the best Free Exercise argument?
Answer A: The employer loses because the statute does not mention religion on its face
Answer B: Strict scrutiny may apply because the state grants comparable secular exemptions while categorically denying religious ones
Answer C: The employer wins automatically because all Sabbath burdens are unconstitutional
Answer D: Rational basis always applies because workplace rules are economic regulation
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The exemption scheme undercuts general applicability by favoring secular reasons over similar religious reasons.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: General applicability also matters. Comparable secular exemptions can trigger stricter review when religious exemptions are denied. C: The trigger is not automatic protection for all Sabbath burdens; it is the unequal exemption structure. D: Free Exercise scrutiny can increase when the rule is not generally applicable, even in a workplace setting.
lb_rule_reference: A law is not generally applicable when it allows comparable secular exemptions while denying religious exemptions without adequate justification.
