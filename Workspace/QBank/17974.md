Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Diversity Jurisdiction
Question: Creditor, a citizen of State A, has a contract claim against Debtor, also a citizen of State A. To sue in federal court, Creditor assigns the claim to Friend, a citizen of State B, for one dollar, with a private agreement that Friend will return all recovery to Creditor. Friend sues Debtor in federal court based on diversity. Should the court accept diversity jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because Friend and Debtor are citizens of different states on the pleadings
Answer B: Yes, because any assignment transfers citizenship for federal jurisdiction
Answer C: No, because the assignment appears collusive and made to invoke federal jurisdiction
Answer D: No, but only if the amount in controversy is less than $75,000
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal jurisdiction cannot be manufactured through an improper or collusive assignment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A collusive assignment made to create diversity is not enough. B: Assignments may be real, but collusive assignments to manufacture jurisdiction are barred. D: Even a high-value claim cannot cure collusive creation of diversity.
lb_rule_reference: Federal courts lack diversity jurisdiction when a party has been improperly or collusively made or joined to invoke jurisdiction.
