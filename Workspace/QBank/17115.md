Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: A merchant orally agreed to provide Benjamin with 700 custom floor tiles for Benjamin's shop and to install them. The total price was $7,000, with most of the price attributable to the tiles and only a small installation charge. No writing was signed, and the seller had not begun manufacturing the tiles before Benjamin canceled. Which Statute of Frauds analysis is strongest?
Answer A: The UCC goods Statute of Frauds is irrelevant because installation labor is included.
Answer B: The common-law one-year provision controls because installation might take several weeks.
Answer C: The contract is enforceable because custom floor tiles are always specially manufactured, even before any substantial beginning or commitment.
Answer D: If the predominant purpose is the sale of goods, UCC §2-201 applies; absent a writing or exception, the oral contract is unenforceable.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: For a mixed goods-services transaction, the predominant-purpose analysis determines the source law. If the dominant purpose is sale of goods, the UCC goods Statute of Frauds applies. Here the tile sale predominates, and no writing or exception is stated.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because incidental installation does not automatically remove a goods-dominant contract from Article 2. B: B is wrong because several weeks does not trigger the one-year provision, and the source-law issue points to goods. C: C is wrong because the specially manufactured goods exception requires more than the buyer's custom need; substantial beginning or procurement must occur before repudiation.
