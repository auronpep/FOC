Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Ezekiel sues a delivery company after being struck by a blue truck. Ezekiel offers testimony that, five minutes after the collision, a blue delivery truck with the company's logo was seen speeding away two blocks from the accident. The witness did not see the license plate or the driver. The company objects that the testimony does not prove it was the same truck. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because the witness did not identify the driver.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because a logo is never enough to connect a vehicle to a party.
Answer C: Admit the testimony because timing, location, color, and logo have some tendency to connect the company's truck to the collision; missing details affect weight.
Answer D: Admit the testimony only if Ezekiel first proves the truck's license plate number.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The testimony has a logical and conditional connection to the disputed fact: whether the company's truck struck Ezekiel. The missing license plate and driver identification weaken the inference but do not eliminate relevance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because driver identity is not the only route to vehicle connection. B: B is wrong because a logo can contribute to a circumstantial foundation. D: D is wrong because a license plate is not required for threshold relevance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / FRE 401
lb_student_diagnostic: Treating weak circumstantial vehicle identity as no foundation.
