Subtopic: Hearsay and Circumstances of Its Admissibility
Outline: Statement by opposing party
Question: Eve's lawyer prepared a privileged memo summarizing witness Ruth's account of an accident. The opposing party later deposed Ruth directly and now seeks to call Ruth at trial to testify about what she personally observed. Eve objects that Ruth's testimony is barred because her lawyer's memo about Ruth is work product. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude Ruth's testimony because any fact appearing in work product becomes protected.
Answer B: Exclude Ruth's testimony unless Eve waives work-product protection.
Answer C: Admit Ruth's testimony only if the opposing party first obtains the lawyer's memo.
Answer D: Allow Ruth to testify from personal knowledge; work-product protection covers the lawyer's litigation materials, not facts independently obtained from the witness.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Work-product doctrine protects trial-preparation materials and attorney mental processes; it does not make underlying facts or independent witness testimony inadmissible. The opposing party obtained Ruth's account directly through deposition. The decisive axis is protected material versus independent proof source.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This converts work product into fact immunity. B: B is wrong because No waiver is needed to use independently obtained witness testimony. C: C is wrong because The memo is not a prerequisite to calling the witness.
lb_rule_reference: FRCP 26(b)(3) / fact versus work-product material
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating work product as a substantive evidence exclusion instead of a discovery protection for materials.
