Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff, a citizen of Texas, sued defendant, a citizen of Oklahoma, in Oklahoma state court for $200,000. Defendant removed based on diversity. Because defendant is an Oklahoma citizen, the forum-defendant rule barred removal. Plaintiff waited 60 days after removal before moving to remand on that ground. How should the court treat the late remand motion?
Answer A: Grant remand because the forum-defendant rule is a nonwaivable SMJ defect
Answer B: Grant remand because any removal defect may be raised at any time
Answer C: Deny remand if the objection was untimely, because complete diversity and amount existed and the forum-defendant defect was procedural
Answer D: Deny remand only because federal-question jurisdiction exists
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal court had diversity SMJ, and the forum-defendant objection was waived by missing the remand deadline.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The forum-defendant rule is procedural, not a subject-matter jurisdiction limit. B: Procedural defects must be raised within the statutory time. D: The facts support diversity, not federal question.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff must timely move to remand for procedural removal defects; subject-matter jurisdiction defects may be raised later.
