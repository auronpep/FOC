MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: False imprisonment
Question: A finance company employed a collector to visit borrowers and demand payment. During a scheduled visit, the collector stood in the borrower's doorway and refused to let the borrower leave for ten minutes unless she signed a payment plan. The collector was paid a commission on collected accounts and had been told to "push hard but stay legal." The borrower sued the finance company for false imprisonment based on respondeat superior. Is the finance company likely vicariously liable for the collector's false imprisonment?
Answer A: No, because false imprisonment is intentional and therefore personal to the collector
Answer B: Yes, because the confinement occurred during an assigned collection visit and was a foreseeable outgrowth of that work
Answer C: Yes, but only if the finance company specifically ordered the collector to block exits
Answer D: No, because commission pay makes the collector an independent contractor
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The collector acted during authorized time and place while trying to collect the debt. The wrongful method was closely connected to the assigned collection task.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intentional torts can be within scope when closely tied to assigned duties. Coercive collection pressure is a foreseeable job-related friction in this role. C: Vicarious liability does not require authorization of the tort itself. Scope can exist when the wrongful act is closely connected to the employee's assigned work. D: Compensation method alone does not determine status. The facts say the company employed the collector and assigned collection visits.
lb_rule_reference: An employer may be vicariously liable for intentional torts committed in the course of assigned collection work when the conduct is a foreseeable outgrowth of job-related pressure.
