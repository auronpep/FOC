Question: 60 years ago by a properly executed and recorded deed, a father conveyed Greenacre, a tract of land: "To my son for life, then to my son's widow for her life, then to my son's child or children in equal shares." At that time, the son was six years old. Shortly thereafter, the father died testate. His son was his only heir at law. The father's will left his entire estate to his local church. 25 years ago, when he was 41, the son married his first wife who was then 20 years old; they had one child. The first wife and the child were killed in an automobile accident three years ago when the child was 21. The child died testate, leaving his entire estate to a charity. His father was the child's sole heir at law. Two years ago, the son remarried. This year, the son died testate, survived only by his widow, to whom he left his entire estate. The common law Rule Against Perpetuities is unchanged by statute in the jurisdiction. In an appropriate action to determine the ownership of Greenacre, the court should find that title is vested in
Answer A: A: the church, because the son's widow was unborn at the time of conveyance and, hence, the remainder violated the Rule Against Perpetuities.
Answer B: B: the widow, because her life estate and her inheritance from the son (who was the father's sole heir at law and who was the child's sole heir at law) merged the entire title in her.
Answer C: C: the charity, because the child had a vested remainder interest (as the only child of the son) that he inherited, the life estate to the son's widow being of no force and effect.
Answer D: D: the widow for life under the terms of the father's deed, with the remainder to the charity as the successor in interest to the son's only child.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. It is important to remember that vested interests are NOT subject to the RAP. Despite the fact that the son was only six years old and that his future wife was not yet born at the time the father conveyed the property to him, all interests would clearly vest or fail within the son's life. The widow's interest in a life estate vested upon her marriage to the son. Likewise, the son's child's interest vested upon his birth.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The church has no interest in the tract of land; the tract of land was conveyed away before the father's death. B is incorrect. The child's future possessory interest vested upon his birth and was devisable by him, whether or not he survived his father's widow. C is incorrect. The widow's life estate future interest became vested upon her marriage to the son. The charity will only be entitled to present possession upon the widow's death.
