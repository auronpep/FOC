Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal civil case, the judge admits a properly authenticated recording of Solomon's voice from a deposition. Rebekah offers a second recording of an anonymous threat and asks the jury to compare the two voices. Solomon objects that only an expert can compare voices. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection if the anonymous recording is otherwise authenticated sufficiently; the trier of fact may compare it with an authenticated specimen.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because jurors may compare handwriting but not voices.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Rebekah proves the anonymous recording's speaker by a preponderance before the jury hears it.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Solomon admits the anonymous threat was his.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Authentication may be supported by comparison with an authenticated specimen by an expert or by the trier of fact, and voice identification does not categorically require an expert. The anonymous recording still needs a threshold foundation, but the jury can make the comparison if that threshold is met.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because comparison is not limited to handwriting. C: C is wrong because the threshold is sufficient evidence to support a finding, not a judge's preponderance finding for the ultimate identity. D: D is wrong because an admission is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(3), (5)
lb_student_diagnostic: Expert-required false positive and preponderance-by-judge over-demand.
