MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Falling below the standard of care
Question: A gym placed heavy dumbbells on a sloped display shelf beside a busy walkway. Several employees had seen the dumbbells roll when the shelf was bumped. A rubber lip costing little and taking minutes to install would have stopped the rolling. The gym did not install it, and a dumbbell rolled onto a customer's foot. Which fact most strongly supports breach?
Answer A: The precaution was cheap and would have reduced a known risk of serious injury
Answer B: The customer was injured, so breach is automatic
Answer C: The dumbbells were heavy, so the gym was strictly liable
Answer D: The gym avoided breach because no statute required a rubber lip
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Reasonable-care analysis considers burden, probability, and likely severity. A low-cost guard against a known heavy-object risk strongly supports breach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Injury alone does not prove breach. The breach evidence is the failure to take a reasonable precaution before the injury. C: The claim is ordinary negligence, not strict liability. The issue is reasonable care. D: Common-law reasonable care can require precautions even without a specific statute.
lb_rule_reference: Breach can be supported when a reasonable actor would take a low-burden precaution against a foreseeable, serious risk.
