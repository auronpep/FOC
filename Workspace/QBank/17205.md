Question: A store clerk is told to carry a boxed tablet from the stockroom to the front register for a waiting customer. While walking through the store, the clerk hides the tablet under his coat and leaves with it, intending to keep it. What is the best common-law theft offense?
Answer A: Larceny, because the clerk had only custody and trespassorily took property still possessed by the employer
Answer B: Embezzlement, because every employee who converts workplace property embezzles it
Answer C: False pretenses, because the clerk implicitly deceived the employer by leaving
Answer D: No theft offense, because the clerk touched the tablet with permission
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The clerk was merely handling the item for the employer; possession had not passed to him.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Embezzlement requires lawful possession, not mere custody. C: False pretenses requires obtaining title by false representation; no title transfer occurred. D: Permission to handle property as an employee does not equal consent to take possession for personal use.
lb_rule_reference: A servant or employee who has only custody of an employer's property and converts it can commit larceny because possession remains with the employer.
