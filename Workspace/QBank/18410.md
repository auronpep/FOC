Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Peter is charged with robbing a store. The robber drove away in a white van. The prosecutor offers a rental agreement for a white van rented in Peter's name three hours before the robbery. A clerk testifies that the renter showed a driver's license bearing Peter's name and photograph. Peter objects that the clerk cannot be certain Peter was the person who rented it because the clerk saw many customers that day. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the rental agreement because the license and rental facts are sufficient to support a finding that Peter rented the van.
Answer B: Exclude the rental agreement because the clerk lacks perfect memory.
Answer C: Exclude the rental agreement unless the prosecutor produces surveillance video from the rental counter.
Answer D: Admit the rental agreement only if Peter admits he rented the van.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The rental agreement, license check, matching vehicle type, and timing provide enough evidence for a reasonable juror to connect Peter to the van. The clerk's uncertainty affects weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because perfect memory is not required for threshold foundation. C: C is wrong because surveillance video is not the exclusive proof channel. D: D is wrong because direct admission is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / FRE 401
lb_student_diagnostic: Demanding perfect witness certainty instead of sufficient circumstantial foundation.
