Question: Officers arrested Terrence for armed robbery and transported him to the police station. While waiting in a holding cell, no officer was present or speaking with Terrence. Terrence suddenly called out to an officer walking past and said, "Hey, I did it. I robbed that store. I just want you to know I didn't mean to hurt anybody." No Miranda warnings had been given. Terrence later moves to suppress the statement. Should Terrence's statement be suppressed?
Answer A: Yes, because Terrence was in custody when he made the statement and had not been given Miranda warnings
Answer B: Yes, because the officer's presence near the holding cell constituted the functional equivalent of interrogation
Answer C: No, because Terrence's statement was spontaneous and volunteered without any interrogation by police
Answer D: No, because Terrence's statement was not incriminating since he claimed he did not mean to hurt anyone
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Miranda's protections apply when a suspect is subjected to custodial interrogation. Interrogation means express questioning or its functional equivalent — words or actions by police reasonably likely to elicit an incriminating response. A completely unprompted, self-initiated statement satisfies neither prong. Terrence was not questioned; he volunteered the confession of his own free will. The statement is admissible under *Miranda v. Arizona*, which explicitly preserves the admissibility of volunteered statements.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Miranda warnings are required before custodial interrogation — not before all statements made in custody. A volunteered statement made without any prompting by police is not the product of interrogation. Terrence spoke spontaneously with no officer questioning him, which places his statement entirely outside Miranda's scope. B: The functional equivalent of interrogation requires words or actions by police that they should know are reasonably likely to elicit an incriminating response. An officer merely walking past a holding cell — without speaking, gesturing, or engaging in any conduct directed at the suspect — does not meet this standard. Terrence initiated the communication entirely on his own. D: The statement "I did it. I robbed that store" is plainly incriminating. Moreover, the correct reason for admissibility has nothing to do with whether the statement is incriminating — it is admissible because it was volunteered without interrogation. The incrimination or exculpation of the statement is legally irrelevant to the Miranda analysis.
lb_rule_reference: Volunteered statements made without any interrogation are admissible even without Miranda warnings; Miranda protects against compelled responses to interrogation, not against a suspect's spontaneous choice to speak.
