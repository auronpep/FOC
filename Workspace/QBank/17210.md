Question: Mary owned a farm. She conveyed the farm to Joseph by deed stating, “Mary reserves an easement over the farm for Peter, who owns the neighboring orchard, to use the old lane.” Peter was not a party to the deed and gave no separate written release or grant. Under the traditional common-law rule, what is the result of the attempted reservation for Peter?
Answer A: Peter has a valid easement because any deed may reserve rights for any named person.
Answer B: Peter has a valid easement because neighboring landowners always receive access over old lanes.
Answer C: The attempted reservation is invalid because, under the traditional rule, a reservation cannot create a new interest in favor of a stranger to the deed.
Answer D: Peter receives fee title to the lane because the deed used the word “reserves.”
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the traditional common-law rule, a reservation in a deed could reserve a new interest for the grantor but not for a third party who was a stranger to the deed. The stem expressly asks for that traditional rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong under the stated traditional rule. B: B is wrong because adjacency and old-lane use do not automatically create an express easement. D: D is wrong because an easement reservation would create a use right, not fee title, and here the third-party reservation fails under the traditional rule.
lb_rule_reference: Reservation in favor of third party / traditional common law
