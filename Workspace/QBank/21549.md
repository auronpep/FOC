Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Before signing a contract with Samuel to run a conference, Mary spent $3,000 investigating several possible conference locations. After signing the contract with Samuel, Mary spent $9,000 on nonrefundable deposits for the selected location. Samuel then breached, and Mary's expected profits are too uncertain to prove. Which amount is the strongest reliance-damages claim against Samuel?
Answer A: $12,000, because all expenses Mary incurred before the conference are recoverable.
Answer B: $3,000, because investigation costs came first.
Answer C: Nothing, because uncertain profits bar all damages.
Answer D: $9,000, because the location deposits were incurred after the contract in reliance on Samuel's promised performance.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages ordinarily protect expenditures made in reliance on the contract. Mary's post-contract nonrefundable deposits were made to perform the contract. The earlier investigation costs were incurred before the Samuel contract and are not the strongest reliance claim against Samuel absent facts tying them to his promise.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it includes precontract investigation costs not shown to be reliance on Samuel's contract. B: B is wrong because the precontract investigation is weaker than the post-contract deposits. C: C is wrong because reliance is a fallback when profits are too uncertain.
