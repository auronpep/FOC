Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: “Necessary and Proper” Clause power
Question: Congress creates a federal licensing database to administer a valid federal regulation of interstate drug shipments. A challenger argues the database is unconstitutional because Congress could administer the shipment rules without it. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The database is invalid unless it is absolutely indispensable to the regulatory scheme
Answer B: The database may be valid if it is a proper means rationally related to executing the valid commerce regulation
Answer C: The database is valid even if it has no connection to any enumerated power
Answer D: The database is invalid because federal databases are never proper
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Congress may select convenient means to implement its enumerated commerce power.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Necessary and Proper does not mean indispensable. C: The clause supports execution of enumerated powers; it is not freestanding. D: The Constitution does not categorically ban databases used to implement valid powers.
lb_rule_reference: The Necessary and Proper Clause permits Congress to choose means that are rationally related and appropriate to executing an enumerated power; the means need not be absolutely indispensable.
