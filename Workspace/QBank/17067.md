Outline: Nuisance
Question: A small rendering plant began operating next to a residential subdivision. For six months, a strong rancid odor entered nearby homes every evening, forcing residents to keep windows closed and preventing normal outdoor use of yards. The plant met zoning rules and had no physical runoff onto the homes. May the residents have a private nuisance claim?
Answer A: Yes, because severe recurring odors can substantially and unreasonably interfere with use and enjoyment of land
Answer B: Yes, but only if the plant also trespassed on the land
Answer C: No, because compliance with zoning defeats nuisance as a matter of law
Answer D: No, because odors are never legally cognizable injuries
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Private nuisance does not require a physical invasion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Trespass is a separate tort; nuisance can be based on intangible interference. C: Regulatory compliance is relevant but does not automatically bar nuisance. D: Odors may be actionable when they cross the substantial and unreasonable threshold.
lb_rule_reference: Odor, noise, vibration, or similar intangible conditions can support private nuisance if they substantially and unreasonably interfere with use and enjoyment of land.
