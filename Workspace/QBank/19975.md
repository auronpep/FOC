Subtopic: Rights in Land
Outline: Scope
Question: Peter held an easement to use an 8-foot gravel path across Martha's land for access to his cottage. The path developed ruts. Peter announced that he would enter Martha's land with heavy machinery, widen the path to 24 feet, pave it, and add lighting so tour buses could reach the cottage more easily. The easement deed did not mention buses, widening, paving, or lighting. What is Martha's best objection?
Answer A: Peter may do all the work because an easement holder has full ownership of the easement strip.
Answer B: Peter may do all the work because any improvement labeled maintenance is automatically within scope.
Answer C: Peter may make reasonable repairs needed to use the easement, but widening, paving, lighting, and bus access may exceed the easement's scope and increase the servient burden.
Answer D: The easement terminated automatically because the gravel path developed ruts.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement holder may make reasonable repairs necessary to enjoy the easement, but repair authority does not permit material expansion beyond the grant's scope. Widening an 8-foot cottage path to a 24-foot paved, lighted bus route likely exceeds maintenance and increases the servient burden.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an easement is a limited use right, not fee ownership. B: B is wrong because labels do not control; the work must be reasonably tied to the easement's scope. C is correct for the reasons above. D: D is wrong because deterioration creates a repair issue, not automatic termination.
lb_rule_reference: Repair access limited by easement scope
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is accepting the word maintenance without checking whether the proposed work changes the scope and burden.
