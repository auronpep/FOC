Question: Deborah hired Nathan to repair a roof. The signed writing stated that Nathan would repair the roof for $12,000. It contained no merger clause and the court finds it final but not complete and exclusive. Deborah offers evidence that, before signing, Nathan orally agreed to do the same roof repair for $9,000. How should the court rule? How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the oral price because the writing was not complete and exclusive.
Answer B: Admit the oral price because prior oral statements are always allowed when there is no merger clause.
Answer C: Exclude the oral price because it contradicts the written price term.
Answer D: Exclude the entire written contract because the parties negotiated different prices.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A partial integration may be supplemented by consistent additional terms, but it cannot be contradicted by prior or contemporaneous terms. A $9,000 price for the same work directly contradicts the written $12,000 price.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because partial integration still bars contradictory terms. B: B is wrong because absence of a merger clause does not admit contradictory price evidence. D: D is wrong because the prior negotiation does not void the written contract.
