Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A tenant sues a landlord in state court for state-law retaliation after the tenant complained about unsafe wiring. The landlord removes, arguing that the tenant could also have sued under a federal housing statute but chose not to. The complaint contains no federal cause of action and no necessary federal element. Was removal proper on federal-question grounds?
Answer A: Yes, because the tenant could have pleaded a federal claim
Answer B: No, because the complaint pleads only state law and does not necessarily raise a federal issue
Answer C: Yes, because removal is broader than original jurisdiction
Answer D: No, because tenants can never sue in federal court
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The well-pleaded complaint rule lets the plaintiff choose state-law claims when they independently support relief.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Jurisdiction depends on the claim actually pleaded, not an unasserted federal alternative. C: A case is removable only if it could have been filed originally in federal court, subject to removal rules. D: Tenant status is not the issue; pleaded federal law is.
lb_rule_reference: The plaintiff is generally master of the complaint; a federal claim that could have been pleaded does not create federal-question jurisdiction if the complaint actually pleads only state law.
