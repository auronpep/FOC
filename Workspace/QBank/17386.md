MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Self-defense
Question: In a jurisdiction that requires retreat before using deadly force, Lena is standing in a hallway when Nora slaps her and raises a hand to slap her again. A stairwell door is open behind Lena. Instead of leaving, Lena pushes Nora backward with both hands, causing Nora to stumble but not suffer serious injury. Lena is charged with battery. What is Lena's best defense?
Answer A: Self-defense, because she used reasonable nondeadly force to stop an imminent nondeadly attack
Answer B: No defense, because Lena had a safe route to retreat
Answer C: No defense, because a slap is never enough to permit self-defense
Answer D: Self-defense only if Nora intended serious bodily harm
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Lena faced an imminent battery and responded with nondeadly force. The jurisdiction's retreat rule is relevant before deadly force, not before every defensive push or shove. Because Lena's response was proportionate and immediately defensive, self-defense applies.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The retreat rule generally limits deadly force. Lena did not use deadly force; she used a proportionate nondeadly push against an ongoing slap. C: A threatened battery can justify reasonable nondeadly defensive force. The law limits the response; it does not require the defendant to absorb every nondeadly attack. D: Serious bodily harm is not required before using nondeadly defensive force. A reasonable belief in imminent unlawful contact can justify a proportionate nondeadly response.
lb_rule_reference: Retreat rules, where recognized, generally concern deadly force; a defendant may use reasonable nondeadly force without first retreating
