Outline: Power of termination
Question: Solomon conveyed Marketacre “to Nathan and his heirs, but if the land is ever used as a tavern, Solomon may enter and terminate the estate.” Nathan later opened a tavern on the land. Solomon has not acted. What interest does Solomon have after the tavern opens?
Answer A: A reversion that became automatically possessory when the tavern opened.
Answer B: A right of entry, also called a power of termination.
Answer C: A possibility of reverter that already made Solomon owner.
Answer D: No interest, because Nathan's deed used the words “and his heirs.”
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Solomon has a right of entry, also called a power of termination. The deed uses conditional language and expressly says Solomon may enter and terminate. Nathan's breach gives Solomon the power to terminate, but title does not shift automatically.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a condition subsequent does not create an automatically possessory reversion. C: C is wrong because a possibility of reverter follows durational language and automatic termination. D: D is wrong because words of inheritance do not erase the express condition subsequent.
lb_rule_reference: Fee simple subject to condition subsequent / power of termination
