Question: Miriam hired Samuel to build a wooden fence around her garden for $8,000. The fence was completed and usable, but one gate opened inward instead of outward as specified. The mistake did not affect safety and would cost $200 to correct. Miriam refused to pay the contract price. What is Samuel's best claim?
Answer A: He substantially performed and may recover the contract price less the cost to correct the gate.
Answer B: He cannot recover anything because he did not strictly comply with every specification.
Answer C: He may recover only if Miriam expressly waived the gate specification before completion.
Answer D: He may recover the full contract price with no deduction because any usable fence is perfect performance.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: In common-law service or construction contracts, substantial performance of constructive conditions generally entitles the performer to the contract price less damages for minor defects. The gate defect is minor and easily corrected, so Samuel's payment right matures subject to an offset.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because strict compliance is not required for constructive conditions. C: C is wrong because waiver is not necessary when substantial performance suffices. D: D is wrong because substantial performance does not eliminate damages for the defect.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law substantial performance
