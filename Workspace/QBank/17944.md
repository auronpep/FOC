Question: The defendant refuses to answer interrogatories. Without attempting to confer, the plaintiff files a motion asking the court to compel answers and immediately enter default judgment as a sanction. What is the most accurate procedural point?
Answer A: The motion should generally include a certification that the movant in good faith conferred or attempted to confer before seeking court action
Answer B: The court must enter default judgment whenever a party refuses to answer interrogatories
Answer C: The plaintiff cannot seek an order compelling interrogatory answers
Answer D: If the motion is granted, expenses must always be awarded with no exceptions
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 37 expects a good-faith effort to resolve discovery disputes before a motion to compel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Default is a severe sanction, usually tied to disobedience of a court order or extreme misconduct. C: A motion to compel is the ordinary tool for inadequate discovery responses. D: Fees may be denied if the movant filed before attempting in good faith to obtain discovery, the nondisclosure was substantially justified, or other circumstances make an award unjust.
lb_rule_reference: A motion to compel generally must include a good-faith certification of conferral, and if granted, the court usually requires the resisting party or counsel to pay reasonable expenses unless an exception applies.
