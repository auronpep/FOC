Question: After being arrested for burglary, a client privately tells a defense lawyer, “I broke into the warehouse last month, and I need you to defend me.” The client seeks advice about the pending charge and does not ask the lawyer to conceal evidence or commit any future misconduct. The prosecutor subpoenas the lawyer to repeat the client's statement. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Compel the testimony because admissions of past crimes are never privileged.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because the client made a confidential communication to obtain legal advice about past conduct.
Answer C: Compel the testimony because the crime-fraud exception applies whenever the client admits criminal conduct.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony only if the lawyer reasonably believed the client was innocent.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications made for the purpose of obtaining legal advice. The crime-fraud exception targets communications seeking advice or assistance for future or ongoing crime or fraud, not ordinary defense advice about past conduct. The decisive axis is past-crime legal advice versus future wrongdoing.
