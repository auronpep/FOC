Outline: Appointment and removal
Question: Congress creates a consumer finance agency headed by one Director who serves a six-year term. The Director may issue binding regulations, bring enforcement actions in federal court, and impose civil penalties. The statute says the President may remove the Director only for inefficiency, neglect of duty, or malfeasance. The President removes the Director because of policy disagreement. The Director sues for reinstatement. Which is the best constitutional analysis?
Answer A: The removal restriction is likely unconstitutional because it insulates a single director exercising substantial executive power from presidential control
Answer B: The removal restriction is valid because Congress may protect any agency official from policy-based removal
Answer C: The removal restriction is invalid only if the Director was appointed without Senate confirmation
Answer D: The restriction is valid because civil penalties are judicial, not executive, power
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A single agency head wielding significant executive authority is different from a multimember expert commission. For-cause protection in that setting can impermissibly restrict the President's Article II control.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: That principle is not unlimited. Single-director executive agencies with substantial executive power raise stronger Article II concerns. C: A removal limit can violate Article II even if the appointment was proper. D: Rulemaking, enforcement, and penalty authority are executive functions for removal-control analysis.
lb_rule_reference: A restriction on removing a single agency director who exercises substantial executive power is constitutionally suspect because it can unduly limit presidential control.
