Outline: Motions to dismiss
Question: In a federal civil action, the district court denies the defendant's motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim. The case proceeds toward discovery and trial. The defendant wants to appeal immediately because the motion raised an important legal issue. Is the denial immediately appealable as a final judgment?
Answer A: Yes, because important legal issues are always immediately appealable
Answer B: Yes, because denial of a Rule 12(b)(6) motion ends the case
Answer C: No, because the order does not end the litigation on the merits
Answer D: No, because federal cases can never be appealed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The final judgment rule generally requires an order ending the case for all claims and parties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Importance alone does not satisfy finality. B: Denial keeps the case alive. D: Federal appeals are available after final judgment or a valid exception.
lb_rule_reference: Importance or inconvenience does not itself create appealability.
