Question: Caleb agreed to buy Deborah's farm, with closing “conditioned on Caleb receiving an environmental report satisfactory to Caleb.” The report disclosed a minor issue. Caleb told Deborah, “I am satisfied enough; let's close.” Deborah then refused to close, arguing that the environmental condition had not been strictly satisfied. Who is most likely to prevail?
Answer A: Deborah, because any unsatisfied condition can be invoked by either party.
Answer B: Caleb, because the condition was for Caleb's protection and Caleb could waive it.
Answer C: Deborah, because waiver of a condition always requires new consideration.
Answer D: Caleb, but only if the environmental report was objectively perfect.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A condition inserted for one party's protection can generally be waived by the protected party. The environmental report condition protected Caleb, and Caleb chose to proceed. Deborah cannot invoke Caleb's protective condition to avoid closing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every party may invoke every condition; the condition's protective purpose matters. C: C is wrong because waiver of a condition does not always require new consideration. D: D is wrong because Caleb's waiver, not objective perfection, is the decisive fact.
lb_rule_reference: Condition inserted for one party's protection
