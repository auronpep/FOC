Outline: Lay opinion testimony
Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Noah testifies that he saw Mary use a cleaning spray and that Mary developed a rash later that day. Noah has no medical or scientific training. Mary asks Noah, “Did the spray cause Mary's rash?” The manufacturer objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because Noah personally saw Mary use the spray.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because causation is always a permissible lay inference.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because medical or scientific causation requires specialized knowledge beyond Noah's ordinary perception.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Mary later calls a dermatologist.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Noah may describe what he observed—the spray use and the later rash. But whether the spray medically caused the rash requires specialized scientific or medical knowledge. That opinion is outside lay testimony.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Noah's perception of spray use and rash does not qualify him to testify about medical causation. B: B is wrong because causation is not always a lay inference. D: D is wrong because the ruling turns on Noah's lack of specialized basis, not later witness order.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 701(c) / medical causation as specialized knowledge
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests causation overreach and the specialized-knowledge boundary.
