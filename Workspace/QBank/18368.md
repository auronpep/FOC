Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Naomi sues a delivery company after a crate allegedly split open and injured her. Naomi offers the cracked crate. A warehouse employee testifies that he marked the crate with a red tag immediately after the accident, placed it in a locked storage area, and recognizes the red tag and crack pattern. The company objects that the employee is not a forensic evidence technician. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the crate if the employee's testimony is sufficient to support a finding that it is the accident crate.
Answer B: Exclude the crate because only a forensic technician can authenticate real evidence.
Answer C: Exclude the crate unless Naomi proves the crate's condition is unchanged beyond dispute.
Answer D: Admit the crate only if the company's manager also identifies it.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness with knowledge may authenticate an item by testifying that it is what the proponent claims. The employee's marking, storage, and recognition can support that finding. Specialized forensic testimony is not required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a forensic technician is not required for every physical object. C: C is wrong because unchanged condition need not be beyond dispute if the object remains probative. D: D is wrong because the opponent's manager need not identify it.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(1) / FRE 401
lb_student_diagnostic: Wrong proof-channel trap: requiring an unnecessarily specialized authenticator.
