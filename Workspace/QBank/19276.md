Question: A supplier sued a retailer for theft after its own inventory records showed the missing goods had been shipped to a different customer. The supplier filed anyway to pressure the retailer in a price dispute. The theft case was dismissed on the merits in the retailer's favor after months of litigation expense. Is the retailer likely to prevail on a malicious prosecution claim against the supplier?
Answer A: Yes, because the supplier initiated proceedings without probable cause, for an improper purpose, and the case ended in the retailer's favor
Answer B: No, because civil lawsuits can never support malicious prosecution
Answer C: Yes, because every lawsuit that a defendant wins was maliciously prosecuted
Answer D: No, because the retailer must show the supplier misused a subpoena after filing
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts support initiation, lack of probable cause, malice or improper purpose, favorable termination, and damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Civil proceedings can support malicious prosecution where the required elements are met. C: Favorable termination is necessary but not sufficient; lack of probable cause and malice also matter. D: Subpoena misuse is an abuse-of-process theory; malicious prosecution targets wrongful initiation.
lb_rule_reference: Malicious prosecution requires initiating proceedings without probable cause and with malice, favorable termination, and damages.
