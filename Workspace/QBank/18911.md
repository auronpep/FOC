Question: A city allows a controversial speaker to reserve a public auditorium for a lecture about immigration policy. After opponents threaten to protest loudly, the city cancels only that lecture, stating that the speaker's message is likely to anger listeners. What is the best First Amendment analysis?
Answer A: The cancellation is valid because listener anger lets the city silence the speaker
Answer B: The cancellation is content based because it targets the speaker's message and likely listener reaction
Answer C: The cancellation is content neutral because it is motivated by public safety
Answer D: The cancellation is valid because public auditoriums are never forums for private speech
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The city singled out the lecture because of its subject and anticipated reaction, creating a serious heckler's-veto problem.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Hostile audience reaction is not a license to suppress protected speech. C: A safety label does not make message-targeted cancellation content neutral. D: A public auditorium opened for private lectures can create forum limits on government exclusion.
lb_rule_reference: Government may not suppress protected speech based on anticipated hostile audience reaction unless it satisfies the demanding rules for unprotected speech or a valid neutral regulation.
