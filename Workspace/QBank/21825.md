Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A tenant kept a gentle dog that had lived in the apartment building for six years without biting, lunging, growling, or threatening anyone. One afternoon, a neighbor stepped on the dog's tail in a crowded elevator. The startled dog bit the neighbor's ankle. The tenant had complied with the building's leash rules. The neighbor sues the tenant for strict liability for animal harm. Is the neighbor likely to prevail on a strict liability claim against the tenant for harm caused by the dog?
Answer A: Yes, because owners are strictly liable whenever their pets injure another person
Answer B: No, because a domestic dog without known dangerous propensities does not create strict liability on these facts
Answer C: Yes, because the tenant must prove the dog had never startled before
Answer D: No, because strict liability never applies to animals kept in apartment buildings
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The dog was domestic and had no known dangerous propensity. The neighbor might explore negligence if the tenant mishandled the dog, but strict liability is not established.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Domestic pet strict liability requires known dangerous propensity. The dog had no history of biting, threatening, or other warning behavior. C: The plaintiff must show the owner knew or had reason to know of a dangerous propensity. The facts affirmatively show a long peaceful history, and ordinary startle response is not enough. D: Location in an apartment building is not the rule. A tenant could be strictly liable for a domestic animal with known dangerous propensities or for a wild animal.
lb_rule_reference: A domestic pet owner is not strictly liable absent knowledge or reason to know of the animal's dangerous propensity, though negligence may still be possible.
