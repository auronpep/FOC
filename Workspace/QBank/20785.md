Question: Ruth owned Meadowacre in fee simple. Ruth signed and delivered to Boaz a general warranty deed stating, “I convey Meadowacre to Boaz for life.” Boaz accepted and later argued that the general warranty label gave him a fee simple. What estate did Boaz receive?
Answer A: A life estate, because the deed's estate description controls the interest conveyed even though the deed includes general warranty language.
Answer B: A fee simple, because a general warranty deed always conveys the largest estate the grantor owns.
Answer C: No estate, because a warranty deed cannot convey a life estate.
Answer D: A fee simple only if Boaz paid fair market value for Meadowacre.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a general warranty deed, and the decisive deed-type/estate-description lock is the interest expressly conveyed. Ruth owned a fee simple, but she conveyed Meadowacre to Boaz for life. Warranty language may affect covenant liability; it does not enlarge the stated life estate into a fee simple.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because deed type does not override the estate limitation in the granting language. C: C is wrong because a deed can convey a life estate. D: D is wrong because payment does not expand the estate described in the deed.
lb_rule_reference: Warranty language does not enlarge the estate expressly conveyed
