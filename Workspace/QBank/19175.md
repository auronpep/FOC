Question: During a sudden flash flood, Malik breaks the window of a closed cabin and enters to avoid being swept away. No one is inside, and Malik leaves a note with his contact information before departing the next morning. Malik is charged with criminal trespass and property damage. Which defense best fits Malik's conduct?
Answer A: Duress, because Malik felt forced by the emergency
Answer B: Necessity, because he chose a lesser harm to avoid imminent natural danger with no reasonable alternative
Answer C: Self-defense, because Malik protected himself from the flood
Answer D: No defense, because property damage can never be justified
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Malik faced imminent danger from a natural force and committed limited property crimes to prevent greater harm to life. He had no reasonable legal alternative and did not create the emergency. Those facts fit necessity, not duress.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Legal duress involves coercion by a human threat. A flood is natural pressure, so the better doctrine is necessity. C: Self-defense concerns force against persons threatening unlawful force. Natural forces are analyzed under necessity. D: Necessity can justify limited property damage when it avoids a greater imminent harm and no legal alternative exists.
lb_rule_reference: Duress involves human threats; necessity involves natural or circumstantial pressure and requires a lesser-evil choice with no reasonable legal alternative
