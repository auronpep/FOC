Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A city ordinance made building owners responsible for keeping adjacent public sidewalks safe during construction. A building owner hired an independent contractor to cover an excavation but did not inspect the work. The contractor left a large unguarded hole, and a pedestrian fell in. Can the owner avoid liability solely because the unsafe work was done by an independent contractor?
Answer A: Yes, because independent contractor status always shifts all tort responsibility
Answer B: Yes, unless the contractor was the owner's employee for payroll purposes
Answer C: No, because the ordinance-created sidewalk-safety duty may be nondelegable
Answer D: No, but only because construction is always abnormally dangerous
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The owner may remain responsible for ensuring the protected duty is performed safely.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Nondelegable duties are an exception. B: The duty can remain with the owner regardless of payroll classification. D: The better analysis is nondelegable duty, not automatic abnormal-danger classification.
lb_rule_reference: A principal with a nondelegable duty cannot avoid liability merely by hiring an independent contractor to perform that duty.
