Question: In a federal contract trial, Joshua testifies that he personally heard Rachel say, “I accept the offer.” Rachel objects that Joshua secretly recorded the conversation and must produce the recording instead of testifying. Joshua is not offering the recording. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because any recorded conversation must be proved by the recording.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Joshua proves the recording was lost without bad faith.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Joshua may testify from personal knowledge about the words he heard; the recording is not required unless its contents are the proof vehicle.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the recording is played for the jury first.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Testimony about an independently perceived conversation is testimonial evidence. The original-writing rule is triggered when a party seeks to prove the contents of the recording, not when a witness testifies to what the witness personally heard.
