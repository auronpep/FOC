Outline: Robbery
Question: Police arrested Rico for a robbery committed by a masked person known to witnesses only as "Red Ace." During booking, before warnings, a detective assigned to the robbery case asked Rico, "What street name do you use with your crew?" The answer "Red Ace" was written on the intake form and offered at trial. Does the routine booking exception make Rico's answer admissible in the prosecution's case-in-chief?
Answer A: Yes, because the question was asked during booking
Answer B: Yes, because nicknames are biographical information as a matter of law
Answer C: No, because the question was reasonably likely to elicit an incriminating response and was not routine administrative booking
Answer D: No, because all alias evidence is barred by the Fifth Amendment
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The detective used booking to ask an investigative alias question tied to the charged robbery. Without Miranda warnings, the answer is excluded from the prosecution's case-in-chief.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The setting does not control when the question is investigative and likely to elicit incriminating information. B: This question targeted the robbery alias known from witnesses and was asked by the investigating detective. It was interrogation. D: Alias questions may be admissible when routine and noninvestigative. The defect is the investigative purpose here.
lb_rule_reference: The routine booking exception does not apply when police ask a question reasonably likely to elicit incriminating information rather than administrative biographical data.
