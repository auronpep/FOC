Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Offers of proof
Question: Joseph sues Benjamin for fraud. Joseph calls Mary and asks, “What did Benjamin tell you during the meeting?” Benjamin objects on hearsay grounds. The court sustains the objection before Mary answers. Joseph's lawyer says only, “We need this testimony for our case,” and makes no further record of what Mary would say. After judgment for Benjamin, Joseph argues on appeal that Mary's testimony was wrongly excluded. How should the appellate court treat the claim?
Answer A: Review the exclusion because Joseph objected to the court's ruling at trial.
Answer B: Review the exclusion because any excluded testimony is preserved automatically.
Answer C: Decline ordinary review because Joseph did not inform the court of the substance of Mary's excluded testimony and it was not apparent from context.
Answer D: Decline review only if Benjamin proves that Mary's testimony would have been inadmissible for every possible purpose.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When evidence is excluded, the proponent preserves a claim of error by informing the court of the substance of the evidence through an offer of proof unless the substance is apparent from the context. Joseph's general statement that the testimony was needed did not tell the court what Mary would have said. The ordinary claim of error was not preserved.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the party harmed by exclusion must make the substance known; merely disagreeing with the ruling is not enough. B: B is wrong because excluded evidence is not automatically preserved. D: D is wrong because the preservation problem exists before appellate merits review; Benjamin need not prove every possible inadmissibility route.
