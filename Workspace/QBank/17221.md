Question: A public school requires all students to attend a neutral health class that includes nutrition, exercise, and basic disease-prevention lessons. A parent objects to one lesson and claims substantive due process gives parents a constitutional right to veto any public school lesson they dislike. What is the best response?
Answer A: Parents have a fundamental right to veto every public school lesson
Answer B: The parent loses automatically because parents have no constitutional rights involving children
Answer C: The parental right does not include a constitutional veto over every neutral public school curriculum decision
Answer D: The school can prevail only by proving the lesson survives strict scrutiny in every detail
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim overstates the scope of parental substantive due process rights in public school curriculum.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Those rights do not create a blanket veto over public school curriculum. B: Parents do have recognized rights, but the claimed veto is too broad. D: The first issue is whether the claimed veto falls within the fundamental right's scope.
lb_rule_reference: Parents have fundamental rights concerning custody, care, and upbringing, but that does not give them a constitutional veto over every public school curriculum decision.
