Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A patient sues a hospital in federal court for state-law negligence. The complaint alleges that the hospital violated a federal recordkeeping regulation and that the violation is evidence of negligence. The case turns mainly on what the nurses did during one shift, and deciding the regulation issue would not affect federal policy beyond this dispute. Does the federal regulation allegation automatically create federal-question jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because any reference to a federal regulation creates federal-question jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because negligence cases in federal court have no amount-in-controversy requirement
Answer C: No, not automatically; a fact-bound federal regulation issue in a state negligence claim may be insufficiently substantial
Answer D: No, unless the defendant plans to deny violating the federal regulation
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal issue appears as one negligence fact and is not described as substantial to the federal system as a whole.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A federal ingredient is not enough. The federal issue must be necessarily raised, disputed, substantial, and compatible with the federal-state balance. B: The issue is whether the claim arises under federal law, not the amount requirement. D: A disputed issue is only one requirement. The issue must also be substantial and fit the federal-state balance.
lb_rule_reference: A state-law claim may arise under federal law only when it necessarily raises a disputed and substantial federal issue that a federal court may hear without disrupting the federal-state balance.
