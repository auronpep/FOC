Question: Daniel used a private road across Esther's land to reach his field. Esther never gave permission. But each year, before the growing season ended, Esther locked a gate across the road for six weeks and Daniel did not use the road during that time. The jurisdiction requires continuous adverse use for 10 years, and Daniel has no 10-year period without these annual interruptions. Does Daniel have a prescriptive easement?
Answer A: Yes, because any use repeated over many years is continuous even if the owner blocks it annually.
Answer B: No, because Esther's recurring physical interruptions prevented continuous use for the statutory period.
Answer C: Yes, because Esther's locked gate proves Daniel's use was adverse.
Answer D: No, but only because roads cannot be acquired by prescription.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Prescription requires continuous use for the statutory period. Effective physical interruption by the owner can prevent the prescriptive period from running. Esther's annual locked-gate interruptions kept Daniel from establishing a continuous 10-year period.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because repeated use is not enough if the owner effectively interrupts it. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because adversity alone does not cure lack of continuity. D: D is wrong because roads can be the subject of prescriptive easements.
lb_rule_reference: Owner interruption defeats continuity
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating the clock as cumulative rather than requiring an uninterrupted prescriptive period.
