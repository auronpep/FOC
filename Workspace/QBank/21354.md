Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Noah signed a deed conveying Harboracre to Jonah at a closing table. Noah handed the deed to Jonah and said, “This deed transfers Harboracre to you today. The surveyor still needs to finish the new legal sketch before you record it.” Jonah accepted the deed, but the surveyor never finished the sketch. Who owns Harboracre?
Answer A: Jonah, because Noah's words and handoff showed present intent to transfer title; the unfinished survey was only a later ministerial or recording matter.
Answer B: Noah, because no deed can be delivered until every survey-related recording step is complete.
Answer C: Noah, because title cannot pass until a deed is recorded.
Answer D: Jonah only if the deed stated that consideration had already been paid in full.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is Noah's present intent. Noah stated that the deed transferred Harboracre today and handed it to Jonah. The unfinished survey sketch affected later recording mechanics, not the present delivery of title between Noah and Jonah.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because not every later ministerial step postpones delivery. C: C is wrong because recording is not required for title passage between the parties. D: D is wrong because a consideration recital is not required for deed delivery.
lb_rule_reference: Present transfer words control delivery even when later ministerial steps remain
