Outline: Inadvertent disclosure
Question: A plaintiff answers an interrogatory by identifying two treating physicians. A month later, the plaintiff learns that a third physician treated the same injury and has important records. The defendant has no other notice of the third physician. What must the plaintiff do?
Answer A: Nothing, because discovery responses are judged only when served
Answer B: Timely supplement or correct the response unless the information has otherwise been made known to the defendant
Answer C: Wait until trial and disclose the third physician only if the defendant objects
Answer D: Supplement only if the defendant serves a second interrogatory asking for updates
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The third physician is material and not otherwise known, so supplementation is required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 26(e) creates a duty to supplement or correct materially incomplete or incorrect responses. C: Supplementation must be timely during discovery. D: The supplementation duty is triggered by the party's learning that its response is materially incomplete or incorrect.
lb_rule_reference: A party who has made a disclosure or responded to discovery must supplement or correct it in a timely manner if the party learns the response is materially incomplete or incorrect and the information has not otherwise been made known.
