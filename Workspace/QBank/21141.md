MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A developer used explosives to break rock for a parking garage next to several homes. The developer hired certified blasters and followed industry precautions. A blast nevertheless sent rock fragments through a neighbor's window, injuring the neighbor. The neighbor sues for strict liability based on abnormally dangerous activity. Is the developer likely strictly liable?
Answer A: Yes, because the injury resulted from the abnormal risk of blasting despite reasonable care
Answer B: No, because the developer hired certified blasters and followed industry precautions
Answer C: No, because strict liability applies only to wild animals and products
Answer D: Yes, because strict liability covers every harm occurring anywhere near a blasting site
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Blasting near homes is a classic abnormally dangerous activity, and flying rock fragments are within the abnormal risk that supports strict liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Due care does not defeat activity-based strict liability when the harm flows from the abnormal risk. C: Abnormally dangerous activities are a separate strict-liability lane. D: Liability is limited to harms from the abnormal risk. This injury qualifies, but the rule is not unlimited.
lb_rule_reference: Blasting in an inappropriate context can be abnormally dangerous, and strict liability applies to harm caused by the abnormal blasting risk even when reasonable care is used.
