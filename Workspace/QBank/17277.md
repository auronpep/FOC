Outline: Attorney-client
Question: Abraham received a privileged legal memo about a threatened lawsuit. Hoping Isaac would invest in Abraham's business, Abraham forwarded the memo to Isaac with the note, “This should reassure you that the case is weak.” Isaac had no legal claim or defense in common with Abraham and had no joint defense arrangement. What is the best privilege ruling?
Answer A: The memo remains privileged because Abraham and Isaac had a shared financial interest.
Answer B: The memo remains privileged because Isaac promised to keep it secret.
Answer C: The memo remains privileged because the lawsuit had not yet been filed.
Answer D: The forwarding likely waived privilege because Abraham disclosed the memo to a nonclient investor for a commercial purpose, not a shared legal defense.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Common-interest protection requires a shared legal interest, not merely a shared financial or commercial interest. Abraham used the memo to seek investment, outside the legal-advice circle. The decisive axis is legal interest versus business interest.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is common-interest overclaim. Financial alignment is not enough. B: B is wrong because A confidentiality promise alone does not preserve attorney-client privilege. C: C is wrong because The filing date is not the key. Disclosure outside the privileged legal circle can waive before or after suit is filed.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / common-interest boundary
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is stretching common interest to cover ordinary commercial sharing of legal advice.
