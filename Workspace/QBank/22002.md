


BARMATRIX Q#: 22002
Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: A man rented a room in his friend's house. The man and his friend signed a lease agreement that gave the friend access to all of the house's common areas, such as the kitchen, living room, and bathrooms. After living together for several months, the man got angry at his friend for always leaving his shoes and dirty socks scattered around the living room floor. The man bought a padlock, removed all of his friend's socks and shoes, and locked the living room door. The man said the friend was now banned from entering that room. The friend told the man he was being unreasonable, but the friend continued to live in the rest of the house. However, the friend stopped paying rent until the man “grew up.” The man sued his friend for the unpaid rent. The jurisdiction followed the majority rule. Should the court rule in the man's favor?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the friend is only entitled to a rent abatement.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the friend did not move out.
Answer C: C: No, because the friend was actually evicted.
Answer D: D: No, because the man's actions were unreasonable.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: In the majority of states, a tenant who is actually evicted from a part of the premises but chooses to stay is relieved of the duty to pay rent. Here, the man maliciously banned the friend from a portion of the property the friend was entitled to inhabit in the lease. Consequently, the friend was no longer required to pay the man any rent. A is incorrect because the majority of states find that a tenant is entitled to withhold all rent in this type of situation. B is incorrect because the friend was not required to move out before he could withhold rent. D is incorrect because a court in this situation would not make an inquiry into the reasons behind the landlord's decision.
