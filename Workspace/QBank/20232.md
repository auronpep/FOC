Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Esther signed a deed conveying Vineacre to Daniel as a gift and recorded it with a note saying that she wanted Daniel to own the land immediately. Daniel was unaware of the deed for several months. When he learned of it, he did not object and began paying the property taxes. Did title pass to Daniel?
Answer A: No, because Daniel did not know about the deed on the recording date.
Answer B: No, because a gift deed requires consideration from the grantee.
Answer C: Yes, because Esther's recording showed present delivery intent and Daniel's acceptance of the beneficial deed was presumed and confirmed by his conduct.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Daniel had signed the deed before Esther recorded it.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive locks are delivery by present intent and acceptance. Esther recorded the deed with immediate-transfer intent. Because the deed was beneficial, Daniel's acceptance was presumed absent rejection and later confirmed when he paid taxes, so title passed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because prior awareness is not always required for acceptance of a beneficial deed. B: B is wrong because consideration is not required for deed validity or acceptance. D: D is wrong because the grantee generally need not sign the deed.
lb_rule_reference: Acceptance of a beneficial deed is presumed when the grantee does not reject it
