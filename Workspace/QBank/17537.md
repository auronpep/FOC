


BARMATRIX Q#: 17537
Outline_code: 63030100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: Before the whiskey left the factory, an angry employee added a poison to it that could not have been discovered by reasonable inspection. While the man was driving in a reasonable manner, the poison caused him to die. As a result, the man's car struck a pedestrian, injuring her. If the pedestrian asserts a claim against the factory, the court should find for
Answer A: A: the factory, because the employee deliberately poisoned the liquor before it left the factory.
Answer B: B: the factory, because the pedestrian did not purchase or consume the factory's product.
Answer C: C: the pedestrian, because the liquor contained poison when it left the factory.
Answer D: D: the factory, because the poison could not have been detected by reasonable inspection.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The manufacturer of a product is strictly liable for damage that results from a defect in the product that existed at the time the manufacturer placed that product in the stream of commerce. In this connection, a product is defective if its condition would defeat the expectations of the reasonable consumer. Since a reasonable consumer would not expect liquor to contain poison, the liquor was defective. Since that defect existed when the liquor left the factory, the factory is strictly liable for the pedestrian's injuries.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect because strict liability is imposed regardless of the reason for the existence of the defect. B is incorrect because strict liability is applied for the benefit of any foreseeable plaintiff regardless of whether he or she was a purchaser, consumer, or bystander. D is incorrect because strict liability (i.e., liability without fault) does not depend on the defendant's unreasonable conduct.
