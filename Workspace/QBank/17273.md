Outline: Motion for new trial
Question: A federal civil jury returns a general verdict for the plaintiff. In written answers, the same jury finds that the defendant did not breach any duty and that the plaintiff suffered no legally caused injury. The answers are consistent with each other but inconsistent with the general verdict. Which statement best describes the court's options?
Answer A: The court must always enter judgment on the general verdict and ignore the answers
Answer B: The court may enter judgment according to the answers, send the jury back for further consideration, or order a new trial
Answer C: The court must dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction
Answer D: The court must question each juror individually and then choose the answer most jurors prefer
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: That set of options matches the rule for consistent answers that are inconsistent with the general verdict.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Consistent answers that conflict with the general verdict cannot simply be ignored. C: Verdict inconsistency is a trial-procedure problem, not a jurisdictional defect. D: The rule supplies defined options; it does not require a juror-by-juror preference poll to rewrite the verdict.
lb_rule_reference: When answers to written questions are consistent with each other but inconsistent with the general verdict, the court may approve judgment according to the answers, direct further jury consideration, or order a new trial.
