Question: During a psychotic episode, Bianca attacks a sleeping person with a heavy lamp while believing she is striking a department-store mannequin. She understands that she is swinging a lamp, but she does not understand that the object is a human being. The jurisdiction applies M'Naghten. Is Bianca's insanity claim viable?
Answer A: No, because Bianca knew she was swinging a lamp
Answer B: No, because M'Naghten requires inability to control conduct
Answer C: Yes, because she did not know the nature and quality of the act due to mental disease
Answer D: Yes, because any mistake of fact is an insanity defense
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Bianca's delusion prevented her from understanding that she was striking a human being. That lack of awareness satisfies M'Naghten's nature-and-quality prong, assuming the mental disease caused the delusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The nature-and-quality inquiry asks whether she understood what she was actually doing, including that she was striking a person rather than an object. B: M'Naghten is cognitive, not volitional. Bianca's claim concerns understanding the nature of the act. D: Ordinary factual mistakes are not automatically insanity. This claim works only because the mistake is caused by mental disease and satisfies M'Naghten.
lb_rule_reference: M'Naghten is satisfied if, due to mental disease, the defendant did not know the nature and quality of the act
