MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Res ipsa loquitur
Question: A mall escalator suddenly reversed direction during normal use, throwing a shopper backward. The mall contracted for maintenance but retained responsibility for keeping the escalator open or closed and received all maintenance reports. The shopper was standing normally and did not touch the controls. The shopper sues the mall for negligence and lacks direct proof of the specific mechanical failure. Is res ipsa loquitur likely available in the shopper's negligence claim against the mall?
Answer A: No, because the shopper cannot identify the precise mechanical defect
Answer B: Yes, because the sudden reversal during normal use can support a negligence inference without direct defect proof
Answer C: No, because hiring a maintenance contractor always defeats the mall's responsibility for res ipsa
Answer D: Yes, because res ipsa requires judgment for the shopper once the escalator malfunctions
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Escalators do not ordinarily reverse during normal use absent negligence, the mall probably controlled operational responsibility, and the shopper did not contribute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Res ipsa may be used precisely when direct proof of the specific defect is unavailable but the event ordinarily suggests negligence. C: The mall retained operational responsibility and maintenance reports. Delegating service does not automatically defeat probable responsibility for res ipsa purposes. D: Res ipsa permits an inference of negligence; it does not always require judgment for the plaintiff.
lb_rule_reference: Res ipsa loquitur can allow a negligence inference from a malfunction ordinarily associated with negligence when the defendant probably controlled maintenance and the plaintiff did not cause the event.
