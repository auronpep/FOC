Question: A coastal state limits commercial shellfish licenses for nonresident citizens after documented overharvesting by transient fleets that do not comply with state conservation reporting. The nonresident limit is tied to enforcement costs and is reviewed annually. What is the correct Article IV framework?
Answer A: The law is invalid automatically because it discriminates against nonresidents
Answer B: The law is valid automatically because conservation is important
Answer C: The court should ask whether the state has a substantial reason and whether the nonresident limit substantially relates to it
Answer D: Pike balancing governs because the plaintiffs earn money
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Article IV allows justified nonresident discrimination only when both substantial reason and fit are shown.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The state may justify discrimination with a substantial reason and substantial relationship. B: The state must connect the discrimination to the reason with substantial fit. D: The question frames discrimination against nonresident citizens in a common calling, which triggers the Article IV standard.
lb_rule_reference: A state may discriminate against nonresident citizens regarding a protected privilege only if it has a substantial reason and the discrimination bears a substantial relationship to that reason.
