Question: A plaintiff sues a defendant in federal court for injuries from a car crash. The defendant wants to counterclaim against the plaintiff for an unrelated unpaid personal loan from years earlier. What is the best classification and jurisdiction analysis?
Answer A: The loan claim is compulsory because it is between the same parties
Answer B: The loan claim is permissive and generally needs an independent jurisdictional basis
Answer C: The loan claim is automatically within supplemental jurisdiction because it is a counterclaim
Answer D: The loan claim can be asserted only in state court and never as a federal counterclaim
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The loan dispute is unrelated to the crash. It is permissive under Rule 13(b), and permissive counterclaims generally need independent federal jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 13(a) asks whether the claim arises from the same transaction or occurrence. C: Unrelated permissive counterclaims usually do not form part of the same case or controversy. D: A permissive counterclaim can be asserted in federal court if a valid jurisdictional basis exists.
lb_rule_reference: A counterclaim that does not arise from the same transaction or occurrence is permissive under Rule 13(b) and generally needs independent subject matter jurisdiction.
