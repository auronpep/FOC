Question: In a federal civil case, Hannah offers a document as a business record. During foundation testimony, Hannah's lawyer asks the records custodian, “Isn't it true that this document satisfies every legal requirement for the business-record exception?” The opposing party objects before the custodian answers. What is the best objection?
Answer A: The question improperly asks the witness to decide the legal admissibility issue that belongs to the court.
Answer B: The question is improper only because the document should have been shown to the jury first.
Answer C: The objection should be overruled because every foundation witness decides admissibility.
Answer D: The objection should be sustained only if the case is criminal rather than civil.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The court decides preliminary questions of admissibility. A foundation witness may supply facts, but the witness does not decide whether those facts satisfy the legal requirements for admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because jury-first presentation is not the issue. C: C is wrong because witnesses provide foundation facts; they do not rule on admissibility. D: D is wrong because the judge's admissibility role is not limited to criminal cases.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a) / preliminary fact is judge question
