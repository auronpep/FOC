Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: In a federal crash case, Isaac claims a truck's brakes failed on an icy hill. Isaac offers a video experiment showing a different model truck stopping on a dry, level road in summer conditions. Isaac says the video will help the jury understand braking. The defendant objects under Rule 403. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the video because any experiment involving a truck is relevant.
Answer B: Admit the video because experiments are never subject to Rule 403.
Answer C: Exclude the video only if the defendant proves the experiment was intentionally staged to mislead.
Answer D: Exclude the video if the dissimilar conditions create a substantial risk of misleading the jury that outweighs its probative value.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Experimental evidence can be excluded when dissimilar conditions make it misleading or confusing. A dry, level-road test using a different model may have little probative value on icy-hill brake failure and may mislead the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the experiment must be sufficiently connected to the disputed conditions. B: B is wrong because experiments are subject to Rule 403. C: C is wrong because intent to mislead is not required for exclusion.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats experiment label as self-authenticating probative value.
