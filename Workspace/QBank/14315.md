


BARMATRIX Q#: 14315
Outline_code: 41030402
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Relations Between Federal and State Governments
Outline: Dormant Commerce Clause
Question: After several well-publicized deaths caused by fires in products made from highly flammable fabrics, a state enacted a statute prohibiting "the manufacture or assembly of any product in this state which contains any fabric that has not been tested and approved for flame retardancy by the Zetest Testing Company." The Zetest Testing Company is a privately owned and operated business located in the state. For many years, a fabric mill, located in the state has had its fabrics tested for flame retardancy by a competing testing company, located in a different state. The competitor is a reliable organization that uses a process for testing and approving fabrics for flame retardancy identical in all respects to that used by the Zetest Testing Company. Because the fabric mill wishes to continue to have its fabric tested solely by the competing testing company, the fabric mill files an action in its state court challenging the constitutionality of the statute as applied to its circumstances. In this suit, the court should hold the statute to be
Answer A: A: constitutional, because it is reasonably related to the protection of the reputation of the fabric industry located in the state.
Answer B: B: constitutional, because it is a legitimate means of protecting the safety of the public.
Answer C: C: unconstitutional, because it denies to the fabric mill the equal protection of the laws.
Answer D: D: unconstitutional, because it imposes an unreasonable burden on interstate commerce.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. The court should hold the statute to be unconstitutional in this case because requiring that all fabric be tested by a single company imposes an unreasonable burden on commerce where companies in neighboring states use the same testing methods.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Even if the statute is reasonably related to a legitimate state interest, it unduly interferes with interstate commerce. B is incorrect. As stated above, even if the statute were reasonably related to a legitimate interest, it is unconstitutional because it unduly interferes with interstate commerce. Moreover, the safety of the public would be equally well-served by requiring that a particular process be used to test fabrics rather than that a particular company do the testing. C is incorrect. The statute does not treat the fabric mill differently than any other company. Therefore, there is no equal protection issue.
