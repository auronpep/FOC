Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A climbing harness was designed to be lightweight for long ascents while meeting industry strength standards. A plaintiff argues the harness should have used a steel frame that would have prevented the plaintiff's bruising in a fall, but the steel frame would make the harness too heavy for its intended climbing use and create new fall risks. What is the best design-defect analysis?
Answer A: The product is defective because any design that would have reduced this injury must be adopted
Answer B: The product is not necessarily defective merely because a heavier design would reduce one injury
Answer C: The product has a manufacturing defect because the plaintiff fell while wearing it
Answer D: The manufacturer is immune because climbing is dangerous
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The alternative design must be reasonable. The steel frame would undermine the harness's intended climbing utility and introduce new risks.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Design defect weighs reasonableness, utility, and new risks. A safer-in-one-way alternative may be unreasonable if it destroys product function or creates greater risks. C: There is no fact that this harness departed from specifications. D: Dangerous use context does not create blanket immunity; it informs the design reasonableness analysis.
lb_rule_reference: A proposed alternative design must be reasonable in light of risk, utility, cost, and product function; safer in one respect is not automatically required.
