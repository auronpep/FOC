Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Damages
Question: A catering company had a signed contract to supply a conference. A rival knew of the contract and threatened to report the conference organizer to a regulator on a fabricated safety complaint unless the organizer canceled and hired the rival. The organizer canceled, and the catering company lost the contract profit. Is the catering company likely to prevail on an intentional interference with contract claim against the rival?
Answer A: No, because competitors may always seek each other's customers
Answer B: Yes, because the rival knowingly used improper threats to cause breach of an existing contract
Answer C: No, because interference requires a defamatory statement to the public
Answer D: Yes, but only if the contract was impossible to terminate for any reason
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts show a valid contract, knowledge, intentional improper interference, breach, and damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The privilege does not protect threats based on fabricated regulatory complaints that induce breach of a known contract. C: Interference can be based on threats or other improper means; public defamation is not required. D: The facts show a signed contract and breach; the law does not require absolute nontermination.
lb_rule_reference: Intentional interference with contract requires a valid contract, defendant's knowledge, intentional improper interference causing breach, and damages.
