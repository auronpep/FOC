Question: Hannah signed a deed conveying Hillacre to Samuel, handed it to him, and said, “This land is yours now. Do not record the deed until after I die.” Samuel accepted the deed and kept it. Hannah died a month later, and Samuel then recorded it. Who owned Hillacre immediately after Hannah handed Samuel the deed?
Answer A: Hannah, because a deed cannot be delivered if recording is delayed until after the grantor's death.
Answer B: Hannah, because title cannot pass until the deed is recorded.
Answer C: Samuel, because Hannah's words and handoff showed present intent to transfer title, and recording was only a later notice step.
Answer D: Samuel only if he paid consideration before Hannah died.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is present intent. Hannah said the land was Samuel's now and physically delivered the deed. The instruction delaying recording did not postpone title transfer; recording affects notice and priority, not deed delivery between these parties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because delayed recording is not the same as delayed delivery. B: B is wrong because recording is not required for title to pass between grantor and grantee. D: D is wrong because consideration is not required for deed delivery.
lb_rule_reference: Present transfer words can create delivery even when recording is delayed until after death
