Question: In a federal fraud trial, Daniel refuses to place his hand on a Bible or use religious language. He tells the judge, “I solemnly affirm that I will tell the truth, and I understand I can be punished if I lie.” The prosecutor objects that Daniel cannot testify unless he takes a religious oath. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because a solemn affirmation designed to impress the duty of truthfulness is sufficient.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the Federal Rules require a religious oath.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Daniel explains his religious beliefs to the jury.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if both parties agree to waive the oath requirement.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Before testifying, a witness must give an oath or affirmation in a form designed to impress the duty to testify truthfully. The rule does not require religious language or a Bible oath. Daniel's affirmation is sufficient if it impresses the truth-duty on his conscience.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because an affirmation may substitute for a religious oath. C: C is wrong because the witness need not disclose religious beliefs to the jury. D: D is wrong because the parties do not waive the truth-duty requirement; the affirmation satisfies it.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 603 / oath or affirmation flexible form
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests religious-oath overread and the flexible form of the truth-duty requirement.
