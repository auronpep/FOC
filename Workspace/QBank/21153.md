Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police suspected Jada of storing illegal files on her laptop but lacked a warrant. An officer asked Jada's roommate to "look through the laptop and send us anything suspicious." The roommate did so and found incriminating files. Jada moves to suppress the files. Is the Fourth Amendment implicated?
Answer A: No, because the roommate, not the officer, physically searched the laptop
Answer B: No, because roommates may always search each other's computers
Answer C: Yes, because the roommate acted as a police agent when searching at the officer's request
Answer D: Yes, but only because all laptop searches require warrants even when purely private
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The officer used the roommate to do what police could not do directly without a warrant. That state involvement makes the search attributable to the government.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Police requested and directed the search. The roommate acted as a government agent for Fourth Amendment purposes. B: Access to common areas does not automatically authorize searching another person's laptop, especially at police direction. D: A purely private laptop search would not implicate the Fourth Amendment. The decisive fact is police agency.
lb_rule_reference: A search by a private actor is subject to the Fourth Amendment when the actor is functioning as an instrument or agent of the government.
