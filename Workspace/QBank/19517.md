Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Gideon contracted with Rebecca to provide machines for Gideon's established workshop. Rebecca breached. At the time of contracting, Rebecca knew Gideon would use the machines to fill ordinary workshop orders. After the breach, Gideon's accountant prepared a damages report using preexisting sales records, cancelled orders, and variable-cost data to calculate $25,000 in net lost profits. Rebecca argues the report cannot be used because it was prepared after the breach. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Gideon cannot recover because all proof of lost profits must exist at the time of contracting.
Answer B: Gideon may use post-breach analysis to prove the amount with reasonable certainty, while foreseeability is assessed from what Rebecca had reason to know at contracting.
Answer C: Gideon may recover the $25,000 only if Rebecca acted willfully.
Answer D: Gideon may recover only gross cancelled-order revenue because the accountant's cost analysis came after the breach.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The time-of-contracting rule concerns foreseeability. Evidence created after breach may be used to prove the amount of loss, so long as it relies on reliable data and establishes lost net profits with reasonable certainty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because damages proof need not have been prepared at contract formation. C: C is wrong because willfulness is not a general prerequisite to lost-profit recovery. D: D is wrong because net lost profits require cost analysis; a post-breach report can supply that proof if reliable.
lb_rule_reference: Lost profits / proof developed after breach
