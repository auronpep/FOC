Question: An undercover officer sells powdered sugar to Jules while representing it as cocaine. Jules believes the powder is cocaine and pays for it. The officer arrests Jules before Jules leaves with the package. Jules is charged with attempted possession of cocaine. What is Jules's best impossibility argument?
Answer A: Jules is not guilty because the powder was not actually cocaine
Answer B: Jules is guilty of attempt because he intended to possess cocaine and acted on that belief
Answer C: Jules is not guilty because the seller was an undercover officer
Answer D: Jules is guilty only if he tested the powder before paying
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Jules's intended conduct, if the facts were as he believed, would have been criminal possession of cocaine. The fact that the package was fake does not bar attempt liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Jules believed the powder was cocaine and intended to possess cocaine. The fake substance is factual impossibility, not a defense to attempt. C: This is an attempt charge. The officer's status does not erase Jules's intent and act. D: The prosecution can prove belief and intent without a chemical test by the defendant.
lb_rule_reference: Buying fake contraband from an undercover officer can support attempt liability when the defendant believes the item is contraband and intends to possess it.
