Question: Rachel and Jacob owned adjoining buildings. A recorded agreement gave Rachel “the right to use the existing wall on Jacob's land for support of Rachel's one-story garage.” Rachel later demolished the garage and began constructing a three-story apartment building using the same wall for support. An engineer testified that the new building would impose substantially greater load on the wall. Jacob seeks an injunction. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Rachel may proceed because any support easement allows any future building on the dominant land.
Answer B: Rachel may proceed because the wall is still being used for support.
Answer C: Jacob may object only after the wall collapses.
Answer D: Jacob should prevail because the grant allowed support for a one-story garage, not a materially greater structural burden.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An express support easement is limited by the purpose and burden stated in the grant. A right to use a wall to support a one-story garage does not automatically authorize a three-story apartment building that substantially increases the load.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a support easement is not unlimited future structural support. B: B is wrong because the same general category of use can still exceed scope if the burden materially increases. C: C is wrong because injunctive relief may prevent an unauthorized burden before collapse. D is correct because the grant fixed the supported structure and expected burden.
lb_rule_reference: Support easement / materially increased structural burden
