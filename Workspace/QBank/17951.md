Question: A defendant privately met with an ordained minister in the minister's office and said, “I stole the money, and I need spiritual forgiveness.” The minister was acting as a spiritual adviser, and the conversation was intended to remain confidential. The prosecutor subpoenas the minister to repeat the confession. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Compel the testimony because no privilege can apply unless the statement was made to a lawyer.
Answer B: Compel the testimony because confessions of crime are never privileged.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony because it was a confidential penitential communication to clergy acting as a spiritual adviser.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony only if the defendant was a formal member of the minister's congregation.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The clergy-penitent privilege protects confidential communications made to clergy in a spiritual or penitential capacity. The decisive facts are clergy role, spiritual purpose, and confidentiality. A criminal confession is not automatically stripped of privilege.
