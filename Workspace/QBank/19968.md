Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: An employee sues her employer in federal court under a federal discrimination statute. In the same complaint, she also asserts a state-law claim against the employer for a car accident in the employer's parking lot that occurred three years earlier and involved different witnesses and facts. No diversity exists. Does supplemental jurisdiction cover the car-accident claim?
Answer A: Yes, because the same plaintiff and defendant are already in federal court
Answer B: Yes, because any federal claim lets the plaintiff add all state claims
Answer C: No, because the car accident is unrelated to the discrimination claim and lacks a common nucleus
Answer D: No, because supplemental jurisdiction applies only in diversity cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The unrelated state claim does not share operative facts with the federal employment claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Same parties are not enough without a common nucleus of operative fact. B: Section 1367(a) requires relatedness as part of the same case or controversy. D: Supplemental jurisdiction often applies with federal-question anchor claims too.
lb_rule_reference: A state-law claim does not fall within Section 1367(a) merely because it is asserted by the same plaintiff against the same defendant.
