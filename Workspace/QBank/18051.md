Question: Elijah conveyed an interior parcel to Martha and retained the surrounding land. At severance, the interior parcel had no legal access to a public road except by crossing Elijah's retained land. A neighbor, John, orally told Martha that she could cross his field for now, but he would not sign anything and could stop her at any time. Martha claims an easement by necessity over Elijah's retained land. What is the best result?
Answer A: Martha has no easement by necessity because John's oral permission gives her enough access.
Answer B: Martha may have an easement by necessity because a revocable neighbor license is not secure legal access and the parcel was landlocked at severance.
Answer C: Martha has a prescriptive easement over John's field as soon as John gives permission.
Answer D: Martha has no claim because necessity easements require a preexisting visible road.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Strict necessity focuses on legal access at severance. A neighbor's revocable oral permission is a license, not secure legal access. Because Martha's parcel was landlocked by Elijah's conveyance, she may have an easement by necessity over Elijah's retained land.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because revocable permission from a neighbor is not the same as legal access. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because permission defeats adversity and does not instantly create prescription. D: D is wrong because a preexisting visible road is not required for an easement by necessity.
lb_rule_reference: Strict necessity and revocable permission decoy
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating a revocable license as if it eliminated strict necessity.
