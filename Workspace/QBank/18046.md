Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Esther contracted to produce a public exhibit for Gideon. Before Esther bought display materials, Gideon clearly repudiated. Esther then spent $12,000 buying custom display materials that could not be reused, hoping Gideon would change his mind. Gideon did not. May Esther recover the $12,000 as reliance damages?
Answer A: Yes, because the materials were purchased for the contract.
Answer B: Yes, because reliance damages include all expenditures made before lawsuit.
Answer C: No, because avoidable expenditures incurred after a clear repudiation are generally not recoverable.
Answer D: No, because reliance damages are never available in service contracts.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages protect reasonable expenditures caused by the contract, but a party may not increase damages by incurring avoidable costs after a clear repudiation. Esther's post-repudiation purchase was avoidable and generally unrecoverable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because contract purpose alone does not make post-repudiation spending reasonable. B: B is wrong because the timing of repudiation matters, not merely the timing of the lawsuit. D: D is wrong because reliance damages can be available in service contracts.
