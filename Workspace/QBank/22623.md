MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Equal Protection Clause
Question: A state police department opened up a special unit to go undercover to infiltrate and spy on white supremacist paramilitary groups trying to set up an all- white county within the state. A special unit position came with higher pay and greater authority. An Asian officer applied for the position and was rejected in favor of a less- qualified white candidate. The department admitted that its decision was primarily based on the Asian candidate's race. The Asian candidate sued, arguing that the department violated his constitutional rights. Is the court likely to rule in the Asian candidate's favor?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the department is unlikely to show that its decision is necessary to achieve a compelling state interest.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the department violated the candidate's equal protection rights.
Answer C: C: No, because the department is likely to show that its decision is necessary to achieve a compelling state interest.
Answer D: D: No, because the department is likely to show that its decision is rationally related to a legitimate state interest.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Not all racial qualifications are unlawful. Racial classifications are valid under the Equal Protection Clause if they are necessary to achieve a compelling state interest. Here, an Asian officer would not be able to infiltrate a white supremacist group. Consequently, the racial classification in this situation is constitutional. A and B incorrect because under these circumstances the department is likely to show that the racial discrimination in its decision is necessary to achieve a compelling state interest. D is incorrect because the decision would be subject to strict scrutiny, not rational basis.
