Question: Elizabeth signed a deed conveying Cottageacre to Mary. Elizabeth gave the deed to John and said, “Take this to Mary now. I have conveyed Cottageacre to her.” John delivered the deed to Mary that afternoon. Mary accepted. When did the title route become effective?
Answer A: Only when Mary later recorded the deed.
Answer B: Only when Elizabeth died.
Answer C: Never, because John was Elizabeth's messenger rather than Mary's agent.
Answer D: When the deed was delivered to Mary through John under Elizabeth's present instruction and Mary accepted.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is Elizabeth's present instruction to transfer the instrument to Mary. A grantor may use a messenger to deliver a deed. John's role as Elizabeth's messenger did not defeat delivery because he actually delivered the deed to Mary and Mary accepted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because recording is not required for delivery between the parties. B: B is wrong because Elizabeth intended a present conveyance, not a death transfer. C: C is wrong because delivery through the grantor's messenger can be effective when the deed reaches the grantee.
lb_rule_reference: Delivery may occur through the grantor's messenger when the grantor intends immediate delivery to the grantee
