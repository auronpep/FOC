Question: A cafe customer left the cafe and walked onto a public sidewalk. An unknown passerby, with no prior connection to the cafe and no earlier disturbance there, suddenly shoved the customer and ran away. The cafe had not undertaken sidewalk security and had no control over the passerby. The customer sues the cafe for negligence for failing to prevent the attack. Is the customer likely to establish that the cafe owed a negligence duty to control the passerby?
Answer A: Yes, because businesses must prevent all criminal acts near their entrances
Answer B: No, because the cafe lacked a recognized relationship or undertaking that made this random attack controllable
Answer C: Yes, because the cafe is vicariously liable for anyone who injures a customer nearby
Answer D: No, because no defendant can ever owe a duty for another person's intentional act
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A duty to control another person requires more than a sudden random criminal act. The cafe had no relationship with the attacker and no undertaking or notice that made the risk controllable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The cafe had no relationship with or control over the random passerby, no undertaking, and no prior facts making this attack sufficiently foreseeable and controllable. C: The passerby was not the cafe's employee or agent. The theory is direct duty to control a third person, not vicarious liability. D: Duties can arise for third-party acts through special relationships, negligent entrustment, or undertakings. Those facts are simply absent here.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant generally has no duty to control an unrelated third person absent a special relationship, undertaking, or sufficiently foreseeable and controllable risk.
