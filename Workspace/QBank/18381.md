Question: A newspaper truthfully reported that firefighters rescued a driver from a burning car on a public bridge during rush hour. The article included the driver's name and the fact that he was treated for smoke inhalation. The facts were obtained from the public emergency report. The driver sued for public disclosure of private facts. Is the newspaper likely liable?
Answer A: No, because the truthful rescue facts were newsworthy and tied to a public event
Answer B: Yes, because any medical treatment detail is always private and nonnewsworthy
Answer C: Yes, because truth is required for liability
Answer D: No, but only because the newspaper did not identify the driver
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Public rescue details from a public emergency report are legitimate news, even if personally embarrassing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Newsworthiness can defeat liability when facts relate to a public event. C: Truth is necessary for this tort, but newsworthiness can still bar liability. D: The article did identify the driver; the defense is newsworthiness.
lb_rule_reference: Public disclosure of private facts does not impose liability for truthful facts that are legitimately newsworthy or part of a public event.
