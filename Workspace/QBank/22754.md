Stem / Fact Pattern: A man sued a company in federal court claiming violations of federal civil rights and employment laws. Ten days after service of the last pleading directed to an important fact issue, the man filed a written demand for a jury trial on that issue with the court and served it on the company. Ten days after his demand, the man reconsidered and moved to have his jury demand withdrawn. The company opposed the man’s motion. How should the court rule on the man’s motion?
Answer A: A: Allow the motion, because the man made the original jury demand.
Answer B: B: Allow the motion, because the man made the motion within 21 days of the last pleading directed to the issue.
Answer C: C: Deny the motion, because the man did not request withdrawal within seven days of the jury demand.
Answer D: D: Deny the motion, because the company did not consent to the withdrawal.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Fed. R. Civ. P. 38 requires a party who desires a jury trial on a fact issue to file a written demand with the court and serve it on the opposing party within 14 days after the service of the last pleading directed to that issue. Once a jury demand is made, it cannot be withdrawn unless all parties consent to the withdrawal. Here, since the company opposes withdrawal, the court should deny the man’s motion. A is incorrect because the mere fact that the man made the original jury demand does not give him free rein to withdraw it. B and C are incorrect because the question of whether withdrawal is appropriate is not dictated by when the withdrawal is made.
