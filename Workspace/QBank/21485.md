Question: A diversity case involves a state-law defense. State practice assigns one factual predicate of the defense to the judge. Federal practice strongly favors jury determination of disputed facts in civil actions. No Federal Rule directly answers the allocation issue. Which analysis is best?
Answer A: The state judge-allocation rule automatically controls because it may affect the outcome
Answer B: The federal court should consider twin aims and any strong federal interest in jury allocation
Answer C: Federal practice always wins whenever a jury is mentioned
Answer D: State practice never applies to defenses
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When no Federal Rule directly controls, Byrd-style analysis considers federal interests alongside Erie concerns.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Outcome effect does not end the Erie analysis. C: A federal interest can be strong, but it is not a slogan that skips the analysis. D: State substantive defenses often apply; the issue is allocation of decisionmaker.
lb_rule_reference: Erie balances twin aims with countervailing federal interests when no Federal Rule directly controls.
