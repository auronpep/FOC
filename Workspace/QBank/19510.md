Question: Abraham signed a deed conveying Oliveacre to Isaac. Abraham handed the signed deed to Isaac and said, “Read this over. I am still deciding whether to give you the land, and it is not yours now.” Isaac kept the paper and recorded it the next day. Abraham then denied that title had passed. Who owns Oliveacre?
Answer A: Abraham, because Abraham's words showed no present intent to transfer title when Isaac received the paper.
Answer B: Isaac, because recording the signed deed made the conveyance effective even if Abraham lacked delivery intent.
Answer C: Isaac, because physical possession of the original deed is conclusive delivery.
Answer D: Abraham only if Isaac paid no consideration for Oliveacre.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity lock is delivery by present intent. Abraham expressly said he was still deciding and that Oliveacre was not Isaac's now. Isaac's physical possession and recording could not cure the missing delivery intent, so title did not pass.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because recording is a notice or priority act, not a substitute for delivery. C: C is wrong because physical possession is evidence of delivery but is not conclusive when the grantor's words negate present intent. D: D is wrong because consideration is not the delivery lock.
lb_rule_reference: Physical possession of the deed is not delivery without present intent to transfer title
