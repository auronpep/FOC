Outline: Bias
Question: In a federal robbery prosecution, Abigail testifies that she saw David commit the robbery. On cross-examination, Abigail denies that the prosecutor promised to dismiss pending charges against her if she testifies. David then offers a signed agreement between Abigail and the prosecutor stating that Abigail's charges will be dismissed after she testifies. The prosecutor objects that the agreement is extrinsic impeachment material. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the agreement because extrinsic impeachment evidence is always barred after a witness denies the point on cross-examination.
Answer B: Exclude the agreement because plea and charging negotiations may never be shown to a jury.
Answer C: Admit the agreement to show Abigail's possible bias or motive to testify favorably for the prosecution.
Answer D: Admit the agreement only if Abigail has first been convicted of a crime involving dishonesty.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Evidence that a witness has a deal with the prosecution is classic bias impeachment. Extrinsic evidence offered to show bias is not barred by the rule limiting extrinsic proof of specific conduct offered solely for truthfulness character. The agreement is admissible to show Abigail's motive to favor the prosecution, subject to ordinary limits.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the extrinsic-evidence bar for truthfulness-specific acts does not bar bias evidence. B: B is wrong because the evidence is offered to show bias, not to prove the validity of a plea negotiation. D: D is wrong because bias impeachment does not require a conviction.
lb_rule_reference: Bias impeachment / extrinsic proof of motive to testify
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the use of impeachment material to prove bias and the distinction between bias and truthfulness-character routes.
