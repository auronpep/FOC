Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: An officer checked a court database that showed an active arrest warrant for Lila. The court clerk had failed to remove the warrant after it was quashed the week before. The officer had no reason to know of the clerical error, arrested Lila, and found narcotics during a search incident to arrest. Should the narcotics be suppressed?
Answer A: Yes, because the warrant was invalid when Lila was arrested
Answer B: Yes, because good faith applies only to warrants physically signed by a judge that day
Answer C: No, because the officer reasonably relied on a court clerical record and suppression would not deter police misconduct
Answer D: No, because a quashed warrant remains valid until police personally learn of it
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The error was the court clerk's, not police misconduct, and the officer's reliance was objectively reasonable. Suppression is generally not required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Exclusion depends on deterrence and police culpability. The officer reasonably relied on a court clerical record. B: The doctrine also considers objectively reasonable reliance on records in some settings. D: The warrant was not actually valid. Admission turns on good-faith reliance and deterrence, not continuing validity.
lb_rule_reference: The exclusionary rule is aimed at deterring police misconduct; good-faith reliance on negligent court clerical error may not require suppression.
