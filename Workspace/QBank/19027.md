Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: Iris fired at Nolan intending to kill him. The bullet missed Nolan and grazed Riley, who was standing nearby. Riley survived. The prosecution charged Iris with attempted murder of Riley. Is Iris guilty of attempted murder of Riley?
Answer A: Yes, because Iris intended to kill Nolan and that intent transfers to Riley
Answer B: Yes, because Riley was actually injured by the shot
Answer C: No, because Iris did not intend to kill Riley, though she may be guilty of attempted murder of Nolan
Answer D: No, because factual impossibility is a defense to attempted murder
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Iris specifically intended to kill Nolan and took a substantial step by firing at him, so attempted murder of Nolan is supported. But Riley was an unintended surviving victim. Transferred intent does not supply the specific intent needed for attempted murder of Riley.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Attempted murder requires intent to kill the attempted-murder victim. Because Riley survived, there is no completed homicide to receive transferred intent. B: Injury alone does not establish attempted murder. The prosecution must prove intent to kill Riley, not merely injury to Riley. D: Factual impossibility is not the relevant defense. The problem is the absence of intent to kill Riley.
lb_rule_reference: Attempted murder requires specific intent to kill the person charged as the attempted-murder victim; transferred intent generally does not create attempted murder for an unintended surviving victim.
