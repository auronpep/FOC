Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A lab reagent is sold in 2018. At that time, published studies and industry testing did not reveal a rare neurological risk. A reliable study first identifies the risk in 2024. A scientist injured in 2019 sues for failure to warn about the risk on the 2018 label. What is the manufacturer's strongest defense?
Answer A: The risk was not known or reasonably knowable when the product was sold
Answer B: The manufacturer is liable because later science eventually discovered the risk
Answer C: The manufacturer is liable because all rare risks must be listed before they are discovered
Answer D: The manufacturer is immune because scientific products are never products
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Warning duties generally turn on risks known or reasonably knowable at the relevant time, not pure hindsight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Later discovery alone does not prove the risk was warnable when sold. C: A duty to warn does not require impossible knowledge. D: The issue is knowability, not product status.
lb_rule_reference: A warning claim is weak when the risk was not known or reasonably knowable under scientific knowledge available when the product was sold.
