


BARMATRIX Q#: 22214
Outline_code: 35030203
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Physician-patient
Question: A professor was on trial for criminal battery after having a fight with a staff member in his office. The professor defended himself by claiming that he acted in self- defense when the staff member attacked him. The professor wants to testify that a student had told the professor that the staff member was “psycho” and “wanted to kill someone.” Should the court admit the professor's testimony?
Answer A: A: Yes, because he is defending himself against a criminal charge.
Answer B: B: Yes, because he wants to testify as to what the student told him.
Answer C: C: No, because the statement does not meet the applicable hearsay exception.
Answer D: D: No, because there is no indication that the student is unavailable to testify.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Hearsay is an out- of- court statement offered as proof of the matter asserted. Here, the professor is defending himself by claiming self- defense. In that case, the truth of the statement, that the staff member was a “psycho” and “wanted to kill someone” doesn't matter. What matters is that the professor was told this and likely feared the staff member, making his use of force in defending himself reasonable. Thus, the statement isn't hearsay and is admissible.A is incorrect because the fact that this is a criminal case does not change the analysis. C is incorrect because the professor's testimony isn't hearsay in the first place. D is incorrect because the student's availability does not change the analysis.
