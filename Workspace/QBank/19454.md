MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Elena and Frank conspired to steal cars from a dealership lot and resell them. While Elena waited in the getaway car, Frank broke into the lot's office to disable the alarm. An employee caught Frank in the office, and Frank struck the employee, breaking her arm, before fleeing. Elena did not know Frank was going to enter any building or assault anyone. The jurisdiction follows Pinkerton. Frank is convicted of assault and battery. The prosecutor now charges Elena with the same assault and battery. Is Elena liable for the assault and battery committed by Frank under the Pinkerton doctrine?
Answer A: No, because Elena did not participate in or authorize the assault
Answer B: No, because the assault was outside the scope of the car-theft conspiracy
Answer C: Yes, because the assault was a natural and probable consequence of the conspiracy
Answer D: Yes, and Elena would also be liable for any crimes Frank committed at any time while the conspiracy existed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Pinkerton liability requires that the substantive offense (1) was committed by a co-conspirator, (2) was in furtherance of the conspiracy, and (3) was a natural and probable consequence of the conspiracy. Frank committed assault while attempting to execute the theft plan. Being surprised by an employee and using force to escape is a foreseeable incident of breaking into a secured facility at night. All three Pinkerton elements are satisfied, and Elena is liable despite having no advance knowledge of the assault.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Pinkerton eliminates the personal-participation requirement for co-conspirators. Elena is liable for Frank's substantive crimes if they were in furtherance of the conspiracy and were a natural and probable consequence. The question is foreseeability, not participation. B: The scope of the conspiracy includes not just the exact planned acts but also crimes that are natural and probable consequences of executing the plan. Violence by a co-conspirator to prevent detection during a theft is a reasonably foreseeable consequence of the conspiracy. Courts routinely hold that assaults committed to neutralize witnesses or security threats are within Pinkerton scope. D: Pinkerton is not unlimited. It only covers crimes (1) in furtherance of the conspiracy, (2) that are natural and probable consequences, and (3) committed while the conspiracy is ongoing. A crime Frank committed for personal reasons unrelated to the conspiracy — e.g., a bar fight the same evening — would not be imputed to Elena. The "any crime at any time" formulation is a dangerous overbroad misstatement.
lb_rule_reference: Under Pinkerton v. United States, a conspirator is vicariously liable for any substantive crime committed by a co-conspirator that (1) was in furtherance of the conspiracy, (2) was the natural and probable consequence of the conspiracy, and (3) was committed while the conspiracy was ongoing
