Question: Eve met with her lawyer, Sarah, about a fraud investigation. Eve invited her friend Naomi to sit in the room only for moral support. Naomi was not translating, helping Sarah give legal advice, or acting as a legal assistant. Are Eve's statements during the meeting protected by attorney-client privilege against Naomi's testimony?
Answer A: Yes, because Eve subjectively expected Sarah to keep the meeting confidential.
Answer B: Yes, because Naomi promised not to repeat what she heard.
Answer C: No, because Eve voluntarily disclosed the communications in the presence of an unnecessary third party.
Answer D: No, but only because fraud investigations are never privileged.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege requires confidentiality. Voluntary disclosure in the presence of a friend who is not necessary to legal advice destroys confidentiality for those communications. The decisive axis is unnecessary third-party presence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This misses the objective confidentiality requirement. Eve's expectation of Sarah's silence does not cure disclosure to Naomi. B: B is wrong because A private promise by Naomi is not the same as preserving the evidentiary privilege. D: A is wrong because This misses the objective confidentiality requirement. Eve's expectation of Sarah's silence does not cure disclosure to Naomi. B is wrong because A private promise by Naomi is not the same as preserving the evidentiary privilege.
