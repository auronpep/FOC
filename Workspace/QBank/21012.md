MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Battery
Question: At a courthouse security line, a lawyer became annoyed when a paralegal stepped in front of him. To make the paralegal move, the lawyer deliberately shoved the paralegal's rolling trial bag from behind with his foot. The bag lurched forward, struck the paralegal's calf, and knocked a stack of papers from her hand onto the floor. The paralegal felt the impact but was not hurt. She did not see the lawyer move the bag until after it hit her. The paralegal sues the lawyer for battery. Is the lawyer liable for battery?
Answer A: No, because the lawyer never touched the paralegal with his own body
Answer B: No, because the paralegal suffered no bodily injury
Answer C: Yes, because the lawyer intended offensive contact and offensive contact resulted through the rolling bag
Answer D: Yes, but only because the paralegal was put in apprehension when she later saw what the lawyer had done
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The lawyer deliberately shoved the bag to make the paralegal move, so he intended at least an offensive contact or an imminent apprehension of such contact. The bag then struck the paralegal's calf. Battery can occur through indirect contact, and bodily injury is not required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Battery can be committed through an object or other indirect force. The relevant contact is the harmful or offensive contact with the plaintiff's person, and the bag struck the paralegal's calf because the lawyer intentionally moved it. B: Battery does not require bodily injury. Offensive contact is enough if it would offend a reasonable sense of personal dignity. A deliberately caused impact to the leg in a courthouse line can satisfy the contact element even if no physical harm results. D: Battery requires harmful or offensive contact to result; apprehension alone would point toward assault, not battery. Here the lawyer is liable because the bag actually struck the paralegal. Her later realization is not the contact element and is not needed for liability.
lb_rule_reference: Battery requires intent to cause harmful or offensive contact, or apprehension of such contact, and harmful or offensive contact results; bodily injury is not required.
