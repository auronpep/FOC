Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Noah owned a cabin bordered on one side by Adam and on the other side by Eve. Noah signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey my cabin to my neighbor.” The deed did not name Adam or Eve and gave no other way to identify which neighbor was meant. Noah privately meant Adam. Who owns the cabin?
Answer A: Adam, because Noah privately intended to convey the cabin to him.
Answer B: Noah, because the deed does not identify an ascertainable grantee.
Answer C: Adam and Eve as equal tenants in common, because both were neighbors.
Answer D: Whichever neighbor records the deed first.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantee identity. A grantee must be identified or reasonably ascertainable from the instrument and admissible identifying facts. Because “my neighbor” could mean either Adam or Eve and no selection method appears, Noah's private intent cannot cure the defect, so title remains with Noah.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because private intent alone does not identify the grantee. C: C is wrong because the deed did not convey to both neighbors. D: D is wrong because recording cannot determine the grantee of an otherwise indefinite deed.
