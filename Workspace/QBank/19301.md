Question: A state prohibits dentists from advertising that a whitening treatment "permanently prevents all future staining" when clinical evidence shows the claim is false. A dentist argues the ban violates the First Amendment because advertising is speech. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The ban is invalid because all commercial advertising receives strict scrutiny
Answer B: The ban is valid because false or misleading commercial claims may be banned
Answer C: The ban is invalid because commercial speech is never regulable
Answer D: The ban needs no First Amendment analysis because commercial speech is entirely unprotected
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The state may prohibit false commercial claims without treating them as protected truthful advertising.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Commercial speech has its own framework, and false claims may be prohibited. C: False or misleading commercial speech is regulable. D: Truthful, nonmisleading commercial speech is protected, even though false claims may be banned.
lb_rule_reference: False or misleading commercial speech, or commercial speech proposing unlawful activity, may be prohibited without the protection given to truthful nonmisleading commercial speech.
