MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: Mara believes it is a felony to import a certain herbal tea without a federal license. In fact, no law restricts importing that tea. Mara orders the tea from abroad, intending to violate what she thinks is the licensing law. Federal agents intercept the package and charge her with attempted illegal importation. What is Mara's best defense?
Answer A: She is guilty because she believed she was committing a felony
Answer B: She is not guilty because the conduct she intended to complete would not violate any criminal law
Answer C: She is guilty because federal agents intercepted the package before delivery
Answer D: She is not guilty only because she used the mail rather than carrying the tea herself
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Importing the tea without a license is not a crime. Mara intended conduct that the law does not prohibit, so this is pure legal impossibility rather than factual impossibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Attempt punishes intent to commit an actual crime. If the completed conduct would not be criminal under any law, pure legal impossibility can defeat the attempt. C: The interception is not decisive. Even if the package arrived, Mara would not have committed a crime. D: The method of importation does not matter when there is no criminal prohibition at all.
lb_rule_reference: Pure legal impossibility can defeat attempt when the defendant's completed intended conduct would not be a crime, even if the defendant believed it was unlawful.
