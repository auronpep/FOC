Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Trial judge’s discretion to control order and presentation of evidence
Question: In a federal arson trial, the prosecutor wants to call Daniel, a chemist, before calling the fire investigator who collected the samples Daniel tested. Daniel is leaving the country that evening. The prosecutor represents that the investigator will testify later that the samples came from the burned building. The defendant objects that the prosecutor has not yet connected Daniel's tests to the fire scene. How should the court rule?
Answer A: The court may allow Daniel to testify subject to later connection if that order is reasonable and does not unfairly prejudice the defendant.
Answer B: The court must exclude Daniel permanently because foundation has not already been proved.
Answer C: The court must allow Daniel because witness availability always overrides foundation objections.
Answer D: The court must let the jury decide whether the samples are connected without any court control over the order of proof.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A judge may control the order of proof and allow testimony subject to later connection when reasonable. The court may require the promised foundation later and take appropriate action if the connection is not supplied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because foundation need not always precede every related witness. C: C is wrong because availability does not automatically override foundation; the judge controls the order. D: D is wrong because the judge still manages the proof sequence and admissibility foundation.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / out-of-order foundation subject to connection
