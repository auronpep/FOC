Question: Mira and Jon robbed a jewelry store at gunpoint. As they ran to the getaway car, Jon fired toward a store employee who was chasing them. The shot missed the employee but killed a pedestrian. Mira did not fire any weapon and did not know Jon would shoot. Under the common-law agency theory, is Mira guilty of felony murder for the pedestrian's death?
Answer A: No, because Mira did not personally fire the fatal shot
Answer B: No, because Jon intended to shoot the employee, not the pedestrian
Answer C: Yes, because a co-felon killed a bystander during immediate flight from an armed robbery
Answer D: Yes, but only if Mira specifically ordered Jon to shoot
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Robbery is a qualifying felony. Jon, a co-felon, fired while escaping and before the robbers reached a place of safety. Under the agency theory, the killing is attributable to participants in the robbery, including Mira.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Felony murder can impose liability on felony participants for killings committed by co-felons in furtherance of the felony during the transaction or immediate flight. B: Felony murder does not require intent to kill the particular victim. A death caused by a co-felon during immediate flight from robbery is within the doctrine. D: Felony murder does not require proof that Mira ordered the killing. Participation in the qualifying felony plus the co-felon's in-furtherance killing during flight is enough.
lb_rule_reference: Under the agency theory, felony murder can attach when a co-felon, acting in furtherance of the felony, causes a death during the felony transaction or immediate flight.
