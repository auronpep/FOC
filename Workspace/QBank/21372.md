Question: Congress enacts a statute expressly allowing states to limit shipment of a particular hazardous material into their territory if the state files a safety plan. A state files the plan and adopts the shipment limit. A carrier challenges the state limit solely under the Dormant Commerce Clause. What is the best response?
Answer A: The DCC still invalidates the law because states may never burden interstate shipments
Answer B: The state law is insulated from the DCC challenge because Congress clearly authorized this burden
Answer C: The law is valid only if the state is a market participant
Answer D: The law is invalid because hazardous-material regulation is exclusively federal
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The dormant limit is a default inferred from congressional silence; clear congressional authorization changes the analysis.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Congress can authorize state burdens that would otherwise violate the dormant limit. C: The facts supply clear congressional authorization, not state participation in a market. D: The stated federal statute expressly permits state limits under filed plans.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may authorize state laws that would otherwise burden interstate commerce, but the authorization must be clear.
