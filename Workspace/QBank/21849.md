Question: Noah owned North Lot and South Lot. Noah conveyed South Lot to Isaac by deed. The deed stated, “Noah reserves, for the benefit of North Lot, a right to cross the existing lane on South Lot to reach the public road.” Noah retained North Lot. Isaac argues that the deed gave him South Lot free of any easement because an owner cannot reserve a right in land being conveyed. What is the best result?
Answer A: The deed created a reserved easement appurtenant benefiting North Lot and burdening South Lot.
Answer B: The reservation is invalid because a grantor can never reserve an easement in land conveyed to another.
Answer C: The reservation created only a personal license that ends whenever Noah sells North Lot.
Answer D: The reservation is invalid because it was included in the deed rather than in a separate easement agreement.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A grantor may reserve an easement over the conveyed parcel for the benefit of retained land. The deed identifies North Lot as the benefited dominant estate, South Lot as the burdened servient estate, and the existing lane as the route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because reservations commonly create easements in favor of retained land. C: C is wrong because the deed ties the benefit to North Lot, making the easement appurtenant rather than merely personal. D: D is wrong because the reservation may be created in the conveyance deed itself.
lb_rule_reference: Reservation of easement for retained land
