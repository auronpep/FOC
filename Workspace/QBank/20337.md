Question: In a federal accounting trial, Joseph offers a chart summarizing 4,000 invoices. The invoices are admissible business records and were made available to Mary for inspection two months before trial. Mary objects that the chart itself cannot be evidence unless each invoice is separately admitted first. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a summary chart is never evidence.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless all 4,000 invoices are read to the jury.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because a proper summary of voluminous admissible records may itself be admitted as evidence when the underlying materials are available.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Mary stipulates that the calculations are accurate.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 1006 permits a summary, chart, or calculation to prove the contents of voluminous admissible writings that cannot be conveniently examined in court, provided the underlying materials are made available.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 1006 summaries can be evidence. B defeats the purpose of the summary rule. B: A is wrong because Rule 1006 summaries can be evidence. B defeats the purpose of the summary rule. D is wrong because accuracy can be tested without a stipulation. D: D is wrong because accuracy can be tested without a stipulation.
