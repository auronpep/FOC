Question: A state agency administers a construction contracting program that sets aside 30% of all agency contracts for minority-owned businesses. The agency's justification is that minority-owned construction firms face systemic discrimination in the construction industry and in society generally, resulting in underrepresentation in government contracts. The agency's program was adopted without any study of prior discrimination by the agency itself in its own contracting history. A non-minority-owned construction company that was denied a contract as a result of the set-aside challenges the program under the Equal Protection Clause. Under Equal Protection strict scrutiny, which analysis most accurately addresses the set-aside program's constitutionality?
Answer A: The program is constitutional because remedying general societal discrimination against minority-owned businesses is a compelling government interest sufficient to justify a race-conscious contracting program
Answer B: The program is constitutional because a 30% set-aside is narrowly tailored — it does not fully exclude non-minority firms from all contracts, and it uses a percentage rather than an absolute bar
Answer C: The program fails strict scrutiny because: (1) remedying general societal discrimination is not a compelling interest unless tied to specific identified past discrimination by the agency itself; and (2) the program is not narrowly tailored given the absence of any prior-discrimination study
Answer D: The program fails strict scrutiny because any race-conscious government contracting program is per se unconstitutional after recent Supreme Court decisions
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Croson and Adarand, a race-conscious government contracting program must survive strict scrutiny. Remedying general societal discrimination is not a compelling interest; the government must identify specific, documented discrimination in its own contracting programs. And without prior-discrimination findings, the program cannot be narrowly tailored — it lacks the necessary predicate that would limit its scope and duration. The program fails on both prongs.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Supreme Court has held that remedying general societal discrimination — as opposed to specific identified prior discrimination by the government entity itself — is not a sufficiently compelling interest to justify a race-conscious set-aside. The government must identify specific discrimination in its own programs or in the relevant market sector, not just point to general societal patterns. B: Narrow tailoring under strict scrutiny requires more than not being a total bar. The program must be tied to identified discrimination, must be no broader than necessary, must include waiver provisions for when the goal cannot be met, and must be time-limited. A blanket 30% set-aside without prior-discrimination findings is not narrowly tailored regardless of the percentage. D: The Supreme Court has not held that all race-conscious government contracting programs are per se unconstitutional. A narrowly tailored program tied to identified specific prior discrimination by the government entity could theoretically survive strict scrutiny. The constitutional problem here is the specific program's failure to satisfy the two-pronged test — not a categorical prohibition.
lb_rule_reference: A government contracting set-aside program that reserves a percentage of contracts for minority-owned businesses must be narrowly tailored — tied to identified, specific prior discrimination by the government entity and no more expansive than necessary — to survive strict scrutiny.
