Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A utility company operates ordinary high-voltage distribution lines along public streets. A homeowner alleges that electricity is dangerous and sues the utility only on a strict-liability theory after a line falls during a storm and damages a fence. What is the utility's strongest response to abnormally dangerous activity strict liability?
Answer A: Electricity is dangerous, so the utility is strictly liable for any harm involving a power line
Answer B: Ordinary utility distribution is not automatically an abnormally dangerous activity merely because electricity is dangerous
Answer C: The utility is strictly liable only if the homeowner proves a prior outage
Answer D: The utility avoids all tort liability because the line fell during a storm
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The activity is common and its risks are generally addressed by care standards. Strict liability requires more than danger alone.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Dangerous does not automatically mean abnormally dangerous. Common utility service is usually evaluated under negligence or other rules, not automatic strict liability. C: Prior-outage knowledge is not the abnormally-dangerous-activity test. D: The question asks only strict liability. The utility may have other liability issues depending on facts; strict liability is not automatic.
lb_rule_reference: A dangerous activity is not automatically abnormally dangerous when it is common, socially ordinary, and its risks can be addressed through reasonable care.
