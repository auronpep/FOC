Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: Simone and her neighbor Dennis had been feuding for years over property boundaries. One afternoon, Dennis walked onto what Simone considered her property and began trimming a hedge. Simone became furious, ran inside, grabbed her hunting rifle, ran back outside, and immediately shot Dennis twice in the chest, killing him. The confrontation lasted approximately ninety seconds from start to finish. Dennis had made no threatening gesture and said nothing before being shot. What is the most serious homicide offense for which Simone can be convicted?
Answer A: First-degree murder, because Simone retrieved a weapon before shooting, establishing premeditation
Answer B: Voluntary manslaughter, because Dennis's trespass constituted adequate provocation
Answer C: Second-degree murder, because Simone intended to kill but did not premeditate and the provocation was legally insufficient for voluntary manslaughter
Answer D: No homicide, because Simone reasonably believed Dennis was violating her property rights and she had the right to defend her property
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Simone intentionally shot Dennis twice with a rifle — clear intent to kill. But the brief, frenzied ninety-second confrontation, driven by rage rather than cool reflection, does not satisfy premeditation and deliberation for first-degree murder. And Dennis's trespass (hedge trimming) is legally insufficient provocation for voluntary manslaughter — a reasonable person would not kill over a property boundary dispute. The result is second-degree murder: an intentional killing without premeditation and without adequate provocation to reduce to manslaughter.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Premeditation requires prior deliberation about the killing — a decision formed after a period of cool reflection. Simone acted in approximately ninety seconds, in an escalating rage, in direct response to a trespass occurring at that moment. The very brief time gap and the heat of the confrontation undermine cool reflection. While this is a close call, most courts would hold that ninety seconds is insufficient for premeditation under these facts, and the frenzy of the moment also undermines deliberation. B: Adequate provocation requires conduct sufficient to inflame a reasonable person to the point of losing self-control. A neighbor trimming a hedge — even on disputed property — is a classic example of legally insufficient provocation. A reasonable person would not be provoked to kill by this conduct. The voluntary manslaughter reduction is unavailable. D: Defense of property never justifies the use of deadly force to protect mere property interests. Deadly force may only be used in self-defense or defense of others when a person reasonably fears imminent death or serious bodily harm. Hedge-trimming by a trespasser poses no threat of death or serious injury, so no defense-of-property justification is available.
lb_rule_reference: Second-degree murder covers intentional killings that are not premeditated; the presence of an intent to kill, without adequate provocation and without the cooling-off period issue, places the offense at second-degree murder, not voluntary manslaughter
