Question: A store negligently left a slick spill in an aisle. A customer slipped, broke an ankle, and missed three weeks of work during recovery. The store argues that lost wages are pure economic loss and therefore cannot be recovered in negligence. How should the court treat the lost-wage claim?
Answer A: Bar it, because all financial losses are pure economic losses
Answer B: Bar it unless the customer also proves a contract with the store
Answer C: Allow it if the missed work was caused by the ankle injury
Answer D: Allow it only as nominal damages
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Lost wages are recoverable economic consequences of the store's tort-caused physical injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Lost wages tied to a physical injury are parasitic economic damages, not standalone pure economic loss. B: Tort-caused physical injury can support lost wages without a contract. D: Proven lost wages flowing from physical injury are compensatory damages.
lb_rule_reference: Economic consequences such as lost wages are recoverable when they flow from tort-caused personal injury.
