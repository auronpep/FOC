MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A neighbor learned that a resident had received treatment for depression. The neighbor told two close friends in a private text thread and asked them not to repeat it. The information was true, private, and embarrassing. The friends did not share it further. Is the neighbor likely liable for public disclosure of private facts?
Answer A: No, because the disclosure was not public enough to satisfy the publicity requirement
Answer B: Yes, because any disclosure to one person is enough
Answer C: No, because truth is a complete defense to private-facts disclosure
Answer D: Yes, because depression treatment is always newsworthy
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A small private disclosure, without further spread, usually is not publicity for this privacy tort.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Defamation publication and private-facts publicity are different standards. C: The tort concerns truthful private facts; truth does not defeat it. D: The issue is publicity, and the facts do not make the private treatment newsworthy.
lb_rule_reference: Public disclosure of private facts requires publicity to the public or to enough people that the matter is substantially certain to become public; disclosure to a small private circle is usually insufficient.
