Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Service of Process
Question: Defendant's first Rule 12 motion argues only insufficient service of process under Rule 12(b)(5). The motion does not mention lack of personal jurisdiction, even though the PJ facts were known. After the court allows Plaintiff to cure service, Defendant files a new motion under Rule 12(b)(2) for lack of personal jurisdiction. How should the court treat the Rule 12(b)(2) motion?
Answer A: Grant it because any service objection automatically preserves personal jurisdiction
Answer B: Deny it because Defendant waived the PJ objection by omitting it from the first Rule 12 motion
Answer C: Grant it because personal jurisdiction can be raised at any time like subject-matter jurisdiction
Answer D: Deny it only if venue is also proper
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Defendant preserved service but not personal jurisdiction. The later 12(b)(2) motion is waived if the defense was available earlier.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: They are separate waivable defenses. The available PJ defense had to be included in the first Rule 12 motion. C: Personal jurisdiction is waivable and subject to Rule 12(h)(1). D: The waiver analysis does not depend on venue.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant must timely assert each available Rule 12(b)(2), 12(b)(4), and 12(b)(5) defense; preserving a service objection does not automatically preserve a personal-jurisdiction objection.
