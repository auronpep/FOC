MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A driver negligently crashed into a bicyclist, leaving the bicyclist lying in a traffic lane. A passerby ran into the lane to pull the bicyclist to safety and was struck by a slowly moving car whose driver could not stop in time. The original driver argues that the passerby's rescue attempt superseded liability. Is the rescue attempt likely superseding?
Answer A: Yes, because rescuers choose to get involved
Answer B: Yes, because any second car breaks the causal chain
Answer C: No, because a rescue attempt in response to roadway peril is foreseeable
Answer D: No, because rescuers always recover even when their acts are reckless
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The original crash created peril in a traffic lane. A passerby rescue and traffic-related injury are within the foreseeable rescue chain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rescue is a foreseeable response to peril created by negligence. Ordinary rescue efforts generally do not supersede. B: Intervening acts do not automatically supersede. Traffic danger during a road rescue is foreseeable. D: Rescue is generally foreseeable, but reckless or bizarre rescue can exceed the scope. These facts show ordinary rescue.
lb_rule_reference: Rescue efforts are generally foreseeable when the defendant's negligence places another in peril, unless the rescue is reckless or bizarre.
