Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A new driver with a learner's permit drove in light rain on a city street. Because she was nervous, she followed another car at one car length while traveling thirty miles per hour. When the car ahead stopped at a crosswalk, the new driver rear-ended it. The injured front-seat passenger sues the new driver for negligence. What standard of care applies to the passenger's negligence claim against the new driver?
Answer A: The standard is what a reasonably careful beginner driver would have done
Answer B: The standard is what a reasonably prudent driver would have done under similar conditions
Answer C: The standard is the driver's own best effort given her nervousness
Answer D: The standard is strict liability because new drivers are especially dangerous
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The driver engaged in an ordinary adult activity and receives no lower standard for being new or nervous. Rain and traffic conditions inform the objective standard.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Inexperience generally does not lower the negligence standard. Drivers are held to the objective standard of a reasonably prudent person under the circumstances. C: Negligence does not ask whether the defendant personally tried hard. It uses an objective standard unless a recognized adjustment applies. D: The claim is negligence, and the applicable standard is objective reasonable care, not strict liability.
lb_rule_reference: Inexperience generally does not lower the objective reasonable-person standard in negligence.
