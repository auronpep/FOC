Question: In a federal negligence trial, Sarah calls her friend Rachel to testify about a collision. On direct examination, before Rachel has described what she saw, Sarah's lawyer asks, “Abraham was driving too fast when he hit you, wasn't he?” Abraham objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the question suggests the desired answer on direct examination.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because leading questions are never permitted in federal court.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because a witness may always be led when the question concerns speed.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because Rachel is Sarah's witness and Sarah may question her in any form she chooses.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Leading questions generally should not be used on direct examination unless needed to develop the witness's testimony. This question supplies the conclusion counsel wants Rachel to adopt, and the facts do not show a hostile, adverse, child, confused, or preliminary witness reason for leading.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because leading questions are sometimes permitted, including ordinarily on cross and when needed to develop testimony. C: C is wrong because speed does not create a special license to lead on direct. D: D is wrong because the party calling a witness does not control question form absolutely.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(c) / leading on direct
