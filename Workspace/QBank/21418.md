Question: In a federal assault trial, Samuel testifies that he has no relationship with the victim, David. The defense offers a lease signed by Samuel and David showing that they have lived together for six years. The prosecution objects that the lease is extrinsic evidence of a specific act and cannot be used to attack Samuel's truthfulness. The defense offers the lease to show Samuel's bias in favor of David, not merely to show Samuel is generally untruthful. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the lease because Rule 608(b) bars all extrinsic evidence that affects credibility.
Answer B: Exclude the lease because bias can be shown only by cross-examination, never by a document.
Answer C: Admit the lease because the extrinsic-evidence bar for truthfulness-specific acts does not bar otherwise admissible extrinsic evidence offered to show bias.
Answer D: Admit the lease only if Samuel has first testified to David's character for truthfulness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 608(b)'s extrinsic-evidence bar applies when the evidence is offered solely to prove specific conduct to attack or support character for truthfulness. It does not bar extrinsic evidence offered for another impeachment purpose, such as bias. The lease is offered to show Samuel's relationship with David and motive to favor him.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 608(b) is not a blanket ban on all extrinsic credibility evidence. B: B is wrong because bias may be proved with extrinsic evidence, subject to ordinary limits. D: D is wrong because the item is about Samuel's bias, not character-for-truthfulness testimony.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(b) limitation / extrinsic bar does not cover bias, contradiction, or capacity when independently relevant
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the scope of Rule 608(b) and separates truthfulness-character impeachment from bias impeachment.
