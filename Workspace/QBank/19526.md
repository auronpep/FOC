Outline: Intermediate scrutiny (quasi-suspect) classifications
Question: A state fitness academy for emergency responders admits women under a lower physical standard than men. The state says it wants to increase female participation, but it has no evidence that the lower standard measures job-related ability or that the different standard is substantially related to responder safety. What is the best Equal Protection analysis?
Answer A: The policy receives rational basis because physical fitness is not a fundamental right
Answer B: The policy survives automatically because physical differences between sexes exist
Answer C: Intermediate scrutiny applies, requiring an important objective and a substantially related means
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies because all classifications involving emergency services are suspect
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A sex-based standard must be justified with an exceedingly persuasive explanation and fit, not broad participation goals alone.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The policy classifies by sex, so intermediate scrutiny applies. B: Real differences do not eliminate the need for a substantial relation to an important objective. D: Importance of the field does not convert sex classifications into strict scrutiny.
lb_rule_reference: Under intermediate scrutiny, the government must show an important objective and means substantially related to that objective; invoking a real difference does not end the tailoring inquiry.
