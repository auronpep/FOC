Question: Hector aimed a pistol at Victor intending to kill him. Victor ducked as Hector fired, and the bullet struck and killed Paula, who was standing behind Victor. Hector was charged with murder for Paula's death. Is Hector guilty of murder for Paula's death?
Answer A: No, because Hector intended to kill Victor, not Paula
Answer B: No, because transferred intent applies only to battery, not homicide
Answer C: Yes, because his intent to kill Victor transfers to Paula's completed homicide
Answer D: Yes, but only of involuntary manslaughter because Paula's death was accidental
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Hector purposely fired at Victor intending to kill him. The bullet instead killed Paula. For completed homicide, the intent transfers from the intended victim to the actual victim, so Hector is guilty of murder.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intent to kill transfers for completed homicide. Hector's intent to kill Victor supplies the malice for Paula's death. B: Transferred intent is a classic rule for completed homicide as well as other completed result crimes. D: The doctrine transfers Hector's murderous intent to the actual death. The accidental identity of the victim does not reduce the homicide to manslaughter.
lb_rule_reference: For completed homicide, intent to kill the intended victim can transfer to an unintended victim who is actually killed.
