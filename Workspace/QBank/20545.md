Question: A state inheritance statute allows marital children to inherit from their fathers automatically but bars nonmarital children from inheriting unless a paternity order was entered before the father's death. A nonmarital child has clear DNA proof of paternity but no pre-death order. What Equal Protection tier applies to the classification?
Answer A: Rational basis, because inheritance is property regulation
Answer B: Intermediate scrutiny, requiring a substantial relation to an important interest
Answer C: Strict scrutiny, because all family classifications are suspect
Answer D: No scrutiny, because children cannot challenge inheritance statutes
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The statute burdens nonmarital children as a class, so the state must justify the fit between the rule and interests such as orderly estate administration or proof reliability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Nonmarital-birth classifications generally receive intermediate scrutiny. C: Nonmarital-birth classifications use intermediate scrutiny, not strict scrutiny. D: A child denied inheritance by state law may challenge the classification.
lb_rule_reference: Classifications based on nonmarital birth generally receive intermediate scrutiny and must be substantially related to an important governmental interest.
