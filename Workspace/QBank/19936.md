Outline: Nonresponsive answers
Question: Before Deborah answers any question in a federal trespass trial, Joshua says, “I move to strike any nonresponsive answer Deborah may give.” The judge has not heard Deborah's answer and has no reason to think she will volunteer inadmissible information. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the motion as premature, while retaining authority to control the witness and strike any improper answer if one occurs.
Answer B: Grant the motion because counsel may strike answers before they are given.
Answer C: Deny the motion because nonresponsive answers are never subject to court control.
Answer D: Grant the motion only if Deborah is a hostile witness.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A motion to strike addresses evidence after it is given. Before any answer exists, the motion is premature. The court can still instruct the witness, control examination, and strike an improper answer if it occurs.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because there is nothing yet to strike. C: C is wrong because nonresponsive answers can be controlled or stricken after they occur. D: D is wrong because hostility does not determine whether a premature motion to strike is proper.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(1); FRE 611(a) / premature motion to strike
