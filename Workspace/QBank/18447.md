Question: Nico forged a signature on a bank form, a felony in the jurisdiction. As he hurried out of the bank lobby, an elderly customer tripped over her own cane, fell, and died from a head injury. Nico never touched her and did not create a crowd or disturbance. Can Nico be convicted of felony murder based on the forgery?
Answer A: Yes, because forgery is a felony and a death occurred nearby
Answer B: Yes, because any death during flight from a felony is felony murder
Answer C: No, because forgery is not a qualifying dangerous felony and the death was not caused by a felony danger
Answer D: No, because felony murder requires intent to kill
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A felony label is insufficient. Forgery is not an inherently dangerous felony in this setting, and the customer's fall did not result from a danger created by a qualifying predicate. Felony murder is not supported.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Felony murder does not apply to every felony near a death. Forgery is not the kind of inherently dangerous predicate that supplies malice here. B: The phase inquiry matters only after a qualifying predicate exists. The forgery predicate fails. D: Felony murder does not require intent to kill. The real defect is the predicate felony and causation.
lb_rule_reference: Felony murder requires a qualifying inherently dangerous or enumerated felony; a felony label alone is not enough.
