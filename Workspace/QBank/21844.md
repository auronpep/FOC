Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Caleb prepared a one-page deed. At the top it read, “I, Caleb, convey my shop at 6 Damascus Road to Joshua,” and Caleb wrote his signature directly under that sentence. The rest of the page contained the legal description and delivery instructions. Caleb delivered the deed to Joshua intending an immediate transfer, and Joshua accepted. Caleb later argued that the deed failed because his signature was not at the bottom of the page. Who owns the shop?
Answer A: Caleb, because a deed signature is valid only if it appears after every word of the instrument.
Answer B: Caleb, because delivery instructions after the signature automatically revoke the conveyance.
Answer C: Joshua only if Caleb signs again at the bottom.
Answer D: Joshua, because Caleb signed the instrument with intent to authenticate the conveyance.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantor authentication, not mechanical signature placement. Caleb signed the instrument with intent to authenticate the conveyance, and the deed otherwise identified the parties, land, and present transfer. The signature's location does not defeat validity on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the validity lock is authentication intent, not a universal bottom-of-page rule. B: B is wrong because delivery instructions after the signature do not automatically revoke an otherwise present conveyance. C: C is wrong because a second signature is unnecessary when the original signature authenticates the deed.
