Question: In a federal assault trial, Isaac requests exclusion of witnesses. Rebekah, a nonparty witness, is married to Isaac and will testify about what she saw. Rebekah has no statutory right to attend and has not been designated as any party's representative. Isaac argues that she may remain because she is his spouse. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Allow Rebekah to remain because spouses of parties are automatically exempt from sequestration.
Answer B: Allow Rebekah to remain only if the opposing party agrees.
Answer C: Exclude Rebekah unless she fits a Rule 615 exception, because being a spouse of a party does not by itself exempt a nonparty witness.
Answer D: Exclude Rebekah only if Isaac first proves she intends to tailor her testimony.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 615 contains specific exceptions. A nonparty witness is not exempt merely because she is married to a party. Without a statutory right, representative designation, or essential-person showing, she may be excluded.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A invents a spouse exception. B gives the opponent unnecessary control over a rule-based ruling. D imposes a tailoring-intent requirement the rule does not require.
