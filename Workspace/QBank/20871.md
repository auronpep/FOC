Question: Deborah agreed to buy Elijah's workshop if she obtained a loan by July 1. The condition protected Deborah. Deborah decided she wanted a different property and made no loan application, even though several banks were available. On July 1, she told Elijah the financing condition failed and refused to close. What is Elijah's strongest argument?
Answer A: Deborah cannot rely on the condition because she failed to make reasonable good-faith efforts to obtain the financing.
Answer B: The condition failed automatically because no loan was obtained, so Deborah is discharged in all events.
Answer C: Deborah's duty arose only if Elijah obtained the loan for her.
Answer D: The financing condition is invalid because conditions involving third parties are never enforceable.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A party whose duty is conditioned on an event may have an implied obligation to make reasonable good-faith efforts to cause the event when the contract and circumstances require it. Deborah cannot deliberately avoid applying for financing and then rely on nonoccurrence to escape.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because nonoccurrence is not absolute when the protected party failed required efforts. C: C is wrong because the buyer, not the seller, was positioned to seek her financing. D: D is wrong because third-party financing conditions are common and enforceable.
lb_rule_reference: Condition nonoccurrence caused by failure of efforts
