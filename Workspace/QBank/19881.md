Outline: Motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law
Question: During trial, a defendant properly moves for JMOL on causation after the plaintiff is fully heard. The court denies the motion, and the jury later returns a verdict for the plaintiff. Judgment is entered the same day. Thirty-five days after entry of judgment, the defendant files a renewed JMOL motion on the same causation ground. What is the best ruling on the renewed JMOL motion?
Answer A: Grant it automatically because the defendant preserved the causation ground at trial
Answer B: Deny it because Rule 50(a) motions can never be renewed after a verdict
Answer C: Deny it as untimely because it was filed more than 28 days after entry of judgment
Answer D: Grant it only if the plaintiff consents to the late filing
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 50(b) imposes a 28-day deadline after entry of judgment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Preservation is necessary, but the renewed motion still must be timely. B: A properly preserved Rule 50(a) motion can be renewed under Rule 50(b). D: Party consent does not replace the Rule 50(b) timing requirement.
lb_rule_reference: A renewed JMOL motion under Rule 50(b) must be filed no later than 28 days after entry of judgment.
