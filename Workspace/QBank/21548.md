Question: In a federal contract trial, Ruth is called as a witness but speaks only Hebrew. The court appoints Joseph to interpret. Joseph has interpreted in court before, but the judge forgets to administer any oath or affirmation to him. The opposing party objects before Ruth begins testifying through Joseph. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Exclude Ruth as incompetent because she cannot testify in English.
Answer B: Require Joseph to be qualified and to give an oath or affirmation to make a true translation before interpreting.
Answer C: Allow Joseph to interpret without an oath because only witnesses, not interpreters, must swear or affirm.
Answer D: Allow Ruth to testify only if a bilingual juror confirms Joseph's translation after each answer.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness is not incompetent merely because she needs an interpreter. Under the Federal Rules, an interpreter must be qualified and must give an oath or affirmation to make a true translation. The court should correct the interpreter-channel problem before testimony proceeds.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because language barriers can be addressed through a qualified interpreter. C: C is wrong because interpreters also must give an oath or affirmation. D: D is wrong because translation is handled through a qualified interpreter, not juror verification.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 604 / interpreter qualification and oath
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests whether the student routes the problem to interpreter qualification rather than witness incompetency.
