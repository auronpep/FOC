Question: A plaintiff sues a defendant in federal court for a car accident under diversity jurisdiction. The defendant wants to counterclaim for a small unpaid personal loan from five years earlier. The loan dispute is unrelated to the accident and does not independently satisfy federal jurisdiction. What is the best result?
Answer A: The counterclaim may proceed because all counterclaims fall within supplemental jurisdiction
Answer B: The counterclaim may proceed because Rule 13(b) creates federal jurisdiction for permissive counterclaims
Answer C: The counterclaim cannot proceed in this federal action absent an independent jurisdictional basis
Answer D: The counterclaim is compulsory because both claims involve money damages
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The unrelated loan claim is permissive and lacks supplemental or independent federal jurisdiction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Supplemental jurisdiction requires the same case or controversy. B: A joinder rule does not supply subject-matter jurisdiction. D: Compulsory status turns on the same transaction or occurrence, not the remedy type.
lb_rule_reference: A permissive counterclaim is procedurally allowed, but if it does not form part of the same case or controversy, it needs an independent subject-matter jurisdiction basis.
