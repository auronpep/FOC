Question: The jurisdiction recognizes imperfect self-defense. After repeated nonviolent arguments with Dario, Mei saw him reach quickly into his coat during a tense encounter. She honestly believed he was drawing a gun, but a reasonable person would have seen he was reaching for his phone. Mei shot and killed him. What is the best effect of Mei's belief?
Answer A: Complete acquittal, because Mei honestly feared deadly harm
Answer B: Voluntary manslaughter, because the jurisdiction recognizes imperfect self-defense for honest but unreasonable deadly-force beliefs
Answer C: Murder with no mitigation, because unreasonable beliefs are legally irrelevant everywhere
Answer D: Involuntary manslaughter, because Mei made a negligent mistake
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts supply a jurisdiction recognizing imperfect self-defense. Mei honestly but unreasonably believed deadly force was necessary. That mitigates murder to voluntary manslaughter rather than justifying the killing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Complete self-defense requires a reasonable belief. Her belief was honest but unreasonable. C: The question states this jurisdiction recognizes imperfect self-defense, which gives mitigating effect to an honest but unreasonable belief. D: Mei intentionally used deadly force. Imperfect self-defense mitigates murder to voluntary manslaughter, not involuntary manslaughter.
lb_rule_reference: In jurisdictions recognizing imperfect self-defense, an honest but unreasonable belief in the need for deadly force may reduce murder to voluntary manslaughter, but it is not a complete justification.
