


BARMATRIX Q#: 22784
subject_display: Civil Procedure
topic: Jurisdiction and Venue
subtopic: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
difficulty: 94010000
Stem / Fact Pattern: A man, who was a citizen of State A, sued a corporation for violations of federal copyright law in state court in State B. His claim was for an injunction that he valued at over $100,000. The corporation, which was incorporated in State B and had its primary place of business in State C, filed a petition to remove the action to federal district court in State B. Should the action be removed to federal district court in State B?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the man is suing for violations of federal copyright law.
Answer B: B: Yes, because there is complete diversity of citizenship.
Answer C: C: No, because the corporation is a citizen of State B.
Answer D: D: No, because the amount in controversy requirement was not met.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: If a plaintiff raises a federal question, a defendant may remove the action to federal court even if that defendant is a citizen of the state in which the claim was originally brought. This differs from the rule for an action that may be removed to federal court based on diversity jurisdiction. In that case, if the defendant is a citizen of the state where the action was brought, he or she cannot remove the action to federal court. Here, because the man’s claim raised a federal question, any issue regarding the removal’s appropriateness based on diversity jurisdiction is irrelevant. Therefore, B, C, and D are incorrect.
