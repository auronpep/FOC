


BARMATRIX Q#: 22827
Outline_code: 91090100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jury Trials
Outline: Right to Jury Trial
Question: A man filed suit in federal court against an airline seeking damages from injuries sustained in a plane crash. The man demanded a jury trial. During the trial, several jurors became ill and were excused. Eventually, only five jurors were left. Because of all the excused jurors, the parties' attorneys stipulated to the return of a verdict from a five- person jury. The jury deliberated and returned a verdict for the airline. The man filed a motion for a new trial, arguing that the five- person jury was not large enough to return a verdict. Should the court grant the motion for a new trial?
Answer A: A: No, because the parties stipulated to a five- person jury.
Answer B: B: No, because the court properly excused the jurors due to illness.
Answer C: C: Yes, because there must be at least six jurors on a federal civil jury.
Answer D: D: Yes, because there must be at least 12 jurors on a federal civil jury.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 48(a), “a jury must begin with at least six and no more than 12 members.” However, the parties can stipulate to a verdict returned by less than six jurors under Fed. R. Civ. P. 48(b). Here, since the parties stipulated to a verdict with less than six jurors, the court should not grant the motion for new trial. Therefore, C and D are incorrect. B is incorrect because the issue is not whether the jurors were properly excused.
