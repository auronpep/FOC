Question: Peter and John are separately represented co-defendants in a securities case. Their lawyers sign a joint-defense agreement. Peter's lawyer sends John's lawyer a privileged memo analyzing a shared legal defense, with instructions that it remain confidential within the joint-defense group. Does the exchange necessarily waive attorney-client privilege?
Answer A: Yes, because disclosure to any separately represented party is always waiver.
Answer B: No, because disclosure among separately represented parties and counsel sharing a common legal defense can preserve privilege if confidentiality is maintained.
Answer C: Yes, because common-interest protection applies only when the parties have the same lawyer.
Answer D: No, because all co-defendants automatically share every privilege even without confidentiality.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The common-interest doctrine can prevent waiver when privileged communications are shared confidentially among parties and lawyers pursuing a common legal interest. Peter and John share a legal defense and maintained confidentiality. The decisive axis is shared legal interest plus confidentiality.
