Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Freedom of association
Question: An association of licensed food trucks sues a city over an ordinance barring food trucks from a downtown zone. Several identified members used to operate there and lost revenue. The association seeks only an injunction, and the challenge fits the association's purpose. Can the association likely sue on behalf of its members?
Answer A: Yes, because its members have injury, the issue is germane, and individual participation is unnecessary for the injunction
Answer B: Yes, because an association always has standing whenever one member dislikes a law
Answer C: No, because only individual plaintiffs may seek injunctions
Answer D: No, because the association must prove every member lost revenue
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts satisfy the three associational standing elements.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Member injury alone is not enough; germaneness and no need for individual participation must also be satisfied. C: Associations can seek injunctive relief for members if the elements are met. D: At least one member with standing can satisfy the member-injury element.
lb_rule_reference: An association may sue for its members when a member would have standing, the interests are germane to the association's purpose, and neither the claim nor requested relief requires individual member participation.
