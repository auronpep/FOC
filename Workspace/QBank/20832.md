Question: Paul owned Lot 1, where he operated a small café, and Lot 2, an adjoining parking area. Paul sold Lot 1 to Lydia by a deed granting “to the owner of Lot 1 the right for Lot 1 customers and delivery drivers to park on the marked spaces on Lot 2.” Lydia later sold Lot 1 to James. How is the parking right best classified?
Answer A: A license because customers and delivery drivers, rather than Lydia personally, use the parking spaces.
Answer B: An easement in gross because the grant mentions people who may park.
Answer C: An affirmative easement appurtenant because the parking right benefits Lot 1 and may be used by people reasonably connected to Lot 1.
Answer D: A profit because the café earns money from customers who park.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An appurtenant easement benefits land. The parking right is granted to the owner of Lot 1 for customers and delivery drivers connected to Lot 1, so it benefits the dominant parcel. Use by invitees does not make the right personal or in gross.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because invitee use can fall within an appurtenant easement's scope. B: B is wrong because the grant ties the benefit to Lot 1. D: D is wrong because business revenue is not removal of resources from Lot 2.
lb_rule_reference: Appurtenant easement / use by invitees
