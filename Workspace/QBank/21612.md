internal_id: TR-242

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A restaurant negligently served soup in a bowl with a small crack, causing a patron to receive a minor hand burn when the bowl leaked. The patron also had a rare light-triggered neurological disorder. While leaving the restaurant to treat the burn, the patron stepped into bright sunlight and suffered a neurological episode unrelated to the burn itself. Is the restaurant liable for the neurological episode under the eggshell-plaintiff rule?
Answer A: Yes, because a tortfeasor is liable for every consequence suffered by an unusually fragile plaintiff
Answer B: Yes, because the neurological disorder was preexisting
Answer C: No, because the episode was a different, unforeseeable type of harm outside the risk created by the cracked bowl
Answer D: No, because negligence damages never include medical complications
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The foreseeable harm from leaking hot soup was a burn. The sunlight-triggered episode was not an aggravated burn or foreseeable type of harm from the restaurant's negligence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Eggshell covers the extent of a foreseeable type of harm. It does not automatically cover a different harm outside the risk created by the negligent soup service. B: A preexisting condition matters only if the defendant's tort aggravates it within the scope of liability. D: Medical complications can be recoverable when they are caused by and within the scope of the tort.
lb_rule_reference: The eggshell rule makes a defendant liable for unforeseeable extent of a foreseeable type of harm; it does not impose liability for a wholly different, unforeseeable type of harm outside the risk.
