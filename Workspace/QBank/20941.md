Question: A small factory has a city permit to run a ventilation fan. After the factory changes its process, the fan blows heavy metallic dust onto the adjoining owner's patio every afternoon. The factory stays within the permit's operating hours and points to the permit as a complete defense to a private nuisance suit. What is the best result?
Answer A: The permit is a complete defense because the city approved the fan's operation
Answer B: The permit is irrelevant and must be excluded from any nuisance analysis
Answer C: The adjoining owner can still pursue nuisance if the dust substantially and unreasonably interferes with use of the patio
Answer D: The adjoining owner can recover only if the dust permanently dispossesses him from the land
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Permit compliance is evidence, not immunity, when the conduct creates substantial unreasonable interference.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Regulatory approval does not automatically bar a tort claim. B: Compliance evidence may bear on reasonableness, even if it is not dispositive. D: Nuisance does not require dispossession; interference with use and enjoyment can suffice.
lb_rule_reference: A permit or compliance with regulations is relevant to reasonableness but does not automatically immunize a defendant from private nuisance liability.
