Outline: Strict scrutiny (suspect) classifications
Question: A state statute prohibits lawfully present non-citizen aliens from obtaining a license to practice civil engineering. The state justifies the restriction on the grounds that engineers working on public infrastructure projects carry significant responsibilities affecting public welfare, and that state licensure should be reserved for those who have made a full civic commitment to the state — i.e., citizens. A lawfully admitted permanent resident with engineering credentials challenges the license exclusion. Under Equal Protection analysis, which standard governs the state's civil engineering license restriction, and how should the political function exception be evaluated?
Answer A: Rational basis, because the state has broad power to determine conditions for professional licensing, and the preference for citizens in licensed professions is a reasonable exercise of state authority
Answer B: Rational basis, because alienage classifications by the federal government and analogous state classifications are governed by the same deferential standard
Answer C: Strict scrutiny applies; the political function exception does not rescue a civil engineering license restriction because engineering does not go to the heart of representative government, and the state cannot show a compelling interest narrowly tailored to citizens-only licensure
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies, and the state automatically loses because alienage classifications by states are categorically prohibited and no exception exists
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: State alienage classifications receive strict scrutiny. The political function exception allows states to reserve positions that involve the formulation or execution of broad public policy for citizens — such as police officers, probation officers, or public school teachers in their policy-making capacity. Civil engineering is a technical profession, not a position that goes to the heart of representative governance. The exception does not apply, and the state is unlikely to survive strict scrutiny because excluding all non-citizens from engineering licenses is not narrowly tailored to any compelling interest in civic commitment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Alienage is a suspect classification. State laws that exclude lawfully present aliens from professional licenses or public benefits receive strict scrutiny, not rational basis. The police power over licensing does not override the classification-based heightened review. B: The constitutional treatment of federal and state alienage classifications diverges sharply. Federal alienage laws receive rational basis because Congress has plenary power over immigration. State alienage laws receive strict scrutiny because the Equal Protection Clause constrains states more tightly than the federal rational basis standard that applies to the federal immigration power. D: State alienage classifications are not categorically prohibited. They receive strict scrutiny, which is demanding but not automatic invalidation. More importantly, the political function exception creates a genuine carve-out for positions central to self-governance (e.g., police, probation officers, teachers in policy-making roles). The strict scrutiny standard means the state will very often lose — but not always.
lb_rule_reference: Alienage is generally a suspect classification; state laws that exclude lawfully present aliens from public benefits or employment receive strict scrutiny — unless the political function exception applies (positions intimately related to democratic self-governance).
