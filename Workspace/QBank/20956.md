Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Consent
Question: Seller and Buyer sign a written contract stating: "The parties consent to exclusive jurisdiction in the state and federal courts located in State B for any dispute arising from this agreement." Buyer later argues that State B lacks minimum contacts because Buyer has no offices, employees, or property there. What is the best response?
Answer A: Buyer may have consented to personal jurisdiction through the valid forum-selection clause
Answer B: The clause affects only venue and can never affect personal jurisdiction
Answer C: The clause is irrelevant because minimum contacts can never be waived
Answer D: The clause is enforceable only if it also chooses State B substantive law
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A valid forum-selection clause can establish consent to personal jurisdiction for disputes within the clause.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A clause consenting to jurisdiction can also waive or satisfy personal-jurisdiction objections. C: Personal jurisdiction is an individual right that can be waived or consented to. D: A forum-selection clause can establish consent without a matching choice-of-law clause.
lb_rule_reference: A party may consent to personal jurisdiction through a valid, enforceable forum-selection clause; the clause must be distinguished from a mere choice-of-law provision.
