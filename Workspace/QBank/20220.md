Question: A truck driver negligently hit a city bridge, requiring the city to close the bridge for repairs. No property owned by a nearby cafe was damaged, and no cafe employee or customer was injured. The closure reduced foot traffic, and the cafe lost profits for two weeks. The cafe sues the truck driver for negligence to recover the lost profits. Is the cafe likely to recover its lost profits in a negligence claim against the truck driver?
Answer A: Yes, because lost profits were foreseeable once the bridge closed
Answer B: Yes, because negligence allows all ripple losses caused by careless conduct
Answer C: No, because the cafe alleges only pure economic loss without property damage, personal injury, or a special relationship
Answer D: No, because lost profits are never recoverable in tort
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The cafe's loss is financial only and not tied to its own damaged property, personal injury, or special duty relationship. The economic-loss rule generally bars the negligence claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Foreseeability alone does not usually make pure economic loss recoverable in negligence. The cafe had no personal injury, property damage, or special relationship. B: Tort law limits pure economic ripple claims to avoid open-ended liability. A recognized duty or physical/property harm is usually required. D: Lost profits can be recoverable in some torts or when tied to property damage or a recognized special duty. The problem here is pure economic loss in ordinary negligence.
lb_rule_reference: In negligence, pure economic losses are generally not recoverable absent personal injury, property damage, a special relationship, or another recognized tort duty.
