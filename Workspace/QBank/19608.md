Question: David hired Solomon to build cedar cabinets for a study for $18,000, payable when the cabinets were installed. Solomon installed all cabinets in the agreed locations using the agreed cedar. One drawer stuck slightly because a runner needed a $150 adjustment. David refused to pay anything until every drawer worked perfectly. Which result is most likely?
Answer A: Solomon may recover the contract price less damages for the minor defect.
Answer B: Solomon may recover nothing because exact performance is always a condition to payment.
Answer C: Solomon may recover only in restitution because substantial performance never permits contract-price recovery.
Answer D: David may cancel the contract because any defect in construction is a total breach.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under common law, substantial performance of a construction or installation contract satisfies the constructive condition to the owner's payment duty, subject to an offset for defects. Solomon completed the essential work, and the drawer issue was minor and easily corrected. David's payment duty matured, reduced by damages for the defect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because constructive conditions are not usually enforced with strict-perfect-performance force. C: C is wrong because substantial performance permits contract recovery less damages, not only restitution. D: D is wrong because a minor correctable defect is not a total breach.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law constructive conditions / substantial performance
