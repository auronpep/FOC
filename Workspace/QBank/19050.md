Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff files a state-court action asserting only state-law breach-of-contract claims. The complaint does not establish the parties' citizenships. For fourteen months, the parties litigate in state court. In month fifteen, the plaintiff files an amended complaint that for the first time explicitly alleges the parties' citizenships and a claim for $95,000, disclosing that federal diversity jurisdiction would have existed from the start. The defendant files a notice of removal within 30 days of the amended complaint. The plaintiff moves to remand, arguing that the removal is time-barred. The governing forum is initially state court, now removed, under the MBE baseline. Is the removal time-barred?
Answer A: No, because the 30-day removal clock ran from the amended complaint — the first paper showing removability — and the defendant filed within that window
Answer B: Yes, because the action has been pending for fifteen months, exceeding the one-year outer limit for diversity removal under § 1446(c)(1), and there is no indication of plaintiff bad faith
Answer C: No, because the one-year limit runs from when the case first becomes removable, not from original commencement — and here the case became removable only fifteen months after filing when the amended complaint disclosed diversity
Answer D: No, because the one-year limit under § 1446(c)(1) applies only to removal based on newly joining diverse parties, not to removal based on a newly disclosed original party's citizenship
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1446(c)(1) provides that a case may not be removed on diversity grounds more than one year after commencement of the action, unless the district court finds that the plaintiff acted in bad faith to prevent removal. Here, the action commenced over fifteen months before the notice of removal. The one-year absolute bar applies. There are no facts suggesting bad faith by the plaintiff. Even though the defendant filed within 30 days of the amended complaint, the independent one-year outer limit bars removal. The court should remand.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1446(c)(1) imposes an additional outer limit in diversity cases: removal is barred if made more than one year after commencement of the action, unless the plaintiff acted in bad faith to prevent removal. The action commenced fifteen months ago; the one-year limit has passed. The 30-day window and the one-year bar are independent requirements — both must be satisfied. Here, the one-year bar independently defeats removal. C: Section 1446(c)(1) runs from "commencement of the action" in state court — not from when removability first became apparent. The commencement is the date of original filing. Courts have uniformly held that the one-year clock starts at the original filing, not at the date of first notice of removability. D: The one-year limit under § 1446(c)(1) applies to all diversity-based removals, regardless of whether the change that triggered removability was a new party or newly disclosed information about existing parties. The statute draws no such distinction.
lb_rule_reference: 28 U.S.C. § 1446(c)(1) — a diversity case may not be removed more than one year after commencement of the action in state court, unless the district court finds that the plaintiff acted in bad faith to prevent removal.
