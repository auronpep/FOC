Question: Samuel, a company driver, was interviewed by corporate counsel after a crash. Counsel told Samuel that she represented the company, not Samuel, and that the company controlled any privilege. The company later chose to disclose the interview notes to the plaintiff. Samuel objects that the notes are privileged and that he does not waive. Who controls the privilege on these facts?
Answer A: The company controls the privilege because the interview was conducted for legal advice to the corporate client after Samuel was told counsel represented the company.
Answer B: Samuel controls the privilege because he was the person who spoke.
Answer C: Both Samuel and the company must consent to any waiver in every corporate interview.
Answer D: No privilege could ever have attached because Samuel was only a driver.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When corporate counsel interviews an employee for the purpose of advising the corporation and makes clear that counsel represents the company, the corporate client generally holds the privilege. The company may waive it; the employee does not control it merely because he supplied facts. The decisive axis is holder and client identity.
