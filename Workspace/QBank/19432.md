Question: A contractor negligently dropped a toolbox from scaffolding, breaking a pedestrian's shoulder. The pedestrian later suffered nightmares and anxiety about walking near construction sites. The pedestrian sues the contractor for negligence and seeks damages for medical bills, pain, and emotional distress tied to the shoulder injury. Must the pedestrian satisfy a standalone negligent-infliction-of-emotional-distress category to recover emotional-distress damages?
Answer A: Yes, because emotional distress is never recoverable unless the plaintiff was in the zone of danger without contact
Answer B: Yes, because all emotional damages require proof of outrageous conduct
Answer C: No, because emotional distress tied to the physical injury is recoverable as ordinary negligence damage
Answer D: No, because emotional distress damages are presumed in every tort
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff was physically injured by the negligence. Distress resulting from that injury may be recovered without proving a standalone NIED category.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The pedestrian suffered a physical injury. Emotional distress tied to that injury is ordinary parasitic damage. B: This is a negligence claim with physical injury, not IIED. D: The reason is the physical injury and causation, not a universal presumption in every tort.
lb_rule_reference: When a negligence plaintiff suffers physical injury, emotional distress caused by that injury is generally recoverable as part of ordinary compensatory damages without satisfying standalone NIED gates.
