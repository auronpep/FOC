Outline: Homicide
Question: Acting independently and without any coordination, two strangers — Fiona and Grant — each shot the same victim, Hector, in vital organs at virtually the same moment. Medical testimony established that either wound alone would have been instantly fatal, and that both wounds occurred within a fraction of a second of each other. There is no basis to determine which wound "caused" death first. Fiona and Grant were prosecuted separately. Is Fiona the cause of Hector's death?
Answer A: No, because under the but-for test, Hector would have died from Grant's shot regardless of Fiona's act, so Fiona's shot was not a but-for cause of death
Answer B: No, because causation cannot be assigned to either Fiona or Grant when the order of causation cannot be determined
Answer C: Yes, because Fiona's wound was independently sufficient to cause Hector's death and was a substantial factor in bringing it about
Answer D: Yes, but only if the jury finds that Fiona's wound was inflicted before Grant's
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Where two independently sufficient causes simultaneously contribute to a death, courts apply the substantial factor test rather than the but-for test. Fiona fired a wound that was instantly fatal on its own — it was a substantial contributing factor to Hector's death. Exonerating Fiona on but-for grounds when she fired a lethal shot would be doctrinally incoherent. Both Fiona and Grant are causally responsible under the substantial factor test.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The but-for test breaks down when two independent, simultaneously sufficient causes each would have caused the result without the other. Courts apply the substantial factor test in these cases: was each defendant's act a substantial factor in bringing about death? Here, Fiona fired a wound instantly fatal in isolation — her act was manifestly a substantial factor. Applying strict but-for analysis would paradoxically exonerate both killers. B: The inability to determine which wound caused death first does not preclude liability when both wounds were independently sufficient to cause death. Under the substantial factor test, both Fiona and Grant are causes. The prosecution need not prove which wound "won" — it need only prove that Fiona's wound was a substantial factor in causing Hector's death. D: The substantial factor test does not require temporal priority. When two acts are simultaneously sufficient and both are independently sufficient causes, both actors are liable regardless of which act preceded the other by fractions of a second. Requiring a temporal finding would allow one or both defendants to escape liability based solely on the limits of forensic precision.
lb_rule_reference: When two independent forces, each sufficient to cause death, simultaneously contribute to a death, the but-for test fails (neither is necessary), but both actors are still causally responsible under the substantial factor test
