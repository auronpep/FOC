Question: Naomi granted an electric company “a fifteen-foot-wide easement corridor for underground lines and access for maintenance.” Naomi later built a storage shed in the middle of the corridor. The shed did not touch the underground lines, but it blocked the company's only practical maintenance access to a junction box. The company seeks an order requiring Naomi to remove the shed. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Naomi may keep the shed because the servient owner may use the land in any way that does not physically touch the lines.
Answer B: Naomi may keep the shed because utility easements never restrict surface use.
Answer C: The company should prevail because the servient owner may not use the land in a way that unreasonably interferes with the granted maintenance access.
Answer D: The company should prevail only if the shed destroys all economic use of the corridor.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The servient owner may use the burdened land in ways that do not unreasonably interfere with the easement. Here, the grant includes maintenance access, and the shed blocks the only practical access to the junction box. That interference exceeds permissible servient use.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because interference can occur even without physical contact with the utility line. B: B is wrong because surface use may be limited when it obstructs the easement. C is correct because the shed blocks granted maintenance access. D: D is wrong because total economic wipeout is a takings concept, not the standard for easement interference.
lb_rule_reference: Servient use / no unreasonable interference
