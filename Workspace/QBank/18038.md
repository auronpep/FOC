Question: Sarah signed a deed stating, “I convey Blackacre to Isaac.” The deed accurately described Blackacre, identified Isaac as grantee, and Sarah delivered it to Isaac with present intent to transfer title. Isaac accepted. The deed contained no clause reciting payment, love and affection, or any other consideration. Sarah later argued that the deed failed for that reason. Who owns Blackacre?
Answer A: Isaac, because a deed need not recite consideration if the deed formalities and delivery requirements are satisfied.
Answer B: Sarah, because a deed without a consideration clause is merely an unenforceable promise.
Answer C: Sarah, because Isaac did not sign a separate promise to pay consideration.
Answer D: Isaac only if he records the deed before Sarah withdraws the gift.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is the deed-formality checklist, not a consideration clause. Sarah signed and delivered a writing with present transfer words, an identifiable grantee, and a sufficient description. Isaac accepted, so title passed even though the deed did not recite consideration.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a present conveyance in a signed and delivered deed is not reduced to a mere promise by the absence of a consideration clause. C: C is wrong because the grantee's signature or promise to pay is not a deed-validity requirement. D: D is wrong because recording affects notice and priority, not validity between Sarah and Isaac.
lb_rule_reference: No consideration clause required for deed validity
