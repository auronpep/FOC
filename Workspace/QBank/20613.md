Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Esther sues a manufacturer after a pressure valve burst. Esther offers the broken valve. The valve bears the manufacturer's trademark and a serial-number format that a former employee of the manufacturer recognizes as the format used on its valves. The manufacturer objects that Esther has not called the employee who assembled the valve. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the valve because only the assembler may authenticate a product.
Answer B: Exclude the valve unless Esther proves the serial number is unique to this one valve.
Answer C: Admit the valve if the trademark, serial-number format, and witness testimony are sufficient to support a finding that the valve was made by the manufacturer.
Answer D: Admit the valve only if the manufacturer concedes it made the valve.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The proponent may authenticate or identify an item through circumstantial evidence and testimony from a witness with knowledge. The trademark and serial-number testimony can be enough to support a finding of manufacturer identity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because authentication does not require the assembler. B: B is wrong because uniqueness of the serial number is not required at the threshold stage. D: D is wrong because the opponent's concession is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901 / FRE 401
lb_student_diagnostic: Wrong proof-channel trap: assuming only one witness can authenticate a physical item.
