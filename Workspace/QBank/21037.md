Outline: Receipt of stolen property
Question: A stranger offers Sam twenty boxed phones for $100 each in a parking lot at midnight. Several boxes have store security tags attached, and the stranger says, "Do not ask where they came from." Sam replies, "I do not want to know," pays cash, and loads the phones into his car. Is there sufficient evidence of knowledge for receiving stolen property?
Answer A: No, because the seller never expressly said the phones were stolen
Answer B: No, because a low price alone can never prove knowledge
Answer C: Yes, because Sam received the phones while deliberately avoiding obvious stolen-property facts
Answer D: Yes, but only if Sam later resells the phones
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts support knowledge or belief at receipt. Sam deliberately avoided confirming what the obvious signs showed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Knowledge can be inferred from the receipt-time facts, including security tags, extreme price, parking-lot sale, and deliberate avoidance. B: The price is not alone; it is paired with security tags, midnight sale, cash, and deliberate avoidance. D: Receiving stolen property is complete upon knowing receipt and control; resale is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Receiving stolen property requires knowing or believing the property is stolen at receipt; deliberate avoidance of clear stolen-property facts can support that knowledge.
