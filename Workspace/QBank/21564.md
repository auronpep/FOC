Question: In a federal civil trial, Deborah calls Luke, a records custodian. On direct, Deborah's lawyer asks, “You are the records custodian for Jericho Bank, correct?” and “These are the bank's ordinary account records, correct?” The opposing party objects that both questions are leading. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because leading questions are always forbidden on direct.
Answer B: Overrule the objection if the questions concern preliminary, undisputed matters and are used to develop the testimony efficiently.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Luke is hostile.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only because records custodians are expert witnesses.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court has discretion to permit leading questions as necessary to develop testimony, including preliminary or undisputed background matters. The questions efficiently establish Luke's role and the record foundation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is too rigid. C wrongly makes hostility the only direct-examination exception. D is wrong because the ruling does not depend on custodian-as-expert status.
