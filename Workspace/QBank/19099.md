Question: After voluntarily drinking heavily, Simon throws a heavy glass bottle toward a crowd from a balcony. The bottle narrowly misses Dana. Simon is charged with attempted murder. Witnesses say Simon was laughing and yelling nonsense, not aiming at anyone in particular. How may Simon use voluntary intoxication?
Answer A: To argue he lacked the specific intent to kill required for attempted murder
Answer B: To obtain acquittal from all charges arising from the bottle throw
Answer C: Not at all, because voluntary intoxication is never relevant to attempt
Answer D: Only if Simon's intoxication was caused by a mental disease
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Attempted murder requires a specific intent to kill. Voluntary intoxication can be relevant to whether Simon formed that intent. If he acted recklessly without aiming to kill a particular person, attempted murder may fail, though other crimes may remain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: It affects only the specific intent element of attempted murder. It does not automatically defeat assault, battery, reckless endangerment, or other non-specific-intent offenses. C: Attempt crimes require specific intent. Voluntary intoxication may negate that specific intent. D: For voluntary intoxication and specific intent, no mental disease is required. The issue is whether intoxication prevented intent to kill.
lb_rule_reference: Attempted murder requires specific intent to kill; voluntary intoxication may be relevant if it prevents formation of that intent
