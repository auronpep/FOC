Outline: False pretenses
Question: Eli wants to buy a food truck. He tells the seller, "My bank has already approved the loan and wired the funds; the cashier's check will clear tomorrow." Eli knows no loan exists. Relying on the statement, the seller signs title to the food truck over to Eli and gives him the keys. What is Eli's best theft offense?
Answer A: False pretenses, because Eli obtained title by lying about an existing financing fact
Answer B: Larceny by trick, because all fraud-induced transfers are possession-only takings
Answer C: No offense, because the statement concerned money to be paid tomorrow
Answer D: Embezzlement, because Eli later kept the food truck
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Eli lied about present financial status and obtained title as a result. That fits false pretenses.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The seller signed over title, not just temporary possession. C: Eli lied about present facts: existing loan approval and wired funds. D: Eli did not first receive lawful possession as an entrusted agent or bailee. He obtained title by fraud.
lb_rule_reference: False pretenses requires obtaining title through a false representation of present or past material fact made with intent to defraud.
