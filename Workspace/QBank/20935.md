Question: Both Abigail and Isaac are merchants who deal in wool. After an oral agreement for Abigail to buy 1,200 bundles of wool from Isaac, Isaac sent Abigail a signed email confirmation sufficient against Isaac. Abigail received it and knew its contents. Within 10 days, Abigail telephoned Isaac and said, “I object to your confirmation,” but she sent no written objection. Can Isaac use the confirmation to satisfy the Statute of Frauds against Abigail?
Answer A: No, because Abigail did not sign the email.
Answer B: Yes, because between merchants the confirmation can bind the recipient unless the recipient gives written objection within 10 days.
Answer C: No, because an oral objection within 10 days is enough.
Answer D: Yes, but only for goods Abigail actually received and accepted.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Between merchants, a confirmation sufficient against the sender and received by the other merchant satisfies UCC §2-201 against the recipient unless the recipient gives written notice of objection within 10 days. Abigail's oral objection is not enough.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the merchant-confirmation rule can satisfy the SOF against a nonsigning merchant recipient. C: C is wrong because the statute requires written notice of objection. D: D is wrong because received-and-accepted goods are a separate exception; the confirmation itself can satisfy the writing requirement.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 / merchant confirmation
