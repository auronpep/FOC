Outline: Robbery
Question: Before police arrange any identification procedure, a robbery victim sees a local news report showing the defendant's mug shot and captioning him as the robbery suspect. The victim calls police and says that is the robber. Police did not provide the photo to the news station or encourage the broadcast. The defendant moves to suppress on due process grounds. Should the court suppress the identification as a due process violation?
Answer A: No, because the suggestive exposure was not arranged by police
Answer B: Yes, because any suggestive influence on a witness requires constitutional suppression
Answer C: Yes, because reliability is always irrelevant once a witness has seen a mug shot
Answer D: No, because news reports are always admissible evidence at a criminal trial
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The due process identification framework addresses improper state-arranged procedures. The broadcast may affect weight, but it was not police-arranged suggestiveness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Due process suppression requires improper state involvement, not every suggestive circumstance a witness encounters. C: Reliability may matter to evidentiary weight, but the constitutional due process claim fails first for lack of police-arranged suggestiveness. D: The news report itself is not the evidence being admitted. The issue is witness identification, and the correct reason is lack of police-arranged suggestiveness.
lb_rule_reference: A due process identification claim generally requires unnecessarily suggestive police conduct; a suggestive circumstance not arranged by law enforcement is not enough for constitutional suppression.
