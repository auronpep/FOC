Question: Abraham contracted with Sarah to buy Sarah's delivery business. As part of the price, Abraham promised to pay Mary, one of Sarah's creditors, $12,000. The contract required Sarah to transfer all delivery permits to Abraham before Abraham's payment duties matured. Sarah failed to transfer any permits, and Abraham was unable to operate the business. Mary sues Abraham for the $12,000. Is Abraham liable to Mary?
Answer A: Yes, because Mary was not responsible for Sarah's failure to transfer the permits.
Answer B: No, because Mary's rights as an intended beneficiary are subject to Abraham's contract defense based on Sarah's material breach.
Answer C: Yes, because a third-party beneficiary is always insulated from defenses between the contracting parties.
Answer D: No, but only because Mary failed to give consideration directly to Abraham.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Mary is an intended beneficiary, but her rights are derivative of the contract that created the promise. Abraham may assert against Mary a defense arising from Sarah's material breach of the same contract. Sarah's failure to transfer the permits prevented Abraham's payment duty from maturing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Mary's lack of fault does not make her rights greater than Sarah's contract rights. C: C is wrong because intended beneficiaries take subject to contract defenses. D: D is wrong because beneficiary consideration is not required; the problem is Sarah's material breach.
