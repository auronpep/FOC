Question: A mother stood on the sidewalk and watched a negligent driver hit her child in a crosswalk. The mother understood at once that the child had been injured and immediately developed severe physical symptoms from shock. The mother sues the driver for negligent infliction of emotional distress. Is the mother likely within a recognized bystander NIED category?
Answer A: Yes, because she closely related to the injured child and contemporaneously perceived the injury
Answer B: Yes, because any witness to an accident may recover for emotional distress
Answer C: No, because only the physically injured child can ever recover in negligence
Answer D: No, because the mother must prove the driver intended to scare her
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The mother is a close family member, witnessed the injury-producing event, and suffered serious distress with physical symptoms.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Bystander NIED is limited; relationship and contemporaneous awareness matter. C: Recognized bystander NIED categories can allow recovery for emotional distress. D: NIED is negligence-based and does not require intent to frighten.
lb_rule_reference: Bystander NIED generally requires a close familial relationship, contemporaneous awareness of the injury-producing event, and serious distress, often with physical manifestation depending on jurisdiction.
