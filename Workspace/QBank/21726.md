Question: Jacob agreed to buy 50 garden tillers from Leah, a merchant seller, for a total price of $10,000 and paid a $2,000 deposit. The contract contained no liquidated-damages clause. Jacob repudiated before delivery, and Leah justifiably withheld the goods. Leah resold the tillers in a commercially reasonable way and established $700 in Article 2 damages, including proper incidental expenses. Under UCC §2-718, what restitution, if any, may Jacob recover from Leah?
Answer A: $2,000, because Leah resold the goods and Jacob should receive his full deposit back.
Answer B: $800, because the deposit is reduced by the $500 statutory retention amount and Leah's $700 established damages.
Answer C: $1,500, because Leah may retain only $500 when there is no liquidated-damages clause.
Answer D: Nothing, because a defaulting buyer forfeits any deposit paid before breach.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is restitution of a defaulting buyer's deposit under UCC goods law, subject to the seller's expectation-damages offset. Jacob is the buyer, Leah is the merchant seller, and the goods were withheld after Jacob's repudiation. With no valid liquidated-damages clause, UCC §2-718 allows statutory retention of the lesser of 20% of the total price or $500; 20% of $10,000 is $2,000, so the statutory amount is $500. Leah also offsets $700 in established Article 2 damages. The formula is $2,000 deposit − $500 statutory retention − $700 seller damages = $800. The result scope is offset restitution.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because resale does not require a full refund when the seller has statutory retention and damages offsets. C: C is wrong because it applies the $500 statutory amount but ignores the seller's established damages, a WAL-REM-016 shortcut. D: D is wrong because default does not automatically forfeit all buyer restitution; that is an all-or-nothing breach-punishment error under WAL-REM-005.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-718 deposit restitution — absent a valid liquidated-damages clause, buyer restitution is reduced by statutory retention and seller damages offset
