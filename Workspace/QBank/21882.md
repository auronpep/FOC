Question: Congress creates temporary federal magistrate positions to supervise document production in complex federal enforcement cases. The magistrates have limited duties, operate under review by Article III judges, and may be removed at the end of the assignment. Congress authorizes the federal district courts to appoint them. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The appointment route may be valid if the magistrates are inferior officers and the appointing body is a court of law
Answer B: The appointment route is invalid because every federal officer must be appointed by the President with Senate confirmation
Answer C: The appointment route is invalid because courts may never appoint officers with any connection to executive enforcement
Answer D: The appointment route is valid only if Congress itself confirms each magistrate
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Inferior officers may be appointed through routes Congress designates, including by courts of law. The limited duties and supervision support inferior-officer treatment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Article II allows Congress to vest appointment of inferior officers in the President alone, courts of law, or heads of departments. C: Courts of law can be assigned appointment authority for inferior officers when the office is compatible with judicial functions and constitutional limits. D: Congress does not appoint or confirm inferior officers through ordinary committee approval. The Constitution specifies permitted appointment routes.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may vest appointment of inferior officers in the President alone, courts of law, or heads of departments, but principal officers must be appointed by the President with Senate confirmation.
