MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Foreseeable plaintiffs
Question: A truck driver negligently backed into a parked car in a loading zone. A blogger two miles away read an alert about the crash, rushed out to film it, and tripped over a curb outside his own apartment. He sues the driver for negligence. Is the blogger likely within the driver's duty zone?
Answer A: Yes, because the blogger was injured after the driver's negligence
Answer B: Yes, because negligent drivers owe duties to everyone who hears about their accidents
Answer C: No, because the blogger was outside the foreseeable risk field created by the backing truck
Answer D: No, because a parked-car collision can never be negligent
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The backing truck endangered nearby people and property, not a remote blogger who chose to run from his apartment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Hindsight injury does not create duty to a remote person who was not endangered by the backing truck. B: Negligence duty does not extend to everyone who later learns of the event and reacts. D: The driver may have been negligent toward nearby foreseeable plaintiffs. The blogger loses because duty does not extend to him.
lb_rule_reference: A remote person who reacts to news of an accident generally is not within the original zone of physical risk.
