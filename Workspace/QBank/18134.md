Question: A town rezones one apartment complex into an industrial zone. The ordinance is facially neutral. But the record shows the town changed its usual notice procedure, ignored its planning staff, and several council members said the complex's immigrant residents "do not belong here" before voting. What is the strongest Equal Protection argument against the ordinance?
Answer A: The ordinance cannot be challenged because it does not mention race or national origin
Answer B: Discriminatory purpose may be inferred from the statements and procedural departures, triggering heightened scrutiny
Answer C: The ordinance receives strict scrutiny only if every resident of the complex belongs to the same protected class
Answer D: The ordinance automatically survives because zoning always receives rational basis
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Official statements, irregular sequence, and procedural departures can support a finding of discriminatory purpose behind a neutral law.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Facial neutrality does not defeat a claim supported by proof of discriminatory purpose. C: Equal Protection purpose analysis does not require every affected person to share the same trait. D: Discriminatory purpose can move a facially neutral zoning law into heightened scrutiny.
lb_rule_reference: A facially neutral law may trigger heightened scrutiny if the challenger proves discriminatory purpose through factors such as history, sequence of events, departures from procedure, and official statements.
