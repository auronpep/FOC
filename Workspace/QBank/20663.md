MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Trial judge’s discretion to control order and presentation of evidence
Question: In a federal patent trial, Abraham wants to use a large animated diagram during opening statement to show how his device allegedly works. The diagram has not yet been authenticated, and the judge is concerned that the animation may look like proven evidence rather than a preview. The judge permits Abraham to refer to the device generally but bars the animation until evidence supports it. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The ruling is improper because demonstrative aids may always be used in opening statement.
Answer B: The ruling is proper as a reasonable control over presentation and the risk that the jury will treat an unsupported demonstrative as evidence.
Answer C: The ruling is improper unless the animation is hearsay.
Answer D: The ruling is proper only if the judge finds the device is invalid before trial begins.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The judge may reasonably control the presentation of evidence and courtroom aids. It is proper to delay or limit an unsupported demonstrative aid if it risks confusing the jury or presenting unproved facts as evidence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because demonstratives in opening remain subject to court control. C: C is wrong because the issue is presentation control, not hearsay. D: D is wrong because the judge need not decide the merits to control the timing and use of a demonstrative aid.
