Question: Congress makes it a federal crime to possess a smartphone within one block of any school, with no requirement that the phone moved in interstate commerce or was used commercially. The findings say school safety affects the national economy because unsafe schools reduce workforce productivity. What is the best Commerce Clause objection?
Answer A: The statute regulates noneconomic local possession and relies on an attenuated chain of aggregate economic effects
Answer B: The statute is valid because Congress may aggregate any conduct that has some long-term effect on the economy
Answer C: The statute is valid because phones are products sold in interstate commerce
Answer D: The statute is invalid because Congress may never regulate activity near schools
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Without a commerce hook, jurisdictional element, or economic class of activity, the statute resembles invalid regulation of local noneconomic conduct.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The substantial-effects doctrine is not a general license to regulate noneconomic local conduct through attenuated reasoning. C: The statute lacks a jurisdictional element tying the regulated possession to interstate commerce. D: A properly drafted statute with a valid commerce hook or another enumerated power might be valid.
lb_rule_reference: Congress generally may not regulate purely local noneconomic activity based only on attenuated aggregate effects on interstate commerce.
