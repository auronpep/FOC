Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal assault trial, Miriam offers a knife found in Daniel's car. The officer who seized it testifies that she scratched her initials on the handle and placed it in the evidence locker. The evidence log shows that another officer removed it for photographing and returned it the same afternoon, but that officer is unavailable to testify. Daniel objects that the chain of custody is incomplete. What is the proper ruling?
Answer A: Exclude the knife because every person who handled it must testify.
Answer B: Exclude the knife because the unavailable officer's handling makes the knife hearsay.
Answer C: Admit the knife only if Daniel stipulates that it is the same knife.
Answer D: Admit the knife if the markings and log provide sufficient evidence that it is the same knife; the missing handler affects weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A perfect chain of custody is not required for every physical object. The officer's initials and evidence log provide sufficient evidence for a reasonable finding that the knife is what Miriam claims. The missing handler can be used to attack weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because every handler need not testify if the foundation is otherwise sufficient. B: B is wrong because the knife is physical evidence; the custody issue is authentication, not hearsay. C: C is wrong because Daniel's stipulation is not necessary.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(a)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats every chain gap as automatic exclusion.
