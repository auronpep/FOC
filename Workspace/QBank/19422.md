Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Trial judge’s discretion to control order and presentation of evidence
Question: Sarah sues Samuel for nuisance, claiming that Samuel's factory makes a loud grinding sound every night. Sarah has already called four neighbors who described the same sound at the same times. She now seeks to call eight more neighbors to give the same description. Samuel objects that the testimony will be cumulative. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: The court must allow all eight witnesses because each witness personally heard the sound.
Answer B: The court may limit the additional testimony if the added witnesses would needlessly waste time without adding meaningful proof.
Answer C: The court must exclude all further testimony because four witnesses are always enough to prove a repeated event.
Answer D: The court may limit the testimony only if Samuel proves the witnesses are lying.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may control the presentation of evidence to avoid wasting time. Even relevant firsthand testimony can be limited when it becomes needlessly cumulative.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because personal knowledge does not create a right to present unlimited cumulative proof. C: C is wrong because there is no fixed number of witnesses that automatically ends proof. D: D is wrong because cumulative limits do not require proof that the witnesses are dishonest.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / cumulative evidence
