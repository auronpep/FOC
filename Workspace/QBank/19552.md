Question: Joseph orally orders 200 ceremonial robes from Esther, each embroidered with Joseph's family crest and measurements for specific performers. The robes are not suitable for sale to others in Esther's ordinary business. Before Joseph repudiates, Esther cuts the custom fabric and begins embroidery on 80 robes. Joseph then refuses to buy and asserts the Statute of Frauds. What is the best result?
Answer A: Esther may enforce the contract under the specially manufactured goods exception.
Answer B: Esther cannot enforce the contract because Joseph signed no writing.
Answer C: Esther can enforce the contract only for the 80 robes already embroidered.
Answer D: Esther can enforce the contract only if Joseph admitted the contract in court.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A goods contract lacking a sufficient writing can be enforceable if the goods are specially manufactured for the buyer, unsuitable for sale to others in the seller's ordinary course, and the seller made a substantial beginning or procurement commitment before receiving repudiation notice. These facts satisfy that route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because §2-201(3)(a) is an exception to the writing requirement. C: C is wrong because the exception is not limited to the exact pieces already completed if the contract and substantial beginning requirements are met. D: D is wrong because admission is a separate exception, not the only one.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(3)(a)
