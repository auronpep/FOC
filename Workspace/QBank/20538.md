Subtopic: Hearsay and Circumstances of Its Admissibility
Outline: Statement by opposing party
Question: After a collision, a delivery driver privately told her lawyer, “The traffic light was red when I entered the intersection,” while seeking legal advice. During a later deposition, the opposing lawyer asks the driver, “What color was the traffic light when you entered the intersection?” The driver's lawyer instructs her not to answer, arguing that she already gave that fact to counsel in confidence. How should the court rule if the opposing party moves to compel an answer?
Answer A: Deny the motion, because any fact included in a confidential lawyer-client consultation becomes privileged.
Answer B: Grant the motion as to the underlying fact, while preserving the privilege against asking what the driver said to her lawyer.
Answer C: Deny the motion unless the opposing party first shows substantial need for the exact statement made to the lawyer.
Answer D: Grant the motion as to both the traffic-light fact and the words spoken to counsel because the fact is relevant to liability.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications made for legal advice. It does not immunize the underlying facts known by the client. The opposing party may ask what happened at the intersection, but may not force the driver to disclose what she said or wrote to her lawyer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it converts factual knowledge into privilege merely because the client communicated the fact to counsel. C: C is wrong because substantial-need language belongs to ordinary work-product analysis, not to a deposition question seeking a witness's own factual knowledge. D: D is wrong because relevance does not erase the privilege protecting the confidential communication itself.
lb_rule_reference: Attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications, not underlying facts
lb_student_diagnostic: Student thinks privilege protects information as such, rather than the communication channel.
