Question: Aaron owned Hillacre in fee simple. Hillacre had an appurtenant easement over Roadacre, which Ruth owned in fee simple. Aaron then leased Roadacre from Ruth for one year. During the lease term, Aaron possessed Roadacre as tenant, but Ruth retained fee simple title. After the lease ended, Ruth argued that the easement had terminated by merger. Is Ruth correct?
Answer A: No, because merger requires unity of ownership of the dominant and servient estates in the same person and in the same quality of title; Aaron held only a leasehold in Roadacre.
Answer B: No, because easements can never terminate by merger.
Answer C: Yes, because any possession of the servient land by the dominant owner terminates the easement.
Answer D: Yes, because a one-year lease is treated as fee simple ownership for merger purposes.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Ruth is not correct. Easement merger requires unity of ownership of the dominant and servient estates in the same person and in the same quality or duration of title. Aaron owned Hillacre in fee simple but held only a one-year leasehold in Roadacre, so the easement did not merge into unified fee ownership.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because easements can terminate by merger when the unity requirements are met. C: C is wrong because possession of the servient land is not enough; the relevant estates must unite. D: D is wrong because a leasehold is not equivalent to fee simple ownership for merger of the easement.
lb_rule_reference: Easement merger requires same owner and same quality of estate
