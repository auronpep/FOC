Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Abraham contracted to build a festival stage for Sarah for $30,000. Before Sarah breached, Abraham reasonably spent $12,000 preparing the site. Sarah proves that, if the contract had been fully performed, Abraham would have had to spend another $25,000 to complete the stage. If Abraham elects reliance damages, what amount may he recover?
Answer A: $12,000, because reliance damages always equal all expenditures before breach.
Answer B: $30,000, because the contract price is the proper reliance measure.
Answer C: Nothing, because Abraham would not have made a profit.
Answer D: $5,000, because Abraham's $12,000 reliance must be reduced by the $7,000 loss he would have suffered had the contract been performed.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages may be reduced by any loss the breaching party proves the injured party would have suffered had the contract been performed. Abraham spent $12,000. If performance would have cost $37,000 total on a $30,000 contract, Abraham would have lost $7,000. His reliance recovery is $12,000 minus $7,000, or $5,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reliance damages are not allowed to put Abraham in a better position than performance would have. B: B is wrong because contract price is an expectation measure, not the reliance measure. C: C is wrong because a losing contract reduces reliance damages; it does not necessarily eliminate all recovery.
