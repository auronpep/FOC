Question: Benjamin agreed to sell monthly textile shipments to Naomi. The signed contract contained a merger clause and a clause requiring written modifications. One shipment arrived five days late, and Naomi claimed the delay caused store-display costs. After the shipment arrived, the parties orally agreed that Naomi would keep the shipment, Benjamin would issue a $3,000 credit, and the late-shipment dispute for that shipment would be closed. Benjamin issued the credit, and Naomi used it on the next invoice. Naomi later sued for additional late-delivery damages from that same shipment. What is Benjamin's strongest Content/Meaning defense?
Answer A: The parol evidence rule bars Naomi's damages claim because the original writing had a merger clause.
Answer B: The no-oral-modification clause makes the later credit agreement legally irrelevant even though Naomi accepted the credit.
Answer C: Naomi's claim fails only if the textile trade has a usage that late shipments never cause damages.
Answer D: The later executed credit agreement can be treated as a post-breach settlement or waiver of that shipment dispute, not as a prior oral term varying the original writing.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a later agreement resolving an existing performance dispute is not governed by the parol evidence rule as if it were a prior term. The trigger facts are the late shipment, the later credit agreement, Benjamin's issuance of the credit, and Naomi's use of it. The decisive lock is timing and function: the agreement addresses a post-breach dispute for one shipment rather than the original contract's integrated content.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the merger clause does not decide a later settlement of a dispute. B: B is wrong because no-oral-modification language is not an automatic answer when the later agreement is executed and operates as waiver or settlement of a claim. C: C is wrong because trade usage is not the relevant route; the parties' own later dispute-resolution conduct is.
