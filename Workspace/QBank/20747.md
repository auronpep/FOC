Outline: Assault
Question: An apartment landlord controlled the only hallway entrance. Tenants reported three assaults in that hallway after the exterior lock broke. The landlord ignored repair requests. A fourth assault occurred in the same hallway through the broken entrance. Does the landlord have a plausible negligence duty?
Answer A: No, because criminal acts always eliminate a landlord's duty
Answer B: No, because tenants control every part of an apartment building
Answer C: Yes, because repeated similar assaults and control of the broken common-area lock support a duty
Answer D: Yes, but only because landlords are vicariously liable for all tenant injuries
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The landlord had notice of a recurring danger in a controlled common area and ignored a security defect tied to that danger.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Prior similar assaults and landlord control of common-area security can make crime foreseeable enough to create a duty. B: The landlord controlled the common hallway entrance and lock. D: The claim is direct negligence for failing to maintain common-area security, not automatic vicarious liability.
lb_rule_reference: A landlord may owe reasonable security duties for foreseeable criminal acts in common areas under the landlord's control.
