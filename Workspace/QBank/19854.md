Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Gideon signed a deed conveying Meadowacre to Naomi as a gift. Gideon placed the original deed in a properly addressed envelope to Naomi, gave it to a courier, and told the courier, “This deed is delivered; Meadowacre is Naomi's now.” The courier lost the envelope before Naomi physically received it. Naomi later learned what happened and accepted the gift. Who owns Meadowacre?
Answer A: Gideon, because Naomi never physically received the original deed.
Answer B: Naomi, because Gideon parted with control of the deed with present-transfer intent and Naomi accepted the beneficial conveyance.
Answer C: Gideon, because a lost deed is automatically void even if delivery was intended.
Answer D: Naomi only if she paid value before the courier lost the envelope.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is Gideon's relinquishment of control with present transfer intent. A grantor need not always complete a hand-to-hand transfer if the deed is put beyond the grantor's control for delivery and the grantee accepts the beneficial conveyance. The courier's loss of the paper did not itself restore title to Gideon.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because physical receipt is not always essential to delivery. C: C is wrong because loss of the instrument after delivery intent and relinquishment of control does not automatically void the transfer. D: D is wrong because consideration is not required for a gift deed.
lb_rule_reference: Delivery can occur without successful hand-to-hand receipt if the grantor parts with control with present-transfer intent
