Outline: Common objections to questions
Question: In a federal breach-of-contract trial about whether Levi delivered wheat on June 1, Levi's lawyer asks a witness, “What color were Martha's kitchen curtains five years earlier?” No party claims the curtains relate to the contract, delivery, credibility, or damages. Martha objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the question seeks irrelevant information.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because all questions about color are prejudicial.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because any question may be asked during direct examination.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because relevance is always for the jury alone.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Evidence must be relevant to a fact of consequence or credibility issue. The color of unrelated curtains five years earlier has no stated connection to the contract dispute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the problem is lack of relevance, not unfair prejudice. C: C is wrong because direct examination is not unlimited. D: D is wrong because the judge screens relevance for admissibility.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401/FRE 611 / relevance objection
