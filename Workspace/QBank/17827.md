Question: Carla is kidnapped by armed criminals. They point guns at her and order her to shoot a security guard who is lying on the floor unarmed. They say they will kill Carla immediately if she refuses. Carla shoots and kills the guard. She is charged with murder and raises duress. Under the common law, what is the result of Carla's duress claim?
Answer A: Duress is a complete defense because Carla faced immediate death
Answer B: Duress reduces murder to manslaughter automatically
Answer C: Duress fails because it is not a common-law defense to intentional homicide
Answer D: Duress fails only because the criminals were private actors rather than government officials
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Carla intentionally killed an innocent, unarmed guard. At common law, duress does not excuse intentional homicide, even when the coercive threat is immediate and deadly. Carla may be morally sympathetic, but the doctrine bars the defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common law creates a categorical exception for intentional homicide. Duress does not justify deliberately killing an innocent person. B: The common-law rule is not automatic mitigation. Duress is unavailable for intentional homicide; it does not itself convert murder into manslaughter. D: Duress usually involves private human threats. The problem is not the source of the threat; it is the intentional-homicide limitation.
lb_rule_reference: At common law, duress is not a defense to intentional homicide, even when the threat is imminent and unavoidable
