Question: Lydia granted a gas company “an easement to inspect, maintain, and repair one buried gas line under the rear strip of Lydia's land.” A technician asked to enter the rear strip during business hours to inspect a valve. The inspection would take fifteen minutes, require no digging, and cause no damage. Lydia refuses, arguing that the company may enter only after a leak has already occurred. Which is the best result?
Answer A: The company may enter at a reasonable time to inspect the valve because inspection is expressly within the easement's scope.
Answer B: The company may enter only after the gas line explodes or leaks.
Answer C: The company may enter any part of Lydia's land at any hour because it holds a utility easement.
Answer D: Lydia may terminate the easement because no repair is currently needed.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The express grant includes inspection, maintenance, and repair. A brief, nondamaging inspection during reasonable hours within the granted strip is directly within that scope and need not await an emergency.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the grant does not require an existing leak. C: C is wrong because the easement is limited by location and reasonableness. D: D is wrong because lack of an immediate repair need does not terminate an inspection easement.
lb_rule_reference: Maintenance easement / reasonable inspection
