Question: Daniel contracted with Esther to buy Esther's store. Daniel promised to pay Esther's mother, Sarah, $2,000 each month for support. Sarah learned of the promise and signed a letter Daniel requested, stating that she accepted the monthly payments. After six months of payments, Daniel and Esther agreed to settle all obligations between them and stop future payments to Sarah. Sarah did not consent. Can Sarah enforce the future monthly payments?
Answer A: Yes, because she was an intended beneficiary whose rights vested by requested assent and acceptance of performance.
Answer B: No, because settlement between the original parties always controls.
Answer C: No, because Sarah was a family member and therefore only a moral beneficiary.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Esther assigns the settlement agreement to Sarah.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Sarah was the intended recipient of direct support payments. Her requested written acceptance and acceptance of performance vested her rights. Daniel and Esther could not later eliminate Sarah's vested future-payment rights without Sarah's consent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because original-party settlement cannot cut off vested beneficiary rights. C: C is wrong because family status is not a bar when the contract shows direct intent. D: D is wrong because assignment is not the enforcement route.
