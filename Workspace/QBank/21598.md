Question: Deborah agreed to buy Caleb's restaurant. The contract stated, “Buyer's duty to purchase is subject to Buyer obtaining a liquor license by September 1.” Deborah promptly filed a complete application, attended every hearing, and supplied every requested document. The city denied the license for reasons unrelated to Deborah's conduct. What is the best result?
Answer A: Deborah must buy because permit conditions are merely covenants.
Answer B: Deborah's purchase duty did not mature because the express license condition failed despite her good-faith efforts.
Answer C: Caleb may recover the purchase price because Deborah substantially performed the license condition.
Answer D: Deborah is liable because she assumed all risk of government denial whenever she signed the contract.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Deborah's duty to purchase was expressly conditioned on obtaining the license. Because she made good-faith efforts and the license was denied for independent reasons, the condition did not occur and her duty did not mature.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the license language created a condition precedent, not merely a covenant. C: C is wrong because substantial performance does not satisfy the express license condition. D: D is wrong because the facts do not show she assumed the risk of denial despite the condition.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law express condition precedent
