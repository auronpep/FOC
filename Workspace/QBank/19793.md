Question: The district court orders a defendant to produce nonprivileged business records. The defendant argues production will be expensive and seeks immediate appeal under the collateral order doctrine. Is immediate appeal likely available?
Answer A: Yes, because costly discovery orders are always collateral orders
Answer B: Yes, because every order separate from the merits is appealable
Answer C: No, because ordinary discovery orders generally wait for final judgment review
Answer D: No, because discovery orders can never be challenged in any way
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The collateral order doctrine is narrow and does not swallow the final judgment rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Cost and inconvenience do not make an ordinary discovery order immediately appealable. B: The doctrine also requires conclusive resolution, importance, and effective unreviewability after final judgment. D: They may be reviewed later or through extraordinary routes in rare cases.
lb_rule_reference: The collateral order doctrine is narrow and does not cover ordinary litigation-management rulings.
