Question: A student annoyed by a classmate's typing took the classmate's laptop from a table and hid it in a nearby cabinet for two hours. The laptop was returned undamaged, but the classmate missed the only window to submit an online assignment. The classmate sues the student for trespass to chattels. Is the classmate likely to prevail in a trespass to chattels claim against the student?
Answer A: Yes, because the student intentionally dispossessed the classmate of the laptop and caused loss of use
Answer B: No, because the laptop was returned undamaged
Answer C: No, because the facts show conversion or nothing
Answer D: Yes, but only if the classmate can recover the laptop's full market value
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Temporarily hiding the laptop was intentional dispossession and caused meaningful loss of use. That supports trespass to chattels.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Trespass to chattels can rest on dispossession or loss of use, not only physical damage. C: The two-hour hiding and undamaged return support trespass to chattels without necessarily reaching the serious dominion required for conversion. D: Full market value is the conversion measure. Trespass to chattels generally measures actual loss, impairment, or loss of use.
lb_rule_reference: Trespass to chattels requires intentional interference causing dispossession, impairment, or loss of use; full-value conversion requires more serious dominion.
