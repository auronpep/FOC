


BARMATRIX Q#: 22085
Outline_code: 81020700
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Rights in Land
Outline: Zoning
Question: A man bought two residential lots on a state barrier island. He intended to build two single- family homes on the lots. Two years after the man bought the property, the state legislature enacted a zoning law that barred anyone from building permanent habitable structures on state barrier islands. The law was aimed to protect against erosion and the destruction of the islands, which were important to sea life. Specifically, endangered sea turtles used the islands during mating season. The man sued, claiming that he was entitled to compensation because the zoning law created a taking of his land. Is the man entitled to compensation?
Answer A: A: No, because the zoning regulation is not a physical appropriation of the land.
Answer B: B: No, because the zoning regulation is a valid protection of the state citizens' health, safety, morals, and welfare.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the zoning regulation denied the man all economic use of his land.
Answer D: D: Yes, because his intended use was grandfathered in before the zoning regulation was passed.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: If a zoning regulation takes away all economic use of the land, the regulation results in a taking under the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments, and the landowner is entitled to just compensation. Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992). Here, because the owner cannot build any permanent structure on his lots, the zoning regulation has taken away all economic use of his land. A is incorrect because a physical appropriation is not required for a taking. B is incorrect because even if the zoning regulation was valid, it still resulted in a taking, entitling the man to just compensation. D is incorrect because the man's compensation rights were not created by the fact that he had the land two years before the regulation was passed.
