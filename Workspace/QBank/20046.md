Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Affirmative duties to act
Question: A boat operator negligently steered too close to a swimmer, creating a wake that pushed the swimmer into rocks. The operator saw the swimmer struggling in the water and could have safely thrown a flotation ring, but instead left. The swimmer's injuries worsened before another boat arrived. Which statement best describes the operator's duty?
Answer A: The operator had no duty because there is never a duty to rescue
Answer B: The operator had a duty to use reasonable care because the operator created the peril
Answer C: The operator had a duty only if the swimmer paid for rescue services
Answer D: The operator is strictly liable for every later injury because a duty arose
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The operator's negligent wake put the swimmer in danger, creating a duty to take reasonable aid measures.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Creating the peril is a recognized affirmative-duty exception. C: Risk creation can create a duty even without a contract. D: The duty is to use reasonable care; plaintiff still must prove breach, causation, and damages.
lb_rule_reference: A person who creates a risk or peril generally has a duty to use reasonable care to aid or protect the endangered person.
