Outline: Marital Communications Privilege
Question: Noah told his wife Ruth, “I hid the delivery truck,” while their adult neighbor Mary was sitting with them at the table. In a later federal theft prosecution, the government calls Ruth to repeat Noah's statement. Noah asserts marital communications privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because the words were spoken to Noah's wife.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because the presence of an unnecessary third party defeats confidentiality.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Mary also testifies.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Noah and Ruth are divorced at trial.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The marital communications privilege requires a confidential communication between spouses. Speaking in the presence of an unnecessary adult third party defeats confidentiality on these facts. The decisive axis is confidentiality, not the marital relationship alone.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is relationship shortcut. A statement to a spouse is not privileged unless it is confidential. C: C is wrong because The third party's testimony is not needed to destroy confidentiality; the communication was made in that person's presence. D: D is wrong because Divorce is not the decisive fact. The problem is lack of confidentiality when the statement was made.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / confidentiality and third-party presence
lb_student_diagnostic: Student recognizes a marital communication but misses the confidentiality gate.
