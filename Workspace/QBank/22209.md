


BARMATRIX Q#: 22209
Outline_code: 32020302
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: The defendant was on trial for kidnapping and raping a young woman. The defendant sought to introduce evidence at trial that a few weeks prior to the incident, the woman's mother had found birth control pills in the woman's purse. When she found the pills, the woman's mother allegedly told the woman that she didn't want her to see her boyfriend so often. The defendant argued that this event gave the woman a motive for accompanying him and engaging in sex with him. Should the defendant be allowed to introduce evidence of the woman's mother finding the birth control pills?
Answer A: A: No, because evidence used to prove the sexual behavior of the alleged victim is generally inadmissible.
Answer B: B: No, because there is no clear connection between the finding of the birth control pills and the mother telling the woman that she didn't want her to see her boyfriend so often.
Answer C: C: Yes, but only as evidence of the woman's desire to accompany the defendant.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the possession of birth control pills was not evidence of sexual behavior.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 412(a), evidence of a rape victim's past sexual behavior is inadmissible. Consequently, the court would not allow the defendant to introduce evidence of the birth control pills. B is incorrect because FRE 412(a)'s exclusion does not depend upon the evidence's connection to other events. C and D are incorrect because the evidence is inadmissible.
