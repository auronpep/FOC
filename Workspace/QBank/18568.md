Outline: Settlement negotiations and offers of compromise
Question: Immediately after a minor collision, before anyone had made a claim or disputed liability, the driver said to the other motorist, “I ran the red light, and I will pay for the bumper.” At trial, the other motorist offers the driver's statement “I ran the red light” to prove negligence. The driver invokes Rule 408. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the statement because no disputed claim or compromise negotiation existed when it was made.
Answer B: Exclude the statement because any offer to pay after an accident is a compromise negotiation.
Answer C: Exclude the statement because Rule 408 bars all admissions connected to possible settlement.
Answer D: Admit the statement only if the driver repeats it at trial.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 408 requires compromise negotiations about a disputed claim. A spontaneous post-accident admission before a claim or dispute exists is not barred by Rule 408 merely because it includes an offer to pay. The decisive axis is trigger fact: disputed claim and compromise posture.

C is wrong because Potential settlement is not enough; the statement was not made during compromise negotiations about a disputed claim.
