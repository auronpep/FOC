Outline: Motion for new trial
Question: A jury returns a verdict for a plaintiff after a close products trial. The defendant preserved JMOL but cannot show the plaintiff had no legally sufficient evidentiary basis. The judge nevertheless concludes the verdict is seriously against the weight of the evidence and that a new jury should hear the case. Which post-trial remedy best matches the judge's concern?
Answer A: Renewed JMOL, because any disagreement with the jury's weighing requires judgment for the defendant
Answer B: Relief from judgment for clerical mistake
Answer C: Dismissal for lack of subject matter jurisdiction
Answer D: New trial, because the verdict may be set aside for being against the weight of the evidence
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: New trial relief can order another trial without entering judgment for the movant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: JMOL is for legal insufficiency, not simply a verdict the judge finds against the weight of the evidence. B: A weight-of-the-evidence concern is not a clerical error in the judgment. C: The problem concerns post-trial evidentiary weight, not adjudicatory power.
lb_rule_reference: A court may grant a new trial when the verdict is against the weight of the evidence, even when the stricter JMOL standard is not met.
