Question: After games, a public school coach quietly kneels alone at midfield for a brief personal prayer after players are released to leave. The school disciplines him under a rule banning all employee religious expression visible to students, even when the employee is not leading students or speaking for the school. What is the coach's strongest argument?
Answer A: The school may ban the prayer because any visible religious expression by a public school employee establishes religion
Answer B: The discipline is constitutionally suspect because the prayer is private expression and the facts do not show school-led coercion
Answer C: The coach wins automatically because school employees have unrestricted speech rights at all school events
Answer D: The school may discipline him only if every player actually complains
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The coach is not leading students or speaking through an official program. A blanket ban on private religious expression can burden Free Speech and Free Exercise without being required by Establishment concerns.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Establishment Clause does not require schools to suppress all private religious expression by employees. C: Public schools can regulate employee conduct in some settings, especially if coercion or official endorsement exists. The facts here matter. D: Coercion analysis does not require every student to complain, and lack of complaints is not the sole issue.
lb_rule_reference: School prayer cases require attention to government speech and coercion; private religious expression by a school employee is not automatically establishment.
