Question: In a federal false-imprisonment case, Officer Ezra testifies that he detained Gideon after hearing a radio dispatch stating, “A man in a green jacket just robbed the market.” Gideon objects that the dispatch is hearsay. Officer Ezra offers the dispatch only to show why he detained Gideon, not to prove that a robbery actually occurred. How should the court treat the dispatch?
Answer A: Exclude it because every out-of-court statement is hearsay.
Answer B: Admit it for the limited purpose of explaining Officer Ezra's conduct, with an appropriate limiting instruction if requested.
Answer C: Admit it as substantive proof that Gideon robbed the market.
Answer D: Submit to the jury the question whether the dispatch is hearsay.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The judge determines the admissible purpose. If the statement is offered to show effect on the listener or reason for action rather than truth, it is not hearsay for that limited purpose, and the court may give a limiting instruction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because hearsay depends on truth purpose. C: C is wrong because the offered purpose is limited and nontruth. D: D is wrong because admissibility and limiting purpose are for the court.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a), 105, 801(c)
