Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: After discovery closes in a product-liability case, the manufacturer moves for summary judgment. The manufacturer points out that the plaintiff has no expert testimony or other admissible evidence that the product was defective. Defect is an element on which the plaintiff bears the trial burden. The plaintiff responds only that the manufacturer has not conclusively proven the product was safe. How should the court treat the motion?
Answer A: Deny it because the manufacturer must conclusively disprove defect
Answer B: Deny it because plaintiffs never need evidence until trial
Answer C: Grant it if the plaintiff lacks evidence on defect after adequate discovery
Answer D: Grant it only if the manufacturer files a Rule 50 motion first
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff bears the burden on defect and has no evidence creating a genuine dispute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A movant can point to the absence of evidence on the nonmovant's essential element. B: At Rule 56, the nonmovant must point to admissible record evidence, not just allegations. D: Rule 50 applies at trial; Rule 56 governs pretrial summary judgment.
lb_rule_reference: After adequate discovery, summary judgment may be granted when the nonmovant fails to produce evidence creating a genuine dispute on an essential element.
