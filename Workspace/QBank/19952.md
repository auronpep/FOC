Question: For five years, Caleb bought grain from Lydia under annual written contracts. In each prior year, Lydia allowed Caleb a 2% discount for payment within ten days, though the writings were silent. This year's signed grain contract contains a merger clause and is also silent on discounts. Caleb pays within ten days and deducts 2%. Lydia objects that the merger clause bars all prior course evidence. Should evidence of the prior discount practice be admitted?
Answer A: No, because a merger clause in a goods contract bars all course-of-dealing evidence.
Answer B: No, because the prior contracts were separate agreements.
Answer C: Yes, because prior transactions between the same parties may establish a course of dealing that explains or supplements the current goods contract.
Answer D: Yes, but only if the discount is a usage of trade followed by every grain seller in the region.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Course of dealing is a sequence of prior transactions between the parties that may establish a common basis for interpreting their agreement. Under the UCC, course-of-dealing evidence may explain or supplement even a final goods writing when reasonably consistent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a merger clause does not automatically eliminate UCC course evidence. B: B is wrong because prior contracts are precisely the source of course-of-dealing evidence. D: D is wrong because a private course of dealing can matter even without industry-wide usage.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 1-303 / course of dealing despite merger clause
