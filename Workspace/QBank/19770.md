Question: Mara takes a new prescription exactly as directed. The label warns only of mild drowsiness. Unknown to Mara and her doctor, the medication causes a rare psychotic reaction in her. During the episode, Mara walks out of a store with merchandise while believing she is carrying her own belongings from her apartment. She is charged with larceny. Which statement best describes Mara's intoxication defense?
Answer A: It fails because Mara knowingly swallowed the medication
Answer B: It may succeed because the unexpected prescribed-drug reaction could be involuntary intoxication and negate larceny intent
Answer C: It fails because intoxication can never negate larceny
Answer D: It succeeds automatically because every prescription side effect is involuntary intoxication
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Mara took the medication as prescribed and had no reason to know it would cause psychosis. That can make the intoxication involuntary. Her belief that she carried her own belongings also negates larceny's intent to steal another's property. The defense is therefore viable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The relevant question is whether she knowingly consumed an intoxicant or had reason to anticipate the intoxicating effect. Taking a prescribed medication as directed without warning can support involuntary intoxication. C: Larceny requires intent to permanently deprive another of property. Intoxication, especially involuntary intoxication, can negate that required mental state. D: Not every side effect qualifies. The defense depends on whether the defendant had reason to know of the intoxicating effect and whether the effect negated mens rea or met the insanity standard.
lb_rule_reference: Intoxication from prescribed medication can be involuntary when taken as directed without reason to know it would cause an intoxicating or psychotic reaction
