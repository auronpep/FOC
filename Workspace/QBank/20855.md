Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A zoo kept a rare wild bird in an enclosed aviary. A visitor standing outside the enclosure suffered an allergic reaction to pollen from ornamental flowers planted along the visitor path. The bird did not escape, touch the visitor, or cause the pollen exposure. The visitor sues the zoo for strict liability based on possession of a wild animal. Is the zoo strictly liable under wild-animal strict liability?
Answer A: Yes, because a wild animal was nearby when the visitor was injured
Answer B: No, because the allergic reaction did not result from the wild bird's characteristic danger
Answer C: Yes, because strict liability covers every injury at a zoo
Answer D: No, because wild-animal liability requires proof that the zoo was negligent
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Wild-animal liability is not a general premises-insurance rule. The injury must flow from the animal's relevant risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The harm came from unrelated pollen, not from the bird's characteristic dangerous propensities. C: The rule is tied to the animal and its risks, not every condition on the premises. D: Negligence is not required for wild-animal strict liability. The claim fails because the harm is outside the animal risk.
lb_rule_reference: Wild-animal strict liability is limited to harms resulting from the animal's characteristic dangerous propensities, not every unrelated condition near the animal.
