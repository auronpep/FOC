Question: A plaintiff files a complaint, and the defendant files an answer. No counterclaim is asserted. Two weeks later, with trial months away, the defendant moves under Rule 12(c) for judgment on the pleadings, arguing that the pleadings show a complete legal defense. The plaintiff argues that Rule 12(c) is unavailable because the defendant already answered. Is Rule 12(c) available in this posture?
Answer A: Yes, because the pleadings are closed and the motion is early enough not to delay trial
Answer B: No, because judgment on the pleadings must be filed before any answer
Answer C: No, because Rule 12(c) requires completed discovery
Answer D: Yes, but only if both parties attach affidavits
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Once the complaint and answer are filed and no reply is required, pleadings are closed. Rule 12(c) is designed for this post-answer posture if it will not delay trial.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Rule 12(c) is specifically available after the pleadings are closed, which usually means after the answer. C: Rule 12(c) is based on the pleadings, not a completed factual record. Discovery completion is not required. D: A Rule 12(c) motion ordinarily tests the pleadings. Outside materials can change the posture, but affidavits are not required.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 12(c) allows a party to move for judgment on the pleadings after the pleadings are closed, but early enough not to delay trial.
