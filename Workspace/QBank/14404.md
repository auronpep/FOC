Question: On November 1, an accountant and a lawyer contracted for the sale by the accountant to the lawyer of the law books the accountant had inherited from his father. The lawyer agreed to pay the purchase price of $10,000 when the accountant delivered the books on December 1. On November 10, the lawyer received a signed letter from the accountant that stated: "I have decided to dispose of the book stacks containing the law books you have already purchased. If you want the stacks, I will deliver them to you along with the books on December 1 at no additional cost to you. Let me know before November 15 whether you want them. I will not sell them to anyone else before then." On November 14, the lawyer faxed and the accountant received the following message: "I accept your offer of the stacks." The accountant was not a merchant with respect to either law books or book stacks. The accountant is contractually obligated to deliver the stacks because
Answer A: A: the lawyer provided a new bargained-for exchange by agreeing to take the stacks.
Answer B: B: the accountant's letter (received by the lawyer on November 10) and the lawyer's fax- message of November 14 constituted an effective modification of the original sale-of-books contract.
Answer C: C: the lawyer's fax-message of November 14 operated to rescind unilaterally the original sale-of- books contract.
Answer D: D: the accountant's letter (received by the lawyer on November 10) waived the bargained-for consideration that would otherwise be required.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. The accountant proposed a unilateral modification of the contract, which the lawyer validly accepted before it lapsed. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Agreeing to take the stacks will not constitute new consideration. C is incorrect. The lawyer's November 14 communication served to accept the modification, without discharging the original obligation. D is incorrect. The UCC allows for modification without new consideration.
