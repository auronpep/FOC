Question: Noah conveyed Orchardacre “to Peter so long as Peter uses the land as an orchard, and if Peter stops using it as an orchard during Peter's life, then to Paul.” Peter later stopped using the land as an orchard during his life and opened a store. Who owns Orchardacre after Peter opens the store?
Answer A: Peter, because “so long as” creates only a covenant.
Answer B: Noah, because “so long as” always leaves a possibility of reverter in the grantor.
Answer C: Peter, unless Noah reenters.
Answer D: Paul, because Peter's estate automatically shifted to the named third party.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Although “so long as” is durational language, the deed gives the future interest to Paul, a third party. Peter therefore has a fee simple subject to executory limitation, not a fee simple determinable. When the stated event occurs during Peter's life, title automatically shifts to Paul.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the deed contains operative limitation and gift-over language, not merely a promise. B: B is wrong because the grantor did not retain the future interest. C: C is wrong because no right of entry is reserved to Noah.
