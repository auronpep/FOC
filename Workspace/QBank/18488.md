Question: Noah sues "John Doe Driver" before limitations expires because he does not know who drove the truck. The actual driver receives no notice within the Rule 4(m) period. After limitations expires, Noah learns the driver's name and seeks relation back. Is relation back likely?
Answer A: Yes, because John Doe pleading always satisfies mistake
Answer B: Yes, because notice after limitations is enough
Answer C: No, because the new driver lacked timely notice and the omission was not shown to be a Rule 15(c) mistake
Answer D: No, because claims against drivers can never be amended
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The party-change requirements are not met on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Lack of knowledge is not automatically the required mistake. B: Timely notice to the new party is required. D: Proper party-change amendments can relate back.
lb_rule_reference: Party-change relation back requires that the new party knew or should have known it would have been named but for a mistake concerning identity.
