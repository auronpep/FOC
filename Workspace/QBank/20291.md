Question: In a trademark case, the district court enters an order labeled "case management order." The order commands the defendant to stop using the challenged mark in all advertising until trial. The defendant immediately appeals, arguing the order is functionally an injunction. Is the defendant's characterization likely right?
Answer A: Yes, because the order commands the defendant to stop doing something and functions as an injunction
Answer B: Yes, because every case-management order is appealable
Answer C: No, because only orders using the word "injunction" can be appealed immediately
Answer D: No, because injunction orders can never be appealed before final judgment
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Injunction appealability turns on the order's practical effect, not only the caption.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Routine case-management orders are not automatically appealable. C: Courts look to substance and effect, not just labels. D: Injunction orders are a recognized statutory exception to the final judgment rule.
lb_rule_reference: Orders granting, refusing, modifying, or dissolving injunctions are generally immediately appealable, even if the district court uses another label.
