


BARMATRIX Q#: 22840
Outline_code: 95070400
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Discovery
Question: A woman was injured by a falling brick as she passed a construction site. The construction site's insurance company interviewed all of the construction workers at the scene and wrote down their responses relating to the accident and possible instances of negligence. The insurance company gave a transcript of the interviews to the construction site owner. The woman sued the owner and sought a copy of the transcript. She argued that she needed the transcript because the workers would be hostile to any of her lawyer's questions out of concern with keeping their jobs. The owner objected to the woman's request. Will the court grant the woman's request for the interview transcripts?
Answer A: A: No, because they are protected as work product.
Answer B: B: No, because they are protected by qualified immunity.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the transcripts were prepared by the insurance company.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the woman claimed the workers would be hostile to her attorney's questions.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(3)(A) gives qualified immunity for “documents and tangible things that are prepared in anticipation of litigation.” Here, the interview transcripts would count under this rule. However, the Rule also states that such documents are discoverable if “the party shows that it has substantial need for the materials to prepare its case and cannot, without undue hardship, obtain their substantial equivalent by other means.” A court would likely find the woman's argument regarding the hostility of the workers valid and allow the woman to obtain the transcripts because the workers' hostility would make the information impossible to obtain without “undue hardship.” A and B are incorrect because the woman could likely obtain the transcripts for the reason stated above. C is incorrect because the transcripts would still be protected by qualified immunity even if they were prepared by the insurance company. Consequently, the issue is whether the woman can show undue hardship in obtaining the information.
