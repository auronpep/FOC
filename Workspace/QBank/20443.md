Question: A privately owned software company fires an employee without notice or a hearing after an internal investigation. The company has no government contract, receives no public funding, and is not performing a traditional exclusive public function. The employee sues the company directly under the Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause. What is the company's strongest constitutional defense?
Answer A: Due process does not apply because the firing is purely private conduct not fairly attributable to the state
Answer B: Due process applies because any unfair private firing is a constitutional deprivation
Answer C: Due process applies because all corporations are state actors
Answer D: Due process applies only if the employee seeks money damages rather than reinstatement
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Fourteenth Amendment requires state action.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Constitutional due process is not a general rule for private employment fairness. C: Corporate status alone does not make decisions state action. D: The state-action requirement does not turn on the requested remedy.
lb_rule_reference: The Due Process Clauses restrict government action, not purely private conduct, unless the private actor's conduct is fairly attributable to the state.
