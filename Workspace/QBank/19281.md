Outline: Duress
Question: An armed kidnapper video-calls Lina and shows Lina's child tied to a chair. The kidnapper says, "Transfer the company's emergency cash account now or I will shoot your child while you watch." Lina is alone in a locked office, the transfer takes seconds, and she has no way to alert police before the threatened shooting. Lina transfers the funds and is charged with embezzlement. Does Lina have a duress defense?
Answer A: No, because the threat was not directed at Lina personally
Answer B: No, because duress is unavailable for all financial crimes
Answer C: Yes, because she faced an imminent threat to her child and had no reasonable legal alternative
Answer D: Yes, because any threat to property supports duress
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The threat was immediate, involved serious bodily harm or death to Lina's child, came from a human coercer, and left no time for legal alternatives. The charged offense is nonhomicide. Duress is available.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Duress can include imminent threats to close family members. Lina's child is within that protected category. B: Duress can excuse nonhomicide financial crimes if all elements are met. D: The qualifying threat is to the child's life, not to property. Threats to property alone ordinarily do not satisfy duress.
lb_rule_reference: Duress may be based on an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm to the defendant or a close family member, if no reasonable legal alternative exists
