Question: Gang members force Dana at gunpoint to swallow pills that they say will "make her cooperate." The pills cause a severe psychotic reaction. Minutes later, Dana breaks a store window while believing she is opening a curtain in her own home. She is charged with malicious destruction of property. Which statement best describes Dana's intoxication defense?
Answer A: She has no defense because intoxication is never a complete excuse
Answer B: She may have a complete defense because the intoxication was coerced and prevented her from understanding the nature of her act
Answer C: She has only a partial defense because all intoxication rules are limited to specific intent
Answer D: She has no defense unless the gang members intended the exact property damage
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Dana did not voluntarily ingest the pills; she was forced at gunpoint. The resulting psychosis made her believe she was opening a curtain rather than breaking a store window. Involuntary intoxication can completely excuse when it negates the required mental state or creates a state equivalent to legal insanity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Involuntary intoxication is treated differently and can be a complete defense if it negates mens rea or satisfies the applicable insanity standard. C: The specific-intent limitation does not govern involuntary intoxication in the same way. Involuntary intoxication may be a full excuse. D: Dana's defense turns on her coerced ingestion and resulting mental state, not on whether the coercers predicted the exact property damage.
lb_rule_reference: Involuntary intoxication caused by coercion or unknowing ingestion can be a complete defense if it negates mens rea or creates a condition equivalent to legal insanity
