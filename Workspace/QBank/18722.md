Question: A student is suspended for a semester after a university disciplinary hearing. She sues alleging denial of due process. By the time the appeal is heard, the semester has ended, but the suspension remains on her permanent record and affects scholarship eligibility. Is the case necessarily moot because the suspension term ended?
Answer A: Yes, because the semester is over and the court cannot turn back time
Answer B: Yes, because due process claims always expire after punishment ends
Answer C: No, because the continuing record and scholarship effects may be redressable collateral consequences
Answer D: No, but only because all student cases evade review
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may still grant effective relief by addressing continuing consequences of the challenged suspension.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ongoing collateral consequences can keep the dispute live. B: Completed action can still have ongoing legal or practical consequences. D: The stated facts support ongoing consequences; no broad student-case rule is needed.
lb_rule_reference: A case is not moot if the challenged judgment or action continues to impose collateral legal consequences that effective relief can address.
