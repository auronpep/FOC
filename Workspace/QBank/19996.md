Outline: False imprisonment
Question: Freight supervisor Tomas locked a storage warehouse from the outside while worker Priya was inside, believing the facility was empty at the end of the shift. When Priya realized she was locked in, she found that a small ventilation window six feet off the ground was unlatched. Priya, a petite woman of five feet two inches, could not safely reach the window without a ladder; there was no ladder inside. She waited four hours until another worker arrived and unlocked the building. Priya suffered no physical injury but experienced significant distress. Tomas claims no false imprisonment occurred because the ventilation window was available. Under common law, did Tomas commit false imprisonment of Priya?
Answer A: No, because Tomas did not know Priya was inside and therefore lacked the intent to confine her
Answer B: No, because the ventilation window provided Priya with a means of egress, so she was not completely confined
Answer C: Yes, because all reasonable means of egress were blocked — the window was not a reasonable escape route given its height and Priya's inability to reach it safely
Answer D: Yes, but only if Priya was aware of her confinement throughout the entire four-hour period
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Common-law false imprisonment requires: (1) intentional (2) confinement of another (3) within boundaries set by the defendant, (4) with no reasonable means of escape. The ventilation window at six feet with no ladder does not constitute a reasonable means of escape for a person of Priya's stature and without equipment to reach it. Courts have consistently held that confinement is established when the only theoretical exits are unreasonable, dangerous, or practically unavailable. Whether Tomas knew Priya was inside affects his culpability level but on these facts the confinement element is the focus.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: False imprisonment at common law requires the intent to confine, which can be satisfied by conduct that knowingly or unlawfully restricts another's freedom. The key analytical issue on these facts is the egress question, not intent. Many jurisdictions also recognize that negligent confinement can give rise to civil false imprisonment even without specific criminal intent, though the criminal charge typically requires intentional restraint. On the bar exam, the intended issue here is the egress element. B: The standard is not whether any theoretical exit exists, but whether all reasonable means of egress were blocked. A window six feet up with no ladder, practically unreachable by the victim given her physical characteristics, is not a reasonable means of escape. Courts look to whether the victim can practically and safely exit, not merely whether a hole in the structure exists. D: The common law is split on whether the victim must be conscious of the confinement at the time it occurs, but even jurisdictions requiring awareness do not require continuous awareness for the entire duration. Priya was aware of her confinement once she realized she was locked in. There is no requirement that the victim was aware the entire time. This answer imposes a standard that no jurisdiction applies.
lb_rule_reference: False imprisonment requires that all reasonable means of egress be blocked; a theoretical means of escape that is unreasonable or dangerous does not negate the confinement
