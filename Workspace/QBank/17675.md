Question: A bus driver negligently slammed the bus door on a passenger's backpack. A small, sealed device hidden in the backpack emitted a harmless radio pulse that unexpectedly disabled a sculptor's pacemaker in a studio two blocks away. Nothing about the backpack suggested electronics or danger beyond the bus doorway. Under the majority Palsgraf approach, what is the driver's best defense to the sculptor's negligence claim?
Answer A: The sculptor was an unforeseeable plaintiff outside the apparent zone of danger
Answer B: The driver owed no duty to anyone on the bus
Answer C: The sculptor loses only because physical injury was absent
Answer D: The driver is strictly liable because the device was inside the backpack
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The driver could foresee harm near the doorway, not a hidden remote pacemaker injury two blocks away.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The driver did owe duties to foreseeable passengers and nearby persons. C: The facts include a pacemaker injury; the issue is foreseeable plaintiff and risk. D: Negligence does not become strict liability merely because an unusual device was involved.
lb_rule_reference: Under the majority Palsgraf approach, no duty is owed to an unforeseeable plaintiff outside the zone of apparent danger.
