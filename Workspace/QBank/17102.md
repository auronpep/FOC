Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Benjamin is charged with vandalizing a school window with a rock. A custodian found a rock with fresh glass dust on the windowsill five minutes after the break. The prosecutor offers the rock. Benjamin objects that many rocks were on the school grounds and no one saw him throw that specific rock. How should the court rule, assuming the rock is authenticated as the one found on the sill?
Answer A: Admit the rock because its location and condition have some tendency to connect it to the broken window; the lack of an eyewitness goes to weight.
Answer B: Exclude the rock because no witness saw Benjamin throw it.
Answer C: Exclude the rock unless the prosecutor proves by a preponderance that it was the exact rock thrown.
Answer D: Admit the rock only if Benjamin admits he touched it.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The rock's location and glass dust make it probative of how the window was broken. The fact that many rocks existed and no witness saw the throw weakens the connection but does not destroy relevance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because direct eyewitness proof is not required for relevance. C: C is wrong because the proponent need not prove the exact merits fact by a preponderance before admission. D: D is wrong because an admission by Benjamin is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Demanding eyewitness or conclusive connection for weak physical evidence.
