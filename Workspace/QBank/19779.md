Question: A retailer signed a contract to buy crates of pears from Naomi at $40 per crate. The contract contained both a merger clause and a signed clause requiring written modifications. After packaging costs fell, Naomi invoiced the next six monthly shipments at $38 per crate, and the retailer paid those invoices. Naomi accepted the payments without objection. For the seventh shipment, Naomi invoiced $40 per crate and sued when the retailer paid $38. The retailer offers the six-month invoicing and payment history. Should the court admit the history for the purpose of showing waiver or modification of the price term?
Answer A: No, because a merger clause bars all evidence that is outside the original writing.
Answer B: No, because an express price term can never be affected by later course of performance.
Answer C: Yes, because the post-signing invoice-and-payment history is relevant to waiver or modification, subject to any valid prospective retraction issue.
Answer D: Yes, because trade usage always controls over the parties' written price.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that post-signing course of performance is relevant to show waiver or modification of a term inconsistent with that conduct, subject to Article 2's modification and retraction rules. The trigger facts are the six post-signing invoices at $38, the retailer's matching payments, and Naomi's acceptance without objection. The decisive locks are timing and offered purpose: the retailer is not using prior negotiations to contradict the writing; it is using later performance to show the operative price practice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because merger-clause absolutism does not bar post-signing waiver evidence. B: B is wrong because express terms can be waived or modified by later conduct under the proper rules. D: D is wrong because the case turns on the parties' own course of performance, not an industry usage hierarchy.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 1-303(f); UCC 2-209 / course performance as waiver or modification
