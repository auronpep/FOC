Question: Adam granted Eve “an easement to maintain one buried drainage pipe across Adam's pasture.” When the pipe clogged, Eve had the right to enter the pasture reasonably to repair it. Instead, Eve paved a permanent service road across the pasture to make future access easier. The service road was not described in the grant and prevented Adam from grazing that portion of the pasture. Adam sues to remove the paved road. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Eve may keep the road because any maintenance right includes any permanent structure that makes future maintenance easier.
Answer B: Eve may keep the road because a drainage easement gives title to the surface above the pipe.
Answer C: Adam may remove the entire drainage pipe because Eve exceeded the easement once.
Answer D: Adam should prevail against the paved road because reasonable repair access does not authorize a permanent additional burden outside the grant.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement holder may make reasonable use necessary to maintain the easement, but may not impose a materially greater permanent burden not granted. Temporary entry to repair a buried pipe is different from paving a permanent service road that interferes with grazing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because convenience for future maintenance does not authorize every permanent improvement. B: B is wrong because an easement is not fee title to the surface. C: C is wrong because misuse does not automatically terminate or destroy the valid pipe easement. D is correct because the road exceeds reasonable maintenance scope.
lb_rule_reference: Maintenance access / no permanent expansion
