


BARMATRIX Q#: 22853
Outline_code: 92080105
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: A man sued a county fair for negligence in federal court. The man claimed that he was injured at the fair when a rollercoaster he was riding in went off its tracks. A few days after discovery was closed, the fair filed a motion for summary judgment. In support of its motion, the fair filed the sworn affidavits of three employees and three other people who all said the man was actually watching a magic show on the other side of the fair when the rollercoaster accident happened. In response, the man filed his own affidavit claiming that he was at the fair at the time of the accident. He also filed the affidavits of two people who stated that they heard from other people at the fair that the man had been on the rollercoaster at the time of the accident. Should the court grant the fair's motion for summary judgment?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the fair supported its motion with the affidavits of three people who were not fair employees.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the man did not sufficiently contradict the fair's evidence.
Answer C: C: No, because the man supported his motion with the affidavits of two witnesses besides himself.
Answer D: D: No, because the fair did not file the affidavit of any officially designated representative.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A court “shall grant summary judgment if the movant shows that there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a). Importantly, to defend against a summary judgment motion, the other party must produce admissible evidence that shows that there is a genuine dispute as to a material fact. The fair produced six affidavits stating that the man was at a magic show at the time of the rollercoaster accident. In response, the man has only produced three affidavits. The man's affidavit only states that he was at the fair at the time of the accident, not that he was on the rollercoaster. The other two affidavits are inadmissible hearsay because the people only stated that they had heard from others that the man was on the rollercoaster. Thus, the man did not produce any admissible evidence to show a genuine dispute, so the court should grant the fair's summary judgment motion. A is incorrect because there does not appear to be any dispute regarding the veracity of the witnesses. C is incorrect because the other affidavits were inadmissible hearsay. D is incorrect because the fair did not have to support its motion with any specific type of affidavit.
