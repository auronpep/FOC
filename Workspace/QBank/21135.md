MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Damages
Question: A driver negligently ran a stop sign and missed a pedestrian by a few inches. The pedestrian was startled but did not fall, seek medical care, lose wages, suffer physical symptoms, or suffer property damage. The pedestrian sues the driver for negligence and asks for nominal damages to vindicate the driver's breach of duty. Is the pedestrian likely to recover nominal damages in a negligence claim against the driver?
Answer A: Yes, because the driver breached a duty of reasonable care
Answer B: Yes, because nominal damages are available for every tort
Answer C: No, because negligence requires actual harm and the pedestrian suffered none
Answer D: No, because drivers owe no duty to pedestrians who are not actually hit
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The pedestrian alleges a breach but no personal injury, property damage, physical symptoms, lost wages, or other compensable loss. Negligence therefore fails at the damages element.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Negligence requires actual harm. Breach alone does not support nominal damages in a negligence claim. B: Nominal damages may be available for some intentional dignitary or property torts, but they generally are not available for negligence absent actual loss. D: Drivers owe reasonable care to foreseeable pedestrians. The missing element is damages, not duty.
lb_rule_reference: Negligence is not actionable without legally cognizable actual harm; nominal damages are generally unavailable for negligence.
