Question: After indictment for forgery, police obtain a court order requiring Aaron to copy several neutral sentences so experts can compare his handwriting to a forged check. Aaron's lawyer is not present. Aaron argues that the handwriting sample must be suppressed because counsel was absent. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The sample is not suppressible on Sixth Amendment grounds because providing a handwriting exemplar is not generally a critical stage
Answer B: The sample must be suppressed because every post-indictment police procedure requires counsel
Answer C: The sample must be suppressed because handwriting is testimonial
Answer D: The sample is admissible only if Aaron chose the sentences himself
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Physical exemplars ordinarily do not involve legal confrontation or strategic rights requiring counsel. The postcharge timing alone does not make every evidence-gathering event critical.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Attachment does not mean counsel is required at every event. The event must be a critical stage. C: A handwriting exemplar is generally physical identifying evidence, not testimonial content. D: The counsel issue does not turn on who selected neutral exemplar text.
lb_rule_reference: Compelling a defendant to provide a physical exemplar, such as handwriting, is generally not a critical stage requiring counsel, even after formal charges.
