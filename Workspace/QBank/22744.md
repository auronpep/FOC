


BARMATRIX Q#: 22744
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A State A university filed a request for a permanent injunction in State B federal court to stop a business from posting online advertisements that made fun of the university's football coach. The business was incorporated in State B and had its primary place of business in State C, but the majority of the business's stores were in State A. The university believed that stopping the advertisements was worth $100,000. The business calculated that stopping the advertisements would cost it $20,000 in lost business. The business moved for dismissal, arguing that the federal court did not have jurisdiction over the university's complaint. Does the State B federal court have jurisdiction over the university's complaint?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the business was incorporated in State B.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the university thought stopping the advertisements was worth $100,000.
Answer C: C: No, because the business calculated that the cost of stopping the advertisements was $20,000.
Answer D: D: No, because the majority of the business's stores were in State A.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: In determining whether an injunction meets the $75,000 amount in controversy requirement for diversity jurisdiction, courts look at the larger of the injunction's value to the plaintiff or cost to the defendant. Here, since the university believes that the injunction is worth $100,000 the claim exceeds the $75,000 threshold. A is incorrect because the simple fact the business was incorporated in State B would not automatically give the State B federal court jurisdiction over the claim. C is incorrect because courts look at the larger of the injunction's value to the plaintiff or cost to the defendant when determining the amount- in- controversy requirement. D is incorrect because the university's claim meets diversity requirements. The university is a citizen of State A. For corporations, citizenship is determined by the corporation's primary place of business and place of incorporation. Here, the question states that the business is incorporated in State B and has its primary place of business in State C despite the fact that the majority of the business's stores are in State A.
