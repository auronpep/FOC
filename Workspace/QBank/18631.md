Question: A state statute allows residents to file personal-injury suits in state court with ordinary filing fees, but requires citizens of other states to post a large security bond before the same claims may proceed. The state offers no evidence that nonresident plaintiffs create special collection problems. What is the strongest Article IV argument?
Answer A: Nonresident court access for ordinary civil claims is a protected privilege, and the state lacks a substantial justification
Answer B: The bond is valid because states may always favor residents in their courts
Answer C: The bond violates only DCC because lawsuits are commerce
Answer D: The bond is invalid only if the plaintiff is a corporation
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Access to courts is an important privilege, and the facts do not show a substantial reason or fit for the discriminatory bond.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Article IV limits discrimination against nonresident citizens in access to important civil processes. C: The direct burden is on nonresident citizens' access to courts, an Article IV privilege. D: Article IV protects natural citizens of other states, not corporations.
lb_rule_reference: Article IV protects nonresident citizens from discrimination in access to state courts for important civil claims.
