Question: A state statute authorizes conservation easements in gross held by qualified land trusts. Noah signed and recorded a conservation easement granting a land trust the right to prohibit subdivision, commercial construction, and wetland filling on Noah's farm. The land trust owns no neighboring land. Which statement best classifies the land trust's interest?
Answer A: The interest is invalid because any nonpossessory land-use right must benefit a neighboring dominant parcel.
Answer B: The interest is a profit because the land trust may preserve natural resources.
Answer C: The interest may be a valid conservation easement in gross because the statute authorizes that negative land-use restriction without a dominant parcel.
Answer D: The interest is only a revocable license because the land trust has no right to enter the farm daily.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The statute supplies the source for a conservation easement in gross. The land trust's right is a negative land-use restriction against subdivision, commercial construction, and filling. Because the statute authorizes this in-gross arrangement, the lack of a neighboring dominant parcel is not fatal.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the statute authorizes an in-gross conservation easement. B: B is wrong because preserving resources is not the same as removing resources under a profit. D: D is wrong because a negative easement or conservation easement can restrict land use without requiring daily physical entry.
