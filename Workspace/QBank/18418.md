MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Damages
Question: A driver negligently injured a runner's knee. A doctor recommended six weeks of low-risk physical therapy that would likely restore the runner's mobility and reduce later pain. The therapy was affordable, nearby, and commonly prescribed. The runner refused because he was annoyed at having to rearrange his schedule. As a result, his recovery took much longer and his lost wages increased. In the runner's negligence claim against the driver, how should the avoidable extra lost wages be treated?
Answer A: The runner may recover all later lost wages because the driver caused the original injury
Answer B: The runner is barred from all recovery because refusing therapy is comparative fault
Answer C: The runner's recovery may be reduced for the extra wages that reasonable therapy likely would have avoided
Answer D: The runner must undergo any treatment that would reduce damages, no matter the risk or burden
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The recommended therapy was reasonable, low-risk, affordable, and likely effective. The runner need not lose all recovery, but avoidable additional losses may be excluded.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A plaintiff must take reasonable steps to mitigate avoidable damages. Losses that reasonable therapy likely would have avoided may be reduced. B: Failure to mitigate usually limits avoidable damages; it does not erase recovery for the original injury or automatically operate as a total comparative-fault bar. D: Mitigation requires reasonable steps, not any conceivable measure. Plaintiffs need not accept unreasonable or dangerous treatment.
lb_rule_reference: A negligence plaintiff must take reasonable steps to mitigate avoidable damages, but need not undertake unreasonable, dangerous, or unusually burdensome measures.
