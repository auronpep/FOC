Question: A town ordinance prohibits "ritual animal slaughter by religious groups" but allows hunters, restaurants, and secular pest-control companies to kill animals under similar conditions. A church challenges the ordinance. What is the strongest Free Exercise argument?
Answer A: The ordinance triggers strict scrutiny because it targets religious conduct while allowing comparable secular conduct
Answer B: The ordinance receives rational basis because animal welfare is a legitimate government interest
Answer C: The ordinance is valid because all killing of animals can be regulated
Answer D: The ordinance is invalid only if the church proves the town dislikes religion generally
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The law is not neutral or generally applicable because it singles out religious conduct and underincludes secular conduct.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A legitimate interest does not avoid strict scrutiny when the law targets religion. C: The law selectively burdens religious conduct while exempting comparable secular conduct. D: Textual targeting and underinclusion can trigger strict scrutiny without proving broad anti-religion animus.
lb_rule_reference: A law that targets religion or religious practice is not neutral and triggers strict scrutiny under the Free Exercise Clause.
