Question: Tia takes her roommate's bicycle without permission to ride three blocks to a store. She plans to return it within twenty minutes and parks it in a locked bike rack while shopping. Police stop her before she returns home. Is Tia guilty of common-law larceny?
Answer A: Yes, because any unauthorized use of property is larceny
Answer B: Yes, because the bicycle moved from one place to another
Answer C: No, because she intended only a short, safe temporary use and return
Answer D: No, because bicycles are not personal property
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: These facts show unauthorized borrowing, not intent to permanently deprive or create a substantial risk of loss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Larceny requires intent to permanently deprive, not merely unauthorized temporary use. B: Asportation alone does not supply intent to permanently deprive. D: A bicycle is personal property capable of being stolen.
lb_rule_reference: A taking for temporary use generally is not larceny unless the intended use creates a substantial risk of permanent loss or otherwise shows intent to permanently deprive.
