MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff files a state-court complaint against a defendant asserting: (1) a federal civil rights claim under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 arising from the defendant's actions as a government contractor on a specific incident; and (2) a state-law breach-of-contract claim concerning an entirely unrelated commercial lease between the same parties. The defendant removes the entire action, citing federal-question jurisdiction over the § 1983 claim. The plaintiff argues that the removal was improper because the state contract claim cannot independently support federal jurisdiction and cannot be maintained in federal court. The governing forum is initially state court, now removed, under the MBE baseline. How should the federal court proceed?
Answer A: Remand the entire case to state court because the state contract claim is not removable, and a non-removable claim in the complaint bars removal of the entire action
Answer B: Retain the § 1983 claim under federal-question jurisdiction, and sever and remand the state contract claim, which has no federal jurisdictional basis
Answer C: Retain both claims because supplemental jurisdiction under § 1367(a) extends to any claim against the same defendant in the same action
Answer D: Remand the entire case because federal courts should decline jurisdiction over mixed cases to promote judicial economy and avoid fragmentation of litigation
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under § 1441(c), when a removable federal claim is joined with a claim that is not within original or supplemental jurisdiction, the district court retains the federal claim and must sever and remand the non-jurisdictional state claims. Here, the § 1983 claim supports federal-question jurisdiction; the unrelated state contract claim shares no common nucleus with the § 1983 claim (they involve entirely different transactions) and therefore falls outside § 1367(a) supplemental jurisdiction. The court should retain the § 1983 claim and sever and remand the state contract claim to state court.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1441(c) expressly addresses this situation. When a claim not within the district court's original or supplemental jurisdiction is joined with a removable federal claim, the court may remove the entire action but must sever and remand the non-removable state claim. The federal claim is properly in federal court; the unrelated state claim should be severed and remanded. C: Section 1367(a) requires the supplemental claim to share a common nucleus of operative fact with the anchor claim. The § 1983 civil rights claim arises from a specific incident involving the defendant's government contracting role; the state contract claim arises from a commercial lease — these are entirely unrelated. No common nucleus exists, and § 1367(a) does not support supplemental jurisdiction over the unrelated state claim. D: Section 1441(c) reflects Congress's judgment on this precise situation: the federal claim is severed and retained, and the state claim is remanded. The court is not free to remand a properly removed federal-question claim on efficiency grounds. The defendant has a right to a federal forum for the § 1983 claim, and the statute provides the severance/remand mechanism rather than whole-case remand.
lb_rule_reference: 28 U.S.C. § 1441(c) — when a removable federal-question claim is joined with a non-removable claim (e.g., an unrelated state claim over which supplemental jurisdiction does not lie), the entire action may be removed; the district court must sever and remand the state claims that have no independent basis for federal jurisdiction.
