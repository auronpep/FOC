Question: Elizabeth kept a personal diary for years before any dispute arose. After she was sued, she sent the diary to her lawyer with a cover note asking for legal advice. The opposing party requests the preexisting diary entries, not the cover note or any lawyer response. Are the diary entries protected merely because Elizabeth sent them to her lawyer?
Answer A: Yes, because all documents transmitted to a lawyer become attorney-client privileged.
Answer B: No, a preexisting document does not become privileged or work product merely because it is later sent to counsel, although the cover communication seeking advice may be protected.
Answer C: Yes, because the diary is now in the lawyer's litigation file.
Answer D: No, because clients can never create privileged writings.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Sending a preexisting document to a lawyer does not immunize the document from discovery if it was not privileged or work product before. The transmittal communication seeking legal advice may be privileged, but the preexisting diary entries are not protected merely by delivery to counsel. The decisive axis is preexisting object versus privileged communication.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is lawyer-transmission overclaim. Delivery to counsel does not transform existing evidence. B is wrong because Correct answer C is wrong because Lawyer-file location is not enough to create privilege or work product.
