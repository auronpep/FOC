Question: Samuel contracts to sell “the northern field” to Hannah. Samuel owns two fields north of his house. Samuel means the rocky northern field; Hannah means the fertile northern field. Both meanings are reasonable from the contract language, and neither party knows or has reason to know the other party's meaning. What is the likely result?
Answer A: Samuel's meaning controls because he is the seller.
Answer B: Hannah's meaning controls because she is the buyer.
Answer C: There is no contract for either field because the parties attached materially different reasonable meanings and neither knew of the other meaning.
Answer D: The court must choose the field with the higher market value.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When both parties attach materially different reasonable meanings to an ambiguous term and neither knows or has reason to know the other's meaning, there is no mutual assent as to that term. The contract fails for lack of assent on the subject matter.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B are wrong because neither buyer nor seller status automatically controls. D is wrong because market value does not solve the assent problem. B: A and B are wrong because neither buyer nor seller status automatically controls. D is wrong because market value does not solve the assent problem. D: D is wrong because market value does not solve the assent problem.
