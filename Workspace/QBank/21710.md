Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: NorthCo runs an online store from State A. Its website lets customers in all states select shipping to their home address. NorthCo knowingly accepts a State B order, ships a blender to State B, and sends a warranty email to the State B buyer. The blender injures the buyer in State B. The buyer sues NorthCo in State B. What is the best personal-jurisdiction conclusion?
Answer A: Jurisdiction is impossible because websites are visible everywhere and therefore never count as purposeful contacts
Answer B: Specific jurisdiction likely exists because NorthCo knowingly sold and shipped the product into State B and the claim arose from that sale
Answer C: Jurisdiction exists only if NorthCo is incorporated or headquartered in State B
Answer D: Jurisdiction exists because the buyer lives in State B, even if NorthCo did not ship there
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: NorthCo created a forum contact by accepting and shipping the State B order, and the injury arises from that contact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: This website was used for an actual forum sale and shipment tied to the claim. C: Specific jurisdiction can exist without at-home status when the claim is linked to purposeful forum contacts. D: The relevant fact is NorthCo's forum-directed sale and shipment, not the buyer's residence standing alone.
lb_rule_reference: Specific jurisdiction may exist when the defendant uses an interactive website to sell and ship the product into the forum and the claim arises from that forum sale.
