Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officer Reyes observed Morgan run a red light and pulled her over. When Reyes asked for Morgan's license, Morgan refused, cursed at the officer, and began walking rapidly away. Reyes grabbed Morgan's arm to stop her. Morgan wrenched free and began running. Reyes tackled Morgan, causing Morgan to suffer a broken wrist. The entire stop occurred on a public sidewalk. Morgan later sued under 42 U.S.C. § 1983, arguing the takedown was excessive force. Under *Graham v. Connor*, which standard governs whether Officer Reyes used constitutionally permissible force?
Answer A: Whether Reyes subjectively intended to harm Morgan
Answer B: Whether the force was the minimum necessary to complete the arrest
Answer C: Whether Reyes's force was objectively reasonable under the totality of the circumstances as judged from the perspective of a reasonable officer on the scene
Answer D: Whether Reyes had a reasonable belief that deadly force was necessary to prevent escape
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Graham v. Connor* (1989) established that all Fourth Amendment excessive force claims are analyzed under an objective reasonableness standard, not through the lens of hindsight or the officer's subjective intent. Courts weigh the severity of the crime, whether the suspect poses an immediate threat, and whether the suspect is actively resisting or evading arrest.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Graham v. Connor* explicitly rejected a subjective intent standard. The inquiry is objective: what would a reasonable officer on the scene have done, not what the particular officer was thinking. B: Graham* does not require police to use the least possible force. It requires objectively reasonable force under the totality of the circumstances, including the severity of the crime, whether the suspect poses a threat, and whether the suspect is actively resisting or fleeing. D: The *Garner* deadly force rule applies when an officer uses potentially lethal force to stop an escaping suspect; a tackle causing a broken wrist is non-deadly force evaluated under general *Graham* reasonableness, not the heightened *Garner* standard.
lb_rule_reference: A warrantless arrest in a public place is valid if supported by probable cause; force used during arrest must be objectively reasonable under *Graham v. Connor*, judged from the perspective of a reasonable officer on the scene without the benefit of hindsight
