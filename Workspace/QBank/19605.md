Outline: Spousal Privileges
Question: Jacob sues Paul in federal court for civil fraud. Jacob calls Leah, Paul's current wife, to testify that she saw Paul shred invoices. Leah is willing to testify. Paul objects on the ground that a spouse may not testify adversely against a spouse. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because spousal privilege applies in every proceeding involving a married person.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because Leah's testimony would harm marital harmony.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if the shredded invoices are independently admissible.
Answer D: Overrule the objection, because the federal adverse spousal testimonial privilege is a criminal-case privilege and the proposed testimony is only an observation.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal adverse spousal testimonial privilege is aimed at adverse testimony in criminal cases. This is a civil fraud action, Leah is willing, and she is describing an observed act rather than a confidential marital communication. The decisive axis is proceeding type.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is privilege absolutism. The federal testimonial privilege does not bar all spousal testimony in all proceedings. B: B is wrong because Marital-harmony policy does not by itself create an evidentiary privilege outside the recognized rule. C: C is wrong because The admissibility of the invoices is a separate proof issue. The objection is to Leah's testimony, not the invoices.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / criminal-case adverse testimony limit
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is applying the criminal adverse-testimony privilege to a civil observation question.
