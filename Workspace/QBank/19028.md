Question: Off duty, a city accountant writes a letter to the local newspaper alleging waste in the city budget. The letter is not part of her job duties. The city fires her, claiming only that criticism from employees is embarrassing. What framework applies?
Answer A: The employee has no First Amendment rights because she works for the city
Answer B: Strict scrutiny applies automatically to every public-employee discipline case
Answer C: The court balances her citizen speech on public waste against the city's legitimate workplace interests
Answer D: The city wins automatically because embarrassment is always workplace disruption
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: She spoke off duty, outside official duties, on public budget waste, so the Pickering-style balance is central.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Public employees may have protection when speaking as citizens on matters of public concern. B: Public-employee speech uses a specialized citizen/public-concern and balancing framework. D: Embarrassment alone is not the same as a sufficient efficiency or disruption interest.
lb_rule_reference: If a public employee speaks as a citizen on a matter of public concern, courts balance the employee's speech interest against the government's workplace-efficiency interests.
