Question: A gym downloaded a customer's social-media photo from a public page and placed it on billboards saying, "Train here and look like this." The customer never consented and is not famous. The ad was flattering and did not imply any false fact. Is the customer likely to prevail on an appropriation claim against the gym?
Answer A: No, because the ad was flattering and caused no reputational harm
Answer B: Yes, because the gym used the customer's likeness in advertising without consent
Answer C: No, because only celebrities have appropriation claims
Answer D: Yes, but only because the gym stated a false fact about the customer
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The gym used the customer's identity to promote its business, and the customer did not consent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Appropriation protects identity from unauthorized use for advantage; reputational harm is not required. C: Non-celebrities can have appropriation claims when their name or likeness is used for another's advantage. D: Appropriation does not require falsity; the unauthorized commercial use is enough.
lb_rule_reference: Unauthorized use of a person's name, likeness, or identity for commercial or other advantage can support appropriation liability.
