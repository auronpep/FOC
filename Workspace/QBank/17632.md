Question: Moses sold a small inn to Peter. In the sale contract, Peter promised to provide Naomi with one free room each month for one year. Peter told Naomi about the arrangement, and Naomi stayed in the room for the first three months. Peter then agreed with Moses to cancel the free-room clause. May Naomi enforce the remaining free-room promise?
Answer A: Yes, because accepting performance under the promised arrangement manifested assent and vested her rights before cancellation.
Answer B: No, because Naomi was not a party to the inn-sale contract.
Answer C: No, because original parties can always cancel a donee-beneficiary promise.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Naomi paid Moses part of the inn purchase price.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Naomi was the direct recipient of Peter's promised performance. By accepting the promised performance over several months, she manifested assent to the beneficiary arrangement before Peter and Moses attempted to cancel it. Her vested rights cannot be cut off without her consent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because an intended beneficiary need not be a party. C: C is wrong because original parties cannot cancel after the beneficiary's rights vest. D: D is wrong because Naomi need not pay consideration to enforce as an intended beneficiary.
