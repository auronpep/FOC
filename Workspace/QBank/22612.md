


BARMATRIX Q#: 22612
Outline_code: 43020202
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Judicial Review
Outline: Congressional limitations
Question: A baby was born in Country A to parents who were citizens of the United States. The baby's parents requested that the baby's birthplace be recorded as “Country B” on his passport. “Country B” was recognized by some other countries as another country within the borders of Country A. However, in accordance with the Foreign Relations Act of 2003, which did not recognize Country B as a foreign state, the United States State Department instead listed the baby's birthplace as “Country A.” The parents sued the Secretary of State in federal district court, arguing that the Act was unconstitutional. Which is the strongest argument that the Act is unconstitutional?
Answer A: A: The Act is unnecessary and improper to carry out Congress's diplomatic power over foreign states.
Answer B: B: Without his or her consent, Congress cannot change the citizenship of any citizen.
Answer C: C: The President has the exclusive power to recognize foreign states.
Answer D: D: The Act violates the Equal Protection Clause because the baby is being denied his right to equal citizenship status.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The President has the exclusive right to recognize foreign states. Zivotofsky v. Kerry, 135 S. Ct. 2076 (2015). Here, the Act unconstitutionally usurps the President's right to recognize foreign nations. A is incorrect because the Necessary and Proper Clause grants Congress the power to make any laws necessary to carry out one of its enumerated powers. As stated above, the President has the exclusive power to recognize foreign states. B is incorrect because the issue here is not changing or denying United States citizenship to any citizen. D is incorrect because this is not an instance of someone being denied equal protection of the law.
