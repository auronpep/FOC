Question: In a contract case, a party obtains a court order requiring production of factual portions of an investigator's witness notes because the witness is unavailable. The notes also contain defense counsel's margin comments ranking which witness statements are "dangerous," "weak," and "best for trial." How should the court treat the margin comments?
Answer A: Require production of the comments because any order compelling work product reaches the entire file
Answer B: Protect the comments because they reveal counsel's mental impressions and legal strategy
Answer C: Treat the comments as attorney-client privilege only if the witness was the client
Answer D: Order production because mental impressions are discoverable whenever they are relevant
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Opinion work product receives special protection even when factual material is produced.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Compelling ordinary work product does not eliminate protection for counsel's mental impressions. C: The better protection is opinion work product, not attorney-client privilege. D: Relevance does not override the special protection for counsel's mental impressions.
lb_rule_reference: Even when ordinary work product is discoverable, the court must protect against disclosure of counsel's mental impressions, conclusions, opinions, and legal theories.
