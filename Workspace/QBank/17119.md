Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Moses owned a vineyard. He signed a deed stating, “I convey my vineyard to Aaron,” and delivered it intending an immediate transfer. Aaron had died one week before the deed was delivered. Aaron's will left all of his property to Joshua. Joshua claims the vineyard under Aaron's will. Who owns the vineyard?
Answer A: Moses, because no title vested in Aaron when the deed was delivered to a dead grantee.
Answer B: Joshua, because Aaron's will left all of Aaron's property to Joshua.
Answer C: Aaron's estate, because the deed named Aaron as grantee.
Answer D: Joshua, because consideration is not required for a valid deed.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is a living grantee capable of taking title at delivery. Aaron was dead when the deed was delivered, so title could not vest in him. Because Aaron took nothing, his will transferred nothing in the vineyard, and title remains with Moses.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a will can pass only property that vested in the testator. C: C is wrong because naming a dead person does not vest title in that person's estate. D: D is wrong because the no-consideration rule does not cure the absence of a living grantee.
lb_rule_reference: Living grantee / title cannot vest in dead person
