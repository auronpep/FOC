Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A magazine ran a story about illegal gambling clubs and used a large photo of a teacher leaving a community center. The caption read, "Neighbors leaving the gambling den." The teacher had been attending a tutoring program in the same building and had no connection to gambling. Thousands of readers saw the article. Is the teacher likely to prevail on a false light claim against the magazine?
Answer A: No, because the article did not expressly say the teacher committed a crime
Answer B: Yes, because the widespread caption-photo combination falsely implied the teacher was leaving a gambling den
Answer C: No, because false light requires proof of special economic loss
Answer D: Yes, but only because the magazine used the teacher's likeness for advertising
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The magazine gave broad publicity to a misleading implication highly offensive to a reasonable person.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: False light can rest on a misleading juxtaposition or implication that places the plaintiff in a false offensive light. C: False light does not require trade-libel-style special damages. D: The claim turns on a false implication in editorial content, not use of identity for the magazine's advertising advantage.
lb_rule_reference: False light requires widespread publicity that places the plaintiff in a false or misleading light highly offensive to a reasonable person.
