Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A courier is assigned to deliver packages along a city route. While driving between two delivery stops, she turns one block off the route to buy coffee at a drive-through. As she exits the drive-through and returns to the delivery route, she negligently hits a pedestrian. Is the courier's employer likely vicariously liable?
Answer A: Yes, because a slight personal detour can remain within the scope of employment
Answer B: Yes, because employers are liable for all torts committed by employees at any time
Answer C: No, because any personal purpose automatically removes the employee from the scope of employment
Answer D: No, because respondeat superior applies only to intentional torts
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A minor deviation for personal comfort during an assigned route is often a detour, not a frolic.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The tort must be within the scope of employment. C: Slight detours do not automatically defeat scope. D: It commonly applies to negligence committed within scope.
lb_rule_reference: An employer may be vicariously liable for an employee's tort committed during a minor detour that remains within the scope of employment.
