Outline: Larceny
Question: Dana went to a bar and drank twelve beers over the course of an evening. Heavily intoxicated, she walked out wearing another patron's leather jacket, believing it was her own. Security footage shows Dana picked up the jacket from a coat rack two tables away from where she had been sitting. The next morning, sober, Dana looked at the jacket and immediately recognized it was not hers; she returned it within an hour. The prosecutor charges Dana with larceny. Which of the following is Dana's strongest defense to the larceny charge?
Answer A: Dana lacked the capacity to form any criminal intent because she was severely intoxicated
Answer B: Dana's conduct was not a voluntary act because she was unaware of what she was doing
Answer C: Dana lacked the specific intent to permanently deprive the owner of the jacket because she mistakenly believed it was her own
Answer D: Dana has a complete defense because she returned the jacket the next morning
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Larceny requires a trespassory taking with intent to permanently deprive. Dana's honest mistake of fact — believing the jacket was hers — negates this specific intent. The mistake need not be reasonable for specific-intent crimes; it need only be genuine. Voluntary intoxication is relevant here because it supports the credibility of the honest mistake, but the core defense is lack of the required specific intent. Prompt return also corroborates that she had no subjective intent to steal.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Voluntary intoxication does not eliminate all criminal intent; it only negates specific intent. The defense is not "no capacity" but "no specific intent formed." Courts do not recognize a blanket incapacity defense for voluntary intoxication. B: Dana physically and consciously picked up the jacket; the act was voluntary. Voluntary intoxication goes to mens rea (the intent to steal), not to whether the physical act was voluntary. D: Subsequent return is not a defense to larceny. Larceny is complete at the moment of taking with the required intent. Post-taking return may mitigate punishment but does not negate the completed offense. Here, however, the defense works for a different reason: no specific intent was formed at the time of taking.
lb_rule_reference: Larceny is a specific-intent crime requiring intent to permanently deprive; voluntary intoxication is a defense only to specific-intent crimes
