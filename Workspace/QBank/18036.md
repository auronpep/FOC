MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A pest-control company fumigated a small apartment building with a toxic gas rarely used in residential neighborhoods. The company followed the label instructions and ventilated the building for the recommended time. Residual gas remained in one unit, and the returning tenant suffered lung irritation. Is the company subject to strict liability?
Answer A: Yes, because the tenant's injury resulted from the toxic-gas risk that made the fumigation abnormally dangerous
Answer B: No, because the company followed the label instructions
Answer C: No, because fumigation always counts as ordinary pest control
Answer D: Yes, but only if the tenant proves the company intended the gas to remain
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The harm came from residual toxic gas, the abnormal risk of the fumigation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Due care does not defeat strict liability for harm within the abnormal risk. C: The facts emphasize a rare toxic gas used in a residential setting; context matters. D: Strict liability does not require intent to cause the residual exposure.
lb_rule_reference: Fumigation with toxic gas can be abnormally dangerous when it creates a serious risk that cannot be eliminated by due care; strict liability covers inhalation harm from residual gas.
