Question: An electronics repair shop regularly sells replacement laptop batteries from its parts counter. It sold a customer a replacement battery that had an internal manufacturing defect when sold. The battery overheated during ordinary use and burned the customer's hand. May the customer pursue strict products liability against the shop?
Answer A: No, because the shop primarily performs repair services
Answer B: Yes, if the battery was defective when sold and caused physical injury
Answer C: No, unless the shop designed the battery
Answer D: Yes, but only if the customer proves negligent inspection by the shop
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A commercial component supplier can be in the products distribution chain. The defective battery caused physical harm, not just disappointed product value.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The shop regularly sells replacement batteries as products. The claim targets a defective product it commercially supplied. C: Strict products liability is not limited to designers or manufacturers; commercial sellers can be defendants. D: Strict products liability does not require proof that the seller negligently inspected.
lb_rule_reference: A commercial supplier of a defective component can face strict products liability when the component defect causes tort-type harm.
