


BARMATRIX Q#: 22132
Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: A young Asian couple wanted to rent a house in a city neighborhood. The neighborhood was a historically African- American neighborhood and was recognized as such by the National Historic Registry. Because of recent national financial crises and the closing of several local companies, many of the homes were in foreclosure, and many of the African- American families had moved to other states. After learning about the Asian couple's interest, a local historian went to the house's owner and asked her not to rent to the Asian couple and instead try to find an African- American couple to rent to. The historian was worried that otherwise the neighborhood would lose its historical character. The house's owner went to her lawyer and asked for advice. May the house's owner refuse to rent the house to the Asian couple?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the neighborhood has been recognized as an African- American neighborhood by the National Historic Registry.
Answer B: B: Yes, because she is renting her own private property.
Answer C: C: No, because it would be a violation of the Fair Housing Act.
Answer D: D: No, because it would be a violation of the Equal Protection Clause.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The Fair Housing Act bans discrimination based on race, ethnicity, religion, national origin, gender, and disability in the sale or rental of housing. 42 U.S.C. §3601. Consequently, the house owner cannot refuse to rent to the Asian couple based on their race. A and B are incorrect because refusing to rent to the Asian couple would be a violation of the Fair Housing Act. D is incorrect because the Equal Protection Clause applies to state action, and this is a private home rental.
