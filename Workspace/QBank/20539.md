Outline: Freedom of religion
Question: A state unemployment system denies benefits to workers who leave jobs without "good cause." The agency routinely finds good cause for secular family obligations and transportation conflicts, but denies good cause to an employee who left after her employer required work during a religious observance. The agency says religious reasons are categorically personal choices. What is the employee's strongest Free Exercise argument?
Answer A: The denial is immune from Free Exercise review because unemployment benefits are not criminal penalties
Answer B: Heightened scrutiny may apply because the state grants individualized secular exemptions while categorically rejecting comparable religious reasons
Answer C: The denial is valid because government may always classify religious reasons as personal choices
Answer D: The denial is invalid only if the statute names the employee's faith
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The good-cause system requires individualized judgment. Treating religious reasons worse than secular reasons undermines neutrality and general applicability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Denial of benefits can burden religious exercise when the system makes individualized exceptions but devalues religious reasons. C: Free Exercise doctrine forbids singling out religious reasons for disfavored treatment in an individualized exemption system. D: Discretionary administration and unequal treatment of exemptions can trigger heightened scrutiny even without naming a faith.
lb_rule_reference: A benefits system with individualized good-cause exemptions may not deny religious reasons less favorably than comparable secular reasons without triggering heightened scrutiny.
