Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: Miriam is charged with murder. She claims the victim was standing directly over her when she fired in self-defense. The prosecutor offers one autopsy photograph showing the angle of the wound and powder marks around it. Miriam objects that the photograph is disturbing. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Exclude the photograph because all disturbing photographs are unfairly prejudicial.
Answer B: Admit the photograph if its probative value on distance and angle is not substantially outweighed by the risk of unfair prejudice.
Answer C: Exclude the photograph because the medical examiner can describe the wound verbally.
Answer D: Admit the photograph only if Miriam agrees that the photograph is not emotional.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The photograph is probative of distance, angle, and the self-defense account. Although disturbing, it may be admitted if its probative value is not substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice. The availability of verbal testimony may affect balancing, but it does not automatically require exclusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because disturbing evidence is not automatically excluded. C: C is wrong because alternative proof may be considered, but it does not make the photograph inadmissible as a matter of law. D: D is wrong because the opponent's agreement that evidence is not emotional is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats emotional impact as automatic exclusion without weighing probative need.
