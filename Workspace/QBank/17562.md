Question: Samuel agrees in a signed goods contract to supply “all tablets required by Ezra's school for the year.” Ezra sues after Samuel sells tablets to another school and claims the writing made Samuel the exclusive supplier for Ezra's school. Samuel argues that the phrase merely set a possible quantity if Ezra chose to buy. Which fact most strengthens Ezra's interpretation?
Answer A: For the prior three years, Samuel supplied all tablets the school required under contracts using the same phrase, and neither party bought or sold tablets outside that arrangement.
Answer B: Ezra's school lost money when it had to buy some tablets elsewhere.
Answer C: Samuel believed he could make more profit by selling to another school.
Answer D: Ezra's students preferred Samuel's tablets over other tablets.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The call asks which fact best supports the meaning of the contract term. Prior transactions using the same phrase in an exclusive requirements pattern create a course of dealing that directly supports Ezra's interpretation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because loss relates to damages, not what the contract meant. C: C is wrong because Samuel's profit motive does not define the obligation. D: D is wrong because student preference may explain demand but not the meaning of “all tablets required.”
