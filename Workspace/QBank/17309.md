Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: A postal inspector intercepted a package containing child pornography addressed to a suspect's home. Working with the inspector, police applied for an anticipatory warrant to search the home. The warrant stated it could be executed only after the package was delivered to and accepted by an occupant at the residence. Police delivered the package; the suspect's wife accepted it and brought it inside. Officers executed the warrant and found the package and additional contraband. The suspect challenges the warrant, arguing no probable cause existed at the time of issuance because contraband was not yet at the residence. Was the anticipatory warrant constitutional?
Answer A: No, because probable cause must exist at the moment the warrant is issued, and the contraband had not yet arrived at the home
Answer B: No, because the warrant's triggering condition had to appear on the face of the warrant for it to be constitutionally valid
Answer C: Yes, because there was probable cause to believe the triggering condition (delivery and acceptance) would occur and that contraband would then be present, satisfying the Grubbs test
Answer D: Yes, because the wife's acceptance of the package constituted consent to search the entire residence
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: United States v. Grubbs upheld anticipatory warrants conditioned on a future event. The test requires probable cause to believe (1) the triggering condition will come to pass and (2) if it does, the contraband will be at the described location. The package was intercepted and confirmed to contain contraband; delivery to a specific address was essentially certain. The triggering condition (delivery and acceptance) occurred before execution. The warrant was constitutional and suppression is denied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: United States v. Grubbs upheld anticipatory warrants as constitutional. The probable cause analysis for an anticipatory warrant is forward-looking: there must be probable cause to believe (1) the triggering event will occur and (2) if it does, contraband will be found at the described location. Here, a package containing contraband was en route — making both conditions virtually certain. The anticipatory nature of the warrant does not defeat probable cause. B: Grubbs held that the Fourth Amendment does not require the triggering condition to appear on the face of the warrant. The warrant need only describe the place to be searched and the items to be seized with particularity. The triggering condition may be described in the supporting affidavit. D: The wife's acceptance of delivery is the triggering condition, not a consent to search. Consent to search requires a voluntary, knowing agreement to allow a search, which is different from accepting a postal delivery. The correct basis for the search's validity is the anticipatory warrant, not consent.
lb_rule_reference: An anticipatory warrant is valid if there is probable cause to believe (1) the triggering condition will occur and (2) when it does, contraband will be at the described location; execution before the condition occurs is unconstitutional (United States v. Grubbs)
