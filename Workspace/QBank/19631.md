Question: In a federal partnership dispute, Jonathan calls Abigail and asks, “Please tell the jury everything that happened during your ten years working with the business.” The opposing party objects to the form of the question. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the question calls for an uncontrolled narrative rather than focused testimony.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because witnesses may answer only yes-or-no questions.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the witness has personal knowledge of her employment.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because narrative answers are required in civil cases.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The judge may control the mode of examination to keep testimony focused and efficient. An invitation to describe everything over ten years is a classic narrative/overbroad question.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because witnesses need not be limited to yes-or-no answers. C: C is wrong because personal knowledge does not cure an overbroad narrative form. D: D is wrong because no rule requires narrative answers in civil cases.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / narrative question
