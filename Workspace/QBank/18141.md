Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Rachel sues Samuel after a collision on a mountain road during a snowstorm. Samuel offers a weather report showing clear skies at a beach town 90 miles away at the same time. Samuel offers no evidence connecting that beach weather to the mountain road conditions. Rachel objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the report because weather anywhere in the state is relevant to driving conditions.
Answer B: Admit the report because it shows that snow was not universal that day.
Answer C: Exclude the report because, without a connection to the accident location, it does not make the mountain-road conditions more or less probable.
Answer D: Exclude the report only if Rachel proves the beach town has a different climate.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The consequential fact is the condition of the mountain road at the time of the collision. A weather report from a distant beach town has no shown probative link to that fact. This is not merely weak proof; it lacks the connection needed to matter.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevance requires a connection to the relevant place and issue. B: B is wrong because the fact that snow was not universal does not by itself bear on the mountain road. D: D is wrong because Samuel, as proponent, must supply the probative connection; Rachel need not disprove it.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Mistaking any fact about the broader world for a fact of consequence in the case.
