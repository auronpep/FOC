Question: John agreed to buy Lydia's house. The contract stated, “Buyer's duty to purchase is subject to Buyer obtaining a mortgage commitment by May 1.” John submitted one incomplete application and ignored the lender's requests for pay stubs and tax records because he wanted out of the deal. The lender denied the application. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: John is excused because the financing condition did not occur.
Answer B: John cannot rely on the failed financing condition because he did not make the required good-faith efforts to obtain financing.
Answer C: Lydia must prove that the lender would certainly have approved the loan.
Answer D: John is excused because financing conditions are always outside the buyer's control.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A buyer whose duty is conditioned on financing must generally make reasonable good-faith efforts to obtain financing. John's deliberate failure to complete the application process prevents him from using the failed condition as an excuse.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because nonoccurrence does not protect the party who failed to pursue the condition. C: C is wrong because certainty of approval is not required when John's own lack of effort caused the denial. D: D is wrong because financing conditions often require the buyer's cooperation and good-faith effort.
lb_rule_reference: Financing condition / good-faith efforts
