Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Nonresponsive answers
Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Mary asks Peter, “Did you examine the ladder?” Peter answers, “Yes, and the manufacturer had paid two other claims about similar ladders.” No one objects. After three more witnesses testify, the manufacturer moves to strike Peter's statement about the two other claims. How should the court rule?
Answer A: The court may deny the motion as untimely because the manufacturer did not move to strike when the volunteered answer was given.
Answer B: The court must grant the motion because a motion to strike is timely whenever it is made before closing argument.
Answer C: The court must deny the motion because nonresponsive answers can never be stricken.
Answer D: The court must grant the motion only if Mary also objects.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A motion to strike must be timely. When a witness volunteers objectionable matter in an answer, the opponent ordinarily must move promptly so the court can correct the record and, if needed, instruct the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because waiting until before closing is not automatically timely. C: C is wrong because nonresponsive or inadmissible answers can be stricken. D: D is wrong because the party harmed by the evidence may move to strike without the questioner joining.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(1) / timeliness of motion to strike
