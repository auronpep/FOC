Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Identifications
Question: A victim spends ten minutes speaking with an unmasked robber in a well-lit shop and gives police a detailed description within minutes. The next day, police show the victim a photo array in which the defendant's photo is brighter than the others. The victim immediately identifies the defendant and explains details matching the earlier description. Should the identification be suppressed solely because the array was suggestive?
Answer A: Yes, because any suggestive photo array must be excluded
Answer B: No, because strong reliability factors may make the identification admissible despite suggestiveness
Answer C: Yes, because a photo array can never be used after a face-to-face robbery
Answer D: No, but only because the victim was confident
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The victim had a long, well-lit view, gave an accurate description quickly, and made an immediate identification consistent with that description. Those factors support reliability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Suppression requires both improper suggestiveness and a substantial likelihood of misidentification. Strong reliability can defeat suppression. C: Photo arrays are not categorically barred; the due process question is suggestiveness and reliability. D: Confidence is one factor, not the only factor. The full reliability picture matters.
lb_rule_reference: Even a suggestive police-arranged photo procedure may be admitted if reliability factors show no substantial likelihood of irreparable misidentification.
