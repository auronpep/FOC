


BARMATRIX Q#: 14097
Outline_code: 92080203
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motion for relief from judgment or order
Question: A designer filed suit against a toy company in federal court. The court had both subject-matter jurisdiction and personal jurisdiction over the parties. The federal district court entered a judgment dismissing the designer's action for failure to prosecute. The designer's attorney did not monitor the court's docket and, as a result, failed to learn of the dismissal promptly. Two years after the entry of the dismissal and judgment, the designer's attorney filed a motion seeking relief from the judgment. Is the court likely to grant the designer's motion for relief from judgment?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the court should have notified the designer's attorney of the dismissal and judgment through means other than recording them on the court's docket.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the designer should not suffer the prejudice of the dismissal due to minor negligence by his attorney.
Answer C: C: No, because the designer's attorney waited too long from the entry of the judgment to make the motion seeking relief from the judgment.
Answer D: D: No, because the error of a party's attorney is never a ground for relieving the party from a final judgment.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. Pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 60(b), when the reason for seeking relief from a judgment is "mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect," a Rule 60(b) motion must be made no more than one year after the entry of the judgment. Here, the designer's attorney negligently waited two years after the court's entry of the dismissal for failure to prosecute and thus is not eligible under FRCP 60(b) to file a motion for relief from the judgment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. It is the attorney's responsibility to find out about an entry of judgment against his client and, if necessary, to make a timely Rule 60(b) motion seeking relief from the judgment. B is incorrect. After more than a year from the entry of judgment, the judgment is final. Therefore, even though it is unfortunate that the designer has to suffer the high price of a dismissal with prejudice as the result of minor negligence by his attorney, the interest in the finality of the judgment takes precedence. D is incorrect. Although FRCP 60(b)(1) states that the error of one's attorney can be a ground for relieving a party from a final judgment, this type of motion must be made no more than one year after the entry of the judgment. As the fact pattern indicates, it has been two years since the judgment was entered against the designer.
