Outline: Inadvertent disclosure
Question: In a patent-related contract dispute, the plaintiff seeks technical manuals that are relevant to the parties' performance obligations. The defendant shows the manuals contain trade-secret manufacturing details. The plaintiff agrees that outside counsel only access and filing restrictions would allow the case to proceed without public disclosure. What is the court's best discovery approach?
Answer A: Deny all discovery because trade-secret material is never discoverable
Answer B: Compel unrestricted public production because relevance always defeats confidentiality
Answer C: Use an appropriate protective order, such as limited access and filing restrictions, if good cause is shown
Answer D: Convert the request into a motion for summary judgment
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Protective orders can balance relevant discovery against confidentiality and trade-secret concerns.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Confidential or trade-secret status may justify protection, not automatic total immunity. B: The court can protect confidential information while allowing necessary discovery. D: The issue is how to manage discovery, not whether the merits can be resolved under Rule 56.
lb_rule_reference: For good cause, a court may issue a protective order to protect a party from annoyance, embarrassment, oppression, undue burden, or disclosure of trade secrets or confidential information.
