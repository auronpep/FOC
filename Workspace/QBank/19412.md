MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Assumption of risk
Question: Before joining a private amateur cycling race, a rider signed a clear release stating that she assumed ordinary negligence risks from course layout, loose gravel, and volunteer signaling. During the race, she saw loose gravel on a marked turn, continued at high speed, and fell. There is no evidence of reckless or intentional conduct by the organizer. She sues the organizer for negligence based on the gravel and course marking. Is the organizer likely to have a valid express assumption-of-risk defense to the rider's negligence claim?
Answer A: Yes, because the clear release covered the ordinary negligence risk that caused the fall
Answer B: Yes, because assumption of risk always requires no written agreement
Answer C: No, because comparative fault has abolished all assumption-of-risk defenses
Answer D: No, because a release can never apply to recreational activities
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The rider signed a clear release covering ordinary negligence risks from course layout, gravel, and signaling, and the injury arose from those covered risks. Express assumption of risk can bar the ordinary negligence claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: This is an express assumption-of-risk case because the rider signed a clear release. The rule does not depend on saying writings are unnecessary. C: A clear express release may still bar ordinary negligence claims within its scope. Comparative fault does not necessarily abolish express assumption of risk. D: Clear recreational releases may be enforceable for ordinary negligence depending on scope and policy. The facts keep the claim within the covered ordinary risks.
lb_rule_reference: A clear express assumption-of-risk agreement may bar an ordinary negligence claim within its scope, but it does not automatically bar claims outside the risks or conduct covered.
