Question: Eric was indicted for burglary. Police arranged a post-indictment in-person lineup without notifying Eric's attorney. The only eyewitness was a neighbor who had glimpsed a figure through a frosted window from forty feet away for about two seconds before the lights went out. The neighbor had no prior acquaintance with Eric. At the lineup, the neighbor identified Eric. At trial, when asked to identify the burglar, the neighbor again pointed to Eric. Eric's attorney objected and moved to suppress both identifications. What is the correct ruling on Eric's motion?
Answer A: Suppress only the lineup identification; permit the in-court identification because it is independent of the lineup
Answer B: Deny the motion as to both identifications because a lineup with an honest mistake by police does not trigger the exclusionary rule
Answer C: Suppress both identifications because the Sixth Amendment lineup violation tainted the out-of-court identification and the in-court identification has no independent source
Answer D: Suppress both identifications and dismiss the charges because the prosecution cannot proceed without eyewitness identification
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The post-indictment in-person lineup without counsel violated Eric's Sixth Amendment right under *United States v. Wade*, requiring suppression of the lineup identification. The in-court identification is also suppressible unless the prosecution proves by clear and convincing evidence an independent source for the witness's ability to identify Eric. Here, the neighbor's original observation — two seconds through frosted glass at forty feet in poor lighting, with no prior acquaintance — does not provide a reliable independent basis. The prosecution cannot overcome the taint. Both identifications must be suppressed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The independent source exception requires the prosecution to prove that the witness has a basis to identify the defendant entirely apart from the tainted lineup. Here, the neighbor's only observation was a two-second glimpse through frosted glass from forty feet in the dark — a weak original observation, and no prior acquaintance. The lineup is likely the only reason the neighbor can identify Eric. Without an independent source, the in-court identification must also be suppressed. B: The good faith exception (*United States v. Leon*) applies to Fourth Amendment violations involving reliance on facially valid warrants, not to Sixth Amendment right-to-counsel violations. The Wade right to counsel at a post-indictment lineup does not have a good-faith exception. Conducting the lineup without notifying counsel violates the Sixth Amendment regardless of the officers' intent. D: Suppression of identification testimony is not a basis to dismiss charges. The prosecution retains the ability to proceed on other evidence — physical evidence, forensic evidence, statements, circumstantial proof. Suppression is the appropriate remedy for the constitutional violation; dismissal is not. The case may become harder to prove, but that is the consequence of the violation, not grounds for dismissal.
lb_rule_reference: When a post-indictment lineup is conducted without counsel and the witness had no prior independent knowledge of the defendant, neither the out-of-court identification from the lineup nor the subsequent in-court identification may be admitted because no independent source exists to purge the taint.
