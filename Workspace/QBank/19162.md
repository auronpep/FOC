Question: Ruth hired David to paint the exterior of her house for $12,000, payable “when the exterior painting is completed.” David finished the entire exterior, but a small section behind a storage shed was uneven and would cost $300 to correct. Ruth refused to pay anything, arguing that David did not literally complete the job. Which result is most likely?
Answer A: Ruth owes nothing because any incomplete performance prevents the payment duty from arising.
Answer B: Ruth must pay the contract price less the cost to correct the minor defect.
Answer C: Ruth must pay only the reasonable value of David's labor because substantial performance is unavailable in service contracts.
Answer D: Ruth may cancel the contract and recover all prior payments because the uneven section is a total breach.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under common law, substantial performance of a constructive condition generally makes the other party's payment duty mature, subject to damages for defects. David substantially performed the exterior painting, and the remaining defect is minor and easily valued. Ruth must pay the contract price reduced by the correction cost.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it applies strict express-condition treatment to a constructive condition. C: C is wrong because substantial performance is a contract route in service/work contracts, not limited to quantum meruit. D: D is wrong because a minor defect is not a total breach that allows cancellation of the entire bargain.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law constructive conditions / substantial performance
