Question: After notice and hearing, a federal district court denies a motion for preliminary injunction. The plaintiff seeks immediate appeal. The defendant argues no appeal may occur until final judgment. Is immediate appeal available?
Answer A: Yes, because orders denying preliminary injunctions are immediately appealable under Section 1292(a)(1)
Answer B: No, because interlocutory appeals are never allowed
Answer C: No, because only orders granting injunctions are appealable
Answer D: Yes, but only if the order also resolves all damages claims
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Injunction orders have a statutory interlocutory appeal route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Statutory exceptions exist. C: Orders refusing injunctions are included. D: Section 1292(a)(1) does not require final judgment on all claims.
lb_rule_reference: Section 1292(a)(1) allows interlocutory appeal from orders granting, refusing, modifying, or dissolving injunctions.
