Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A federal case proceeds through answer, discovery, and pretrial conference. One week before trial, the judge notices that the complaint alleges only state-law claims between nondiverse parties and no supplemental-jurisdiction anchor. Neither party has objected to subject matter jurisdiction. What should the court do?
Answer A: Proceed to trial because subject matter jurisdiction was waived by delay
Answer B: Proceed to trial because only a party, not the judge, can raise jurisdiction
Answer C: Dismiss the action because lack of subject matter jurisdiction can be raised at any time
Answer D: Transfer the action automatically to any state court with venue
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 12(h)(3) requires dismissal whenever the court determines it lacks subject matter jurisdiction. The late procedural stage does not cure the defect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Subject matter jurisdiction is not waivable. Delay by the parties does not create federal adjudicatory power. B: The court has an independent duty to examine subject matter jurisdiction and must act when it is missing. D: Rule 12(h)(3) calls for dismissal when the federal court lacks subject matter jurisdiction. Automatic state-court transfer is not the federal rule in this posture.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 12(h)(3) requires dismissal whenever the court determines that it lacks subject matter jurisdiction.
