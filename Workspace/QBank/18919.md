Outline: Conviction of crime
Question: In a federal civil trial, Solomon testifies for the plaintiff. The defendant seeks to impeach Solomon with a 4-year-old felony theft conviction. After the conviction, Solomon received a certificate of rehabilitation based on a finding that he had been rehabilitated. Solomon has not been convicted of any later crime. Solomon objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the conviction because it is less than 10 years old.
Answer B: Admit the conviction because all felony convictions are admissible against nondefendant witnesses.
Answer C: Exclude the conviction because a conviction covered by a certificate of rehabilitation is not admissible if the witness has not later been convicted of a serious crime.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction only if the pardon was based on innocence rather than rehabilitation.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 609(c) excludes a conviction if it has been the subject of a pardon, annulment, certificate of rehabilitation, or equivalent procedure based on a finding of rehabilitation and the person has not later been convicted of a crime punishable by death or imprisonment for more than one year. The facts satisfy that exclusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because recency does not override Rule 609(c). B: B is wrong because felony status does not defeat the rehabilitation exclusion. D: D is wrong because a finding of innocence is one exclusion route, but rehabilitation with no later serious conviction is another.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(c) / pardon, annulment, or rehabilitation
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the pardon/rehabilitation exclusion rather than the felony or age shortcut.
