Outline: Reliance damages
Question: David agreed to lend Solomon $100,000 so Solomon could convert an old storage building into a bakery. In reasonable reliance on the signed loan agreement, Solomon spent $30,000 demolishing part of the old building and $5,000 obtaining permits. David then repudiated the loan. Solomon cannot prove the bakery would have earned profits with reasonable certainty. The demolition increased the land's market value by $10,000. What is Solomon's best damages measure?
Answer A: $135,000, consisting of the promised loan plus all reliance expenditures.
Answer B: $100,000, the amount of the promised loan.
Answer C: $25,000, the reliance expenditures caused by David's breach minus the retained land-value benefit.
Answer D: Nominal damages only, because Solomon cannot prove the bakery's expected profits.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When expectation damages are too speculative, reliance damages may protect expenditures made in preparation for or performance of the contract, adjusted to prevent overcompensation. Solomon spent $35,000 in reliance, but retained a $10,000 land-value increase, so the best measure is $25,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reliance damages do not add the promised loan to reliance expenditures; that would overcompensate Solomon. B: B is wrong because the loan amount is not the loss measure when the issue is failed financing and uncertain profits. D: D is wrong because speculative expectation does not preclude recovery of provable reliance expenditures.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance damages / speculative expectation fallback
