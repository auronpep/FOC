Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Two employees, Mira and Dane, separately remove security tags from expensive watches during the same overnight shift. They communicate by nods, place the watches in the same trash bag near the exit, and leave together after Dane texts, "Same plan as last week." Neither says, "I agree to steal watches." They are charged with conspiracy to commit larceny. What is the best defense argument, and how should it be evaluated?
Answer A: The conspiracy charge fails because there was no express spoken agreement
Answer B: The facts can support conspiracy because coordinated conduct and the text permit inference of a shared larceny plan
Answer C: The conspiracy charge fails because larceny was not completed before they left
Answer D: The conspiracy charge succeeds only if both employees signed a written plan
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The nods, shared stash location, joint exit, and reference to "same plan" support a circumstantial inference that Mira and Dane agreed to steal the watches.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Agreement can be inferred from coordinated conduct and communications showing a shared criminal objective. C: Conspiracy is complete upon agreement, and completion of the target offense is not required. D: Written proof is not required. Criminal agreements are often proved by circumstantial evidence.
lb_rule_reference: A conspiracy agreement may be proved by circumstantial evidence showing a shared criminal objective; an express formal contract or direct statement of agreement is not required.
