Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A fitness instructor licensed her photo to a supplement company for one printed brochure during January. In March, the company used the same photo in online ads for a new product line. The instructor did not consent to online ads or later use. Can the company rely on the January license?
Answer A: No, because the later online advertising exceeded the license's time and medium scope
Answer B: Yes, because any consent to use a photo is permanent and unlimited
Answer C: Yes, because the photo was flattering
Answer D: No, but only because the company stated a false fact about the instructor
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Consent to one limited use is not consent to all later commercial identity uses.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Consent is bounded by the agreed time, medium, and purpose. C: Appropriation does not require reputational harm or unflattering use. D: The facts do not require falsity; the issue is unauthorized commercial use.
lb_rule_reference: Consent or a license to use a likeness is limited by its scope; use outside the agreed time, medium, or purpose can support appropriation.
