Question: After discovery closes and a settlement is announced, a nonparty who knew about the litigation for a year moves to intervene, claiming an interest in the transaction. Existing parties show reopening the case would cause substantial delay and prejudice. What is the best reason to deny intervention?
Answer A: Intervention can be denied as untimely even if the movant claims a relevant interest
Answer B: A claimed interest automatically makes intervention timely
Answer C: Intervention must be allowed because settlement has not yet been approved
Answer D: Timeliness matters only for permissive intervention, not intervention of right
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Timeliness is a threshold requirement for both intervention routes, and prejudice and delay are relevant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A valid interest does not erase the timeliness requirement. C: Late-stage intervention can still be untimely when it prejudices existing parties. D: Rule 24 requires a timely motion for both intervention of right and permissive intervention.
lb_rule_reference: Timeliness is required for both intervention of right and permissive intervention and is evaluated in light of all circumstances, including stage of proceedings, prejudice, and reason for delay.
