MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Battery
Question: A patient signed a consent form for a minor cosmetic injection. Before the injection began, she clearly told the doctor, "I changed my mind. Do not inject me." The doctor said she was being silly and intentionally gave the injection anyway. The patient sues for battery. Can the doctor rely on the signed consent form?
Answer A: No, because the patient revoked consent before the contact occurred
Answer B: Yes, because written consent can never be withdrawn
Answer C: Yes, because the injection was minor
Answer D: No, because consent is never a defense to battery
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Consent is limited by revocation. The later injection occurred after clear withdrawal of permission.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Consent can be revoked before the contact, even if previously written. C: Unauthorized offensive or harmful contact can be battery even if minor. D: Consent can be a defense when valid and unrevoked; it fails here because it was withdrawn.
lb_rule_reference: Consent can be revoked before the contact occurs; later intentional contact outside the renewed consent can support battery.
