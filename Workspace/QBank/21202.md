Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Arcway Manufacturing Corp. is incorporated in Delaware. Its executive suite — CEO, CFO, General Counsel, and Board of Directors — operates from offices in the State of Marleston. All major corporate decisions, long-term strategy, supply-chain oversight, and financial reporting originate from Marleston. However, Arcway operates its four production plants in the State of Dunbar, employing 8,000 workers there and generating 71% of its total manufacturing output from Dunbar facilities. Arcway's CFO estimates that Dunbar-based revenue accounts for 60% of company-wide revenue. A Dunbar resident injured in an Arcway plant accident in the State of Fenwick (unrelated to Dunbar operations) sues Arcway in Dunbar federal court. Arcway moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction, arguing its principal place of business is in Marleston, not Dunbar. Is Dunbar a proper forum for general jurisdiction over Arcway?
Answer A: Yes, because Arcway's 8,000 Dunbar employees and 60% of revenue from Dunbar operations make Dunbar Arcway's de facto home state for general jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because Arcway is subject to general jurisdiction in any state where it maintains a significant operational presence
Answer C: No, because Arcway's nerve center — where its executives direct and coordinate corporate activity — is in Marleston, making Marleston (and Delaware) its home states for general jurisdiction, not Dunbar
Answer D: No, because the claim arises from a plant accident in Fenwick, not Dunbar operations, so Dunbar lacks both general and specific jurisdiction over Arcway
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under *Hertz*, the PPB is the nerve center: where the corporation's high-level officers direct, control, and coordinate its activities. All of Arcway's executive decision-making — CEO, CFO, General Counsel, Board — operates from Marleston. Dunbar is the site of operational production, not corporate control. Arcway's home states for general jurisdiction are Delaware (incorporation) and Marleston (nerve center/PPB). Dunbar is neither, and the claim does not arise from Arcway's Dunbar contacts. The motion to dismiss should be granted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Hertz Corp. v. Friend* (2010) definitively adopted the nerve-center test and rejected production-based or employee-headcount tests for principal place of business. The PPB is where the corporation's officers direct, control, and coordinate its activities — here, Marleston. A company can have most of its workforce in one state while being "at home" for jurisdictional purposes in another state where its executives operate. Dunbar operational activity does not make Dunbar the PPB. B: Daimler* rejected the proposition that general jurisdiction attaches wherever the corporation has significant operations. A corporation is "at home" only in its state of incorporation and its PPB state (determined by the nerve-center test). Arcway's significant Dunbar operations do not make Dunbar a home state. D: The answer correctly identifies that specific jurisdiction is unavailable but conflates the basis for denying general jurisdiction. General jurisdiction fails not because the claim doesn't arise from Dunbar contacts — that's a specific-PJ relatedness analysis — but because Dunbar is not one of Arcway's home states under the nerve-center test. The answer reaches the right conclusion but mixes up the general-PJ and specific-PJ analytical frameworks. Procedural-posture precision requires applying the at-home test to general jurisdiction and the relatedness test to specific jurisdiction separately.
lb_rule_reference: For general personal jurisdiction purposes (and also for diversity jurisdiction), a corporation's principal place of business is determined under the "nerve center" test: the single location from which the corporation's high-level officers direct, control, and coordinate its activities. *Hertz Corp. v. Friend* (2010). This is typically the corporate headquarters, not the location of maximum operational activity.
