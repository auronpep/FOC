MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Jacob is charged with assaulting Aaron outside a market. The only disputed issue is whether Jacob was the attacker. Jacob offers evidence that another customer, Eli, had a bruised hand the next day. Jacob offers no evidence that Eli was at the market, knew Aaron, or had any connection to the assault. The prosecutor objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because any other person's injury could suggest another attacker.
Answer B: Admit the evidence because the prosecution must disprove every possible alternative suspect.
Answer C: Admit the evidence only if Eli refuses to testify.
Answer D: Exclude the evidence because, without a connection between Eli and the assault, the bruised hand does not make Jacob's identity less probable.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Alternative-suspect evidence must have a meaningful link to the crime. A stranger's bruised hand, without any connection to the market, victim, or event, does not make Jacob's identity less probable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an injury to any other person is too disconnected without a link to the event. B: B is wrong because the prosecution need not disprove every speculative alternative. C: C is wrong because Eli's availability does not supply the missing probative connection.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Confusing speculative alternative-cause evidence with probative alternative-suspect evidence.
