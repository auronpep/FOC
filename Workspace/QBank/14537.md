Question: A flour wholesaler contracted to deliver to a producer of fine baked goods her flour requirements for a one-year period. Before delivery of the first scheduled installment, the flour wholesaler sold its business and "assigned" all of its sale contracts to a miller, another reputable and long-time flour wholesaler. The original flour wholesaler informed the baked goods producer of this transaction. Assume that the baked goods producer accepted the miller's delivery of the first installment as set out in the original contract between the flour wholesaler and the baked goods producer. However, the baked goods producer paid the contract price for that installment to the flour wholesaler and refused to pay anything to the miller. In an action by the miller against the baked goods producer for the contractual amount of the first installment, who will most likely prevail?
Answer A: A: The baked goods producer, because she had not expressly agreed to accept the miller as her flour supplier.
Answer B: B: The baked goods producer, because the payment of the contractual installment to the flour wholesaler discharged her obligation.
Answer C: C: The miller, because he is a third-party beneficiary with vested rights.
Answer D: D: The miller, because the flour wholesaler validly assigned his rights and delegated his duties under the original contract to the miller.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. The miller and the flour wholesaler have comparable reputations and experience, and there is no other indication that the baked goods producer would have a substantial interest in having the flour wholesaler perform instead. Thus, this was a valid assignment and delegation of duties, and the miller will prevail. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Express agreement by the baked goods producer was not necessary to validly delegate duties to the miller. B is incorrect. The baked goods producer had received notice of the valid assignment, which means its obligation will not be discharged until payment is made to the miller (the assignee), not the flour wholesaler (assignor). C is incorrect. The miller's action will succeed, but because he is an assignee who validly performed the flour wholesaler's duty, as explained above. The miller is not a third party beneficiary because the contract between the wholesaler and the baked goods producer did not have the intention of benefitting the miller as a third party.
