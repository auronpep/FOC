Question: Police arrest Amina on suspicion of robbery but have not filed a complaint, information, or indictment, and no preliminary hearing has occurred. That afternoon, police place Amina in a lineup without counsel. The lineup includes five people of similar appearance, and the witness identifies Amina. Was Amina's Sixth Amendment right to counsel violated?
Answer A: No, because formal adversary proceedings had not begun before the lineup
Answer B: Yes, because every in-person lineup requires counsel regardless of timing
Answer C: Yes, because Amina was under arrest when the lineup occurred
Answer D: No, because lineup procedures can never be challenged before trial
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A lineup can be a critical stage only after the Sixth Amendment has attached. Here, the lineup was precharge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The Wade rule applies to post-attachment corporeal lineups, not every lineup before formal proceedings. C: Arrest alone is not the formal commencement needed for Sixth Amendment attachment. D: A precharge lineup can still be challenged under due process if it is unnecessarily suggestive and unreliable. The Sixth Amendment claim fails only because the right had not attached.
lb_rule_reference: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel at a lineup applies only after formal adversary proceedings have begun; precharge lineups may still be challenged under due process if suggestive and unreliable.
