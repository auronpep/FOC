Question: A tenant sues a landlord for breach of a repair covenant. The landlord moves for summary judgment. The landlord's only support is an affidavit from a property manager stating, "I heard from someone in accounting that the tenant was told repairs were finished." The manager admits she did not inspect the property, attend any conversation with the tenant, or review any repair record. What is the best objection to relying on the affidavit for summary judgment?
Answer A: Affidavits are never allowed on summary judgment
Answer B: The affidavit is too short to support summary judgment
Answer C: The affidavit is automatically enough because it was signed under oath
Answer D: The affidavit does not show personal knowledge or admissible facts from a competent witness
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The manager reports hearsay and speculation, not facts she personally knows and could competently testify about at trial.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 56 permits affidavits and declarations, but they must satisfy competency requirements. B: Length is not the issue. Personal knowledge and admissible factual content are. C: A sworn statement still must be based on personal knowledge and admissible facts.
lb_rule_reference: A summary judgment affidavit or declaration must be made on personal knowledge, set out facts admissible in evidence, and show the declarant is competent to testify.
