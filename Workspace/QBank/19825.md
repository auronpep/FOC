Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A prescription drug carried a detailed warning to prescribing physicians about a rare liver risk. The patient's physician received and understood the warning but prescribed the drug after discussing the risk. The manufacturer did not send a separate warning letter directly to the patient. In the patient's failure-to-warn claim, what is the manufacturer's strongest argument?
Answer A: The warning duty may have been satisfied by an adequate warning to the prescribing physician
Answer B: The manufacturer is liable because it did not warn the patient directly
Answer C: The manufacturer is liable because rare risks never need physician judgment
Answer D: The manufacturer has no duty because prescription drugs are never products
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the learned-intermediary doctrine, the prescribing physician is the warning intermediary for prescription products.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Prescription product warnings may be directed to the physician, who evaluates and communicates risks to the patient. C: Seriousness affects adequacy, but it does not erase the learned-intermediary route. D: Prescription drugs are products; the issue is how the warning duty is discharged.
lb_rule_reference: For prescription products, a manufacturer may satisfy its warning duty by giving adequate warnings to the prescribing physician.
