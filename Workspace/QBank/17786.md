Question: Carmen was charged with first-degree murder. Her appointed attorney knew from the initial client interview that Carmen claimed she was at a family birthday party during the killing and that five relatives could corroborate the alibi. The attorney never contacted any of the relatives, citing a heavy caseload. At trial, no alibi evidence was presented. Carmen was convicted. She later filed a motion for new trial based on ineffective assistance. Her appellate attorney located all five relatives, each of whom confirmed the alibi. Has Carmen established the deficient performance prong of Strickland?
Answer A: No, because the attorney's decision not to call witnesses was a legitimate strategic choice within the range of professional judgment
Answer B: No, because defendants have no constitutional right to have counsel present every possible defense
Answer C: Yes, because failure to investigate a known, readily available alibi defense falls below the objective standard of reasonableness
Answer D: Yes, and prejudice is automatically presumed because the alibi was Carmen's only defense
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under *Strickland v. Washington*, the deficient performance prong requires that counsel's conduct fell below an objective standard of reasonableness under prevailing professional norms. The duty to investigate is fundamental. The attorney knew about the alibi witnesses from the first interview. Failure to contact five identified witnesses — citing caseload rather than any strategic reason — is objectively unreasonable and cannot be insulated as a strategic choice. Carmen has established deficient performance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The strategic judgment presumption protects actual strategic decisions made after reasonable investigation, not decisions made *without* investigation. The attorney never spoke to the relatives and cited caseload, not strategy. Failure to investigate a known, readily available defense is objectively unreasonable and constitutes deficient performance. B: While counsel is not required to pursue every conceivable defense, counsel is required to conduct reasonable investigation. When a client identifies specific, accessible witnesses who can confirm an alibi, failing to contact them falls below the constitutional minimum. D: Prejudice is not automatically presumed from failure to investigate; it must be demonstrated. The presumption-of-prejudice rule applies to complete denial of counsel at a critical stage (*Cronic*), not to errors during representation. Carmen must separately show a reasonable probability that the outcome would have been different had the alibi witnesses been called.
lb_rule_reference: Under Strickland, deficient performance means counsel's conduct fell below an objective standard of reasonableness; courts presume strategic decisions are reasonable, but failure to investigate an available, known defense is not protected as strategy
