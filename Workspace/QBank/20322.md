Question: After a factory explosion, defense counsel's investigator interviews an eyewitness who later dies. The investigator writes a factual summary of what the witness saw, without counsel's mental impressions. The plaintiff seeks the summary and shows there is no other way to obtain the witness's account. What is the best analysis?
Answer A: The summary is absolutely protected because it was prepared by counsel's investigator
Answer B: The summary may be discoverable if the plaintiff shows substantial need and inability to obtain the equivalent without undue hardship
Answer C: The summary is attorney-client privileged because the witness talked to an investigator
Answer D: The summary is discoverable only if it would be admissible at trial without any hearsay objection
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A factual witness summary prepared for litigation is ordinary work product, not automatically immune from discovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Ordinary work product can be overcome by substantial need and undue hardship. C: A third-party witness interview is not a confidential client-lawyer communication. D: Discoverability does not turn solely on trial admissibility.
lb_rule_reference: Ordinary work product may be discovered if the requesting party shows substantial need and cannot, without undue hardship, obtain the substantial equivalent by other means.
