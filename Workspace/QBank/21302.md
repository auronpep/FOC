


BARMATRIX Q#: 21302
Outline_code: 63030100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: If the breeder brings an action in strict liability against the wholesaler for the value of the birds that died, a court is most likely to find for
Answer A: A: the breeder, because the poisonous nature of the seeds was a defect.
Answer B: B: the breeder, because it was reasonable for the dealer to resell the seeds without inspection.
Answer C: C: the wholesaler, since the wholesaler had no contractual relationship with the breeder.
Answer D: D: the wholesaler, because the breeder will be unable to recover damages from the dealer.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Commercial suppliers, such as the wholesaler, are strictly liable if they sell a defective product that injures a foreseeable plaintiff, such as a purchaser. Here, the seeds were defective since they were poisonous, and the breeder was a foreseeable plaintiff.
Wrong Answer Explanations: Since the dealer sold the seeds in the same package in which they came, the dealer's failure to inspect them was foreseeable whether or not it was reasonable. B is incorrect because a foreseeable intervention, even if unreasonable, does not free an antecedent wrongdoer from liability. Although liability for breach of warranty may require privity, the theories of negligence and strict liability in tort do not. C is therefore incorrect. D is incorrect because strict liability is imposed on the seller of a product that is in a defective condition when sold, without regard to the possibility that some other seller of the same product might be liable. Thus, a manufacturer and retailer of a defective product may be jointly and severally liable as joint tortfeasors.
