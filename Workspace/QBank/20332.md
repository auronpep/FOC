Question: A small roaster began operating behind a row of townhouses. Its exhaust fan ran every evening and sent a bitter odor into the shared courtyards for months. A nearby townhouse owner kept her windows shut, stopped using her patio, and paid for temporary air filters. She also claimed that guests stopped booking her short-term rental because the neighborhood was "less pleasant." The roaster complied with all permits but could have installed a taller vent stack at modest cost. In the owner's private nuisance claim against the roaster, which damages are most likely recoverable?
Answer A: All rental revenue the owner hoped to earn, because nuisance law compensates every foreseeable financial effect of the interference
Answer B: Only nominal damages, because the roaster complied with its permits and caused no physical injury
Answer C: Reasonable loss-of-use and mitigation expenses tied to the odor interference, but not every remote rental expectation
Answer D: No damages, because private nuisance requires interference with title rather than personal comfort on the land
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts support a substantial and unreasonable interference with land use. The strongest damages are loss of use and reasonable filter expenses, while speculative or remote booking losses require a tighter causal and loss-type showing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Nuisance protects use and enjoyment of land, but remote hoped-for revenue is not automatically recoverable merely because it was foreseeable. The owner must connect the claimed loss to the land interference and reasonable mitigation. B: Compliance with permits is evidence but not a complete nuisance defense. Substantial and unreasonable interference with use and enjoyment of land can support actual damages without bodily injury. D: Private nuisance protects use and enjoyment of land, including substantial odors, smoke, and noise. It does not require interference with title.
lb_rule_reference: A private nuisance is a substantial and unreasonable interference with another person's use and enjoyment of land, and recoverable loss must be tied to that protected land interest rather than remote business disappointment.
