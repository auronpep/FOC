Question: In a federal diversity action tried to a jury, the plaintiff proves serious injury and obtains a liability verdict, but the damages award is plainly inadequate. The plaintiff asks the federal judge to increase the award without a new jury determination. What should the judge say about additur?
Answer A: Additur is unavailable in federal court; the court may consider a new trial on damages if the standard is met
Answer B: Additur is required whenever the plaintiff proves the jury awarded too little
Answer C: Additur is available only if the defendant objects
Answer D: Additur is the same as renewed JMOL, so the Rule 50(a) preservation rule controls
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal courts may not impose additur as a substitute for the jury's damages determination.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Federal practice does not require or permit additur. C: A defendant's objection does not create federal authority to use additur. D: Additur concerns damages adjustment; JMOL concerns legal sufficiency of evidence.
lb_rule_reference: In federal court, additur is not available; if damages are inadequate, the court may grant a new trial on damages when the standard is met.
