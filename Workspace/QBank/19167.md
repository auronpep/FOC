Question: Jules illegally starts a campfire during a posted fire ban in a dry forest. The fire spreads toward Jules's car. To escape, Jules breaks into a ranger station and steals keys to a locked gate. Jules is charged with burglary and theft and claims necessity. What is the best response to Jules's necessity claim?
Answer A: Necessity succeeds because Jules faced danger from a fire
Answer B: Necessity succeeds because property crimes are always excused in emergencies
Answer C: Necessity likely fails because Jules recklessly created the emergency
Answer D: Necessity fails because only human threats can create necessity
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Jules's own unlawful campfire created the danger. A defendant who creates the emergency, especially recklessly, generally cannot invoke necessity to justify later crimes committed to escape it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Necessity usually requires that the defendant not create the emergency. Jules recklessly created the fire by violating the ban. B: Necessity is conditional, not automatic. Created emergency and reasonable alternatives must be evaluated. D: Human threats are duress; natural or circumstantial dangers are necessity. The issue is created danger, not source type.
lb_rule_reference: Necessity generally requires that the defendant not have created the emergency and that no reasonable legal alternative existed
