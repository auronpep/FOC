Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Isaac hired Rachel to perform consulting services for $50,000. Before Isaac breached, Rachel reasonably incurred $8,000 in nonrefundable travel expenses and $3,000 in software-license fees solely for the project. Rachel cannot prove any expected profit with reasonable certainty. What amount is most appropriate as reliance damages?
Answer A: $50,000, the contract price.
Answer B: $11,000, the reasonable expenses Rachel incurred in reliance on the contract.
Answer C: The reasonable value of Isaac's benefit, even if Isaac received no consulting work.
Answer D: Nothing, because lost profits are uncertain.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages compensate the injured party for reasonable expenditures made in reliance on the contract when expectation is unavailable or too speculative. Rachel's reliance expenditures total $11,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because contract price is not automatically the reliance measure. C: C is wrong because benefit to Isaac is restitution, not reliance. D: D is wrong because uncertain profits do not bar proven reliance expenditures.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance damages / proven preparation costs
