Question: A private daycare owner was falsely named in a neighborhood news site as having left children unattended during a fire drill. The article concerned child safety, a matter of public concern. The site failed to call the owner or check the fire department log, which showed the owner was not present that day. The owner seeks actual damages for lost enrollment, not presumed or punitive damages. In the owner's defamation claim for actual damages, what fault standard must the owner satisfy?
Answer A: Actual malice, because any media defendant reporting on public concern gets that standard
Answer B: Negligence as to falsity, because the plaintiff is private and seeks actual damages
Answer C: Strict liability, because the statement was false and caused lost enrollment
Answer D: No fault standard, because public-concern reports are privileged absolutely
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: For a private plaintiff on a public concern, the Constitution requires at least negligence for actual damages; actual malice is needed for presumed or punitive damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A private plaintiff seeking actual damages in a public-concern case need prove at least negligence, not actual malice. C: Constitutional defamation rules still require fault; falsity and damages alone do not create strict liability. D: Public concern affects fault and damages rules; it does not create absolute privilege for false factual reports.
lb_rule_reference: A private plaintiff in a public-concern defamation case must prove at least negligence for actual damages, but need not prove actual malice for actual damages.
