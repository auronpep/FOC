Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Abraham owned a cabin. He prepared a writing stating, “I convey my cabin on Jericho Road to Isaac.” At the bottom, Abraham wrote only “A.A.” and told Isaac, “Those are my initials, and I use them as my signature for this deed.” Abraham then delivered the writing to Isaac intending an immediate transfer, and Isaac accepted. Abraham later claimed the deed failed because he did not write his full name. Who owns the cabin?
Answer A: Abraham, because only a full handwritten legal name can sign a deed.
Answer B: Isaac, because Abraham adopted the initials as his signature on the deed.
Answer C: Abraham, because initials can authenticate a contract but never a deed.
Answer D: Isaac only if a witness also signs under the initials.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantor authentication. A deed does not require the grantor's full legal name if the mark, initials, or symbol is intended to authenticate the conveyance. Abraham expressly adopted the initials as his signature, so the signature gate is satisfied and title passes to Isaac.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a full legal-name signature is not the lock; authentication intent is. C: C is wrong because the rule is not limited to contracts. D: D is wrong because a witness signature is not required for baseline deed validity on these facts.
lb_rule_reference: Grantor signature / adopted initials
