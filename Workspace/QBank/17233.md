Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: Leah sues Simon for injuries from a collision. Leah bears the burden to prove that Simon ran a red light. On cross-examination, Simon shows that Leah previously misstated the time of the accident in a deposition. Simon argues that the impeachment shifts the burden to Leah to prove her credibility before the jury may consider her testimony. How should the court respond?
Answer A: The burden shifts to Leah to prove her credibility by a preponderance.
Answer B: The jury must disregard Leah's testimony because any impeachment destroys personal knowledge.
Answer C: The court must decide whether Leah is telling the truth before the jury may hear her testimony.
Answer D: The burden of persuasion on negligence remains with Leah, and the impeachment affects the weight and credibility of her testimony.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Impeachment evidence may affect the jury's evaluation of credibility, but it does not create a separate burden requiring Leah to prove her credibility before her testimony can be considered. Leah still bears the burden of persuasion on her claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because impeachment does not create a new burden of persuasion on credibility. B: B is wrong because impeachment does not automatically nullify testimony. C: C is wrong because the judge does not decide ordinary witness truthfulness for the jury.
