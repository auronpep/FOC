Question: Isaac owns Lot 1 and Lot 2 in fee simple. He uses a private lane on Lot 2 to reach a barn on Lot 1. While Isaac still owns both lots, he writes in his personal notes, “Lot 1 has an easement over Lot 2 for the lane.” No lot has been sold or transferred. What is the best statement about the current property interest?
Answer A: No present easement exists because a person generally cannot hold an easement over land that the same person owns in the same estate.
Answer B: A present easement appurtenant exists because Isaac wrote down the lane use.
Answer C: A revocable license exists because Isaac gave himself permission to use the lane.
Answer D: A profit exists because the lane helps Isaac reach the barn.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement requires separate benefited and burdened ownership interests. A person generally cannot have an easement over the same estate that person owns. Isaac's lane use may become relevant if the lots are later severed, but there is no present easement while unity of ownership remains.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a personal note does not create an easement against oneself while both lots are commonly owned. C: C is wrong because a license is permission given to another to use land, not permission from an owner to himself. D: D is wrong because a profit involves removing resources from land.
lb_rule_reference: Unity of ownership / no present easement
