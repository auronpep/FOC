Outline: Nature and Type
Question: Moses signed a deed stating, “For love and affection, I convey my cottage to Miriam.” Moses handed the deed to Miriam and said, “The cottage is yours now.” Miriam accepted the deed. Moses later claimed that the deed was void because Miriam paid no money for the cottage. Who owns the cottage?
Answer A: Moses, because a deed transferring land must be supported by bargained-for consideration.
Answer B: Miriam, because a delivered deed can validly convey land as a gift.
Answer C: Moses, because love and affection can support a will but not a deed.
Answer D: Miriam only if she later pays at least nominal consideration.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is execution and delivery rather than bargained-for consideration. Moses signed and delivered a deed using present words of transfer, Miriam was a living and ascertainable grantee, and Miriam accepted. The gift character of the transaction does not prevent title from passing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because deed validity does not require contract consideration. C: C is wrong because a gift deed can convey land when the deed formalities and delivery are satisfied. D: D is wrong because nominal payment after acceptance is unnecessary once the delivered deed passes title.
lb_rule_reference: Gift deed / no bargained-for consideration required
