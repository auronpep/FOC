Outline: Attorney-client
Question: Moses is charged with fraud and retains lawyer Ruth. Moses speaks only Hebrew, while Ruth speaks only English. Ruth hires Miriam, a professional interpreter, solely to translate the private legal consultation and instructs Miriam to keep the consultation confidential. At trial, the prosecutor subpoenas Miriam to testify about what Moses said to Ruth during the meeting. How should the court rule on Moses's attorney-client privilege objection?
Answer A: Overrule it because the presence of any third party destroys confidentiality.
Answer B: Sustain it because Miriam was a necessary agent who enabled the confidential lawyer-client communication.
Answer C: Overrule it unless Miriam, rather than Moses, personally asserts an interpreter-client privilege.
Answer D: Overrule it because oral communications through an interpreter are not protected unless the interpreter was appointed by the court.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications made for legal advice, and confidentiality is not destroyed by the presence of an agent reasonably necessary to facilitate that advice. Miriam was retained only to translate the private consultation, so her presence preserved rather than defeated the privilege.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it overstates third-party waiver. Unnecessary third parties are different from agents needed to communicate with counsel. C: C is wrong because the privilege belongs to the client, not to the interpreter. Miriam's role is relevant to confidentiality, not holder status. D: D is wrong because court appointment is not the dividing line. The dividing line is whether the third party was reasonably necessary to legal advice and kept within the confidential channel.
lb_rule_reference: Necessary interpreter/agent does not destroy attorney-client confidentiality
lb_student_diagnostic: Student uses an any-third-party shortcut and misses the necessary-agent preservation rule.
