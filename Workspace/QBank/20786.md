Question: Noah hired Sarah for six months of landscaping services. The signed agreement required monthly pruning, stated that the writing was complete, and required written changes. During a drought, Noah orally instructed Sarah to skip pruning and spend the same time deep-watering new trees instead. Sarah followed that instruction for three months while Noah watched and praised the work. Noah later sued Sarah for failing to prune and objected when Sarah offered testimony about the drought instruction and Noah's acceptance of the substituted work. Should the court admit Sarah's testimony for the purpose of showing later waiver or modification of the pruning requirement?
Answer A: No, because the merger clause bars all evidence outside the signed landscaping agreement.
Answer B: Yes, because the instruction and accepted substituted work occurred after signing and are offered to show later waiver or modification, not a prior inconsistent term.
Answer C: No, because waiver of a services contract term can be shown only through UCC course-of-performance evidence.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Sarah first proves a landscaping trade usage that droughts always excuse pruning.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that the parol evidence rule and merger clauses address prior or contemporaneous terms in an integrated writing; they do not bar proof of later waiver or modification. The trigger facts are Noah's post-signing instruction, Sarah's substituted watering work, and Noah's repeated acceptance. The decisive locks are timing and offered purpose. Sarah offers the testimony to show a later change in operative scope.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it treats the merger clause as barring later conduct. C: C is wrong because UCC course-of-performance doctrine is not required to prove waiver of a services contract term. D: D is wrong because the parties' own later instruction and acceptance are enough to support admissibility for waiver; trade usage is not required.
