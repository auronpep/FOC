Question: Caleb is indicted for robbery. Two days later, detectives show the victim a six-photo array that includes Caleb's photograph. They do not notify Caleb's lawyer. The array is not otherwise suggestive, and the victim identifies Caleb. Did the photo array violate Caleb's Sixth Amendment right to counsel?
Answer A: No, because the Sixth Amendment does not require counsel at a photo array, even after indictment
Answer B: Yes, because indictment means counsel must attend every identification procedure
Answer C: Yes, because photo arrays are more suggestive than lineups as a matter of law
Answer D: No, because the Sixth Amendment never attaches until the first witness testifies at trial
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The accused is not physically present at a photo display. The post-indictment counsel rule applies to corporeal confrontations, not photo arrays.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Attachment is necessary but not sufficient. The identification format matters; photo arrays are outside the Sixth Amendment counsel rule. C: The facts say the array was not otherwise suggestive, and the Sixth Amendment question is about counsel, not a per se photo-array rule. D: The right did attach at indictment. The reason there is no violation is that a photo array is not a counsel-required critical stage.
lb_rule_reference: There is no Sixth Amendment right to counsel at a photo array, even after indictment, because the accused is not present at a corporeal confrontation.
