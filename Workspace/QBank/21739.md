Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: A school hired Priscilla to supply uniforms. Before signing the final contract, Priscilla sent a written bid stating that “winter uniforms” would include wool hats. The final signed contract required “winter uniforms” but did not define the phrase. The writing had no merger clause. When a dispute arises, the school offers the prior written bid to show that “winter uniforms” meant uniforms with wool hats. What is the best analysis?
Answer A: The bid is not parol evidence because it was written rather than oral.
Answer B: The bid is automatically barred because all precontract writings are excluded.
Answer C: The bid may be considered if offered to explain the ambiguous phrase “winter uniforms,” but its status as a prior writing does not by itself exempt it from PER analysis.
Answer D: The bid voids the final contract because the final contract omitted the hat term.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Parol evidence can include prior written agreements or writings outside the final contract. The bid is not automatically admissible merely because it is written. But it may be used to explain an ambiguous term if the court permits interpretive evidence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because prior writings can be parol evidence. B: B is wrong because prior writings may be admissible for a proper interpretive purpose. D: D is wrong because omission of the definition does not void the contract.
lb_rule_reference: Prior writing is parol if outside final writing
lb_student_diagnostic: Student thinks the PER applies only to oral statements.
