


BARMATRIX Q#: 14113
Outline_code: 92080104
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motions to dismiss
Question: A teacher from State A filed a diversity action against a janitor from State B in State B federal court. The janitor was served with a summons from the court, but not a copy of the complaint. Which motion or pleading should the janitor's attorney file?
Answer A: A: An answer, including an affirmative defense of lack of personal jurisdiction.
Answer B: B: An answer, including an affirmative defense of insufficient service of process.
Answer C: C: A motion to dismiss for insufficient process.
Answer D: D: A motion to dismiss for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure (FRCP 4(c)(1)) states that a summons must be served with a copy of the complaint. Thus, the federal Rules specifically require that defendants be served with both a summons and complaint. Here, the facts indicate that the defendant had only been served with a summons but not a complaint. Therefore, the janitor should file a motion to dismiss for insufficient process.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The federal district court in State B would have personal jurisdiction over a State B citizen. B is incorrect. It would be far more efficient for the janitor to file only a motion for insufficient process than for him to file an answer, in which he would have to respond to all of the complaint's allegations. D is incorrect. There is nothing in the facts to suggest that the court lacks subject-matter jurisdiction.
