Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Adam signed a paper stating, “I convey Lot 3 in Judah County,” but the paper did not name or describe any grantee. Adam handed the paper to Eve and orally said, “This is for you.” Eve accepted the paper and recorded it. Who owns Lot 3?
Answer A: Eve, because Adam orally identified her when he handed over the paper.
Answer B: Eve, because recording supplies any missing deed term.
Answer C: Eve, because the land was described and Adam signed the paper.
Answer D: Adam, because the deed did not identify a grantee with reasonable certainty.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantee identity in the instrument. A deed must identify or make ascertainable the grantee. Adam's oral statement and Eve's recording do not supply the missing deed term, so title remains with Adam.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an oral statement cannot replace the deed's failure to identify a grantee. B: B is wrong because recording does not validate a deed with no grantee. C: C is wrong because signature and land description are not enough without a grantee.
lb_rule_reference: Missing grantee / oral statement does not supply deed term
