Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Miriam operated a shop that earned an average of $30,000 in monthly gross sales and $12,000 in monthly net profit after variable costs. Noah breached a contract to repair Miriam's only delivery truck, causing the shop to close for one month. Miriam saved $2,000 in variable delivery expenses during the closure that were not already included in the net-profit figure. The shutdown was otherwise foreseeable and proved. What is Miriam's lost-profit recovery?
Answer A: $30,000, the gross sales lost during the month.
Answer B: $12,000, the shop's average monthly net profit.
Answer C: $32,000, gross sales plus saved expenses.
Answer D: $10,000, the net profit reduced by additional expenses Miriam saved because of the breach.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Lost profits are net profits, not gross revenue. Miriam's starting net profit is $12,000. Because she also saved $2,000 in variable delivery expenses not already reflected in that net-profit figure, the recovery is $10,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because gross revenue overcompensates by ignoring costs. B: B is wrong because it fails to subtract the additional saved expense. C: C is wrong because it stacks gross revenue and saved costs in the wrong direction.
lb_rule_reference: Expectation damages / net lost profits
