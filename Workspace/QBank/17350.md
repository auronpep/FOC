Question: Esther agreed to install shelves in Noah's store by Monday morning. She completed the installation Monday afternoon, three hours late. Noah was able to open the store as usual and suffered no lost sales. Noah refused to pay the contract price, asserting that any late performance is a total breach. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Noah's payment duty is discharged because any missed deadline is a total breach.
Answer B: Esther's delay is at most a minor breach on these facts, so Noah must pay subject to any provable damages.
Answer C: Esther may recover only in restitution because late performance destroys the contract.
Answer D: Noah may refuse payment unless Esther proves the delay was impossible to avoid.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A minor delay with no meaningful harm generally creates, at most, a partial breach and damages claim; it does not automatically discharge the other party's payment duty. Esther's performance substantially achieved the contract purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every late performance is total breach. C: C is wrong because the contract remains the basis for payment when substantial performance occurred. D: D is wrong because impossibility is not required to avoid total forfeiture for a minor delay.
