Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A licensed wildlife exhibitor kept a cougar in a reinforced private compound. The exhibitor complied with all permit rules and hired trained staff. During a tour, the cougar lunged through a feeding slot and clawed a visitor who was standing in the marked viewing area. The visitor sues the exhibitor for strict liability. Is the exhibitor likely strictly liable?
Answer A: Yes, because the cougar is a wild animal and the clawing injury arose from its characteristic danger
Answer B: No, because the exhibitor complied with all permit rules and used trained staff
Answer C: No, unless the cougar had previously clawed another visitor
Answer D: Yes, because every animal owner is strictly liable for every injury caused by an animal
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Wild-animal strict liability focuses on animal status and characteristic risk. The cougar's clawing injury is the type of danger that makes possession of the animal strictly liable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Reasonable care and permits do not defeat strict liability for harm caused by a wild animal's characteristic danger. C: Wild-animal strict liability does not require prior attacks. D: Strict liability is not automatic for all animals; the correct route is wild-animal status plus characteristic risk.
lb_rule_reference: A possessor of a wild animal is strictly liable for harm caused by the animal's characteristic dangerous propensities, even if the possessor used reasonable care.
