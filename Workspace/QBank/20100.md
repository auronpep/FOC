Question: A seller sues Mary, alleging an oral agreement for Mary to buy 1,200 woven baskets. No writing was signed. In court, Mary testifies, “I agreed to buy baskets, but only 500, not 1,200.” The seller seeks enforcement for all 1,200 baskets. What is the strongest Statute of Frauds result?
Answer A: The seller may enforce the contract for all 1,200 baskets because Mary admitted a contract existed.
Answer B: The seller may enforce none of the contract because Mary disputes the quantity.
Answer C: The seller may enforce all 1,200 baskets if the market price proves the larger quantity was reasonable.
Answer D: The contract is enforceable under the admission exception only up to the 500 baskets Mary admitted.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The admission exception does not open enforcement beyond the quantity admitted. Mary admitted an agreement for 500 baskets only. Without a writing or other exception, the seller cannot enforce the alleged 1,200-basket quantity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the admission exception has a quantity cap. B: B is wrong because Mary admitted a contract for 500 baskets. C: C is wrong because market reasonableness cannot expand the admitted quantity.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(3)(b)
