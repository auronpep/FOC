Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers unlawfully peeked through a warehouse door and saw stolen appliances. Before that illegal peek, a separate detective had already prepared a warrant application based on surveillance videos and a reliable informant. The application did not mention or rely on the illegal peek. The warrant issued, and officers seized the appliances. The defendant moves to suppress. What is the best argument for admitting the appliances?
Answer A: The appliances are admissible because the warrant rested on information independent of the illegal peek
Answer B: The appliances must be suppressed because police saw them illegally before obtaining the warrant
Answer C: The appliances are admissible because all warrant searches are valid
Answer D: The appliances must be suppressed unless the officers can show inevitable discovery
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The warrant application was based on surveillance and informant information separate from the illegal observation. That independent lawful source supports admission.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Prior illegality does not require suppression when the evidence is later obtained from an independent lawful source. C: Warrants do not automatically cure taint. This warrant works only because it was supported independently. D: The facts directly support independent source; the government need not rely on inevitable discovery.
lb_rule_reference: Evidence initially seen during an unlawful search may be admitted if later obtained through a genuinely independent lawful source.
