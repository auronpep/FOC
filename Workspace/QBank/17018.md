Outline: Custom
Question: During an amateur hockey game, a player delivered a hard shoulder check while both players were skating toward the puck. The check violated no league safety rule and was a customary part of play. The checked player fell and sued for battery. Can the checking player rely on consent?
Answer A: Yes, because the contact was within the ordinary scope of the sport as played
Answer B: No, because consent to a sport never covers intentional contact
Answer C: No, because the plaintiff was injured
Answer D: Yes, but only because the player did not intend any contact
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: By participating, the plaintiff impliedly consented to customary game contacts within the rules and ordinary play.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Many sports involve intentional contacts that are within the scope of implied consent. C: Injury does not defeat consent when the contact was within the consented scope. D: The player did intend contact; the privilege comes from consent within the sport's scope.
lb_rule_reference: Participation in a contact sport implies consent to contacts within the ordinary rules and customs of the game, but not to conduct outside that scope.
