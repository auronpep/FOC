Question: Hannah held a recorded access easement over James's land. The county filed a condemnation action to acquire the easement route for a flood-control project. The condemnation order expressly extinguished all private access easements over the route and provided for compensation to owners of all property interests taken. Hannah insists she may keep using the route instead of claiming compensation. What is the best result?
Answer A: Hannah may keep using the route because recorded easements are immune from condemnation.
Answer B: Hannah receives nothing because only the fee owner has a compensable property interest.
Answer C: Hannah automatically receives fee title to the route because her easement was taken.
Answer D: Hannah's easement may be extinguished by condemnation, but she may be entitled to compensation for the value of the easement interest taken.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement is a property interest that may be taken by eminent domain. If the condemnation proceeding properly takes and extinguishes the easement, the holder ordinarily does not keep using the route but may be entitled to just compensation for the property interest taken.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because easements are not immune from eminent domain. B: B is wrong because easement holders can have compensable property interests. C: C is wrong because condemnation of an easement does not give the holder fee title. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Condemnation of easement interest / compensation route
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is choosing either continued use or no payment; condemnation can terminate the use right while creating a compensation claim.
