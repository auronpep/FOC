Question: Miriam devised Blueacre “to Aaron for life, then to Joshua.” Aaron leased Blueacre to Caleb for 20 years. Five years later, Aaron died. Joshua had not joined in or consented to the lease. What is Caleb's right to remain on Blueacre after Aaron's death?
Answer A: Caleb may remain for the rest of the 20-year term because Aaron signed a valid lease.
Answer B: Caleb may remain only if he pays rent directly to Joshua.
Answer C: Caleb has a fee simple subject to Joshua's remainder.
Answer D: Caleb has no right to remain against Joshua because Aaron's life estate ended.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Aaron could lease only the interest he owned: a life estate. When Aaron died, his life estate ended and Joshua's remainder became possessory. Because Joshua did not consent to the lease, Caleb cannot enforce the lease against Joshua after Aaron's death.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a life tenant cannot bind the remainderman beyond the life estate without consent. B: B is wrong because a new rent arrangement would require Joshua's agreement. C: C is wrong because Caleb received only a leasehold interest from Aaron, not a fee simple.
lb_rule_reference: Life tenant lease / duration of transferred interest
