Question: A merchant seller orally agrees to sell 700 clay pots to Hannah, who is buying them for her home garden and is not a merchant. The seller sends Hannah a signed confirmation. Hannah reads it but does not object in writing within 10 days. Hannah later refuses to buy the pots and asserts the Statute of Frauds. Can the seller rely on the merchant-confirmation rule to satisfy the SOF against Hannah?
Answer A: Yes, because the seller is a merchant and signed the confirmation.
Answer B: Yes, because Hannah failed to object within 10 days.
Answer C: No, unless the pots were specially manufactured.
Answer D: No, because the merchant-confirmation rule applies only between merchants.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: UCC §2-201(2) applies between merchants. The fact that the seller is a merchant does not make the rule available against a nonmerchant consumer recipient who did not sign the writing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because both sides must be merchants for the merchant-confirmation rule. B: B is wrong because the 10-day objection mechanism applies only between merchants. C: C is wrong because specially manufactured goods is a separate exception; it is not the merchant-confirmation rule.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(2) — between merchants only
