Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Daniel owned two farms in the same county, one near a river and one near a road. He signed and delivered a deed stating only, “I convey my farm to Esther.” The deed gave no address, county, acreage, map, nickname, or other identifier. Daniel privately meant the river farm, but he did not express that intent in the deed or in any incorporated writing. Who owns the river farm?
Answer A: Esther, because Daniel privately intended to convey the river farm.
Answer B: Esther, because a deed may always be corrected by oral testimony after delivery.
Answer C: Esther and Daniel as tenants in common in both farms.
Answer D: Daniel, because the deed does not identify which farm was conveyed with reasonable certainty.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is property description. Because Daniel owned two farms and the deed gave no objective way to identify which farm was conveyed, the description is insufficient. Daniel's private intent cannot supply the missing land identity, so title to the river farm remains with Daniel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because unexpressed intent does not cure an insufficient property description. B: B is wrong because extrinsic evidence may explain a description but cannot replace a missing one on these facts. C: C is wrong because the deed did not create fractional interests in both farms.
