Question: Anna sues Officer Peter in federal court. Her complaint asserts a federal civil-rights claim and a supplemental state-law battery claim arising from the same arrest. Officer Peter seeks notes from Anna's confidential treatment sessions with therapist Luke. Anna invokes State A's broader statutory psychotherapist-patient privilege. Officer Peter says the notes are relevant to both Anna's claimed emotional distress on the federal claim and her state-law battery damages. How should the court route the privilege source-law issue?
Answer A: Apply State A privilege law to the entire case because one claim is based on state law and the treatment occurred in State A.
Answer B: Identify the claim or defense for which the notes are offered and apply the corresponding privilege source; in a mixed-use case, the court must address that claim-specific or mixed-use routing instead of selecting one source rule by forum label alone.
Answer C: Apply federal privilege law to the entire case because federal court jurisdiction automatically displaces every state privilege whenever a federal claim appears.
Answer D: Admit the notes without a privilege analysis because their relevance to emotional distress makes them independently admissible.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 501 makes federal common-law privilege the federal default, but in a civil case state privilege law governs a claim or defense for which state law supplies the rule of decision. In a mixed federal/state action, the court should ask what claim or defense the evidence is offered to prove and then handle any mixed-use problem directly. The decisive axis is source law by claim/use, not the courthouse, the relationship label, or relevance alone.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it treats the state claim and state location as a blanket source-law answer for the whole action. The source-law question must be tied to the claim or defense for which the evidence is offered. C: C is wrong because federal jurisdiction is not the only variable. Rule 501 expressly gives state privilege law a role in civil claims or defenses governed by state law. D: D is wrong because relevance does not defeat a privilege objection. Privilege is an independent admissibility barrier that must be routed before use.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 mixed federal/state privilege routing
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats mixed federal/state litigation as requiring one privilege source for the whole lawsuit instead of asking why the evidence is offered.
