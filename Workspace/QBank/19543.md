Question: A buyer ordered 100 clay tablets from a seller for $10 each. The seller delivered 95 tablets. The buyer immediately noticed the shortage but kept and used all 95 tablets in the buyer's school. The buyer then refused to pay anything because the tender was not perfect. What is the most likely result?
Answer A: The buyer owes nothing because the seller did not make perfect tender.
Answer B: The buyer must pay for the accepted tablets at the contract rate, subject to any damages for the shortage.
Answer C: The buyer owes only the reasonable value of the tablets, not the contract price.
Answer D: The buyer may keep the 95 tablets without payment because it gave notice of the shortage.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: After accepting nonconforming goods, a buyer must pay at the contract rate for the goods accepted, while preserving claims for damages caused by the nonconformity. Acceptance prevents the buyer from using the shortage as a complete no-payment defense for goods kept and used.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because perfect tender gives rejection rights before acceptance; acceptance changes the consequence. C: C is wrong because accepted goods are generally paid for at the contract rate subject to damages. D: D is wrong because notice preserves damages; it does not permit free retention of accepted goods.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-607 / acceptance with damages
