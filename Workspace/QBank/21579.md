Question: A plaintiff files a federal contract action seeking damages. The defendant answers and denies liability. Forty days after the answer, the plaintiff serves a written jury demand for the first time. No later pleading directed to the damages issue has been served. What is the likely effect of the late demand?
Answer A: It is timely because a jury demand may be made any time before trial begins
Answer B: It is timely because damages claims can never be waived from jury trial
Answer C: It is late and ordinarily waives the jury right unless the court grants relief
Answer D: It is late only if the defendant objects within 24 hours
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The demand came more than 14 days after the answer, the last pleading directed to the issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 38 sets a deadline tied to the last pleading directed to the jury-triable issue. B: Even a legal jury right can be waived by failing to make a timely demand. D: The rule does not make waiver depend on a 24-hour objection.
lb_rule_reference: A party must serve a written jury demand no later than 14 days after the last pleading directed to the jury-triable issue, or the jury right is waived absent relief from the court.
