Question: In a federal civil trial, Ruth testifies for the plaintiff. On cross-examination, the defendant asks Ruth whether she was convicted three years ago of criminal fraud. Ruth objects that the conviction was only a misdemeanor and that she is embarrassed by it. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because misdemeanor convictions are never admissible to impeach a witness.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because conviction impeachment is available only in criminal cases.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because a conviction requiring proof of fraud involves a dishonest act or false statement and must be admitted to attack truthfulness.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Ruth first testifies that she has a reputation for truthfulness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 609(a)(2), evidence of a conviction must be admitted to attack a witness's character for truthfulness if the court can readily determine that establishing the elements required proving, or the witness admitted, a dishonest act or false statement. Criminal fraud qualifies, regardless of whether the offense was a misdemeanor.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a dishonest-act or false-statement conviction is admissible regardless of punishment. B: B is wrong because Rule 609 applies in civil as well as criminal cases. D: D is wrong because the conviction may attack truthfulness without prior truthful-character testimony.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(a)(2) / dishonest-act conviction must be admitted
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the dishonest-act route and the trap that only felonies or criminal cases count for conviction impeachment.
