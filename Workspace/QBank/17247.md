Question: In a federal personal-injury trial, Mark testifies that after the accident he saw Esther clutch her shoulder, limp, and grimace whenever she tried to lift her arm. Mark is asked whether Esther appeared to be in pain. The defendant objects that pain is a medical issue. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because pain can be described only by a physician.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because Mark's opinion is based on ordinary observations of Esther's conduct and appearance.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Esther's medical records have already been admitted.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Mark can diagnose the exact injury causing the pain.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A lay witness may give a helpful opinion that someone appeared to be in pain when the opinion is based on observed conduct and appearance. Mark is not diagnosing the injury; he is describing the apparent condition he observed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and D confuse apparent pain with medical diagnosis. C invents a medical-record prerequisite for lay observation.
