Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Common objections to questions
Question: In a federal contract trial, on direct examination, Lydia testifies about the date she mailed a notice. On cross-examination, Mark asks only whether Lydia had trouble seeing the mailbox because of heavy rain. On redirect, Lydia's lawyer asks her to describe a separate phone call about settlement negotiations. Mark objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because the redirect question goes beyond the subject opened on cross.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because redirect examination is never allowed.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because redirect may cover any relevant subject.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because settlement negotiations are always admissible on redirect.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Redirect generally responds to matters raised on cross, subject to the court's control. The separate settlement phone call was not opened by cross-examination about visibility at the mailbox.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because redirect is allowed. C: C is wrong because redirect is not automatically wide open. D: D is wrong because settlement evidence has its own limits and is not automatically admissible merely because it is asked on redirect.
