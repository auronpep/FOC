Question: Hannah sold a working grain mill to Caleb for $90,000, payable in three installments. The contract also stated that Hannah would send Caleb a nonessential historical report about the mill's prior owners within 30 days. Hannah delivered the mill, but sent the report a week late. Caleb refused to pay the first installment. What is the likely result?
Answer A: Caleb may refuse payment because every promise in a contract is a condition to every other promise.
Answer B: Hannah may recover the installment because the late historical report was a collateral covenant breach, not a failure of the main performance condition.
Answer C: Caleb may cancel the sale because delivery of the report was an express condition to payment.
Answer D: Hannah may recover only if Caleb first proves he read the late report.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The report promise was collateral to the main exchange and did not go to the mill's delivery or function. Its late delivery may create damages if Caleb proves loss, but it does not discharge Caleb's matured installment payment duty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not all promises are conditions. C: C is wrong because the contract did not condition payment on timely delivery of the report. D: D is wrong because Caleb's reading of the report is irrelevant to the matured installment duty.
lb_rule_reference: Independent covenant / damages-only consequence
