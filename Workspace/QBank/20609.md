Question: A city planning board issues a final written decision denying a landowner's permit and stating that no further local appeal is available. The denial prevents the landowner from building unless the decision is set aside. The landowner immediately brings a constitutional challenge in federal court. How does the final denial affect ripeness?
Answer A: It supports ripeness because the government position is final and presently burdens the plaintiff
Answer B: It defeats ripeness because no one has been prosecuted
Answer C: It makes the case moot because the board already acted
Answer D: It gives standing to every city resident who dislikes the denial
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A final denial with immediate legal consequences gives the court a concrete dispute fit for review.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Ripeness can rest on a final denial and present hardship even without prosecution. C: The denial creates an ongoing injury; it does not end the controversy. D: The final denial burdens the landowner, not every concerned resident.
lb_rule_reference: Ripeness is stronger when the government has taken a final or concrete position that creates present legal consequences for the plaintiff.
