MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Gina and Raul conspire to steal a truckload of electronics and split the goods. They complete the theft and divide the goods. A week later, without telling Gina, Raul burns a storage shed to destroy evidence of his own fingerprints. The original agreement did not include any coverup plan. Prosecutors charge Gina for the arson under a conspiracy theory. What is Gina's strongest argument?
Answer A: The arson occurred after the conspiracy's theft objective was complete and was not part of the original agreement
Answer B: Every coverup act automatically extends a conspiracy forever
Answer C: Gina is liable because she once conspired with Raul
Answer D: Gina is liable only because the stolen items were electronics
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The conspiracy's central objective was stealing and dividing the electronics, which had ended. Raul's later personal coverup was not automatically in furtherance of the finished conspiracy.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Concealment extends the conspiracy only when it is part of the agreed objective or otherwise in furtherance of an ongoing conspiracy. C: Prior membership does not create liability for later acts outside the conspiracy's scope after it has ended. D: The object stolen does not establish that later arson was part of the conspiracy.
lb_rule_reference: A conspiracy generally ends when its central objective is achieved or abandoned; later concealment acts are not automatically in furtherance unless concealment was part of the original agreement.
