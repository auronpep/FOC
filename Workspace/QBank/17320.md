MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Solicitation
Question: Sam agreed to help his friend Tyler intimidate a business rival, Wade, by vandalizing Wade's car. Sam kept watch outside while Tyler broke the car windows. When Wade unexpectedly came out of his building to confront Tyler, Tyler punched Wade in the face, breaking his nose. Sam did not know Tyler planned to assault anyone and had not agreed to any assault. The jurisdiction applies the natural and probable consequences doctrine. Tyler is convicted of assault and battery. Is Sam liable as an accomplice to the assault and battery on Wade?
Answer A: No, because Sam did not agree to the assault and had no knowledge Tyler would hit Wade
Answer B: Yes, because once Sam aided in any crime he is liable for all criminal acts Tyler committed that day
Answer C: Yes, because assault by the principal during a confrontation arising from the vandalism was a natural and probable consequence
Answer D: No, because the natural and probable consequences doctrine applies only to conspiracy, not accomplice liability
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the natural and probable consequences doctrine, Sam is liable for crimes committed by Tyler that were natural and probable results of the criminal enterprise Sam facilitated. Sam helped Tyler vandalize the car of a known business rival at the rival's premises. It is a natural and probable consequence of that scheme that Wade would confront Tyler and that Tyler might use force in response. The assault is not a random or unforeseeable deviation; it is exactly the kind of escalation one would expect. Sam is liable for the assault and battery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The natural and probable consequences doctrine extends liability beyond the specific crime aided. Sam aided the vandalism. The question is whether assault was a natural and probable consequence of vandalizing someone's car at their premises. A confrontation with the car's owner — and the possibility of violence — is a foreseeable result of this kind of criminal enterprise. B: The natural and probable consequences doctrine is not unlimited. The additional offense must be a foreseeable result of the aided crime. Sam would not, for example, be liable if Tyler had robbed a bank later that afternoon — that is not a natural consequence of car vandalism. The doctrine requires a connection between the aided crime and the uncharged crime. D: The natural and probable consequences doctrine applies to accomplice liability independently of whether a conspiracy is charged. It is a doctrine of complicity, not a doctrine of conspiracy alone. Courts regularly apply it to aider-and-abettor cases.
lb_rule_reference: Under the natural and probable consequences doctrine, an accomplice is liable not only for the crime he aided and abetted but also for any other crimes committed by the principal that were a natural and probable consequence of the criminal enterprise the accomplice aided
