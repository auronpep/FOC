Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: Isaac sues after a machine collapsed. A repairman, Boaz, removed a fractured bolt from the machine and kept it in his toolbox for several days before giving it to Isaac's lawyer. Boaz testifies that he recognizes the bolt by its unusual spiral fracture and because he photographed it beside the machine before removing it. The defendant objects that the bolt was not immediately sealed or marked. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the bolt because it was not sealed immediately after removal.
Answer B: Exclude the bolt because a lawyer received it before trial.
Answer C: Admit the bolt only if every person who saw the toolbox testifies.
Answer D: Admit the bolt if Boaz's testimony and photographs sufficiently support a finding that it is the same bolt; custody weaknesses affect weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The proponent can authenticate a physical object through a witness with knowledge and distinctive characteristics. The lack of immediate sealing or marking does not automatically bar admission when the witness can identify the object and supporting photos exist.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because immediate sealing is not a categorical admissibility requirement. B: B is wrong because transfer to counsel does not itself destroy authentication. C: C is wrong because every possible handler or observer need not testify.
