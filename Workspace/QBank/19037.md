Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A small farm kept a goat that had repeatedly charged visitors at the fence and rammed a gate hard enough to dent it. The owner knew of the behavior but told a repair worker, "He is harmless; just walk through." The goat charged and knocked the worker down. The worker sues for strict liability. Which answer best states the strict-liability analysis?
Answer A: The owner may be strictly liable because the owner knew of the goat's dangerous charging behavior
Answer B: The owner cannot be strictly liable unless the goat had previously injured a person
Answer C: The owner is strictly liable only if goats are legally classified as wild animals
Answer D: The worker loses because entering the farm converts every animal injury into premises liability
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A domestic animal can trigger strict liability when the owner knows of dangerous propensities. Charging and ramming behavior supplied that knowledge, and the injury matched the risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Dangerous propensity may be shown by threatening behavior short of actual injury. C: Domestic animals with known dangerous propensities can also support strict liability. D: The call tests animal strict liability. Premises status does not erase the known-propensity analysis.
lb_rule_reference: A domestic animal's known dangerous behavior can support strict liability even if the behavior is not a prior completed injury.
