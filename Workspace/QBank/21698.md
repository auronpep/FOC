Question: During a deposition in a pending lawsuit, Witness falsely testified that Contractor forged invoices connected to the disputed project. The testimony was relevant to the claims. Witness disliked Contractor and hoped the testimony would harm Contractor's reputation. Contractor sues Witness for defamation. What is Witness's strongest defense?
Answer A: Qualified privilege, which is lost because Witness had ill will
Answer B: No privilege, because the statement was false
Answer C: Absolute privilege, because the relevant statement was made in a judicial proceeding
Answer D: No defense, because malice defeats every privilege
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The statement was relevant deposition testimony in a pending case. Absolute judicial-proceeding privilege protects it despite alleged ill will.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Relevant deposition testimony in a judicial proceeding is generally absolutely privileged. B: Absolute privilege can bar defamation liability even for false relevant testimony. D: Malice does not defeat absolute privilege.
lb_rule_reference: Relevant statements made in judicial proceedings are generally absolutely privileged from defamation liability, even if made with improper motive.
