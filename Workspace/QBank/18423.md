


BARMATRIX Q#: 18423
Outline_code: 35030302
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Settlement negotiations and offers of compromise
Question: At the trial of a personal injury action brought by the plaintiff against the defendant and arising out of an automobile accident, the plaintiff attempted to testify that immediately following the accident, the defendant offered to pay $500 to fix the plaintiff's car. Over objection by the defendant's attorney, the plaintiff's statement should be
Answer A: A: excluded, because to admit such evidence would violate public policy.
Answer B: B: admitted, because the court could give the defendant an opportunity to explain his offer.
Answer C: C: admitted, as a declaration against the defendant's pecuniary interest.
Answer D: D: admitted, as an implied admission of a party.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: For various reasons, the law recognizes a policy to encourage settlements. Since a party might refrain from making a settlement offer if he or she believed that his or her offer could be used as circumstantial evidence of his or her liability, this policy prohibits the admission as evidence of the offerer's liability of an offer to settle or compromise a claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: Since the offerer's explanation for his or her offer would not always prevent the offerer from convincing a jury of his or her liability, policy would prevent the admission of evidence of the offer even if the offerer was given an opportunity to explain it. B is therefore incorrect. Although a declaration against pecuniary interest may be an exception to the hearsay rule, and although the admission of a party is an exception to the hearsay rule under common law, and not hearsay at all under FRE 801(d)(2), C and D are incorrect since the settlement offer is being excluded, not because it is hearsay, but because of the policy to encourage settlements.
