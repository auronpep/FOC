MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Appointment and removal
Question: Congress creates an accounting oversight board whose members investigate firms and impose sanctions. Board members may be removed only for good cause by commissioners of an independent agency. Those commissioners, in turn, may be removed by the President only for cause. A sanctioned firm argues that the board's structure violates Article II. Which argument is strongest?
Answer A: The structure is likely unconstitutional because two layers of for-cause protection restrict presidential control over officers who exercise executive power
Answer B: The structure is valid because any removal protection for an independent body is constitutionally permissible
Answer C: The structure is invalid only if board members are principal officers
Answer D: The structure is valid because courts may sever any unconstitutional removal limit after the fact
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The President cannot directly remove board members and also cannot freely remove the commissioners who supervise them. That double insulation can impair the President's Article II duty to ensure faithful execution of the laws.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Dual-layer insulation raises a distinct Article II problem, especially where the board exercises executive enforcement authority. C: Dual-layer removal protection can be problematic even for inferior officers who exercise executive power. D: Possible remedy does not answer whether the structure violates Article II in the first place.
lb_rule_reference: Dual layers of for-cause protection can impermissibly restrict the President's ability to ensure faithful execution of the laws.
