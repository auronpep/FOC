Question: In a federal fraud case, Esther offers a signed invoice from Daniel to show that Daniel had notice of the delivery date printed on the invoice. Daniel objects that the invoice should not be admitted to prove the goods were actually delivered. Esther says she offers it only to show notice. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the invoice because a document must be admissible for all purposes or no purpose.
Answer B: Admit the invoice for the limited purpose of showing notice if properly authenticated, and give a limiting instruction on request.
Answer C: Admit the invoice as conclusive proof that the goods were delivered.
Answer D: Exclude the invoice unless Daniel first admits the delivery occurred.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A documentary exhibit may be admissible for a limited purpose. If the invoice is authenticated and relevant to notice, the court may admit it for that limited use and instruct the jury accordingly on proper request.
