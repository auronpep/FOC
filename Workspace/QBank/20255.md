MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Affirmative duties to act
Question: During a school field trip, a teacher collected student medical forms and learned that one student carried an epinephrine injector for a severe nut allergy. At lunch, the student began wheezing after eating mislabeled trail mix. The teacher did nothing for several minutes because the teacher did not want to interrupt the tour. The student sues the teacher for negligence. Is the student likely to establish that the teacher owed an affirmative duty in the negligence claim?
Answer A: Yes, because the school-student relationship during the trip required reasonable protective action
Answer B: No, because the teacher did not create the student's allergy or the mislabeled trail mix
Answer C: Yes, because anyone who sees a medical emergency must intervene
Answer D: No, because affirmative duties exist only for common carriers and innkeepers
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A school supervising a student has a special relationship that can require reasonable affirmative care. The teacher knew of the allergy and the emergency during school custody.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Risk creation is not the only affirmative-duty exception. A school-student special relationship can independently require reasonable protective action. C: The duty here does not arise from universal bystander status. It arises from the teacher's supervisory relationship with the student. D: They are examples, not the entire category. Schools supervising students can also owe affirmative protective duties.
lb_rule_reference: A special relationship, such as school and student during school custody, can create an affirmative duty to take reasonable protective action.
