Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: A supplier sued a retailer in federal court for failing to pay for a shipment of tablets. The retailer defended by saying the tablets were defective but did not assert any counterclaim for its repair costs. After final judgment for the supplier, the retailer files a new federal suit seeking repair costs from the same defective shipment. What is the supplier's strongest response?
Answer A: The later repair-cost claim is likely barred because it arose from the same transaction and should have been asserted as a compulsory counterclaim
Answer B: The later repair-cost claim is automatically allowed because defendants never have to bring counterclaims
Answer C: The later repair-cost claim is allowed because it seeks damages instead of declaratory relief
Answer D: The later repair-cost claim is barred only if it was actually tried to a jury in the first case
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The repair-cost claim is logically tied to the same shipment and opposing claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Rule 13(a) requires many same-transaction counterclaims to be raised in the first action. C: The compulsory-counterclaim analysis turns on transaction and occurrence, not the remedy label. D: Omission of a compulsory counterclaim can bar later litigation even though that omitted claim was not tried.
lb_rule_reference: A claim arising from the same transaction or occurrence as the opposing party's claim generally must be pleaded as a compulsory counterclaim or it may be barred in a later action.
