Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Self-defense
Question: At a party, Leon punches Craig in the face without provocation, knocking him down. Leon then backs away across the room and says loudly, "I'm done, I don't want to fight." Craig gets up, grabs a baseball bat, and charges at Leon. Leon, unable to escape and fearing serious injury, strikes Craig with a chair, breaking Craig's arm. Leon is charged with battery. Which of the following best describes Leon's legal position?
Answer A: Leon has no defense because he was the initial aggressor
Answer B: Leon has no defense because he caused the situation
Answer C: Leon may claim self-defense because he effectively withdrew before Craig escalated to deadly force
Answer D: Leon may claim self-defense only if he tried to retreat first
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The initial-aggressor rule bars self-defense only until the aggressor withdraws. A complete, communicated withdrawal ("I'm done, I don't want to fight") restores the right to self-defense. Once Leon withdrew, Craig became the new aggressor by arming himself with a bat and charging. A bat used against a person constitutes deadly force (capable of causing serious bodily harm or death). Leon's response — using a chair to defend himself — is proportionate to the threat and legally justified after his effective withdrawal.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The initial-aggressor rule does not permanently bar all self-defense claims. Once an aggressor completely and genuinely withdraws and communicates that withdrawal, the right to self-defense is restored. Leon's loud, clear statement that he was done — made before Craig grabbed the bat — satisfies the withdrawal requirement. B: Causation of a prior conflict does not permanently forfeit all defensive rights. The law allows an aggressor to regain self-defense rights through withdrawal. Once withdrawal is complete and communicated, Craig's subsequent escalation (deadly weapon) makes Craig the current aggressor. D: Retreat and withdrawal are different concepts. Withdrawal is about an aggressor re-entering self-defense eligibility by ending his own aggression. Retreat (under minority rules) is a separate duty that applies before using deadly force, not a prerequisite to regaining the right to self-defense after aggressor withdrawal. Moreover, under the majority rule there is no duty to retreat at all — and even under minority retreat rules, no retreat is required when retreat is impossible.
lb_rule_reference: An initial aggressor forfeits the right to claim self-defense unless (1) the aggressor completely withdraws from the fight and communicates that withdrawal, after which the other party becomes the new aggressor, or (2) the victim responds to non-deadly force with deadly force
