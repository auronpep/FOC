Question: A state university handbook says tenured lab managers may be fired only for cause after documented misconduct. A tenured lab manager is terminated immediately after a budget meeting, with no notice of reasons and no opportunity to respond. The university argues that public jobs never create property interests. What is the best due process analysis?
Answer A: The employee may have a property interest because the rules limit termination to cause
Answer B: No public employee can ever have a property interest in a job
Answer C: The employee has a property interest only if the termination also violated equal protection
Answer D: The employee loses because a hearing is required only after criminal charges
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A for-cause limitation can create a legitimate claim of entitlement to continued public employment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: For-cause rules can create an entitlement protected by procedural due process. C: Procedural due process does not require a separate equal protection violation. D: Civil government deprivations of property can also require process.
lb_rule_reference: A public employee can have a property interest in continued employment when state law, contract, or rules limit removal to cause.
