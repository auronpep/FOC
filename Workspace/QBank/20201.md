Question: Hannah signed a written contract agreeing to sell her farm to Samuel in sixty days. Samuel paid a deposit. The contract satisfied the Statute of Frauds for a land-sale contract, but Hannah had not yet delivered a deed. Before closing, Samuel demanded possession on the ground that he already held legal title. Who holds legal title before the deed is delivered?
Answer A: Hannah, because a signed land-sale contract can create contract or equitable rights but does not itself transfer legal title as a deed.
Answer B: Samuel, because the contract satisfied the Statute of Frauds.
Answer C: Samuel, because he paid a deposit.
Answer D: Hannah only if she tenders the deposit back immediately.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The surface route is a land-sale contract, but the Titles lock asks whether legal title has transferred by deed. A Statute-of-Frauds-compliant contract may create contract or equitable consequences, but Hannah had not delivered a deed conveying legal title. Legal title therefore remains with Hannah before closing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a contract writing is not the same as a deed of legal conveyance. C: C is wrong because payment of a deposit does not pass legal title. D: D is wrong because refund rights do not decide the title-transfer lock.
