Subtopic: Rights in Land
Outline: Scope
Question: Isaac owned Lot A, which was benefited by a recorded easement over Leah's driveway “for ingress and egress to Lot A.” At the time of the grant, Lot A had a single-family home. Isaac later converted the home into three lawful residential apartments. The number of cars using the driveway increased, but the driveway was still used only to reach Lot A, and the increased use did not physically damage Leah's land. Leah sues to terminate the easement. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Leah may terminate the easement because any increased use automatically ends an easement.
Answer B: Leah may terminate the easement because the grant did not expressly mention apartment tenants.
Answer C: Leah may not terminate the easement if the increased use is a reasonable evolution within the granted ingress-and-egress purpose.
Answer D: Leah may terminate the easement because nonuse by Isaac personally constitutes abandonment.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The scope of an easement is measured by the grant's purpose and the reasonable expectations of the parties. Increased use does not automatically terminate an easement if the use remains within the granted purpose and does not unreasonably burden the servient estate. The driveway is still being used only for ingress and egress to Lot A.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because increased use is not automatic termination. B: B is wrong because the easement benefits access to Lot A, not merely Isaac personally. D: D is wrong because abandonment requires intent or conduct showing relinquishment; nonuse by Isaac personally does not matter if the easement continues to serve Lot A.
lb_rule_reference: Express easement scope / reasonable evolution / misuse versus termination
