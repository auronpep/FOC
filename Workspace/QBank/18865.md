Outline: Attorney-client
Question: Nathan receives an email from his lawyer, Esther, analyzing Nathan's legal exposure in a contract dispute. Nathan forwards the email to Joseph, an outside accountant, with the note, “Tell me whether this lawsuit will affect next quarter's budget.” Joseph is not retained by Esther, is not helping Esther provide legal advice, and does not need the legal analysis to transmit information between Nathan and Esther. In discovery, the opposing party subpoenas Joseph for the forwarded email. Nathan objects that the email is attorney-client privileged. What is the likely privilege consequence of Nathan's forwarding the email?
Answer A: There is no waiver because Joseph is a professional who usually keeps client financial information confidential.
Answer B: There is no waiver because Nathan subjectively intended Joseph to keep the email private.
Answer C: The privilege is waived for the forwarded communication because Nathan actually disclosed legal advice to a nonnecessary third party.
Answer D: Joseph now holds the privilege because he received the email and can decide whether to produce it.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications for legal advice. Voluntary disclosure of the privileged communication to a third party who is not necessary to obtain or transmit legal advice can waive confidentiality for the disclosed communication. Joseph was consulted for budget consequences, not to assist counsel in legal advice. The decisive axis is completed unnecessary disclosure, not Nathan's subjective desire for privacy.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because professional confidentiality is not the same as attorney-client privilege. Joseph's accounting role does not preserve legal-advice confidentiality unless he is necessary to the legal-advice channel. B: B is wrong because intent to keep information private does not undo actual disclosure to a nonnecessary third party. D: D is wrong because receiving a privileged email does not make Joseph the privilege holder. The client holds the privilege, but disclosure may waive it.
lb_rule_reference: Disclosure to nonnecessary third party can waive attorney-client privilege
lb_student_diagnostic: Student confuses business confidentiality and intended privacy with privilege-preserving necessity.
