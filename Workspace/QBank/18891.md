Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Isaac contracted to sell Miriam 100 tablets for $400 each. Isaac repudiated. When Miriam learned of the repudiation, the market price for comparable tablets was $455 each. Miriam did not immediately cover, but she reasonably spent $900 obtaining substitute quotes and arranging inspection. A month later, the market price rose to $470 each. What is Miriam's proper recovery?
Answer A: $7,900.
Answer B: $5,500.
Answer C: $6,400.
Answer D: Nothing, because Miriam did not cover immediately.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under UCC Article 2 for buyer damages after seller repudiation. Because Miriam did not use cover as the measure, the market formula uses the market price when she learned of the breach: (100 × $455 − 100 × $400) + $900 = $6,400. The limiting gates are market-date selection and mitigation; cover is permitted but not mandatory on these facts. The result scope is limited market damages plus incidentals, and the later $470 market price is not the formula baseline.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it uses the later $470 market price, a WAL-REM-004 time-of-breach or market-date substitution. B: B is wrong because it omits the reasonable incidental expense. D: D is wrong because failure to cover does not automatically forfeit buyer damages.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §§2-713 and 2-715 — market damages at time buyer learned of breach plus incidentals
