Question: David signed and recorded a dedication granting the city “a public pedestrian easement across the marked walkway on David's parcel connecting the street to the public park.” The city accepted the dedication. The instrument did not convey fee title to the walkway. What is the best classification of the public's interest in the walkway?
Answer A: A private easement appurtenant to the city hall building.
Answer B: A public easement allowing pedestrian use without transferring fee title to the walkway.
Answer C: A revocable license because individual pedestrians did not sign the dedication.
Answer D: Fee simple ownership of the walkway by every member of the public.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A dedication can create a public easement: a nonpossessory public right to use land for the dedicated purpose. The instrument granted a public pedestrian easement and expressly did not convey fee title.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the right is public and not tied to city hall as a dominant parcel. C: C is wrong because public easement rights do not require each user to sign the dedication. D: D is wrong because an easement is a use right, not fee ownership.
lb_rule_reference: Public easement / dedication
