MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A theater negligently left a dark cable across an aisle. A patron tripped and suffered a knee injury. Because of a rare condition, the knee injury required far more treatment than an average knee injury. The theater argues that the full injury was not foreseeable because the condition was rare. Is the theater likely liable for the full aggravation caused by the fall?
Answer A: Yes, because tripping over an aisle cable foreseeably causes physical injury, and the theater takes the patron as found
Answer B: Yes, because defendants are liable for every condition the plaintiff had before the accident
Answer C: No, because the exact severity of the knee injury was not foreseeable
Answer D: No, because the cable caused only a fall, not a medical condition
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The type of harm, physical injury from a trip, was foreseeable. The eggshell rule covers the unusual extent caused by the tort.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The defendant is liable for tort-caused aggravation, not unrelated baseline disease. C: The law does not require foreseeability of exact severity after a foreseeable physical injury occurs. D: The fall aggravated the condition. A tortfeasor is liable for the aggravation caused.
lb_rule_reference: Proximate cause requires foreseeability of the type of harm, not the exact extent or severity, once duty and breach are established.
