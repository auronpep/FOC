Outline: Conviction of crime
Question: In a federal civil trial, Leah testifies for the defendant. The plaintiff seeks to impeach Leah with a conviction for obstructing a government investigation. The statute under which Leah was convicted can be violated either by physically blocking investigators or by making a false report. The judgment and plea record show only that Leah pleaded guilty under the statute; they do not show which method she used. Leah objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the conviction automatically because obstruction always involves dishonesty.
Answer B: Admit the conviction under Rule 609(a)(2) only if the court can readily determine that the elements required proving, or Leah admitted, a dishonest act or false statement.
Answer C: Exclude the conviction automatically because obstruction is never a felony.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction under all circumstances because the conviction did not occur in this case.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 609(a)(2) requires admission only when the court can readily determine that establishing the elements of the crime required proving, or the witness admitted, a dishonest act or false statement. A broad offense label is not enough if the conviction could rest on non-deceitful conduct. Other Rule 609 routes may still require separate analysis if the offense was punishable by more than one year.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the offense label does not automatically establish a false-statement conviction when the statute has multiple methods. C: C is wrong because the problem does not state the punishment level, and misdemeanor/felony status is not the only route. D: D is wrong because convictions from other cases may be used for impeachment if Rule 609 is satisfied.
