Question: Moses sold a set of tools to David. In the written sale contract, David promised to pay Esther $5,000 as part of the purchase price. Before signing, Moses knowingly lied to David that the tools were new, even though they were badly used. David promptly rescinded the sale contract after discovering the fraud. Esther then sued David for the $5,000. What is the likely result?
Answer A: David is not liable because a beneficiary's right is subject to David's defense that the underlying contract was induced by Moses's fraud.
Answer B: David is liable because Esther did not participate in the fraud.
Answer C: David is liable because an intended beneficiary's right is independent of the contract that created it.
Answer D: David is not liable only if Esther personally made a fraudulent statement to David.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: An intended beneficiary enforces the contract that made the promise for her benefit. If the promisor has a valid defense to enforcement of that contract, such as fraud by the promisee inducing the agreement, the beneficiary ordinarily takes subject to that defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because Esther's innocence does not eliminate David's defense to the underlying contract. C: C is wrong because the beneficiary's right is derivative, not independent of the contract. D: D is wrong because the defense arises from Moses's fraud in the contract Esther seeks to enforce.
