Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: Police arrested Nina on suspicion of insurance fraud. During booking and without Miranda warnings, an officer asked Nina her name, address, and employer. Nina stated she worked at "Precision Claims Consultants." The officer then asked, "And what's your role there?" Nina replied, "I handle claim settlements." The state seeks to introduce all of Nina's booking responses to establish that she had access to the fraudulent claims at issue in the case. Which of Nina's responses, if any, should be suppressed?
Answer A: All responses should be suppressed because Nina was in custody during booking and had received no Miranda warnings
Answer B: No responses should be suppressed because all questions were asked during the booking process
Answer C: Only the response about her role at the company should be suppressed because that question exceeded routine booking and constituted investigative interrogation
Answer D: Only the employer information should be suppressed because Nina's employer is self-incriminating in the context of a fraud charge
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The booking exception covers questions necessary to complete the administrative record: name, address, date of birth, employer. These are admissible. However, "What's your role there?" was not an administrative question — it was a targeted follow-up designed to elicit information directly relevant to the fraud charge (access to claims). This question constituted interrogation without Miranda warnings while Nina was in custody. Only that response must be suppressed; the routine booking answers remain admissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The booking exception exempts routine administrative questions from Miranda's interrogation requirement. Name, address, and employer are classic booking questions. Not all in-custody questioning without warnings violates Miranda. Suppressing everything overreads Miranda. B: The booking exception does not immunize all questions asked during booking — only those that are routine administrative questions. "What is your role there?" is not a standard booking question; it is an investigative follow-up designed to establish Nina's access to the fraudulent files. It is reasonably likely to elicit incriminating information about the charged crime and constitutes interrogation outside the booking exception. D: The test for whether a booking question requires Miranda warnings does not depend on whether the answer turns out to be incriminating. The question "Who is your employer?" is a standard booking question regardless of what the answer reveals. The booking exception applies to the form and purpose of the question, not to the incriminating nature of the response.
lb_rule_reference: Routine booking questions (name, address, date of birth, height, weight) are not interrogation under Miranda; a question that goes beyond administrative processing and is designed to gather evidence is interrogation even if asked during booking.
