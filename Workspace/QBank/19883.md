Outline: Expectation damages
Question: David agreed to renovate Leah's shop for $80,000. David expected total performance costs of $63,000, for a $17,000 profit. Before Leah wrongfully canceled, David had spent $28,000. Of that amount, $10,000 was for materials David could use without loss on another job. David avoided all remaining costs. What is David's proper expectation recovery?
Answer A: $45,000, because David recovers the $28,000 spent plus the $17,000 profit.
Answer B: $35,000, because David recovers costs incurred plus profit, reduced by the $10,000 value of reusable materials.
Answer C: $80,000, because Leah canceled the renovation contract.
Answer D: $17,000, because David can recover only profit and never incurred costs.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Expectation damages must avoid overcompensation. David's baseline is $28,000 incurred costs plus $17,000 expected profit, but he must credit the $10,000 value of materials he can reuse without loss. The proper recovery is $35,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores the salvage/reuse credit. C: C is wrong because full contract price ignores costs saved and reusable materials. D: D is wrong because David also lost expenditures already made, except to the extent he can reuse them.
lb_rule_reference: Common law expectation damages; salvage and reuse offset
