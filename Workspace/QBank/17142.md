MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff, a citizen of New York, sues a defendant corporation, a citizen of Pennsylvania, for $100,000 in a diversity action in federal court. During litigation, the defendant impleads a third-party defendant under Rule 14, asserting that the third party is liable to indemnify the defendant. The third-party defendant is also a citizen of New York. The third-party defendant then asserts a $30,000 counterclaim against the original defendant. The third-party defendant also seeks to assert a $30,000 claim against the original plaintiff for contribution, arguing that the original plaintiff was partly responsible for the underlying accident. The governing forum is federal court under the MBE baseline. May the federal court exercise supplemental jurisdiction over (1) the third-party defendant's counterclaim against the original defendant, and (2) the third-party defendant's claim against the original plaintiff?
Answer A: The court may exercise supplemental jurisdiction over both claims because both arise from the same transaction and § 1367(a) covers all related claims in the case
Answer B: The court may exercise supplemental jurisdiction over both claims because the § 1367(b) bar applies only to the original plaintiff's claims, not to claims by third-party defendants
Answer C: The court may not exercise supplemental jurisdiction over either claim because the third-party defendant is a New York citizen and the original plaintiff is also a New York citizen, destroying diversity
Answer D: The court may exercise supplemental jurisdiction over the third-party defendant's counterclaim against the original defendant but not over the claim against the original plaintiff, because direct plaintiff-adverse claims trigger § 1367(b)
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under § 1367(b), in a diversity-only case, supplemental jurisdiction does not extend to claims by plaintiffs against persons joined under Rules 14, 19, 20, or 24. The third-party defendant here is not the original plaintiff; it is a party brought in as a defendant. The § 1367(b) limitation applies to claims asserted by original plaintiffs (or those in the plaintiff's position), not by third-party defendants. The third-party defendant's counterclaim against the original defendant and its claim against the original plaintiff both share a common nucleus of operative fact with the main action and fall within § 1367(a) supplemental jurisdiction without being blocked by § 1367(b).
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1367(b) limits supplemental jurisdiction in diversity-only cases. However, the § 1367(b) bar applies specifically to claims BY PLAINTIFFS — not by defendants or third-party defendants. The third-party defendant asserting a counterclaim against the original defendant and a claim against the original plaintiff is not a "plaintiff" in the original action. Section 1367(b) does not bar those claims. Both claims share a common nucleus with the main case and may be maintained. C: Complete diversity is tested only between original plaintiffs and original defendants in the main case. The presence of a non-diverse third-party defendant does not destroy the original action's diversity or eliminate supplemental jurisdiction over related claims. The third-party defendant's claims are analyzed under § 1367, not under the original § 1332 diversity analysis. D: Section 1367(b) bars claims BY THE PLAINTIFF (original plaintiff or persons asserting a plaintiff-side position) against newly joined parties — not claims against the plaintiff asserted by others. The third-party defendant asserting a claim against the original plaintiff is not the plaintiff. Section 1367(b) does not apply; § 1367(a) supplemental jurisdiction is available for that claim.
lb_rule_reference: 28 U.S.C. § 1367(b) — in diversity-only cases, supplemental jurisdiction does not extend to claims by plaintiffs against non-diverse parties joined under Rules 14, 19, 20, or 24 if that would destroy the complete diversity required by § 1332.
