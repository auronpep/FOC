Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Levi is charged with assaulting James with a narrow-bladed tool. An officer testifies that, ten minutes after the assault, she found a narrow awl in Levi's backpack. A medical witness says the victim's wound was consistent with an awl but admits that several other narrow tools could have caused the wound. Levi moves to strike the medical witness's testimony as insufficiently probative. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant the motion, because the medical witness could not identify the awl as the exact tool used.
Answer B: Deny the motion, because the testimony has some probative value and the uncertainty goes to weight.
Answer C: Grant the motion, because the evidence is unfairly prejudicial whenever several tools could have caused the wound.
Answer D: Deny the motion only if the judge first finds that the awl probably caused the wound.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The testimony links the kind of tool found with the kind of wound. It is not conclusive, but it tends to make the prosecution's theory more probable. The jury decides how much weight to give the witness's concession.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because exact identification is not required for relevance. C: C is wrong because ordinary damaging force is not unfair prejudice, and the alternative-tool problem is a weight issue. D: D is wrong because the judge does not have to find the ultimate causation fact before the evidence may be considered.
