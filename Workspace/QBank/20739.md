Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: DeltaSpring, a component manufacturer in Idaho, sold valves to a machine assembler in Idaho. DeltaSpring had no distributors, advertising, dealers, or customers in Columbia. The assembler independently sold a finished machine to a Columbia buyer. When the valve allegedly failed in Columbia, the buyer sued DeltaSpring in Columbia. DeltaSpring knew the assembler sold machines nationwide but did not know where any particular machine would go. Is DeltaSpring subject to specific jurisdiction in Columbia based only on the component's arrival there through the assembler?
Answer A: Yes, because DeltaSpring could foresee that the finished machine might be sold nationwide
Answer B: Yes, because every product injury creates jurisdiction where the injury occurs
Answer C: No, because the facts show only a unilateral downstream sale, not DeltaSpring's purposeful targeting of Columbia
Answer D: No, because product-liability defendants can be sued only in their state of incorporation
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: DeltaSpring sold in Idaho to an Idaho assembler and did not advertise, distribute, or otherwise serve the Columbia market. The component's arrival through another party is not enough.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Foreseeability of travel is not enough without purposeful forum targeting or service of the forum market. B: Injury location alone does not replace minimum contacts. D: Product defendants may face specific jurisdiction in forums they purposefully serve for related claims.
lb_rule_reference: Stream-of-commerce contacts require purposeful availment of the forum market, not merely awareness that a product might end up there.
