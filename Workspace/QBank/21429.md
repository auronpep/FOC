Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Reporter in State A writes an online article accusing a State B restaurant chain of food-safety violations at its State B locations. Reporter emails the article to State B food bloggers and buys targeted ads shown only to State B readers. The restaurant sues Reporter in State B for defamation based on the article. What is the best analysis of specific personal jurisdiction?
Answer A: Jurisdiction is supported because Reporter targeted State B readers about State B conduct, and the claim arises from that targeting
Answer B: Jurisdiction exists whenever a plaintiff resides in the forum and suffers reputational harm there
Answer C: Jurisdiction fails because online articles can never support personal jurisdiction
Answer D: Jurisdiction fails unless Reporter also owns property in State B
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: State B was the focal point of the conduct and audience. The defamation claim is tied to forum-directed acts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Residence and harm alone are not enough; the key is express aiming at the forum. C: This was not mere passive accessibility; Reporter targeted a State B audience. D: Specific jurisdiction can rest on forum-aimed conduct tied to the claim.
lb_rule_reference: In intentional-tort cases, specific jurisdiction is stronger when the defendant expressly aims conduct at the forum and the forum is the focal point of the conduct and harm.
