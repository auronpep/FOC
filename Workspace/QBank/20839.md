Question: Lawyer Moses interviewed witness Leah and wrote notes summarizing Leah's account, adding stars next to facts he considered legally decisive and comments about which points would support summary judgment. The opposing party can depose Leah directly but seeks Moses's notes. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Compel the notes because Leah's factual account is not privileged.
Answer B: Compel the notes after redacting Leah's name.
Answer C: Compel the notes if the opponent agrees to use them only for impeachment.
Answer D: Protect the notes, especially the portions revealing Moses's mental impressions and legal theories, because the opponent can obtain Leah's facts directly.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Attorney interview notes prepared for litigation are work product, and the mental-impression portions receive special protection. The opponent can obtain the underlying facts by deposing Leah rather than taking counsel's prepared notes. The decisive axis is work-product channel versus direct fact discovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This confuses underlying facts with counsel's notes about those facts. B: B is wrong because Redacting the witness name does not remove the attorney mental impressions. C: C is wrong because An impeachment-only promise does not eliminate work-product protection.
lb_rule_reference: FRCP 26(b)(3)(B) / attorney interview notes
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating attorney notes as the same thing as witness facts and missing the direct-discovery alternative.
