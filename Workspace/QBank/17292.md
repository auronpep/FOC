Question: A statute authorizes an agency to grant hardship waivers from a federal reporting requirement. The same statute says either house of Congress may cancel any waiver by passing a resolution within 30 days. The House passes a resolution canceling a company's waiver, and the agency tells the company it must comply immediately. What is the company's best constitutional challenge?
Answer A: The House resolution is invalid because it changes the company's legal rights without bicameralism and presentment
Answer B: The House resolution is valid because Congress may always supervise agencies by resolution
Answer C: The House resolution is valid because the agency originally granted the waiver
Answer D: The House resolution is invalid only if the Senate objects
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Canceling the waiver has legal effect outside Congress. That legislative action must satisfy constitutional lawmaking procedures, not a one-house veto.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Oversight does not let one house alter legal rights without bicameralism and presentment. C: Once Congress changes the legal consequences of the waiver, it is legislating and must use the constitutional process. D: The constitutional defect is the absence of bicameral passage and presentment, not whether the other house complains.
lb_rule_reference: When Congress takes action with legal effect outside the legislative branch, it generally must comply with bicameralism and presentment; a one-house legislative veto is invalid.
