Question: A retailer hired Peter to serve as operations manager for one year at a stated salary. The written employment agreement also provided that the retailer would give Peter a laptop and phone to use during the job. After Peter accepted, the retailer argued that Article 2 governed the agreement because it included movable equipment. Which law supplies the basic formation and modification rules for the agreement?
Answer A: Article 2, because the agreement includes a laptop and phone.
Answer B: Article 2, because every written employment agreement involving movable objects is a sale of goods.
Answer C: Common law, because the dominant transaction is employment services and the equipment is incidental.
Answer D: Neither Article 2 nor common law, because employment agreements are never contracts.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The source-law lock comes first. Although the agreement mentions movable equipment, its dominant purpose is employment services. Common law controls basic formation and modification issues for the employment contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because goods being present does not automatically make Article 2 control a services-dominant contract. B: B is wrong because written form and movable incidental equipment do not convert employment into a goods sale. D: D is wrong because employment agreements can be contracts; the source-law classification is the issue.
lb_rule_reference: common_law
lb_student_diagnostic: Student applies Article 2 because goods appear incidentally, instead of asking what the transaction predominantly is.
