Question: In a federal narcotics trial, the government offers a vial of powder allegedly taken from Elijah. The arresting officer, evidence custodian, and chemist all testify. Each identifies the case number, seals, and initials on the vial. Elijah objects that because powder is fungible, it can never be admitted as real evidence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because fungible items are inadmissible as a matter of law.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless the powder has a unique natural marking.
Answer C: Overrule the objection if the chain and markings are sufficient to support a finding that the vial is the item seized.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only as demonstrative evidence.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Fungible evidence often requires careful chain-of-custody proof, but it is not inadmissible as a category. Seals, labels, initials, and testimony can be sufficient.
