Question: Esther writes to a bank: “If you extend $20,000 of credit to my brother Benjamin this month, I will guarantee repayment.” The bank silently extends the credit on the last day of the month. Esther has no way to know whether the bank extended the credit. The bank waits six months before telling Esther and then sues on the guaranty after Benjamin defaults. What is Esther's best formation argument?
Answer A: The bank accepted automatically by extending credit, and notice could never be required.
Answer B: The guaranty failed because Esther died before the bank gave notice.
Answer C: The bank may have needed to give Esther seasonable notice of acceptance because Esther had no reason to know the requested performance occurred.
Answer D: The guaranty was a gift promise and could not be accepted by performance.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A unilateral guaranty offer can be accepted by the requested extension of credit, but in circumstances where the guarantor has no reason to know the performance occurred, seasonable notice of acceptance may be required. A six-month delay gives Esther a strong notice-based formation defense.
