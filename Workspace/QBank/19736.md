Question: Samuel and Ruth owned a parcel as tenants in common. The parcel was physically large enough to divide into two lots of equal acreage. But the zoning ordinance required each separate lot to have at least 100 feet of street frontage. If the parcel were divided in kind, one resulting lot would have only 50 feet of frontage and could not legally be used as a separate buildable lot. Samuel seeks partition in kind. Ruth argues that partition by sale is more appropriate. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Partition in kind must be ordered because physical acreage is the only relevant question.
Answer B: The zoning restriction may make partition in kind impracticable, supporting partition by sale or another remedy.
Answer C: Samuel is automatically entitled to a variance because co-owners have a right to divide land.
Answer D: The zoning ordinance is automatically a taking because it affects partition.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Partition analysis may consider whether physical division would produce legally usable parcels. Zoning restrictions can make an otherwise physical division impracticable or inequitable. Here, one divided lot would lack the required frontage and could not legally be used as a separate buildable lot.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because physical acreage is not the only consideration. C: C is wrong because a variance is not automatic and depends on the applicable zoning standards. D: D is wrong because a land-use restriction affecting partition is not automatically a taking.
