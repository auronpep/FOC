Question: A diverse plaintiff sues for $90,000 in compensatory damages. The defendant moves to dismiss, arguing that discovery will probably show the claim is worth only $40,000. No statute or contract caps recovery below $75,000. Should the court dismiss for lack of the amount in controversy?
Answer A: Yes, because likely recovery below $75,000 defeats jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because the defendant disputes damages
Answer C: No, because it is not legally certain on these facts that plaintiff cannot recover more than $75,000
Answer D: No, but only if the claim arises under federal law
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff pleaded more than $75,000 in good faith and no legal cap appears.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The test is legal certainty, not probability of recovery. B: A damages dispute does not itself defeat amount in controversy. D: This is a diversity amount question, not a federal-question requirement.
lb_rule_reference: A good-faith amount allegation controls unless it appears to a legal certainty that the plaintiff cannot recover more than $75,000.
