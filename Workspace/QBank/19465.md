internal_id: TR-171

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A shopping center hired a roofing contractor to repair leaks. A basic license check would have shown that the contractor had three recent safety suspensions for dropping tools from roofs. The shopping center skipped the check because the contractor offered a low price. While repairing the roof, a contractor employee negligently dropped a hammer onto a shopper. Is the shopping center likely liable to the shopper for negligent hiring of the contractor?
Answer A: No, because a principal is never liable for the torts of an independent contractor
Answer B: Yes, because the shopping center unreasonably failed to check readily discoverable safety suspensions before hiring the contractor
Answer C: Yes, but only because roofing is abnormally dangerous strict-liability work
Answer D: No, because the shopper must prove the shopping center personally dropped the hammer
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The shopping center had a duty to use reasonable care in selecting a contractor for work posing risks to shoppers. The skipped check would have revealed directly relevant safety problems.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The claim is direct negligent hiring. A principal may be liable for its own failure to use reasonable care in selecting a competent contractor. C: Ordinary roofing is not automatically abnormally dangerous strict-liability work. The stronger theory is the shopping center's direct negligent hiring. D: Direct negligent hiring can be a proximate cause of the injury if hiring an unsafe contractor created the risk that materialized.
lb_rule_reference: A principal may be directly liable for negligently hiring an unsafe independent contractor even when the principal is not vicariously liable for all contractor torts.
