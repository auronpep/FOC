Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Damages
Question: A driver negligently injured a pedestrian's shoulder. A doctor recommended low-risk physical therapy that would likely restore use of the shoulder within a month. The pedestrian unreasonably refused therapy for no medical or personal reason, causing six extra months of avoidable pain and lost work. The pedestrian sues for all losses. How should the refusal affect damages?
Answer A: It bars the entire negligence claim
Answer B: It has no effect because plaintiffs never have mitigation duties
Answer C: It may reduce recovery for losses that reasonable therapy would have avoided
Answer D: It converts the claim into contributory negligence before the accident
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The pedestrian remains able to recover for tort-caused harm, but not for damages unreasonably allowed to accumulate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Failure to mitigate generally reduces avoidable damages; it does not erase liability for the original injury. B: Plaintiffs must take reasonable steps to avoid unnecessary additional damages. D: Mitigation addresses avoidable damages after injury, not breach or pre-accident comparative fault.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff must take reasonable steps to mitigate avoidable damages, and unreasonable refusal can reduce recovery for avoidable increments.
