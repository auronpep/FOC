Question: In a federal personal-injury trial, Noah calls Ruth, an employee of the defendant's liability insurer, to testify that the plaintiff's claimed injury is exaggerated. The plaintiff asks Ruth on cross-examination whether her employer will have to pay any judgment against Noah. Noah objects that evidence of insurance is inadmissible. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because liability insurance is never admissible in a negligence action.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless the plaintiff first proves that Noah acted negligently.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because insurance may be used to prove Noah acted wrongfully.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because the insurance relationship is offered to show Ruth's possible bias, not to prove Noah's negligence.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 411 bars liability-insurance evidence when offered to prove negligence or wrongful conduct, but it allows the evidence for another purpose, such as showing a witness's bias or prejudice. Ruth's employment by the insurer gives her a potential motive to minimize damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because insurance evidence is not absolutely barred. B: B is wrong because the admissibility for bias does not depend on first proving negligence. C: C is wrong because the insurance may not be used to prove wrongful conduct; the valid use is bias.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 411 / insurance evidence for bias
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests policy-exclusion absolutism and the permitted-purpose route for insurance bias.
