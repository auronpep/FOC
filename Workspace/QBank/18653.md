Outline: Clergy and penitent
Question: During an open prayer circle attended by twelve congregants, Joel said, “I burned the records and need prayer.” Pastor Amos was present, as were many other adults. In a federal obstruction prosecution, Joel seeks to prevent one of the congregants from testifying about what Joel said. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because Pastor Amos heard the statement.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because the statement concerned repentance.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Pastor Amos personally testifies.
Answer D: Overrule the objection, because the statement was made in an open group setting and was not confidential.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Clergy privilege requires a confidential communication to clergy in the protected capacity. Joel made the statement in an open group of congregants; it was not confidential as to the testifying congregant. The decisive axis is confidentiality, not religious setting alone.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Clergy presence does not make a public or group statement confidential. B: B is wrong because Penitential subject matter is not enough without confidentiality. C: C is wrong because The testifying witness is a congregant who heard a nonconfidential statement; the pastor's testimony is not required.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / confidentiality in clergy communications
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating religious subject matter or clergy presence as a substitute for confidentiality.
