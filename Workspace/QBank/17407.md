Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Isaac signed a written contract to sell Jacob a vineyard. Before signing, Isaac allegedly said that the sale would not be effective unless a county water permit was issued. The final writing described the land and price but did not mention the permit. Jacob argues that UCC Article 2 bars the oral condition because the writing is final. What is the best source-law response?
Answer A: Article 2 governs because grapes are grown on the land.
Answer B: Common-law real-property contract principles govern because the contract is for sale of land, not goods.
Answer C: Article 2 governs because every written sales contract is a sale of goods.
Answer D: No contract rule applies because oral conditions are always unenforceable.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A contract for the sale of land is not a sale of goods under Article 2. The source-law lock is common-law/real-property contract doctrine, and the alleged oral condition should be analyzed under that route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the presence of crops on land does not make the land-sale contract itself a goods contract. C: C is wrong because Article 2 does not govern all written sales contracts. D: D is wrong because oral condition-precedent evidence may be considered in appropriate cases.
lb_student_diagnostic: Land/goods source-law confusion.
