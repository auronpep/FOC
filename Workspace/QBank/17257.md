Outline: Personal knowledge
Question: In a federal arson trial, Mary testifies that from her apartment she heard a loud explosion, smelled smoke, and saw flames coming from a storage shed. Mary did not see anyone near the shed. The prosecutor asks Mary to testify that Aaron set the fire. Aaron objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because Mary heard the explosion and smelled smoke.
Answer B: Exclude all of Mary's testimony because she did not see who started the fire.
Answer C: Sustain the objection to the statement that Aaron set the fire, but allow Mary to testify about the explosion, smoke, and flames she perceived.
Answer D: Overrule the objection if the jury could infer from Mary's testimony that Aaron set the fire.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Mary has personal knowledge of what she heard, smelled, and saw. She does not have personal knowledge that Aaron set the fire because she did not observe the actor or act. The court should permit her sensory observations but exclude the unsupported conclusion about Aaron.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because sensory observations do not support all conclusions the proponent might want. B: B is wrong because Mary can still testify to what she perceived. D: D is wrong because the jury may draw inferences from admissible evidence, but Mary cannot testify to an ultimate fact she did not perceive.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 / sensory observations and limited scope
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests limited-scope personal knowledge and prevents converting sensory perception into unsupported identification.
