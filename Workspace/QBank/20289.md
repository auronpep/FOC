MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A cafe negligently failed to mop a small water spill near the counter, creating a slipping risk. A customer noticed the spill, stepped safely around it, and then fainted because the wet floor reminded him of a childhood storm. He fell and broke his wrist. Is the cafe's unmopped spill likely a proximate cause of the wrist injury?
Answer A: Yes, because the spill was a but-for cause of the customer's emotional reaction
Answer B: Yes, because all injuries occurring near a negligent condition are within the risk
Answer C: No, because fainting from a memory trigger is not the kind of slip-and-fall risk created by the wet floor
Answer D: No, because businesses owe no duty to customers who see a hazard
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The harm type is outside the ordinary risk that required mopping.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: But-for cause alone does not establish risk-scope proximate cause. B: The injury must be tied to the type of risk that made the condition negligent. D: Seeing the hazard may affect comparative fault, but it does not erase duty as stated.
lb_rule_reference: Proximate cause requires that the harm fall within the general type of risk that made the conduct negligent; a different, freakish harm may be outside the risk scope.
