Outline: Bias
Question: In a federal assault trial, Caleb testifies for the prosecution that he barely knows the victim, Gideon. The defense offers a lease showing that Caleb and Gideon have shared an apartment for three years. The prosecution objects that the lease is extrinsic evidence on a collateral matter. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the lease because all extrinsic evidence affecting credibility is barred.
Answer B: Exclude the lease because bias can be shown only by asking Caleb questions on cross-examination.
Answer C: Admit the lease only if Caleb has first testified about Gideon's character for truthfulness.
Answer D: Admit the lease because it is relevant to Caleb's possible bias or motive to favor Gideon, subject to ordinary controls.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Bias is a central form of impeachment. Extrinsic evidence may be used to show a witness's relationship, interest, or motive to shade testimony, subject to the court's ordinary control and balancing rules. The lease tends to show a close relationship with the victim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the collateral bar does not categorically exclude bias proof. B: B is wrong because bias may be proved extrinsically. C: C is wrong because the issue is Caleb's bias, not character-for-truthfulness testimony.
lb_rule_reference: Bias impeachment / extrinsic proof permitted subject to court control
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests bias as a separate impeachment purpose and collateral-bar overread.
