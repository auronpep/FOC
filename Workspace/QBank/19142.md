Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Hannah agreed to buy 1,000 sacks of seed from Isaac at $12 per sack. Their writing identified the seed, quantity, and price, and both parties signed it. The writing did not specify the exact delivery date or place. Isaac later refused to deliver, arguing that no contract formed because those terms were missing. Is Isaac likely correct?
Answer A: Yes, because Article 2 requires every delivery term to be stated before a goods contract can form.
Answer B: Yes, because common-law indefiniteness rules always govern contracts for seed.
Answer C: No, because under Article 2 a goods contract does not fail for open terms if the parties intended to contract and there is a reasonably certain basis for a remedy.
Answer D: No, but only because Hannah paid the full price before delivery.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Article 2 allows formation even when one or more terms are left open, so long as the parties intended to make a contract and there is a reasonably certain basis for an appropriate remedy. The writing identifies the goods, quantity, and price, supporting formation despite open delivery details.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Article 2 is more flexible about open terms. B: B is wrong because seed is goods and Article 2 controls the source-law gate. D: D is wrong because advance payment is not required for the contract to form.
