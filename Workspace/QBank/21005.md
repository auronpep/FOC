Question: A bookstore employee accidentally left a customer asleep in a reading room and locked the room for the night. Ten minutes later, another employee found the customer still asleep, unlocked the room, and let her out before she woke. The customer suffered no physical harm and learned what happened only after checking the security log the next day. The customer sues the bookstore for false imprisonment. Is the customer likely to prevail?
Answer A: Yes, because the room was locked while she was inside
Answer B: Yes, because she learned about the confinement the next day
Answer C: No, because she was unaware while confined and suffered no harm from the brief lock-in
Answer D: No, because false imprisonment requires a public official's arrest
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Even though the room was locked, the customer was asleep throughout the brief period, woke only after release, and suffered no harm from the confinement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: False imprisonment generally also requires awareness of confinement at the time or harm from the confinement. Neither is present. B: Later discovery is not awareness of confinement while it occurs and does not supply harm. D: Private actors can commit false imprisonment. The claim fails here because awareness or harm is missing.
lb_rule_reference: False imprisonment generally requires awareness of confinement at the time or resulting harm from the confinement.
