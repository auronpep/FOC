Question: Congress enacts a statute under Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment creating liability for purely private restaurants that discriminate against customers. The statute does not rely on commerce power and does not address state involvement or state enforcement. What is the strongest objection to Section 5 as the power source?
Answer A: Section 5 targets enforcement of Fourteenth Amendment limits on state action, not purely private conduct
Answer B: Section 5 allows Congress to regulate any private unfairness
Answer C: Section 5 applies only to federal officials, not states
Answer D: Congress can regulate private restaurants only by asking each state for permission
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Fourteenth Amendment state-action requirement limits Section 5 enforcement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Moral wrong does not create Fourteenth Amendment state action. C: Section 5 enforces the Fourteenth Amendment against state action. D: Congress needs an enumerated power, not state permission.
lb_rule_reference: Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment authorizes Congress to enforce Fourteenth Amendment limits on state action, not to regulate purely private conduct unrelated to state action.
