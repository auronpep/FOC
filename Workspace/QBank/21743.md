Question: In a federal civil action after a car crash, Peter testifies that he spoke with Isaac moments before Isaac drove away. Peter says Isaac smelled of alcohol, could not stand straight, and fumbled with his keys. Peter is asked whether Isaac appeared too impaired to drive safely. Isaac objects that the question bears on an ultimate issue. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because opinion testimony may never touch an ultimate issue.
Answer B: Overrule the objection if the opinion is rationally based on Peter's perceptions and helpful to deciding Isaac's condition.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Peter is a toxicologist.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Peter states Isaac's exact blood-alcohol concentration.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Opinion testimony is not automatically objectionable merely because it embraces an ultimate issue. The proposed lay opinion is grounded in Peter's perceptions of Isaac's appearance and conduct and would help the jury evaluate impairment. It does not require toxicology expertise for that limited observation.
