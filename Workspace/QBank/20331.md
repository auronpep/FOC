Question: Samuel contracted with Leah's studio to design and install a custom lobby display. The signed agreement allocated most of the price to design labor and installation, with a smaller amount for light panels. It stated that the display would be built “according to the approved concept drawings,” and the agreement also contained a merger clause. Leah installed the panels but used a different frame shape than the drawings showed. Which statement is least likely to be accepted?
Answer A: Because the predominant purpose was design and installation services, common-law interpretation rules rather than Article 2 should generally control.
Answer B: The approved concept drawings may be treated as part of the agreement if the signed writing incorporated them.
Answer C: The merger clause automatically bars the court from considering the approved drawings, even though the writing refers to them.
Answer D: If the frame-shape term is incorporated, the court may use that term to determine whether Leah's display conformed to the agreement.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a merger clause does not automatically exclude material that the writing itself incorporates by reference. The trigger facts are the service-predominant mixed transaction, the reference to approved concept drawings, and the alleged deviation from those drawings. The decisive lock is term status: incorporated drawings are not merely prior extrinsic negotiations; they may be part of the final contract content. C is least likely because it treats merger language as an absolute bar.
