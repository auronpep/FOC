Question: A company responds to a document request by saying only, "All responsive documents are privileged." It gives no dates, authors, recipients, document types, or general subject descriptions. The plaintiff moves to compel a proper privilege log. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Deny the motion because saying "privileged" is always enough
Answer B: Grant the motion to require a proper privilege description that does not reveal the protected substance
Answer C: Order production of every document because privilege logs are forbidden
Answer D: Require the company to disclose the full legal advice in the log
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The opposing party must have enough information to evaluate the privilege or work-product claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The rule requires an express claim and a description sufficient to assess the assertion. C: A deficient log may require correction, but privilege logs are the ordinary method for asserting protection. D: The description should permit assessment without revealing the privileged content itself.
lb_rule_reference: A party withholding discoverable information on privilege or work-product grounds must expressly make the claim and describe the nature of the withheld material without revealing the protected information.
