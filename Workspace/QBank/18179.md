Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A kitchen knife is sold with a sharp exposed blade. The blade is not defective, and its sharpness is visible and ordinary for a knife. A user cuts his hand while slicing vegetables and sues the manufacturer for failure to warn that the blade can cut skin. Is the failure-to-warn claim likely strong?
Answer A: Yes, because every dangerous product needs a warning for every possible injury
Answer B: Yes, because the user suffered physical injury
Answer C: No, because the cutting risk of an ordinary sharp knife is obvious
Answer D: No, because knives are never products for products-liability purposes
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The risk that a sharp knife can cut skin is apparent to ordinary users.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Products liability does not require warnings for obvious and commonly understood risks. B: Injury alone does not create a warning defect. D: A knife is a product; the issue is obvious danger.
lb_rule_reference: A manufacturer generally has no duty to warn of obvious, commonly understood product dangers.
