Question: Mary signed a deed conveying Gardenacre to Elizabeth and mailed it to Elizabeth with a letter stating, “Keep this paper safely. It is not to take effect until I die.” Elizabeth kept the deed but did nothing else. Mary died without executing a will. Does Elizabeth take Gardenacre by the deed?
Answer A: Yes, because Elizabeth physically received the signed deed before Mary died.
Answer B: Yes, because Mary used a deed rather than a will.
Answer C: Yes, but only if Elizabeth records the deed before Mary's heirs object.
Answer D: No, because Mary's letter showed no present intent to transfer title during Mary's life.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is present transfer intent. Mary's letter expressly postponed effectiveness until death. Physical receipt of the paper did not create deed delivery when the grantor intended a testamentary transfer rather than a present conveyance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because physical receipt alone does not establish delivery. B: B is wrong because using a deed form does not cure lack of present intent. C: C is wrong because recording cannot transform an undelivered testamentary deed into a valid lifetime conveyance.
lb_rule_reference: A deed intended to become effective only at death lacks present delivery
