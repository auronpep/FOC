Question: Paul orally agreed to buy wool from Hannah. Hannah sued, alleging an oral contract for 900 bundles. In his answer, Paul admitted that he had orally agreed to buy 300 bundles but denied any agreement for 900. There was no signed writing and no goods had been delivered. Under UCC §2-201, what is the maximum quantity enforceable based on Paul's admission?
Answer A: None, because an admission in pleadings can never satisfy the Statute of Frauds.
Answer B: 300 bundles, because the admission exception enforces the contract only up to the quantity admitted.
Answer C: 900 bundles, because any admission proves the existence of the entire alleged oral contract.
Answer D: A commercially reasonable quantity, because the court supplies quantity when the writing is missing.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: UCC §2-201 permits enforcement if the party against whom enforcement is sought admits in pleading, testimony, or otherwise in court that a contract was made, but the contract is not enforceable beyond the quantity admitted. Paul admitted 300 bundles only.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an in-court admission is a statutory exception. C: C is wrong because the admitted quantity caps enforcement. D: D is wrong because the UCC does not supply a quantity for Statute of Frauds enforcement.
