Question: A plaintiff has already taken 10 depositions in a federal case. Without obtaining the defendant's consent or court leave, the plaintiff notices an eleventh deposition and schedules it for two full days of questioning. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The notice is proper because any witness with relevant information may be deposed without limit
Answer B: The plaintiff needs stipulation or court leave for the eleventh deposition and for exceeding the one-day, seven-hour limit
Answer C: The notice is proper if the witness is a nonparty because nonparty depositions do not count toward the limit
Answer D: The only defect is that the plaintiff must serve interrogatories before taking another deposition
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The proposed deposition exceeds both the presumptive number and duration limits.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 30 imposes presumptive limits on number and duration. C: Nonparty depositions still count toward the presumptive deposition limit. D: The federal rules do not impose a general interrogatory-first sequence.
lb_rule_reference: A party generally needs leave of court to take more than 10 depositions or to depose a witness who has already been deposed, and a deposition is limited to one day of seven hours unless otherwise stipulated or ordered.
