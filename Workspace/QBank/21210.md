MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Injurious falsehood (trade libel)
Question: A restaurant critic wrote, "This new blender feels flimsy and is not worth the counter space." The critic had actually tried the blender and honestly disliked it. The manufacturer alleged that the review was unfair but could not identify any canceled order, lost customer, or other specific pecuniary loss. Is the manufacturer likely to prevail on a trade libel claim against the critic?
Answer A: Yes, because harsh product criticism is trade libel whenever sales may suffer
Answer B: No, because the review was opinion-based and no special pecuniary loss was shown
Answer C: Yes, because trade libel does not require proof of lost sales
Answer D: No, because only competitors can commit trade libel
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The critic gave an evaluative opinion from use, and the manufacturer lacks specific lost sales or other special damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Trade libel requires false disparagement and specific pecuniary loss; opinionated criticism and speculative harm are not enough. C: Trade libel generally requires special damages in the form of specific pecuniary loss. D: The claim can involve noncompetitors, but it fails here for lack of falsity and special damages.
lb_rule_reference: Trade libel is not established by a harsh opinion or by product criticism without falsity, malice, and specific pecuniary loss.
