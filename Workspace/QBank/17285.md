Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Paul contracted with Priscilla to provide accounting software for Paul's shop. Priscilla breached, and Paul proved $6,000 in lost net profits during the downtime. Paul also seeks $20,000 because the breach caused him anxiety and embarrassment with customers. The contract was an ordinary commercial software contract. What is Paul's likely recovery?
Answer A: $26,000, because all harms caused by breach are recoverable.
Answer B: $20,000, because emotional distress is a better measure than lost profits.
Answer C: Nothing, because lost profits are never available in ordinary commercial contracts.
Answer D: $6,000, because the proven lost net profits are expectation damages, while ordinary emotional distress is not recoverable here.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: In an ordinary commercial contract, proven lost net profits may be recoverable as expectation damages if the gates are met. Emotional distress and embarrassment are generally not contract damages absent special circumstances not present here.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because contract damages do not include every harm caused by breach. B: B is wrong because emotional distress is not the better commercial expectation measure. C: C is wrong because lost profits may be recoverable when proven with reasonable certainty.
lb_rule_reference: Expectation damages / emotional loss excluded
