Outline: Conviction of crime
Question: In a federal civil trial, Nathan testifies for the defendant. The plaintiff seeks to impeach Nathan with a seven-year-old felony conviction. Nathan shows that he received a certificate of rehabilitation for that conviction, but the plaintiff shows that Nathan was later convicted of another felony punishable by more than one year in prison. Nathan objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the earlier conviction because any certificate of rehabilitation makes the conviction inadmissible forever.
Answer B: Exclude the earlier conviction unless it involved a false statement.
Answer C: Do not exclude the earlier conviction on the basis of the certificate alone because Nathan later had another qualifying felony conviction.
Answer D: Admit the earlier conviction only if it is more than ten years old.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A conviction is excluded under FRE 609(c)(1) after a rehabilitation-based pardon, annulment, certificate, or equivalent procedure only if the person has not later been convicted of a crime punishable by death or imprisonment for more than one year. Nathan's later qualifying felony prevents the certificate from automatically excluding the earlier conviction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because rehabilitation-based exclusion is defeated by a later qualifying felony. B: B is wrong because the felony route may apply even without false statement. D: D is wrong because being old is not the route for admission; the issue is the effect of the rehabilitation certificate.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(c)(1) / rehabilitation finding plus later felony exception
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests rehabilitation certificates and the later-felony exception.
