Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A cleaning company signed a document calling each cleaner an "independent contractor." In practice, the company required cleaners to wear company uniforms, use company equipment, follow a minute-by-minute cleaning checklist, accept assigned buildings, and work under an on-site supervisor who could change their methods. While rushing to meet the supervisor's schedule, a cleaner negligently left a slick floor unmarked, causing a tenant to fall. The tenant sues the cleaning company for vicarious liability. Is the tenant likely to prevail on a vicarious liability claim against the cleaning company?
Answer A: No, because the written agreement calls the cleaner an independent contractor
Answer B: Yes, because the company controlled the cleaner's manner and means, supporting employee status and ordinary respondeat superior
Answer C: Yes, because independent contractors are always within an apparent-agency exception when they wear a uniform
Answer D: No, because the tenant must show the company was negligent in hiring the cleaner
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The cleaner was not merely an independent contractor in substance. The company controlled how the work was done, so the cleaner's negligence during assigned cleaning may be imputed under ordinary respondeat superior.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Labels do not control when the principal controls the manner and means of the work. The company's uniform, equipment, checklist, assignments, and supervisor point toward employee status. C: Apparent agency is not automatic from a uniform, and the stronger point here is actual control over manner and means. The answer uses the wrong branch of the contractor tree. D: The tenant's claim can proceed vicariously if the cleaner is functionally an employee acting within scope. Direct negligent hiring is not required for respondeat superior.
lb_rule_reference: A contract label does not control; if the principal controls the manner and means of work, the worker may be treated as an employee for vicarious liability.
