Question: Samuel owned a pasture burdened by Rebecca's access easement over a farm lane. The easement did not prohibit gates. To keep cattle from wandering, Samuel installed a gate across the lane and immediately gave Rebecca a key. Opening the gate adds less than one minute to each trip and does not prevent ordinary access. Is Samuel's gate automatically unlawful?
Answer A: No, because a servient owner may make reasonable use of the land, including a gate for livestock, if the gate does not unreasonably interfere with the easement.
Answer B: Yes, because a servient owner may never place any object on an easement route.
Answer C: Yes, because installing a gate terminates the easement by merger.
Answer D: No, because the gate gives Samuel the right to revoke Rebecca's easement whenever he chooses.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The servient owner retains the right to use the servient land in ways that do not unreasonably interfere with the easement. A livestock gate with a key that creates only a minimal delay can be reasonable if it preserves access.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the servient owner is not barred from all use of the easement area. C: C is wrong because merger concerns unity of ownership, not installing a gate. D: D is wrong because a reasonable gate does not make an easement revocable.
lb_rule_reference: Servient use / reasonable noninterference
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is assuming either that the easement holder controls the entire strip or that the servient owner can revoke access.
