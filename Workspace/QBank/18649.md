MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Assault
Question: A tenant raised his fist toward a landlord during an argument in a hallway. Standing fifteen feet away, the tenant said, "If the security guard were not between us, I would punch you right now." The guard stood directly between them, and the tenant did not move closer. The landlord sues the tenant for assault. Is the landlord likely to prevail in an assault claim against the tenant?
Answer A: Yes, because the tenant raised his fist and used threatening words
Answer B: No, because the tenant lacked apparent present ability to make immediate contact
Answer C: No, because the landlord was not actually struck
Answer D: Yes, because the landlord was offended by the tenant's language
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Assault requires reasonable apprehension of imminent contact. The guard blocked access, the tenant stayed away, and the tenant's own words made the threat conditional.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The tenant's words and the guard's position showed that contact would not occur immediately. The facts negate imminent apprehension. C: Assault does not require contact. The correct reason is the absence of imminent apprehension under the whole context. D: Offense alone is not assault. The plaintiff must reasonably apprehend imminent harmful or offensive contact.
lb_rule_reference: Words accompanying an apparent threat can negate imminence when they show the defendant will not make immediate contact.
