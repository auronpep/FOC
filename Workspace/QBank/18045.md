Outline: Falling below the standard of care
Question: A company used explosives to demolish a concrete smokestack. A shock wave from the explosion knocked a pedestrian down on the sidewalk outside the barricade. The pedestrian sues only under abnormally dangerous activity strict liability. The company argues that the pedestrian cannot identify any careless act by the blasting crew. What is the best response to the company's argument?
Answer A: The pedestrian must prove careless blasting because all tort claims require breach of reasonable care
Answer B: The lack of a careless act does not defeat strict liability if the activity was abnormally dangerous and caused harm within its risk
Answer C: The pedestrian automatically wins because strict liability never requires causation
Answer D: The pedestrian loses unless the company violated a demolition statute
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The theory turns on abnormal danger and risk-scope causation, not on proving that the blasting crew acted unreasonably.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Strict liability for abnormally dangerous activity does not require proof of negligent breach. C: Strict liability still requires that the activity caused the injury and that the injury was within the relevant risk. D: The strict-liability claim does not depend on negligence per se.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff asserting abnormally dangerous activity strict liability need not prove negligent breach if the activity is abnormally dangerous and the injury is within the abnormal risk.
