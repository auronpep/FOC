Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Hana and Ilya agree to rob a payroll van. The next day they wait at the delivery route with masks and a replica gun, but police arrest them before the van arrives. They are charged with conspiracy to commit robbery and attempted robbery. Assuming both elements are proved, do the offenses merge?
Answer A: Yes, because conspiracy always merges into any later attempt
Answer B: No, conspiracy and attempted robbery may both stand if each is proved
Answer C: Yes, because attempt is less serious than completed robbery
Answer D: No, but only if the van contained cash
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The agreement and the later substantial step are distinct bases of liability. The conspiracy non-merger rule allows both convictions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Conspiracy punishes the agreement; attempt punishes the substantial step toward the target. Conspiracy does not merge into attempt. C: Merger here turns on the relationship between conspiracy and attempt, not severity. D: The merger rule does not depend on what the van contained.
lb_rule_reference: Conspiracy does not merge into attempt; a defendant may be convicted of conspiracy and attempted target offense when both agreement and attempt are proved.
