Question: Mary ordered a custom wedding song from Daniel. The contract stated, “Final payment is due only if Mary is personally satisfied with the song.” Daniel delivered a song that Mary privately told her sister was “exactly what I wanted.” After finding a cheaper musician, Mary told Daniel she was dissatisfied and refused to pay. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Mary cannot rely on the satisfaction condition if her stated dissatisfaction was not honest and in good faith.
Answer B: Mary owes nothing because personal satisfaction clauses create absolute discretion.
Answer C: Daniel can recover only if a reasonable person would like the song.
Answer D: Daniel can recover only in restitution because satisfaction conditions make contract price unavailable.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Even subjective personal satisfaction conditions require honest good-faith dissatisfaction. If Mary actually was satisfied and invoked dissatisfaction only to avoid paying, the condition is treated as satisfied or excused and the payment duty matures.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because subjective discretion is limited by good faith. C: C is wrong because this personal taste setting uses subjective good faith, not objective reasonable satisfaction. D: D is wrong because if the condition is satisfied or excused, Daniel may claim the contract price rather than only restitution.
lb_rule_reference: Satisfaction condition / subjective good faith
