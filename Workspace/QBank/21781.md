Question: A city park contains permanent monuments donated by private groups and accepted by city council vote. The city rejects a proposed permanent monument because it does not fit the city's chosen historical theme. The sponsor argues that the park is a public forum and the city must accept all monuments on equal terms. What is the best analysis?
Answer A: The permanent monument selection is likely government speech, so ordinary public forum access rules do not control
Answer B: The city must accept every proposed monument because parks are traditional public forums
Answer C: The city may reject only religious monuments, because Establishment concerns override speech analysis
Answer D: The sponsor wins automatically because private groups donated earlier monuments
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Selecting permanent monuments in a city park is typically treated as the government's own expressive message.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A park may be a forum for private demonstrations, but permanent monument selection can be government speech. C: The facts do not make religion the controlling classification. D: Private donation does not prevent the accepted permanent monument from becoming part of government speech.
lb_rule_reference: The government may select the messages it expresses as government speech, but it cannot use the doctrine to mask regulation of private speech in a forum.
