Question: Congress enacts a statute authorizing the President to freeze transactions with foreign companies that supply surveillance equipment to hostile governments. The statute requires the President to make written findings and to report each freeze to Congress within 10 days. After making the required findings, the President orders banks to block payments to a listed foreign supplier. A domestic importer challenges the order, arguing that only Congress can regulate commerce with foreign entities. What is the strongest constitutional defense of the President's order?
Answer A: The order is valid because the President is acting under express statutory authorization, placing the action at maximum authority
Answer B: The order is invalid because Congress may never delegate foreign-commerce implementation to the President
Answer C: The order is valid only because foreign affairs are always immune from judicial review
Answer D: The order is invalid unless the President first obtains a judicial warrant for each blocked payment
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Congress authorized the transaction freeze and imposed procedures the President followed. Under the Youngstown framework, presidential authority is at its maximum when executive action rests on congressional authorization plus any independent Article II power.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Congress may authorize the executive to implement foreign-commerce sanctions within statutory limits. The question does not present a standardless delegation problem. C: Foreign-affairs context does not automatically bar judicial review. The better answer is that the President is acting with congressional authorization. D: This is a separation-of-powers and statutory-authority issue, not a criminal search warrant problem.
lb_rule_reference: Presidential authority is strongest when the President acts with express or implied congressional authorization.
