Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: A record store leased a space in the landlord's building. The term of the lease was two years, and rent was due at the beginning of each month. At the end of the two- year term, the tenant stayed in possession of the property and sent the landlord his monthly rent check. The landlord cashed the check and told the tenant that he was holding the tenant to another two- year term and increasing the rent by 5 percent. May the landlord hold the tenant to another two- year term with a 5 percent rent increase?
Answer A: A: No, because the landlord can only hold the tenant to a month- tomonth lease.
Answer B: B: No, because the landlord can only hold the tenant to a lease for the original rent.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the tenant stayed in possession of the property.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the rent increase was not unconscionable.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: If the tenant in a commercial lease lasting over one year holds over after the ending of the lease, the landlord can either evict the tenant or hold the tenant to a year- to- year lease at the original rent. Importantly, a landlord who chooses to keep a tenant in a situation like this cannot raise the rent unless he or she has informed the tenant of the rent increase before the end of the original lease. Thus, A, C, and D are incorrect.
