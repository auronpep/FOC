Question: Benjamin owned a landlocked parcel that had an easement by necessity over Lydia's land to reach the only public road. Years later, the county built a new public road directly along Benjamin's parcel, giving Benjamin legal and practical access without crossing Lydia's land. Benjamin still wants to use Lydia's road because it is shorter. May Benjamin continue using the easement by necessity?
Answer A: Yes, because an easement by necessity becomes permanent once it arises.
Answer B: No, because an easement by necessity generally ends when the necessity that justified it ceases.
Answer C: Yes, because convenience is enough to preserve an easement by necessity.
Answer D: No, but only because Lydia now owns Benjamin's land by operation of law.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Benjamin may not continue using the easement by necessity. An easement by necessity is implied to prevent landlocking and generally lasts only as long as the necessity exists. Once Benjamin's parcel has adequate access to a public road, convenience alone does not preserve the necessity easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because easements by necessity are tied to continuing necessity. C: C is wrong because convenience is not necessity. D: D is wrong because termination of the easement does not transfer ownership of Benjamin's parcel to Lydia.
lb_rule_reference: Easement by necessity / termination when necessity ceases
