MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A utility performed a rare high-pressure test on an old gas pipeline running beneath a residential block. The utility used qualified engineers and followed accepted protocols. A section ruptured during the test, causing an explosion that damaged a nearby home. May the homeowner recover under strict liability?
Answer A: Yes, because the explosion damage resulted from the abnormal risk of the high-pressure test in that setting
Answer B: No, because the utility used qualified engineers and accepted protocols
Answer C: No, because pipeline testing is part of ordinary utility maintenance
Answer D: Yes, but only if the homeowner proves the engineers selected the wrong test pressure
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts support an abnormally dangerous activity and the harm flowed from the pressure-explosion risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Due care does not defeat strict liability for harm within the abnormal risk. C: The specific rare high-pressure test near homes may be abnormal even if maintenance generally is common. D: That would be negligence-style proof; strict liability focuses on the abnormal activity and risk.
lb_rule_reference: A rare high-pressure test near homes can be abnormally dangerous when it creates a serious explosion risk that cannot be eliminated by reasonable care.
