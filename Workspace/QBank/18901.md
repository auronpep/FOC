MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Damages
Question: To sell a small printing business, the owner gave a buyer a spreadsheet falsely showing $90,000 in monthly revenue. The owner knew the true revenue was less than half that amount and said the spreadsheet was "from the accountant" so the buyer would close quickly. The buyer reasonably relied on it and overpaid. Is the buyer likely to prevail on a fraud claim against the owner?
Answer A: No, because business sellers are allowed to exaggerate value
Answer B: Yes, because the owner knowingly made a material false statement to induce reasonable reliance that caused loss
Answer C: No, because fraud does not apply to purely economic losses
Answer D: Yes, but only if the buyer proves the owner also interfered with a third-party contract
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts satisfy false material fact, scienter, intent, justifiable reliance, causation, and damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A specific false revenue spreadsheet is a factual misrepresentation, not mere puffery. C: Fraud is a recognized business tort for economic loss caused by intentional misrepresentation. D: Fraud turns on misrepresentation and reliance between these parties; no third-party contract is required.
lb_rule_reference: Fraud requires a false material fact, scienter, intent to induce reliance, justifiable reliance, causation, and damages.
