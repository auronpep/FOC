Question: In Gabriel's federal civil action against Lydia, Lydia argues during trial that Gabriel never requested a ruling on a discovery sanction. The judge checks the docket in the same case, which shows that Gabriel filed such a motion and that the court denied it three months earlier. May the court take judicial notice of the docket and order for that limited purpose?
Answer A: No, because a judge may never notice records from the same case.
Answer B: No, because judicial notice is available only for facts outside court files.
Answer C: Yes, to establish the existence of the motion and order, but not to prove the truth of any disputed facts recited in the motion.
Answer D: Yes, to establish conclusively that every factual statement in Gabriel's prior motion was true.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A court may take judicial notice of its own records to establish the existence and procedural content of filings and orders. That does not mean every factual assertion in a filing is accepted as true.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B are wrong because court records may be judicially noticed for appropriate limited purposes. D is wrong because judicial notice of a court record is not notice of the truth of disputed factual assertions inside the record. B: A and B are wrong because court records may be judicially noticed for appropriate limited purposes. D is wrong because judicial notice of a court record is not notice of the truth of disputed factual assertions inside the record. D: D is wrong because judicial notice of a court record is not notice of the truth of disputed factual assertions inside the record.
