internal_id: TR-174

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A quarry conducted lawful blasting, an activity treated as abnormally dangerous in the jurisdiction. Before any blast occurred that day, a visitor walked across the quarry's parking lot and slipped on a banana peel dropped by a lunch vendor. The visitor was not injured by vibration, debris, sound, dust, or explosives. The visitor sued the quarry for strict liability based on the blasting activity. Is the visitor likely to prevail on the strict liability claim against the quarry?
Answer A: Yes, because blasting is abnormally dangerous and the injury occurred at the blasting site
Answer B: No, because the injury did not result from the risk that makes blasting abnormally dangerous
Answer C: Yes, because strict liability replaces all negligence and premises rules at a blasting site
Answer D: No, because strict liability requires proof that the quarry failed to use reasonable care
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The visitor's injury came from an ordinary premises hazard, not explosion, vibration, debris, or other blasting risk. Strict liability is clipped at the risk boundary.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Strict liability is limited to harms that result from the abnormal risk. A parking-lot slip on a banana peel is unrelated to blasting. C: Strict liability does not replace other tort rules for unrelated risks. The visitor might have a negligence claim, but not strict liability for blasting. D: Strict liability does not require lack of care. The claim fails because the harm was outside the abnormal risk, not because care was proven.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity does not extend to injuries unrelated to the abnormal risk that justifies strict liability.
