Question: A state requires every interstate freight train to stop for a one-hour inspection at every small town, even though the same automated track sensors already detect the safety issue. The rule applies to all trains but causes severe nationwide rail delays and produces little additional safety benefit. What is the best DCC analysis?
Answer A: The rule is immune from review because it applies equally to in-state and out-of-state trains
Answer B: The rule may be invalid because the interstate burden is clearly excessive compared with the weak local benefit
Answer C: The rule is facially discriminatory because all trains cross state lines
Answer D: The rule is valid only if Congress expressly prohibits it
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Severe rail delays plus minimal added safety fit the Pike invalidity pattern.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Pike still balances incidental burdens against local benefits. C: Heavy interstate burden is not the same as discrimination against out-of-state commerce. D: The dormant limit can apply absent congressional action.
lb_rule_reference: Evenhanded laws can violate the Dormant Commerce Clause when their incidental burden on interstate commerce is clearly excessive in relation to weak local benefits.
