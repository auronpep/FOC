Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff in Dakota wanted to sue a Wyoming resident in Dakota on an unrelated loan dispute. The plaintiff falsely told the defendant that the defendant had won a free medical consultation in Dakota and that attendance was required to receive insurance benefits. The defendant traveled to Dakota only because of that lie. The plaintiff's process server served the defendant as soon as he arrived. What is the defendant's strongest argument against personal jurisdiction?
Answer A: The defendant can challenge jurisdiction because his presence in Dakota was procured by fraud for service
Answer B: The defendant cannot object because tag jurisdiction always applies whenever a body is inside the forum
Answer C: The defendant cannot object because personal jurisdiction is never waivable
Answer D: The defendant can object only if the loan agreement selected Wyoming courts
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Tag jurisdiction assumes valid service during voluntary presence. Fraudulently inducing the defendant into the forum solely to serve him is a recognized challenge to that basis.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Fraud or trickery used to procure presence can undermine service. C: PJ is waivable, but this defendant is timely objecting to defective service or jurisdiction. D: The challenge here is fraudulent procurement of service, not contractual consent.
lb_rule_reference: Personal service on a voluntarily present individual is powerful, but courts may set aside service when the plaintiff fraudulently induced the defendant to enter the forum solely to be served.
