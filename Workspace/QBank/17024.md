Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A roommate told one close friend, in confidence, that the plaintiff had once received treatment for anxiety. The fact was true, private, and embarrassing. The friend did not repeat it. Is the plaintiff likely to prevail on a public disclosure of private facts claim against the roommate?
Answer A: Yes, because the information was true and embarrassing
Answer B: No, because the disclosure to one friend lacks the required publicity
Answer C: Yes, because public disclosure claims do not require publication at all
Answer D: No, because anxiety treatment is always newsworthy
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A confidential statement to one person does not constitute the broad publicity required for public disclosure of private facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The tort requires publicity to the public or a large group; telling one person is not enough. C: This is a disclosure claim, and publicity is an element. D: The record does not make this private treatment newsworthy; the actual defect is the lack of broad publicity.
lb_rule_reference: Public disclosure of private facts requires publicity to the public or many people; disclosure to one person usually is not enough.
