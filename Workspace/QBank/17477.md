Question: A statute prohibits "threatening to commit a violent crime against another person." Defendant, whose own threat is unprotected, argues the law is facially overbroad because one can imagine rare protected statements that might be chilled. What is the best statement of the overbreadth standard?
Answer A: One hypothetical protected application always invalidates a speech statute
Answer B: A defendant can never raise overbreadth if his own speech is unprotected
Answer C: The statute is facially overbroad only if protected applications are substantial relative to its legitimate sweep
Answer D: Overbreadth applies only to commercial advertising
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The doctrine requires substantial overbreadth, not isolated edge cases.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Overbreadth must be substantial relative to the statute's legitimate scope. B: First Amendment overbreadth can allow third-party facial challenges in proper cases. D: Overbreadth is a general First Amendment facial-challenge doctrine.
lb_rule_reference: A speech statute is facially overbroad only if its unconstitutional applications are substantial in relation to its legitimate sweep.
