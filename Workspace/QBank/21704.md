Question: A landowner hires an independent contractor to demolish an old building with explosives near a public sidewalk. The contractor negligently fails to secure the area, and flying debris injures a pedestrian. The landowner argues that contractor status eliminates all liability. What is the best response?
Answer A: The landowner is automatically free from liability because the contractor controlled the blast
Answer B: The landowner may be liable because demolition with explosives involves inherent risk to others
Answer C: The landowner is liable only if the pedestrian was on the landowner's property
Answer D: The landowner is liable only if the contractor intended to injure the pedestrian
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Delegation does not necessarily shift away responsibility for inherently dangerous work.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Inherently dangerous work is an exception to the default independent-contractor rule. C: The exception can protect foreseeable third parties outside the property. D: The issue is negligence in inherently dangerous work, not intent.
lb_rule_reference: A hirer may be liable for an independent contractor's negligence in performing inherently dangerous work because the risk cannot be avoided merely by delegation.
