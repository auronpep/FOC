


BARMATRIX Q#: 22815
Outline_code: 94020000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: A nurse domiciled in State A sued a doctor domiciled in State B in federal district court in State A. The nurse claimed that the doctor negligently injured her during a fire drill. The doctor moved to dismiss the claim for lack of personal jurisdiction. The court denied the motion and set deadlines for discovery and trial dates. The doctor appealed the denial of his motion. Should the appellate court hear the doctor's appeal?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the appellate court's decision could end the nurse's action.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the doctor's appeal raises a constitutional question.
Answer C: C: No, because the appellate court lacks jurisdiction over the appeal.
Answer D: D: No, because the district court's decision on personal jurisdiction is a final decision.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: 28 U.S.C. §1291 gives courts of appeals jurisdiction over all final decisions of the district courts of the United States. The issue of personal jurisdiction is not a final order because it does not finally dispose of the lawsuit. Consequently, the appeals court would not have jurisdiction over the doctor's appeal of that issue. Therefore, A, B, and D are incorrect.
