Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Abigail sues a city after tripping in a sidewalk hole at noon. She offers a photograph taken by Daniel at 3 p.m. the same day showing a jagged hole at the location of the fall. The city objects that the photograph is irrelevant because the sidewalk could have changed during the three hours after Abigail fell. Assuming the photograph is authenticated, how should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the photograph because any time gap makes condition evidence irrelevant.
Answer B: Exclude the photograph unless Abigail proves the sidewalk did not change during the three-hour interval.
Answer C: Admit the photograph only if Daniel personally saw Abigail fall.
Answer D: Admit the photograph because it has some tendency to show the sidewalk condition, while the time gap affects weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The photograph tends to make the existence and nature of the sidewalk defect more probable. The three-hour interval makes the inference weaker, but it does not destroy threshold relevance. The city can argue changed conditions to the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a time gap is not an automatic relevance bar. B: B is wrong because the proponent need not prove no change occurred before admission. C: C is wrong because Daniel's personal observation of the fall is not required to authenticate a later photo of the condition.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Treating temporal weakness as inadmissibility rather than weight.
