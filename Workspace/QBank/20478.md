Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Negligence per se
Question: A traffic statute requires drivers to stay right of the centerline. A driver traveling carefully saw a child suddenly fall from a scooter into the driver's lane. To avoid hitting the child, the driver crossed the centerline and sideswiped an oncoming car. The driver had no safer option. The oncoming driver sues for negligence and argues negligence per se based on the centerline violation. Is the centerline violation conclusive negligence per se in the oncoming driver's negligence claim?
Answer A: Yes, because every unexcused traffic violation conclusively proves the entire negligence claim
Answer B: No, because the emergency may excuse the statutory violation and leave ordinary negligence for the factfinder
Answer C: No, because traffic statutes never apply in civil negligence cases
Answer D: Yes, because emergency excuses apply only to professional rescuers
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The driver crossed the line to avoid a sudden child-in-the-road emergency not of the driver's making. A valid excuse can prevent the violation from being conclusive negligence per se.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The facts support an excuse because violating the statute was necessary to avoid a sudden greater danger. Also, negligence per se would not prove every element. C: Traffic statutes can support negligence per se when class, risk, violation, and no-excuse requirements are met. The issue here is excuse. D: A sudden emergency can excuse statutory noncompliance for ordinary actors when compliance would be impossible or more dangerous.
lb_rule_reference: A statutory violation may be excused when compliance was impossible or more dangerous under a sudden emergency not caused by the defendant.
