Question: In a federal arson trial, the prosecutor offers a bottle allegedly containing accelerant. The bottle passed through three unknown persons, arrived at the lab unsealed, and the lab analyst cannot say whether it is the same bottle taken from the scene. The defendant objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Admit the bottle because chain-of-custody gaps always affect only weight.
Answer B: Admit the bottle if the prosecutor says in argument that it is the same bottle.
Answer C: Exclude the bottle because the proponent has not produced evidence sufficient to support a finding that it is the same item.
Answer D: Exclude the bottle only because liquid evidence is never admissible.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Although many chain gaps affect only weight, the proponent must still offer sufficient evidence that the item is what it claims. Here the missing links and unsealed condition defeat threshold authentication.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A overstates the weight-only principle. B is not evidence. D is categorical and false. B: A overstates the weight-only principle. B is not evidence. D is categorical and false. D: A overstates the weight-only principle. B is not evidence. D is categorical and false.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(a); FRE 403 / broken chain exclusion
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests when physical-object chain defects become an admissibility problem.
