Question: A federal officer removed after impeachment sues in federal court, arguing that the Senate used an unfair procedure for receiving evidence during the impeachment trial. The claim asks the court to decide whether the Senate properly tried the impeachment. What is the likely justiciability result?
Answer A: The case is likely nonjusticiable because impeachment trial procedures are textually committed to the Senate
Answer B: The court must decide the merits because every claim of unfairness is justiciable
Answer C: The case is moot because the officer was already removed
Answer D: The case is ripe only if the House also agrees to review
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Constitution commits the trial of impeachments to the Senate, making this kind of procedural challenge a political-question candidate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A fairness label does not overcome textual commitment to another branch. C: The stronger bar is political question based on textual commitment, not mere expiration of the proceeding. D: House agreement is not the ripeness test for this judicial challenge.
lb_rule_reference: Challenges to the Senate's constitutionally assigned impeachment trial procedures can present nonjusticiable political questions because the matter is textually committed to the Senate.
