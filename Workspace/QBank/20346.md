Outline: Privileges and Immunities Clause of Article IV, Section 2
Question: A state documents that nonresident commercial shellfish harvesters have repeatedly overharvested a fragile public shellfish bed and left before enforcement actions can be completed. The state temporarily limits nonresident commercial harvesting while applying ordinary conservation rules to residents and creates a prompt permit process for nonresidents who post an enforcement bond. What is the state's best Article IV defense?
Answer A: A state can never justify discrimination against nonresident citizens in a common calling
Answer B: The state can point to a substantial reason tied to nonresidents and a limited remedy closely fitted to that problem
Answer C: The state wins because conservation always permits any nonresident exclusion
Answer D: The state loses because Article IV applies only to corporations
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts identify a peculiar nonresident enforcement problem and a tailored condition.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A substantial reason closely fitted to a nonresident-caused problem can justify some discrimination. C: The state still must show a substantial reason and close fit. D: Natural nonresident citizens can invoke Article IV.
lb_rule_reference: A state may discriminate against nonresident citizens regarding a fundamental privilege only if nonresidents are a peculiar source of the problem and the discrimination closely fits a substantial reason.
