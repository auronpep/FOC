Question: Benjamin borrowed $12,000 from Paul, promising to repay $1,000 on the first day of each month for twelve months. The agreement contained no acceleration clause. Benjamin missed the first two payments but told Paul he would resume payments the next month. Paul immediately sued for the full $12,000 balance. What may Paul recover now?
Answer A: Only the matured missed installments, absent an acceleration clause or total repudiation.
Answer B: The full unpaid balance because any missed installment is automatically a total breach.
Answer C: Nothing, because suing for installments splits a cause of action.
Answer D: The full balance only if Benjamin is insolvent, even without repudiation or an acceleration clause.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A missed installment is ordinarily a breach as to the matured installment. Without an acceleration clause or facts showing total repudiation, Paul may sue for the installments currently due, not the entire future balance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a missed installment does not automatically accelerate future duties. C: C is wrong because matured installment obligations may generally be sued on as they become due. D: D is wrong because insolvency alone does not create acceleration without a contractual or legal basis.
lb_rule_reference: Partial breach / no acceleration absent clause
