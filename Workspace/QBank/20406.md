Question: Joseph orally agreed to sell Benjamin 1,000 sacks of grain for $6 per sack. Joseph later signed and sent Benjamin a note stating, “Confirming our grain deal: 600 sacks at $6 per sack.” Benjamin did not sign anything. Joseph delivered no grain. Benjamin sues Joseph for 1,000 sacks. Assuming the writing otherwise satisfies UCC §2-201, how much is enforceable against Joseph?
Answer A: None, because Benjamin did not sign the note.
Answer B: Only 600 sacks, because the goods contract is not enforceable beyond the quantity shown in the signed writing.
Answer C: All 1,000 sacks, because the oral agreement controls over a mistaken quantity in the writing.
Answer D: All 1,000 sacks, but only if Benjamin proves market price damages.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC §2-201, a writing for goods is not insufficient merely because it omits or incorrectly states some terms, but the contract is not enforceable beyond the quantity of goods shown in the writing. Joseph signed a writing showing 600 sacks, so enforcement against Joseph is capped at 600 sacks.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the writing is signed by Joseph, the party against whom enforcement is sought. C: C is wrong because UCC §2-201 caps enforcement at the written quantity. D: D is wrong because damages proof does not expand the enforceable quantity.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 / quantity cap
