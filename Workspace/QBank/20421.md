Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Kidnapping
Question: Gina is indicted for kidnapping. While awaiting trial, she tells another detainee details about the victim's location. The detainee had not been contacted by police, had received no instructions, and had no promise of benefit. The detainee later volunteers the information to prosecutors hoping for favorable treatment. Does admitting Gina's statement violate the Sixth Amendment?
Answer A: No, because the detainee was not acting as a government agent when Gina made the statement
Answer B: Yes, because any statement to another detainee after indictment is treated as police interrogation
Answer C: Yes, because the detainee hoped to receive a benefit from prosecutors
Answer D: No, because the Sixth Amendment does not apply after indictment until trial starts
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The right had attached, but Massiah requires government-agent involvement. A private detainee who later chooses to report information was not yet a government agent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The government must have used or directed the listener as an agent at the time of elicitation. C: A unilateral hope for leniency does not make the detainee a government agent before any government direction or agreement. D: The Sixth Amendment had attached. The missing element is agency, not attachment.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant's post-attachment statement to a private person is not suppressible under Massiah unless the listener was acting as a government agent who deliberately elicited the statement.
