Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A kitchen store regularly sold boxed appliances. It sold a sealed blender made by a manufacturer. The blender's blade assembly had been improperly fastened before shipment, causing the blade to detach during normal use and cut the buyer's hand. The store had no practical way to discover the defect without opening and disassembling the sealed unit. The buyer sues the kitchen store under strict products liability. Is the buyer likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim against the kitchen store?
Answer A: No, because the store did not manufacture the blender
Answer B: Yes, because the store was a commercial seller and the defective blender caused personal injury
Answer C: No, because the store used reasonable care by selling the blender in its sealed box
Answer D: Yes, because privity between the buyer and store is required and is present
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The store regularly sold appliances and placed the defective blender in the stream of commerce. A commercial seller may be strictly liable for personal injury caused by a defect existing when the product left the distribution chain.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Strict products liability can apply to commercial sellers in the distribution chain, including retailers. The plaintiff need not sue only the manufacturer. C: Reasonable care is not a defense to strict products liability. It may matter to negligence, but the products claim turns on commercial seller status, defect, causation, and injury. D: Privity is not required for strict products liability. The buyer's direct purchase does not hurt the claim, but it is not the controlling reason.
lb_rule_reference: A commercial seller in the distribution chain may be strictly liable for a defective product that causes personal injury, even if the seller did not manufacture or inspect the product.
