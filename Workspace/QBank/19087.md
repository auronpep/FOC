Question: A public high school suspends a student for five days for fighting. Before imposing the suspension, the principal tells the student the charge, summarizes the teacher reports, and gives the student a chance to explain. The student argues due process required a full trial-type hearing with counsel and cross-examination. What is the best due process analysis?
Answer A: The school provided the kind of minimal notice and opportunity to respond generally required for a short suspension
Answer B: No process is ever required for public school discipline
Answer C: A full judicial trial is always required before any suspension
Answer D: Due process is satisfied only if the student wins the discipline dispute
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Due process is flexible; a short suspension ordinarily does not require a full trial-type hearing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Students can have protected interests in public education and reputation. C: Due process is flexible and does not demand a full trial for ordinary short suspensions. D: Procedural due process concerns fair process, not a guaranteed favorable outcome.
lb_rule_reference: Before a short public school suspension, due process generally requires notice of the charges, an explanation of the evidence if denied, and an opportunity for the student to present a response.
