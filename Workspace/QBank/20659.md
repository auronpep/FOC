Outline: Personal knowledge
Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Naomi testifies that minutes after Aaron fell from a ladder, she examined the ladder and saw a cracked rung. The ladder was later discarded by a warehouse employee before trial. The manufacturer objects that Naomi cannot testify because the ladder is not available for the jury to inspect. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because the physical object must be produced before any witness may describe it.
Answer B: Admit the testimony because Naomi personally observed the ladder's condition; the missing ladder affects weight and other objections, not personal knowledge.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony unless Naomi took a photograph of the cracked rung.
Answer D: Admit the testimony only if the jury first finds that the ladder was actually defective.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Naomi may testify to the condition she personally observed. The absence of the ladder may support cross-examination or other arguments, but it does not by itself defeat her personal knowledge of what she saw shortly after the fall.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because real evidence need not be produced as a condition to eyewitness testimony about what the witness perceived. C: C is wrong because a photograph is not required. D: D is wrong because the jury weighs whether to believe Naomi after the court admits threshold-supported testimony.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 / object condition perceived / missing object
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests physical-object false positives and the difference between personal observation and production of the object itself.
