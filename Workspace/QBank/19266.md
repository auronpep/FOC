Outline: Motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law
Question: In a jury trial for breach of warranty, the buyer rests without offering any evidence that the product was defective when delivered. The seller moves for judgment as a matter of law on the defect element. No witness, document, or admission supports the buyer on that element. What standard should the court apply?
Answer A: Grant JMOL if no reasonable jury would have a legally sufficient evidentiary basis to find for the buyer on defect
Answer B: Grant JMOL only if the seller proves the buyer filed the complaint in bad faith
Answer C: Deny JMOL if the judge personally thinks the buyer is sympathetic
Answer D: Deny JMOL automatically because any civil case that reaches trial must go to the jury
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: After the buyer has been fully heard, the issue is whether the trial evidence can support a reasonable jury finding.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Bad faith is not the Rule 50 standard for evidentiary sufficiency. C: Sympathy does not create a legally sufficient evidentiary basis for a jury finding. D: Rule 50 exists to remove issues from the jury when the evidence is legally insufficient.
lb_rule_reference: The court may grant JMOL when a reasonable jury would not have a legally sufficient evidentiary basis to find for the nonmoving party on the issue.
