Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Self-defense
Question: During an argument at a bar, Victor shoves Marcus hard into a wall. Marcus is unhurt but furious. Marcus pulls out a pistol and shoots Victor in the chest, killing him. Marcus claims he feared Victor might hit him again even harder. No weapon was visible on Victor at any time during the altercation, and multiple witnesses confirm the shove was the only physical act. Which of the following best describes Marcus's self-defense claim?
Answer A: Valid, because Marcus had a reasonable fear of continued physical harm
Answer B: Valid, because any physical contact justifies self-defense
Answer C: Invalid, because deadly force was disproportionate to a non-deadly shove
Answer D: Invalid, because Marcus was the initial aggressor
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Common-law self-defense requires that the force used be proportionate to the threat faced. Deadly force is only permissible when the defendant reasonably believes he faces an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm. A shove, absent a weapon or other aggravating facts, constitutes non-deadly force. Responding to non-deadly force with deadly force (a gunshot) is disproportionate and forfeits the self-defense justification. Marcus is guilty of an unjustified killing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Proportionality is essential. A shove is non-deadly force. Deadly force (a firearm) may only be used in response to an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm. Victor's shove, however painful or offensive, did not create that level of threat. B: Self-defense does not permit any response to any physical contact. The rule specifically limits the level of force to what is reasonably necessary and proportionate to the threat faced. Physical contact permits only proportionate non-deadly force in return. D: On these facts, Victor shoved first, making Victor the aggressor — not Marcus. The claim fails not because of the initial-aggressor rule but because of the proportionality requirement. Misidentifying who started the fight leads to the right outcome (no self-defense) for the wrong reason.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant may use only proportionate force in self-defense; deadly force is justified only when facing an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm, not in response to a non-deadly attack
