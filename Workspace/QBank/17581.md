Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: Nora, a citizen of Oregon, sues Owen, a citizen of Idaho, in federal court for $90,000 for breach of a delivery contract. Owen counterclaims for $20,000, alleging that Nora damaged his truck during the same delivery. Does the court have subject matter jurisdiction over Owen's counterclaim?
Answer A: Yes, because it arises from the same transaction or occurrence and falls within supplemental jurisdiction
Answer B: No, because the counterclaim does not independently exceed $75,000
Answer C: No, because defendants cannot use supplemental jurisdiction
Answer D: Yes, because any counterclaim in any federal case is automatically within SMJ
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The counterclaim shares the same delivery facts as the original claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A related supplemental counterclaim does not need independent amount. C: Defendant claims are often the clearest supplemental claims. D: A permissive unrelated counterclaim may need its own jurisdictional basis.
lb_rule_reference: A counterclaim arising from the same transaction or occurrence as the plaintiff's claim generally satisfies Section 1367(a).
