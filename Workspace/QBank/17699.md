MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Affirmative duties to act
Question: A cyclist carelessly knocked a pedestrian into a shallow drainage ditch. The pedestrian was conscious but unable to climb out because of an ankle injury. The cyclist saw the danger of rising water, said "not my problem," and rode away without calling for help. Did the cyclist have a duty to take reasonable aid measures?
Answer A: No, because there is never a duty to rescue another person
Answer B: No, because the cyclist did not intend to hurt the pedestrian
Answer C: Yes, because the cyclist's conduct created the peril
Answer D: Yes, but only if the pedestrian paid the cyclist for help
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A person who creates a dangerous situation must use reasonable care to prevent further harm, such as calling for help.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The cyclist created the peril by knocking the pedestrian into the ditch, creating a duty to take reasonable steps. B: Negligence duty does not require intent. Creating peril can trigger a duty to aid. D: A duty can arise from creating the risk even without a contract.
lb_rule_reference: A person who creates a risk of harm may have a duty to take reasonable steps to aid or protect those endangered.
