Outline_code: 44040202
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Substantive due process
Question: State A conducted a study showing that a high percentage of State A high school students went to college out- of- state and never returned to State A. The study also showed that the lack of young people in the state was driving down property values and causing businesses to relocate to other states. To combat the problem, the State A legislature passed a law giving a 95 percent tuition discount to state residents who chose to attend college in State A. To get the tuition discount, a student had to be a resident of State A for over one year before applying to a State A college. A student moved to State A and two months later applied to State A College. The student was accepted, but she was told that she would be required to pay full tuition until she had lived in State A for more than one year. The student sued the state, arguing that the law was unconstitutional because of the one- year residency requirement. Should the court find the new law constitutional?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the law is necessary to protect the state's compelling governmental interest in its economy.
Answer B: B: Yes, because education is not a fundamental right.
Answer C: C: No, because the 95 percent tuition discount acts as a penalty and not a benefit.
Answer D: D: No, because the 95 percent tuition discount unfairly burdens the student's right to travel.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Because education is not a fundamental right, one- year residency requirements for instate tuition have been upheld as constitutional. A is incorrect because education is not a fundamental right and it is thus not necessary for the state to prove that the one- year residency requirement is necessary to protect a compelling governmental interest. C and D are incorrect because the size of the tuition discount does not change the analysis.
