Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Naomi and Jacob orally agree that Jacob will buy 1,000 linen bags from Naomi for $3,000. Later, Jacob signs a note stating, “I agree to buy linen bags from Naomi for $3,000.” The note does not state any quantity. Jacob refuses to buy the bags, and Naomi sues to enforce the 1,000-bag agreement. Is the signed note sufficient under the UCC Statute of Frauds?
Answer A: Yes, because the total price appears in the writing.
Answer B: No, because a UCC goods memorandum must show a quantity, and the contract is not enforceable beyond the quantity shown.
Answer C: Yes, because the quantity may always be supplied by oral testimony.
Answer D: No, because the writing must contain every term agreed by the parties.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A UCC goods memorandum is not insufficient merely because it omits or incorrectly states many terms, but enforceability is limited to the quantity of goods shown in the writing. A writing that shows no quantity is insufficient to enforce the 1,000-bag agreement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because price is not a substitute for quantity. C: C is wrong because oral testimony cannot supply the missing quantity for SOF enforcement. D: D is wrong because the UCC does not require every term; quantity is the critical cap.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(1)
