Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Taxing and spending power
Question: Congress imposes a modest annual excise tax on a product and directs the revenue to the Treasury. The legislative record shows Congress also hoped the tax would discourage overuse of the product. A challenger argues that any behavioral motive destroys taxing power. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The tax is invalid because Congress may never hope to influence behavior through taxation
Answer B: The tax may be valid if it functions as a tax, even though Congress also had a regulatory motive
Answer C: The tax is valid only if Congress has commerce power to ban the product outright
Answer D: The tax is invalid unless every taxpayer approves it by referendum
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The exaction is modest, raises revenue, and is collected as a tax.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Taxes often influence conduct; regulatory motive alone is not fatal. C: Taxing power is an independent enumerated power when its requirements are met. D: Constitutional taxing power does not require taxpayer referendum approval.
lb_rule_reference: A federal tax is not invalid merely because Congress also intended to influence behavior, so long as it functions as a tax rather than a punitive regulatory penalty.
