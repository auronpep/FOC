MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Burglary
Question: At 2:00 in the afternoon on a sunny day, Rick breaks a window and enters the ground-floor office of a law firm to steal cash from the petty cash box. The office is in a commercial building that no one lives in; the building is used exclusively for legal work during business hours. Rick takes $500 and leaves. Is Rick guilty of common-law burglary?
Answer A: Yes, because Rick broke and entered with intent to commit a felony inside
Answer B: Yes, because most modern jurisdictions have eliminated the nighttime and dwelling requirements
Answer C: No, because common-law burglary requires entry into a dwelling at nighttime, and both elements are absent here
Answer D: No, because Rick did not intend to commit a felony — stealing $500 is only a misdemeanor
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Common-law burglary requires: (1) a breaking, (2) an entering, (3) of a dwelling house of another, (4) at nighttime, (5) with intent to commit a felony therein. Rick's entry occurred at 2:00 p.m. — clearly daytime, not nighttime. The structure was a commercial office building, not a dwelling used for sleeping. Both the nighttime requirement and the dwelling requirement are independently absent. Rick may be guilty of criminal trespass, larceny, or malicious mischief, but not common-law burglary.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common-law burglary has two additional requirements that are not met: (1) the entry must occur at nighttime, and (2) the structure must be a dwelling house. Rick entered at 2:00 p.m. in broad daylight — not nighttime. The structure is a commercial office — not a dwelling. Both missing elements independently defeat the burglary charge. B: The question asks about common-law burglary, which is the default rule tested on the MBE when no statute is specified. Common-law burglary requires nighttime and a dwelling. When an MBE question tests "common-law burglary," apply the traditional five elements, not modern statutory expansions. D: Whether larceny of $500 is a felony or misdemeanor depends on jurisdiction, and the question does not provide a threshold. More importantly, this is not the reason Rick is not guilty of burglary. The two dispositive missing elements are the nighttime requirement and the dwelling requirement. Even if the intent-to-commit-a-felony element were met, burglary would still fail on nighttime and dwelling grounds. Choosing this answer reflects correct knowledge of one element but misses the central common-law issues.
lb_rule_reference: At common law, burglary requires: breaking and entering a dwelling house of another at nighttime with intent to commit a felony therein. "Nighttime" means when it is dark enough that a person's face cannot be discerned by natural light. The structure must be a dwelling — a place used regularly for sleeping.
