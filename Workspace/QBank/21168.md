Outline: Larceny
Question: Nora sees a painting hanging in her cousin's apartment and honestly believes it is the same painting her grandmother left to Nora in a will. Without asking, Nora takes the painting to her own home, intending to keep it as her inheritance. Nora's belief is mistaken; the painting belongs to the cousin. Is Nora guilty of larceny?
Answer A: Yes, because Nora did not ask permission before taking the painting
Answer B: Yes, because her belief had to be legally correct to matter
Answer C: No, because her sincere belief in a right to the specific painting negates larcenous intent
Answer D: No, because paintings are real property
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Nora intended to take what she believed was hers, not to steal another's property.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A sincere claim of right can negate larcenous intent even when the taking is civilly wrongful. B: The common-law claim-of-right doctrine focuses on sincere belief, even if mistaken. D: A movable painting is personal property; the defense is claim of right.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant who honestly believes he has a right to the specific property taken generally lacks the larcenous intent required for larceny.
