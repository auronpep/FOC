


BARMATRIX Q#: 22871
Outline_code: 97100300
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: A woman was injured when a man lost control of his car and drove it through her bedroom window. She sued the man in federal district court for $80,000 in damages related to her broken leg. After a jury trial, the court awarded the woman $30,000. The woman sued the man again, this time claiming $100,000 in property damage for the damage done to her home. The man moved to dismiss, arguing that the woman's new claim was barred. How should the court rule?
Answer A: A: Deny the motion, because the woman is now claiming damages for property.
Answer B: B: Deny the motion, because the woman won her first claim.
Answer C: C: Grant the motion, because the woman was only awarded $30,000 in the first claim.
Answer D: D: Grant the motion, because the woman's claim for property damage arose from the same accident.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Claim preclusion, also known as res judicata, bars a second claim if it is based on the same common core of operative facts as a prior claim. Frier v. City of Vandalia, 770 F.2d 699 (7th Cir. 1985). Here, since the woman's second claim is based on the same accident, the claim is barred. A is incorrect because the mere fact that the woman is claiming different damages would not change the nature of the claim. B and C are incorrect because the fact that the woman won $30,000 in her first claim would not change the analysis.
