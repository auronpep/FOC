Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Falling below the standard of care
Question: A supplier has a one-year exclusive contract to provide flour to a bakery. A competitor learns of the contract, tells the bakery that it will secretly reimburse any damages for breach, and urges the bakery to cancel immediately. The bakery cancels and buys from the competitor. Is the supplier likely to have an intentional-interference claim against the competitor?
Answer A: Yes, because the competitor knew of the contract and improperly induced its breach
Answer B: Yes, because competition is always tortious when it causes lost profits
Answer C: No, because only a party to the contract can be liable for interference
Answer D: No, because an existing contract can never support a tort remedy
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: This is more than ordinary competition; the competitor intentionally procured a breach of an existing contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Ordinary competition is not automatically improper interference. C: Interference liability is aimed at outside actors. D: Improper interference with an existing contract is a recognized business tort.
lb_rule_reference: Liability for interference with an existing contract generally requires knowledge of the contract and intentional, improper inducement causing breach.
