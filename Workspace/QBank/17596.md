Question: Protesters stand on a public sidewalk outside city hall holding signs criticizing a new ordinance. The city orders them to leave because "city property is not a place for political debate." The protesters do not block entrances or traffic. What is the strongest First Amendment response?
Answer A: The sidewalk is likely a traditional public forum, so the city cannot exclude political debate merely because it dislikes debate on city property
Answer B: The city may exclude all speech because it owns the sidewalk
Answer C: The protesters lose because political speech receives less protection than commercial speech
Answer D: The protesters lose unless they prove the city previously designated the sidewalk as a forum
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Public sidewalks are classic public forums for peaceful speech and protest.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Government ownership does not eliminate traditional public forum rights. C: Political speech is at the core of First Amendment protection. D: Traditional public forums do not need a special designation.
lb_rule_reference: Streets, sidewalks, and parks traditionally open to public expression are traditional public forums where content-based restrictions trigger strict scrutiny and content-neutral TPM rules must satisfy intermediate scrutiny.
