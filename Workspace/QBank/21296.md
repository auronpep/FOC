Subtopic: Remedies
Outline: Duty to mitigate
Question: Naomi contracted with Jacob to provide staging for a conference for $5,000. Five days before the conference, Jacob repudiated. Naomi could have hired an equivalent staging company for $6,400 in time for the conference, but she declined because she thought Jacob should bear the consequences of canceling. Naomi canceled the conference and lost $18,000 in net ticket profit. Assume the ticket profit was otherwise foreseeable and certain. What is Naomi's maximum recovery from Jacob?
Answer A: $18,000, because the lost ticket profit was foreseeable and certain.
Answer B: $1,400, the reasonable substitute-performance premium.
Answer C: Nothing, because Naomi failed to mitigate.
Answer D: $19,400, the lost ticket profit plus the substitute-performance premium.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under common law. The breach posture is repudiation of a services contract, and the loss category includes both a direct substitute-performance differential and a claimed consequential profit. The mitigation gate controls: Naomi could have avoided the $18,000 lost profit by obtaining equivalent staging for $1,400 more than the contract price. The legally authorized formula is $6,400 reasonable substitute cost − $5,000 contract price = $1,400. The result scope is limited recovery; mitigation excludes avoidable downstream loss but does not forfeit the whole claim. The answer does not decide any tort or punitive issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it uses foreseeability-only overreach and ignores the mitigation gate, triggering WAL-REM-003. C: C is wrong because failure to mitigate does not eliminate all damages; it excludes only avoidable loss, a WAL-REM-009 error. D: D is wrong because it stacks the avoidable profit with the substitute premium and overcompensates Naomi, triggering WAL-REM-008.
lb_rule_reference: Common law avoidable consequences — reasonable substitute performance limits avoidable downstream loss
