Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Jacob agreed to provide sound equipment for Leah's first public singing event. Jacob breached, and Leah cancelled the event. Leah had sold no tickets, had no prior paid performances, had no comparable sales data, and testified only that she “expected the event to become famous” and hoped to make $80,000 in profits. What lost-profit amount may Leah recover?
Answer A: $80,000, because Jacob's breach caused the cancellation.
Answer B: No lost profits, because Leah has not proven the amount with reasonable certainty.
Answer C: $80,000 if Jacob knew Leah hoped to make that amount.
Answer D: The reasonable rental value of Jacob's equipment, because that is always the measure of lost profits.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Lost profits must be proven with reasonable certainty. Leah's hope and unsupported estimate do not supply a reliable basis for net profits. Causation alone is not enough.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because causation does not replace proof of amount. C: C is wrong because foreseeability alone does not establish reasonable certainty. D: D is wrong because equipment rental value is not the lost-profit formula.
lb_rule_reference: Expectation damages / speculative profits
