Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: Esther testifies that her engraved watch was stolen. Police later recover a watch from Levi. Esther testifies that she recognizes the watch because it bears her initials on the back and the same serial number listed on the warranty card she kept at home. Levi objects that Esther is not a watch expert. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because Esther's knowledge of the initials and serial number can authenticate the watch.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because only a watch expert may identify a watch by serial number.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because the warranty card, rather than Esther's testimony, is the best evidence of the watch.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Levi admits possessing the watch.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness with knowledge may identify an item, and distinctive characteristics such as initials and serial number support authentication. Expert testimony is not required for this ownership identification.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a lay owner with knowledge can authenticate the item. C: C is wrong because Esther is identifying the physical watch, not proving the contents of the warranty card as the operative fact. D: D is wrong because Levi's admission is unnecessary.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(1), 901(b)(4)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student demands expert proof for ordinary distinctive-object identification.
