Outline: Nonresponsive answers
Question: In a federal civil assault trial, Ruth's lawyer asks Naomi, “Please tell the jury everything you saw from the time David entered the room until the police arrived.” Naomi gives a two-minute narrative describing the entire sequence. David moves to strike the narrative solely because Naomi did not answer in short sentences. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant the motion because all narrative answers are automatically inadmissible.
Answer B: Grant the motion because a witness may answer only yes-or-no questions in federal court.
Answer C: Deny the motion because the broad question invited a narrative answer, though the court may control future questioning to avoid waste or confusion.
Answer D: Deny the motion only if Naomi is an expert witness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The answer was responsive to the broad narrative question. The court has discretion to control mode and order, but a narrative answer is not automatically stricken as nonresponsive when the question invited it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because narrative testimony is not automatically inadmissible. B invents a yes-or-no-only rule. B: A is wrong because narrative testimony is not automatically inadmissible. B invents a yes-or-no-only rule. D is wrong because responsiveness does not depend on expert status. D: D is wrong because responsiveness does not depend on expert status.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / narrative answer invited by narrative question
