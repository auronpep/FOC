MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Murder
Question: During a noisy card game, Soren accused Malik of palming a card. Malik immediately grabbed a heavy glass bottle from the table and smashed it into Soren's temple, intending to kill him. Soren died. No facts show prior planning, waiting, or reflection. What homicide offense is best supported?
Answer A: First-degree murder, because Malik intended to kill Soren
Answer B: Second-degree murder, because Malik intentionally killed without proven premeditation
Answer C: Voluntary manslaughter, because being accused of cheating is provocation
Answer D: Involuntary manslaughter, because the bottle was an improvised weapon
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Malik intended to kill, so murder is supported. But there are no planning, waiting, or cool-reflection facts. The most serious supported offense is murder short of first degree.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intent to kill establishes malice but does not automatically establish premeditation and deliberation. The facts show immediate action. C: Words and accusations alone are generally insufficient provocation at common law. D: A spontaneously selected weapon can still be used with intent to kill. The lack of advance weapon procurement affects degree, not malice.
lb_rule_reference: A sudden intentional killing can be murder without being first-degree murder when premeditation and deliberation are not proven.
