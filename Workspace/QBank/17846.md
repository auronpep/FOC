Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Jeremiah is charged with threatening a witness. The prosecutor offers a public post from an account named “JusticeNow” stating, “The witness should stay quiet.” The prosecutor offers no evidence tying the account to Jeremiah, no distinctive information in the post, no admission by Jeremiah, and no device or subscriber evidence. Jeremiah objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the post because threats against witnesses are always relevant.
Answer B: Admit the post because anonymous posts are admissible if they mention the case.
Answer C: Exclude the post only if Jeremiah proves the account belonged to someone else.
Answer D: Exclude the post because the prosecutor has not produced evidence sufficient to support a finding that Jeremiah authored, adopted, or controlled it.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The post may be relevant only if it is sufficiently connected to Jeremiah. Without evidence of authorship, adoption, control, or distinctive content tying the post to him, the foundation is missing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevance does not bypass authentication. B: B is wrong because case-related content alone does not connect the post to Jeremiah. C: C is wrong because Jeremiah does not bear the burden to disprove authorship before the prosecutor lays a foundation.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901
lb_student_diagnostic: Relevance-overbreadth and burden-reversal trap in anonymous digital evidence.
