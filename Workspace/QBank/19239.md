Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: A contractor sues for breach of two separate contract duties: payment for completed work and reimbursement for equipment rentals. The owner admits in discovery that the completed work was accepted and unpaid, but sharply disputes the rental reimbursement term. The contractor moves for summary judgment only on the payment duty. May the court grant summary judgment limited to the payment duty?
Answer A: No, because summary judgment must resolve the entire civil action or nothing at all
Answer B: Yes, if no genuine dispute of material fact exists on the payment duty, even though another issue remains for trial
Answer C: No, because any disputed fact anywhere in the case defeats every summary judgment motion
Answer D: Yes, but only if both parties consent to partial summary judgment
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The undisputed payment issue can be resolved while the rental dispute proceeds.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 56 permits a narrower ruling on part of a claim or defense. C: Only disputes over facts material to the requested ruling defeat that part of the motion. D: Consent is not required if the Rule 56 standard is met and the parties have proper notice and opportunity to respond.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 56 allows summary judgment on a claim, defense, or part of a claim or defense when no genuine dispute of material fact exists as to that part.
