Outline: Conviction of crime
Question: In a federal civil trial, Lydia testifies for the plaintiff. The defendant seeks to impeach Lydia with a felony conviction from six years ago. Lydia shows that the conviction was later annulled based on a finding that she was innocent. The defendant argues that the conviction is still relevant to credibility. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the conviction because it is less than ten years old.
Answer B: Admit the conviction because annulment affects punishment but not impeachment.
Answer C: Exclude the conviction because an annulment based on a finding of innocence makes the conviction inadmissible for Rule 609 impeachment.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction only if the defendant failed to give written notice.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 609(c) makes a conviction inadmissible for impeachment if it has been annulled, pardoned, or otherwise set aside based on a finding of innocence. The conviction is excluded even though it is less than ten years old.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 609(c) can exclude a conviction that would otherwise be recent. B: B is wrong because innocence-based annulment directly affects admissibility. D: D is wrong because written notice is part of the old-conviction rule, not the reason for exclusion here.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(c) / pardon or annulment based on rehabilitation or innocence
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the pardon/annulment gate and the distinction between time limits and innocence-based nullification.
