Question: In a federal fraud trial, Naomi testifies for Jacob and denies that Jacob promised her any benefit for testifying. Jacob's opponent then offers a signed note from Jacob promising Naomi a share of the recovery if she testifies. Jacob objects that the note is extrinsic evidence on a collateral matter. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the note because extrinsic evidence is always barred on credibility issues.
Answer B: Exclude the note because Naomi denied the promise on cross-examination.
Answer C: Admit the note only if Naomi first testifies that she is an untruthful person.
Answer D: Admit the note because it is extrinsic evidence offered to show Naomi's bias or interest, not merely a collateral attack on character for truthfulness.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Bias is a proper impeachment purpose, and extrinsic evidence may be used to prove bias when relevant and otherwise admissible. The note directly tends to show Naomi's financial interest in Jacob's success. The collateral-bar and Rule 608(b) truthfulness-specific-act limits do not bar the note when it is offered for bias.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because extrinsic evidence is not categorically barred for credibility; bias may be proved extrinsically. B: B is wrong because denial does not insulate bias evidence. C: C is wrong because bias does not depend on truthfulness-character testimony.
lb_rule_reference: Bias impeachment / extrinsic proof permitted
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests collateral-bar overread and the distinction between bias proof and truthfulness-specific-act proof.
