MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A laboratory conducted a rare process using a highly toxic gas beside an apartment building. The laboratory used the best available containment system. A small pressure surge caused gas to escape, and a tenant inhaled it and became ill. On a strict-liability claim, what is the laboratory's best argument?
Answer A: It cannot be strictly liable because it used the best available containment system
Answer B: It is strictly liable because the toxic gas escaped and caused the kind of serious harm that made the activity abnormal
Answer C: It is strictly liable only if the tenant proves the pressure surge was foreseeable
Answer D: It cannot be strictly liable because laboratories are common in cities
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The illness resulted from the precise risk of serious toxic exposure that made the activity abnormally dangerous.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Due care does not defeat strict liability for harm within the abnormal risk of the activity. C: The key is the abnormal activity and harm within its risk, not negligence-style proof of a foreseeable mechanical failure. D: The issue is the specific rare toxic-gas process beside residences, not laboratories as a general category.
lb_rule_reference: Handling highly toxic substances in a way that creates an unavoidable serious risk to nearby people can be abnormally dangerous, and strict liability covers harm from an escape of that substance.
