Question: Sarah hired Daniel to handle “complete permit-management services” for a restaurant renovation. The signed services agreement used that phrase but did not define it. The agreement also stated that the services were “as described in Schedule A.” Daniel later obtained the initial building permit but refused to schedule final inspections, saying inspections were outside the phrase. Which additional fact would most strengthen Sarah's Content/Meaning argument that Daniel's services included scheduling final inspections?
Answer A: Before signing, Daniel told Sarah, “We handle everything,” but the signed agreement did not repeat that statement.
Answer B: Schedule A was attached to the signed agreement, was identified by name in the agreement, and listed “scheduling final inspections” as part of complete permit management.
Answer C: Sarah lost two weeks of restaurant revenue because the final inspections were not scheduled.
Answer D: The city generally requires a final inspection before a restaurant may open.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that material incorporated by reference into a final writing is treated as part of the agreement itself, not merely as outside parol evidence. The trigger facts are the undefined service phrase and the agreement's reference to Schedule A. The decisive locks are term status and purpose: Sarah needs a source that defines the written scope of “complete permit-management services.” B supplies the strongest meaning source because the attached schedule is incorporated into the writing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is tempting because it supports Sarah's expectation, but it is a broad prior negotiation statement and is weaker than an incorporated definition. C is wrong because damages do not define the contract term. D is wrong because a regulatory requirement may explain why inspections matter, but it does not prove Daniel promised to schedule them.
