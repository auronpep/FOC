Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Hannah sues Simon after a wagon collision, claiming the collision caused numbness in her hand. Simon offers Hannah's clinic record from two weeks before the collision noting that Hannah reported occasional hand numbness. Hannah objects that the record does not prove the collision caused no injury because her numbness became worse afterward. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the record because it does not prove the collision caused no injury.
Answer B: Admit the record because it has some tendency to show a possible preexisting condition; the degree of similarity and worsening affect weight.
Answer C: Exclude the record unless Simon offers expert testimony that the earlier numbness was identical.
Answer D: Admit the record only to show Hannah's character for exaggeration.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The prior report of similar symptoms has some tendency to show a preexisting condition or alternate cause. It need not completely disprove injury from the collision. Differences in severity are for the jury to weigh.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because evidence can be relevant even if it does not conclusively prove the ultimate defense. C: C is wrong because expert testimony may strengthen the evidence but is not always required for threshold relevance. D: D is wrong because the record is offered to show condition/causation, not character.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401; FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Student excludes alternate-cause evidence because it is incomplete.
