MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Solicitation
Question: Sasha asks Tyler to steal exam files from a professor's office that night. Tyler agrees to think about it but does not commit. An hour later, Sasha panics, calls Tyler, and says, "Do not do it. I was wrong to ask." Tyler never goes near the office. Sasha is charged with solicitation to commit burglary. Under common law, what is the best result?
Answer A: Sasha is not guilty because she withdrew before Tyler acted
Answer B: Sasha is guilty because the solicitation was complete when the request was communicated
Answer C: Sasha is not guilty because Tyler never expressly agreed
Answer D: Sasha is guilty only if Tyler actually entered the professor's office
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The offense is the intentional request that another commit a crime. Sasha's later call may prevent burglary, but it does not erase the completed solicitation under common law.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Solicitation was complete when Sasha communicated the request with criminal intent. Later remorse does not undo the completed offense at common law. C: Agreement is not an element of solicitation. The request alone, if made with the required intent, is enough. D: Solicitation is an inchoate offense and does not require completion of the target crime.
lb_rule_reference: At common law, solicitation is complete upon communication, and later withdrawal or remorse generally does not erase the completed solicitation.
