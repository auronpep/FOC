Outline: Embezzlement
Question: A nonprofit treasurer is authorized to receive donation checks, deposit them into the nonprofit's account, and maintain the checkbook. Instead, after receiving a $4,000 donation check payable to the nonprofit, she deposits it into the nonprofit account and writes herself a check for personal rent. What is the best theft offense?
Answer A: Larceny, because any taking of another's money is larceny
Answer B: False pretenses, because the donor was misled
Answer C: Embezzlement, because she lawfully possessed the funds as treasurer and fraudulently converted them
Answer D: No theft offense, because she had authority to sign nonprofit checks
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The treasurer's role gave lawful control over the nonprofit funds. Writing herself a rent check was fraudulent conversion of property she was entrusted to manage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: She began with lawful possession or control over the funds as treasurer. Conversion after lawful possession points to embezzlement. B: The facts do not show the treasurer obtained title from the donor by a false statement. The wrong is later conversion of entrusted funds. D: Authority to sign checks for nonprofit purposes did not authorize personal conversion.
lb_rule_reference: An agent who lawfully receives possession of another's money for a limited purpose and later converts it can commit embezzlement.
