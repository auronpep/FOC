


BARMATRIX Q#: 22172
Outline_code: 31010400
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Witnesses
Question: A woman was bitten by her neighbor's dog. The woman sued her neighbor for negligence, claiming that she was injured when the neighbor's broken gate opened and allowed her dog to get out. The neighbor claimed that she didn't know the gate was broken. In response, the woman wanted to call a witness to testify that he had told the neighbor the day before that her gate looked broken and was probably prone to opening by itself. Is the witness's testimony admissible?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the testimony is admissible as a present sense impression.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the testimony shows the neighbor had notice that something was wrong with her gate.
Answer C: C: No, because the testimony is inadmissible hearsay.
Answer D: D: No, because the witness has not been shown to be an expert.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Whether the neighbor had notice that something was wrong with the gate would be relevant in proving the woman's negligence claim. Here, since the testimony was being used to show that the woman had notice that the gate might be broken, it was not hearsay. A is incorrect because a “present sense impression” refers to circumstances where the statement is describing or explaining an event or condition made while the declarant was perceiving the event or condition, or immediately thereafter. The witness's testimony here would not be classified as a present sense impression. C is incorrect because the testimony is not being used to prove the truth of the matter asserted. D is incorrect because the witness did not need to be an expert to show that the neighbor had notice.
