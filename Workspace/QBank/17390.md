Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Roles of judge and jury
Question: In a federal robbery trial, the prosecutor asks to approach the bench before questioning Sarah about a disputed identification procedure. The prosecutor explains that the admissibility issue may require discussion of facts the jury should not hear unless the evidence is admitted. The defendant objects that all evidentiary discussions must occur in open hearing before the jury. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Require the admissibility discussion to occur before the jury because jurors decide all factual disputes.
Answer B: Allow the court to manage the discussion outside the jury's hearing if doing so reasonably avoids exposing the jury to potentially inadmissible matter.
Answer C: Refuse to hear the issue until after Sarah answers the question.
Answer D: Exclude Sarah's testimony entirely because the prosecutor anticipated an objection.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may exercise reasonable control over the mode and order of presenting evidence. Managing an evidentiary issue outside the jury's hearing can be proper when it avoids exposing the jury to potentially inadmissible matter.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the judge controls admissibility discussions and need not expose the jury to inadmissible matters. C: C is wrong because waiting until after the answer may defeat the purpose of the control measure. D: D is wrong because anticipating an objection does not require exclusion of the testimony.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / avoiding jury exposure to disputed admissibility matter
