Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Investigatory power
Question: A House committee subpoenas the President's personal bank records from a private accounting firm. The committee states that it is considering financial-disclosure legislation for federal officials. The subpoena is broad and seeks ten years of records. The President objects that the request is designed mainly to expose private information and burdens the presidency. What is the best description of the court's task?
Answer A: Enforce the subpoena automatically because Congress has unlimited investigative power over presidential records
Answer B: Quash the subpoena automatically because presidential records can never be obtained by Congress
Answer C: Evaluate whether the subpoena serves a valid legislative purpose and is appropriately tailored in light of separation-of-powers concerns
Answer D: Enforce the subpoena only if the committee is conducting an impeachment inquiry
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A court should balance Congress's legitimate legislative need against burdens on the presidency, considering whether the subpoena is no broader than reasonably necessary and whether Congress has an adequate legislative purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Congressional power is broad but not unlimited, especially when directed at the President. Courts examine legislative purpose, need, breadth, and separation-of-powers burdens. B: Presidential status does not create absolute immunity from congressional subpoenas, especially for private records held by third parties. D: Congress can investigate for legislative purposes outside impeachment, subject to separation-of-powers limits.
lb_rule_reference: Congressional subpoenas for presidential information require a valid legislative purpose and careful separation-of-powers balancing; Congress does not have unlimited access.
