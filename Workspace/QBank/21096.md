Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Cross-examination and rebuttal of character witnesses
Question: At his federal civil fraud trial, Noah voluntarily testifies on direct examination that he “never handled the charity account.” On cross-examination, he is asked detailed questions about the same account transfers. Noah invokes the Fifth Amendment and refuses to answer any details about the transfers. Which ruling is most proper?
Answer A: Allow Noah to testify broadly in his favor and then invoke the Fifth Amendment to block all cross-examination on the same subject.
Answer B: Require Noah to answer every question in the entire case because any testimony waives the Fifth Amendment globally.
Answer C: Permit the court to treat Noah's voluntary testimony on the account as a waiver at least as to fair cross-examination on the same subject.
Answer D: Strike Mary's complaint because Noah invoked the privilege.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness who voluntarily gives testimony on a subject cannot use the Fifth Amendment as both sword and shield to block fair cross-examination on that same subject. The waiver is limited to the subject matter opened. The decisive axis is subject-matter waiver, not global loss of privilege.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This permits one-sided testimony without fair testing of the same subject. B: B is wrong because This overstates waiver. Testimony on one subject does not waive the privilege for every possible incriminating question. D: D is wrong because Invocation does not automatically dispose of the opposing party's claim.
lb_rule_reference: Fifth Amendment / waiver by testimony on same subject
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is missing the limited waiver created by voluntary testimony or is turning it into a global waiver.
