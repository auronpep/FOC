Question: A state statute provides that people who are more than six months behind on child-support payments may not obtain a marriage license, even if they are complying with a court-approved repayment plan. The state says the rule encourages payment and should receive rational basis review. What level of scrutiny most likely applies?
Answer A: Rational basis, because marriage is regulated by the state and therefore never fundamental
Answer B: Strict scrutiny, because the law directly interferes with the fundamental right to marry
Answer C: Intermediate scrutiny, because every family-related law receives intermediate scrutiny
Answer D: No scrutiny, because due process never protects decisions about family life
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A licensing bar tied to support arrears directly burdens the ability to marry.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The fundamental right to marry limits state regulation that directly and substantially interferes with marriage. C: Fundamental rights generally trigger strict scrutiny, not intermediate scrutiny. D: Substantive due process protects certain family and marriage decisions.
lb_rule_reference: Laws that directly and substantially interfere with the fundamental right to marry are subject to strict scrutiny.
