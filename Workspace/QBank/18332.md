


BARMATRIX Q#: 18332
Outline_code: 32020402
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Prohibited uses of character evidence
Question: The plaintiff sued the defendant for battery. At trial, the defendant offered the testimony of the local parish priest, who stated that he had known the defendant for years and that he and everyone else in the community thought of him as a peaceable man who would never resort to violence except in self-protection. The defendant offered the testimony as circumstantial evidence to prove that he did not strike the plaintiff without justification. If the plaintiff's attorney objects to the testimony of the parish priest, the testimony should be
Answer A: A: excluded, because it is being offered as circumstantial evidence to prove that the defendant did not strike the plaintiff without justification.
Answer B: B: admitted, because the priest testified that his own opinion of the defendant coincided with what the community thought about him.
Answer C: C: admitted, because it states the reputation of the defendant.
Answer D: D: admitted, for the limited purpose of establishing the defendant's state of mind at the time of the occurrence.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Character evidence is not ordinarily admissible for the purpose of proving a person's conduct on a particular occasion. Thus, if evidence of the defendant's character is offered to prove anything about his conduct on the occasion of the incident in question, it is not admissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect because a witness who testifies to a person's reputation is not required to know that person or to have any personal opinion about him. C is applicable only in criminal prosecutions. If the evidence were allowed for the purpose stated in D, it would be to prove that the defendant did not strike the plaintiff without justification. D is therefore incorrect for the same reasons that make A correct.
