MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Felix joins a conspiracy to steal computers from a warehouse. Before the theft, Felix privately decides he wants out and stops attending meetings. He never tells the others, never retrieves the access card he gave them, and never alerts authorities. The others later use the access card to steal the computers. Felix claims he withdrew. Is Felix's withdrawal claim likely effective?
Answer A: Yes, because he privately changed his mind before the theft
Answer B: No, because mere absence and private regret do not effectively withdraw from the conspiracy
Answer C: Yes, because withdrawal is automatic once a conspirator misses a meeting
Answer D: No, but only because computers are valuable
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Felix did not communicate withdrawal, defeat the plan, retrieve the access card, or alert police. His silent absence is insufficient.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Withdrawal requires more than a private change of heart. He needed an affirmative act such as communicating withdrawal or neutralizing his aid. C: Absence may be evidence in context, but it is not automatically effective withdrawal. D: Property value does not determine whether withdrawal was effective.
lb_rule_reference: Withdrawal from conspiracy generally requires an affirmative act communicating withdrawal or defeating the purpose of the conspiracy; mere absence or private change of mind is insufficient.
