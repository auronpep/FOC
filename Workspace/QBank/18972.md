MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Res ipsa loquitur
Question: A patient awoke from abdominal surgery with a surgical sponge left inside her body. The operation involved a surgeon, nurses, and a hospital team. The patient was unconscious and could not identify which team member failed to count or remove the sponge. May the patient rely on res ipsa loquitur despite not identifying the exact negligent actor?
Answer A: No, because res ipsa requires the plaintiff to identify the exact person who was careless
Answer B: No, because multiple medical workers always defeat res ipsa
Answer C: Yes, because leaving a sponge inside a patient permits an inference of negligence by responsible medical actors
Answer D: Yes, because res ipsa conclusively proves punitive damages
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: This is a classic res ipsa setting: the event ordinarily does not happen without negligence, the patient did not contribute, and the surgical team had responsibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Res ipsa can apply when the injury ordinarily does not occur absent negligence and the defendants had responsibility while the patient could not know the exact actor. B: Multiple responsible actors do not automatically defeat res ipsa where the patient was unconscious and the instrumentality was within the surgical team's control. D: Res ipsa permits a negligence inference; it does not automatically prove punitive damages.
lb_rule_reference: Res ipsa does not always require literal exclusive physical control if the defendant had probable responsibility for the instrumentality.
