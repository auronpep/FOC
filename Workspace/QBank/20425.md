Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A roofer left loose metal panels on a roof overnight despite a forecast of strong winds. During the forecasted windstorm, the panels blew off and injured a pedestrian on the sidewalk below. Does the windstorm likely supersede the roofer's negligence?
Answer A: Yes, because all natural forces are superseding causes
Answer B: Yes, because weather is never within a contractor's duty
Answer C: No, because the wind was a foreseeable condition within the risk created by leaving panels loose
Answer D: No, because proximate cause is automatically satisfied whenever actual cause exists
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The injury resulted from the type of risk that made the conduct negligent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Foreseeable natural forces generally do not cut off liability. B: The forecasted wind was exactly why securing the panels mattered. D: Proximate cause is a separate limitation, though it is satisfied here.
lb_rule_reference: A natural force is not superseding when it is a foreseeable condition that makes the defendant's precaution necessary.
