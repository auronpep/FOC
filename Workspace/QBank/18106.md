MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Several people discuss stealing phones from a warehouse while Jonah sits nearby at the same table. Jonah hears the plan and says nothing. He does not offer help, share information, receive a role, or agree to participate. Police arrest everyone and charge Jonah with conspiracy to commit larceny. What is Jonah's strongest argument?
Answer A: Jonah is not guilty because the facts show knowledge and presence but no agreement to join the theft plan
Answer B: Jonah is guilty because he heard the plan and did not object
Answer C: Jonah is guilty because conspiracy does not require completion of larceny
Answer D: Jonah is not guilty only because phones are easy to resell
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Conspiracy requires agreement to pursue the criminal objective. Mere presence and knowledge, without words or conduct showing assent, do not establish that element.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Passive knowledge and failure to object are not enough, without more, to prove conspiratorial agreement. C: The missing element is Jonah's agreement. A true no-completion rule does not fill that gap. D: Resale value does not decide whether Jonah agreed to join.
lb_rule_reference: Mere presence at the scene of planning, knowledge of a criminal plan, or association with criminals is not enough for conspiracy without agreement to join the criminal objective.
