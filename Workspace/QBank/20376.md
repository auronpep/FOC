Question: The day after serving the complaint, a plaintiff serves document requests on the defendant. The parties have not held a Rule 26(f) conference, and there is no court order, stipulation, or special rule authorizing early discovery. The defendant objects to timing. What is the most accurate response?
Answer A: The requests are generally premature because discovery usually cannot be sought before the Rule 26(f) conference
Answer B: The requests are timely because the complaint has been served
Answer C: The requests are timely only if they seek privileged information
Answer D: The requests are void because document requests are not allowed in federal practice
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The default timing rule requires the Rule 26(f) conference first unless an exception applies.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Service of the complaint is not the ordinary trigger for opening discovery. C: Privilege restricts discovery; it does not make early discovery timely. D: Rule 34 document requests are available, but timing matters.
lb_rule_reference: A party generally may not seek discovery before the parties have conferred under Rule 26(f), unless authorized by rule, stipulation, or court order.
