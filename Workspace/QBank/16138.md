Outline_code: 71040200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Two undercover police officers received an anonymous tip that the defendant was engaged in buying and selling stolen cars. The two officers got a car from the police impound lot and then met with the defendant. After the officers told the defendant that the car was stolen, the defendant offered to buy it. When the defendant handed the officers the money, they placed him under arrest. The defendant is charged with conspiracy to receive stolen property. The jurisdiction follows the common law view. The court should find him
Answer A: guilty, because he believed the car was stolen.
Answer B: guilty, because the officers told him the car was stolen.
Answer C: not guilty, because neither officer intended to participate in the purchase or sale of a stolen vehicle.
Answer D: not guilty, because the car that he agreed to purchase from the officers was not actually stolen.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the common law, a criminal conspiracy is an agreement between two or more persons to commit a crime. Without such an agreement, there can be no conspiracy. Since neither officer actually intended to commit a crime, the defendant never made such an agreement with either of them, even though he believed he did. For this reason, he cannot be guilty of conspiracy.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A, B, and D are therefore incorrect.
