Question: Abigail filled out a printed deed form for her house, named Solomon as grantee, and signed it. In the transfer clause, she wrote, “Solomon is to have this house after my death.” Abigail handed the paper to Solomon and said, “Keep this safe until I die; I am not giving up the house now.” Abigail died without executing a will. Solomon claims the house under the paper. Who owns the house?
Answer A: Abigail's estate, because the paper did not make a present transfer of title.
Answer B: Solomon, because the paper was on a printed deed form.
Answer C: Solomon, because consideration is not required for a deed.
Answer D: Solomon, because physical delivery of a signed paper always transfers title.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is present operative transfer language paired with delivery intent. Abigail's words stated a transfer only after death and expressly denied present transfer. The printed deed form, lack of consideration, and physical handoff do not cure the testamentary character of the paper, so title remains in Abigail's estate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a deed label or form cannot substitute for present transfer language. C: C is wrong because the no-consideration rule does not cure absence of present conveyance. D: D is wrong because delivery turns on intent to pass present title, not mere physical possession.
