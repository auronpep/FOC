Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: Defendant was sued in federal court and answered without objecting to personal jurisdiction, even though all facts supporting a PJ objection were known. Two months later, Plaintiff amended the complaint to add a new damages theory based on the same transaction and the same defendant. Defendant then moved to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant the motion because every amended complaint restarts all Rule 12 defenses
Answer B: Grant the motion because personal jurisdiction can never be waived
Answer C: Deny the motion because Defendant waived the available PJ defense by omitting it from the first response
Answer D: Deny the motion only if Plaintiff proves the court has supplemental jurisdiction
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The amended complaint did not create a new jurisdictional defect. The original omission waived the available Rule 12(b)(2) defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A new response deadline does not automatically revive a personal-jurisdiction defense that was available and waived earlier. B: Personal jurisdiction is waivable and must be timely asserted. D: The question is waiver of personal jurisdiction, not subject-matter jurisdiction over amended claims.
lb_rule_reference: A personal-jurisdiction defense available at the first Rule 12 response is waived if omitted; an amended complaint does not automatically revive the waived defense when the relevant jurisdictional facts did not change.
