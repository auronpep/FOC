Question: A defendant in a criminal case is ordered to provide a handwriting exemplar so that the jury can compare it with a threatening note. The defendant refuses, arguing that forcing him to provide the exemplar violates the Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the refusal because any compelled evidence from a defendant is testimonial.
Answer B: Sustain the refusal because handwriting comparison is always protected by attorney-client privilege.
Answer C: Reject the refusal because a handwriting exemplar is physical identifying evidence, not compelled testimonial communication.
Answer D: Reject the refusal only if the defendant first testifies in his own defense.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The Fifth Amendment privilege protects compelled testimonial self-incrimination. A handwriting exemplar used as physical identifying evidence generally is not testimonial. The decisive axis is testimonial communication versus physical characteristic.
