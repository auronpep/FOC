Outline: Defamation
Question: A town has a three-member zoning board. A local radio host falsely said, "One of the three zoning board members took cash to approve the mall project." The host then described the member as "the only architect on the board." Listeners knew that the plaintiff was the only architect on the board. Can the plaintiff satisfy the of-and-concerning requirement?
Answer A: Yes, because the context reasonably identified the plaintiff to listeners
Answer B: No, because the radio host did not state the plaintiff's name
Answer C: No, because statements about public boards cannot defame individuals
Answer D: Yes, but only if every listener already knew the plaintiff personally
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff need not be named if the audience can reasonably identify her from the statement and context.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Exact naming is not required when context points to the plaintiff. C: A small group plus identifying facts can make the statement about an individual. D: The requirement is reasonable identification by recipients, not universal personal familiarity.
lb_rule_reference: A statement can be of and concerning a plaintiff without naming the plaintiff when context reasonably identifies the plaintiff to recipients.
