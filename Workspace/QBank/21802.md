Question: In a jurisdiction that allows easements to be extinguished by prescription, John owned an easement across Peter's land. Peter built a locked gate across the easement route, told John that John had no right to pass, and kept the gate locked continuously and openly for the full limitations period. John knew about the obstruction and did not sue or otherwise object during that period. What is the likely result?
Answer A: John's easement remains fully enforceable because easements can never be lost by prescription.
Answer B: John's easement remains enforceable because Peter's locked gate was on Peter's own land.
Answer C: John's easement was extinguished because Peter openly and adversely prevented its use for the limitations period.
Answer D: Peter acquired fee simple title to John's land because Peter blocked the easement.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The easement was extinguished. In a jurisdiction recognizing prescriptive termination, the servient owner can extinguish an easement by openly, continuously, and adversely obstructing the easement for the limitations period while the easement holder fails to enforce the right.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the question states that the jurisdiction recognizes prescriptive extinguishment. B: B is wrong because hostile obstruction on the servient land can defeat the easement when maintained for the required period. D: D is wrong because extinguishing an easement does not transfer ownership of the dominant parcel.
lb_rule_reference: Prescription / hostile obstruction of easement
