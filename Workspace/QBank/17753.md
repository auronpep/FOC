Question: In a federal civil trial, Priscilla testifies for the plaintiff. The plaintiff then calls Aquila to testify that Priscilla is truthful because she is a devout member of a religious community that teaches honesty. The defendant objects. The plaintiff offers the testimony only to support Priscilla's credibility. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony because religious devotion is strong evidence of truthfulness.
Answer B: Admit the testimony if Aquila personally shares Priscilla's religious beliefs.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony because evidence of religious beliefs or opinions is not admissible to support or attack a witness's credibility.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony only if Priscilla's character for truthfulness has not first been attacked.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Religious beliefs or opinions may not be used to attack or support a witness's credibility. The proposed testimony is offered precisely to support Priscilla's credibility from religious belief, so it is inadmissible for that purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A and B are wrong because religious belief is not a permissible credibility-support route. D is wrong because even after an attack, support must use a proper truthfulness route; religious belief is not that route. B: A and B are wrong because religious belief is not a permissible credibility-support route. D is wrong because even after an attack, support must use a proper truthfulness route; religious belief is not that route. D: D is wrong because even after an attack, support must use a proper truthfulness route; religious belief is not that route.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 610 / religious belief not admissible to support or attack credibility
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests improper reliance on religious belief as credibility support rather than proper opinion/reputation truthfulness evidence.
