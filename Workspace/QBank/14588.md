Question: A state statute provides as follows: “In all criminal cases, whenever the U.S. Constitution permits, the burden of proof as to a defense claimed by the defendant shall rest on the defendant, and the magnitude of the burden shall be as great as the Constitution permits.” The same state defines the crime of forcible rape as follows: “Forcible rape consists of sexual penetration inflicted on an unconsenting person by means of force or violence. Consent of the victim is a complete defense to a charge of rape.” At a defendant's trial for forcible rape, he testified that the alleged victim had consented to having sexual intercourse with him. How should the trial judge instruct the jury regarding the burden of proof on the issue of consent?
Answer A: A: The defendant must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that the victim consented.
Answer B: B: The defendant must prove by clear and convincing evidence that the victim consented.
Answer C: C: The defendant must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the victim consented.
Answer D: D: The prosecution must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the victim did not consent.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. Due process demands that the prosecution maintain the burden of proving each and every element of the charged offense beyond a reasonable doubt, including the lack of consent, as laid out in the statute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Shifting the burden of an element of the offense to the defendant violates due process, regardless of the standard of proof. B is incorrect. Again, the burden to prove this element must remain on the prosecution; the level of proof required has no bearing on this outcome. C is incorrect. As stated above, the burden may not shift to the defendant at all, irrespective of the level of proof required.
