Outline: Privileges
Question: A camera owner saw a thief take his camera and run into a crowd. The owner gave up the chase and went home. Two days later, he saw the thief at a cafe holding the camera. Without warning, the owner punched the thief and took the camera. The thief sues for battery. Can the owner rely on recapture of chattels?
Answer A: No, because the owner waited until hot pursuit had ended before using force
Answer B: Yes, because the camera truly belonged to the owner
Answer C: Yes, because any force is allowed to recover stolen chattels
Answer D: No, but only because the owner failed to call police first
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The privilege is narrow and does not authorize delayed self-help violence after pursuit ends.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Ownership alone does not permit force after hot pursuit has ended. C: Force must be reasonable and within the privilege's timing limits. D: The core defect is delayed force after hot pursuit, not a universal police-call requirement.
lb_rule_reference: Recapture of chattels is narrowly limited; reasonable force is privileged only in prompt hot pursuit or when the possessor's claim is fresh and wrongful possession is clear.
