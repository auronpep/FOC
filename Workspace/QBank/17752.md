Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A scooter had a defective brake cable when sold by a commercial retailer. The rider also exceeded the posted safe speed for the path. The jurisdiction applies comparative fault to strict products claims. The brake failed and the rider crashed; evidence assigns part of the harm to the rider's speed. How should the rider's conduct affect recovery?
Answer A: It is irrelevant because comparative fault never applies to strict products claims
Answer B: It automatically bars all recovery because the rider was careless
Answer C: It may reduce recovery according to comparative responsibility
Answer D: It converts the claim into negligence only
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The product defect and rider speed both contributed. In the stated comparative-fault jurisdiction, fault allocation can reduce damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The jurisdiction applies comparative fault to strict products claims. B: Comparative fault generally reduces recovery according to responsibility rather than automatically barring it. D: Comparative responsibility can be applied within a strict products claim without changing the underlying theory.
lb_rule_reference: In comparative-fault jurisdictions, plaintiff carelessness in product use may reduce recovery rather than automatically bar it.
