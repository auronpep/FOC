Question: Solomon agreed in a signed writing to sell Leah 100 clay lamps for $2,000. The writing contained all delivery, quantity, price, and warranty terms and stated that it was the complete and exclusive statement of the agreement. Leah offers evidence that, before signing, Solomon orally promised to include five additional lamps for free. The alleged promise is not supported by any course of performance, course of dealing, or usage of trade. How should the court rule? How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the oral promise because it is consistent with the sale of lamps.
Answer B: Admit the oral promise because Article 2 always admits consistent additional terms.
Answer C: Admit the oral promise because it concerns goods.
Answer D: Exclude the oral promise because the writing was complete and exclusive and the promise is merely an additional prior term.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under UCC 2-202, consistent additional terms may supplement a final writing unless the court finds the writing was intended as a complete and exclusive statement of the agreement. Here, the extra-lamps promise is a prior additional term barred by the complete and exclusive writing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because consistency alone is not enough when the writing is complete and exclusive. B: B is wrong because Article 2 allows consistent additional terms only when the writing is not complete and exclusive. C: C is wrong because a goods contract does not automatically admit every prior oral term.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-202 / complete and exclusive writing / consistent additional term barred
