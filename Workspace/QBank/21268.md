Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A construction rental company regularly rents scaffolds to contractors. It rented a scaffold with a defective locking pin that was present when the scaffold left the company's warehouse. A worker using the scaffold normally fell when the platform collapsed and suffered physical injury. In the worker's strict products liability claim against the rental company, what is the company's strongest defense?
Answer A: The company cannot be strictly liable because it rented rather than manufactured the scaffold
Answer B: The company can be strictly liable because it commercially supplied a defective product that caused physical injury
Answer C: The company is liable only if the worker had contractual privity with it
Answer D: The company is not liable because strict products liability covers sales but never commercial rentals
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The company regularly rents scaffolds, the defect existed when the product left its control, and the defect caused tort-type physical injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Strict products liability can reach commercial suppliers in the distribution chain, including regular commercial lessors. C: Strict products liability does not require privity between injured user and commercial supplier. D: Regular commercial lessors can be treated as product suppliers for strict products liability.
lb_rule_reference: Strict products liability can apply to commercial lessors and distributors in the product supply chain, not only to manufacturers.
