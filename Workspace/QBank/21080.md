Question: Bradley, who had a prior DUI conviction and knew he had a drinking problem, attended a party where he drank heavily for four hours. Despite being visibly impaired, Bradley got behind the wheel of his car at 2:00 a.m. and drove at 80 mph through a busy downtown area. He ran a red light and struck a pedestrian legally crossing the street, killing her. Multiple witnesses saw Bradley weaving at high speed for several blocks before the collision. What is the most serious homicide offense for which Bradley can be convicted?
Answer A: Involuntary manslaughter, because Bradley's intoxication negated his ability to consciously appreciate the risk he was creating
Answer B: No homicide charge, because Bradley did not intend to harm anyone and was too intoxicated to form the required mental state
Answer C: Murder (depraved-heart), because Bradley voluntarily became intoxicated knowing it would impair his driving and then drove at extreme speed through a pedestrian area, consciously disregarding an extreme risk to human life
Answer D: Second-degree murder, because Bradley intended to drive and driving is an inherently dangerous act
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Depraved-heart murder requires conscious disregard of an extreme and unjustifiable risk to human life. Bradley had a prior DUI — he knew drunk driving was deadly. He voluntarily drank to severe intoxication, then drove at 80 mph through a downtown area at 2 a.m., weaving for several blocks. The prior DUI establishes knowledge of the risk before intoxication. Voluntary intoxication cannot be used to avoid depraved-heart liability when the risk was appreciated before drinking began. This conduct demonstrates indifference to human life sufficient for murder.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Voluntary intoxication does not negate the conscious disregard required for depraved-heart murder when the defendant knew of the risk before drinking and chose to drink and drive anyway. Bradley had a prior DUI conviction — he was aware that drunk driving was dangerous. He voluntarily became intoxicated. A defendant cannot drink himself into a lower level of culpability by voluntarily eliminating the very awareness the law requires. B: Depraved-heart murder does not require intent to kill. It requires conscious disregard of an extreme risk. Bradley's prior DUI conviction shows he understood the danger of drunk driving, satisfying pre-existing awareness. His driving conduct — 80 mph weaving through downtown at 2 a.m. — was extremely dangerous. The voluntary decision to drink and drive, with prior knowledge of the risk, satisfies the culpability standard. D: Intentionally driving a car is not an inherently dangerous act (it is a lawful activity). Second-degree murder in its classic form requires intent to kill or intent to cause grievous bodily harm. The correct charge is depraved-heart murder based on extreme recklessness, not second-degree murder based on the intent-to-drive theory. D conflates the intent to perform an act with intent to cause harm.
lb_rule_reference: Voluntary intoxication does not negate the conscious disregard element of depraved-heart murder when the defendant was aware of the extreme risk before becoming intoxicated; getting drunk and then knowingly driving can supply the required malice
