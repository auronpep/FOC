


BARMATRIX Q#: 22185
Outline_code: 33040301
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Hearsay and Circumstances of Its Admissibility
Outline: Requiring unavailability of declarant
Question: A politician was on trial for the murder of a local businessman. The prosecutor wanted to introduce the testimony of a bartender who overheard a boxer tell his girlfriend, “That politician gave me so much money to kill that businessman last week that I'm taking you on a trip! We're going around the world! And I'm buying this whole bar a drink!” The boxer then shouted to the bartender that, “I'm so happy that the next round is on me!” Everyone in the bar cheered. The next day, the boxer went missing and hasn't been seen since. Is the bartender's testimony admissible?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the bartender is testifying to the boxer's excited utterance.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the bartender is testifying to the boxer's statement exposing him to criminal liability.
Answer C: C: No, because the bartender's testimony is hearsay without any exception.
Answer D: D: No, because the bartender's testimony is being used in a criminal trial.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: If a declarant is unavailable for trial and makes a statement exposing him or her to civil or criminal liability, that statement is admissible as a hearsay exception. Here, the boxer is missing and he made a statement implicating him in the businessman's murder. Thus, the bartender's testimony is admissible.A is incorrect because a statement is admissible as an excited utterance when the declarant makes a statement regarding a startling event and is still excited by that startling event. Here, while the boxer is certainly excited about taking his girlfriend around the world, the murder happened a week before, so the statement is likely too far away from the murder to qualify as an excited utterance. C is incorrect because the boxer is missing and made a statement against his penal interest. D is incorrect because the fact that this is a criminal trial does not change the analysis.
