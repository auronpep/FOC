Question: A buyer orders 50 brass lamps from a merchant seller. The seller immediately emails, “We accept and will ship the brass lamps Friday.” On Friday, the seller discovers it has no brass lamps and ships 50 iron lamps with a note stating, “offered as an accommodation.” The buyer rejects the iron lamps and sues for breach. What is the best analysis?
Answer A: The accommodation note means no contract was ever formed.
Answer B: No contract was formed because the shipment did not match the order.
Answer C: The buyer's rejection of the iron lamps waived any claim that the seller breached.
Answer D: The contract was already formed by the seller's earlier promise to ship brass lamps; the later shipment breached that contract.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Article 2 permits acceptance by a prompt promise to ship. The seller accepted before the later nonconforming shipment. Once the contract existed, the later wrong shipment could not retroactively convert the earlier acceptance into a counteroffer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A: accommodation notice can prevent acceptance by a nonconforming shipment, but here acceptance already occurred by the seller's earlier promise. B: the later wrong shipment does not erase the earlier promise-based acceptance. C: rejection of nonconforming goods does not waive a breach claim.
