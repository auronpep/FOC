Question: A grantor executed an instrument in the proper form of a warranty deed purporting to convey a tract of land to his church. The granting clause of the instrument ran to the church “and its successors forever, so long as the premises are used for church purposes.” The church took possession of the land and used it as its site of worship for many years. Subsequently, the church decided to relocate and entered into a valid written contract to sell the land to a buyer for a substantial price. The buyer wanted to use the land as a site for business activities and objected to the church's title. The contract contained no provision relating to the quality of title the church was bound to convey. There is no applicable statute. When the buyer refused to close, the church sued the buyer for specific performance and properly joined the grantor as a party. Is the church likely to prevail?
Answer A: A: No, because the grantor's interest prevents the church's title from being marketable.
Answer B: B: No, because the quoted provision is a valid restrictive covenant.
Answer C: C: Yes, because a charitable trust to support religion will attach to the proceeds of the sale.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the grantor cannot derogate from his warranty to the church.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. The church holds title to the land by a warranty deed that conveyed a fee simple determinable. The possibility of reverter held in the grantor makes this title unmarketable because a reasonable person would not purchase property with such a limitation attached.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. This answer reaches the correct conclusion with the wrong legal reasoning. The grantor's future interest in the property (i.e., the possibility of reverter) is enough to make the title unmarketable; the possibility of reverter does not need to also act as a restrictive covenant in order for the title to be unmarketable. C is incorrect. This answer choice assumes that because the church can use the proceeds from the sale, the title must be marketable, which is incorrect. The limiting aspect of a fee simple determinable is enough to make the church's title unmarketable. D is incorrect. The facts do not suggest that the grantor and the church had a land sale contract; the grantor conveyed a title, in fee simple determinable, to the church. With a fee simple determinable comes a possibility of reverter that is retained in the grantor, making this title unmarketable.
