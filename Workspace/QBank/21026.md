Question: A driver negligently hit a pedestrian in a crosswalk. The pedestrian had a chronic back disease that already required monthly treatment. Medical evidence shows the collision caused a new fracture and aggravated the disease for six months, but the underlying disease would have required treatment even without the collision. In the pedestrian's negligence claim, what damages may the pedestrian recover for the back condition?
Answer A: All costs of the chronic back disease for life, because the driver takes the plaintiff as found
Answer B: Only the damages that an average healthy pedestrian would have suffered
Answer C: The fracture and the six-month aggravation caused by the collision
Answer D: No back-related damages because the disease existed before the collision
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The driver takes the pedestrian as found but pays only for the harm caused by the tort, including aggravation of the preexisting disease.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The driver is liable for tort-caused aggravation, not for the baseline disease that would have existed anyway. B: Once physical injury to a pedestrian is foreseeable, the eggshell rule makes the driver liable for the full tort-caused aggravation even if unusually severe. D: A preexisting condition does not bar recovery for aggravation caused by negligence.
lb_rule_reference: A negligent defendant takes the plaintiff as found and is liable for tort-caused aggravation of a preexisting condition, not for the entire condition unrelated to the accident.
