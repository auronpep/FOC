Question: An armed robber points a gun at a cashier standing behind the counter and orders her to open the cash register next to her. The robber takes the cash from the register while the cashier stands frozen inches away. Is the taking from the register a taking from the person or immediate presence?
Answer A: Yes, because the cash was under the cashier's immediate control and taken by intimidation
Answer B: No, because robbery requires property to be physically attached to the victim
Answer C: No, because cash register money belongs to the store, not the cashier
Answer D: Yes, but only because the robber later escaped with the cash
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Immediate presence includes property close enough to the victim that force or intimidation prevents control.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Immediate presence is broader than physical attachment. C: Robbery protects possessory control and immediate presence, not only ownership. D: The immediate-presence and intimidation elements were satisfied at the counter.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law robbery is larceny from the person or immediate presence of another by force or intimidation.
