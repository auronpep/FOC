Question: Hannah owned an express recorded easement to cross a strip of land owned by James to reach the public road. The city condemned the strip for a public transit station. The condemnation permanently destroyed Hannah's ability to use the easement. The city paid James for the fee value of the strip but refused to pay Hannah, arguing that only fee owners are entitled to compensation. Hannah seeks compensation for the loss of her easement. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Hannah may be entitled to compensation because an easement is a compensable property interest when taken or destroyed by condemnation.
Answer B: Hannah receives nothing because an easement appurtenant is not a property interest.
Answer C: Hannah receives nothing because the government did not physically occupy Hannah's dominant parcel.
Answer D: Hannah receives nothing because recording affects notice only and can never affect compensation.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement is a property interest. If government condemnation takes or destroys that interest, the easement holder may be entitled to compensation, often through apportionment of the condemnation award. The condemnation permanently destroyed Hannah's recorded access easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because an easement appurtenant is a property interest. C: C is wrong because the taking destroyed Hannah's easement even though the physical occupation occurred on the servient land. D: D is wrong because recording is not the compensation source, but the existence of the easement matters; recording helps establish the interest and notice.
lb_rule_reference: Condemnation / compensable easement interest
