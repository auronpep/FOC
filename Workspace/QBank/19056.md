Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal assault trial, Peter offers a knife. The officer who seized the knife testifies that he did not mark it at the scene but recognizes it because the handle has a distinctive crack shaped like a crescent and because he photographed that crack when he seized it. The knife offered in court has the same crack. The defendant objects that the officer failed to mark the knife at the scene. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because testimony from a witness with knowledge and distinctive characteristics can authenticate the knife.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because an item must be marked at the scene to be authenticated.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because the photograph, not the knife, is the best evidence of the crack.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the defendant admits the knife is the same one.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness with knowledge may authenticate an item, and distinctive characteristics can support identification. Marking the item is helpful but not required when the officer can identify it by distinctive features and photographs.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because marking at the scene is not a categorical authentication requirement. C: C is wrong because the issue is identifying the knife, not proving the contents of a writing, recording, or photograph. D: D is wrong because the defendant's admission is unnecessary.
