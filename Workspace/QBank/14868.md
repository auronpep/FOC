Question: A defendant is on trial for nighttime breaking and entering of a warehouse. The warehouse owner had set up a camera to take infrared pictures of any intruders. After an expert establishes the reliability of infrared photography, the prosecutor offers the authenticated infrared picture of the intruder to show the similarities to the defendant. The photograph is
Answer A: A: admissible, because an expert witness can point out to the jury the similarities between the person in the photograph and the defendant.
Answer B: B: admissible, allowing the jury to compare the person in the photograph and the defendant.
Answer C: C: inadmissible, because there was no eyewitness to the scene available to authenticate the photograph.
Answer D: D: inadmissible, because infrared photography deprives a defendant of the right to confront witnesses.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. The infrared photograph, once authenticated, can be identified by comparison by the trier of fact with the defendant. The photograph is admissible as substantive evidence and the jury should be allowed to compare the person in the photograph and the defendant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The expert witness is not required to point out the similarities to the jury before the photograph is identifiable and admissible. C is incorrect. There does not need to be an eyewitness to the scene available to authenticate the photograph if the photo is already authenticated by the testimony of the expert witness. D is incorrect. The photograph is not a witness that the defendant has a right to "confront." Because the jury is able to make a comparison between the photograph and the defendant, the photograph is admissible.
