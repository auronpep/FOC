Question: Martha signed and recorded a deed conveying Figacre to Lazarus as a gift. Lazarus did not know about the deed when it was recorded, but the conveyance was beneficial to him. When Lazarus learned of the deed two weeks later, he kept the benefit and did not reject it. Martha then claimed the deed failed because Lazarus had not accepted at the moment of recording. What is the best result?
Answer A: The deed failed because acceptance must be express and simultaneous with recording.
Answer B: The deed failed because a gift deed requires consideration before acceptance can be presumed.
Answer C: The deed is effective because acceptance of a beneficial deed is presumed absent rejection, and Lazarus did not reject it.
Answer D: The deed is effective only if Lazarus signed the recorded deed.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive acceptance lock is the presumption of acceptance for a beneficial conveyance. Lazarus's lack of awareness at the moment of recording did not defeat the deed where the gift was beneficial and he did not reject it after learning of it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because acceptance need not be express and simultaneous in this setting. B: B is wrong because consideration is not required for gift-deed acceptance. D: D is wrong because a grantee signature is generally not required.
lb_rule_reference: Acceptance of a beneficial deed is presumed absent rejection
