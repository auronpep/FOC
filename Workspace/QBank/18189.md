Question: Rachel sold Peter a stone sculpture that was already severed from the land and stored in Rachel's barn. The written receipt stated that Peter could enter Rachel's barn once within 10 days to retrieve the sculpture. The receipt did not identify any land owned by Peter and did not use the word easement. How is Peter's right to enter best classified?
Answer A: A profit because Peter will remove stone from Rachel's property.
Answer B: An easement appurtenant because Peter may enter a specific building.
Answer C: A real covenant because the receipt is in writing.
Answer D: A license coupled with an interest because Peter may enter only to retrieve personal property he bought.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A license coupled with an interest allows entry onto land as necessary to remove or protect property in which the entrant has an interest. Peter bought the already-severed sculpture and may enter once to retrieve that personal property.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a profit involves removing natural resources or produce from land, not retrieving an already-severed chattel. B: B is wrong because no dominant parcel is identified and the limited retrieval right is not an appurtenant easement. C: C is wrong because the writing records a limited access permission tied to a chattel, not a running land-use covenant.
lb_rule_reference: License coupled with an interest
