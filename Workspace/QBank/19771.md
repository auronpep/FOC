Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Robbery
Question: Officers chased a robbery suspect through a crowded bus terminal and arrested him after seeing him toss an object behind a row of seats. Before giving Miranda warnings, an officer asked, "Where is the gun?" The suspect said, "Behind the blue seats." Officers immediately recovered a loaded pistol where passengers were walking. Is the suspect's statement identifying the gun's location admissible?
Answer A: No, because the suspect was in custody and had not yet received Miranda warnings
Answer B: No, because the officer's question was designed to elicit incriminating evidence
Answer C: Yes, because the question was limited to locating an immediate public safety threat
Answer D: Yes, because the public safety exception permits full unwarned questioning after arrest
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The officer asked a narrow question about the location of a gun in a crowded terminal. That was reasonably prompted by an immediate safety concern, so Miranda warnings were not required before that limited question.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The public safety exception permits limited unwarned questioning when an immediate threat exists. A loaded gun in a crowded terminal creates that threat. B: The question was also reasonably necessary to protect the public from an unsecured loaded gun. The emergency purpose brings it within the exception. D: The exception is narrow. It does not authorize general interrogation about the robbery once the immediate danger is handled.
lb_rule_reference: Under the public safety exception, police may ask limited unwarned questions reasonably prompted by an immediate safety threat, and the answer may be admissible.
