Question: In a diversity action, the defendant immediately files a motion for sanctions under a state statute that allows sanctions as soon as a frivolous complaint is filed. The defendant did not serve a Rule 11 motion 21 days before filing it. The requested sanction targets the plaintiff's federal complaint. How should the federal court handle the sanctions request?
Answer A: Grant it because state sanctions policy controls every diversity filing
Answer B: Deny the party motion because Rule 11's safe harbor was not followed
Answer C: Convert the sanctions motion into summary judgment
Answer D: Ask the jury whether the complaint was frivolous
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The party's Rule 11 motion cannot be filed before service and expiration of the safe-harbor period.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 11 directly governs sanctions for federal-court filings and has its own safe-harbor process. C: Sanctions and merits judgment use different procedures and consequences. D: Rule 11 sanctions are decided by the court under the rule's standards and process.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 11 governs sanctions for representations made in federal filings and includes its own procedural requirements, including the safe-harbor process for party motions.
