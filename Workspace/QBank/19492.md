Question: Daniel's parcel was landlocked when it was severed from Esther's parcel, creating an easement by necessity over Esther's driveway. Years later, Daniel bought an adjoining strip of land in fee simple. The strip connects Daniel's parcel directly to a public road and can legally and practically be used for access. Daniel still prefers Esther's driveway because it is shorter. Does Daniel's easement by necessity continue solely because Esther's driveway is more convenient?
Answer A: Yes, because an easement by necessity is permanent once it arises at severance.
Answer B: Yes, because the shortest route is always legally necessary.
Answer C: No, but only because Daniel's purchase transferred fee title in Esther's driveway to him.
Answer D: No, because the necessity generally ends once Daniel has permanent legal and practical access to a public road, and convenience alone is insufficient.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement by necessity lasts only as long as the necessity exists. Daniel acquired permanent legal and practical access through land he owns in fee simple. The fact that Esther's driveway is shorter makes it convenient, not necessary.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because necessity easements can end when necessity ends. B: B is wrong because shortest does not mean necessary. C: C is wrong because buying an adjoining strip does not transfer title to Esther's driveway. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Necessity terminates when permanent legal access arises
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating necessity as frozen at severance and ignoring later permanent access that eliminates strict necessity.
