Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: In a federal burglary trial, the prosecutor offers a key found in Miriam's coat pocket. The prosecutor claims the key opens the store's rear door. A locksmith testifies that the key's cuts are consistent with the store's rear-door lock, but he cannot say the key definitely opens that lock. Miriam objects that the key is inadmissible unless the judge first finds that it actually opens the door. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the key because the locksmith cannot say it definitely opens the door.
Answer B: Exclude the key unless the judge finds by clear and convincing evidence that it opens the door.
Answer C: Admit the key only if Miriam admits it opens the door.
Answer D: Admit the key if there is evidence sufficient for a reasonable juror to find that it opens the door; the jury decides the condition's weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: When relevance depends on a conditional fact, the judge screens for evidence sufficient to support a finding of that fact. The judge need not decide conclusively that the key opens the door. If the threshold is met, the jury decides what weight to give the connection.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because conditional relevance does not demand certainty. B: B is wrong because the judge does not apply a clear-and-convincing standard to this conditional fact gate. C: C is wrong because a party admission is not required for conditional relevance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b)
