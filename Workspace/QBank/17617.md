Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal civil assault case, Esther heard a caller threaten her over the phone but did not know the caller then. Two weeks later, during a recorded mediation session, Esther heard Daniel speak for several minutes under circumstances clearly identifying him. At trial, Esther says the threatening caller had the same voice as Daniel. Daniel objects that Esther did not know his voice when the threat was made. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because voice identification may be based on hearing the voice at any time under circumstances connecting it to the speaker.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because voice familiarity must exist before the disputed call.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because familiarity acquired during litigation can never support voice identification.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the threatening call was recorded.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Voice identification may be based on hearing the voice at any time, so long as the circumstances connect the voice to the alleged speaker. Unlike nonexpert handwriting familiarity, voice familiarity is not limited to pre-litigation familiarity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because voice familiarity need not predate the disputed call. C: C is wrong because it imports the handwriting familiarity limit into voice identification. D: D is wrong because a recording of the threat is not required for Esther's voice-identification testimony.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(5)
lb_student_diagnostic: Handwriting-rule import: student wrongly applies the litigation-acquired handwriting familiarity restriction to voice identification.
