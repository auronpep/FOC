Question: A former tenant sues a landlord for failure to return a modest security deposit. The tenant asks for every email sent by every property employee over a ten-year period about all tenants in all buildings. The landlord offers to search emails for the tenant, the apartment, and the disputed deposit period. How should the court most likely handle the request?
Answer A: Compel the full ten-year request because any email about any tenant might reveal a pattern
Answer B: Deny all email discovery because security-deposit cases are too small for discovery
Answer C: Tailor the request to a narrower search that is relevant and proportional to the deposit dispute
Answer D: Compel the full request only if the landlord has more resources than the tenant
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 26 permits proportional limits rather than automatic full production or total denial.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Some pattern information may be relevant, but the full request is disproportionate to the case. B: Smaller cases can still justify focused discovery that is relevant and proportional. D: Resources are one factor, not the only proportionality test.
lb_rule_reference: Discovery must be proportional to the needs of the case, considering the issues, amount in controversy, access to information, resources, importance of discovery, and burden or expense.
