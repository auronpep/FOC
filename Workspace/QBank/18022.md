Outline: Judicial notice
Question: In a federal civil case, Paul argues that a federal regulation was in force on the date of the events. The judge consults the Code of Federal Regulations and determines the text of the applicable regulation. Paul then asks the judge to instruct the jury that it must accept the regulation's legal effect as a judicially noticed adjudicative fact. How should the judge respond?
Answer A: Give the instruction because every legal source consulted by the court is an adjudicative fact.
Answer B: Give the instruction only if the regulation is printed in a government publication.
Answer C: Refuse the Rule 201 instruction because determining governing law is the court's legal function, not judicial notice of an adjudicative fact for the jury.
Answer D: Submit the regulation's legal meaning to the jury because judicial notice never applies to legal materials.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 201 concerns judicial notice of adjudicative facts. Determining the content and legal effect of governing law is a judicial function, not a Rule 201 adjudicative-fact instruction for the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because law is not transformed into an adjudicative fact merely because the court consults it. B: B is wrong because the source's official status does not make the legal determination a Rule 201 jury fact. D: D is wrong because the judge, not the jury, determines governing law.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 201(a) / legal determinations versus adjudicative facts
