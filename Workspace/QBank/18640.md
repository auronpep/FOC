Outline: Burglary
Question: Police lawfully arrest a suspect for burglary and take his smartphone from his pocket. Without a warrant, an officer opens the phone's photo gallery and finds pictures of stolen property. The phone was secured and no emergency existed. Should the photos be suppressed?
Answer A: No, because search incident to arrest allows police to search everything found on an arrestee
Answer B: Yes, because officers generally need a warrant to search digital contents of a secured phone after arrest
Answer C: No, because a phone is identical to a wallet or cigarette pack
Answer D: Yes, but only if the burglary charge is later dismissed
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Police may seize and secure the phone, but opening its digital files without a warrant or emergency exceeds search-incident authority.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Digital contents of a phone receive special protection and generally require a warrant. C: Modern cell phones contain vast digital records and are not treated like ordinary pocket containers for search-incident purposes. D: The search's validity is assessed when it occurred, not by later dismissal.
lb_rule_reference: Search incident to arrest permits limited physical searches but generally does not authorize warrantless search of digital contents of a cell phone.
