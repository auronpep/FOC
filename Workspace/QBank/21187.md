Subtopic: Rights in Land
Outline: Creation
Question: Before speaking with Aaron, Miriam spent $18,000 improving a rough track across Aaron's land, believing Aaron would not object. After the work was complete, Miriam asked Aaron whether she could use the track. Aaron replied, “You may use it this year.” At the end of the year, Aaron revoked permission. Does Miriam have an easement by estoppel based on the $18,000 expenditure?
Answer A: No, because the expenditure occurred before Aaron's permission and therefore was not reliance on his promise.
Answer B: Yes, because any improvement made on another's land creates an easement.
Answer C: Yes, because Aaron's one-year permission retroactively made the earlier expenditure reasonable reliance.
Answer D: No, but only because easement by estoppel applies only to commercial land.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Estoppel requires reliance on the landowner's permission or representation. Miriam spent the money before Aaron made any promise. Aaron's later one-year permission did not cause the earlier expenditure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because improvements alone do not create an easement without permission-based reliance. C: C is wrong because later permission does not retroactively cause earlier reliance. D: D is wrong because estoppel is not limited to commercial land.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance must follow the permission or promise
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is spotting money spent but not checking whether the spending was induced by the owner's permission.
