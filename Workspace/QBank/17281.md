Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: “Necessary and Proper” Clause power
Question: Congress enacts a statute requiring all adults to take an annual civics course, explaining that the requirement is "necessary and proper to improve national political knowledge." The statute identifies no other enumerated power that the course requirement helps implement. What is the best objection?
Answer A: The Necessary and Proper Clause is not a standalone power; Congress must tie the law to another enumerated power
Answer B: The statute is valid because anything useful to national governance is necessary and proper
Answer C: The statute is valid because Congress has general police power over education
Answer D: The statute is invalid only if it discriminates based on viewpoint
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Necessary and proper supports the execution of other powers but does not create a general power to legislate for useful goals.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Utility is not enough without an enumerated power being carried into execution. C: Congress lacks general police power. D: Even without a speech defect, Congress still needs enumerated authority.
lb_rule_reference: The Necessary and Proper Clause helps Congress carry out other enumerated powers; it is not an independent standalone source of regulatory authority.
