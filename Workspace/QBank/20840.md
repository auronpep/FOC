Question: In a federal contract case, the plaintiff wants bank records from a bank that is not a party. The plaintiff serves interrogatories on the bank asking it to list all relevant transfers and produce supporting documents. The bank objects that it is not a party. What discovery device should the plaintiff generally use for the nonparty bank records?
Answer A: Interrogatories, because any person with relevant information must answer them
Answer B: A Rule 45 subpoena for the nonparty documents
Answer C: A renewed JMOL motion
Answer D: No discovery device, because nonparties are categorically immune from federal discovery
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 45 is the ordinary mechanism for compelling documents from a nonparty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Interrogatories are directed to parties, not nonparties. C: JMOL is a trial sufficiency motion, not a discovery device. D: Nonparties may be subject to subpoenas within the rule's limits.
lb_rule_reference: Interrogatories are served on parties; document discovery from a nonparty is generally obtained through a Rule 45 subpoena.
