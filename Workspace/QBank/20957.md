Question: A merchant seller and a merchant buyer orally agree to a sale of 600 jars of oil. The seller promptly sends a signed confirmation stating the quantity. The buyer receives it and, within 10 days, calls the seller and says, “I object to your confirmation,” but sends no written objection. The buyer later asserts the Statute of Frauds. What is the best result under the UCC merchant-confirmation rule?
Answer A: The oral objection preserves the buyer's Statute of Frauds defense because any timely objection is enough.
Answer B: The buyer's defense succeeds because the buyer did not sign the confirmation.
Answer C: The seller may use the confirmation against the buyer because the buyer did not give written notice of objection within 10 days.
Answer D: The confirmation is ineffective because it did not include every term of the oral contract.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The merchant-confirmation rule requires the recipient merchant to give written notice of objection within 10 days after receiving the confirmation. An oral objection is not enough to preserve the SOF defense under that rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the rule specifies a written objection. B: B is wrong because recipient signature is not required when the merchant-confirmation rule applies. D: D is wrong because a UCC SOF writing need not contain every term; quantity is the key term.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(2) — written objection within 10 days
