Question: Owen voluntarily drinks heavily and, during a sudden argument, pulls a knife and kills Blake. The prosecution charges first-degree murder, requiring premeditation and deliberation. The evidence of Owen's intoxication strongly supports that he could not reflect or plan, but he still intentionally stabbed Blake. What is the most accurate effect of the intoxication evidence?
Answer A: It may negate premeditation, but it does not automatically eliminate all homicide liability
Answer B: It requires complete acquittal because Owen was too drunk to be responsible
Answer C: It has no possible relevance because homicide is always general intent
Answer D: It creates an insanity defense if Owen does not remember the stabbing
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Premeditation and deliberation are heightened mental elements. Voluntary intoxication may be relevant to whether Owen formed them. But Owen still intentionally killed, so the evidence may reduce the degree rather than produce acquittal.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Voluntary intoxication is not a general excuse. It may negate specific elements, not erase all homicide liability. C: First-degree murder requires premeditation and deliberation in this jurisdiction. Those heightened elements can be affected by intoxication evidence. D: Later lack of memory is not insanity. The issue is whether intoxication negated premeditation at the time.
lb_rule_reference: Voluntary intoxication may negate premeditation and deliberation for first-degree murder, but it does not automatically acquit or negate malice murder
