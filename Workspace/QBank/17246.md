Outline: Responsibility
Question: Priya has a severe psychotic disorder. During an episode, she understands that taking a pharmacy's medication without paying is against the law in an abstract sense, but her delusion makes her believe the medication belongs to her and that she has a divine duty to take it immediately. Experts testify that the disorder left her without substantial capacity to appreciate the criminality of taking the medication. The jurisdiction follows the MPC/ALI insanity test. How should Priya's insanity claim be analyzed?
Answer A: It fails because she had some abstract awareness that theft is illegal
Answer B: It fails because planned conduct can never be insane
Answer C: It may succeed because the disorder left her without substantial capacity to appreciate criminality
Answer D: It succeeds automatically because any psychotic disorder satisfies the MPC test
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The MPC/ALI test is broader than pure M'Naghten. It asks whether, because of mental disease or defect, the defendant lacked substantial capacity to appreciate criminality or to conform conduct to law. The expert testimony ties Priya's disorder to a lack of substantial appreciation, so the claim is viable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The MPC test asks whether she lacked substantial capacity to appreciate criminality, not whether she had zero abstract knowledge. B: The MPC test focuses on substantial capacity to appreciate criminality or conform conduct. Some planning does not automatically defeat the defense. D: The diagnosis alone is not enough. The disease must cause lack of substantial capacity. The facts here support that link, but it is the link, not the label, that matters.
lb_rule_reference: Under the MPC/ALI test, a defendant is not responsible if mental disease causes lack of substantial capacity to appreciate criminality or conform conduct to law
