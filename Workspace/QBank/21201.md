Question: A state requires local restaurants to post sanitation grades. A restaurant challenges the law, arguing that the state has not identified a Commerce Clause basis for the regulation. What is the best response?
Answer A: The state does not need Commerce Clause authority because states have general police power subject to federal constitutional limits
Answer B: The state law is invalid because only Congress may regulate businesses engaged in commerce
Answer C: The state law is valid only if Congress gave the state permission
Answer D: The state law is invalid because sanitation is a national market issue
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Commerce Clause is a federal power source; states generally regulate health and safety under police power unless limited by federal law or rights.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: States regulate local businesses under police power, subject to limits like preemption and the Dormant Commerce Clause. C: States do not need congressional permission for ordinary health and safety laws unless federal law otherwise controls. D: National economic relevance does not strip states of police power.
lb_rule_reference: States possess general police power to regulate health, safety, welfare, and morals, subject to constitutional limits; Congress instead needs enumerated authority.
