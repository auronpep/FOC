Question: After her conviction for murder, a woman filed an application for a writ of habeas corpus. What does the woman have to show to succeed with her application?
Answer A: A: That there was an unlawful detention by a preponderance of the evidence.
Answer B: B: That there was an unlawful detention beyond a reasonable doubt.
Answer C: C: Clear and convincing evidence that there was an unlawful detention.
Answer D: D: Nothing, because the state has to provide clear and convincing evidence that the detention was lawful.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A prisoner may challenge the lawfulness of his or her detention through a writ of habeas corpus. The prisoner has the burden of proof in proving his or her claim. A writ of habeas corpus is civil in nature, so the prisoner must show that there is an unlawful detention by a preponderance of the evidence. Consequently, B, C, and D are incorrect.
