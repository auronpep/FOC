Question: A state university creates an outreach program for applicants who are first-generation college students, live in low-income census tracts, or attend high schools that historically send few graduates to college. Applicants of all races may qualify. University officials say the program may also increase racial diversity. Does the outreach program trigger strict scrutiny solely because officials discussed racial diversity?
Answer A: Yes, because any awareness of racial effects is a racial classification
Answer B: Yes, because education programs always use strict scrutiny
Answer C: No, because the program uses race-neutral criteria and is not alleged to have a discriminatory purpose
Answer D: No, because Equal Protection never applies to public universities
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: First-generation status, income geography, and school-sending patterns are not facial racial classifications; strict scrutiny is not triggered solely by diversity-related policy hopes.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Awareness of racial effects is not the same as classifying applicants by race. B: Education context does not itself select strict scrutiny. D: State university action is government action subject to constitutional limits.
lb_rule_reference: Race-neutral criteria do not trigger strict scrutiny merely because officials hope the policy will improve racial diversity, unless the policy classifies by race or is adopted with discriminatory purpose.
