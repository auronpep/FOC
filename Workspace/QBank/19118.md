Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: Martha sues "Alpha Delivery, Inc." for a crash. Within the Rule 4(m) period, related company "Alpha Logistics, LLC" receives the complaint and knows Martha meant to sue it because it operated the truck. Limitations later expires. Martha seeks to amend to name Alpha Logistics. May the amendment relate back?
Answer A: Yes, if the Rule 15(c) notice and mistake requirements are satisfied
Answer B: No, changing parties can never relate back
Answer C: Yes, because notice after judgment is enough
Answer D: No, because the same occurrence test applies only to plaintiffs
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The new party had timely notice and knew it was the intended defendant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Rule 15(c) includes a party-change route. C: Timely notice within the Rule 4(m) period matters. D: The same-occurrence requirement is part of party-change relation back too.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 15(c) party-change relation back requires same occurrence, Rule 4(m)-period notice, no prejudice, and mistake concerning identity.
