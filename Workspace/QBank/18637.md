Question: In a federal civil trial, Esther testifies for the plaintiff. The plaintiff then calls Aaron to testify that Esther is truthful because she strictly follows a religious teaching against lying. The defendant objects. The plaintiff offers the testimony only to support Esther's credibility. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because religious devotion is strong opinion evidence of truthful character.
Answer B: Overrule the objection if Aaron shares Esther's religious beliefs.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because religious beliefs or opinions may not be used to support or attack a witness's credibility.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Esther's truthfulness has not first been attacked.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 610 bars evidence of a witness's religious beliefs or opinions when offered to attack or support the witness's credibility. The testimony is offered only to support Esther's credibility from religious belief, so it is inadmissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because religious belief is not a permissible credibility-support route. B: B is wrong because the witness's shared belief does not avoid Rule 610. D: D is wrong because even if truthfulness had been attacked, religious belief remains an improper basis for credibility support.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 610 / religious belief not credibility opinion or reputation
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the bar on using religious belief as credibility character evidence.
