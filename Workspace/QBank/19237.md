Question: In a federal damages trial, Solomon prepares a one-page chart summarizing totals already admitted through five invoices. Solomon does not offer the chart as a Rule 1006 summary of voluminous records, but only to help his accountant explain the arithmetic. The opponent objects that the chart cannot be considered substantive evidence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the chart because all charts must satisfy Rule 1006.
Answer B: Allow the chart as an illustrative aid if accurate and helpful, while making clear that the invoices and testimony are the evidence.
Answer C: Admit the chart as conclusive proof of damages.
Answer D: Exclude the chart unless all five invoices are too voluminous to examine in court.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A chart can function as an illustrative aid even when it is not a Rule 1006 summary. The court may allow it to help the witness explain admitted evidence, while limiting its evidentiary effect.
