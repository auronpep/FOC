Question: Peter signed and delivered a deed stating, “For no consideration, I convey my cabin to Andrew.” The deed accurately described the cabin, and Andrew accepted. Peter later argued that the deed was void because it expressly stated that no consideration was given. Who owns the cabin?
Answer A: Peter, because the deed admitted that no consideration supported the transfer.
Answer B: Andrew, because a deed can convey title without consideration when the deed is otherwise valid and delivered.
Answer C: Peter, because the statement “for no consideration” makes the deed testamentary.
Answer D: Andrew only if he later gives Peter nominal consideration.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is execution and delivery, not consideration. Peter used present transfer words, signed the deed, delivered it, and Andrew accepted. The deed's express statement that no consideration was paid does not prevent title from passing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because lack of consideration does not void an otherwise valid delivered deed. C: C is wrong because the deed used present conveyance language and was delivered; it was not testamentary merely because it was gratuitous. D: D is wrong because later nominal payment is unnecessary.
lb_rule_reference: Express no-consideration deed can still convey title
