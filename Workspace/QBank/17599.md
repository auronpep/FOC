


BARMATRIX Q#: 17599
Outline_code: 61020402
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A man was snowmobiling with his friend as a passenger. The man negligently cut across a road and an oncoming car struck the snowmobile. The friend was severely injured. The driver of the car called an ambulance, and the ambulance picked up the friend. Because of the serious nature of the friend's injuries, the ambulance drove in excess of the speed limit and crashed into a pole. The friend was killed in the crash. The friend's estate sued the man. Assuming the estate may recover for the injuries sustained during the initial car crash, can it recover for the friend's death?
Answer A: A: No, because the ambulance broke the chain of liability.
Answer B: B: No, because the ambulance was negligent by speeding.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the ambulance's rescue attempt was foreseeable.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the death was preceded by the friend's serious injuries.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A negligent defendant is liable for additional damage caused by foreseeable rescue efforts. This is true even if these rescue efforts are negligent in themselves. When faced with a severally injured person, it is foreseeable that an ambulance driver would drive faster than the speed limit and negligently crash into a pole. Consequently, the man is also liable for his friend's death in the ambulance accident.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect because a foreseeable rescue does not break the chain of liability. B is incorrect because it was foreseeable that the ambulance might drive negligently and crash into a pole. D is incorrect because the simple fact the death happened after the friend's injuries would not make the man liable for the friend's death. Importantly, there is no connection between the accident injuries and the actual death.
