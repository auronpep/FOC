


BARMATRIX Q#: 22851
Outline_code: 97100300
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: A woman sued a car dealership for negligence in state court. She claimed that she had bought a new car from the car dealership and the car dealership had failed to put on the tires correctly. She argued that because of the car dealership's failure, one of the car's tires came off and caused her to crash into a telephone pole. After a trial, the jury found that the accident was actually caused by the woman texting on her phone. The woman then filed a breach of warranty suit against the car dealership in federal court. In the second suit, the woman claimed that the car dealership sold her a defective car because it had failed to put on the tires correctly, which led to her hitting a telephone pole. The car dealership filed a motion to dismiss. Should the court dismiss the woman's claim?
Answer A: A: No, because the first claim was made in state court.
Answer B: B: No, because negligence and breach of warranty were two different causes of action.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the two claims arose out of the same transaction or occurrence.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the first claim was decided by a jury.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Claim preclusion, also known as res judicata, bars a second claim if it is based on the same common core of operative facts as a prior claim. Frier v. City of Vandalia, 770 F.2d 699 (7th Cir. 1985). For claim preclusion to apply, a party has to show that (1) the prior judgment is a valid, final judgment on the merits; (2) the claim is brought by the same plaintiff against the same defendant; and (3) the same claim is involved in the second suit. Here, there was a valid, final judgment by a jury in the first claim. Second, the same woman is suing the same car dealership. Finally, both the negligence claim and the breach of warranty claim arise from the same transaction or occurrence (specifically, the tires coming off and causing an accident). Consequently, claim preclusion would bar the woman's second claim, and the court should dismiss it. A is incorrect because claim preclusion would still apply even if the first claim was made in state court. B is incorrect because the two claims are arising from the same transaction or occurrence. D is incorrect because claim preclusion only requires a valid, final judgment, not one specifically found by a jury.
