Question: The jurisdiction requires safe retreat before using deadly force outside the home. In a parking lot, Hale saw Reed approach with a knife from across the lot. Hale was standing next to his running car with the driver's door open and could have driven away safely. Instead, Hale shot and killed Reed. Does Hale have a complete self-defense justification?
Answer A: Yes, because Reed approached with a knife
Answer B: Yes, because the castle doctrine applies to any place Hale lawfully occupies
Answer C: No, because Hale could retreat safely and the jurisdiction requires retreat before deadly force
Answer D: No, because deadly force is never allowed against a knife
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Even though Reed's knife was a deadly threat, the supplied jurisdiction required safe retreat outside the home. Hale could have driven away safely, so complete self-defense fails.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The jurisdiction requires safe retreat outside the home. Hale had a safe avenue by driving away. B: The facts limit the exception to the home. A parking lot is not covered. D: A knife can justify deadly defensive force if retreat is not safely required and available. The retreat rule is decisive here.
lb_rule_reference: In a jurisdiction imposing a duty to retreat before deadly force, a defendant must retreat if safely possible unless an exception such as the home applies.
