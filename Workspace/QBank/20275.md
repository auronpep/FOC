Outline: Rule of completeness
Question: Mary sues Joseph for breach of contract. Mary introduces the first paragraph of a letter from Joseph admitting, “I failed to deliver the grain by the promised date.” Joseph asks the court to admit the final paragraph of the same letter, which says, “I have always tried to be generous to my neighbors.” Joseph argues that because Mary used part of the letter, the entire letter must be admitted. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the final paragraph because Rule 106 makes the whole letter admissible once any part is introduced.
Answer B: Admit the final paragraph only if Joseph proves that Mary acted in bad faith by offering the first paragraph.
Answer C: Exclude the final paragraph because it does not fairly explain, qualify, or correct the admitted paragraph.
Answer D: Exclude the final paragraph only because character evidence is never admissible in a contract case.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 106 does not create a right to introduce every remaining portion of a statement. It permits only those additional parts or related statements that fairness requires to be considered at the same time. A general statement about generosity does not correct or explain the admission that Joseph missed the delivery date.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because completeness is limited to parts needed for fairness, not the entire writing. B: B is wrong because bad faith is not required; the question is whether the omitted part is necessary to correct a misleading impression. D: D is wrong because the stronger reason is the lack of a completeness connection, not a categorical character rule.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 106 / unrelated remainder excluded
