Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: Moses owned a vineyard. Standing in the vineyard, Moses told Aaron, “I give you this vineyard right now.” Aaron thanked Moses and immediately began pruning the vines. No deed, memorandum, or other writing was signed by Moses. A week later, Moses denied that title had passed. Who owns legal title to the vineyard?
Answer A: Aaron, because Moses used present words of gift and Aaron took possession.
Answer B: Aaron, because consideration is not required for a deed.
Answer C: Moses, because a conveyance of legal title to land by deed requires a writing signed or otherwise authenticated by the grantor.
Answer D: Moses only until Aaron records the oral conveyance.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is the writing required for a land conveyance. Moses used present oral words, but no signed writing conveyed the vineyard. Aaron's possession and the absence of consideration do not replace the Statute of Frauds writing lock, so legal title remains with Moses.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because present oral intent and possession do not themselves pass legal title by deed. B: B is wrong because the no-consideration rule applies after a deed otherwise satisfies the writing and validity gates. D: D is wrong because an oral conveyance cannot be recorded to cure the missing deed writing.
