Question: After Isaac is indicted for robbery, police conduct an uncounseled in-person lineup. The lineup ID is suppressed. At trial, the victim testifies that during the robbery she stood face-to-face with the robber for five minutes in a bright lobby and gave police a detailed description before the lineup. The prosecutor asks the victim to identify the robber in court. Should the in-court identification be permitted?
Answer A: Yes, because the victim's strong original observation provides an independent source for the in-court identification
Answer B: No, because suppression of a lineup identification automatically suppresses all later identifications
Answer C: No, because in-court identifications are inherently suggestive and therefore always excluded
Answer D: Yes, because the lineup violation was harmless once the victim testified live
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The lineup ID is suppressed, but the victim's direct, prolonged, well-lit observation and prior description support an independent basis for identifying Isaac in court.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: An in-court identification may survive if it rests on a source independent of the tainted lineup. C: The relevant question after a tainted lineup is independent source, not automatic exclusion of all courtroom identifications. D: Live testimony does not automatically cure taint. The reason this ID may be admitted is independent source.
lb_rule_reference: After an unconstitutional lineup, an in-court identification may be admitted if the prosecution proves an independent source for the witness's ability to identify the defendant.
