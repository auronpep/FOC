Question: Daniel hired Sarah to coordinate security for a museum opening. The signed services agreement required Sarah to provide “security coordination for evening events,” but it did not define the phrase. After several items were stolen during the opening, Daniel claimed the phrase required Sarah to arrange licensed off-duty guards, while Sarah claimed it required only volunteer scheduling. Daniel wants to strengthen his Content/Meaning argument. Which additional fact best supports Daniel's interpretation of the security-coordination term?
Answer A: Before signing, Sarah failed to disclose that she had never personally hired off-duty guards for a museum event.
Answer B: The signed agreement stated that Sarah's attached proposal was incorporated, and the proposal defined security coordination as arranging licensed off-duty guards.
Answer C: The signed agreement contained a merger clause and therefore must be interpreted from its four corners alone.
Answer D: The contract was not signed by the off-duty guards who would have worked at the event.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that material incorporated by reference is part of the agreement itself and may define an otherwise ambiguous services term. The trigger facts are the undefined phrase “security coordination” and the incorporated proposal. The decisive locks are term status and offered purpose: Daniel is using the proposal to define a contract term, not merely to prove breach or damages. B is the strongest meaning-source fact because it makes the guard requirement part of the written content.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Sarah's experience may support a different defense or performance argument, but it does not directly define the disputed term. C: C is wrong because it states a legal conclusion rather than a supporting fact and overreads the merger clause. D: D is wrong because the off-duty guards were potential performers, not parties whose signatures are needed to interpret Daniel and Sarah's agreement.
