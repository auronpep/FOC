Question: A diversity plaintiff brings a state-law defamation claim. The plaintiff dies while the case is pending. The forum state's substantive law says defamation claims do not survive the plaintiff's death. The estate asks the federal court to continue the claim under general federal procedure. What should the federal court do?
Answer A: Apply state survival law because it determines whether the state-created claim continues to exist
Answer B: Always continue the claim because federal courts prefer merits decisions
Answer C: Dismiss only if diversity is destroyed by the death
Answer D: Use the defendant's home-state survival rule automatically
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Survival is tied to the substantive state right.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Federal policy preference does not create a surviving state claim. C: The issue is survival of the claim, not citizenship. D: Klaxon and forum-state choice principles govern, not defendant residence alone.
lb_rule_reference: Federal diversity courts apply state law defining the existence, scope, and survival of state-created claims.
