Question: A plaintiff files a complaint asserting a legal theory that is rejected by every controlling case. The complaint does not acknowledge those cases or make any argument to extend, modify, or reverse existing law. The defendant seeks Rule 11 sanctions after following the required procedure. Which point best supports sanctions?
Answer A: Rule 11 sanctions are proper whenever the filing party eventually loses
Answer B: The legal contention lacked support in existing law and did not make a nonfrivolous argument to change the law
Answer C: Sanctions are unavailable because Rule 11 applies only to discovery requests
Answer D: Sanctions are mandatory because any rejected legal theory is frivolous
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Legal contentions need a reasonable legal basis or a nonfrivolous law-change argument.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Losing is not enough; Rule 11 asks about the certification made after reasonable inquiry. C: Rule 11 generally does not govern discovery requests, but it does apply to court papers such as complaints. D: A rejected theory can still be nonfrivolous if reasonably grounded in law or a good-faith law-change argument.
lb_rule_reference: By presenting a paper to the court, an attorney certifies after reasonable inquiry that legal contentions are warranted by existing law or by a nonfrivolous argument to change the law.
