Question: In a federal contract trial, Isaac testifies for Rachel. On cross-examination, the defendant shows that Isaac is Rachel's business partner and will receive part of any recovery. Rachel then calls Deborah to testify that Isaac has a reputation for truthfulness. The defendant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because any attack on credibility permits truthful-character rehabilitation.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because showing Isaac's financial bias does not itself attack Isaac's character for truthfulness.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because bias and truthfulness are the same credibility trait.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Isaac admitted the partnership on cross-examination.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A showing of bias attacks the witness's motive or interest, not the witness's general character for truthfulness. Truthful-character rehabilitation by reputation or opinion is allowed only after an attack on character for truthfulness, not after every credibility attack.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because credibility attacks are not all truthfulness-character attacks. C: C is wrong because bias and truthfulness are distinct credibility theories. D: D is wrong because the admissibility of rehabilitation does not turn on whether Isaac admitted the bias fact.
lb_rule_reference: Bias impeachment does not open truthful-character support
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the boundary between E3 bias and D6 truthfulness rehabilitation.
