


BARMATRIX Q#: 22677
Outline_code: 42010100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Legislative Branch
Question: After negotiation, the President of the United States and the President of a foreign country reached an executive agreement. Under the terms of the agreement, profits earned by the country's corporations in the United States would not be subject to taxation in the United States, and profits earned by United States corporations in the country would not be subject to taxation in the country. The plaintiff is a corporation from the foreign country operating in the state. A state statute imposes a tax on all income earned within the state and designates the State Revenue Service as the agency that collects it. The State Revenue Service demands that the plaintiff pay income tax as required by state law. The plaintiff challenged the state law as unconstitutional. What is the plaintiff's best argument?
Answer A: A: The law is invalid because no state may tax imports or exports without the consent of Congress.
Answer B: B: The law is invalid under the Supremacy Clause.
Answer C: C: The law is invalid under the Necessary and Proper Clause.
Answer D: D: The law is invalid because the power to tax is the power to destroy.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the Supremacy Clause of Article VI of the United States Constitution, the statutes and treaties of the United States are the supreme law of the land. This means that a state law that is inconsistent with any valid federal law or treaty is invalid. Although an executive agreement is not a treaty, it has the same effect as a federal law under the Supremacy Clause. Since the application of the state income tax law is inconsistent with the executive agreement that prohibits the taxation of the foreign country's corporations within the United States, the Supremacy Clause may make the state law invalid as applied to the plaintiff. Although the United States Constitution prohibits the states from taxing imports or exports without congressional consent, A is incorrect because the state income tax is not a tax on imports or exports. The Necessary and Proper Clause gives Congress the power to do whatever is necessary and proper in carrying out its other powers. C is incorrect because the constitutionality of an act of Congress is not in question. It has been said that states are prevented from taxing the federal government because “the power to tax is the power to destroy.” This argument is not applicable in this case because the state is making no attempt to tax the federal government. D is therefore incorrect.
