Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Foreseeable plaintiffs
Question: A station employee negligently shoved a boarding passenger who was carrying a bulky crate. The crate fell toward people standing immediately beside the boarding area. One nearby commuter jumped back to avoid being struck and fell on the platform. Is the commuter likely within the class of foreseeable plaintiffs?
Answer A: Yes, because people standing immediately beside the shove were foreseeably endangered by the falling crate
Answer B: No, because Palsgraf means only the person directly touched by the defendant can sue
Answer C: No, because the commuter's fall occurred after the crate dropped
Answer D: Yes, but only because the employee intended to injure the commuter
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Unlike a remote plaintiff, the nearby commuter was physically in the apparent risk zone created by shoving a passenger with a bulky crate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The foreseeable-plaintiff limit is not a direct-contact requirement. Nearby persons in the risk zone can be foreseeable. C: A foreseeable reaction by a nearby person to avoid physical impact does not place the commuter outside the risk zone. D: Negligence duty does not require intent to injure.
lb_rule_reference: A plaintiff physically near the negligent act and within the apparent zone of physical risk is more likely a foreseeable plaintiff.
