Question: At midnight, Owen uses a copied key to enter a hotel room rented by a traveling nurse who is staying there for the week. Owen enters intending to steal her laptop from the desk. Can the hotel room satisfy the dwelling-of-another element for common-law burglary?
Answer A: No, because a hotel is a commercial building rather than a private house
Answer B: No, because a temporary occupant can never have a dwelling interest
Answer C: Yes, because the rented hotel room was occupied as the nurse's nighttime lodging
Answer D: Yes, but only if Owen also intended to assault the nurse
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The room was the nurse's place of habitation during the stay. Owen entered at night intending theft, so the dwelling element is satisfied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The rented room is being used as the nurse's lodging and can be treated as her dwelling for burglary purposes. B: Dwelling status can include temporary lodging used for sleeping. D: Intent to commit theft, a felony, can satisfy the intent element. Intent to assault is not required.
lb_rule_reference: A hotel room used as a person's nighttime lodging can be a dwelling of another for burglary purposes.
