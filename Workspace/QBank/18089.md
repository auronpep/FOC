Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Leah contracted with Matthew to provide music for a festival for a flat $40,000 payment to Leah. Before Matthew breached, Leah spent $20,000 preparing for the festival. Matthew proves that, if he had performed, Leah would have had to spend another $30,000 to complete her performance. If Leah seeks reliance damages, what is the proper amount?
Answer A: $20,000, because reliance damages always equal expenditures already made.
Answer B: $40,000, because that was the contract price.
Answer C: $10,000, because Leah's $20,000 reliance is reduced by the $10,000 loss she would have suffered on the contract.
Answer D: Nothing, because a losing contract always eliminates every remedy.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: If the breaching party proves the injured party would have lost money had the contract been performed, that loss reduces reliance damages. Leah's total cost would have been $50,000 on a $40,000 contract, a $10,000 loss. Her $20,000 reliance recovery is reduced to $10,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores the proved losing-contract offset. B: B is wrong because contract price is not reliance damages. D: D is wrong because the losing contract reduces reliance; it does not automatically eliminate all recovery if expenditures exceed the proved loss.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance damages / losing-event offset
