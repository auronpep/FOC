Question: Esther privately met with Deborah, a licensed clinical social worker, for trauma counseling. In a later federal civil case, the opposing party subpoenas Deborah to recount Esther's counseling statements. Esther objects on psychotherapist-patient privilege grounds. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection, because the privilege applies only to psychiatrists with medical degrees.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because social workers are never covered by federal privilege law.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if Deborah prescribed medication.
Answer D: Sustain the objection, because confidential therapy communications with a licensed clinical social worker fall within the federal psychotherapist-patient privilege.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal psychotherapist-patient privilege extends to confidential treatment communications with covered mental-health professionals, including licensed clinical social workers. The decisive axis is therapeutic role and confidentiality, not medical-degree status.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This overnarrows the protected professional category. B: B is wrong because This ignores the recognized federal scope for licensed clinical social workers in therapy. C: C is wrong because Medication authority is not the privilege trigger; confidential psychotherapy is.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / licensed social worker treatment communication
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is limiting the privilege to physicians rather than asking whether the professional was functioning as a protected therapist.
