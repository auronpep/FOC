


BARMATRIX Q#: 14106
Outline_code: 92080201
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law
Question: A caterer filed a diversity action against a doctor in federal court in State A. The caterer alleged that the doctor failed to pay $85,000 due on a valid service contract from a recent party that the doctor hosted. The doctor did not dispute the existence of the contract between the parties. At a jury trial, the doctor testified that he paid the $85,000 debt. The caterer testified that the doctor never paid the debt for the service contract, and produced a receipt showing that the $85,000 payment to the caterer was for a boat the caterer sold to the doctor. At the close of the trial, the caterer filed a motion for judgment as a matter of law. Is the court likely to grant the caterer's motion for judgment as a matter of law?
Answer A: A: Yes, because there is no genuine issue of material fact.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the judge should credit the caterer's testimony over the doctor's.
Answer C: C: Yes, because a jury verdict for the doctor would be a gross miscarriage of justice.
Answer D: D: Yes, because a reasonable jury would not have a legally sufficient basis to find for the doctor.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: D is correct. A judgment as a matter of law is a motion made by a party, during a trial, claiming that the opposing party has insufficient evidence to reasonably support its case. The facts support a motion for judgment as a matter of law because the doctor has not provided any evidence to prove that he has paid the caterer. Therefore, it should be granted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. This answer states the standard for granting the pre-trial summary judgment motion. B is incorrect. This answer states what is known as the thirteenth juror standard and is not what a judge is supposed to ask. Such an approach grants insufficient deference to the jury. C is incorrect. This answer does not state the standard for a motion for judgment as a matter of law. Instead, it implicates something closer to the standard for a motion for a new trial.
