Question: Moses contracted with Joseph to buy Joseph's cart business. Joseph was required to give Moses a written inventory list before closing. Moses accepted the business without the list and signed a closing statement saying, “Inventory list waived.” The contract also required Moses to pay Joseph's daughter Rachel $3,000. Moses later refused to pay Rachel, arguing that Joseph never gave the inventory list. May Rachel enforce the payment promise?
Answer A: Yes, because Moses waived the inventory-list condition before refusing to pay Rachel.
Answer B: No, because Rachel cannot enforce unless Joseph delivered every document listed in the contract.
Answer C: No, because Moses may assert even a waived condition against a beneficiary.
Answer D: Yes, but only if Joseph assigned his claim to Rachel.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A promisor may assert valid contract defenses against a beneficiary, but Moses waived the inventory-list condition at closing. Having waived that condition, Moses cannot use it later to defeat Rachel's beneficiary claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because strict compliance with a waived condition is not required. C: C is wrong because a waived defense is not available merely because the claimant is a beneficiary. D: D is wrong because Rachel enforces as an intended beneficiary, not as Joseph's assignee.
