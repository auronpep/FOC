Question: On a weekend from home, a student posts a vulgar complaint about not making the varsity team. The post does not threaten anyone, target another student, or cause disruption at school. The school suspends her from all extracurricular activities solely because administrators dislike the tone. What is the strongest First Amendment objection?
Answer A: Schools have unlimited authority over all off-campus student speech
Answer B: Schools have no authority over any off-campus student speech
Answer C: The discipline is suspect because the off-campus post caused no material disruption or rights invasion
Answer D: The discipline is valid because vulgarity is always unprotected
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Off-campus vulgar criticism without disruption or targeting is strongly protected.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: School authority is weaker for off-campus speech and requires more than dislike of tone. B: Serious threats, harassment, or material school disruption may permit regulation. D: Vulgarity alone does not make off-campus student speech categorically unprotected.
lb_rule_reference: Off-campus student speech receives substantial protection, but schools may have authority when speech targets the school community and foreseeably causes material disruption or invades rights.
