Outline: Personal knowledge
Question: In a federal trespass trial, Samuel testifies that Deborah called him and said, “Moses is in the warehouse.” Samuel did not see whether Moses was actually in the warehouse. The owner offers the testimony only to explain why Samuel immediately went to the warehouse and found a broken lock. Moses objects that Samuel lacks personal knowledge that Moses was there. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony for the limited purpose of showing why Samuel went to the warehouse, because Samuel has personal knowledge that Deborah made the statement.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because Samuel lacks personal knowledge that Moses was in the warehouse.
Answer C: Admit the testimony as proof that Moses was in the warehouse when Deborah called.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony unless Deborah is called and repeats the statement in court.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Samuel has personal knowledge that he heard Deborah make the statement. If the statement is offered only to show its effect on Samuel and explain his conduct, not to prove that Moses was actually in the warehouse, Samuel's lack of personal knowledge of Moses's location does not bar the limited use. A limiting instruction may be appropriate on request.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it treats the limited nontruth purpose as if the statement were offered to prove Moses's location. C: C is wrong because Samuel may not use the statement to prove the truth of Moses's location without a hearsay route. D: D is wrong because Deborah need not testify when the statement is admitted only for Samuel's listener-effect purpose.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 and 801 / personal knowledge of statement made / limited nontruth use
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests statement-purpose routing: personal knowledge of the words is enough for effect on listener, but not for truth of the matter asserted.
