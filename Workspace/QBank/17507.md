Subtopic: Defenses to Enforceability
Outline: Mistake / Lack of Mutual Assent
Question: Moses buys a collection of handmade lamps from Aaron after both parties read an article predicting that lamps from that region would soon triple in value. Six months later, the market for the lamps collapses, and the collection is worth much less than Moses paid. Moses seeks rescission, arguing that both parties were mistaken about the value of the lamps. Which statement is correct?
Answer A: Moses may not rescind merely because both parties were mistaken in a prediction about future market value.
Answer B: Moses may rescind because every large change in value is a mutual mistake.
Answer C: Moses may rescind only if Aaron also lost money on the sale.
Answer D: Moses may rescind because a buyer never assumes market-price risk.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Mutual mistake concerns an erroneous belief about a fact existing at the time of contracting, not a failed prediction about future market value. A later market collapse is value disappointment and ordinary bargain risk, not a mutual mistake about a basic existing assumption.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because a change in value does not itself establish mistake about an existing fact. C: C is wrong because the seller's profit or loss is not the test. D: D is wrong because buyers often assume market-price risk absent a special risk allocation.
lb_rule_reference: Future value prediction / no mistake defense
