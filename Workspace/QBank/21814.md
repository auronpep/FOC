Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Foreseeable plaintiffs
Question: A grocery store employee negligently left a clear puddle of oil in the main entrance aisle for twenty minutes after seeing it. A customer entering the store slipped. Because of a rare bone condition, the customer suffered multiple fractures from a fall that would have bruised most people. The customer sues the store for negligence, and the store argues that no duty was owed because the severe fractures were not foreseeable. Is the customer likely to establish duty in the negligence claim against the store?
Answer A: No, because the store could not foresee the customer's rare bone condition
Answer B: Yes, because an entering customer was a foreseeable plaintiff endangered by the oily entrance aisle
Answer C: No, because foreseeability of injury severity is always required before a negligence duty arises
Answer D: Yes, because the store is automatically liable for all injuries from any condition it knows about
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Before the accident, a store customer entering through the main aisle was plainly within the class endangered by an unattended oil puddle, so duty exists even though the injury was unusually severe.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The customer was foreseeably within the slip risk created by oil in the entrance aisle. The unusual extent of injury does not defeat duty to a foreseeable entrant. C: Duty does not require foreseeability of the exact severity of harm. It asks whether this plaintiff or plaintiff class was foreseeably endangered. D: The claim is negligence, not strict liability. Duty exists here, but the plaintiff still must prove breach, causation, and damages.
lb_rule_reference: A foreseeable plaintiff remains within the duty field even if the extent of the eventual harm is unexpectedly severe.
