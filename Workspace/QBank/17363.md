Question: Police seized Nia's phone under a valid warrant but could not unlock it. A court order compelled Nia to orally state the memorized passcode. The government did not already know the passcode and offered no immunity. Nia objected under the Fifth Amendment privilege. What is Nia's strongest argument?
Answer A: The order compels testimonial communication because stating a memorized passcode reveals the contents of Nia's mind
Answer B: The order violates the privilege because all physical evidence from a suspect is testimonial
Answer C: The order is valid because warrants override the Fifth Amendment privilege
Answer D: The privilege cannot apply because Nia was not being interrogated in custody
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The order requires Nia to communicate knowledge. That differs from providing a physical characteristic and can be protected testimonial compulsion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The rule is not that all evidence is testimonial. The passcode is different because stating it communicates knowledge. C: A search warrant may authorize seizure/search of the phone, but it does not automatically compel testimonial disclosure without privilege analysis. D: The privilege against compelled testimonial self-incrimination can apply outside custodial interrogation.
lb_rule_reference: The Fifth Amendment privilege protects compelled testimonial incrimination; revealing the contents of one's mind, such as a memorized passcode, can be testimonial.
