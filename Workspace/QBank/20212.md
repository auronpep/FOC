Question: Leah hired Samuel to deliver meals to Leah's grandfather, Benjamin, for six months. The written contract identified Benjamin by name and required Samuel to deliver the meals directly to him. Samuel stopped delivering after two months and argued that Benjamin could not sue because Benjamin was not the promisee. Before any defenses are considered, what is Benjamin's correct route?
Answer A: Benjamin has no route because only Leah, the promisee, may sue.
Answer B: Benjamin is an assignee because Leah's contract mentioned him by name.
Answer C: Benjamin is an intended third-party beneficiary because the contract required direct performance to him.
Answer D: Benjamin is only an incidental beneficiary because the contract also benefited Leah.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Benjamin's correct route is intended third-party beneficiary. The contract identified him and required Samuel to perform directly to him. That route must be classified before considering any separate defenses Samuel may have.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because intended beneficiaries can enforce. B: B is wrong because naming a person to receive performance is not an assignment. D: D is wrong because a contract may benefit the promisee while also intending direct performance to the third party.
