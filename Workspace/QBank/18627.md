Outline: Truthfulness
Question: In a federal criminal trial, John testifies that, in his opinion, Matthew is a truthful person. On cross-examination, the prosecutor asks John, “Did you know that Matthew was dismissed from a bookkeeping job after admitting he falsified invoices?” The prosecutor has a good-faith basis for the question. Matthew objects that the question refers to a specific incident. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a character witness may never be asked about specific conduct.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless the prosecutor first proves the dismissal with employment records.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the question may test John's basis for his opinion about Matthew's truthfulness.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Matthew has testified in his own defense.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When a witness testifies about another witness's character for truthfulness, Rule 608(b) permits the court to allow cross-examination about specific acts probative of that character. The question tests John's basis for his opinion; the prosecutor may not prove the incident extrinsically if the sole purpose is truthfulness character.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because specific conduct may be inquired into on cross in this limited setting. B: B is wrong because extrinsic proof is not the proper prerequisite. D: D is wrong because the rule concerns the character witness's testimony, not whether Matthew personally testified.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(b) / cross of character witness about truthfulness basis
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests basis-cross of a truthfulness character witness and distinguishes inquiry from proof of the incident.
