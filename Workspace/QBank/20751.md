MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Res ipsa loquitur
Question: A customer bought a sealed glass jar from a store. Before opening it, the customer dropped the jar twice while carrying it home. Later, the jar shattered while the customer was twisting the lid. The customer sues the store and relies only on res ipsa loquitur. Is res ipsa likely strong against the store?
Answer A: Yes, because glass jars do not shatter without negligence
Answer B: Yes, because res ipsa eliminates all causation questions
Answer C: No, because the customer's own handling may have caused the shattering
Answer D: No, because res ipsa applies only to medical malpractice
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The customer's drops make it hard to infer that the jar probably shattered due to the store's negligence rather than plaintiff-caused damage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The plaintiff's own drops provide a plausible cause, so the circumstances do not cleanly point to the store's negligence. B: Res ipsa is not automatic liability and does not erase plaintiff contribution or causation issues. D: Res ipsa can apply outside medicine. It fails here because plaintiff contribution clouds the inference.
lb_rule_reference: Res ipsa is weakened or unavailable when the plaintiff's own handling may have caused the accident.
