Question: Naomi contracted to sell a house lot to Lydia. The contract stated, “At closing, Naomi will grant Lydia an easement over Naomi's retained driveway.” At closing, Naomi delivered a deed for the house lot, but the deed did not mention the driveway, and no separate easement deed was signed or delivered. Lydia later tried to use the driveway, claiming the contract itself had already created the easement. What is the best answer?
Answer A: Lydia has an express easement because any land-sale contract automatically conveys every promised property right.
Answer B: Lydia has fee title to the driveway because the contract mentioned the driveway.
Answer C: Lydia has a prescriptive easement because she expected to use the driveway after closing.
Answer D: The contract may support a contract or equitable remedy, but it did not itself create a present express easement absent an operative grant or delivery of the easement right.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A promise to grant an easement later is not the same as a present conveyance of the easement. If the closing documents omit the promised easement and no separate easement grant is delivered, the buyer may have a contract or equitable claim, but the contract language alone does not automatically create the express easement as a property interest.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a future promise is not automatically a present conveyance. B: B is wrong because mentioning a driveway in a contract does not convey fee title. C: C is wrong because expectation of use is not adverse use for the prescriptive period.
lb_rule_reference: Contract-only promise / no present grant
