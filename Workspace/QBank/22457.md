


BARMATRIX Q#: 22457
subject_display: Contracts
topic: Performance, Breach, and Discharge
subtopic: Modification of contracts: preexisting duties
difficulty: 56050200
Stem / Fact Pattern: The defendant, a manufacturer of widgets, entered into a valid written contract that called for the sale of 2,000 widgets to the plaintiff at a price of $10 per widget. Several weeks prior to the date set for delivery, the defendant telephoned the plaintiff and advised her that because of difficulty in locating a sufficient supply of frammis rods, the defendant would be unable to deliver more than 1,000 widgets. After discussion, the plaintiff agreed to accept 1,000 widgets at $10 per widget instead of 2,000 as originally agreed. After the defendant delivered 1,000 widgets, however, the plaintiff brought an action against him for damages resulting from breach of contract. Is the plaintiff entitled to damages for breach of contract?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the plaintiff’s agreement to accept 1,000 widgets was not in writing.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the plaintiff’s promise to accept 1,000 widgets was not supported by consideration.
Answer C: C: No, because the plaintiff agreed to accept 1,000 widgets.
Answer D: D: No, because there has been an accord and satisfaction.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: UCC §2- 209(3) provides that an agreement to modify a contract must be in writing if the contract as modified is within the provisions of the Statute of Frauds. Since the contract as modified calls for the sale of 1,000 widgets at $10 per widget for a total price of $10,000, and since the Statute of Frauds requires a contract for the sale of goods with a price of $500 or more to be in writing, the contract as modified falls within the provisions of the Statute of Frauds. Since it is not in writing, it is not enforceable over the objection of the plaintiff. C is therefore incorrect. B is incorrect because UCC §2- 209(1) provides that an agreement to modify an existing contract may be enforceable without consideration. An accord is an agreement to substitute a new obligation for an existing one. To be enforceable, an accord, like any other agreement, must be supported by consideration. Since consideration is a detriment suffered in exchange for a benefit received, and since the defendant suffered no detriment in return for the reduction in his obligation, the agreement to accept 1,000 widgets instead of 2,000 as originally required by the contract is unsupported by consideration. D is therefore incorrect.
