Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A company buys used treadmills, rebuilds them, and sells them to gyms as part of its regular business. During refurbishment, the company installs an incorrect safety key assembly that fails to stop a treadmill when a runner falls. A gym member is injured and sues the refurbishing company in strict products liability. What is the refurbisher's weakest defense?
Answer A: It is not covered because the treadmill was originally used before refurbishment
Answer B: The plaintiff must prove the safety key assembly was defective and caused the injury
Answer C: The plaintiff must show tort-type harm rather than only disappointed economic expectations
Answer D: The refurbisher may dispute whether the product was defective when it left its control
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A business that regularly rebuilds and sells products can be a commercial supplier; used status alone is not a complete defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: as the weakest defense. Defect and causation are genuine elements. C: as the weakest defense. The member has physical injury. D: as the weakest defense. Timing and defect are valid issues.
lb_rule_reference: A business that regularly refurbishes and sells products can be treated as a commercial supplier when its refurbished product is defective.
