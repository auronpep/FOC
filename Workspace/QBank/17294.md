Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: A suspect operated a methamphetamine lab inside a barn located 60 yards from his house. A chest-high fence surrounded the house and yard but did not extend to the barn. The barn doors faced away from the house, and the suspect never took steps to shield the barn from outside view. DEA agents walked to a position adjacent to the barn and, without entering, looked through a small window and observed the lab. The suspect moves to suppress. Should the court grant the motion to suppress?
Answer A: Yes, because the barn is within the property boundary, making it part of the curtilage
Answer B: Yes, because the agents physically approached the barn to conduct their observation
Answer C: No, because under the Dunn factors the barn is not within the curtilage and the agents' observation was not a Fourth Amendment search
Answer D: No, because the agents had a valid warrant to observe drug activity on the property
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: United States v. Dunn instructs courts to consider (1) the proximity of the area to the house (60 yards — not close), (2) whether the area is within a common enclosure (no — the fence stopped at the house and yard), (3) the nature of its use (not domestically related), and (4) steps taken to conceal it (none). All four factors indicate the barn is outside the curtilage. Because it falls in the open fields category, the agents' walk-up observation did not constitute a Fourth Amendment search and suppression is denied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Curtilage is not coextensive with property ownership. Under Dunn, the four factors — not the property line — determine whether an area receives curtilage protection. Here, the barn was outside the enclosed fence, 60 yards from the house, oriented away from the home, and unshielded. Applying Dunn, the barn falls outside the curtilage. B: Jardines applies to the home and its curtilage. Because the barn is not curtilage under Dunn, agents were free to approach it and observe what was visible through the window without triggering the Fourth Amendment. D: The facts state no warrant. More importantly, the correct reason suppression fails is that no search occurred — not that an exception applies. Choosing this answer imports a false fact.
lb_rule_reference: Curtilage is determined by four Dunn factors: proximity to the home, enclosure within the home's fencing, use of the area, and steps taken to conceal it from observation (United States v. Dunn)
