Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: In a federal civil case, Isaac must prove that a shipment arrived after April 1. The court takes judicial notice that April 1 fell on a Monday. Isaac then offers testimony that the shipment arrived “the following Friday.” Isaac argues that judicial notice shifts the burden to Rebecca to prove the shipment arrived on time. How should the court respond?
Answer A: The court's notice of the calendar fact shifts the burden of persuasion to Rebecca.
Answer B: The judicially noticed calendar fact may help the jury understand the timing, but Isaac still bears the burden of persuasion on late arrival.
Answer C: Rebecca must disprove the calendar because all noticed facts shift the burden to the opponent.
Answer D: The jury must disregard the testimony because judicial notice and witness testimony cannot be combined.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Judicial notice of a calendar fact does not shift the burden of persuasion on the disputed issue of late arrival. The noticed fact may supply context, but Isaac still must persuade the jury that the shipment arrived late.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because judicial notice of a background fact does not transfer the burden of persuasion. C: C is wrong because Rebecca need not disprove the noticed calendar fact to defeat the late-arrival claim. D: D is wrong because judicial notice and witness testimony may be used together when each is otherwise proper.
lb_rule_reference: Burden of persuasion; judicial notice decoy
