Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Micah contracted to produce a training course for Abigail for $30,000. Abigail repudiated after Micah reasonably spent $12,000 preparing course materials. Micah cannot prove lost profits with reasonable certainty and seeks reliance damages. Abigail argues that the contract might have been unprofitable, but offers no evidence of Micah's remaining costs or likely loss. What recovery is most appropriate?
Answer A: Nothing, because possible unprofitability defeats all reliance damages.
Answer B: $12,000, because Abigail has not proved that Micah would have suffered a loss on the contract.
Answer C: $30,000, because the contract price is always the minimum reliance award.
Answer D: An amount chosen by the jury without regard to the expenditures proved.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A reliance award may be reduced by a loss the defendant proves the plaintiff would have suffered had the contract been performed. But a speculative assertion that the contract might have been losing is not enough. Micah may recover the $12,000 reliance expenditure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because possible loss is not the same as proved loss. C: C is wrong because reliance damages do not default to the contract price. D: D is wrong because reliance requires proof of expenditures and proper offsets.
lb_rule_reference: Losing-contract offset requires proof
