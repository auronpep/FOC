Outline: Psychotherapist-patient
Question: Rebekah sues a city in federal court. The city shows that Rebekah's confidential therapy notes with Dr. Luke may be the most probative evidence about her memory of the incident. Rebekah has not placed her mental condition at issue; she seeks only physical-injury damages. She asserts psychotherapist-patient privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection, because the notes are highly probative and unavailable from any other source.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because privilege always yields to a defendant's need for impeachment evidence.
Answer C: Sustain the objection, because the federal psychotherapist privilege is not defeated by an ad hoc balancing of need against privacy on these facts.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if the notes contain no statements about the incident.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The recognized federal psychotherapist-patient privilege is not subject to case-by-case need balancing merely because the evidence would be useful. No issue-tender waiver is stated. The decisive axis is privilege protection versus relevance/need override.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is the need override trap. Probative value does not by itself defeat a valid privilege. B: B is wrong because Impeachment value does not automatically override privilege. D: D is wrong because The privilege can protect communications even if they concern the incident; the issue is absence of waiver or exception.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / Jaffee no ad hoc need override
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is using relevance and evidentiary need as if they were exceptions to a valid psychotherapist privilege.
