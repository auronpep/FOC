Question: Leah sues Jonah for breach of a contract. Leah was on a three-way phone call during which Jonah said, “I accept the new delivery date.” Leah later learned that the call had also been recorded by another participant. At trial, Leah testifies from memory about what Jonah said. Jonah objects that the recording is the best evidence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because any recorded conversation must be proved by the recording.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Leah produces the original recording or accounts for its absence.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Leah is testifying to a conversation she personally heard, not proving the contents of the recording.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the recording is unavailable through no fault of Leah.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The original-writing rule applies when a party seeks to prove the contents of a recording. Leah is testifying from personal knowledge about the conversation itself. The existence of a recording does not bar her testimony.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a recorded event may still be proved by a witness with personal knowledge of the event. B: B is wrong because Leah is not proving the recording's contents. D: D is wrong because Leah need not show unavailability when the original-writing rule is not triggered.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 1002; content-versus-event
lb_student_diagnostic: Best-evidence false positive: student treats any recording as the required proof source.
