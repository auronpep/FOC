Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: In a federal contract trial, Sarah testifies that her phone rang and the caller said, “This is Boaz. I accept the offer.” Sarah did not recognize the voice, did not call Boaz's number, and the caller gave no details known only to Boaz. Boaz objects that the call has not been authenticated as his. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony because any caller's self-identification is enough to authenticate the call.
Answer B: Admit the testimony only if Sarah proves the call was recorded.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony only if Boaz proves someone else made the call.
Answer D: Exclude the testimony because the caller's bare self-identification, without corroborating circumstances, is insufficient foundation.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Telephone-call authentication requires circumstances showing the person answering or speaking was the person claimed. A bare self-identification by an unknown caller, without voice familiarity, a call to an assigned number, or distinctive details, is not enough.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because self-identification alone is a common authentication trap. B: B is wrong because a recording is not required. C: C is wrong because the proponent bears the foundation burden; Boaz need not prove an impostor.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(6) / telephone call / self-identification insufficient alone
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the self-identification trap in telephone authentication.
