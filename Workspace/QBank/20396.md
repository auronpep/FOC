Question: A court certifies a Rule 23(b)(3) consumer damages class. The defendant proposes skipping individual notice because notice will be expensive and the class website will contain the complaint and docket. What is the court's notice obligation?
Answer A: No notice is required because absent members are represented by the class representative
Answer B: The court must direct the best notice practicable and provide an opportunity to opt out
Answer C: Notice is required only if the plaintiff class loses on the merits
Answer D: Opt-out rights apply only to Rule 23(b)(2) injunctive classes
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 23(c)(2)(B) requires notice to class members who can be identified through reasonable effort.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 23(b)(3) requires directed notice and opt-out rights. C: Notice and opt-out rights come after certification, not after final merits loss. D: Mandatory opt-out rights are associated with Rule 23(b)(3), not Rule 23(b)(2).
lb_rule_reference: For a Rule 23(b)(3) class, the court must direct the best notice practicable to class members and give them an opportunity to opt out.
