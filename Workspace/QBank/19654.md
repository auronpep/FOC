Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Leah contracted with Jacob to build a garage for $100,000. Jacob expected total costs of $80,000 and therefore expected a $20,000 profit. After Jacob reasonably spent $42,000, Leah repudiated. Jacob would have had to spend another $38,000 to finish, and $6,000 of the materials already purchased can be used on another job. What is Jacob's expectation recovery?
Answer A: $42,000.
Answer B: $56,000.
Answer C: $62,000.
Answer D: $100,000.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under common law after the owner's repudiation of a construction contract. The formula is costs reasonably incurred plus expected profit minus salvage or costs saved: $42,000 + $20,000 − $6,000 = $56,000. The limiting gates are mitigation and offset because reusable materials cannot be counted as a loss. The result scope is partial expectation, and the answer does not award the full price because Jacob was saved the cost of completing performance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it awards costs only and omits expected profit. C: C is wrong because it stacks costs and profit but omits the $6,000 salvage offset, a WAL-REM-008 overcompensation error. D: D is wrong because the full contract price ignores saved completion costs and salvage.
lb_rule_reference: Common law construction expectation — costs incurred plus expected profit minus saved costs and salvage
