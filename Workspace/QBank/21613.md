Subtopic: Remedies
Outline: Duty to mitigate
Question: Mary agreed to buy 200 industrial pumps from Peter for $100 each. Peter repudiated before delivery. Mary reasonably covered by buying substitute pumps for $112 each and paid $600 in reasonable rush freight and inspection charges. Because the cover arrived in time, Mary's factory stayed open and she avoided $25,000 in production-profit loss. What may Mary recover under Article 2?
Answer A: $2,400, the cover differential only.
Answer B: $28,000, the cover differential, incidental charges, and avoided production profit.
Answer C: $3,000, the cover differential plus reasonable incidental charges.
Answer D: Nothing, because Mary covered and therefore suffered no loss.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under UCC goods law, with Mary as buyer and Peter as seller. The breach posture is seller repudiation, and the goods were covered with reasonable substitutes. The formula is the cover differential plus proper incidentals: 200 × ($112 cover price − $100 contract price) = $2,400, plus $600 reasonable freight and inspection charges, for $3,000. The $25,000 production profit was avoided by cover, so it is not a recoverable consequential loss. The result scope is partial Article 2 recovery.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it omits recoverable incidental charges. B: B is wrong because it stacks an avoided consequential profit with the cover recovery, triggering WAL-REM-008 and foreseeability-only overreach. D: D is wrong because successful cover does not erase the buyer's cover differential and incidental damages.
lb_rule_reference: UCC buyer cover — cover differential plus incidentals, excluding consequential loss avoided by cover
