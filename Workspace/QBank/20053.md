Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A city expects a contractor to sue it for breach of a state construction contract. The city files first in federal court seeking a declaration that federal safety rules excuse its nonpayment. The contractor's coercive claim would be a state-law contract claim; the federal rules would be the city's defense. Is there federal-question jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because the city put federal regulations in its declaratory complaint
Answer B: Yes, because a declaratory judgment action is always federal
Answer C: No, because the city is a governmental entity
Answer D: No, because the underlying coercive action would be a state contract claim and the federal issue would be only a defense
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The court realigns the declaratory posture and asks whether the natural coercive action arises under federal law. It does not.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Declaratory pleading cannot manufacture jurisdiction from what would be a defense. B: The Declaratory Judgment Act does not itself create subject matter jurisdiction. C: Party status is not the controlling issue.
lb_rule_reference: For declaratory relief, courts ask whether the coercive action that the declaratory defendant could bring would arise under federal law.
