Question: A mayor maintains a social media page labeled "Mayor's Office Updates." City staff post official announcements, invite residents to comment on city services, and respond during business hours. The mayor deletes comments criticizing a proposed tax increase while leaving supportive comments. What is the strongest First Amendment issue?
Answer A: No issue exists because social media pages are always private property outside the First Amendment
Answer B: The deletion raises a viewpoint-discrimination problem in an official comment forum
Answer C: The mayor may delete criticism because government speech doctrine covers all public comments
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies to every moderation decision on every internet page
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The city opened comments for resident discussion and then removed one side of the debate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Official use of government authority to run a public comment space can create state action. C: Residents' comments in an opened comment space are private speech, not the government's own message. D: The analysis depends on state action, forum creation, and the type of restriction.
lb_rule_reference: When a government official uses official authority to open a public comment space for private speech, viewpoint-based deletion of comments can violate the First Amendment.
