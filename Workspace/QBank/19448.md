MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A warehouse supervisor let a visibly intoxicated friend drive a forklift through the loading area to "try it once." The friend had never operated a forklift and nearly hit two pallets before striking a visitor. The supervisor was not the forklift's owner but had authority to control who used it during the shift. The visitor sues the supervisor for negligence. Is the visitor likely to establish a negligent entrustment duty against the supervisor?
Answer A: No, because only the legal owner of a chattel can be liable for negligent entrustment
Answer B: Yes, because the supervisor supplied a forklift to a visibly intoxicated and inexperienced person
Answer C: Yes, because negligent entrustment makes the supervisor strictly liable for any injury caused by the forklift
Answer D: No, because the friend alone operated the forklift and third-party conduct always cuts off the supervisor's duty
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The supervisor had control over the forklift and allowed a visibly intoxicated, inexperienced person to use it in a loading area, making misuse foreseeably controllable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The key is control over supplying the instrumentality, not formal title. The supervisor had authority to decide who used the forklift. C: Negligent entrustment is a negligence theory. It requires unreasonable entrustment and causal injury, not strict liability for every forklift-related harm. D: The whole point of negligent entrustment is that the entrustor's duty can arise from foreseeable misuse by the third person.
lb_rule_reference: Negligent entrustment can create direct negligence liability when the defendant supplies a dangerous instrumentality to a person known or reasonably known to be likely to misuse it.
