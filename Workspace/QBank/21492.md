Question: Joseph agreed in a signed writing to sell Esther “the gray mare in Stall 3” for $4,000. The writing was final but not complete and exclusive. Before signing, Joseph orally promised two things: first, that he would include the mare's saddle; second, that Esther could instead take the brown mare in Stall 4 for the same price. Esther offers evidence of both oral promises. Which ruling is best?
Answer A: Admit both oral promises because the writing was not complete and exclusive.
Answer B: Exclude both oral promises because each was made before the writing was signed.
Answer C: Admit the saddle promise but exclude the brown-mare promise.
Answer D: Exclude the saddle promise but admit the brown-mare promise.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A consistent additional term may supplement a partial integration, but a prior oral term may not contradict the final written expression. The saddle term adds a collateral item and can supplement the writing. The brown-mare option contradicts the written identification of the gray mare in Stall 3.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because partial integration does not allow contradictory terms. B: B is wrong because prior timing alone does not bar a consistent additional term. D reverses the proper term-by-term analysis. D: A is wrong because partial integration does not allow contradictory terms. B is wrong because prior timing alone does not bar a consistent additional term. D reverses the proper term-by-term analysis.
lb_rule_reference: Split oral package / supplement vs contradict
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats the oral package as all-in or all-out instead of routing each term separately.
