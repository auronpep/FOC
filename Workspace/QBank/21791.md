Outline: Freedom of religion
Question: A public high school allows student-run clubs to meet after school, including chess, environmental, debate, and service clubs. A student religious club applies to meet on the same terms to discuss service projects from a faith perspective. The school denies access solely because the club is religious. What is the club's strongest argument?
Answer A: The school may exclude the club because any religious meeting on campus establishes religion
Answer B: The exclusion is viewpoint discrimination because the school opened a student-club forum but excluded religious discussion of allowed subjects
Answer C: The school may exclude the club because limited forums allow viewpoint discrimination
Answer D: The club wins only if faculty lead the religious discussion
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The school allows clubs to discuss service and other subjects. Excluding a religious perspective on those topics discriminates against viewpoint.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Equal access for private student speech in a neutral forum does not automatically establish religion. C: Even in limited forums, restrictions must be viewpoint neutral. D: Faculty leadership would create more Establishment concern, not less. The club's strength is private student speech.
lb_rule_reference: When a public school opens a limited forum for student clubs, excluding a religious club because of its religious viewpoint can violate free speech and is not automatically required by the Establishment Clause.
