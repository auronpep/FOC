Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: In a federal homicide trial, the prosecutor offers 30 photographs of the same wound from nearly identical angles. The judge admits two photographs that show the wound clearly and excludes the remaining 28 as cumulative. The prosecutor objects that every photograph is relevant. Was the judge's ruling proper?
Answer A: Yes, because the court may admit some probative photographs while excluding needlessly cumulative duplicates.
Answer B: No, because once one photograph is admitted, all related photographs must be admitted.
Answer C: No, because relevant evidence can never be excluded as cumulative.
Answer D: Yes, but only because graphic photographs are always inadmissible.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 403 allows the court to exclude needlessly cumulative evidence. The judge can admit enough photographs to show the relevant point while excluding redundant photographs that add little probative value.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because admitting one exhibit does not require admitting every related exhibit. C: C is wrong because cumulative evidence is expressly within Rule 403's concerns. D: D is wrong because graphic photographs are not categorically inadmissible.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403
lb_student_diagnostic: Student misses selective admission and treats the ruling as all-or-nothing.
