Question: A defendant's negligence injured a plaintiff's knee. One surgeon proposed experimental surgery with a serious risk of paralysis and uncertain benefit. The plaintiff declined the procedure and chose conservative care. The defendant argues all later knee pain is unrecoverable because the plaintiff failed to mitigate. Is the defendant's mitigation argument likely correct?
Answer A: Yes, because mitigation requires the plaintiff to take any step that might reduce damages
Answer B: Yes, because refusal of medical treatment always bars future damages
Answer C: No, because the plaintiff was not required to undergo risky experimental surgery
Answer D: No, because mitigation never applies to medical damages
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The proposed procedure carried serious risk and uncertain benefit, so declining it was not an unreasonable failure to mitigate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Mitigation requires only reasonable steps, not dangerous or highly uncertain treatment. B: Only unreasonable refusal of reasonable treatment reduces avoidable damages. D: Mitigation can apply to medical-damage increments when reasonable treatment is refused; this treatment was not reasonable to require.
lb_rule_reference: The avoidable-consequences doctrine requires reasonable mitigation, not dangerous, experimental, or unusually risky treatment.
