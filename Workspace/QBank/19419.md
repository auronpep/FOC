Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Miriam owned one 100-acre tract in Judah County. She signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey all of my 100-acre tract in Judah County to Aaron, except the western pasture.” On the date of delivery, the western pasture was fenced and was the only part of the tract locally known by that name. Aaron accepted. What is the best result?
Answer A: The deed is invalid because it describes the land by exception rather than by metes and bounds.
Answer B: The deed conveys the western pasture only because exceptions are read as grants.
Answer C: The deed conveys the tract other than the western pasture because both the tract and the exception are reasonably identifiable.
Answer D: The deed is valid only if it states the exact acreage left after the exception.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is property-description sufficiency. A deed may describe land by conveying a larger identified tract and excepting an identifiable portion. Because the 100-acre tract and the fenced western pasture were reasonably identifiable, the deed conveys the remainder to Aaron and excludes the western pasture.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because metes and bounds are not required when the land and exception are otherwise identifiable. B: B is wrong because an exception withholds the described portion rather than granting only that portion. D: D is wrong because exact remaining acreage is unnecessary when the land can be identified with reasonable certainty.
lb_rule_reference: Property description by exception / ascertainable remainder
