Question: A grand jury indicted the defendant on a charge of arson, and a valid warrant was issued for her arrest. A police officer arrested the defendant and informed her of what the warrant stated. However, hoping that the defendant might say something incriminating, he did not give her Miranda warnings. He placed her in the back seat of his patrol car and was driving her to the police station when she said, "Look, I didn't mean to burn the building; it was an accident. I was just burning some papers in a wastebasket." At the station, after being given Miranda warnings, the defendant stated that she wished to remain silent and made no other statements. The defendant moved to suppress the use of her statement to the police officer as evidence on two grounds: first, that the statement was acquired without giving Miranda warnings, and second, that the police officer had deliberately elicited her incriminating statement after she was in custody. As to the defendant's motion to suppress, the court should
Answer A: A: deny the motion.
Answer B: B: grant the motion only on the basis of the first ground stated.
Answer C: C: grant the motion only on the basis of the second ground stated.
Answer D: D: grant the motion on either ground.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. Neither ground is a sufficient basis for granting the motion; Miranda warnings were not required because no custodial interrogation occurred, and by hoping for her to confess does not amount to the police officer deliberately eliciting incriminating statements.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. The statements were voluntarily given and not the subject of custodial interrogation, so Miranda warnings did not need to be given. C is incorrect. The officer did not take any action to deliberately elicit the statement, and the statements in the vehicle were voluntarily given. D is incorrect. Neither ground for the motion has any merit. The statements were voluntarily given, without custodial interrogation, and the defendant's motion to suppress should be denied.
