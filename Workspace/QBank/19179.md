Question: In a federal criminal robbery trial, Caleb testifies for the prosecution that he saw Gideon commit the robbery. Gideon seeks to impeach Caleb with a juvenile adjudication for fraud. If Caleb had been an adult, a fraud conviction would be admissible to attack credibility. The court finds that admitting the adjudication is necessary to fairly determine guilt or innocence. The prosecution objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the adjudication because juvenile adjudications are never admissible for impeachment.
Answer B: Exclude the adjudication because it is offered by a criminal defendant.
Answer C: Admit the adjudication because it is offered in a criminal case against a witness other than the defendant, an adult conviction would be admissible, and admission is necessary to fairly determine guilt or innocence.
Answer D: Admit the adjudication only if Caleb first claims to have a reputation for truthfulness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 609(d) allows a juvenile adjudication only in limited circumstances: it is offered in a criminal case, the adjudication is of a witness other than the defendant, an adult conviction for that offense would be admissible, and admitting it is necessary to fairly determine guilt or innocence. Those conditions are met.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 609(d) contains a limited exception. B: B is wrong because the fact that a criminal defendant offers the evidence does not bar it. D: D is wrong because the route is conviction/adjudication impeachment, not rebuttal to truthful-character evidence.
