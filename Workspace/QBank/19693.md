Question: In a federal robbery trial, the prosecutor offers a red jacket found in a storage locker rented by David. A witness has testified that the robber wore a red jacket, and the prosecutor states that a later witness will connect David to the locker. David objects that the jacket is irrelevant unless the prosecutor first proves conclusively that he used the locker. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the jacket unless the judge first finds beyond dispute that David used the locker.
Answer B: Admit the jacket only if David agrees that he used the locker.
Answer C: Admit the jacket if there is evidence sufficient to support a finding that David used the locker, including subject to later proof of that connection.
Answer D: Exclude the jacket because physical objects are admissible only after an unbroken chain of custody is proved by every custodian.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: When relevance depends on whether a fact exists, the court screens for evidence sufficient to support a finding of that condition. The court may admit the evidence subject to later proof. The jury ultimately decides whether the condition is established and what weight the evidence deserves.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the judge need not find the conditional fact conclusively or beyond dispute. B: B is wrong because the opponent's agreement is not required. D: D is wrong because chain-of-custody concerns can matter, but this item turns on conditional relevance and the locker connection.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / conditional relevance
