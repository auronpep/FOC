Question: Noah is tried for arson. The prosecutor offers a statement by an unavailable watchman as a statement against interest. To show that the watchman was unavailable, the prosecutor gives the judge an affidavit from the watchman's doctor stating that the watchman is too ill to appear. Noah objects that the affidavit is hearsay and therefore the judge may not consider it at the admissibility hearing. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Refuse to consider the affidavit because hearsay rules bind the judge at every preliminary hearing.
Answer B: Let the jury decide unavailability because the affidavit affects the credibility of the prosecutor's proof.
Answer C: Consider the affidavit in deciding the preliminary admissibility question, because the court is not bound by evidence rules except those on privilege.
Answer D: Admit the watchman's statement automatically because any affidavit from a doctor proves unavailability conclusively.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: In deciding preliminary questions of admissibility, the court is not bound by evidence rules except rules on privilege. The judge may consider appropriate materials, including affidavits, to decide unavailability at the admissibility gate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the ordinary hearsay rules do not bind the judge when deciding preliminary admissibility questions. B: B is wrong because unavailability for an exception is a preliminary admissibility question for the judge. D: D is wrong because the affidavit may be considered; it does not automatically or conclusively establish the preliminary fact.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a) / judge may consider inadmissible material except privilege
