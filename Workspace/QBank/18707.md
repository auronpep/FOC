MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police lawfully stopped a car driven by Dana. Theo was a passenger. Without consent or a warrant exception, officers opened a zipped backpack in the trunk. Theo immediately told the officers the backpack was his and that he had placed it in the trunk before the trip. The officers found counterfeit IDs inside. Theo moves to suppress the IDs. What is Theo's strongest standing argument?
Answer A: Theo has standing because he had a personal privacy interest in his own closed backpack
Answer B: Theo lacks standing because passengers never have Fourth Amendment rights during traffic stops
Answer C: Theo has standing only if he owned the car
Answer D: Theo lacks standing because counterfeit IDs are contraband
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A passenger may not have a privacy interest in the car's trunk generally, but he can have one in his own closed container. Theo claimed the backpack as his and had placed it there for the trip.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The issue is not generic passenger status; it is Theo's personal privacy interest in his own closed backpack. C: Ownership of the car is not required to challenge the search of one's own closed container. D: Standing asks about the searched place or item, not lawful ownership of contraband.
lb_rule_reference: Fourth Amendment standing is personal and turns on the defendant's own protected interest in the searched place or item.
