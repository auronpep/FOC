Question: In a federal fraud trial, Mary asks Samuel, “Do you know whether Joseph altered the receipt?” Samuel answers, “I do not know, but Joseph was arrested for fraud ten years ago.” Joseph immediately moves to strike the statement about the arrest. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the motion because Samuel began by saying he did not know.
Answer B: Grant the motion as to the volunteered arrest statement, while leaving the answer that Samuel does not know.
Answer C: Deny the motion because all arrests are admissible to prove fraud.
Answer D: Grant the motion and strike all of Samuel's testimony from the trial.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Samuel's lack-of-knowledge answer is responsive. The volunteered arrest statement went beyond the question and risks improper character use. The court should strike the volunteered portion, not the whole answer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a responsive beginning does not make the volunteered portion proper. C: C is wrong because arrests are not generally admissible to prove a person committed fraud. D: D is wrong because the remedy should be limited to the improper portion.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(1); FRE 611(a) / mixed answer with denial plus volunteered matter
