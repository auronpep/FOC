Question: Moses orally agreed to sell a field to Ruth for $70,000. Ruth paid Moses $10,000 as a deposit but never took possession, made no improvements, and did not change her position in any other way. Moses then refused to convey the field and pleaded the Statute of Frauds. Ruth seeks specific enforcement of the land-sale agreement. What is the most accurate result?
Answer A: Ruth can specifically enforce the agreement because any payment toward land is enough part performance.
Answer B: Ruth generally cannot specifically enforce the oral land-sale agreement because payment alone does not satisfy the land Statute of Frauds part-performance exception.
Answer C: Ruth can enforce the agreement because land-sale contracts are outside the Statute of Frauds if the buyer has paid less than half the price.
Answer D: Ruth automatically owns an equitable interest in the field because she paid a deposit.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An oral contract for the sale of land is within the Statute of Frauds. Payment alone, without possession, improvements, or other acts clearly referable to the alleged sale, generally is insufficient for specific enforcement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because payment alone is not the standard part-performance route. C: C is wrong because the percentage of the price paid does not remove the land contract from the Statute of Frauds. D: D is wrong because paying a deposit does not automatically transfer an equitable ownership interest.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law land contract SOF / payment alone
