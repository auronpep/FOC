Question: A health clinic hired Aaron to renovate treatment rooms. The contract required all electrical work to be performed by a licensed electrician because state law required licensed work in medical facilities. Aaron used an unlicensed friend to perform the electrical work. The city refused to approve use of the treatment rooms until the electrical work was removed and redone. Aaron demanded the full contract price less the cost of redoing the wiring. What is the best result?
Answer A: Aaron substantially performed because most of the visible renovation was complete.
Answer B: The clinic's payment duty has not matured because Aaron's breach was material and prevented lawful use of the treatment rooms.
Answer C: The clinic must pay the full price because licensing requirements are public-law issues, not contract issues.
Answer D: Aaron may recover the full price unless the clinic proves that the unlicensed friend did poor-quality work.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The licensed-electrical-work requirement went to lawful use of the renovated medical space. Aaron's use of an unlicensed person caused approval to be refused and required substantial redo work. That is a material breach defeating substantial performance and preventing the payment duty from maturing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because visible completion is not enough when the defect defeats lawful use. C: C is wrong because the licensing requirement was also a contract term and central to performance. D: D is wrong because the issue is legal compliance and materiality, not merely workmanship quality.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law material breach / constructive condition failure
