Outline: Burden of production and burden of persuasion
Question: In a federal employment case, Paul offers testimony that Mark, a supervisor for Lydia's company, said, “Lydia told me to fire Paul because he complained.” Lydia objects that Mark was not speaking about a matter within the scope of his employment. Paul asks that the jury decide whether Mark's statement qualifies as an employee statement of a party-opponent. Who should decide that preliminary issue?
Answer A: The judge should decide whether the foundational facts for the employee statement have been shown.
Answer B: The jury should decide because the statement concerns credibility.
Answer C: The jury should decide unless Lydia first proves that Mark lacked authority to speak.
Answer D: The judge should admit the statement automatically because any employee statement is admissible against the employer.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Whether a statement qualifies as an employee statement of a party-opponent depends on preliminary facts such as the employment relationship and scope. The court decides those admissibility facts before the statement is admitted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because credibility and admissibility are separate; the court decides the admissibility gate. C: C is wrong because the proponent, Paul, must establish the foundation for the route he invokes. D: D is wrong because not every employee statement qualifies; the scope and timing requirements matter.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a); FRE 801(d)(2)(D)
