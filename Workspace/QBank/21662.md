Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A manufacturer designed sterile bandages to be sealed and bacteria-free. One box left the factory with a broken seal and contamination that was not present in properly made boxes. A patient used a bandage from that box and developed an infection at the wound site. Which products theory best fits?
Answer A: Manufacturing defect, because this box departed from the intended sterile design
Answer B: Design defect, because all bandages are dangerous when used on wounds
Answer C: No strict liability unless the patient proves the factory's inspection was negligent
Answer D: No claim because bandages are medical products and strict liability never applies to them
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The product was supposed to be sealed and sterile. This particular box differed from properly made units and caused physical harm.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The claim does not attack the intended design of all bandages. It attacks one contaminated unit. C: Strict manufacturing defect does not require proof of negligent inspection. D: The facts present a contaminated product unit causing physical harm. There is no blanket medical-product immunity.
lb_rule_reference: A manufacturing defect exists when a particular unit departs from the intended design and is more dangerous than properly made units.
