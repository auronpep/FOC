Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Two merchants orally agree that a buyer will buy 900 wool cloaks from a seller for $18,000. The seller sends the buyer a signed written confirmation stating the quantity and price. The buyer receives it, has reason to know its contents, and does not object in writing within 10 days. The buyer later refuses to accept the cloaks and asserts the Statute of Frauds because the buyer never signed anything. What is the best result under the UCC?
Answer A: The buyer prevails because the party to be charged must always personally sign the writing.
Answer B: The seller can overcome the SOF because a signed confirmation between merchants can bind the recipient who fails to object in writing within 10 days.
Answer C: The seller prevails only if the buyer orally admitted the contract after receiving the confirmation.
Answer D: The buyer prevails because the seller's own writing can satisfy the SOF only against the seller.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Between merchants, a writing in confirmation of the contract that is sufficient against the sender can satisfy the SOF against the recipient if received within a reasonable time, the recipient has reason to know its contents, and the recipient does not object in writing within 10 days.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the merchant-confirmation rule is an exception to recipient-signature formalism. C: C is wrong because admission is a separate exception. D: D is wrong because the merchant-confirmation rule can satisfy the SOF against the silent merchant recipient.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(2) — merchant confirmation
