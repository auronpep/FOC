Question: During a neighborhood dispute, Carla threw a small rock at her neighbor Ben's car window, intending only to crack it — an act that constitutes criminal mischief (a misdemeanor in this jurisdiction). The rock ricocheted off the window frame and struck Ben's four-year-old daughter, who was standing nearby unseen by Carla. The child suffered a fatal skull fracture. Carla had no intent to harm the child and did not know the child was present. Is Carla guilty of involuntary manslaughter?
Answer A: No, because Carla did not know the child was present and therefore could not foresee the risk to her
Answer B: Yes, for depraved-heart murder, because throwing a rock at a residence where people might be present shows indifference to human life
Answer C: Yes, for involuntary manslaughter, because the death resulted from the commission of a malum in se unlawful act
Answer D: No, because criminal mischief is a property crime and cannot form the basis of a homicide charge
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The unlawful-act (misdemeanor-manslaughter) doctrine provides that an unintentional killing during the commission of an unlawful act constitutes involuntary manslaughter, provided the unlawful act is malum in se — inherently wrong or dangerous. Carla was committing criminal mischief (a malum in se misdemeanor), and the death of the child resulted directly from that unlawful act. No specific intent to harm the child is required. This is the paradigm unlawful-act manslaughter case.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The unlawful-act (misdemeanor-manslaughter) doctrine does not require subjective foreseeability of harm to a specific victim. It imposes liability for any death that results from the commission of an unlawful act, provided the act is malum in se (inherently wrong, not just prohibited). Criminal mischief involving throwing objects is typically malum in se. Carla's ignorance of the child is not a defense under this doctrine. B: Depraved-heart murder requires conscious disregard of an extreme and unjustifiable risk. Carla aimed at a car window intending only property damage. The risk level — one thrown rock aimed at a vehicle — does not rise to the extreme risk threshold for depraved-heart murder. This is unlawful-act involuntary manslaughter, not murder. D: The unlawful-act doctrine for involuntary manslaughter applies to malum in se misdemeanors — it is not limited to violent crimes. Criminal mischief involving the throwing of objects that could injure bystanders qualifies as malum in se and can serve as the predicate for involuntary manslaughter when it causes an unintentional death.
lb_rule_reference: Under the misdemeanor-manslaughter (unlawful-act) doctrine, an unintentional killing during the commission of an unlawful act that is not a felony — typically a malum in se misdemeanor — constitutes involuntary manslaughter
