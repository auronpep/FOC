Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Nathan signed a deed conveying Stoneacre to Abigail and gave it to Ezra, Nathan's attorney. Nathan told Ezra, “Hold this until I telephone you with final approval. Until then, it is not delivered.” Ezra misunderstood and mailed the deed to Abigail the same day. Abigail accepted and recorded it. Who owns Stoneacre?
Answer A: Abigail, because she received and recorded the deed before Nathan objected.
Answer B: Nathan, because the deed remained subject to Nathan's final approval and Ezra's mistaken mailing did not create delivery.
Answer C: Abigail, because an attorney's possession of a signed deed always creates delivery.
Answer D: Nathan only if Abigail knew Ezra had misunderstood Nathan's instructions.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is whether Nathan manifested present intent and surrendered control. Nathan expressly withheld delivery until final approval. Ezra held the deed for Nathan and mistakenly released it without authority, so title did not pass despite Abigail's recording.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because receipt and recording do not cure lack of authorized delivery. C: C is wrong because attorney possession does not automatically equal delivery. D: D is wrong because the defect is Nathan's lack of delivery, not Abigail's notice status.
lb_rule_reference: A mistaken release by the grantor's attorney is not delivery when the grantor retained approval control
