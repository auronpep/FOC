Outline: Duty to mitigate
Question: Daniel had one year remaining on a contract to work as a hotel banquet manager at a salary of $84,000. The hotel wrongfully discharged him. A substantially similar banquet-manager position with comparable duties, hours, and status was available in the same city for $65,000, but Daniel refused it because he disliked the competing hotel's owner. Daniel incurred no job-search expenses. What is Daniel's recovery?
Answer A: $84,000, because the original employer breached.
Answer B: $65,000, the amount Daniel could have earned from the comparable job.
Answer C: $19,000, the unpaid salary less the comparable earnings Daniel unreasonably refused.
Answer D: Nothing, because Daniel failed to mitigate.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is expectation under employment/common-law rules. The breach posture is wrongful discharge, and the claimed loss is lost wages. Because substantially comparable employment was available and Daniel unreasonably refused it, the mitigation/offset gate reduces recovery by the amount he could have earned. The formula is $84,000 unpaid salary − $65,000 comparable available earnings = $19,000. The result scope is partial recovery, not forfeiture.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores comparable available earnings. B: B is wrong because the replacement-job salary is an offset, not the damages award. D: D is wrong because mitigation failure reduces avoidable wage loss; it does not erase the entire contract claim under WAL-REM-009.
lb_rule_reference: Common law employment mitigation — comparable available earnings reduce wrongful-discharge damages
