Question: David orally agreed to sell a parcel of land to Esther for $120,000. The parties never signed any writing. At trial, Esther offers testimony from two witnesses who heard the oral agreement. David objects that the testimony cannot make the contract enforceable. Which doctrine is the strongest basis for David's objection to enforcement?
Answer A: The parol evidence rule, because any oral agreement is excluded whenever litigation occurs.
Answer B: The Statute of Frauds, because a land-sale contract generally requires a sufficient writing for enforcement.
Answer C: The best-evidence rule, because testimony about a contract is always secondary evidence.
Answer D: Unconscionability, because land prices must be fair when oral.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The decisive issue is not whether testimony is generally admissible to prove what was said; it is whether the land-sale agreement is enforceable without a sufficient writing or exception. The Statute of Frauds is the proper defense route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the parol evidence rule concerns external evidence offered against a final writing; there is no final integrated writing here. C: C is wrong because the original-writing doctrine is not the enforceability defense for an oral land contract. D: D is wrong because unfair price facts are absent and unconscionability is not the category gate.
