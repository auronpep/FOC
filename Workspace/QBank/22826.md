


BARMATRIX Q#: 22826
Outline_code: 94020000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: A woman who was a citizen of State A brought a negligence action in federal court against a State B corporation and one of its German subsidiaries. The woman alleged that a ladder manufactured by the subsidiary in Germany had injured her. The State B corporation did significant business throughout the United States, including in State A. The German subsidiary conducted no business in the United States and had no employees or bank accounts in the United States. The subsidiary manufactured its ladders for the European and Asian markets, but 3 percent of its ladders were imported and sold in State A by the State B corporation. The subsidiary moved to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. Should the court grant the motion to dismiss?
Answer A: A: No, because the corporation imports and sells the subsidiary's ladders in State A.
Answer B: B: No, because the court has jurisdiction over the State B corporation.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the ladder was manufactured in Germany.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the subsidiary lacks sufficient contacts with State A.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: In International Shoe Co. v. Washington, 326 U.S. 310 (1945), the United States Supreme Court held that a federal court exercises personal jurisdiction over an out- of- state defendant if the defendant has certain minimum contacts with the state. However, in Goodyear Dunlop Tires Operations, S.A. v. Brown, 564 U.S. 915 (2011), the Supreme Court found that a court could not exercise personal jurisdiction over a foreign subsidiary of a United States– based corporation unless the foreign subsidiary engaged in continuous and systematic activities in the forum state. In that case, the Court found that the sale of some of the foreign subsidiary's tires in the forum state was not enough to warrant personal jurisdiction. Similarly, since the ladders here were made for foreign markets and the only contact the German subsidiary had with State A was through some sales of its product by the United States corporation, the court would lack personal jurisdiction over the subsidiary. Therefore, A and B are incorrect. C is incorrect because the United States court could still have jurisdiction over the subsidiary if there were continuous and systematic contacts with State A.
