Question: Joseph owned a recorded easement to cross Miriam's land to reach a fishing cabin. Joseph stopped using the cabin for 12 years because of illness and later because the cabin needed repairs. He never signed a release, never told Miriam he was giving up the easement, and kept the cabin and the old trail entrance intact. Has Joseph abandoned the easement solely because he did not use it for 12 years?
Answer A: Yes, because any nonuse longer than the local adverse-possession period automatically abandons an easement.
Answer B: Yes, because illness is legally equivalent to a written release.
Answer C: No, but only because recorded easements can terminate only by government condemnation.
Answer D: No, because nonuse caused by hardship, without intent or conduct showing relinquishment, is not enough by itself.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Abandonment requires more than nonuse. The facts show a reason for nonuse but no statement or conduct indicating an intent to relinquish the easement. Joseph kept the benefited property and the route entrance intact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because nonuse alone is insufficient. B: B is wrong because illness is not a release. C: C is wrong because recorded easements may terminate by several doctrines. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Abandonment requires intent or inconsistent conduct
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is converting long nonuse into abandonment without identifying intent or inconsistent conduct.
