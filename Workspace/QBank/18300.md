MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: After arresting Omar and placing him in the patrol car, officers did not read Miranda warnings. Knowing Omar had two small children at home, one officer said loudly, "It would be terrible if a child found the loaded pistol before we did." Omar immediately said, "It is under the crib." Police found the pistol there. Was the officer's remark interrogation for Miranda purposes?
Answer A: No, because the officer did not ask Omar a direct question
Answer B: No, because police may always talk to each other in a suspect's presence
Answer C: Yes, because the remark was reasonably likely to elicit an incriminating response from Omar
Answer D: Yes, but only because the pistol was later found
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Omar was in custody, and the child-danger remark was tailored to prompt him to reveal the gun's location. It was the functional equivalent of interrogation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Interrogation includes the functional equivalent of questioning. The officer should have known the remark was likely to elicit Omar's location statement. B: Police conversation can be interrogation when directed at eliciting an incriminating response from a known suspect. D: Interrogation is judged by the likely effect of police words, not by hindsight alone.
lb_rule_reference: Interrogation includes express questioning and its functional equivalent: police words or conduct they should know are reasonably likely to elicit an incriminating response.
