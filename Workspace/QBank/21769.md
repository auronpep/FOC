Question: A buyer contracted to purchase steel bins from Benjamin. The signed agreement required delivery by the fifth day of each month, contained a merger clause, and stated that changes required a signed writing. For two months after signing, Benjamin delivered on the tenth day, and the buyer accepted the bins without objection after its purchasing manager told Benjamin that tenth-day delivery was acceptable. When the buyer later rejected another tenth-day delivery, Benjamin offered evidence of the manager's statements and the two accepted late deliveries. Should the court admit the evidence for the limited purpose of showing waiver or modification of the delivery date?
Answer A: No, because the merger clause bars all evidence outside the signed agreement.
Answer B: No, because a written delivery date cannot be affected by later course of performance.
Answer C: Yes, because post-signing statements and accepted performance are not barred by the parol evidence rule and may show waiver even if a formal modification failed.
Answer D: Yes, but only as trade usage evidence, because the parties' own performance under this contract is irrelevant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that later conduct is not excluded as parol evidence, and under the UCC an attempted modification that fails a signed-writing requirement can still operate as a waiver. A course of performance may also be relevant to show waiver or modification of an inconsistent term. The trigger facts are the post-signing statements, repeated late deliveries, and acceptance without objection. The decisive locks are timing and offered purpose: Benjamin offers the evidence to show later waiver, not to vary the original writing with prior negotiations.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because merger-clause absolutism ignores the post-signing timing and waiver purpose. B: B is wrong because an express date is relevant but can be waived by later conduct. D: D is wrong because the strongest source is the parties' own course of performance, not general trade usage.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-209(4); UCC 1-303(f) / course performance as waiver
