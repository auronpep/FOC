


BARMATRIX Q#: 22830
Outline_code: 94010300
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: An employee sued his employer, claiming he was negligently injured under state law when a piece of machinery broke off in his hand. The man also claimed violation of federal employment safety statutes. The employee claimed $60,000 in damages for his injuries. The employee was a citizen of State A, and the employer was incorporated in State B and had its primary place of business in State A. The employee was injured in a factory the employer owned in State C. May the employee assert both of his claims together in federal court?
Answer A: A: No, because the employee and employer were citizens of the same state.
Answer B: B: No, because the amount in controversy was only $60,000.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the federal court would have subject matter jurisdiction over the state law claim through joinder.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the federal court would have supplemental jurisdiction over the state law claim.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: 28 U.S.C. §1367 states that “in any civil action of which the district courts have original jurisdiction, the district courts shall have supplemental jurisdiction over all other claims that are so related to claims in the action within such original jurisdiction that they form part of the same case or controversy.” Here, since the employee is suing his employer for violation of the federal safety statute, the federal court would have jurisdiction over the claim because it involves a federal question. Because the federal court would have federal jurisdiction, the state law negligence claim involving the same accident could be heard in federal court under the court's supplemental jurisdiction. A and B are incorrect because citizenship and amount in controversy do not matter when the claim involves a federal question. C is incorrect because the federal court's jurisdiction would not arise through joinder.
