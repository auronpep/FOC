Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Mary offers a broken jar bearing a permanent label with the words “Bethlehem Foods” and the company's standard logo. She offers the jar to show it came from Bethlehem Foods. The company objects that no company employee has authenticated the label. How should the court rule on authentication?
Answer A: Exclude the jar because only a company employee may authenticate a label.
Answer B: Exclude the jar because labels are always hearsay.
Answer C: Admit the jar for authentication purposes because trade inscriptions or labels affixed in the course of business may indicate origin, ownership, or control.
Answer D: Admit the jar only if Mary proves the label's statement was made by a live witness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Trade inscriptions, signs, tags, and labels affixed in the course of business can be self-authenticating evidence of origin, ownership, or control. The label is not excluded for lack of a company witness on authentication alone.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a manufacturer employee is not required for this authentication method. B: B is wrong because the issue here is authentication of source, not a hearsay assertion offered for all purposes. D: D is wrong because the label can authenticate without a live declarant.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 902 / trade inscription or label
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests trade-label self-authentication and the live-witness over-demand trap.
