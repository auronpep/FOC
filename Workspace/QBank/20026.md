Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A prescription medication has a known risk of severe tendon rupture in patients taking a common antibiotic. The manufacturer sends doctors a label that says only "use caution with other drugs" and omits the tendon-rupture interaction. A doctor who did not know the specific risk prescribes the drug with the antibiotic, and the patient is injured. What is the patient's best response to the manufacturer's learned-intermediary defense?
Answer A: Learned intermediary never applies to prescription drugs
Answer B: The defense may fail because the warning to the physician was not adequate as to the specific known risk
Answer C: The defense automatically wins because any communication to doctors satisfies the warning duty
Answer D: The claim must be manufacturing defect because the drug injured the patient
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The doctrine changes the warning audience; it does not make a vague warning automatically sufficient.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Prescription drugs are a classic learned-intermediary setting. C: The physician warning must still be adequate. D: The facts test warning content and audience, not a rogue unit.
lb_rule_reference: The learned-intermediary doctrine can route a prescription-product warning to the physician, but the warning to the physician still must be adequate.
