Question: Maya slips into a concert by walking behind a group with tickets. She watches the full performance without paying. She does not take a ticket, wristband, merchandise, or money. Is Maya guilty of common-law larceny?
Answer A: Yes, because she received entertainment without paying for it
Answer B: Yes, because the venue lost the price of a ticket
Answer C: No, because she obtained services rather than taking tangible personal property
Answer D: No, because larceny requires a taking from the victim's body
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: On these facts, Maya obtained admission and entertainment without taking property. A theft-of-services statute might apply, but common-law larceny does not.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common-law larceny requires taking personal property, not merely receiving an unpaid service. B: Lost revenue is not the same as a trespassory taking of personal property. D: Larceny does not require taking from the body; it requires personal property. The answer is right only because no property was taken.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law larceny requires taking and carrying away tangible personal property; obtaining services without paying is not larceny absent a statute or property taken.
