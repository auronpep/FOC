Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: Naomi is charged with leaving the scene of a collision. The prosecutor offers a blue plastic bumper fragment found at the crash site. A mechanic testifies that Naomi's car is missing a piece from its front bumper, that the missing area has the same shape as the fragment, and that the paint color and layered scratch pattern match. Naomi objects that the mechanic cannot state with certainty that the fragment came from her car. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the fragment because exact certainty is required before physical evidence may be admitted.
Answer B: Exclude the fragment because comparison evidence may be given only by a laboratory expert.
Answer C: Admit the fragment because the comparison evidence is sufficient for a reasonable juror to connect it to Naomi's car.
Answer D: Admit the fragment only if an eyewitness saw it fall from Naomi's car.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The mechanic's comparison testimony supplies a sufficient foundation for the jury to find that the fragment came from Naomi's car. Certainty and eyewitness confirmation are not required for threshold admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because authentication/source identity requires sufficient evidence, not certainty. B: B is wrong because a qualified mechanic or witness with knowledge may provide comparison foundation. D: D is wrong because circumstantial source identity can authenticate physical evidence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(a)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats source identity as requiring exact certainty or eyewitness proof.
