Outline: Establishing discrimination
Question: A state bans "long-distance waste" from disposal in state landfills. Legislative history shows the law was adopted to keep out-of-state waste companies from competing with local haulers. In-state waste is rarely long-distance, while out-of-state waste almost always is. What is the best DCC analysis?
Answer A: Apply discrimination analysis because the purpose and effect target out-of-state commerce
Answer B: Uphold it automatically because the text does not say "out of state."
Answer C: Apply rational basis because waste disposal is local
Answer D: Dismiss for lack of standing because companies cannot sue under the DCC
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A neutral label does not end the inquiry when purpose and operation reveal protectionism.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: DCC discrimination can be shown through purpose or practical effect, not only facial terms. C: Local subject matter does not avoid DCC scrutiny when the law protects local commerce. D: Businesses burdened by state regulation can raise DCC challenges.
lb_rule_reference: A law can trigger discrimination analysis when its purpose or practical effect favors in-state commerce over out-of-state commerce, even if the text appears neutral.
