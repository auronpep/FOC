


BARMATRIX Q#: 22735
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A man from State A suffered $100,000 in damages when he fell down an escalator in a hotel in State B. The man sued the hotel and the escalator manufacturer in state court in State B because it had the most favorable law for negligence claims. The hotel was incorporated in State A, but had its primary place of business in State B. The escalator manufacturer was incorporated in State C and had its primary place of business in State A. Twenty- six days after being served, the hotel removed the case to State B federal court. The man moved to have the case remanded back to state court. Should the case be remanded back to state court?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the hotel's removal was not filed in a timely fashion.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the federal court lacks subject- matter jurisdiction.
Answer C: C: No, because the hotel's removal was filed within 30 days of being served.
Answer D: D: No, because the man's claim could have originally been brought in federal court.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: For a state law claim to be heard in federal court, there must be diversity of citizenship between the parties. Here, the man is a citizen of State A. Corporations are citizens of their primary place of business and their place of incorporation. Here, the hotel is incorporated in State A, and the manufacturer's primary place of business is State A. Because there is no diversity of citizenship, the man's claim cannot be heard in federal court. A is incorrect because a notice of removal is timely if it is filed within 30 days of service. C is incorrect because of the lack of diversity of citizenship. D is incorrect because the man's negligence claim would not have original jurisdiction in federal court because there was no diversity of citizenship.
