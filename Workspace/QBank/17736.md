MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Assault
Question: After a neighborhood meeting, a resident pointed at another resident from across a wide lobby and said, "At next week's meeting, I am going to shove you in front of everyone." The speaker then left the building. The listener believed the speaker was serious and worried about attending the next meeting. The listener sues the speaker for assault. Is the listener likely to prevail in an assault claim against the speaker?
Answer A: Yes, because the listener believed the threat and worried about future harm
Answer B: No, because the threat concerned a later meeting rather than imminent contact
Answer C: Yes, because words alone can create assault whenever they are serious
Answer D: No, because assault requires actual physical contact
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The statement threatened contact at next week's meeting. Because the threatened contact was not imminent, the facts do not establish assault.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Assault requires apprehension of imminent contact. Fear of future harm is not enough. C: Words may support assault when they create reasonable apprehension of imminent contact, but a serious future threat still lacks imminence. D: Assault does not require contact. The correct reason is lack of imminent apprehension.
lb_rule_reference: A threat of future harm, without imminent apparent contact, does not establish assault.
