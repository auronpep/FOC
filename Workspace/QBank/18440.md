Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal civil case, Mary offers a digital copy of a voicemail from Peter. Mary testifies that the copy accurately reproduces the voicemail, and Peter raises no genuine dispute about authenticity or unfairness. Mary offers the voicemail to prove Peter's exact words. Peter objects that only the original phone file may be used. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because a duplicate is generally admissible unless there is a genuine question about authenticity or unfairness.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because duplicates of recordings are never admissible.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Mary also offers a written transcript.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Peter first admits that the voicemail is his.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A duplicate is generally admissible to the same extent as the original unless a genuine question is raised about the original's authenticity or admitting the duplicate would be unfair. Peter has raised neither problem.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because duplicates are generally admissible. C: C is wrong because a transcript is not required to use an otherwise admissible duplicate recording. D: D is wrong because Peter's admission is not the only way to authenticate or admit the recording.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 1003
lb_student_diagnostic: Original-only overreach: student ignores the duplicate rule.
