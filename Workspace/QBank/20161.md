


BARMATRIX Q#: 20161
Outline_code: 64010203
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Conversion
Question: The defendant left the car in the sun, which caused its paint to fade. Believing that a new paint job would make the car easier to sell, the defendant had it painted red without consulting the plaintiff. Before it was repainted, the defendant drove the plaintiff's car 4,000 miles on his own personal business. After it was painted, the defendant's customers drove it an additional 1,000 miles while deciding whether to purchase it. Although the value of the plaintiff's car did not change while it was in the defendant's possession, the defendant was unable to sell the car. The plaintiff subsequently asserted a conversion claim against the defendant. If the plaintiff is successful in her conversion action, the most she will be entitled to recover is
Answer A: A: the value that the car had at the time the plaintiff delivered it to the defendant.
Answer B: B: the cost of restoring its paint to the condition that existed at the time she delivered it to the defendant.
Answer C: C: the reasonable value of the car's use for 4,000 miles.
Answer D: D: nothing.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: In an action for conversion, the defendant is liable for the full value of the chattel at the time of conversion. Consequently, the plaintiff can recover the value of the car at the time she delivered it to the defendant. B, C, and D are therefore incorrect.
