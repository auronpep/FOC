Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Diversity Jurisdiction
Question: When the complaint was filed, plaintiff and defendant were both citizens of Oregon. The plaintiff sought $300,000. Two weeks later, defendant moved to Idaho and intended to remain there indefinitely. Plaintiff argues the federal court now has diversity jurisdiction because the parties are citizens of different states. Does the later move create diversity jurisdiction?
Answer A: Yes, because jurisdiction can be created by any later change in citizenship
Answer B: Yes, because the amount in controversy is high enough
Answer C: No, because plaintiff and defendant shared Oregon citizenship when the action was filed
Answer D: No, because a defendant can never change domicile
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The time-of-filing rule controls diversity citizenship.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Diversity is ordinarily tested at filing. B: Amount does not cure lack of complete diversity at filing. D: People can change domicile, but later change usually does not fix diversity for an already filed action.
lb_rule_reference: A later change in citizenship generally does not create or destroy diversity that existed or failed at filing.
