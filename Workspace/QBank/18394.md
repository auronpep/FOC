Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: Two State A plaintiffs sue a State B defendant in federal court over the same bus crash. Plaintiff 1 seeks $120,000. Plaintiff 2 seeks $20,000. Both claims arise from the same crash, and no plaintiff shares citizenship with the defendant. May Plaintiff 2's low-value claim proceed under supplemental jurisdiction?
Answer A: No, because every plaintiff must independently exceed $75,000
Answer B: No, because supplemental jurisdiction never applies in diversity cases
Answer C: Yes, because complete diversity exists and Plaintiff 2's claim shares the same case or controversy with Plaintiff 1's qualifying claim
Answer D: Yes, but only if Plaintiff 2 asserts a federal claim
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Plaintiff 1 supplies original diversity jurisdiction, and Plaintiff 2's related claim does not destroy complete diversity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A related low-amount claim by an additional diverse plaintiff may be supported by supplemental jurisdiction when another plaintiff satisfies the amount requirement. B: Supplemental jurisdiction can apply in diversity cases, subject to Section 1367(b) limits. D: Supplemental jurisdiction can cover related state-law claims.
lb_rule_reference: When at least one diverse plaintiff satisfies the amount requirement, a related claim by another completely diverse plaintiff may fall within supplemental jurisdiction even if that plaintiff's claim is below the amount threshold.
