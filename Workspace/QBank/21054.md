Question: In a lawsuit, Jacob alleges that Lydia orally agreed to buy 800 wooden stools from him for $6,400. No writing was signed. In her deposition filed with the court, Lydia testifies, “I did agree to buy 800 stools from Jacob, but I later changed my mind.” Lydia then asserts the Statute of Frauds. What is the effect of Lydia's admission?
Answer A: The admission has no effect because only a signed writing can ever satisfy the UCC goods Statute of Frauds.
Answer B: The contract may be enforceable to the quantity Lydia admitted.
Answer C: The contract is enforceable only if Lydia also admits the price term in writing.
Answer D: The admission proves only that negotiations occurred, not that any contract could be enforced.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under §2-201(3)(b), a goods contract that lacks a sufficient writing may be enforceable if the party against whom enforcement is sought admits in pleading, testimony, or otherwise in court that a contract for sale was made. Enforcement is limited to the quantity admitted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because admission is a statutory exception. C: C is wrong because price is not required for this exception if the quantity admitted is clear. D: D is wrong because Lydia admitted an agreement to buy 800 stools, not merely negotiations.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(3)(b)
