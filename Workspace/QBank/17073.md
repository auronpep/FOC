Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A grocery chain signed agreements calling its delivery drivers "independent contractors." The chain required drivers to wear chain uniforms, use chain-owned vans, follow exact delivery routes, accept all dispatches, and report to a chain supervisor for driving instructions. A driver negligently injured a pedestrian while delivering groceries. Is the grocery chain likely vicariously liable for the driver's negligence?
Answer A: No, because the written agreement labels the driver an independent contractor
Answer B: Yes, because the chain controlled the manner and means of the delivery work despite the contractor label
Answer C: Yes, but only because grocery delivery is inherently dangerous
Answer D: No, because the driver alone committed the negligent driving act
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Uniforms, company vans, mandatory routes, dispatch control, and supervisor instructions show control over how the driver worked. That supports employee status for respondeat superior.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Labels do not control when the principal controls the manner and means of work. The chain's detailed operational control supports employee status. C: Ordinary grocery delivery is not an inherently dangerous activity. Liability rests on employee status through control, not an independent-contractor exception. D: Vicarious liability makes an employer liable for employee torts within scope even when the employer did not personally commit the negligent act.
lb_rule_reference: A contract label does not control worker status; the principal's right to control the manner and means of work can support employee status and ordinary respondeat superior.
