Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Aaron is charged with assaulting a traveler. Blood was found on Aaron's sleeve shortly after the incident. The victim and thousands of others in the region share the same blood type as the blood on the sleeve. Aaron objects that the blood-type evidence is irrelevant because it is not unique to the victim. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence because blood type must uniquely identify the victim to be relevant.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence unless DNA testing conclusively identifies the victim.
Answer C: Admit the evidence because the matching blood type has some tendency to connect Aaron to the assault; its commonness affects weight.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if Aaron admits he was near the traveler.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A nonunique blood-type match is weak but probative when connected to the timing and location of the assault. The fact that many people share the type is a weight argument.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevance does not require unique identification. B: B is wrong because conclusive DNA proof is not required for admissibility. D: D is wrong because Aaron's admission is not necessary for the evidence to have some tendency to prove connection.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401; FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Student demands scientific certainty before admitting weak identification evidence.
