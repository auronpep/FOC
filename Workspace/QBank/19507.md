


BARMATRIX Q#: 19507
internal_id: TR-063
Outline_code: 61020401
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Cause in fact (actual cause)
Question: A clinic negligently waited six hours before calling a patient with a lab result showing a rare infection. The patient became critically ill during that period. At trial, all experts agree that by the time the lab result reached the clinic, the infection had already progressed past the point where any available treatment could have avoided the critical illness. Prompt notice would have been required by the clinic's procedures, but it would not have improved the patient's outcome. Is the patient likely to prevail in a negligence claim against the clinic for the critical illness?
Answer A: Yes, because the clinic violated its own procedure for prompt notice
Answer B: Yes, because the delay increased the risk that the patient would be harmed
Answer C: No, because the patient cannot prove that prompt notice would have prevented or lessened the critical illness
Answer D: No, because medical providers are liable only for intentional misconduct in communicating test results
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Actual cause is missing. The clinic breached a duty, but the same critical illness would have occurred even if the clinic had called immediately.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Breach does not establish actual cause. The facts state that prompt notice would not have improved the patient's outcome. B: The tested injury is the critical illness, and experts agree that it was already unavoidable. Increased risk alone does not prove that this breach caused this damage. D: Medical providers may be liable for negligent communication when negligence causes harm. This claim fails because causation is absent, not because negligence is categorically unavailable.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant's negligent delay is not an actual cause of injury when the injury was already inevitable before the delay could have changed the outcome.
