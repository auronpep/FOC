Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: David contracted with Leah to renovate “the upper rooms” of Leah's inn. The final signed agreement did not define “upper rooms.” During negotiations, David had sent Leah a written bid listing the two second-floor guest rooms and the attic storage room. Leah offers the bid to explain what “upper rooms” meant. David objects that the bid predates the final writing. Should the court admit the bid?
Answer A: No, because a prior written bid is automatically excluded once a final contract is signed.
Answer B: Yes, because written negotiations are never parol evidence.
Answer C: No, because admitting the bid would automatically add a new building to the contract.
Answer D: Yes, if the phrase “upper rooms” is ambiguous and the bid helps explain its meaning.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A prior writing can be parol evidence, but it may be admitted to explain an ambiguous term. The bid is not offered as an independent additional promise; it is offered to clarify the meaning of “upper rooms.”
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because prior writings are not automatically excluded; purpose matters. B: B is wrong because prior writings can be parol evidence. C: C is wrong because the bid may define the phrase rather than add an unrelated obligation.
