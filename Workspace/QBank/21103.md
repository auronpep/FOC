internal_id: TR-121

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Defamation
Question: A warehouse has only one night-shift safety auditor. A trade blog falsely reported, "The night-shift safety auditor at Harbor Cold Storage has been faking inspection logs for months." Several warehouse customers read the post and canceled meetings with the auditor, even though the blog did not use the auditor's name. Is the auditor likely to prevail in a defamation claim against the blog?
Answer A: No, because the blog did not identify the auditor by name
Answer B: Yes, because the statement asserted a false fact about the auditor and was published to third parties
Answer C: No, because publication requires printing the statement in a newspaper
Answer D: Yes, but only if the auditor proves the blog acted with actual malice
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The post accused the only person in that role of professional dishonesty, readers understood who was meant, and the blog communicated the false factual statement to third parties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A plaintiff need not be named if readers who know the context reasonably understand the statement to refer to the plaintiff. C: Publication in defamation means communication to at least one third party, including online posting. D: The facts do not make the auditor a public official or public figure; the controlling issue is identification and publication.
lb_rule_reference: A defamatory statement must assert a false fact, be reasonably understood as concerning the plaintiff, and be published to a third party.
