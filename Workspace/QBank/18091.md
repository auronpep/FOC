internal_id: TR-221
Outline_code: 61020303
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Custom
Question: A marina followed the local industry custom of leaving fuel hoses across a walkway while refueling rental boats. The marina knew customers often crossed the walkway, and inexpensive hose bridges were available. A customer tripped over a hose and broke an ankle. The marina argues it cannot have breached because every marina in town used the same setup. Does the marina's compliance with industry custom conclusively defeat breach?
Answer A: Yes, because industry custom conclusively defines reasonable care
Answer B: Yes, because negligence requires violation of a statute
Answer C: No, because custom is relevant but the factfinder may still find the custom unreasonable
Answer D: No, because custom evidence is never admissible in negligence cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The marina's custom evidence helps it, but it does not control. Foreseeable pedestrian use and a cheap precaution can support breach despite industry practice.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Custom is evidence, not automatic immunity. A jury may find the custom unreasonable when cheap precautions would reduce a foreseeable risk. B: Ordinary negligence can be proven without a statutory violation. The question is reasonable care under the circumstances. D: Custom is generally relevant evidence. The problem is treating it as dispositive.
lb_rule_reference: Industry custom is relevant evidence of reasonable care, but it is not automatically conclusive when the custom itself may be unreasonable.
