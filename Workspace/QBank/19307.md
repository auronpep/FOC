MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A photographer standing on a public sidewalk took a photo of a musician arguing loudly with a taxi driver on a busy street. The musician was embarrassed when the photo later appeared in an article about downtown traffic disputes. The musician sues for intrusion upon seclusion. Is the musician likely to prevail on intrusion?
Answer A: Yes, because the photo embarrassed the musician
Answer B: Yes, because later publication is enough for intrusion
Answer C: No, because the photographer observed and photographed a public street event
Answer D: No, because privacy claims require false statements
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The musician was arguing in public view on a busy street. The photographer did not invade a private space or seclusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Intrusion requires invasion of a private place, conversation, or matter; embarrassment in public is not enough. B: Intrusion is about the method of obtaining information, and the conduct occurred in public. D: Intrusion does not require falsity. This claim fails because there was no private intrusion.
lb_rule_reference: Observing or photographing a person in a public place generally is not intrusion upon seclusion absent invasion of a private place or matter.
