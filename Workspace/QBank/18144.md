Subtopic: Rights in Land
Outline: Running with the land
Question: Adam owned two adjoining lots served by a private road. He conveyed Lot 2 to Eve by deed. The deed stated: “The owner of Lot 2, and all successors, shall pay one-half of the reasonable cost of maintaining the private road used by Lot 1 and Lot 2.” Eve later sold Lot 2 to Jacob, whose deed referred to the road-maintenance covenant. Moses, the owner of Lot 1, repaired the road and demanded $80,000 from Jacob, claiming that amount was half the cost. At trial, Moses proved that some road work was needed, but he did not prove that all the work was necessary or that the amount charged was reasonable. Moses seeks damages from Jacob. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Jacob owes nothing because affirmative covenants to pay money never touch and concern land.
Answer B: Jacob owes the full $80,000 because the covenant runs with Lot 2.
Answer C: Jacob owes nothing unless Moses seeks an injunction rather than damages.
Answer D: Jacob is liable only for the reasonable and proven share of necessary maintenance costs.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A real covenant may support damages against a successor when the covenant is in writing, the parties intended it to run, it touches and concerns the land, the required privity exists, and the successor has notice. A road-maintenance covenant can touch and concern land because it affects access, use, and value. But the amount owed must still be proven and reasonable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because affirmative payment covenants tied to land use, such as road maintenance, may touch and concern land. B: B is wrong because running status does not automatically prove the amount due. C: C is wrong because damages are the real covenant remedy; injunction is the equitable servitude remedy.
lb_rule_reference: Real covenant burden / affirmative maintenance covenant / proof of damages
