Question: In a federal battery trial, Sarah offers a dented bronze cup that she says Abraham threw at her. Sarah testifies that she picked up the cup immediately after the incident, placed it in a marked bag, and gave it to the clerk. Abraham objects that the judge has not personally found that the cup is the same cup. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Admit the cup if there is evidence sufficient to support a finding that it is what Sarah claims, leaving weight to the jury.
Answer B: Exclude the cup unless the judge personally finds beyond dispute that it is the same cup.
Answer C: Exclude the cup because physical objects are demonstrative aids, not evidence.
Answer D: Admit the cup only if Abraham stipulates that the cup is authentic.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Real evidence must be authenticated by evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims. Sarah's testimony can supply that threshold; the jury can evaluate weight and credibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B overdemands foundation. C misclassifies the physical cup; it is real evidence. D is wrong because authenticity can be proved without a stipulation.
