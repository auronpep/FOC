Question: Police beat Daniel and threatened further violence until he confessed to robbery. The court found the confession involuntary and barred it from the prosecution's case-in-chief. At trial, Daniel testified that he had never been at the store. The prosecutor seeks to use the coerced confession only to impeach Daniel. May the coerced confession be used for impeachment?
Answer A: Yes, because suppressed statements can always be used to impeach a defendant who testifies falsely
Answer B: Yes, because the jury can decide how much weight to give a coerced confession
Answer C: No, because an involuntary confession is inadmissible for any purpose
Answer D: No, because Miranda warnings were not given after Daniel confessed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Physical violence and threats made the confession involuntary. Due process forbids using the coerced statement even to impeach Daniel's testimony.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: That impeachment rule does not apply to coerced involuntary confessions. Due process bars use for any purpose. B: Involuntariness is an admissibility bar. The court cannot send a coerced confession to the jury for impeachment weight. D: The dispositive defect is coercion by violence and threats. Later warnings would not cure that coerced confession.
lb_rule_reference: An involuntary coerced confession is inadmissible for any purpose, including impeachment, because due process bars its use.
