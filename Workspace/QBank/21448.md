Question: A photographer lent a camera to a friend for one afternoon so the friend could take family photos. Instead, the friend sold the camera to a resale shop and used the money for rent. The photographer demanded the camera's full value. The photographer sues the friend for conversion. Is the photographer likely to prevail in a conversion claim against the friend?
Answer A: No, because the friend originally received the camera with permission
Answer B: Yes, because selling the camera was serious dominion inconsistent with the photographer's rights
Answer C: No, because conversion requires proof that the camera was physically damaged
Answer D: Yes, but the photographer is limited to rental value for one afternoon
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Unauthorized sale is a classic serious exercise of dominion over another's chattel and supports conversion, with full market value as the ordinary measure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Permission to borrow for one afternoon did not authorize selling the camera. Serious dominion beyond the scope of permission can be conversion. C: Conversion can occur without physical damage. The serious dominion is the forced-sale equivalent. D: Unauthorized sale is conversion, not a minor loss-of-use interference. The remedy is generally full market value at the time of conversion.
lb_rule_reference: Conversion requires serious intentional dominion over another's chattel, such as unauthorized sale, warranting forced-sale damages.
