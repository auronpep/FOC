Question: Daniel is charged with burning the shop of his former business partner, Nathan. The government offers evidence that, six months before the fire, Daniel told Nathan, “I will ruin your shop if you keep my customers.” Daniel objects that six months is too remote. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the statement because it tends to show motive, and the six-month gap ordinarily affects weight rather than eliminating relevance.
Answer B: Exclude the statement because any threat more than one month old is irrelevant as a matter of law.
Answer C: Exclude the statement because motive evidence is admissible only if the defendant repeats the threat on the day of the crime.
Answer D: Admit the statement only if the judge first finds that Daniel actually set the fire.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The statement has a logical connection to motive. The passage of six months may affect how persuasive the evidence is, but it does not automatically destroy relevance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because there is no fixed one-month rule. C: C is wrong because motive evidence need not be made on the day of the crime. D: D is wrong because the judge does not decide the ultimate arson fact before motive evidence is admitted.
