MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Intentional infliction of emotional distress
Question: A collector sought payment on a valid overdue equipment lease. Knowing the lessee was recovering from heart surgery, the collector called the lessee every hour for three days, shouted that deputies were on the way to jail him, and left similar messages with the lessee's receptionist. The lessee suffered severe anxiety attacks requiring treatment. The lessee sues the collector for intentional infliction of emotional distress. Is the lessee likely to prevail in an intentional infliction of emotional distress claim against the collector?
Answer A: No, because the lease debt was valid and overdue
Answer B: Yes, because the collector used extreme and outrageous methods that caused severe emotional distress
Answer C: No, because IIED requires physical impact or bodily injury
Answer D: Yes, but only because the collector was negligent in checking the remedies available
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Repeated threatening calls, false jail threats, workplace humiliation, and exploitation of known medical vulnerability can cross the outrage threshold and caused severe distress.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A legitimate purpose does not license extreme and outrageous collection methods, especially repeated threats exploiting known vulnerability. C: IIED requires severe emotional distress, not physical impact or physical injury. D: The claim turns on intentional or reckless outrageous conduct causing severe distress, not a negligent failure to research remedies.
lb_rule_reference: IIED requires extreme and outrageous conduct, intent or recklessness, causation, and severe emotional distress; a valid underlying demand does not excuse extreme methods.
