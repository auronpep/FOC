Outline: Appointment and removal
Question: A protester was unlawfully removed from a city park during one rally last year. She sues only for an injunction against future removals. She does not allege that she plans to attend another rally or that the city has a continuing policy likely to affect her again. What is the standing defect for injunctive relief?
Answer A: Past injury alone does not show a real and immediate threat of future injury
Answer B: Past injury always supports an injunction against future government conduct
Answer C: The case is ripe because the past event already happened
Answer D: The protester lacks standing because speech injuries are never concrete
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: An injunction requires likely future injury to this plaintiff, not merely a completed past wrong.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Forward-looking relief requires a future threat, not only a historical violation. C: The problem is standing for prospective relief, not ripeness of a completed dispute. D: Speech injuries can be concrete; the missing fact is likely future recurrence for the requested injunction.
lb_rule_reference: Past injury can support damages, but forward-looking injunctive relief requires a real and immediate threat that the plaintiff will be injured again.
