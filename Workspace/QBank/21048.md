Question: A merchant buyer and a merchant seller signed a contract for monthly shipments of cardboard boxes. The contract set a price of $4 per box and required payment within ten days. It also stated, in a signed clause, that modifications had to be in a signed writing. After signing, the buyer claims two later oral understandings: first, that the seller orally agreed to reduce the price to $3.75 for all future shipments, though no shipment was invoiced or paid at that price; second, that the seller allowed March and April payments to be made twenty days late and accepted those payments without objection. Which later understanding can the buyer most plausibly use?
Answer A: Both the price reduction and the late-payment understanding.
Answer B: The price reduction only.
Answer C: The late-payment understanding only.
Answer D: Neither understanding.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a signed no-oral-modification clause can block an unperformed oral modification, but an attempted modification or repeated accepted performance can operate as a waiver. The trigger facts are the signed NOM clause, the unperformed oral price reduction, and the seller's accepted late payments without objection. The decisive lock is term-by-term routing. The unperformed price reduction is barred by the signed-writing requirement; the late-payment conduct can support waiver of the strict ten-day payment term for those payments.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it treats an unperformed oral price change and an accepted payment practice as identical. B: B is wrong because the price reduction is the weaker term under the signed NOM clause. D: D is wrong because the accepted late payments may operate as waiver even if a formal oral modification fails.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-209(2), (4) / NOM clause and waiver split
