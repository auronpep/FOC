Question: In a federal civil trial, Esther sues a religious school. Miriam, a teacher at the school, testifies for the school. Esther asks Miriam whether she is a member of the same religious congregation that owns the school. The school objects under the rule barring evidence of religious beliefs to attack or support credibility. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because the question is offered to show Miriam's possible bias toward the school, not to attack her credibility based on religious belief.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because religious affiliation may never be mentioned when a witness's credibility is at issue.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Esther first proves that Miriam has lied for the congregation before.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Miriam has testified about her own reputation for truthfulness.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 610 bars use of religious beliefs or opinions to attack or support a witness's credibility, but it does not bar evidence of religious affiliation when offered to show bias, interest, or relationship to a party. Miriam's connection to the congregation that owns the defendant school is a permissible bias theory.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because Rule 610 does not bar religious-affiliation evidence offered for bias. C: C is wrong because prior lies are not required to show possible bias. D: D is wrong because bias impeachment is independent of truthful-character testimony.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 610 / religious affiliation offered for bias
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the religious-belief credibility bar versus religious-affiliation bias distinction.
