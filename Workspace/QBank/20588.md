Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Executive immunity
Question: While serving as President and running for reelection, the President personally directs campaign staff to publish a private rival's confidential business files obtained from a campaign volunteer. The act is not connected to any official agency decision, presidential communication with federal officers, or constitutional duty. After leaving office, the former President is sued for damages by the rival. The former President asserts absolute immunity. How should the court most likely classify the conduct?
Answer A: The conduct is automatically official because it occurred while the defendant was President
Answer B: The conduct is likely unofficial because it involved campaign activity and private publication unconnected to presidential duties
Answer C: The conduct is official because all campaign communications by a sitting President involve public policy
Answer D: The conduct is immune if the President believed publication would help national unity
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts tie the act to campaign staff and private political advantage, not to any official presidential responsibility. Unofficial conduct is not protected by presidential immunity merely because it occurred during the term.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Presidential immunity requires classifying the nature of the act. Officeholder status alone does not convert campaign conduct into official presidential action. C: Campaign activity is not automatically an official presidential function. The specific act must be tied to constitutional or statutory duties. D: Subjective motive cannot transform private campaign conduct into an official act.
lb_rule_reference: Presidential immunity does not extend to purely unofficial conduct, including campaign or private conduct outside presidential responsibilities.
