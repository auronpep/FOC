Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Kidnapping
Question: Sal kidnapped a rival by forcing him into a van and tying him up so Sal could question him later. Sal left the rival locked in the van on a hot afternoon while looking for another participant. The rival died from heat exposure. Sal was charged with felony murder predicated on kidnapping. Should the kidnapping predicate be barred by the merger doctrine?
Answer A: Yes, because every felony that causes death merges into the homicide
Answer B: Yes, because Sal did not intend the rival to die
Answer C: No, because kidnapping is an independent felony and the death occurred during that continuing restraint
Answer D: No, but only because heat exposure is always classified as arson for felony-murder purposes
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The kidnapping had an independent felonious purpose: seizure and restraint of the victim for later questioning. It was not merely the assaultive act that killed him. Because death occurred during the continuing kidnapping, the predicate is not barred by merger.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Merger targets assaultive felonies that are integral to the killing itself. Kidnapping is an independent felonious purpose and does not automatically merge. B: Felony murder does not require intent to kill. The qualifying felony supplies the malice when death occurs during the felony transaction. D: Heat exposure is not arson. The valid predicate is kidnapping, not an invented category.
lb_rule_reference: The merger doctrine bars assaultive predicates that are the homicide itself, but an independent felony such as kidnapping can support felony murder when death occurs during that felony.
