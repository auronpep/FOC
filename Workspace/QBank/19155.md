Question: After burglarizing a house at night, Amara ran through the yard carrying stolen jewelry. The homeowner chased her. Before reaching her car or any safe hiding place, Amara pushed the homeowner down a staircase in the yard, killing him. Is felony murder supported?
Answer A: No, because the burglary was complete once Amara left the house
Answer B: No, because the homeowner voluntarily chased Amara
Answer C: Yes, because the death occurred during immediate flight before Amara reached a place of temporary safety
Answer D: Yes, but only if Amara intended to kill the homeowner
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Amara had not escaped to a place of temporary safety. The killing occurred during immediate flight from nighttime burglary, a qualifying predicate. Felony murder is supported.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Felony murder extends through immediate flight until temporary safety. Amara was still fleeing the burglary when the death occurred. B: Pursuit is a foreseeable response to burglary and does not end the felony transaction. D: Felony murder does not require intent to kill when a death occurs during the felony transaction.
lb_rule_reference: The felony-murder transaction includes immediate flight from the felony until the felons reach a place of temporary safety.
