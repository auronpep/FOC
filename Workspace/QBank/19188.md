Question: Rebekah's lawyers produced 20,000 emails in federal discovery after using a privilege-review protocol. One privileged email to trial counsel was accidentally included. The next day, Rebekah's lawyer discovered the mistake, notified opposing counsel, and demanded return or sequestration of the email. Did the production necessarily waive privilege?
Answer A: Yes, because any production to an opponent waives privilege.
Answer B: Yes, because electronic discovery mistakes are treated as intentional disclosures.
Answer C: No, but only if opposing counsel had not yet opened the email.
Answer D: No, if the disclosure was inadvertent, Rebekah took reasonable preventive steps, and she promptly took reasonable steps to rectify the error.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 502(b) prevents waiver from an inadvertent disclosure in a federal proceeding if the holder took reasonable steps to prevent disclosure and promptly took reasonable steps to rectify it. The decisive axis is inadvertence plus reasonable prevention and correction.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is inadvertent-waiver absolutism rejected by Rule 502(b). B: B is wrong because The electronic-discovery setting is one reason Rule 502 exists; it does not convert mistakes into intentional waiver. C: C is wrong because Opening the email is not the Rule 502(b) test. Reasonable precautions and prompt rectification control.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 502(b) / inadvertent disclosure
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is applying an automatic-waiver rule instead of the three-part Rule 502(b) clawback standard.
