Question: Isaac agreed to sell a store to Miriam. The contract said, “Seller promises to remove all old display shelves before closing.” It did not say that closing was conditioned on removal. Isaac removed all but one shelf, which could be removed in an hour and did not affect use of the store. Miriam refused to close. What is the best analysis?
Answer A: Miriam may refuse to close because every seller promise is automatically a condition to the buyer's duty.
Answer B: Isaac's failure is at most a minor covenant breach on these facts; it does not automatically excuse Miriam's closing duty.
Answer C: Miriam may refuse to close because shelf removal is an express condition precedent.
Answer D: Isaac wins only if Miriam waived the shelf-removal promise in writing.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The contract uses promise language, not condition language. A minor failure to perform that promise may create damages, but it does not automatically discharge the buyer's closing duty unless the breach is material or the term is made a condition.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it collapses covenant into condition. C: C is wrong because the contract did not make shelf removal an express condition. D: D is wrong because waiver is not required when the breach is minor and not a condition failure.
