Question: A plaintiff serves a request asking the defendant to admit that a contract was signed by the defendant's president. The defendant denies the request without a reasonable basis. At trial, the plaintiff proves the signature through multiple undisputed records and then seeks the expenses of proving that matter. What is the likely result?
Answer A: Expenses may be awarded because the defendant failed to admit a matter that the plaintiff later proved, absent an applicable exception
Answer B: Expenses are never available for requests for admission
Answer C: The plaintiff must file a new lawsuit to recover those expenses
Answer D: Expenses are automatic for every denied request, even when the denial was reasonable
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 37(c)(2) shifts reasonable expenses when a party unreasonably denies a matter later proven.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Rule 36 admissions are backed by a specific expense-shifting mechanism. C: The expense request is made in the same action. D: Exceptions apply, including when the denial had a reasonable ground or the matter was of no substantial importance.
lb_rule_reference: If a party fails to admit a matter requested under Rule 36 and the requesting party later proves it, the court must generally award reasonable expenses unless an exception applies.
