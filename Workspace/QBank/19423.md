Question: In a federal civil trial, Aaron testifies for the plaintiff. On cross-examination, Aaron denies having been convicted of a felony for receiving stolen property five years ago. The defendant offers a certified copy of the judgment of conviction to prove the conviction for impeachment. Aaron objects that the defendant is using extrinsic evidence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the judgment because extrinsic evidence is never allowed to prove a conviction after denial.
Answer B: Exclude the judgment because receiving stolen property is not necessarily a false-statement crime.
Answer C: Admit the judgment, subject to the Rule 609 requirements and ordinary limits, because a conviction may be proved by the record if the witness denies or forgets it.
Answer D: Admit the judgment only if Aaron is a criminal defendant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Conviction impeachment is a distinct route from Rule 608(b) specific-act impeachment. If a witness denies or forgets the conviction, the proponent may prove the conviction by a proper record, subject to Rule 609 and other limits. The certified judgment is the proper proof channel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the Rule 608(b) extrinsic-evidence bar for specific acts does not bar proof of a conviction under Rule 609. B: B is wrong because a felony conviction may be used against a nondefendant witness subject to Rule 403 even if it is not a false-statement crime. D: D is wrong because Rule 609 applies to witnesses generally.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(a)(1)(A) and certified record proof / denial of conviction
