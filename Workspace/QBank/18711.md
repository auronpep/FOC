Question: HelioDawn Ltd., a corporation organized and headquartered in Singapore, allegedly violated a federal trademark statute by selling counterfeit goods through a global website. It made small sales to customers in many U.S. states, but no single state had enough contacts for state-court personal jurisdiction. The plaintiff filed a federal trademark claim in federal court and invoked Rule 4(k)(2). What is the strongest argument for personal jurisdiction?
Answer A: A federal claim automatically gives every federal court nationwide personal jurisdiction over any foreign defendant
Answer B: Rule 4(k)(2) may apply because the claim arises under federal law, no state has jurisdiction, and the defendant has sufficient U.S. contacts
Answer C: Rule 4(k)(2) cannot apply because only state long-arm statutes can ever authorize federal personal jurisdiction
Answer D: Jurisdiction is proper only if HelioDawn is incorporated in the forum state
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts match Rule 4(k)(2)'s federal long-arm structure: federal claim, foreign defendant not subject to any state's jurisdiction, and aggregate U.S. contacts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A federal claim alone is not enough; a statutory or rule-based service hook is needed. C: Rule 4(k)(2) is a federal authorization path in qualifying cases. D: Rule 4(k)(2) addresses specific jurisdiction for federal claims against defendants outside state-court reach.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 4(k)(2) can supply PJ for a claim arising under federal law if the defendant is not subject to jurisdiction in any state's courts of general jurisdiction and exercising jurisdiction is consistent with the Constitution and laws of the United States.
