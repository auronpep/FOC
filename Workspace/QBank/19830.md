Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Intentional infliction of emotional distress
Question: At an apartment lobby desk, a manager angrily told a tenant, "You are the most useless resident in this building," after the tenant complained about a package. The statement was made once, in front of two people. The tenant was embarrassed, cried that evening, and avoided the lobby for a week. The tenant sues the manager for intentional infliction of emotional distress. Is the tenant likely to prevail in an intentional infliction of emotional distress claim against the manager?
Answer A: Yes, because the manager intentionally used offensive words in public
Answer B: No, because the manager's single insult does not meet the extreme-and-outrageous threshold
Answer C: No, because words can never support IIED liability
Answer D: Yes, because the tenant actually felt emotional distress
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A one-time insult and embarrassment, without special aggravating facts or severe distress, is ordinarily not extreme and outrageous enough for IIED.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: IIED requires extreme and outrageous conduct, not merely insulting or rude words. C: Words can support IIED when accompanied by extreme circumstances such as repetition, abuse of authority, known vulnerability, or severe distress. The issue here is threshold, not a categorical bar. D: Actual upset alone does not establish IIED. The conduct must be extreme and outrageous, and the distress must be severe.
lb_rule_reference: Ordinary insults, rudeness, and indignities usually are not extreme and outrageous enough for IIED, even if upsetting.
