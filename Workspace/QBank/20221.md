Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: Esther is charged with stealing equipment from a loading yard. The prosecutor offers phone-location data showing that Esther's phone connected to a tower covering the loading yard and surrounding streets at the time of the theft. Esther objects that the tower covers several blocks and cannot place her in the yard itself. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the data, because it does not place Esther precisely inside the loading yard.
Answer B: Exclude the data unless the prosecution proves Esther personally held the phone at that moment.
Answer C: Admit the data, because it has some tendency to place Esther near the yard at the relevant time; imprecision goes to weight.
Answer D: Admit the data only if no other person could have used Esther's phone.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The location data makes Esther's presence near the loading yard somewhat more probable. Its imprecision and the possibility of another user affect weight, not threshold relevance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because exact location is not required for relevance. B: B is wrong because personal possession may be a foundation or weight issue, but the data is not irrelevant merely because another inference is possible. D: D is wrong because the proponent need not eliminate every alternative explanation at the relevance stage.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating imprecision as no probative value.
