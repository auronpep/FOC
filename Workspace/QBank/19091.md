Question: A builder agreed to construct custom shelves for Martha, with final payment due “only if Martha is honestly satisfied with the finished shelves.” The builder completed shelves matching the agreed design. Martha privately admitted the shelves were exactly what she ordered but said she was “not satisfied” because she wanted to avoid paying the final installment. If the builder sues for the final installment, what is the likely result?
Answer A: The builder may recover because Martha's bad-faith dissatisfaction does not prevent the payment duty from maturing.
Answer B: Martha owes nothing because subjective satisfaction clauses give the buyer absolute power to refuse payment for any reason.
Answer C: Martha owes nothing unless the builder proves the shelves were objectively perfect in every respect.
Answer D: The builder may recover only in restitution because satisfaction clauses eliminate contract remedies.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A subjective satisfaction condition requires honest good faith. Martha admitted the shelves matched the order and invoked dissatisfaction only to avoid paying. Her bad-faith refusal prevents her from relying on the condition's nonoccurrence, so the payment duty may mature.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because subjective satisfaction is not absolute bad-faith discretion. C: C is wrong because the issue is honest satisfaction, not objective perfection. D: D is wrong because the contract remedy remains available when the condition is treated as satisfied or excused.
lb_rule_reference: Satisfaction condition / good faith
