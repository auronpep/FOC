Question: John agreed to buy wool from Esther for delivery on August 1. The contract also stated, “Seller shall email monthly inventory updates.” Esther delivered conforming wool on August 1 but had failed to send one inventory update in June. John refused to pay, arguing that Esther's missed update ended his payment duty. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: John need not pay because every promise by the seller is a condition to the buyer's payment duty.
Answer B: John may reject the wool because a missed inventory update is a perfect-tender defect.
Answer C: John must pay for the conforming wool, though he may recover any damages caused by the missed update.
Answer D: John's duty to pay is discharged only if Esther's missed update was intentional.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The inventory update promise is a covenant, not a condition to payment for conforming goods. John's payment duty for conforming delivered goods is not automatically discharged by the missed update. His remedy, if any, is damages for the covenant breach.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it collapses covenants into conditions. B: B is wrong because the wool itself was conforming; the missed update does not make the goods nonconforming. D: D is wrong because intent is not the dividing line; the issue is duty status and legal consequence.
