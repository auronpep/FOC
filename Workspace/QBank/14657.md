Question: A statute in the jurisdiction defines murder in the first degree as knowingly killing another person after deliberation. Deliberation is defined as "cool reflection for any length of time no matter how brief." Murder in the second degree is defined as "all other murder at common law except felony-murder." Felony-murder is murder in the third degree. Manslaughter is defined by the common law. At 2 a.m., the defendant held up an all-night liquor store using an assault rifle. During the holdup, two police cars with flashing lights drove up in front of the store. In order to create a situation where the police would hesitate to come into the store (and thus give the defendant a chance to escape out the back), the defendant fired several rounds through the front window of the store. The defendant then ran out the back, but upon discovering another police car there, surrendered quietly. One of the shots he fired while in the store struck and killed a burglar who was stealing items from a closed store across the street. The most serious degree of criminal homicide the defendant is guilty of is
Answer A: A: murder in first degree.
Answer B: B: murder in the second degree.
Answer C: C: murder in the third degree.
Answer D: D: manslaughter.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: B is correct. The act of firing an assault rifle through the front window of the store, especially knowing there were people outside, clearly demonstrates the defendant's reckless indifference to an unjustifiably high risk to human life. This type of action is sufficient to satisfy the malice aforethought requirement for a murder charge. Therefore, the defendant killed a human being, the burglar, with malice aforethought and is guilty of second degree murder under the state statute.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The statute defines murder in the first degree as "knowingly" killing another person after deliberation. Here, the defendant did not "knowingly" kill another person after deliberation; in fact, the defendant lacked the specific intent to kill or to do great bodily harm to anyone, as he was not aware the burglar existed. C is incorrect. Although the defendant likely did commit murder in the third degree (felony murder), that is not the most serious degree of criminal homicide the defendant is guilty of committing. D is incorrect. It ignores the defendant's guilt as to both felony murder and murder in the second degree. The defendant did kill another with malice aforethought but did not knowingly kill another person after deliberation. Therefore, the most he can be guilty of is murder in the second degree.
