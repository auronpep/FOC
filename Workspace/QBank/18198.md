


BARMATRIX Q#: 18198
Outline_code: 81020102
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Rights in Land
Outline: Creation
Question: The landowner owned a small parcel of real estate that fronted on a lake, with a dock providing access to the lake. Because his friend owned a boat and enjoyed fishing on the lake, the landowner told him orally that he could launch his boat from the dock whenever he wanted. The landowner subsequently sold the realty to the buyer, advising him that his friend, the fisherman, had permission to launch his boat from the dock. When he took title, the buyer assured the landowner that he would continue to permit the fisherman to use the dock. The next time the fisherman attempted to do so, however, the buyer ordered him off the realty and told him not to enter it again. If the fisherman sues for an order directing the buyer to permit him to continue using the dock for launching his boat, the court should find for
Answer A: A: the fisherman, because the buyer purchased the realty with knowledge of the fisherman's right.
Answer B: B: the fisherman, because an easement in gross survives the sale of the servient estate.
Answer C: C: the buyer, because the fisherman's right to use the dock terminated or was revoked.
Answer D: D: the buyer, because an easement appurtenant does not survive the sale of the servient estate.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement is an interest in realty. A license is permission to use realty. An interest in realty cannot be conveyed except by written instrument. Thus, the oral permission that the landowner gave the fisherman could not have created an easement and must have created a license. Unless consideration is given for it, a license to use realty is revokable at will by the licensor or his or her successor. In most jurisdictions, a license automatically terminates when the realty is sold by the licensor. In those jurisdictions, the fisherman's license terminated upon the landowner's sale to the buyer. In a few jurisdictions, a license survives the sale of the realty, but if it was a revocable license, it remains revocable. Since the fisherman did not give consideration for the license, it was revocable, and if it survived the sale to the buyer, it was effectively revoked when the buyer ordered the fisherman off the property.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect because knowledge by a grantee that the grantor created a license to use the realty does not cause the license to survive the sale. B and D are incorrect because the oral permission could not have resulted in any easement. D is also incorrect because an easement appurtenant does survive the sale of the servient estate.
