Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Ruth signed and delivered a deed conveying Wheatacre to Boaz, saying, “Wheatacre is yours now.” Boaz accepted. A week later, Boaz handed the original deed back to Ruth for safekeeping while his house was being repaired. No new deed was signed. Ruth later claimed that receiving the paper back restored title to her. Who owns Wheatacre?
Answer A: Ruth, because the person holding the original deed owns the land.
Answer B: Ruth, because Boaz's return of the deed automatically revoked the earlier conveyance.
Answer C: Boaz only if he recorded the deed before returning it to Ruth.
Answer D: Boaz, because title had already passed by valid delivery and returning the paper for safekeeping did not reconvey land to Ruth.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery/result locks are valid delivery followed by no reconveyance. Ruth delivered the deed with present transfer words, and Boaz accepted. Once title passed, Boaz's later return of the paper for safekeeping did not transfer title back; a reconveyance of land would require its own valid conveyance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because possession of the paper is not ownership after title has passed. B: B is wrong because deed delivery is not revoked merely by returning the document. C: C is wrong because recording was not required for title to pass between Ruth and Boaz.
lb_rule_reference: Returning the paper deed for safekeeping after valid delivery does not reconvey title
