Question: Maya is arraigned on a burglary charge and counsel is appointed. The next day, police bring the eyewitness to the station and have Maya stand alone behind one-way glass. Counsel is not notified. The witness identifies Maya as the burglar. What is the best Sixth Amendment argument?
Answer A: The identification should be suppressed because the post-arraignment showup was a corporeal critical stage conducted without counsel
Answer B: There is no Sixth Amendment issue because Wade applies only to multi-person lineups
Answer C: There is no Sixth Amendment issue because one-way glass kept the witness from speaking to Maya
Answer D: The identification is automatically admissible if the witness is certain
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Arraignment attached the right, and a one-person showup with the accused physically displayed to a witness is a corporeal confrontation requiring counsel after attachment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The concern is corporeal confrontation after attachment. A showup can be a critical stage too. C: Identification counsel protects the accused during corporeal confrontation; questioning is not required. D: Certainty may matter to due process reliability, but it does not eliminate a post-attachment counsel violation.
lb_rule_reference: After formal proceedings begin, a corporeal identification confrontation between witness and accused requires counsel unless validly waived.
