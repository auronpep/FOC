Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: In a federal antitrust case, Esther offers an economist's chart comparing prices in 40 markets. The chart is complex, but the economist can explain it and it directly addresses damages. The defendant objects under Rule 403, arguing only that the chart will require the jurors to think carefully. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Exclude the chart because difficult evidence is always confusing.
Answer B: Admit the chart if its probative value is not substantially outweighed by a real danger of confusion or misleading the jury; complexity alone is not enough.
Answer C: Exclude the chart unless every juror already understands economics.
Answer D: Admit the chart only if the defendant agrees it is easy to understand.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 403 concerns confusion, misleading the jury, and related risks, not the mere fact that evidence is complex. If the chart is probative and can be explained, complexity alone does not justify exclusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because difficult evidence is not automatically confusing under Rule 403. C: C is wrong because expert evidence often helps jurors understand unfamiliar subjects. D: D is wrong because the opponent's agreement is not required for admission.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403
lb_student_diagnostic: Student equates difficult proof with Rule 403 confusion.
