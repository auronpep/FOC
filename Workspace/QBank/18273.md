Question: Talia voluntarily becomes intoxicated at a party and later has sex with a person who is under the statutory age of consent. The jurisdiction treats statutory rape as strict liability as to age. Talia argues she was too drunk to realize the person's age. How does Talia's voluntary intoxication affect the charge?
Answer A: It is a defense because intoxication prevented knowledge of age
Answer B: It is a defense if the intoxication was severe enough
Answer C: It is not a defense because age is a strict-liability element
Answer D: It becomes an insanity defense because intoxication impaired perception
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Voluntary intoxication can negate specific intent, but it cannot negate a mental state that the law does not require. Because age is strict liability in this jurisdiction, Talia's intoxication does not defeat the charge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The jurisdiction makes age strict liability. There is no age-related mens rea for intoxication to negate. B: Severity does not matter when the element is strict liability and the intoxication was voluntary. D: Acute voluntary intoxication is not legal insanity absent a separate mental disease or defect.
lb_rule_reference: Voluntary intoxication cannot defeat strict-liability crimes or strict-liability elements because no mens rea exists for intoxication to negate
