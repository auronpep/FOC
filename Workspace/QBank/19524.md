Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal civil fraud case, Joseph testifies that he heard a speaker on a conference call approve a false invoice. Joseph had spoken with Benjamin in person several times during the same week and says the conference-call voice was Benjamin's, though the connection was poor and another speaker occasionally talked over him. Benjamin objects that the identification is too weak to admit. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a poor connection makes any voice identification inadmissible.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Joseph can identify Benjamin's voice with absolute certainty.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if an expert performs a voiceprint analysis.
Answer D: Overrule the objection if Joseph's familiarity supplies enough evidence to support a finding of identity; weaknesses go to weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A familiar witness's voice identification may satisfy the authentication threshold even if the call quality was imperfect. Poor connection and overlapping speakers are weight arguments for the jury, not automatic exclusion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because poor quality does not categorically bar voice identification. B: B is wrong because absolute certainty is not required. C: C is wrong because expert voice analysis is not required when lay familiarity provides sufficient foundation.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(5)
lb_student_diagnostic: Weakness-as-exclusion trap.
