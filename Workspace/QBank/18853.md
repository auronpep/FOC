Question: In a federal civil trial, Rebecca seeks to testify that she personally saw Samuel deliver the machinery. The judge excludes the testimony, saying delivery must be proved by a receipt. Rebecca's lawyer says only, “We object,” and makes no record of what Rebecca would say. The substance of the testimony is not otherwise apparent. On appeal, is the evidentiary claim preserved?
Answer A: Yes, because any exclusion of live testimony is automatically preserved.
Answer B: Yes, because documentary proof was not required.
Answer C: No, because after evidence is excluded the proponent must make the substance apparent by an offer of proof unless it is already apparent from context.
Answer D: No, because testimonial evidence can never prove delivery.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Even if the exclusion was wrong, a party generally must preserve a claim of erroneous exclusion by making an offer of proof unless the substance was apparent. Here the record does not show what Rebecca would have testified to.
