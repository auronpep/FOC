Outline: Delegation Clause
Question: Congress authorizes a workplace-safety agency to set exposure limits for hazardous chemicals at levels "reasonably necessary or appropriate to protect employee health, considering feasibility and available scientific evidence." The agency issues a rule limiting exposure to a solvent used in manufacturing. Employers challenge the statute as an unconstitutional delegation. How should the nondelegation challenge most likely be resolved?
Answer A: The challenge should succeed because Congress may never let agencies make policy judgments
Answer B: The challenge should likely fail because the statute gives the agency a guiding standard tied to health, feasibility, and scientific evidence
Answer C: The challenge should succeed because every agency safety rule presents a major question
Answer D: The challenge should fail only if the President personally approves the exposure limit
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The statute supplies an intelligible principle by directing the agency to protect employee health while considering feasibility and science. That kind of guidance usually satisfies nondelegation review.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Congress may delegate policy implementation if it supplies an intelligible principle. The Constitution does not require Congress to write every exposure limit itself. C: The facts describe ordinary workplace exposure regulation under a specific standard, not a transformative agency claim over a major national policy issue. D: Nondelegation asks whether Congress supplied a sufficient standard, not whether the President signed the final rule.
lb_rule_reference: A statutory delegation usually survives nondelegation review if Congress supplies an intelligible principle to guide the agency.
