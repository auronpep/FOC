Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: A state trooper pulled over Dana for speeding on the interstate. The trooper approached the window and asked, "Have you been drinking tonight?" Without any Miranda warnings, Dana replied, "Just a couple of beers." The trooper then administered field sobriety tests, which Dana failed. Dana was arrested and charged with DUI. Dana moves to suppress her statement about drinking. Should the court suppress Dana's statement that she had consumed alcohol?
Answer A: Yes, because the trooper asked a question designed to elicit an incriminating response before giving Miranda warnings
Answer B: Yes, because Dana was seized within the meaning of the Fourth Amendment and therefore in custody for Miranda purposes
Answer C: No, because a routine traffic stop does not constitute Miranda custody, so no warnings were required before the trooper's question
Answer D: No, because the booking questions exception permits police to ask basic questions without Miranda warnings
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under *Berkemer v. McCarty* (1984), a roadside traffic stop is not Miranda custody even though the driver is not free to leave, because the detention is presumptively temporary and public. The objective reasonable-person test is not satisfied: a reasonable person in Dana's position would understand this as a brief traffic stop, not a formal arrest. Because custody was absent, Miranda warnings were not required and the statement is admissible.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Miranda's interrogation prong is irrelevant unless custody is first established. A routine traffic stop is not custody under *Berkemer v. McCarty*. The stop is brief, occurs in public, and a reasonable person would not believe they were under formal arrest. B: Fourth Amendment seizure and Miranda custody are distinct standards. A Terry stop or traffic stop constitutes a Fourth Amendment seizure but not Miranda custody. Miranda custody requires the more demanding showing that a reasonable person would not feel free to leave in a formal-arrest sense. D: The booking exception applies to routine administrative questions incident to booking after arrest. It does not apply here, and is unnecessary: the correct basis for admissibility is that no custody existed, not that an exception applied.
lb_rule_reference: A routine traffic stop does not constitute Miranda custody because a reasonable person would understand the detention is brief and limited; Miranda warnings are not required before questioning during a traffic stop.
