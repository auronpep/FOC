Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Mary contracted with a bank for a construction loan to convert her warehouse into a clinic. The bank wrongfully refused to fund the loan. Mary cannot prove the clinic's future profits with reasonable certainty. Before the refusal, Mary reasonably spent $32,000 demolishing the old interior and $6,000 obtaining permits for the planned conversion. Even without the loan, the demolition left Mary's warehouse worth $9,000 more than before. What is Mary's reliance recovery?
Answer A: $38,000, because Mary actually paid that amount because of the project.
Answer B: $9,000, because that is the remaining increase in property value.
Answer C: $29,000, the reliance expenditures minus the retained value increase.
Answer D: Mary's projected clinic profits, because the bank knew the loan was for a clinic.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is reliance under common law for the bank's nonperformance. The formula is reasonable reliance expenditures minus value retained to avoid overcompensation: $32,000 demolition + $6,000 permits − $9,000 retained property-value increase = $29,000. The limiting gates are certainty and offset, and the result scope is limited. The answer does not award projected clinic profits because those profits are not proven with reasonable certainty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it omits the retained-value offset and overcompensates Mary. B: B is wrong because retained value is an offset, not the damages measure. D: D is wrong because knowledge of the clinic purpose does not cure speculative proof of profits.
lb_rule_reference: Common law reliance damages — net expenditure loss after retained benefit offset
