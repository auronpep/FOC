Question: Residents sue in federal court claiming that their state's government is no longer republican in form because the legislature uses procedures the residents consider undemocratic. They rely only on the Constitution's guarantee of a republican form of government. What is the likely Article III result?
Answer A: The claim is likely nonjusticiable under political-question doctrine
Answer B: The claim must receive strict scrutiny because voting and democracy are fundamental
Answer C: The claim is moot because the legislature already adopted its procedures
Answer D: The claim is automatically justiciable because every constitutional clause is enforceable in court
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Guarantee Clause claims are generally treated as political questions because courts lack manageable standards for deciding them.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The threshold political-question bar prevents jumping to scrutiny under this theory. C: The issue is not that the controversy ended; it is that the claimed standard is nonjusticiable. D: Some constitutional questions are committed to the political branches or lack manageable standards.
lb_rule_reference: Claims under the Guarantee Clause are generally treated as nonjusticiable political questions because they lack judicially manageable standards and are committed to the political branches.
