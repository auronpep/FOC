MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Foreseeable plaintiffs
Question: A furniture-store employee negligently pushed a lightweight foam display panel off a loading platform into a fenced service area after checking that no workers were present. Unknown to anyone at the store, an adult had climbed into a closed recycling bin in that fenced area to sleep. The foam panel landed on the bin lid, startled the sleeper, and caused him to hit his head while trying to climb out. The sleeper sues the store for negligence. Is the sleeper likely to prevail in a negligence claim against the store?
Answer A: Yes, because the store had a duty to avoid careless conduct that injures any person on its premises
Answer B: No, because the sleeper was not foreseeably within the risk field created by the employee's act
Answer C: Yes, because the serious head injury makes the risk foreseeable in retrospect
Answer D: No, because negligence law never protects people who are trespassing
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Before the accident, a person secretly sleeping inside a closed recycling bin in a fenced service area was not a foreseeable plaintiff endangered by a lightweight panel tossed after a visual check.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The controlling issue is not general premises ownership. The sleeper was hidden in a closed bin inside a fenced service area, so he was not a foreseeable plaintiff within the risk created by pushing the foam panel. C: The seriousness of the eventual harm does not expand the class of foreseeable plaintiffs. Duty is measured before the accident, not after the injury is known. D: The better reason is foreseeable-plaintiff duty, not a universal no-duty rule for trespassers. Some trespassers can be owed limited duties under premises rules.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant's duty does not extend to an unforeseeable hidden plaintiff outside the apparent risk field, even if the plaintiff is badly hurt.
