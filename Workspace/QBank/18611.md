Question: A cyclist was hit by one of two identical stones thrown from opposite sidewalks. One person threw a stone negligently toward traffic. The other threw a stone carefully into an empty trash bin, but a gust may have deflected it. The cyclist sues both throwers but cannot show that the careful thrower breached any duty. May the cyclist use alternative liability to shift the burden to both defendants?
Answer A: Yes, because two possible causes always trigger burden shifting
Answer B: Yes, because the injury was indivisible
Answer C: No, because the cyclist has not shown that all possible defendants acted negligently
Answer D: No, because alternative liability applies only to product cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Alternative liability generally requires negligent conduct by each possible tortfeasor before burden shifting.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The doctrine is narrower than any two-actor uncertainty. B: Indivisible injury alone does not satisfy the doctrine. D: It is not limited to products; the issue here is the missing negligence showing.
lb_rule_reference: Alternative liability does not apply when the plaintiff cannot show that all joined possible causes were negligent.
