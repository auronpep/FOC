Question: A seller agreed to sell 100 wool blankets to a buyer for $5,000. The contract did not specify a credit term. On the delivery date, the seller arrived with conforming blankets and asked for payment. The buyer demanded that the seller leave the blankets and send an invoice payable in 30 days. The seller refused and left with the blankets. If the buyer sues the seller for nondelivery, what is the likely result?
Answer A: The buyer wins because payment for goods is always due a reasonable time after delivery.
Answer B: The buyer wins because the seller must leave the goods before requesting payment.
Answer C: The seller wins because, absent a credit term, tender of conforming goods generally entitles the seller to payment as a concurrent condition.
Answer D: The seller wins only if the buyer was insolvent.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Article 2, tender of delivery generally entitles the seller to acceptance and payment according to the contract. When no credit term is specified, the buyer's payment duty is tied to the seller's tender. The buyer could not demand free delivery with payment 30 days later.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because there is no automatic post-delivery credit term. B: B is wrong because the seller need not surrender conforming goods before payment when no credit term exists. D: D is wrong because the seller's right to concurrent payment does not depend on buyer insolvency.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-507 / 2-511
