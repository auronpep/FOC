Question: David agreed to buy 200 lamps from a seller for cash on delivery. The contract did not require either side to perform first. On the delivery date, the seller arrived at David's warehouse with conforming lamps and asked for payment. David said he would pay a week later after reselling some of the lamps. The seller refused to unload the lamps. Was the seller's refusal to unload a breach?
Answer A: No, because under a cash-on-delivery goods contract, the buyer's tender of payment is generally due when the seller tenders conforming goods.
Answer B: Yes, because a seller must always unload goods before demanding payment.
Answer C: No, but only because David repudiated the contract before the delivery date.
Answer D: Yes, because payment is not due until the buyer has inspected and resold the goods.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Article 2, tender of delivery and tender of payment are ordinarily concurrent unless the contract provides otherwise. The seller tendered conforming goods and could condition delivery on David's payment. David had no right to delay payment for a week.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the seller need not deliver unconditionally before receiving payment in a cash-on-delivery transaction. C: C is wrong because the issue is concurrent tender at delivery, not anticipatory repudiation. D: D is wrong because resale is not the default trigger for payment.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-507 / tender and payment as concurrent conditions
