Question: Peter hired Paul to install three identical display cases in Peter's store for $2,000 per case. Paul installed two cases properly, then refused without justification to install the third. Peter refused to pay anything for the two installed cases. What is Paul most likely entitled to recover in a contract action?
Answer A: Nothing, because failure to complete the third case is a total breach of the entire agreement as a matter of law.
Answer B: The full $6,000 contract price because two of three cases is substantial performance of the whole contract.
Answer C: The contract price for the two completed cases, subject to damages Peter has from the failure to install the third.
Answer D: Only the reasonable value of the materials because divisible contracts cannot use contract prices.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A contract priced separately by identical units is likely divisible. Paul materially breached as to the third case, but he properly completed the first two divisible units. He may recover the contract price for completed units, subject to damages for the breach of the remaining unit.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because divisible contracts avoid an all-or-nothing result. B: B is wrong because Paul did not earn the price for the unperformed third case. D: D is wrong because divisible contract recovery can use the agreed price for completed units.
lb_rule_reference: Divisible contract doctrine
