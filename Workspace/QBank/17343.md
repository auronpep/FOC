Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: In a federal extortion trial, Deborah testifies that she heard a threatening caller whose voice sounded like Gideon's. She had spoken with Gideon in person and by phone for years. Deborah says, “I believe it was Gideon, though the caller spoke softly.” Gideon objects for lack of foundation. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because voice identification must be certain.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because the call was not recorded.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony because only an expert may identify a disguised voice.
Answer D: Admit the testimony because Deborah's prior familiarity with Gideon's voice supplies a foundation; the softness of the voice affects weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Voice identification may be based on hearing the voice at any time under circumstances connecting it with the alleged speaker. Deborah's long familiarity supplies foundation. Her caution and the caller's soft voice affect the weight the jury gives the testimony.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because certainty is not required for admissibility. B: B is wrong because a recording is not required. C: C is wrong because ordinary voice identification can be made by a lay witness familiar with the voice.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(5) / uncertain voice identification
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests foundation sufficiency despite qualified voice identification.
