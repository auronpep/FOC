Outline: Delivery and acceptance
Question: Sarah signed a deed conveying Groveacre to Rebekah. Sarah handed the deed to Rebekah and said, “I am delivering this deed to you now, but you must plant one hundred trees before you record it.” Rebekah accepted the deed. Before the trees were planted, Sarah tried to revoke the conveyance. What is the best result?
Answer A: Sarah owns Groveacre because the condition was not satisfied before Sarah revoked.
Answer B: Sarah owns Groveacre because Rebekah had not yet recorded the deed.
Answer C: Rebekah owns Groveacre because Sarah delivered the deed directly to Rebekah with present-transfer intent; the oral recording condition does not undo delivery.
Answer D: Rebekah owns Groveacre only if she paid consideration before Sarah revoked.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive delivery lock is present intent at the direct grantee delivery. Sarah said she was delivering the deed now and gave the original to Rebekah. The oral condition about planting trees before recording did not prevent title from passing between the parties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a condition attached to a direct delivery to the grantee does not function like an escrow condition when present delivery intent exists. B: B is wrong because recording is not required for delivery between grantor and grantee. D: D is wrong because consideration is not required for a deed to be delivered.
lb_rule_reference: A deed delivered directly to the grantee with present-transfer words is not defeated by an oral condition
