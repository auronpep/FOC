Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Supplemental Jurisdiction
Question: Plaintiff 1, a State A citizen, sues Defendant, a State B citizen, for $120,000 from a bus crash. Plaintiff 2, a State B citizen, seeks to join with a $20,000 claim against the same Defendant arising from the same crash. Plaintiffs argue that supplemental jurisdiction covers Plaintiff 2 because the claims share a common nucleus of fact. May Plaintiff 2 proceed under supplemental jurisdiction in this ordinary diversity case?
Answer A: Yes, because all claims arising from the same accident fall within supplemental jurisdiction
Answer B: Yes, because Plaintiff 1 satisfies the amount in controversy
Answer C: No, because Plaintiff 2 shares citizenship with Defendant and supplemental jurisdiction cannot cure the complete-diversity defect
Answer D: No, but only because Plaintiff 2's claim is below $75,000
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Complete diversity fails once Plaintiff 2 asserts a claim against a same-state defendant in a diversity-only case.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Same accident facts satisfy the common-nucleus idea, but complete diversity is still required in an ordinary diversity case. B: Amount cannot cure the nondiverse plaintiff's citizenship problem. D: The deeper problem is nondiverse citizenship, not merely the lower amount.
lb_rule_reference: In a diversity-only case, supplemental jurisdiction cannot be used to include a plaintiff whose presence destroys complete diversity.
