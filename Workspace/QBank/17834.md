Outline: Actual Takings
Question: Caleb applied for a permit to build a small lawful addition to his home. The city agreed to issue the permit only if Caleb dedicated a 30-foot-wide public bicycle path across the back of his lot. The city made no individualized finding that Caleb's addition would create bicycle traffic or require a path, and the width of the requested path was not tied to any projected impact of the addition. Caleb challenges the condition. Which is the best result?
Answer A: The condition is valid because any permit condition imposed under zoning authority is immune from takings review.
Answer B: The condition is valid because a city may demand any public benefit in exchange for a discretionary permit.
Answer C: The condition is likely invalid unless the city can show an essential nexus and rough proportionality between the condition and the project's impacts.
Answer D: The condition is valid only if the city will own the bicycle path in fee simple.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A land-use exaction requiring dedication of property as a permit condition must satisfy the unconstitutional-conditions framework. The government generally must show an essential nexus and rough proportionality between the condition and the projected impact of the proposed development. The city made no individualized nexus or proportionality showing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because zoning authority does not immunize exactions from takings scrutiny. B: B is wrong because discretionary permitting does not allow unrelated property demands. D: D is wrong because an exaction problem can arise from required dedication of an easement or access right, not only transfer of fee title.
lb_rule_reference: Exaction / essential nexus and rough proportionality
