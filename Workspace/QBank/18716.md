Question: In a federal fraud trial, the prosecutor has already called four bank employees who each testified that Joseph personally opened the disputed account. The prosecutor then offers six additional bank employees who would give the same identification testimony from the same day. Joseph objects that the additional witnesses should not be heard. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection, because relevant evidence can never be excluded when identity is disputed.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because each additional witness has personal knowledge.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because the additional testimony is irrelevant once four witnesses have testified.
Answer D: Sustain the objection in the court's discretion if the marginal probative value is substantially outweighed by cumulative presentation and wasted time.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The testimony remains relevant, but Rule 403 permits exclusion when additional proof adds little and would create needless cumulative presentation or waste time. The court's power is discretionary and rests on balancing, not on irrelevance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because relevant evidence may still be excluded under Rule 403. B: B is wrong because personal knowledge does not prevent exclusion for cumulative proof. C: C is wrong because cumulative evidence can be relevant; the problem is marginal value versus time and repetition.
