Question: A city zoning ordinance allows a household to include parents and children, but forbids a grandmother from living with her grandchildren in the same single-family home. The family challenges the ordinance as direct interference with family living arrangements. What is the best constitutional route?
Answer A: Treat it as ordinary economic zoning with only rational basis and no family liberty concern
Answer B: Analyze it as a substantive due process family-integrity burden
Answer C: Uphold it automatically because grandparents are never family for constitutional purposes
Answer D: Resolve it only under the Dormant Commerce Clause
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The ordinance directly excludes close relatives from living together, implicating protected family liberty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The rule directly burdens close family living arrangements, which can trigger substantive due process protection. C: Protected family arrangements can extend beyond parents and minor children in close family households. D: The dispute is about family living arrangements, not interstate commerce.
lb_rule_reference: Substantive due process protects certain family integrity interests, including close family living arrangements, against unjustified direct interference.
