Outline: Offers of proof
Question: In a federal civil trial, David offers a letter written by Jonathan. Abigail believes the letter is irrelevant and prejudicial, but she makes no objection when it is offered and does not move to strike it after it is read. After losing, Abigail argues that admitting the letter was reversible error. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The claim is ordinarily unpreserved; an appellate court may notice only a plain error affecting a substantial right.
Answer B: The claim is preserved because relevance objections may be raised for the first time after judgment.
Answer C: The claim is preserved because David, as proponent, had to make an offer of proof.
Answer D: The claim is preserved because a party never has to object to documentary evidence.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A party ordinarily preserves a claim of error in admitting evidence by a timely specific objection or motion to strike. Abigail did neither. Rule 103 preserves a narrow plain-error possibility, but that is not ordinary preservation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because relevance and prejudice objections generally must be timely. C: C is wrong because offers of proof relate to excluded evidence, not to evidence admitted over no objection. D: D is wrong because documents are not exempt from objection requirements.
