Question: A testator owned Blackacre, a vacant one-acre tract of land. Five years ago, he executed a deed conveying Blackacre to "the church for the purpose of erecting a church building thereon." Three years ago, the testator died leaving his son as his sole heir at law. His duly probated will left "all my estate, both real and personal, to my friend." The church never constructed a church building on Blackacre and last month the church, for valid consideration, conveyed Blackacre to a developer. The developer brought an appropriate action to quiet title against the son, the friend, and the church, and joined the appropriate state official. Such official asserted that a charitable trust was created which has not terminated. In such action, the court should find that title is now in
Answer A: A: the developer.
Answer B: B: the son.
Answer C: C: the friend.
Answer D: D: the state official.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A is correct. The testator used the language "for the purpose of," which has no effect on the title, so the church held fee simple absolute. Therefore, after the church transferred title to the developer, the developer held in fee simple.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect. As explained above, the title belongs to the developer. The son receives nothing because the testator devised his entire estate to his friend. C is incorrect. The property was given with no restrictions, in fee simple absolute to the church, which validly conveyed it to a developer. D is incorrect. The property never escheated to the state because the testator had a valid will.
