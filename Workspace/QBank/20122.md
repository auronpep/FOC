Question: Kevin walks through a crowded mall and lifts a wallet from a shopper's back pocket without the shopper noticing. Kevin walks away and is twenty feet down the hall when the shopper realizes his wallet is gone and shouts "Stop, thief!" Kevin pushes the shopper to the ground to prevent him from grabbing him back, and runs out of the mall. Is Kevin guilty of robbery?
Answer A: Yes, because Kevin used force in connection with the theft of the wallet
Answer B: No, because Kevin pickpocketed the wallet without the victim's awareness, and robbery requires the victim to be aware of the taking
Answer C: No, because the force occurred after the larceny was already complete, so robbery's force element is not satisfied
Answer D: Yes, because Kevin used force to retain the property after it was taken, which satisfies robbery under the fresh-pursuit doctrine
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Common-law robbery requires a trespassory taking from the person or presence of another, by force or intimidation. The force must occur in the course of — contemporaneously with — the taking. Kevin completed the larceny (pickpocketed the wallet and moved away undetected) before any force was used. The subsequent push was to facilitate escape from a completed crime, not to accomplish the taking. Kevin is guilty of larceny (the pickpocket) and battery (the push), but not robbery, because the force and the taking did not concur.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Robbery requires that force or intimidation be used in the course of taking the property — meaning at or before the moment the taking is accomplished. Kevin completed the larceny (took the wallet with dominion and control and movement away) before any force occurred. The force was used to escape after the larceny was already complete. Common-law robbery requires temporal and causal connection between the force and the taking, which is absent here. B: While robbery typically involves confrontation, victim awareness is not an independent element. Robbery requires force or intimidation, but that force need not occur while the victim is aware — for example, grabbing a bag from a person's hand (purse-snatching) can be robbery even if the victim's attention is elsewhere. The reason Kevin is not guilty of robbery here is the timing of the force (post-taking), not victim unawareness. D: At common law, the majority rule is that force must be used in the course of the taking, not afterward to retain or escape with it. The "fresh pursuit" or "immediate flight" extension is an MPC/modern statutory concept, not the traditional common-law rule tested on MBE questions asking about common law.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law robbery requires that force or intimidation be used in the course of the taking. Force used after the defendant has already achieved complete dominion over the property (i.e., after the larceny is complete) does not convert the prior larceny into robbery.
