Outline: Right to Counsel
Question: After an indictment for aggravated assault, Lee appears before a judge for an initial bail hearing. No lawyer is appointed. The prosecutor argues for high bail and describes Lee as a danger to witnesses. Lee is unable to respond effectively, and the court sets bail far beyond his ability to pay. What is the strongest Sixth Amendment argument?
Answer A: Lee had a right to counsel because the post-indictment bail hearing was a critical stage affecting his pretrial liberty
Answer B: Lee had no right to counsel because bail is administrative and does not determine guilt
Answer C: Lee had no right to counsel unless he first proved he was likely to be acquitted
Answer D: Lee had a right to counsel only if police questioned him at the hearing
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The indictment attached the Sixth Amendment right, and the bail hearing was a critical stage because counsel could materially affect liberty and trial preparation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A proceeding may be critical even if it does not decide guilt. Counsel can protect important liberty and defense interests at bail. C: The right to counsel at a critical stage does not depend on a prehearing showing that the defense will win the case. D: Critical-stage counsel is broader than interrogation. Bail itself can require counsel after attachment.
lb_rule_reference: After formal proceedings begin, the right to counsel applies at critical stages, including proceedings where counsel can affect pretrial liberty or protect trial rights.
