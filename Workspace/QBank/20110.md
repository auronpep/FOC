Question: A cereal buyer and a supplier signed a goods contract requiring delivery in plain cartons. The contract stated that changes had to be in a signed writing. After a holiday promotion was approved, the buyer orally asked the supplier to use bilingual cartons. The supplier used bilingual cartons for three monthly shipments, and the buyer spent money advertising the bilingual packaging. Without notice, the supplier then returned to plain cartons. The buyer claims the bilingual-carton obligation remained operative. What is the buyer's strongest Content/Meaning argument?
Answer A: The parol evidence rule bars the buyer's evidence because the request for bilingual cartons was not in the original writing.
Answer B: The no-oral-modification clause conclusively prevents any later conduct from affecting the carton term.
Answer C: Even if the oral change did not satisfy the signed-writing clause, the supplier's repeated performance may have operated as a waiver, and retraction may be ineffective after material reliance.
Answer D: The buyer must prove lost profits before the court may decide what the carton term required.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a later attempted modification that fails a signed no-oral-modification clause may still operate as a waiver, and waiver retraction may be limited when the other party has materially relied. The trigger facts are the post-signing oral change, three bilingual-carton shipments, and the buyer's promotional spending. The decisive locks are timing and offered purpose: this is later waiver/content evidence, not prior parol evidence. C states the strongest route.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the parol evidence rule targets prior or contemporaneous evidence, not later changes. B: B is wrong because a no-oral-modification clause is not always an absolute bar to waiver and reliance consequences. D: D is wrong because damages are not a prerequisite to identifying the operative carton term.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-209 waiver / retraction after reliance
