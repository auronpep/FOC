MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: SnapServe LLC is a startup incorporated in Colorado that develops a restaurant-reservation app. SnapServe deliberately activated service in the State of Orvale — among 15 other states — by entering data-sharing agreements with Orvale-based restaurant chains, displaying menus in Orvale prices and hours, and geofencing the app to show only Orvale locations to Orvale IP addresses. SnapServe ran targeted digital advertising campaigns in Orvale, spending $80,000 in the past year. An Orvale resident used the app to make a reservation, arrived at the restaurant, and was directed to the wrong table by a SnapServe booking error, causing a dispute with another diner that resulted in physical altercation. She sued SnapServe in Orvale federal court for negligence in causing the confrontation. Orvale's long-arm statute reaches any defendant that "transacts business or performs services in this state, directly or through electronic means." SnapServe moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. How should the court rule on SnapServe's motion?
Answer A: Grant the motion, because SnapServe is incorporated in Colorado and has no physical infrastructure in Orvale
Answer B: Grant the motion, because nationwide app availability is insufficient for purposeful availment — SnapServe did not exclusively target Orvale
Answer C: Deny the motion, because SnapServe deliberately targeted Orvale through geofencing, local data agreements, and targeted advertising — satisfying both the long-arm statute and purposeful availment
Answer D: Deny the motion, because any defendant that generates revenue from forum residents satisfies purposeful availment
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Orvale's long-arm statute reaches defendants that transact business or perform services through electronic means — satisfied by SnapServe's active, geofenced, localized service in Orvale. The due-process step: SnapServe activated Orvale service through data-sharing agreements with Orvale restaurant chains, geo-fenced the app to Orvale IP addresses, and spent $80,000 on Orvale-targeted advertising. These are deliberate, affirmative acts of forum targeting — purposeful availment. The plaintiff's claim arises from SnapServe's Orvale booking service. Both steps are satisfied; the motion should be denied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Physical presence has not been required for personal jurisdiction since *International Shoe*. A defendant that deliberately targets a forum through electronic means — geo-fencing its app, contracting with local partners, running targeted advertising — has purposefully availed itself of the forum. Physical infrastructure is irrelevant to the minimum-contacts analysis. B: Purposeful availment does not require exclusive targeting of the forum. It requires deliberate conduct directed at the forum. SnapServe activated service in Orvale specifically — through data-sharing agreements with Orvale restaurants, geo-fencing to Orvale IP addresses, local pricing/hours, and $80,000 in targeted Orvale advertising. This is affirmative forum-targeting conduct, not passive national availability. D: Revenue from forum residents is a consequence of purposeful availment, not its definition. A defendant with a passive website that forum residents happen to use would generate revenue without purposefully availing itself of the forum. The legal test is whether the defendant deliberately directed activities at the forum — revenue is evidence of that, not a substitute for it.
lb_rule_reference: Specific jurisdiction requires purposeful availment — that the defendant deliberately targeted the forum. For digital products, courts look beyond technical availability to whether the defendant geo-targeted, localized, or specifically contracted to serve the forum market. *Zippo Mfg. Co. v. Zippo Dot Com* (sliding-scale model); *Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court* (2021) ("relate to" standard).
