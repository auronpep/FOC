Question: A city requires a permit for any march on major downtown streets. The ordinance says the police chief may deny a permit whenever a march would be "inappropriate or not in the public interest." It gives no deadline for a decision and no standards for applying those terms. What is the best constitutional objection?
Answer A: Permit requirements for marches are always unconstitutional
Answer B: The scheme gives officials unbridled discretion and lacks objective standards
Answer C: The scheme is valid because traffic control is a legitimate interest
Answer D: The scheme is valid if the chief promises to apply it neutrally
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Standardless licensing lets officials suppress speech before it occurs.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Content-neutral permit systems can be valid if properly constrained. C: A legitimate interest does not cure unbounded discretion over speech permits. D: The ordinance itself needs objective limits and procedures.
lb_rule_reference: A licensing scheme for speech in a public forum must contain narrow, objective, and definite standards, prompt decisionmaking, and review.
