Question: During an armed warehouse burglary, Malik fires a warning shot into the ceiling to scare a guard. He does not intend to hit anyone. The bullet ricochets but misses the guard. Prosecutors charge Malik with attempted felony murder based on the dangerous felony and the near miss. What is Malik's best argument against attempted felony murder?
Answer A: Attempted felony murder is problematic because attempt requires intent to kill, while felony murder can rest on an unintended killing
Answer B: Attempted felony murder is valid whenever the underlying felony is dangerous
Answer C: Attempted felony murder is valid because Malik fired a gun
Answer D: Malik is not guilty only because the bullet struck the ceiling
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Attempt requires the specific intent to commit the target offense. Felony murder supplies malice from the felony for an actual death, but it does not supply intent to kill for an attempted murder charge where no death occurred.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: That completed-offense doctrine does not eliminate attempt's specific-intent requirement. C: Dangerous conduct can support other charges, but the attempt charge requires intent to kill. D: The broader rule is that attempted felony murder lacks the required intent-to-kill foundation. The ceiling fact is evidence, not the doctrine.
lb_rule_reference: Attempt requires specific intent to commit the target crime; because felony murder can be based on an unintended killing, attempted felony murder is generally not a valid charge absent intent to kill.
