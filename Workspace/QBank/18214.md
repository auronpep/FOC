


BARMATRIX Q#: 18214
Outline_code: 94030200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Remand to State Court
Question: After he was fired, a man filed suit in state court in State A against a company. The man's complaint alleged a single federal discrimination claim and a number of state law claims. The company removed the case to federal district court. The man then amended his complaint to remove the discrimination claim because he believed it was untenable and moved to have the case remanded to state court. May the federal district court remand the case to state court?
Answer A: A: No, because the case must be dismissed.
Answer B: B: No, because the state claims were in federal court based on pendent jurisdiction.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the claim was originally brought in state court.
Answer D: D: Yes, because of judicial economy.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: If an action is in federal court based on federal question jurisdiction, a federal court has discretion to remand the case to state court once all the federal questions have been resolved based on principles of fairness and judicial economy. See Carnegie-Mellon University v. Cohill, 484 U.S. 343 (1988). Here, considering the action was originally brought in state court and only state claims remain, it would be more practical and fair to remand the case to the state court. Therefore, A and B are incorrect.
Wrong Answer Explanations: C is incorrect because the district court's discretion to remand the case to state court is not based on the simple fact the case was originally brought in state court.
