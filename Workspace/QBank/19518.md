Outline: Use of impeachment material
Question: In a federal contract action, Nathan testifies in his own defense that he never agreed to buy grain from Miriam. Miriam then offers an authenticated email Nathan sent to his bookkeeper before suit was filed stating, “I agreed to buy Miriam's grain at $8 per bushel.” Miriam did not ask Nathan about the email while he was on the stand. Nathan objects that he had no opportunity to explain the email. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the email because Nathan was not first given an opportunity to explain or deny it.
Answer B: Admit the email only to impeach Nathan's credibility, not for the truth of the agreement.
Answer C: Admit the email as an opposing party's statement; the Rule 613(b) opportunity requirement does not apply to that use.
Answer D: Exclude the email unless it was written under penalty of perjury.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Nathan's own statement is offered against him, so it is an opposing party's statement under FRE 801(d)(2). FRE 613(b)'s opportunity-to-explain requirement for extrinsic evidence of a prior inconsistent statement does not apply to an opposing party's statement under Rule 801(d)(2).
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the 613(b) opportunity requirement does not apply to opposing-party statements. B: B is wrong because the email is not limited to impeachment; it is admissible substantively as Nathan's own opposing-party statement. D: D is wrong because opposing-party statements do not have to be under oath.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 613(b) and 801(d)(2) / opposing-party statement exception to PIS procedure
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests when impeachment-looking material is also substantive evidence because the speaker is an opposing party.
