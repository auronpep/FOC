Question: Boaz signed a recorded deed granting “to Ruth, as owner of Vineyard Lot, and to Ruth's successors as owners of Vineyard Lot, the right to use the well road across Boaz's Orchard Lot.” Ruth later sold Vineyard Lot to Jacob. Jacob's deed did not repeat the well-road language. What is the best classification of the well-road right?
Answer A: An easement appurtenant because the grant ties the benefit to ownership of Vineyard Lot.
Answer B: An easement in gross because Ruth is named in the deed.
Answer C: A revocable license because Jacob's deed did not repeat the well-road language.
Answer D: A profit because access to the well road increases Vineyard Lot's value.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement appurtenant benefits land and generally passes with the dominant estate. Although Ruth is named, the grant identifies her as owner of Vineyard Lot and extends to successors as owners of that lot. The benefit is tied to the land.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because naming a person does not make the right in gross when the grant identifies benefited land. C: C is wrong because an appurtenant easement generally passes with the dominant estate even if a later deed omits it. D: D is wrong because a profit requires removal of resources from land, not merely increased value from access.
lb_rule_reference: Easement appurtenant / person named as owner
