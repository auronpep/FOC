Question: A buyer hired Solomon to install a commercial fountain for $25,000. The contract stated, “Final payment is due only upon written certification by the project engineer that the fountain meets the water-pressure specifications.” The engineer honestly refused certification because the fountain failed the pressure test. Solomon demanded final payment, arguing that the fountain looked beautiful. Who is most likely to prevail?
Answer A: Solomon, because aesthetic success is substantial performance.
Answer B: The buyer, but only if the buyer proves the fountain has no market value.
Answer C: The buyer, because honest engineering certification was an express condition to final payment and the condition did not occur.
Answer D: Solomon, because certification requirements are treated only as covenants.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The parties made written engineering certification an express condition to final payment. The engineer honestly refused certification because the fountain failed the specified performance test. The buyer's final payment duty did not mature.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because substantial performance does not satisfy a strict express condition. B: B is wrong because the condition controls payment duty even if the fountain has some value. D: D is wrong because the language makes certification a condition, not merely a promise.
lb_rule_reference: Express condition / certification
