MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A mining company lawfully stored explosives at a remote site for blasting. One night, a thief broke into the site office, stole cash from a desk, and shoved a security guard while escaping. None of the explosives moved, ignited, or leaked. The guard sues the mining company for strict liability based on its storage of explosives. Is the company strictly liable?
Answer A: Yes, because explosives are inherently dangerous wherever they are stored
Answer B: Yes, because the assault happened at the explosives site
Answer C: No, because the guard's injury resulted from an unrelated theft and assault, not an explosive risk
Answer D: No, because strict liability is unavailable for activities involving commercial mining
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Strict liability does not cover harms outside the scope of the abnormal danger.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The activity may be abnormally dangerous, but strict liability still requires harm from the abnormal explosive risk. B: Location alone does not connect the injury to the risk that makes explosives storage abnormal. D: Commercial status does not bar strict liability when the required abnormal-risk elements are met.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity extends only to the kind of harm that makes the activity abnormally dangerous, not to unrelated intervening misconduct.
