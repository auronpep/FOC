


BARMATRIX Q#: 22049
Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: A woman leased a house from a landlord. The lease was for one year and did not address who was responsible for making repairs to the property. One morning, the woman awoke to a drone crashing through her window. Out on the street, a group of children had been playing with the drone and had accidentally lost control of it. The woman called the landlord to fix the window, but he refused to do so. The woman then had the window fixed herself and deducted the cost of the repair from her next month's rent. The landlord thought the woman paid too much for the window and sued her for the money she deducted from her rent. The jurisdiction followed the common law rule. Should the court rule in the landlord's favor?
Answer A: A: No, because the broken window constituted a constructive eviction.
Answer B: B: No, because the woman was entitled to use self- help to fix the window.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the woman did not give the landlord notice that she was going to fix the window.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the woman had the responsibility to make minor repairs.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the common law, absent contractual terms to the contrary, the tenant has the duty to keep the premises wind and water tight to avoid committing waste. Here, because the lease did not say who had the duty to repair, the woman would have the duty to repair the broken window. A is incorrect because a constructive eviction requires a serious interference with the tenant's use of the property. A broken window is not a serious interference. B is incorrect because the common law gives the tenant the duty to make minor repairs. C is incorrect because the tenant's lack of notice doesn't change the analysis.
