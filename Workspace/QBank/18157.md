Question: A city enforces an ordinance banning leafleting near public transit stops. After a speaker sues, the city announces that it will "pause enforcement during litigation" but keeps the ordinance on the books and reserves the right to enforce it later. The city moves to dismiss as moot. How should the court analyze mootness?
Answer A: The case is moot because the city voluntarily stopped enforcement for now
Answer B: The case is not moot unless the city shows it is absolutely clear the challenged enforcement cannot reasonably recur
Answer C: The case is moot because standing is measured only at the time of dismissal
Answer D: The case is not moot only if the city admits the ordinance is unconstitutional
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The voluntary-cessation exception prevents defendants from stopping temporarily to defeat jurisdiction. The city keeps the ordinance and reserves enforcement authority.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Voluntary cessation does not moot a case unless recurrence is not reasonably expected. A temporary pause with retained authority is not enough. C: Standing is measured at filing; mootness asks whether a live case later ended and whether exceptions apply. D: The mootness question does not require an admission of unconstitutionality; it asks whether recurrence is reasonably expected.
lb_rule_reference: Voluntary cessation does not moot a case unless it is absolutely clear the challenged conduct cannot reasonably be expected to recur.
