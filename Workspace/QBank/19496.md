Question: A state law prohibits driving faster than a posted speed limit. A driver cited for going 56 in a 55 zone argues the law is void for vagueness because close cases near the line can feel arbitrary. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The law is void because every close case proves vagueness
Answer B: The law is not vague merely because borderline applications exist; it gives a clear numerical standard
Answer C: The law is vague unless it lists every possible model of car
Answer D: The law is valid only if each driver gets a pre-ticket hearing
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A posted speed limit gives fair notice and objective enforcement criteria.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Hard or close applications do not necessarily mean the law lacks fair notice or enforcement standards. C: Due process does not require impossible detail when the standard is clear enough. D: The question is whether the law is vague, not pre-enforcement hearing timing.
lb_rule_reference: A law is not void for vagueness merely because some applications are difficult; the question is whether it gives fair notice and meaningful enforcement standards.
