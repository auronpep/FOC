Outline: Independent contractor and nondelegable duties
Question: A grocery cashier saw an old rival shopping in the store. During a scheduled break, the cashier followed the rival into the parking lot and punched him over a personal dispute from years earlier. The cashier's job did not involve security or customer discipline. Is the grocery store likely vicariously liable for the punch?
Answer A: Yes, because the cashier was on the employer's premises
Answer B: Yes, because all customer interactions serve the store
Answer C: No, because the assault was purely personal and unrelated to assigned duties
Answer D: No, because employers are never liable for parking-lot torts
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts do not show foreseeable job friction or a work purpose.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Location alone does not establish scope. B: This was a personal revenge act, not a customer-service interaction. D: The analysis turns on scope, not the parking lot alone.
lb_rule_reference: An employer is usually not vicariously liable for an employee's intentional tort committed for purely personal reasons outside authorized duties.
