Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: After the 21-day amendment-as-of-course period expires, a plaintiff promptly moves for leave to amend a federal complaint to add a missing factual allegation supporting the same claim. Discovery has barely begun, no trial date is set, and the defendant identifies no prejudice beyond having to answer the amended pleading. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny leave because amendments are never allowed after the as-of-course period
Answer B: Grant leave because Rule 15(a)(2) favors amendment absent a valid reason to deny it
Answer C: Deny leave unless the plaintiff proves the amendment will win at trial
Answer D: Grant leave only if the defendant consents in writing
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The amendment is prompt, relates to the same claim, and creates no identified undue prejudice, bad faith, delay, or futility. Leave should be freely given.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: After that period, amendment may still occur with consent or leave of court. Rule 15(a)(2) says leave should be freely given when justice requires. C: The plaintiff need not prove ultimate trial success. The court screens for legal futility and fairness, not final merits proof. D: Written consent is sufficient but not necessary. The court can grant leave when justice requires.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 15(a)(2) provides that courts should freely give leave to amend when justice so requires, subject to reasons such as undue delay, bad faith, repeated failure to cure, undue prejudice, or futility.
