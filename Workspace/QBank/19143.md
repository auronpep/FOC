Question: Police lawfully impound a car after arresting its driver. Department policy requires officers to list valuables in the passenger compartment and trunk. An officer follows the policy, opens the trunk, records property, and finds a bag of stolen jewelry in a suitcase. Is the inventory search likely valid?
Answer A: No, because police always need probable cause to open a trunk
Answer B: Yes, because the car was lawfully impounded and officers followed standardized caretaking procedures
Answer C: Yes, because inventory searches allow police to search anywhere for evidence
Answer D: No, because finding stolen jewelry proves the search was investigative
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The search served inventory purposes and followed department policy, which limits officer discretion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A lawful inventory under standardized procedures can include trunk areas covered by policy. C: Inventory must serve caretaking purposes and follow standardized limits, not general evidence gathering. D: The validity turns on lawful impoundment, standardized policy, and caretaking purpose, not merely what was found.
lb_rule_reference: A valid inventory search must serve caretaking purposes and follow standardized procedures, not be a pretext for evidence rummaging.
