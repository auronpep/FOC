Question: Hannah hired Solomon to provide “monthly accounting support.” The signed agreement contained a merger clause and listed a fixed monthly fee. The parties dispute whether “monthly accounting support” includes payroll tax filings or only bookkeeping reports. Hannah offers negotiation emails in which Solomon described the service as “bookkeeping plus payroll tax filing.” How should the court treat the emails?
Answer A: Exclude them because a merger clause bars all negotiation evidence.
Answer B: Admit them if the phrase is ambiguous and the emails are offered to explain its meaning rather than to add a new fee or service.
Answer C: Exclude them because tax matters are always outside accounting contracts.
Answer D: Admit them only if Hannah proves Solomon intentionally misled her.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A complete integration bars additional or contradictory terms, but it does not necessarily bar evidence offered to explain an ambiguous term. The emails may help define the scope of “monthly accounting support.”
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because merger clauses do not bar all interpretive evidence. C: C is wrong because the issue is what the phrase means in this contract. D: D is wrong because ambiguity explanation does not require proof of fraud.
lb_rule_reference: Complete integration / ambiguity distinction
