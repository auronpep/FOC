


BARMATRIX Q#: 18847
Outline_code: 94010100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Diversity Jurisdiction
Question: A man owned a home in State A and lived there for 30 years. After meeting a woman online, he decided to sell his house and move to her home in State B for the foreseeable future to “give love a try.” Three days later, the man was involved in an accident with a doctor who was domiciled in State B. He sued the doctor for $100,000 in federal district court in State B. Shortly after filing his suit, the man moved back to State A when his relationship with the woman unexpectedly ended after a fight. The doctor moved to dismiss for lack of subject matter jurisdiction.&#10;&#10;Does the district court have jurisdiction over the claim?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the man was a citizen of State A when the injury occurred.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the man was a citizen of State A when the suit was filed.
Answer C: C: No, because the man was a citizen of State B when the injury occurred.
Answer D: D: No, because the man was a citizen of State B when the suit was filed.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal courts have subject matter jurisdiction based on diversity so long as no plaintiff is a citizen of the same state as any defendant. Citizenship of the parties is determined on the date the suit is filed. If a person moves to a state and intends to stay there indefinitely, the person becomes a citizen of that new state. Here, at the time of filing, the man had moved to State B and intended to stay there for the foreseeable future. Consequently, the man was a citizen of State B when the action was filed, meaning both the doctor and the man were citizens of the same state.
Wrong Answer Explanations: Therefore, A, B, and C are incorrect.
