Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: Detectives intentionally questioned Clara in custody without Miranda warnings until she admitted setting a fire. Ten minutes later, in the same room with the same detectives, they read warnings and said, "Now repeat what you just told us." No curative explanation was given. Clara repeated the confession. Is the second warned confession admissible?
Answer A: Yes, because Miranda warnings were given before the second confession
Answer B: Yes, because the first unwarned confession was voluntary
Answer C: No, because the deliberate two-step strategy made the later warnings ineffective
Answer D: No, because all confessions after warnings are inadmissible if police asked any earlier question
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The same detectives immediately used warnings to have Clara repeat an intentionally obtained unwarned confession. Without curative measures, the second confession should be suppressed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The warnings may be ineffective when police deliberately use a question-first strategy and immediately ask the suspect to repeat the unwarned confession. B: This was deliberate two-step interrogation with no curative measures, not an inadvertent first question. D: Not every earlier unwarned question taints a later confession. The deliberate two-step strategy drives the result.
lb_rule_reference: When police deliberately obtain an unwarned confession and then give warnings to repeat it, the later statement may be suppressed unless curative measures make the warnings effective.
