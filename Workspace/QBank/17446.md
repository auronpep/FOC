Outline: Judicial notice
Question: Sarah sues Peter for breach of a contract requiring performance on a specific calendar date. Sarah asks the court to take judicial notice that the date fell on a Monday and supplies an official government calendar whose accuracy is not reasonably questioned. Peter objects that Sarah should instead call a calendar expert. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the request because calendar facts must always be proved through a witness.
Answer B: Deny the request because the court has complete discretion to refuse every request for judicial notice.
Answer C: Take judicial notice, because Sarah requested notice and supplied the necessary information showing a fact that can be accurately and readily determined from a reliable source.
Answer D: Take judicial notice only if Peter agrees that the date fell on a Monday.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The court must take judicial notice if a party requests it and supplies the necessary information showing that the fact is not subject to reasonable dispute. A day-of-week fact from an official calendar can be accurately and readily determined from a source whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a qualifying fact may be noticed without witness testimony. B: B is wrong because the court must take notice when the rule's request-and-information conditions are met. D: D is wrong because the opponent's agreement is not required when the fact is not reasonably disputable.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 201(c)(2) / mandatory notice on request
