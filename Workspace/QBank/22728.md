MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Equal Protection Clause
Question: A man worked as a math teacher in a public school in State A. The school found that many of its students were suffering from bullying, eating disorders, anxiety, and feelings of loneliness. In response, the school instituted a mentoring program where certain teachers would hold office hours after school to give schoolchildren the chance to talk about any problems that they were having. The school hoped that the program would make students feel better about themselves. The school provided a small budget where mentors could take schoolchildren out to a coffee or ice cream shop if the mentor thought that would be a better place to talk. The school had a policy of only allowing female teachers to be mentors because scientific studies showed female teachers were more “motherly” and “emotionally available.” The man applied to be a mentor and was denied according to the school's policy. The man sued, arguing that the school's mentoring policy was unconstitutional. Is a court likely to find the mentoring policy unconstitutional?
Answer A: A: No, because there is a rational connection between the policy and the school's goals.
Answer B: B: No, because the school relied on scientific studies in making its policy decision.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the policy is not substantially related to an important governmental interest.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the policy violates the Privileges and Immunities Clause.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A classification based on gender is subject to intermediate scrutiny, which means that the government has to show that the law is substantially related to an important governmental interest. This answer choice is correct because it states the appropriate standard of review, which the policy is unlikely to pass because beliefs that female teachers are more “motherly” and “emotionally available” are unlikely to support the policy. A is incorrect because gender classifications are subject to intermediate scrutiny, not rational basis review. B is incorrect because relying on scientific studies would not immediately make a gender classification constitutional. D is incorrect because the Privileges and Immunities Clause prohibits discrimination against residents of different states.
