Outline: Attorney-client
Question: Isaac's litigation file contains two documents. The first is a confidential email from Isaac to his lawyer Rebekah asking for legal advice about a disputed accident. The second is Rebekah's later memo to the file evaluating trial strategy, witness weaknesses, and settlement risks. Opposing counsel Paul seeks both documents and argues that all lawyer-file material should be classified under one doctrine. Which classification is most accurate?
Answer A: Both documents are attorney-client privileged only, because both are located in Rebekah's legal file.
Answer B: Neither document is protected, because both are relevant to the lawsuit.
Answer C: The email is an attorney-client communication, and the later strategy memo is work product, likely opinion work product.
Answer D: Both documents are work product only, because both relate to litigation.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The two documents must be classified separately. Isaac's confidential email to Rebekah seeking legal advice is an attorney-client communication. Rebekah's later litigation-strategy memo is trial-preparation material and likely opinion work product because it reflects counsel's mental impressions, strategy, and legal assessment. The decisive axis is protected object, not the physical file location.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the attorney memo is not a client communication merely because it sits in the lawyer's file. It is better classified as work product. B: B is wrong because relevance does not defeat attorney-client privilege or work-product protection. D: D is wrong because the client's confidential legal-advice email is classically attorney-client privileged. Litigation connection alone does not erase the attorney-client route.
lb_rule_reference: Attorney-client protects communications; work product protects litigation-preparation materials
lb_student_diagnostic: Student collapses attorney-client privilege and work product instead of classifying each object separately.
