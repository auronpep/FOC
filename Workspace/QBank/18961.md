Question: Sarah agreed to sell land to Isaac. The contract stated, “Seller's duty to convey is subject to Seller receiving a release from Seller's lender.” The lender refused to release the lien unless Sarah paid a fee. Sarah told Isaac, “I will pay the fee and proceed.” Isaac then tried to cancel, arguing that the lender-release condition had failed. What is the best result?
Answer A: Sarah may waive the lender-release condition because it protected her, so Isaac may not use its nonoccurrence to cancel.
Answer B: Isaac may cancel because any failed express condition excuses both parties.
Answer C: Sarah may waive the condition only if Isaac gives new consideration.
Answer D: Isaac may cancel unless the lender acted in bad faith.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The lender-release condition was for Sarah's protection as seller. She could choose to waive that protection and proceed. Isaac cannot invoke a condition designed for Sarah's benefit after Sarah elects to go forward.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because not every condition benefits both parties. C: C is wrong because a protected party's waiver of a condition does not require new consideration. D: D is wrong because Sarah's power to waive does not depend on proving lender bad faith.
