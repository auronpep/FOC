Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Phoebe agreed to clean a warehouse for Lydia for $4,000. Phoebe fully performed. The reasonable value of Phoebe's work was $5,500 because market prices rose unexpectedly. Lydia refuses to pay. Phoebe sues and argues that because she relied on the contract by performing, she should recover $5,500. What is Phoebe's best recovery?
Answer A: $5,500, because reliance damages always equal the reasonable value of performed services.
Answer B: $1,500, the difference between reasonable value and contract price.
Answer C: Nothing, because rising market prices made the original contract unfair.
Answer D: $4,000, the contract price, because after full performance the ordinary expectation measure controls and reasonable value would overcompensate Phoebe.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: When a valid contract has been fully performed by the plaintiff and the defendant refuses to pay, the ordinary expectation measure is the contract price. Reliance or restitution labels cannot be used to exceed the agreed price merely because the reasonable value of services later appears higher.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because reasonable value is not the default when the contract price governs full performance. B: B is wrong because the measure is not a market increase differential. C: C is wrong because market changes do not make Lydia's payment duty vanish.
