Question: A state statute prohibits private landfills from accepting ordinary solid waste generated outside the state. The state allows identical in-state waste to be buried in the same landfills and says the law conserves landfill space for residents. What is the strongest Dormant Commerce Clause objection?
Answer A: The statute facially discriminates against out-of-state commerce by banning out-of-state waste
Answer B: The statute is valid because conservation is always enough to prefer residents
Answer C: The statute receives only ordinary rational basis because waste is not commerce
Answer D: The statute is valid unless Congress affirmatively forbids it
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The law distinguishes based on origin and reserves landfill capacity for in-state waste.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A legitimate local concern does not automatically save facial discrimination. C: Waste disposal can be commerce, and origin-based exclusion triggers DCC scrutiny. D: In the absence of authorization, the Dormant Commerce Clause limits state discrimination.
lb_rule_reference: A state may not facially discriminate against out-of-state commerce, including by banning out-of-state waste, unless it satisfies the demanding discrimination standard.
