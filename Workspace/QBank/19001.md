Outline: Joinder of claims
Question: A plaintiff sues a general contractor for injuries at a jobsite. The contractor impleads a subcontractor for indemnity. The subcontractor then asserts a negligence claim against the plaintiff arising from the same jobsite incident. Is the subcontractor's claim against the plaintiff procedurally allowed under Rule 14?
Answer A: Yes, if it arises out of the same transaction or occurrence as the plaintiff's claim against the contractor
Answer B: No, because a third-party defendant may assert only indemnity claims
Answer C: Yes, regardless of whether the claim is related to the original incident
Answer D: No, because only plaintiffs may assert claims against third-party defendants
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 14 permits a third-party defendant to assert related claims against the plaintiff.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: After being impleaded, the third-party defendant has additional claim options under Rule 14. C: The third-party defendant's claim against the plaintiff must arise from the same transaction or occurrence. D: Rule 14 gives claim options to the third-party defendant as well.
lb_rule_reference: A third-party defendant may assert against the plaintiff any claim arising out of the transaction or occurrence that is the subject matter of the plaintiff's claim against the third-party plaintiff.
