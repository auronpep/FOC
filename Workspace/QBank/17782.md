Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A news site published a widely viewed article about a public protest. It placed a private citizen's photo under a caption saying, "Protesters arrested for violence," though the citizen had attended peacefully and was not arrested. Editors had the arrest list before publication and saw that her name was not on it. What fault issue is most important for a false light claim?
Answer A: Whether the site knew the implication was false or recklessly disregarded the truth
Answer B: Whether the site disliked the citizen personally
Answer C: Whether the photo was flattering
Answer D: Whether the citizen was physically present when the article was posted
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The public-concern context brings constitutional fault into the false-light analysis.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Constitutional actual malice concerns knowledge or reckless disregard, not personal hostility. C: The issue is the false arrest implication, not whether the photo looked good. D: Presence at publication is not a false-light fault element.
lb_rule_reference: When false light involves a matter of public concern, constitutional fault requirements may require proof of knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard for truth.
