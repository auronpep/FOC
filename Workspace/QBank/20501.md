Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Peter agreed to pay Ezra $100,000 to build a foundation. Peter paid Ezra $60,000 during construction, leaving $40,000 unpaid. Ezra's work was so defective that it had to be removed and redone. Peter reasonably paid a replacement contractor $100,000 to build the foundation correctly. What is Peter's expectation damage from Ezra?
Answer A: $100,000, because Peter paid that amount to the replacement contractor.
Answer B: $60,000, because Peter's total cost after breach exceeded the original contract price by that amount.
Answer C: $40,000, because that was the unpaid balance on Ezra's contract.
Answer D: Nothing, because Peter received some work from Ezra before discovering the defect.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Expectation damages put Peter in the position of receiving the promised foundation for the original $100,000 price. Peter paid Ezra $60,000 and had to pay another $100,000 to obtain the foundation, for a total of $160,000. His loss over the bargain price is $60,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores the $40,000 Peter did not pay Ezra. C: C is wrong because the unpaid balance is not Peter's loss; it is the amount saved under the original contract. D: D is wrong because worthless defective work does not eliminate the owner's cost-to-obtain-performance damages.
lb_rule_reference: Common law construction damages; total defective performance
