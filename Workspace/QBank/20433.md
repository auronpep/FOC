Subtopic: Separation of Powers
Outline: Commerce power
Question: Congress makes it a federal crime to damage a commercial aircraft used in interstate passenger service. A mechanic damages an aircraft while it is parked overnight in one state before its next interstate flight. He argues the conduct is local because the plane was not flying at the moment. What is the strongest basis for federal power?
Answer A: Congress may protect instrumentalities of interstate commerce from intrastate threats
Answer B: Congress lacks power because the plane was stationary
Answer C: Congress lacks power unless passengers were already onboard
Answer D: Congress may regulate only if the mechanic works for the federal government
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A commercial aircraft used in interstate service is an instrumentality of interstate commerce.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Instrumentalities can be protected from local threats even when momentarily stationary. C: The aircraft itself can be the instrumentality. D: Commerce power does not depend on the defendant being a federal employee.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may regulate and protect instrumentalities of interstate commerce, and persons or things in interstate commerce, even from intrastate threats.
