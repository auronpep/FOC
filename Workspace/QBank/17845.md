Question: After a full adversary hearing, a district court enters an order labeled "TRO" barring a distributor from selling a product for ninety days. The distributor did not consent to the long duration. The distributor appeals, arguing the order should be treated like a preliminary injunction. What is the best appealability analysis?
Answer A: The label "TRO" always prevents appellate jurisdiction
Answer B: The order may be appealable because its duration and adversary process make it function like a preliminary injunction
Answer C: The order is appealable only if the distributor first loses at trial
Answer D: The order is never appealable because product-sale disputes are not civil cases
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An extended order after adversary proceedings is different from an ordinary short TRO.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Courts can look beyond the label when the order functions like a preliminary injunction. C: Injunction-like orders can fit an interlocutory appeal exception. D: The subject matter does not eliminate civil appellate jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: Although ordinary short TROs are not usually appealable, an extended adversary order that operates like a preliminary injunction may be treated as appealable.
