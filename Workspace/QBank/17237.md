Question: Jacob granted Leah “the exclusive right to sell Jacob water-purification cartridges” in two counties for one year. The writing stated prices, territory, and Leah's discount schedule, but did not state a monthly supply quantity. Leah advertised the cartridges and obtained several customer orders. Jacob then reserved most of his cartridge inventory for direct accounts outside Leah's territory and shipped Leah's orders late. What is Leah's strongest Content/Meaning argument?
Answer A: Jacob owed no supply obligation because the writing did not state an exact monthly quantity.
Answer B: The exclusive-dealing term carries a best-efforts obligation by Jacob to supply the goods unless the agreement provides otherwise.
Answer C: Leah must prove a prior oral supply promise because best efforts cannot arise from the written exclusive-dealing term.
Answer D: Article 2 imposes only a buyer's duty to promote, not a seller's duty to supply.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A lawful exclusive-dealing agreement in goods imposes, unless otherwise agreed, a seller's best-efforts obligation to supply the goods and a buyer's best-efforts obligation to promote their sale. The trigger facts are the exclusive right to sell goods, Leah's promotional efforts, and Jacob's late or withheld supply. The decisive lock is term object: the content of the exclusive-dealing term supplies an effort obligation even without a precise monthly quantity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the absence of a monthly supply schedule does not eliminate the best-efforts content of exclusive dealing. C: C is wrong because the obligation can arise from the written exclusive-dealing relationship rather than a prior oral promise. D: D is wrong because it states only half the rule and misses the seller's reciprocal best-efforts supply duty.
