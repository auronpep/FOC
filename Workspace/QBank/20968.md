Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Defamation
Question: A restaurant critic wrote, "In my opinion, the owner of Harbor Bistro falsifies health inspection records. I cannot reveal my source, but I have seen the proof." The statement was false. Thousands of readers saw the review, and the owner lost catering contracts. The owner sues the critic for defamation. Is the critic protected from defamation liability merely because the statement began with "in my opinion"?
Answer A: Yes, because every statement framed as opinion is nonactionable
Answer B: Yes, because restaurant reviews are always rhetorical hyperbole
Answer C: No, because the opinion wording implied undisclosed verifiable facts about falsifying records
Answer D: No, but only because the owner lost contracts
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The critic did more than state a subjective view. He claimed hidden proof of a specific false act concerning the owner and published it to third parties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The statement implies undisclosed facts that can be proven true or false: falsified inspection records and hidden proof. B: This review asserted a specific factual accusation of record falsification. D: Damages do not decide the fact-versus-opinion gate. The statement is actionable because it implies specific false facts.
lb_rule_reference: Opinion language can be actionable when it implies undisclosed, verifiable defamatory facts.
