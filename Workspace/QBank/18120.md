Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Federal agents unlawfully searched Omar's storage locker and found counterfeit checks. The prosecutor presented the checks to a grand jury, which indicted Omar for bank fraud. Omar moves to dismiss the indictment because the grand jury considered illegally seized evidence. Should the indictment be dismissed?
Answer A: Yes, because evidence obtained through a Fourth Amendment violation may never be used in any criminal proceeding
Answer B: Yes, because indictment is invalid whenever inadmissible trial evidence was considered
Answer C: No, because the exclusionary rule generally does not apply to grand jury proceedings
Answer D: No, because Fourth Amendment violations are never remedied by suppression
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Omar may seek suppression at trial if the evidence is offered there, but the indictment is not dismissed merely because the grand jury considered the checks.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The exclusionary rule does not apply identically in every proceeding. Grand jury use is generally allowed. B: Grand juries may consider evidence that would be inadmissible at trial, including illegally seized evidence for this purpose. D: Suppression may be available at trial. The limit here is the grand jury proceeding, not the absence of any remedy.
lb_rule_reference: Evidence obtained in violation of the Fourth Amendment is not excluded from grand jury proceedings, and an indictment is not dismissed merely because the grand jury heard that evidence.
