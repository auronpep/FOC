Question: A customer slipped in a grocery store. As paramedics arrived, the store manager said, “We will pay your ambulance and hospital bills.” The manager said nothing about fault. At trial, the customer offers the statement to prove that the store was liable for the fall. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the statement because it shows the manager believed the store was liable.
Answer B: Exclude the statement because offers to pay medical or similar expenses resulting from an injury are not admissible to prove liability.
Answer C: Admit the statement because Rule 409 applies only to payments actually made, not offers.
Answer D: Exclude the statement only if a disputed claim already existed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 409 excludes evidence of furnishing, promising to pay, or offering to pay medical, hospital, or similar expenses resulting from an injury when offered to prove liability. Unlike Rule 408, it does not require an existing disputed claim. The decisive axis is medical-payment policy container plus liability purpose.
