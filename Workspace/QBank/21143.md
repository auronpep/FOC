Question: Ilan set fire to an occupied apartment building at night to collect insurance proceeds. Firefighters arrived while the fire was spreading. One firefighter entered to rescue tenants, was overcome by smoke, and died. Ilan was charged with felony murder. Is felony murder supported?
Answer A: No, because Ilan did not know a firefighter would enter the building
Answer B: No, because firefighters assume the risk of fire
Answer C: Yes, because arson is a qualifying predicate and the firefighter's death was caused by the fire response
Answer D: Yes, but only if Ilan personally intended to kill a tenant
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Arson is a classic felony-murder predicate. The firefighter died while responding to the fire Ilan set, a foreseeable and direct risk of the felony. Felony murder is supported.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Firefighter response is a foreseeable consequence of setting fire to an occupied building. Felony murder does not require knowledge of the specific victim. B: The firefighter's role does not erase causation. Arson creates the rescue risk. D: Felony murder supplies malice through the qualifying felony and death connection; intent to kill is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Arson is a classic felony-murder predicate, and deaths proximately caused by the fire or immediate response can fall within the felony transaction.
