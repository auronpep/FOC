Outline: Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
Question: Peter contracted to buy 50 security cameras from John, with John also agreeing to install them. The invoice allocated 85% of the price to the cameras and 15% to installation. The writing was silent about whether “camera” included mounting brackets. Peter offers usage-of-trade evidence that camera sales in that trade include brackets. Which source-law route is strongest?
Answer A: UCC Article 2 because the predominant purpose is the sale of movable cameras, with installation incidental.
Answer B: Common law because installation labor is mentioned.
Answer C: Real-property law because cameras will be attached to walls.
Answer D: No usage evidence can ever be used when a written contract is silent.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The predominant purpose is the sale of movable goods. Installation is incidental. Article 2 governs the content/meaning dispute, so trade usage may be considered if it is a valid and consistent meaning source.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because incidental installation does not defeat Article 2 when goods predominate. C: C is wrong because attachment to a wall after sale does not make the contract a land transaction. D: D is wrong because silence may be explained or supplemented through valid meaning sources under the proper source-law route.
lb_student_diagnostic: Mixed transaction and usage-source trap.
