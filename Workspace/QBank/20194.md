Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Burglary
Question: Rafael is indicted for burglary and has appointed counsel. While in jail, he tells a guard he wants to speak with detectives about the burglary. Detectives arrive, give Miranda warnings, tell Rafael he has counsel on the case and may stop at any time, and obtain a written waiver. Rafael then confesses. Is the confession admissible over a Sixth Amendment objection?
Answer A: Yes, because Rafael knowingly, intelligently, and voluntarily waived the attached right before speaking
Answer B: No, because an indicted defendant can never waive the Sixth Amendment right outside counsel's physical presence
Answer C: No, because the detectives did not obtain defense counsel's consent before interviewing Rafael
Answer D: Yes, because jailhouse statements are never covered by the Sixth Amendment
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The right had attached, but it was waivable. Rafael initiated contact and received warnings making clear his counsel right before signing a waiver.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel may be waived if the waiver is knowing, intelligent, and voluntary. C: The constitutional question is whether Rafael validly waived his own right. Counsel's advance consent is not the required test on these facts. D: Jailhouse questioning after attachment can violate the Sixth Amendment. This confession is admissible because of valid waiver, not because the setting is exempt.
lb_rule_reference: After attachment, statements about the charged offense may be admitted when the accused validly waives the right to counsel before speaking.
