Outline: Judicial notice
Question: Miriam sues John over a nighttime collision. John asks the court to take judicial notice of the official sunset time for the date and location of the accident. John supplies a government astronomical table whose accuracy is not reasonably questioned. Miriam objects that John must call an astronomer. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because astronomical facts always require expert testimony.
Answer B: Take judicial notice because the sunset time can be accurately and readily determined from a source whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned.
Answer C: Take judicial notice only if Miriam first admits that the government table is correct.
Answer D: Refuse judicial notice because sunset time is never an adjudicative fact.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A fact may be judicially noticed if it can be accurately and readily determined from a source whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned. A reliable official table of sunset time can satisfy that standard; an expert is not required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because judicial notice can replace ordinary proof for qualifying facts. C: C is wrong because the opponent's admission is unnecessary if the source is reliable and the fact is not reasonably disputable. D: D is wrong because the time of sunset at the accident location can be an adjudicative fact.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 201(b)(2) / readily determined reliable source
