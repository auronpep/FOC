


BARMATRIX Q#: 22837
Outline_code: 94040200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Service of Process
Question: A man, who was a citizen of State A, was injured by a fan made by a fan manufacturer. The manufacturer's sole office was in State B, and the fan had been shipped to a store in State A where the man bought it. The man sued the manufacturer in State A federal district court. Neither State A nor State B had statutes that would have allowed the man to serve process on a defendant outside of that particular state. State B was over 300 miles away from State A. Consequently, the man hired a licensed process server to travel to State B and personally deliver the summons and complaint to the manufacturer's president. Does the State A federal court have personal jurisdiction over the manufacturer?
Answer A: A: Yes, because Congress has expressly allowed for nationwide service of process in all claims based on diversity.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the man bought the fan and was injured in State A.
Answer C: C: No, because such service of process was not allowed under State A law.
Answer D: D: No, because State B was over 300 miles away from State A.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(e)(1) requires a party serving process on another to follow the state law of the state where the district court is located. Here, since State A law would not allow service of process on someone in State B, the State A federal court would not have jurisdiction over the manufacturer. Importantly, the manufacturer does not appear to have any presence in State A. A is incorrect because Congress has only allowed for nationwide service of process in certain types of claims. B is incorrect because the mere fact that the man bought the fan and was injured in State A would not subject the manufacturer to personal jurisdiction in State A. D is incorrect because most federal courts hold that a defendant may be served under the 100- mile bulge rule (Fed. R. Civ. P. 4(k)(1)) only if the state where service is made allows such service under its long- arm statute. Here, State B does not allow out- of- state service.
