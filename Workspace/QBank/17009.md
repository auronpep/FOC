Question: Congress offers states grants to improve public clinics. The statute says recipient states must comply with "other health-related obligations later identified by the agency." After a state accepts funds, the agency announces a costly unrelated reporting program and threatens repayment if the state refuses. What is the state's best spending-power objection?
Answer A: The condition was not stated clearly enough for the state to knowingly accept it
Answer B: Congress can impose any later condition because the state accepted federal money
Answer C: The condition is invalid only if it violates the Commerce Clause
Answer D: The condition is valid because healthcare is always a federal police-power subject
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Spending conditions must give clear notice of the obligations attached to federal funds.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Conditions must still satisfy spending-power limits, including clear notice. C: Spending-power conditions have their own requirements. D: Congress does not have a general police power; it relies here on spending.
lb_rule_reference: Conditions on federal funds must be stated clearly so states can knowingly decide whether to accept the funds and the attached obligations.
