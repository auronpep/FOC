


BARMATRIX Q#: 19986
Outline_code: 94010200
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A woman, a citizen of State A, sued a company for violations of the federal National Labor Relations Act in federal district court in State A. The company was incorporated in State B and had most of its factories in State A (including the factory where the woman's claim arose). The company president and most of its decision-makers worked in an office in State C just across the border from State B. The woman claimed $80,000 in damages. Does the federal district court in State A have subject matter jurisdiction over the claim?
Answer A: A: No, because most of the company's factories are in State A.
Answer B: B: No, because the woman's claim arose from a company factory in State A.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the woman is suing under the National Labor Relations Act.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the company president and most of its decision-makers work in an office in State C.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Because the woman is bringing a federal claim, any issues regarding the citizenship of the parties are irrelevant. Therefore, A, B, and D are incorrect.
