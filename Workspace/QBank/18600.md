Question: In a federal theft trial, a store guard named Levi testifies that he took a marked coin from Jonah, placed it in an envelope, and handed the envelope to Officer Gideon. Gideon testifies that he sealed the envelope and delivered it to the court clerk. Jonah objects that Levi is not a police officer and therefore cannot start a chain of custody. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the coin because only law enforcement officers can authenticate physical evidence.
Answer B: Admit the coin if the testimony is sufficient to support a finding that the coin is the one taken from Jonah.
Answer C: Exclude the coin because coins are fungible and can never be traced.
Answer D: Admit the coin only if Jonah admits it belonged to him.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Chain-of-custody testimony can begin with a private witness who has personal knowledge. The question is whether the evidence is sufficient to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A creates a law-enforcement-only rule. C is too broad; markings and testimony can identify the coin. D wrongly requires an admission.
