Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: A pedestrian sues a driver for negligence after a crosswalk collision. The driver moves for summary judgment and submits a declaration saying the light was green. The pedestrian submits a sworn deposition excerpt saying the driver entered on a red light. Both accounts would be admissible at trial. How should the court rule on the driver's motion?
Answer A: Grant the motion, because the judge may credit the driver's declaration if it seems more detailed
Answer B: Grant the motion, because a deposition cannot defeat a declaration
Answer C: Deny the motion, because the competing admissible accounts create a genuine dispute about a material fact
Answer D: Deny the motion only if the pedestrian also proves damages by undisputed evidence
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The light color matters to liability, and the record supports competing versions. A jury must resolve that factual conflict.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Detail may matter to a jury, but Rule 56 does not let the judge choose the more believable admissible account. B: A deposition excerpt can create a Rule 56 dispute if it would be admissible and concerns a material fact. D: The motion described attacks liability. The disputed liability fact is enough to defeat that motion.
lb_rule_reference: Summary judgment is improper when admissible evidence creates a genuine dispute of material fact, because the court may not weigh credibility at Rule 56.
