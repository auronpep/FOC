Outline: Possibility of reverter
Question: David conveyed Orchardacre “to Solomon so long as the land is used as an orchard.” Solomon is still using the land as an orchard. What interest does David retain?
Answer A: A right of entry, because David must choose whether to terminate Solomon's estate.
Answer B: A reversion, because all future interests in grantors are reversions.
Answer C: A possibility of reverter, because Solomon holds a fee simple determinable.
Answer D: No interest, because Solomon has not breached the limitation.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: David retains a possibility of reverter. The phrase “so long as” creates a fee simple determinable in Solomon. David's retained future interest exists immediately, even before breach, and becomes possessory automatically if orchard use ceases.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a right of entry belongs with a fee simple subject to condition subsequent. B: B is wrong because grantor future interests have different labels depending on the estate conveyed. D: D is wrong because current compliance prevents possession from reverting now, but it does not eliminate David's future interest.
lb_rule_reference: Fee simple determinable / possibility of reverter
