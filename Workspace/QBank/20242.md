Question: Congress enacts a statute expressly allowing states to give in-state rail maintenance companies a preference for a specific federally funded rail-repair program. A state adopts the preference exactly as authorized. An out-of-state company brings only a DCC challenge. What is the state's strongest defense?
Answer A: Clear congressional authorization can permit a state burden that would otherwise violate the dormant commerce principle
Answer B: DCC limits apply the same way even after Congress clearly authorizes the burden
Answer C: Authorization counts only if every affected company signs the federal statute
Answer D: Authorization is irrelevant because rail repair is not commerce
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The DCC is dormant only when Congress has not authorized the state burden.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Congress may permit state burdens on interstate commerce. C: Congressional authorization does not require private company consent. D: The better defense is congressional authorization, not absence of commerce.
lb_rule_reference: Congress may authorize state laws that would otherwise burden interstate commerce, but the authorization must be clear.
