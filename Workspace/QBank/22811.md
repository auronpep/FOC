MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Judgments and Verdicts
Question: An investor brought a federal diversity action against a company, alleging breach of contract. After the parties presented their evidence, the court instructed the jury on the law. Neither the investor nor the company filed a motion for judgment as a matter of law. The case went to the jury, and the jury found for the investor. The court entered judgment on the verdict. The company moved for judgment as a matter of law, arguing that there was insufficient evidence to support the investor's claim. Although the court acknowledged that there were some problems with the evidence, the court denied the motion. The company appealed. Should the appellate court consider the company's motion?
Answer A: A: No, because the company did not make a motion for judgment as a matter of law before the case went to the jury.
Answer B: B: No, because sufficiency of the evidence is solely determined by the jury.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the court acknowledged that there were problems with the evidence.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the trial court denied the motion before it was brought to the appellate court.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: “A motion for a judgment as a matter of law may be made at any time before the case is submitted to a jury.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 50(a)(2). Here, the case was submitted to the jury before the company made its motion. Therefore, B, C, and D are incorrect.
