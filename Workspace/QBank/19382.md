MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Falling below the standard of care
Question: A city ordinance required each storefront owner to keep the public sidewalk directly in front of the store reasonably safe during winter. A store hired an independent snow-removal contractor. The contractor negligently left a hidden sheet of ice in front of the store, and a pedestrian fell. The store had no reason to know the contractor would do poor work. Is the store likely liable to the pedestrian in a negligence claim based on the icy sidewalk?
Answer A: No, because the contractor was independent and the store was not negligent in hiring it
Answer B: Yes, because the store had a nondelegable duty to maintain the sidewalk safely under the ordinance
Answer C: Yes, but only because snow removal is abnormally dangerous
Answer D: No, because the pedestrian must sue only the contractor that created the icy condition
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The ordinance placed the sidewalk-safety duty on the store. Hiring an independent contractor may give the store a contract claim against the contractor, but it does not defeat liability to the pedestrian.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: If the ordinance creates a nondelegable duty to keep the sidewalk safe, the store cannot avoid that duty by contracting it out. C: Ordinary snow removal is not abnormally dangerous. The correct exception is nondelegable duty, not strict liability for dangerous activity. D: The pedestrian may have claims against the contractor, but the store remains liable for breach of a nondelegable duty.
lb_rule_reference: A party subject to a nondelegable duty cannot avoid liability for breach of that duty merely by hiring an independent contractor to perform the work.
