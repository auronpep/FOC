Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: Rachel offers a handwritten letter allegedly written by Samuel admitting that he received her money. Rachel calls Naomi, who worked with Samuel for five years before the lawsuit and often saw him write notes. Naomi testifies that the handwriting on the letter looks like Samuel's. Samuel objects that Naomi is not a handwriting expert. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the letter because only a handwriting expert may identify handwriting.
Answer B: Exclude the letter because handwriting can be authenticated only by the alleged writer.
Answer C: Admit the letter if Naomi's familiarity with Samuel's handwriting was not acquired for the current litigation and is sufficient to support a finding of authenticity.
Answer D: Admit the letter only if Samuel first denies writing it under oath.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A nonexpert may give an opinion about handwriting if the familiarity was not acquired for the current litigation. Naomi's pre-litigation workplace familiarity can support authentication; the jury decides weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because expert testimony is not the only handwriting-authentication method. B: B is wrong because the alleged writer need not authenticate the letter. D: D is wrong because a denial by Samuel is not a prerequisite.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(b)(2)
lb_student_diagnostic: Expert-only and author-only authentication traps.
