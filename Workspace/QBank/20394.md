Question: A documentary about illegal gambling showed a teacher walking into a grocery store, then immediately cut to footage of a police raid on a gambling room in the same neighborhood. The edit made it appear that the teacher entered the gambling room, though the producers knew she only went to the grocery store. The documentary was streamed widely. Which privacy claim best fits?
Answer A: False light, because the edit publicized a knowingly misleading and highly offensive implication
Answer B: Public disclosure of private facts, because the grocery trip was true
Answer C: Intrusion, because the documentary was streamed widely
Answer D: No claim unless the teacher proves lost wages
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The claim targets a false public impression created by misleading juxtaposition.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The problem is not truthful private facts; it is a false implication. C: Intrusion concerns how information was obtained, not mere wide broadcast. D: False light does not require the same special-damages showing as ordinary slander.
lb_rule_reference: False light can arise from publicizing a misleading implication about the plaintiff that would be highly offensive, even if the implication is not framed as classic reputational defamation.
