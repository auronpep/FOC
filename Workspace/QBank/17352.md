Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A local safety newsletter summarized a recently filed public inspection report showing that a landlord's apartment building had repeated fire-code violations. The report was embarrassing to the landlord but was publicly available at the city clerk's office and concerned tenant safety. Is the landlord likely to prevail on a public disclosure of private facts claim against the newsletter?
Answer A: Yes, because the newsletter embarrassed the landlord
Answer B: No, because the reported facts came from a public record and concerned tenant safety
Answer C: No, because truthful publications are never actionable under any privacy tort
Answer D: Yes, because a public disclosure claim is identical to defamation
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts were in a public inspection report and involved legitimate public concern about fire safety.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Public disclosure liability does not attach to newsworthy facts already available in a public record. C: Some truthful private facts can support liability; this claim fails because the facts were public record and newsworthy. D: Public disclosure of private facts protects privacy, not reputation, and usually involves true facts.
lb_rule_reference: Public facts, public records, and matters of legitimate public concern generally defeat a public disclosure of private facts claim.
