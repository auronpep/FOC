Outline: Preliminary questions
Question: In a federal trial for assault, the prosecutor offers a knife found in a field near the scene. A witness has testified that the attacker used a knife, and the prosecutor tells the court that the next witness will testify that Joshua ran through that field immediately after the assault. Joshua objects that the knife is irrelevant until the connection is proved. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: The court may admit the knife on the condition that the prosecutor later introduces evidence sufficient to support the connection.
Answer B: The court must exclude the knife permanently because the connecting witness has not yet testified.
Answer C: The court must admit the knife as conclusive proof that Joshua was the attacker.
Answer D: The court must require Joshua to disprove the connection before the knife can be admitted.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Conditional relevance can be handled by admitting evidence subject to later proof of the connecting condition. The judge screens for sufficient support; the jury decides the condition and weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the court may control the order of proof and admit conditionally. C: C is wrong because admission does not make the knife conclusive proof. D: D is wrong because the proponent bears the burden to supply enough evidence of the condition; the opponent need not disprove it first.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / conditional relevance and order of proof
