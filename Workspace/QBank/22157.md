Outline_code: 31010100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: General Provisions
Question: A woman was charged with the murder of her ex- husband, whose body was found in an abandoned building. During trial, the prosecution sought to introduce the testimony of the ex- husband's brother, who planned on testifying that the ex- husband was really upset the morning of the murder and said, “I can't take it anymore! I'm going over to my ex- wife's house to tell her I'm not paying support! We never had any children, and she's perfectly capable of getting a job!” Is the brother's testimony admissible?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the ex- husband's statement was an excited utterance.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the ex- husband's statement showed his intent to go to his ex- wife's house.
Answer C: C: No, because the ex- husband is unavailable.
Answer D: D: No, because the ex- husband's statement is inadmissible hearsay.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 803(3), declarations of a declarant's then- existing intentions are described as statements of present state of mind and are admissible as exceptions to the hearsay rule. They may be relevant to establish that the declarant acted in a way that was consistent with those stated intentions; however, such evidence is relevant only to establish the conduct of the declarant himself or herself. The view taken by the majority and by the FRE is that they are not relevant to establish the conduct of others. Courts generally admit such statements with limiting instructions directing the jury to consider them only on the issue of the declarant's actions. A is incorrect because an excited utterance requires a statement relating to a startling event that was made while the declarant was under the stress of excitement from that event. Here, while the ex- husband seems mad and excited, his statement does not meet the requirements of an excited utterance. C is incorrect because this exception does not require the declarant to be available. D is incorrect because the statement meets the requirements of the then- existing intention exception.
