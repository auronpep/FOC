Outline: Psychotherapist-patient
Question: Mary brings a federal civil-rights action against a city. During discovery, the city seeks notes from confidential counseling sessions Mary had with Dr. Luke, a licensed psychotherapist, after the incident. Mary objects on psychotherapist-patient privilege grounds. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection, because federal evidence law recognizes only attorney-client privilege.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because confidential communications with a licensed psychotherapist for treatment are protected under federal common law.
Answer C: Overrule the objection, because federal courts never recognize privileges created by reason and experience.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if the city first proves the notes contain admissions of liability.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal common law under Rule 501 recognizes a psychotherapist-patient privilege for confidential treatment communications with a licensed psychotherapist. Mary's counseling notes fall within that protected relationship on these facts. The decisive axis is recognized federal privilege scope.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This under-recognizes federal common-law privileges. Psychotherapist-patient privilege is recognized. C: C is wrong because Rule 501 expressly leaves privilege development to federal courts in light of reason and experience. D: D is wrong because Potential evidentiary usefulness does not define whether the relationship and communication are privileged.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / Jaffee psychotherapist-patient privilege
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is either omitting the federally recognized psychotherapist privilege or treating relevance as the privilege gate.
