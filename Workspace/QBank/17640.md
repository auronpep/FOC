Question: Miriam bought a home next to John's small woodworking shed. John lawfully used the shed during daytime hours. Miriam disliked seeing and hearing the shed activity, but she had no covenant, easement, statute, or prior agreement limiting John's use, and the activity did not create dangerous conditions or unusual interference. Miriam claims an easement preventing John from operating the shed. What is the best answer?
Answer A: Miriam has an implied negative easement because she would prefer a quieter view.
Answer B: Miriam has no negative easement merely from ordinary neighboring activity and lacks a recognized source of a land-use restraint.
Answer C: Miriam has a profit because John's activity reduces the value of her land.
Answer D: Miriam has a prescriptive easement because she objected to John's activity after buying her home.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A negative easement or land-use restraint requires a recognized source. Ordinary lawful neighboring activity, without a covenant, easement, statute, or nuisance-level harm, does not create an implied easement preventing that activity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because preference for quiet or view does not create a negative easement. C: C is wrong because a profit requires removal of resources from land. D: D is wrong because objection is not adverse use of John's land for the prescriptive period.
