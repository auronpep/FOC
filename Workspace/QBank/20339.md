Question: A city ordinance bars community centers "promoting homosexual conduct" from renting city meeting rooms. The city allows other advocacy groups to rent the same rooms and identifies no safety, scheduling, or budget reason for the exclusion. Council members state that the ordinance is intended to keep gay-rights groups out of public spaces. What is the strongest Equal Protection problem?
Answer A: The ordinance automatically survives because rational basis always validates moral disapproval
Answer B: The ordinance may fail because the record shows bare animus rather than a legitimate government purpose
Answer C: The ordinance receives no review because renting meeting rooms is discretionary
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies automatically because every sexual-orientation classification is treated as race
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Even under rational-basis review, a government classification cannot rest only on hostility toward the targeted group.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Bare animus or desire to harm an unpopular group is not a legitimate government interest. C: Government discretion must still comply with Equal Protection. D: The core source-grounded exam point here is animus defeating rational basis, not race-level tier selection.
lb_rule_reference: A law motivated by bare animus toward a group can fail even deferential rational-basis review.
