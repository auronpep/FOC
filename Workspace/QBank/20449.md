Question: Police arrested Diane on suspicion of bank robbery based on a witness's description. Before any charges were filed and before the case was presented to a grand jury, detectives conducted an in-person lineup with Diane and five fillers. Diane requested that her attorney be present, but the detective refused and proceeded. The witness identified Diane. A week later, a grand jury indicted Diane. Did the detectives violate Diane's Sixth Amendment right to counsel by proceeding with the lineup without her attorney present?
Answer A: Yes, because Diane requested counsel and her request was denied
Answer B: Yes, because any in-person lineup is a critical stage requiring the presence of counsel
Answer C: No, because the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had not yet attached at the time of the pre-indictment lineup
Answer D: No, because Diane consented to participating in the lineup by appearing without formal objection
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches only upon the initiation of formal adversarial judicial proceedings — indictment, information, arraignment, or similar charging instrument. Diane's lineup occurred before any charges were filed. Without the attachment of the Sixth Amendment, there is no constitutional right to have counsel present at a lineup, even if the suspect requests it. The identification is not excludable on Sixth Amendment grounds. (Due process could still apply if the procedure was impermissibly suggestive and unreliable, but the facts do not raise that issue.)
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Fifth Amendment right to counsel under *Miranda* applies during custodial interrogation, not lineups. The Sixth Amendment right to counsel at a lineup depends on whether formal proceedings have commenced — not on whether the suspect invoked the right. Because no charges had been filed, the Sixth Amendment had not yet attached, and denying counsel at this pre-indictment lineup was constitutionally permissible. B: The *Wade* right to counsel applies only to post-indictment in-person lineups. At the time of Diane's lineup, adversarial judicial proceedings had not commenced — she had not been indicted, charged by information, or arraigned. The Sixth Amendment does not attach until formal charges are initiated. D: While the answer reaches the correct result (no violation), the reasoning is wrong. The correct basis is that the Sixth Amendment had not yet attached. Consent has no bearing on the Sixth Amendment analysis; an already-attached right cannot be waived simply by participating. The question asks why no violation occurred, and "no attachment" is the only constitutionally accurate answer.
lb_rule_reference: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel does not attach at a pre-indictment lineup because adversarial judicial proceedings have not yet commenced.
