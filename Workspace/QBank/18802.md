MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Brightstone Apparel Inc. is incorporated in Florida with its principal place of business in Miami. Brightstone operates 12 retail stores in the State of Caldwell, employs 340 Caldwell residents, and generates 22% of its total revenue from Caldwell sales. It markets extensively to Caldwell consumers through local television and social media. A Caldwell resident purchased a Brightstone jacket at a Brightstone store in Caldwell. While wearing the jacket on a hiking trip in the State of Mantis, she slipped on loose gravel and suffered a broken wrist. She claims the jacket's slick lining contributed to her fall. She sues Brightstone in Caldwell federal court, asserting the jacket was negligently designed. Brightstone moves to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction on the negligent-design claim. Should the Caldwell court grant Brightstone's motion to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction?
Answer A: No, because Brightstone's 12 stores, 340 employees, and 22% revenue from Caldwell create continuous and systematic contacts that subject it to general jurisdiction in Caldwell for any claim
Answer B: No, because the plaintiff purchased the jacket in Caldwell, which creates a sufficient connection between Brightstone's Caldwell contacts and the negligent-design claim
Answer C: Yes, because the plaintiff's negligent-design claim does not arise from or sufficiently relate to Brightstone's Caldwell contacts — the design decision and the injury both occurred outside Caldwell
Answer D: Yes, because Brightstone's corporate decisions are made in Florida, and Caldwell can only exercise specific jurisdiction over conduct performed in Caldwell
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Specific jurisdiction requires that the claim arise out of or relate to the defendant's forum contacts. Brightstone's Caldwell contacts are retail sales and marketing. The plaintiff's claim is for negligent design of the jacket — a design choice made in Florida. The injury occurred in Mantis. Although the jacket was sold in Caldwell, that sale does not create a sufficient nexus between Caldwell and a design-defect theory under *Ford Motor Co.*'s relatedness standard. General jurisdiction is also unavailable (Brightstone is not at home in Caldwell). The motion should be granted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: After *Daimler*, general jurisdiction over a corporation is available only in its state of incorporation or principal-place-of-business state. Brightstone is incorporated in Florida and headquartered in Miami. Its Caldwell contacts, however substantial, do not make Caldwell a home state for general-jurisdiction purposes. B: The negligent-design claim is about how the jacket was designed — a decision made in Florida. The Caldwell sale is a transaction, not the defendant's forum conduct that the claim challenges. *Ford Motor Co.* requires a meaningful connection between the forum contacts and the legal claim; the mere fact that the product was sold in the forum does not automatically make a design-defect claim "arise out of" that sale when the defective design decision occurred outside the forum. Additionally, the injury occurred in Mantis, not Caldwell. The Caldwell sale is a tenuous connection for a claim grounded in design choices made elsewhere. D: Specific jurisdiction does not require that the defendant's conduct be performed in the forum. What matters is that the claim arises from or relates to the defendant's forum contacts. Brightstone could be subject to specific jurisdiction in Caldwell for claims arising from its Caldwell store operations — for example, a slip-and-fall in a Caldwell store. The issue here is that the design-defect claim doesn't relate to those Caldwell contacts, not that out-of-state decisions are categorically unreachable.
lb_rule_reference: Specific personal jurisdiction requires that the plaintiff's claim arise out of or relate to the defendant's forum contacts. A defendant with many forum contacts cannot be sued in that forum on a claim that has no connection to those contacts. *Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court* (2021) clarified the "relate to" prong to require a causal or sufficiently related connection, not mere foreseeability.
