Question: A retailer orally orders 1,000 plain white bowls from a manufacturer for $6,000. The manufacturer begins making the bowls the next day. The bowls are standard items that the manufacturer sells every month to many buyers. Before any writing is signed, the retailer repudiates. The manufacturer claims the specially manufactured goods exception. Does the exception apply?
Answer A: Yes, because the manufacturer began making the bowls before repudiation.
Answer B: No, because the bowls are standard goods suitable for sale to others in the seller's ordinary business.
Answer C: Yes, because any goods made after an oral order are specially manufactured.
Answer D: No, but only because the manufacturer had not completed all 1,000 bowls.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The specially manufactured goods exception requires goods made for the buyer that are not suitable for sale to others in the ordinary course of the seller's business. Plain standard bowls are ordinary inventory, so substantial beginning alone does not satisfy the exception.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because substantial beginning is not enough without unsuitability to others. C: C is wrong because ordinary goods made after an order are not automatically specially manufactured. D: D is wrong because completion of all goods is not the key issue.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201(3)(a) — suitability to others
