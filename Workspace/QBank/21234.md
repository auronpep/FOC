MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Burglary
Question: Detectives asked Milo to come to the station about a burglary. Once he arrived, they took his phone, locked the interview-room door, told him he was not leaving until he "cleared this up," and questioned him for two hours. Milo admitted entering the house. No Miranda warnings were given. Should Milo's admission be suppressed under Miranda?
Answer A: Yes, because the restrictions made the interview custodial and the detectives interrogated him without warnings
Answer B: Yes, because all stationhouse interviews are custodial
Answer C: No, because Milo came to the station voluntarily
Answer D: No, because burglary is not a Fifth Amendment offense
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The locked room, phone seizure, command that Milo could not leave, and extended questioning created restraint comparable to arrest. Because detectives interrogated him without warnings, the statement is suppressible from the prosecution's case-in-chief.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The location alone does not decide custody. Here custody exists because of the restraints, not because every station interview is custodial. C: Voluntary arrival is not conclusive. Police later locked the door, took his phone, and told him he could not leave. D: Miranda is not limited to particular crimes. It applies to custodial interrogation seeking incriminating statements.
lb_rule_reference: Custody is objective and depends on whether a reasonable person would feel freedom curtailed to the degree associated with formal arrest.
