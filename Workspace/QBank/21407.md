Question: Levi is charged in federal court with bank robbery. The government obtains a court order requiring Levi to provide fingerprints and a handwriting exemplar. Levi refuses and asserts that producing them would violate his Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because the exemplars may help convict Levi.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because all compelled evidence from a defendant is testimonial.
Answer C: Overrule the objection, because fingerprints and handwriting exemplars are physical identifying evidence, not compelled testimonial communications.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Levi first testifies at trial.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The Fifth Amendment protects against compelled testimonial self-incrimination, not the compelled display or collection of physical identifying characteristics such as fingerprints or handwriting exemplars. The decisive axis is testimonial versus physical evidence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Incriminating usefulness is not enough; the compulsion must be testimonial. B: B is wrong because This overclaims the Fifth Amendment by covering all compelled evidence. D: D is wrong because Levi's trial testimony is irrelevant to the physical-evidence distinction.
lb_rule_reference: Fifth Amendment / physical-characteristic evidence
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is using the Fifth Amendment to block physical evidence merely because it is incriminating.
