Outline: Bias
Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Mary testifies for the defendant manufacturer. Before trial, Mary settled her own related injury claim against the same manufacturer. As part of that settlement, the manufacturer paid Mary and obtained Mary's promise to cooperate in related litigation. The plaintiff asks Mary about the settlement and cooperation promise. The manufacturer objects under the settlement rule. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because settlement evidence is never admissible in a later trial.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the cooperation promise is inadmissible as a prior inconsistent statement.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the settlement evidence is offered to show Mary's possible bias or motive to cooperate, not to prove the validity or amount of a disputed claim.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the plaintiff first offers the settlement to prove the manufacturer's liability.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 408 bars compromise evidence for proving or disproving the validity or amount of a disputed claim and for certain impeachment uses, but the court may admit it for another purpose, such as proving a witness's bias or prejudice. Mary's settlement and cooperation promise are offered to show her possible motive to favor the manufacturer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because settlement evidence is not absolutely barred. B: B is wrong because the evidence is not being used as a prior inconsistent statement or contradiction. D: D is wrong because liability use would be the prohibited purpose; the valid route is bias.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 408(b) / settlement evidence for bias
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests settlement-policy purpose routing and the bias exception.
