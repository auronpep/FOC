Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: During a lawful traffic stop, an officer sees the driver repeatedly reach under the front seat and notices a knife sheath on the console. The officer orders the driver out and searches under the driver's seat, finding a loaded handgun. The officer does not search the trunk or locked luggage. Was the under-seat search valid?
Answer A: Yes, because officers had a reasonable safety basis to search the passenger area where a weapon could be accessible
Answer B: No, because Terry frisks apply only to clothing, never cars
Answer C: Yes, because any traffic stop permits a full car search
Answer D: No, because officers needed probable cause to believe the car contained contraband
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The reaching movement and knife sheath supported a reasonable belief of danger. The search stayed within the passenger compartment area where a weapon could be hidden and reachable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A limited vehicle protective search is allowed when officers reasonably suspect danger and weapon access. C: The search is valid only because of specific safety facts and limited scope, not because all car searches follow traffic stops. D: This was a safety search for weapons during a stop, not an automobile-exception evidence search.
lb_rule_reference: During a lawful stop, officers may search the passenger compartment for weapons only if they reasonably believe the suspect is dangerous and may gain immediate control of weapons.
