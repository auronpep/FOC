Question: Naomi owned a field of growing barley. She agreed to sell the barley crop, apart from the land, to Ruth for a stated price. Ruth was to harvest the crop herself at the end of the season. Naomi later argued that Article 2 could not apply because the barley was still attached to the land when they contracted. Is Naomi correct?
Answer A: Yes, because anything attached to real property is governed only by real-property law until actually severed.
Answer B: Yes, because Article 2 applies only if the seller severs the goods.
Answer C: Yes, because crops are not goods unless the land itself is sold with them.
Answer D: No, because a contract for the sale apart from the land of growing crops capable of severance can be a contract for the sale of goods under Article 2.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Article 2 treats a contract for the sale apart from the land of growing crops or similar things capable of severance as a contract for the sale of goods, even if the crops are still attached to the realty when the parties contract and regardless of whether buyer or seller will sever them.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Article 2 specifically covers some goods attached to realty. B: B is wrong because the seller-severance limitation applies to certain minerals or structures, not growing crops covered by the crop rule. C: C is wrong because the crop is sold apart from the land; the land itself need not be sold.
