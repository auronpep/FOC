Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff and defendant litigate a state-law contract action to judgment in federal court. After losing, the defendant discovers that both parties were citizens of the same state when the complaint was filed and that no federal claim was pleaded. The defendant raises lack of subject matter jurisdiction for the first time on appeal. How should the appellate court treat the objection?
Answer A: Reject it because all jurisdiction objections are waived if not raised in the first Rule 12 response
Answer B: Reject it because a final judgment cures any SMJ defect
Answer C: Consider it because lack of subject matter jurisdiction can be raised at any time, including on appeal
Answer D: Consider it only if the plaintiff agrees that jurisdiction was lacking
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: SMJ is nonwaivable and must be addressed whenever it appears.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: That waiver rule applies to personal jurisdiction and similar defenses, not SMJ. B: A court without SMJ lacks power; finality does not create jurisdiction. D: Party agreement cannot create or waive federal subject matter jurisdiction.
lb_rule_reference: Lack of subject matter jurisdiction can be raised at any time, including for the first time on appeal, and courts must address it.
