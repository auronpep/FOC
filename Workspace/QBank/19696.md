Question: Esther bought a printing press from Daniel for $90,000. The press was warranted to print 10,000 labels per hour, but it printed only 6,000. Esther accepted the press and timely notified Daniel of the defect. A reasonable $8,000 repair improved the press, but after the repair it was still worth $5,000 less than a fully conforming press. What may Esther recover for the nonconformity, apart from any separate consequential losses?
Answer A: Nothing, because she accepted and repaired the press.
Answer B: $13,000, reflecting the reasonable repair cost plus the remaining value difference.
Answer C: $8,000 only, because repair costs always replace all other accepted-goods damages.
Answer D: $90,000, the full contract price.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Accepted-goods damages may be measured in any reasonable way for the loss resulting from the nonconformity. Where repair reasonably reduces the loss but residual diminished value remains, recovery can include the reasonable repair cost plus the remaining value difference. That totals $13,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because acceptance does not waive damages for nonconformity after proper notice. C: C is wrong because repair costs do not automatically erase residual loss. D: D is wrong because the buyer does not recover the full price while keeping the press.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-714; UCC §2-715
