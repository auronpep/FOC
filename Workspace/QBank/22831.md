


BARMATRIX Q#: 22831
Outline_code: 94020000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Personal Jurisdiction
Question: A woman sued a man in federal district court in State A for $80,000 in damages. The woman served the man at his residence in State B. The man filed an answer that denied all of the woman's allegations. In the answer, he also moved to dismiss the complaint, arguing that the court lacked personal jurisdiction over him. Is the man's motion to dismiss proper?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the man challenged personal jurisdiction in his first response.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the man could challenge personal jurisdiction at any time before a verdict or final judicial decision.
Answer C: C: No, because the man also denied all of the woman's allegations in the same response.
Answer D: D: No, because there was complete diversity between the parties and the amount in controversy was over $75,000.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(2), a defendant may object to personal jurisdiction so long as that objection is asserted in the defendant's first response. Here, since this is the man's first response, he may assert his motion to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction. B is incorrect because an objection to personal jurisdiction is waived if it is not made in the first response. C is incorrect because the fact that the man also denied the woman's allegations in the same response would not change the analysis. D is incorrect because diversity and amount in controversy are issues in subject matter jurisdiction, not personal jurisdiction.
