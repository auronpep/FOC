Question: An officer stopped Nia without reasonable suspicion, kept her driver's license, and told her she could not leave. Thirty seconds later, the officer asked to search her purse. Nia said yes. The officer found stolen checks. Nia moves to suppress. What is the best basis for suppression?
Answer A: Consent always cures an illegal stop
Answer B: Suppression is proper because the consent was obtained during an ongoing illegal stop and did not attenuate the taint
Answer C: Suppression is unavailable because purses have no Fourth Amendment protection in public
Answer D: Suppression is proper only if the officer acted in bad faith
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The officer unlawfully detained Nia, retained her license, and immediately requested consent. The consent was closely tied to the illegal seizure, so the checks are fruit of the violation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Consent following illegality must be both voluntary and sufficiently attenuated from the violation. Immediate consent during an ongoing unlawful detention is tainted. C: A person has a privacy interest in a closed purse. The issue is tainted consent. D: Bad faith is not required for tainted consent after an unlawful seizure.
lb_rule_reference: Consent can validate a search only if voluntary and not the product of an unresolved constitutional violation.
