Question: Abraham owned Blackacre. On Monday, Abraham signed and delivered a quitclaim deed conveying Blackacre to Isaac. On Tuesday, Abraham signed and delivered a general warranty deed conveying Blackacre to Jacob. Assume no recording statute applies. Jacob argues that his warranty deed is superior because Isaac received only a quitclaim deed. Who owns Blackacre?
Answer A: Isaac, because the earlier valid deed transferred Abraham's title before the later warranty deed was delivered.
Answer B: Jacob, because a warranty deed always defeats a prior quitclaim deed.
Answer C: Jacob, because a quitclaim deed transfers only possession and not title.
Answer D: Isaac only if he paid more consideration than Jacob.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive priority lock under the stated common-law assumption is first in time after a valid conveyance. Abraham's quitclaim deed to Isaac transferred whatever title Abraham had on Monday. By Tuesday, Abraham had no title left to convey to Jacob, and the warranty label did not restore it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because deed type alone does not create priority over an earlier valid conveyance. C: C is wrong because a quitclaim deed can transfer title. D: D is wrong because consideration amount is not the deciding lock under these facts.
lb_rule_reference: Warranty deed label does not defeat earlier valid quitclaim
