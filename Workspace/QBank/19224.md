Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Hannah signed a written agreement to buy 200 baskets from Samuel for $2 each. At the same signing meeting, Samuel orally said, “Actually, you may pay only $1.50 each.” The writing was final as to the basket price. Hannah later offers Samuel's oral statement to reduce the price. How should the evidence be treated?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because contemporaneous oral terms are never parol evidence.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because it contradicts the final written price term.
Answer C: Admit the evidence because it was made at the same meeting as the writing.
Answer D: Exclude the written agreement and enforce the oral price only.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The parol evidence rule applies to prior and contemporaneous oral agreements. The oral $1.50 term contradicts the written $2 price and is barred when the writing is final as to price.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because contemporaneous oral terms are classic parol evidence. C: C is wrong because the same-meeting timing does not avoid the rule. D: D is wrong because the inconsistent oral term does not replace the final writing.
lb_rule_reference: Contemporaneous oral contradiction barred
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats contemporaneous oral terms as stronger than prior oral terms.
