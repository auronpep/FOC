Question: Sarah posts a notice: “$1,000 reward to the first person who finds and returns my lost ring.” John reads the notice, finds the ring, and returns it to Sarah. Sarah refuses to pay, arguing that John never promised in advance that he would look for the ring. Is Sarah liable for the reward?
Answer A: Yes, because John accepted the unilateral offer by completing the requested performance after learning of the offer.
Answer B: Yes, because John's act of looking for the ring was a promise to continue searching.
Answer C: No, because John did not sign a written acceptance before returning the ring.
Answer D: No, because reward offers are gratuitous promises and can never be enforceable.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: John knew of the reward offer and completed the requested performance by finding and returning the ring. A unilateral contract was formed on completion of the performance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the contract was formed by completion, not by an implied promise to keep searching. C: C is wrong because a signed advance acceptance was not required by the offer. D: D is wrong because reward offers can be accepted by performance and become enforceable.
lb_rule_reference: common_law
lb_student_diagnostic: Student demanded advance promise or writing for a performance-only reward.
