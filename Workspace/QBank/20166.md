Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: Leah sues Solomon Tools after a saw blade shattered. Leah offers the broken blade, which still bears a factory-stamped Solomon Tools name, logo, and model number. Solomon Tools objects that no employee testified that the company made that blade. How should the court rule on the authentication objection?
Answer A: Sustain it because only a company employee may authenticate a product bearing the company's mark.
Answer B: Overrule it because a trade inscription or label affixed in the course of business may identify origin without extrinsic authentication.
Answer C: Sustain it because the name and logo are hearsay and cannot identify the product's source.
Answer D: Overrule it only if Leah proves the saw blade was not counterfeit beyond reasonable dispute.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A trade inscription, tag, or label purporting to be affixed in the course of business and indicating origin can authenticate the source of a product. Counterfeit or mistake arguments may affect weight or support further objection, but a company employee is not categorically required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a company employee is not the only permissible authentication channel. C: C is wrong because the label is used as an authentication/source identifier, not merely as a testimonial assertion requiring a hearsay exception. D: D is wrong because authentication does not require eliminating every possibility of counterfeit.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(a); FRE 902(7)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats labels as hearsay or requires a source witness despite trade-inscription authentication.
