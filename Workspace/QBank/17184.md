Subtopic: Law Applied by Federal Courts (Erie)
Outline: Erie Doctrine
Question: A plaintiff sues in federal court asserting one federal civil-rights claim and one supplemental state-law defamation claim from the same incident. The state defamation claim has a state-law privilege defense. The plaintiff argues Erie is irrelevant because the court's original jurisdiction rests on a federal question. Is Erie irrelevant to the state-law claim?
Answer A: Yes, because federal-question jurisdiction makes every claim federal
Answer B: Yes, because state defenses never apply in federal court
Answer C: No, because the court applies state substantive law to the supplemental state-law claim
Answer D: No, but only if all parties are diverse
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Erie principles govern state-law claims heard in federal court, even alongside federal claims.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Supplemental jurisdiction does not transform state claims into federal claims. B: State substantive defenses apply to state-law claims. D: Erie can matter for supplemental state claims without diversity.
lb_rule_reference: Federal courts apply state substantive law to state-law claims, including supplemental state claims.
