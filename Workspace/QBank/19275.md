Outline: Marital Communications Privilege
Question: During a private conversation while married, a husband told his wife, “I forged the deed.” Two years later, after the spouses divorced, the wife is called in a federal civil action to repeat the statement. The former husband objects under the marital communications privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because divorce terminates privilege for all marital statements.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because marital communications privilege applies only in criminal cases.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if the wife is unwilling to testify.
Answer D: Sustain the objection because the statement was a confidential communication made during the marriage and the privilege survives divorce.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The marital communications privilege protects confidential communications made between spouses during marriage and generally survives the end of the marriage. Unlike adverse spousal testimony, it is not limited to current marriage at trial. The decisive axis is confidential communication during marriage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is a divorce-ends-privilege error. The communication privilege can survive divorce. B: B is wrong because This confuses marital communications with the adverse spousal testimonial privilege. C: C is wrong because Willingness matters for adverse spousal testimony, not for the protected confidential communication in this stem.
