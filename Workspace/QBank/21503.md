Question: Noah orally agreed to sell land to Abigail. Abigail paid Noah $15,000 as a deposit, but the parties signed no writing. Abigail never took possession or made improvements, and the oral contract is unenforceable under the Statute of Frauds. Noah keeps the deposit and refuses to convey the land. Which statement best describes the consequence?
Answer A: The oral agreement is void, so Abigail has no possible claim of any kind.
Answer B: The Statute of Frauds bars enforcement of the land-sale promise, but Abigail may seek restitution of benefits conferred if Noah would otherwise be unjustly enriched.
Answer C: The Statute of Frauds transfers title to Abigail because she paid a deposit.
Answer D: The Statute of Frauds converts the oral contract into a one-year lease.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The Statute of Frauds is generally an enforceability defense, not a rule that makes the transaction void for all purposes. Although Abigail cannot specifically enforce the oral land sale on these facts, restitution may be available to recover benefits conferred.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because unenforceable is not the same as void for all remedial purposes. C: C is wrong because deposit payment does not transfer title. D: D is wrong because SOF does not rewrite the parties' land-sale agreement into a lease.
