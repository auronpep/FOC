Question: Martha openly and adversely used Lazarus's driveway for 12 years. The jurisdiction's prescriptive period is 10 years. After the 12th year, Lazarus said, “You may continue using the driveway for now.” Two years later, Lazarus revoked that statement and blocked the driveway. What is Martha's best argument?
Answer A: Her only right is a revocable license because Lazarus later used permission language.
Answer B: A prescriptive easement had already matured before Lazarus's later permission statement.
Answer C: She owns the driveway because prescription always transfers title.
Answer D: She has no claim because a driveway can be used only under an express deed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Once the elements of a prescriptive easement have been satisfied for the statutory period, the easement has matured. Lazarus's later permission language does not retroactively make the prior 12 years permissive.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because later permission does not erase an already matured prescriptive easement. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because prescription of an easement creates a use right, not fee title. D: D is wrong because driveway easements may arise by prescription.
lb_rule_reference: Matured prescription not erased by later permission
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is letting later license language rewrite the character of earlier adverse use.
