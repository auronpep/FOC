Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Authentication and Identification
Question: In a federal robbery trial, the prosecutor offers a glove with a tear across the thumb and a dark stain. A witness saw the robber drop a torn glove while fleeing. An officer found the glove at that spot within five minutes, photographed it there, and placed it in an evidence box. The evidence-room clerk testifies that the box was logged but cannot identify the officer who carried it from the scene to the evidence room. The defendant objects that the chain is incomplete. What is the proper ruling?
Answer A: Exclude the glove because the unidentified carrier must testify.
Answer B: Exclude the glove because the stain makes it unfairly prejudicial as a matter of law.
Answer C: Admit the glove only if the prosecutor first proves the stain is blood.
Answer D: Admit the glove because the scene location, timing, tear, photograph, and log sufficiently connect it to the robbery; the carrier gap goes to weight.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The glove is nonfungible and has distinctive circumstances connecting it to the robbery. The unidentified carrier creates a chain weakness, but the combined location, timing, tear, photograph, and log provide sufficient authentication.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because every chain link need not testify when other identification evidence is strong. B: B is wrong because a stain is not automatically unfair prejudice requiring exclusion. C: C is wrong because the glove may be relevant even before the stain is chemically identified.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901(a)
lb_student_diagnostic: Student demands perfect custody and misses distinctive-object authentication.
