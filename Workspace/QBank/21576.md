Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: Plaintiff asserts three claims. The court grants defendant summary judgment on one claim but leaves two claims for trial. The court does not enter Rule 54(b) certification and does not otherwise indicate immediate appealability. Plaintiff files an immediate appeal. Is the partial summary-judgment order immediately appealable as a final judgment?
Answer A: Yes, because every summary-judgment ruling is immediately appealable
Answer B: Yes, because the resolved claim was important
Answer C: No, because claims remain pending and there is no Rule 54(b) certification or exception
Answer D: No, because summary judgment rulings can never be appealed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The case continues on two claims, so the order is not final by default.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Summary judgment can be partial and nonfinal. B: Importance alone does not create finality. D: Final summary judgment can be appealed; this one is partial and uncertified.
lb_rule_reference: An order resolving fewer than all claims or parties is generally not final unless Rule 54(b) certification or another exception applies.
