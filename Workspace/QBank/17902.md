Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Joseph contracted with Mary to supply advertising services for a six-month campaign. Mary breached after one month. Joseph proves with reasonable certainty that he lost $18,000 in net profits for the remaining five months of that campaign. Joseph also seeks $60,000 for profits he hoped to earn from a second campaign that Mary might have ordered the following year, but there was no renewal option, no signed second contract, and no commitment by Mary. What lost profits may Joseph recover?
Answer A: The $78,000 total, because both amounts were possible consequences of the breach.
Answer B: Only the $60,000 future-campaign amount, because it is larger and better protects expectation.
Answer C: The $18,000 proven net profits from the remaining contract term, but not the speculative future-campaign profits.
Answer D: Nothing, because any claim involving lost business profits is speculative.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Joseph may recover lost profits from the breached six-month contract if proven with reasonable certainty. The possible future campaign lacks a firm commitment and is too speculative on these facts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because possible future business is not enough. B: B is wrong because the larger amount is the more speculative one. D: D is wrong because proven lost profits during the existing contract term can be recoverable.
lb_rule_reference: Lost profits / contract term and speculative future expansion
