Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A fireworks company stored a large inventory of display-grade fireworks in a warehouse next to apartment buildings. The company had a valid permit. A spark from unknown origin ignited the fireworks, and the explosion injured a tenant in a neighboring building. The tenant sues for strict liability. Which answer best states the likely strict-liability result?
Answer A: The company may be strictly liable because explosion injury is within the abnormal risk of storing large fireworks in that setting
Answer B: The company cannot be strictly liable because it had a permit
Answer C: The company is liable only if the tenant proves who caused the spark
Answer D: The tenant loses because fireworks are not wild animals
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The quantity, explosive character, and residential context support abnormally dangerous classification, and the injury resulted from explosion risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A permit does not necessarily defeat strict liability for harm caused by an abnormally dangerous activity. C: Strict liability does not require proving negligent ignition when the harm flows from the explosive risk. D: Abnormally dangerous activities are a separate strict-liability category.
lb_rule_reference: Storing large quantities of explosives in a residential setting may be abnormally dangerous, and strict liability reaches explosion injuries within the abnormal risk.
