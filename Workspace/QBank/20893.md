Outline: Bias
Question: In a federal breach-of-contract trial, Ezra testifies against Nehemiah. On cross-examination, Nehemiah asks Ezra whether Nehemiah fired Ezra from a job two months before the contract dispute. Ezra objects that the firing is a prior bad act offered to show Ezra's character. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because the firing may show Ezra's hostility toward Nehemiah and motive to testify against him.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because prior disputes are always propensity evidence.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless the firing resulted in a conviction.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Ezra first testifies that he is a truthful person.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A prior dispute between a witness and a party may show bias, hostility, revenge motive, or interest. The firing is not offered to show Ezra's character; it is offered to explain why Ezra might slant testimony against Nehemiah.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the evidence is offered for bias, not propensity. C: C is wrong because no conviction is required to show bias. D: D is wrong because truthfulness-character testimony is irrelevant to the bias route.
lb_rule_reference: Bias impeachment / prior dispute or grudge
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests hostility/revenge bias and rejects propensity-label overuse.
