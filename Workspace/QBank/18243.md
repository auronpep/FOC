Question: Congress limits eligibility for a federal cash-benefit program to citizens and lawful permanent residents who have lived in the United States for at least five years. A recently admitted lawful permanent resident challenges the federal classification as alienage discrimination. What scrutiny is most likely?
Answer A: Strict scrutiny, because all alienage classifications are suspect
Answer B: Intermediate scrutiny, because benefits are less important than jobs
Answer C: Deferential rational-basis review, because Congress is regulating eligibility in an area tied to immigration
Answer D: No review, because federal statutes are immune from Equal Protection principles
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal alienage classifications are treated deferentially in light of Congress's immigration and naturalization authority.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Federal alienage classifications receive more deferential review because of the federal immigration and naturalization power. B: The federal/state source of the alienage rule is the controlling tier distinction here. D: Equal-protection principles apply to the federal government through due process doctrine, even though review is deferential here.
lb_rule_reference: Federal alienage classifications tied to immigration and naturalization receive deferential review, unlike most state alienage classifications.
