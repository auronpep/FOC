Question: In a products case, the plaintiff seeks a memorandum written by defense counsel that ranks witnesses, evaluates trial themes, and predicts which arguments are likely to persuade the jury. The plaintiff argues that the memorandum is the best available evidence of the defendant's knowledge of the product defect. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Protect the memorandum because it contains counsel's mental impressions and legal strategy.
Answer B: Compel production because relevance defeats work-product protection.
Answer C: Compel production because all trial-preparation materials are discoverable on a showing of need.
Answer D: Protect the memorandum only if it was also sent directly to the client.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Opinion work product—counsel's mental impressions, legal theories, and strategy—receives strong protection. Relevance and evidentiary need do not make legal strategy discoverable in ordinary circumstances. The decisive axis is opinion work product.
