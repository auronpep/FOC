Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Definition
Question: Naomi sues a store after slipping near its entrance at 9 a.m. The store denies that the floor was wet. Naomi offers a weather report showing that heavy rain fell at the store's address between 7:30 and 8:30 a.m. The store objects that the rain could have stopped before Naomi entered and that the floor could have dried. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the weather report, because recent rain has some tendency to make a wet entrance more probable; drying arguments go to weight.
Answer B: Exclude the weather report, because it does not prove the floor was wet at 9 a.m.
Answer C: Exclude the weather report, because weather is background information and never consequential.
Answer D: Admit the weather report only if Naomi produces a witness who saw water on the floor.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The recent rain makes it more probable that water was tracked into the entrance. The report is not conclusive, but Rule 401 requires only some tendency to make a consequential fact more probable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because evidence need not prove the fact by itself to be relevant. C: C is wrong because weather can be consequential when it logically relates to the condition at issue. D: D is wrong because eyewitness confirmation is not required for threshold relevance.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is excluding useful circumstantial context because it is incomplete.
