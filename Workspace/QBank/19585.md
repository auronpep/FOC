MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A boat operator negligently created a wake that knocked a swimmer unconscious. A bystander rowed out to help but clumsily dropped an oar, delaying the rescue and worsening the swimmer's hypothermia. The swimmer sues the boat operator for the worsened injury. What is the best proximate-cause analysis?
Answer A: The bystander's rescue attempt automatically supersedes the boat operator's negligence
Answer B: The boat operator can be liable for worsened harm from an ordinary negligent rescue attempt
Answer C: The swimmer cannot recover because rescuers owe no duties
Answer D: The boat operator is liable only if the rescuer acted intentionally
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The rescue effort is within the risks set in motion by endangering the swimmer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rescue is a foreseeable response to peril. C: The question concerns the original tortfeasor's proximate cause, not only the rescuer's duty. D: Intentional rescue conduct is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Rescue efforts are generally foreseeable responses to danger created by negligence, and ordinary negligence in rescue usually does not supersede.
