Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: Talia fires a pistol at Victor intending to kill him. The bullet misses Victor and passes near Nora, whom Talia did not see and did not intend to harm. Nora is frightened but uninjured. Prosecutors charge Talia with attempted murder of Nora. What is Talia's strongest argument against the attempted murder of Nora charge?
Answer A: Transferred intent generally does not supply the specific intent needed for attempted murder of an unintended victim
Answer B: Talia is guilty of attempted murder of Nora because intent to kill Victor transfers to every nearby person
Answer C: Talia is not guilty of any attempted murder because no one died
Answer D: Talia is guilty of attempted murder of Nora only because Nora was frightened
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Talia may be guilty of attempted murder of Victor, the intended target. But attempted murder of Nora requires intent to kill Nora, and transferred intent does not ordinarily create attempt liability for a person the defendant did not target.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: That rule applies to completed victim crimes when an actual harm occurs. Attempt requires intent as to the attempted victim. C: Attempted murder of Victor can be complete without a death if Talia intended to kill Victor and took a substantial step. D: Fear may matter for assault, but attempted murder requires intent to kill.
lb_rule_reference: Transferred intent can support completed homicide when an unintended victim dies, but it generally does not create attempted murder liability for an unintended person who was not the defendant's target.
