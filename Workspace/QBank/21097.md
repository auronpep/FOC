Question: In a federal tax-evasion prosecution, the government calls the defendant's longtime internist to testify that the defendant said he hid cash income to avoid taxes. The statement was made during an ordinary physical exam. The defendant objects that the statement is protected by physician-patient privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because federal common law does not generally recognize a physician-patient privilege.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because statements to a physician for treatment are privileged in all federal proceedings.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because the doctor owed professional confidentiality to the patient.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the statement was not made for purposes of medical diagnosis or treatment.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Federal common law generally does not recognize a physician-patient privilege. Professional confidentiality and medical hearsay rules are different from an evidentiary privilege. Because this is a federal criminal case and no other privilege applies, the physician-patient objection fails.
