Question: Noah agreed in a signed writing to sell Rachel “100 crates of market peaches.” The writing did not define “crate.” In the regional fruit trade, a “crate” regularly means a 24-pound box. Noah offers evidence of that trade usage. Rachel objects that the writing is final and does not mention trade usage. Should the court admit the trade-usage evidence?
Answer A: No, because final writings under Article 2 can never be explained by trade usage.
Answer B: No, because usage of trade is admissible only if both parties personally discussed it.
Answer C: Yes, if the usage is established and reasonably consistent with the writing, because it helps explain the meaning of “crate.”
Answer D: Yes, but only to prove Rachel breached, not to interpret the agreement.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Article 2, usage of trade can explain or supplement a final writing if reasonably consistent with express terms. A regular trade meaning of “crate” helps define the quantity term.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the UCC expressly permits usage evidence to explain or supplement. B: B is wrong because trade usage can bind parties who are or should be aware of it; a personal discussion is not always required. D: D is wrong because the evidence is offered to interpret the agreement itself.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-202 / usage of trade
