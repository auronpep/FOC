Question: Eli agreed in a signed writing to sell 1,000 jars to Miriam, “delivery on June 1.” In the jar trade, a delivery term of “on June 1” regularly allows delivery during ordinary business hours on June 1, not necessarily at 8:00 a.m. Eli offers trade-usage evidence after Miriam argues that delivery at 3:00 p.m. was late. How should the court treat the trade usage?
Answer A: Exclude it because any usage about delivery time contradicts the written delivery date.
Answer B: Admit it if the usage merely qualifies the meaning of the delivery term and can reasonably be read with the express date.
Answer C: Exclude it unless the writing expressly mentions trade usage.
Answer D: Admit it only if Miriam proves she suffered no loss from afternoon delivery.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Usage evidence may explain or qualify an express term if reasonably consistent with it. A usage that defines the ordinary time of performance on the specified date does not necessarily negate the June 1 delivery term.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because qualifying a term is not always contradicting it. C: C is wrong because UCC course and usage evidence does not require express incorporation in the writing. D: D is wrong because lack of loss is a performance/remedy issue, not the meaning route.
