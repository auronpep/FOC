Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Falling below the standard of care
Question: A delivery company handbook required drivers to walk around the van and check all mirrors before backing up. A driver skipped the walkaround and backed into a pedestrian who had stepped behind the van. At trial, the pedestrian offers the handbook rule to prove breach. How should the jury treat the handbook rule?
Answer A: As conclusive proof of breach because the company chose a stricter rule for itself
Answer B: As irrelevant because private policies can never be considered in a negligence case
Answer C: As evidence of reasonable care that the jury may consider with all other circumstances
Answer D: As negligence per se because the handbook has the same effect as a safety statute
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The jury still decides whether the driver acted as a reasonable person under the circumstances.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Internal rules can be relevant, but they do not automatically become the legal standard. B: The policy can help show what precautions were feasible and expected within the operation. D: Negligence per se depends on a qualifying statute or regulation, not a private manual.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant's internal safety policy is admissible evidence of care but does not conclusively set the legal standard of reasonable care.
