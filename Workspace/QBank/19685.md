Question: A state statute requires every child to attend a public school and makes it unlawful for parents to send children to accredited private schools that satisfy state educational standards. The state says education is important and therefore public schooling may be made exclusive. What is the parents' strongest substantive due process argument?
Answer A: The state has absolute power over education, so parents have no constitutional objection
Answer B: Parents have a protected liberty interest in directing their children's education, including choosing accredited private schooling
Answer C: The statute is invalid only if it discriminates on the basis of race
Answer D: The statute is valid because parents can still help children with homework after school
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A total ban on private schooling directly interferes with recognized parental education rights.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: State education authority is broad but not absolute against parental liberty interests. C: A substantive due process parental-right claim can exist even without a racial classification. D: The statute still eliminates the protected choice of private schooling.
lb_rule_reference: The state may regulate education, but it may not unreasonably interfere with parents' fundamental liberty to direct the upbringing and education of their children, including by banning private schooling.
