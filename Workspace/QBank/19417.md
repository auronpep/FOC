


BARMATRIX Q#: 19417
Outline_code: 31010402
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Form and scope of examination
Question: At the trial of an action for breach of contract brought by the plaintiff against the defendant, the plaintiff's attorney called the plaintiff as a witness on her own behalf. The plaintiff was an expert in dog behavior. On direct examination, the plaintiff's attorney asked, “Do you own a German shepherd with a white forepaw?” The plaintiff said that she did. No other questions were asked on direct examination. On cross-examination, the defendant's attorney asked the plaintiff, “Your dog is generally known to be gentle, isn't that correct?” If the plaintiff's attorney objects to the question, the objection should be
Answer A: A: sustained, because the question went beyond the scope of cross-examination.
Answer B: B: sustained, because the question is leading.
Answer C: C: overruled, because the plaintiff is a hostile witness.
Answer D: D: overruled, because the plaintiff is an expert in dog behavior.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Most jurisdictions hold that cross-examination should be limited to inquiry into matters to which the witness testified on direct examination. Some jurisdictions grant broader latitude. All agree, however, that questions that go beyond the scope of cross-examination are improper. Thus, if the question went beyond the scope of cross-examination, the objection should be sustained.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B is incorrect because leading questions are permitted on cross-examination. A hostile witness is one who has demonstrated anger or hostility to the attorney questioning him or her. C is incorrect because there is no indication that the plaintiff has done so. Since the question is not whether the dog is gentle, but whether the dog is generally known to be gentle, the plaintiff's expertise (or lack of it) on the subject of dog behavior is irrelevant. D is therefore incorrect.
