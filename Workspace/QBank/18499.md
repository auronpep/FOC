Question: A prisoner requests a diet accommodation for religious reasons. Prison officials do not dispute security costs, but they deny the request because a chaplain says the requested diet is "not required by mainstream doctrine." The prisoner submits evidence that he sincerely holds the belief. What is the best constitutional objection to the denial?
Answer A: Officials may deny the request whenever most members of the faith do not follow the same diet
Answer B: Officials may evaluate sincerity but may not reject the claim simply because the practice is not orthodox or central
Answer C: Officials must grant every request that uses religious language
Answer D: Officials may deny the request only if another prisoner objects
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A Free Exercise inquiry can test sincerity, but it cannot turn on whether officials think the belief is doctrinally required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Courts and officials may not make centrality or orthodoxy the controlling test. C: Avoiding truth or centrality inquiries does not bar sincerity review or other lawful limitations. D: The issue presented is improper centrality analysis, not another prisoner's objection.
lb_rule_reference: Courts may assess sincerity in a Free Exercise claim but may not decide whether a religious belief is true, orthodox, or central to a faith.
