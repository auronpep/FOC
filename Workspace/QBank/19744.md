Question: Caleb's parcel was landlocked when it was severed from Miriam's parcel, creating an easement by necessity over Miriam's road. Years later, Jonah, who owned unrelated neighboring land, orally allowed Caleb to cross Jonah's field to reach a public road. Jonah said, “You may cross for now, but I can stop this whenever I want.” Miriam argues that Caleb's necessity easement ended immediately. Is Miriam correct?
Answer A: Yes, because any practical path over any land ends an easement by necessity.
Answer B: Yes, because an oral license over third-party land is the same as permanent legal access.
Answer C: No, but only because easements by necessity can never terminate once created.
Answer D: No, because a revocable oral license over unrelated land does not necessarily eliminate the strict legal necessity that supports the necessity easement.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement by necessity generally ends when the necessity ceases, but the alternative access must be legally sufficient and not merely revocable at another owner's will. Jonah gave only a revocable oral license, so Caleb may still lack permanent legal access.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a practical path is not enough if it is not legally secure. B: B is wrong because a revocable license is not equivalent to permanent access. C: C is wrong because necessity easements can terminate when necessity actually ceases. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Easement by necessity / alternate access must be legally sufficient
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating every new route as the end of necessity without checking the legal quality of the access.
