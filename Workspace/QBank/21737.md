


BARMATRIX Q#: 21737
Outline_code: 64010203
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Conversion
Question: While out hunting for wolves, the defendants saw a large furry animal that they believed was a wolf running through the trees. The defendants opened fire, killing the creature. When they got closer, they realized they had just shot a dog that was owned by a local farmer. The dog did in fact look exactly like a wolf. The farmer sued the defendants for damages, and the defendants countered that they shot the dog by mistake and were acting in good faith. What result?
Answer A: A: The defendants are not liable for the farmer's damages because their mistake was made in good faith.
Answer B: B: The defendants are not liable for the farmer's damages because their mistake was reasonable.
Answer C: C: The defendants are liable for the farmer's damages because it is unreasonable to mistake a dog for a wolf.
Answer D: D: The defendants are liable for the farmer's damages even though they may have been acting in good faith.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A defendant's conduct is not privileged solely by virtue of the fact that he or she made a mistake. Consequently, while the defendants may have made a good faith mistake in mistaking the farmer's dog for a wolf, they will still be liable for the farmer's damages.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A, B, and C are therefore incorrect.
