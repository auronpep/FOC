MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Discovery
Question: A bus driver was driving a bus full of passengers when he ran into a truck. An injured passenger sued the bus company and the trucking company in federal court for damages. The trucking company filed a petition seeking a court order for the bus driver to undergo physical and mental examinations, arguing that the bus driver was either visually impaired or not mentally fit to drive the bus. How should the court rule on the trucking company's petition?
Answer A: A: Grant the petition, because the trucking company alleged that the bus driver was either visually impaired or not mentally fit to drive the bus.
Answer B: B: Grant the petition, because the trucking company can use the bus driver's physical and mental health as a defense.
Answer C: C: Deny the petition, because physical and mental examinations would be a violation of the bus driver's right to privacy.
Answer D: D: Deny the petition, because the trucking company did not show good cause for the examinations.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Fed. R. Civ. P. 35 requires a party seeking a physical or mental examination of another party to show “good cause” and that the other party's physical or mental condition is “in controversy.” Here, the trucking company has only made conclusory statements regarding the bus driver's physical and mental health, and it hasn't shown that the bus driver's physical and mental health is in controversy. Schlagenhauf v. Holder, 379 U.S. 104 (1964). A and B are incorrect because the trucking company only made conclusory statements and did not show “good cause” for the examinations. C is incorrect, because the issue here is that the trucking company only made conclusory statements regarding the examinations.
