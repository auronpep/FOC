Question: A caterer has been discussing a possible summer contract with a company, but no agreement has been reached. A rival caterer offers a lower price and better menu, truthfully explains its services, and wins the contract. The first caterer sues for interference with prospective economic advantage. Is the claim likely to succeed?
Answer A: Yes, because the rival knew the first caterer expected the business
Answer B: Yes, because a lower price is improper interference as a matter of law
Answer C: No, because the rival used lawful competition rather than improper means
Answer D: No, because prospective business relationships are never protected by tort law
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Prospective-advantage claims require more than winning an opportunity fairly.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Knowledge of a prospect plus lawful competition is usually insufficient. B: Competing on price is generally lawful. D: They can be protected when improper interference is shown.
lb_rule_reference: Interference with prospective economic advantage generally requires improper means or purpose; merely winning business through lawful competition is not enough.
