Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Officers see Victor sell cocaine to an undercover officer. They immediately grab Victor, search his pockets, and find marked buy money. Seconds later, they announce that Victor is under arrest for drug distribution and handcuff him. Is the pocket search invalid merely because it preceded the formal arrest words?
Answer A: No, because probable cause existed before the search and the lawful arrest followed immediately
Answer B: Yes, because the words "under arrest" must always be spoken before any search
Answer C: Yes, because drug offenses never permit searches incident to arrest
Answer D: No, because police may search anyone they suspect before deciding whether to arrest
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The search and arrest were part of one continuous encounter, and officers had probable cause before the search. The incident-to-arrest rationale is satisfied.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The formal announcement need not precede the search if probable cause already existed and the arrest promptly followed. C: A lawful custodial arrest for a drug offense can support a search incident to arrest. D: Officers need probable cause and a prompt lawful arrest. Mere suspicion would not be enough.
lb_rule_reference: Search incident to arrest may apply when officers had probable cause before the search and a lawful custodial arrest followed quickly as part of the same encounter.
