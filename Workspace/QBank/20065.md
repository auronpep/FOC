Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Intentional infliction of emotional distress
Question: A debt collector learned that a debtor had recently survived a serious heart attack and had been warned to avoid severe stress. To pressure payment of a disputed bill, the collector called the debtor every night for two weeks, falsely saying an ambulance was waiting outside because "your heart will not survive what we do next." The debtor suffered severe anxiety requiring treatment. The debtor sues for intentional infliction of emotional distress. Is the collector likely liable?
Answer A: No, because demanding payment of a debt is a lawful purpose
Answer B: No, because words alone can never be extreme and outrageous
Answer C: Yes, because the collector repeatedly exploited a known vulnerability with extreme threats that caused severe distress
Answer D: Yes, but only because the bill was disputed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The collector knew of the debtor's fragile condition and used repeated false threats designed to cause severe stress. Severe distress requiring treatment followed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A lawful purpose does not privilege extreme methods that intentionally exploit a known vulnerability. B: Repeated targeted threats exploiting a known medical vulnerability can be extreme and outrageous. D: The decisive facts are the extreme method, knowledge of vulnerability, intent or recklessness, and severe distress.
lb_rule_reference: Repeated targeted conduct exploiting a known vulnerability can satisfy the extreme-and-outrageous element of IIED when severe distress results.
