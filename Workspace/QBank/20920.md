Question: Abraham owns Lot A. Sarah owns adjoining Lot B. Moses owns adjoining Lot C. A recorded deed states: “Lot B is subject to an easement in favor of the owner of Lot A to use the stone drive on Lot B to reach the public road.” Lot C is near the stone drive, but no instrument mentions Lot C. Which statement correctly identifies the parcel rights?
Answer A: Lot B is the dominant estate because the stone drive is physically located there.
Answer B: Lot C is also a dominant estate because it is adjacent to the stone drive.
Answer C: Lot A is the dominant estate, Lot B is the servient estate, and Lot C is neither benefited nor burdened by the stated easement.
Answer D: All three lots are both dominant and servient because all are near the same access route.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The dominant estate is the land benefited by an appurtenant easement, and the servient estate is the land burdened by it. The deed gives the owner of Lot A a right to use a drive on Lot B. Lot A benefits, Lot B is burdened, and Lot C is not included merely because it is nearby.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because physical location of the drive identifies the servient parcel, not the dominant parcel. B: B is wrong because adjacency alone does not make a parcel benefited by an easement. D: D is wrong because proximity to the route does not make every nearby parcel both benefited and burdened.
