MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Individual Rights
Outline: Freedom of speech and expression
Question: A federal agency's internal employee bulletin board is located in the agency break room. The agency posts a policy stating the board is "for official agency business announcements and approved employee benefit information only." An employee who is an organizer for a union posts a flyer announcing an off-campus organizing meeting. A supervisor removes the flyer and the employee argues the removal violates the First Amendment. Has the agency violated the employee's First Amendment rights by removing the flyer?
Answer A: Yes, because the bulletin board is government property and the government may not restrict speech on government-owned property without compelling justification
Answer B: Yes, because restricting union organizing speech violates the National Labor Relations Act and is therefore unconstitutional regardless of forum analysis
Answer C: No, because the bulletin board is a nonpublic forum and the restriction to official announcements is reasonable in light of the forum's purpose and is viewpoint-neutral
Answer D: No, because the employee had no reasonable expectation of using an employer's bulletin board for personal expression in the first place
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An internal agency bulletin board restricted to official business is a nonpublic forum — it has never been opened for general expressive activity and is not by tradition or policy a place for public discourse. In a nonpublic forum, restrictions are valid if reasonable in light of the forum's purpose and viewpoint-neutral. Limiting the board to official announcements is directly related to the forum's administrative purpose, and the policy applies equally to all non-official content regardless of viewpoint. The restriction is valid.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Not all government property is a public forum. An internal agency bulletin board designated for official announcements is a nonpublic forum. The government may impose reasonable, viewpoint-neutral restrictions on speech in nonpublic forums. Strict scrutiny does not apply. B: The question asks about the First Amendment, not statutory labor law. The constitutional forum analysis is controlling here. A statutory NLRA claim is a separate inquiry and does not determine whether the First Amendment permits the policy. On the bar exam, answer the question asked. D: The First Amendment inquiry does not turn on the speaker's reasonable expectation of using the property. It turns on forum classification and whether the restriction is reasonable and viewpoint-neutral. "Reasonable expectation" is Fourth Amendment language. The correct reason the removal is valid is the nonpublic forum doctrine, not the employee's expectations.
lb_rule_reference: In a nonpublic forum, the government may restrict access or regulate speech if the restriction is (1) reasonable in light of the purpose served by the forum and (2) viewpoint-neutral. The government need not allow all expressive activity in nonpublic forums.
