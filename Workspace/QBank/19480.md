Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: A jurisdiction recognizes voluntary and complete abandonment as a defense to attempt. Lena breaks a clinic's side window intending to steal medicine. Once inside, she sees a patient asleep in a chair, feels remorse, leaves all property untouched, calls the clinic to report the broken window, and abandons the theft permanently. She is charged with attempted burglary. What is the best argument for the defense?
Answer A: The defense may apply because Lena's abandonment was voluntary, complete, and not caused by fear of detection or a better opportunity
Answer B: The defense fails automatically because abandonment can never be a defense after any substantial step
Answer C: The defense applies whenever the target crime is not completed
Answer D: The defense fails because remorse is legally irrelevant to every attempt charge
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The jurisdiction recognizes the defense, and Lena permanently stopped because of conscience rather than increased risk or delay. Those facts support voluntary and complete abandonment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The facts specify a jurisdiction recognizing the defense. The question is whether the abandonment was voluntary and complete. C: Noncompletion alone is not enough. The abandonment must be voluntary and complete. D: In a jurisdiction recognizing abandonment, genuine remorse can help show voluntary and complete renunciation.
lb_rule_reference: In a jurisdiction recognizing abandonment as an attempt defense, the abandonment must be voluntary and complete, not motivated by fear, delay, or a decision to find a better opportunity.
