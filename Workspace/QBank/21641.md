Outline: Joinder of parties
Question: A pedestrian sues one driver for negligence arising from a two-car collision. The driver moves to dismiss, arguing that the other driver involved in the collision must be joined because the plaintiff could also have sued that driver. Is the other driver automatically a required party under Rule 19?
Answer A: Yes, because every possible joint tortfeasor must be joined in one action
Answer B: Yes, because Rule 19 requires joinder of every person mentioned in the complaint
Answer C: No, a potential joint tortfeasor is generally not required merely because the plaintiff could have sued that person
Answer D: No, because Rule 19 never applies in tort cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The plaintiff can often obtain complete relief from one tortfeasor, and the absent tortfeasor's possible responsibility does not automatically make that person required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Joint tortfeasors are generally not automatically required parties. B: Rule 19 asks about complete relief, absentee impairment, and inconsistent obligations. D: Rule 19 can apply in tort cases; this fact pattern just does not make the absentee automatically required.
lb_rule_reference: Joint tortfeasors are generally permissive parties, not required parties, because complete relief can usually be awarded among existing parties and contribution rights can be handled separately.
