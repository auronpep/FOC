Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Battery
Question: A camper threw a small rock toward a rival's tent intending to make the rival think he was about to be hit. The rock missed the rival but struck another camper standing nearby. The camper who was hit sues for battery. Can the thrown-rock intent support battery liability to the camper who was hit?
Answer A: Yes, because assault intent toward one person can transfer to battery against another person when contact results
Answer B: No, because the defendant did not intend to hit the actual plaintiff
Answer C: No, because transferred intent applies only to negligence
Answer D: Yes, but only if the rival was also physically touched
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Assault and battery are within the transferred-intent group, and intent can transfer across victims.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Transferred intent defeats this argument when the intended tort and completed tort are in the traditional group. C: Transferred intent is an intentional-tort doctrine, not a negligence shortcut. D: The completed battery against the actual plaintiff can stand without contact with the intended target.
lb_rule_reference: Intent can transfer between intended and actual victims and among traditional intentional torts such as assault and battery.
