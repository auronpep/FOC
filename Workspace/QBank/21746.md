Question: A seller and a buyer contracted for 1,000 lamps. The signed contract stated, “Any change in delivery inspection procedures must be in a signed writing.” Before delivery, the seller orally told the buyer that inspection could occur two days after delivery instead of at delivery, and the buyer arranged staffing based on that statement. The seller later insisted that the buyer lost all rights by not inspecting at delivery. Which statement is most accurate under Article 2?
Answer A: The seller's attempted oral change may operate as a waiver even if it does not satisfy the signed-writing modification clause.
Answer B: The seller's oral statement is ineffective for every purpose because the contract required signed modifications.
Answer C: The buyer has no rights because inspection at delivery was an express condition and did not occur.
Answer D: The seller's statement created a new contract only if supported by consideration.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Article 2, an attempted modification that fails a signed-writing clause can still operate as a waiver. If the buyer relied on the seller's oral permission to inspect later, the seller may be unable to insist on strict inspection-at-delivery procedure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because Article 2 distinguishes failed modification from waiver. C: C is wrong because the seller's waiver may prevent strict reliance on the inspection timing. D: D is wrong because Article 2 modifications do not need consideration, and the issue here is waiver.
