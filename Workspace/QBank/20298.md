Question: In a federal civil trial, Rachel testifies for the plaintiff. The defendant then calls Silas, who testifies that Rachel has a reputation in their community for being untruthful. In response, the plaintiff calls Lydia to testify that, in her opinion, Rachel is truthful. The defendant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a witness's truthful character may never be supported by opinion testimony.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because only Rachel herself may deny Silas's testimony.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Rachel's character for truthfulness has been attacked, so opinion evidence supporting truthfulness is permitted.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Lydia can describe specific truthful acts by Rachel.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Once Rachel's character for truthfulness has been attacked by reputation testimony for untruthfulness, Rule 608(a) permits support by reputation or opinion evidence concerning truthfulness. Lydia's opinion testimony is a permitted form.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because opinion testimony is allowed under Rule 608(a). B: B is wrong because rehabilitation may come from a character witness. D: D is wrong because specific truthful acts are not the proper form of Rule 608(a) support.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(a) / rehabilitation after truthfulness attack
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests proper rehabilitation sequencing after a truthfulness attack.
