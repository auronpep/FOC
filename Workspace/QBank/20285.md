Subtopic: Hearsay and Circumstances of Its Admissibility
Outline: Statements used for non-hearsay purposes
Question: After Lydia sent a demand letter about a highway collision, the trucking company's lawyer sent investigator Silas to record statements from eyewitnesses Mark and Timothy. The recordings contain only the witnesses' factual accounts and no lawyer comments or strategy. Before discovery, both Mark and Timothy die. Lydia moves to compel production of the recordings, showing that she cannot obtain the same information by deposition or interview. What is the most likely work-product ruling?
Answer A: The recordings are absolutely protected because they were collected by an investigator for the company's lawyer after a demand letter.
Answer B: The recordings are not work product at all because witness statements can never be trial-preparation materials.
Answer C: The recordings are attorney-client privileged because the investigator was working for counsel.
Answer D: The recordings are ordinary work product, but they may be ordered produced if Lydia shows substantial need and undue hardship, with any mental impressions protected.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Witness statements collected by a lawyer's investigator after a demand letter are trial-preparation materials. Because the recordings contain factual witness accounts rather than attorney mental impressions, they are ordinary work product. Ordinary work product can be discovered on a showing of substantial need and inability without undue hardship to obtain the substantial equivalent. The witnesses' deaths supply the hardship trigger.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it treats all work product as absolute. Ordinary factual work product has qualified protection. B: B is wrong because factual witness statements collected in anticipation of litigation can be work product. C: C is wrong because attorney-client privilege protects confidential lawyer-client communications, not every factual witness statement gathered for counsel.
lb_rule_reference: FRCP 26(b)(3): ordinary work product may be discovered on substantial need and undue hardship
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats work product as all-or-nothing and fails to distinguish ordinary factual material from attorney mental impressions.
