Question: In a federal criminal trial for arson, Solomon testifies that he saw Daniel carrying a gasoline can near the building. Daniel's lawyer asks Solomon whether he has a pending civil lawsuit against Daniel arising from the same fire. The prosecutor objects that civil claims are irrelevant in a criminal trial. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because civil litigation can never be relevant in a criminal trial.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because Solomon's pending claim against Daniel may show bias or a financial motive to testify against him.
Answer C: Sustain the objection unless Solomon's civil lawsuit has already resulted in a judgment.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Solomon's civil complaint is admissible for the truth of its allegations.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A pending civil claim may give a witness a financial or personal motive to testify against a party. The fact that the trial is criminal does not make the witness's interest irrelevant. The question is proper to show bias.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the trial type does not eliminate bias relevance. C: C is wrong because an actual judgment is not required; the pending claim itself can supply motive. D: D is wrong because the complaint need not be admitted for the truth of its allegations.
lb_rule_reference: Bias impeachment / pending civil claim
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests bias from pending litigation and rejects a civil/criminal relevance swap.
