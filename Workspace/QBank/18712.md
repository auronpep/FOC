Outline: Rule of completeness
Question: Hannah sues Eli for fraud. Hannah introduces a voicemail in which Eli says, “The land is dry.” Eli asks to introduce three other voicemails from the same week. One says, “I mean the hilltop is dry, not the low field.” The other two discuss unrelated repairs to a fence. Hannah objects to all three. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit all three voicemails because they were made during the same week and concern the property.
Answer B: Exclude all three voicemails because Eli is offering his own statements.
Answer C: Admit the voicemail clarifying what Eli meant by “dry,” if fairness requires it, but exclude the unrelated fence voicemails.
Answer D: Exclude the clarifying voicemail unless Eli proves that the low field was actually wet.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Completeness permits only those additional parts or related statements that fairness requires to be considered with the portion introduced. The clarification directly explains the meaning of “dry.” The fence voicemails do not correct the impression created by the introduced statement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because related timing or general subject matter is not enough. B: B is wrong because necessary completion is not barred merely because it is the adverse party's own statement. D: D is wrong because the threshold issue is whether the statement is needed to fairly understand the excerpt, not whether Eli first proves the underlying condition.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 106 / related statement only
