Question: Plaintiff alleges in her complaint that she is "a resident of Texas" and that defendant is "a resident of Oklahoma." She invokes diversity jurisdiction and seeks $200,000. The complaint says nothing about domicile, intent to remain, state of incorporation, or principal place of business. Is the allegation sufficient to plead individual citizenship for diversity?
Answer A: Yes, because residence and citizenship are identical for diversity
Answer B: Yes, because the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000
Answer C: No, because diversity requires citizenship based on domicile, not mere residence
Answer D: No, because diversity jurisdiction never applies to individuals
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The complaint must allege citizenship, not just residence, for individual parties.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Residence can be evidence of domicile, but it is not the same legal concept. B: Amount does not cure a missing citizenship allegation. D: Diversity commonly applies to individuals when citizenship and amount requirements are met.
lb_rule_reference: An individual is a citizen of the state where the individual is domiciled, meaning physical presence plus intent to remain indefinitely.
