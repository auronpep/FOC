Question: Rina sells a stolen tablet to Caleb for cash. Caleb knows the tablet is stolen and buys it for his own use. There is no evidence that Caleb agreed to help Rina steal more goods, resell stolen goods, recruit buyers, or conceal her theft operation. Prosecutors charge Rina and Caleb with conspiracy to traffic stolen property. What is Caleb's strongest argument against conspiracy liability?
Answer A: Knowing purchase of stolen property automatically proves a conspiracy with the seller
Answer B: The facts show only a buyer-seller transaction, not an agreement that Caleb join or further Rina's trafficking scheme
Answer C: Caleb cannot be guilty of any crime because he did not steal the tablet himself
Answer D: Caleb is guilty of conspiracy only because the tablet was electronic
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Caleb's knowing purchase may support a receiving-stolen-property charge, but the facts do not show a conspiratorial agreement to advance a broader criminal enterprise.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A conspiracy requires agreement to pursue a shared criminal objective. A completed buyer-seller transaction alone does not prove agreement to join a broader trafficking venture. C: Caleb may still be guilty of receiving stolen property. The issue is only whether conspiracy has been established. D: The type of property does not supply the missing agreement to a broader criminal objective.
lb_rule_reference: A mere buyer-seller relationship, without evidence of an agreement to further a broader criminal venture, does not automatically establish conspiracy.
