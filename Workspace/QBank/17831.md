Question: Lydia sues an accountant in federal court. To prove reasonable reliance, Lydia files one email from her lawyer stating, “The contract language protects you.” She then withholds other lawyer emails from the same week discussing the same contract language and the same reliance issue. Which ruling best states the waiver consequence?
Answer A: No waiver occurred because Lydia disclosed only one email.
Answer B: Every communication Lydia ever had with any lawyer is waived.
Answer C: The waiver may extend to undisclosed communications on the same subject if the disclosure was intentional and fairness requires considering them together.
Answer D: The waiver extends only to emails sent on the exact same day, regardless of subject matter.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under FRE 502(a), intentional disclosure in a federal proceeding can create subject-matter waiver only when the disclosed and undisclosed communications concern the same subject and fairness requires considering them together. Lydia used one privileged email as a sword on the reliance issue. The decisive axis is selective intentional disclosure plus fairness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is no-waiver absolutism. Intentional use of privileged material can waive more than the single document. B: B is wrong because This is subject-matter waiver absolutism. Rule 502 limits waiver to the same subject and fairness need. D: A is wrong because This is no-waiver absolutism. Intentional use of privileged material can waive more than the single document. B is wrong because This is subject-matter waiver absolutism. Rule 502 limits waiver to the same subject and fairness need. C is wrong because Correct answer D is wrong because This uses an arbitrary timing rule. Subject matter and fairness, not same-day timing, control.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 502(a) / subject-matter waiver
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is either overexpanding or underexpanding waiver instead of applying Rule 502's fairness and subject-matter limits.
