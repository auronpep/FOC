Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: A Nevada reporter published an online article accusing a Colorado restaurant of falsifying health records. The article discussed Colorado inspections, interviewed Colorado customers, bought targeted ads shown only to Colorado readers, and urged Colorado residents to boycott the restaurant. The restaurant sued the reporter in Colorado for defamation. Colorado's long-arm statute reaches the due-process limit. Is Colorado likely to have specific jurisdiction over the reporter?
Answer A: No, because the reporter wrote and uploaded the article from Nevada
Answer B: No, because online publications can never support personal jurisdiction
Answer C: Yes, because the reporter expressly aimed the alleged tort at Colorado and the claim arises from that conduct
Answer D: Yes, but only because the restaurant is incorporated in Colorado
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The article concerned a Colorado business, Colorado inspections, and Colorado customers, and it was advertised to Colorado readers. The forum was the focal point of conduct and harm.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Physical presence is not required when the defendant intentionally aims conduct at the forum. B: This article was not passive or untargeted; it focused on Colorado and targeted Colorado readers. D: The plaintiff's forum status does not itself create PJ over the reporter. The decisive facts are the reporter's forum-directed conduct.
lb_rule_reference: An intentional tort aimed at a forum audience about forum activities can create purposeful direction for claims arising from that conduct.
