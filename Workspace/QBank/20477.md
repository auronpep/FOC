Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A landowner sued a surveyor in federal court for state-law malpractice. The landowner alleges that the surveyor negligently interpreted a federal land-record regulation when preparing a boundary report. The federal regulation is relevant, but the claim is created by state malpractice law and the parties mainly dispute the surveyor's professional care. What is the best federal-question analysis?
Answer A: Federal-question jurisdiction automatically exists because a federal regulation appears in the facts
Answer B: Federal-question jurisdiction automatically exists because malpractice is important to the plaintiff
Answer C: Jurisdiction is doubtful unless the federal issue is necessarily raised, actually disputed, substantial in the federal-system sense, and compatible with federal-state balance
Answer D: Jurisdiction exists only if the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The embedded-issue path is narrow. A state malpractice claim with a federal regulation in the background does not automatically satisfy it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A federal ingredient is not enough by itself. B: Substantiality asks about importance to the federal system, not merely to the litigants. D: Amount is a diversity requirement, not an embedded federal-question requirement.
lb_rule_reference: Embedded federal-question jurisdiction is narrow and requires a necessarily raised, actually disputed, substantial federal issue that a federal court may hear without disrupting the federal-state balance.
