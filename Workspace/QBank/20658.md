Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Conspiracy
Question: Soren joins a conspiracy to steal rare books. Before any theft, he sends a message to every conspirator saying he is withdrawing, retrieves the van he had supplied, and gives police the planned date. The remaining conspirators later steal the books using a different van. Soren is charged with conspiracy and with the later theft under Pinkerton. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Effective withdrawal may cut off liability for the later theft, but it does not automatically erase the earlier conspiracy
Answer B: Withdrawal erases both the conspiracy and every later crime
Answer C: Withdrawal has no effect on any charge once a conspiracy exists
Answer D: Soren avoids all liability only because rare books are unique property
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Soren took affirmative steps to withdraw and neutralize his aid before the theft, which may bar Pinkerton liability for later substantive crimes. But joining the conspiracy already completed the conspiracy offense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Withdrawal has limited consequences. It does not undo the completed agreement offense. C: That is true for the conspiracy charge, but withdrawal can affect later substantive crimes and limitations. D: The withdrawal consequences do not depend on the type of property targeted.
lb_rule_reference: A conspirator who effectively withdraws may avoid liability for later substantive crimes, but withdrawal generally does not erase liability for the conspiracy already joined.
