Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Lena borrowed her aunt's car for the weekend with permission and carried the keys. She was the only occupant when police stopped the car for no valid reason and searched the center console without consent or any warrant exception. They found counterfeit credit cards. Lena moves to suppress the cards. What is the best standing analysis?
Answer A: Lena lacks standing because she was not the car's titled owner
Answer B: Lena has standing because she lawfully possessed and controlled the borrowed car with the owner's permission
Answer C: Lena has standing only if the counterfeit cards were legally hers
Answer D: Lena lacks standing because vehicles never carry reasonable expectations of privacy
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Lena had permission, keys, and sole control of the car. Those facts support a personal privacy interest in the searched console, allowing her to challenge the unlawful search and seizure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Lawful possession, control, and permission to use the car can create a reasonable expectation of privacy. Title ownership is not required. C: The standing question asks whether Lena had a protected interest in the searched vehicle area, not lawful ownership of contraband. D: Reduced protection is not no protection. People can have Fourth Amendment interests in vehicles they lawfully possess.
lb_rule_reference: Lawful possession and control can create a reasonable expectation of privacy in a vehicle; title ownership is not the only path to standing.
