Question: A bakery used a dough mixer with exposed gears. Most small bakeries in the area used the same model without a guard. A removable guard cost little, took minutes to install, and would not slow production. An employee's sleeve was caught in the gears. What is the strongest negligence analysis?
Answer A: The bakery cannot be negligent if it followed the common practice of similar bakeries
Answer B: The bakery may be negligent if a reasonable operator would have installed the inexpensive guard despite the custom
Answer C: The bakery is negligent per se because the guard was inexpensive
Answer D: The employee's injury proves breach without any need to evaluate the bakery's precautions
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Reasonable care can require more than industry custom when the burden is low and the risk is serious.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Custom is relevant but not conclusive. C: Negligence per se requires violation of a qualifying legal rule, not just a low-cost precaution. D: An accident alone does not automatically prove breach.
lb_rule_reference: Compliance with industry custom is evidence of due care, but a jury may still find breach when a cheap, obvious precaution would have reduced a serious risk.
