Outline: Scope
Question: Rachel orally promised Mary, her neighbor, “I will never build above one story so your kitchen will keep its sunlight.” No deed, recorded covenant, statute, or signed writing reflected the promise. Mary made no expenditures in reliance on the promise. Rachel later began building a lawful two-story home. What is Mary's best claim to a negative easement preventing the construction?
Answer A: Mary has an express negative easement because Rachel used the word “never.”
Answer B: Mary likely has no enforceable negative easement because the alleged no-build restriction lacks the required recognized source, such as a writing, covenant, statute, or estoppel facts.
Answer C: Mary has a profit because sunlight is a natural resource coming from Rachel's land.
Answer D: Mary has a prescriptive easement because she received sunlight before Rachel built.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A negative building restriction ordinarily must rest on a recognized enforceable source, such as a written easement, covenant, statute, or estoppel-producing reliance. The facts give only an oral no-build promise and no reliance expenditure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because no magic word in an oral statement creates a real-property interest. C: C is wrong because sunlight is not a resource removed from Rachel's land under profit doctrine. D: D is wrong because passively receiving sunlight is not adverse use of Rachel's land.
lb_rule_reference: Negative easement source / writing requirement
