Question: In a federal negligence trial, Jacob claims Moses ran a stop sign. Jacob's lawyer asks a witness, “You know Moses is a careless person generally, don't you?” Jacob offers the answer to prove Moses probably failed to stop on this occasion. Moses objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because carelessness is relevant in every negligence case.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the question seeks propensity character evidence to prove conduct on the occasion.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because character may always be proved by reputation.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if the witness lacks personal knowledge of the collision.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The question seeks general character for carelessness to prove Moses acted carelessly on the occasion in suit. That is an improper propensity route in this civil negligence posture.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because ordinary relevance does not override the character-propensity bar. C: C is wrong because reputation form does not make an impermissible propensity purpose proper. D: D is wrong because even a witness with character knowledge could not use that testimony for this purpose.
