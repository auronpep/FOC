Question: A merchant posted a reward: “$5,000 to anyone who identifies the thief and provides evidence leading to both arrest and conviction.” Esther gave the police the thief's name, and the thief was arrested. The prosecutor later dismissed the charges for lack of evidence. Esther demanded the reward. What is the most accurate result?
Answer A: Esther earns the reward because identifying the thief was substantial performance.
Answer B: Esther does not earn the reward because the express reward conditions required both arrest and conviction.
Answer C: Esther earns half the reward because one of the two conditions occurred.
Answer D: Esther earns the reward unless the merchant proves the police acted unreasonably.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The reward expressly required evidence leading to both arrest and conviction. Arrest alone did not satisfy all stated conditions, so the duty to pay the reward did not mature.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because substantial performance does not replace express reward conditions. C: C is wrong because the reward did not allocate partial payment for partial occurrence. D: D is wrong because the merchant's payment duty depends on the stated conditions, not police reasonableness alone.
