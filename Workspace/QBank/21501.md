Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Peter signed and delivered to John a writing stating, “I convey my warehouse on Capernaum Street to John.” The writing accurately described the warehouse, and John accepted. The writing did not contain a wax seal, the word “seal,” or any printed seal mark. No statute in the question requires a seal. Who owns the warehouse?
Answer A: Peter, because a seal is always required for a deed.
Answer B: John, because a seal is not required for deed validity under the modern default stated by these facts.
Answer C: Peter, because the lack of a seal makes the writing only a revocable license.
Answer D: John only if the deed is rewritten on a printed form containing a seal.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is the required conveyance formalities under the applicable default. The writing identified the parties and property, used present transfer words, was signed by the grantor, delivered, and accepted. Because no statute in the question requires a seal, the absence of a seal does not defeat title.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the modern default does not make a seal universally required. C: C is wrong because the lack of a seal does not convert an otherwise valid deed into a license. D: D is wrong because a printed form is not required.
lb_rule_reference: Seal not required under modern default
