Question: Mason shot Priya in the leg, severing an artery. At the hospital, doctors said a blood transfusion would likely save her. Priya refused the transfusion for religious reasons and died from blood loss. Mason was charged with homicide. Is Mason the proximate cause of Priya's death?
Answer A: No, because Priya's refusal was a voluntary intervening act
Answer B: No, because the wound was not certain to be fatal if treated
Answer C: Yes, because Mason caused the dangerous wound and Priya's treatment refusal did not supersede it
Answer D: Yes, but only if Mason knew Priya's religious beliefs before shooting
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Mason's shot was the actual cause of the arterial bleeding. Priya's refusal of treatment did not create a new, unrelated risk; it affected the response to Mason's wound. Proximate causation remains.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A victim's refusal of treatment ordinarily does not break causation. Mason created the mortal risk, and the law generally requires him to take the victim as found. B: Causation does not require that the wound be untreatable. The shot caused the bleeding that killed Priya. D: The defendant need not foresee the victim's precise medical or religious response. The causal chain remains because the original wound caused the death.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant generally takes the victim as found; a victim's refusal of treatment for personal or religious reasons ordinarily does not supersede the defendant's causal responsibility.
