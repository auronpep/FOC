Question: A city clerk is hired under a written policy stating that all clerks serve at will and may be dismissed without cause. After a supervisor decides the clerk is not a good fit, the city fires him without a hearing. The clerk claims procedural due process required a pretermination hearing. What is the city's strongest argument?
Answer A: The clerk lacks a property interest in continued employment because the job was at will
Answer B: The clerk wins because government employment always creates a property interest
Answer C: The clerk wins because losing any job is a deprivation of liberty
Answer D: The clerk loses only if the city proves the firing was wise policy
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Without a rule limiting removal, the clerk does not have a legitimate entitlement to keep the job.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: State action alone does not create the protected property interest. C: Ordinary at-will termination is not automatically a liberty deprivation. D: The threshold issue is entitlement, not whether the decision was wise.
lb_rule_reference: An at-will public employee generally lacks a property interest in continued employment unless law, contract, or rules create a legitimate entitlement.
