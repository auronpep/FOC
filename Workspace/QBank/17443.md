Outline: Class actions
Question: Two hundred employees sue their employer over a single biometric timeclock policy. The named plaintiff used the timeclock, claims the same statutory injury as the group, and asks the court to certify a class. The employer argues that a class cannot be certified until the court analyzes whether the named plaintiff and counsel can fairly protect absent members. Which statement best describes the certification inquiry?
Answer A: Numerosity and commonality are enough because the challenged policy affected hundreds of employees
Answer B: Adequacy is unnecessary if the named plaintiff personally suffered the same injury as the class
Answer C: The court must find numerosity, commonality, typicality, and adequacy, and the action must also fit within Rule 23(b)
Answer D: Certification is automatic if the employer used one policy because commonality is the main Rule 23 requirement
Correct Answer: C
lb_rule_reference: A class action must satisfy all four Rule 23(a) prerequisites: numerosity, commonality, typicality, and adequacy of representation, plus one Rule 23(b) category.
