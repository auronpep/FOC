Outline: Motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law
Question: At trial, the defendant never moves for JMOL before the case is submitted to the jury. The jury returns a verdict for the plaintiff. The next day, the defendant files a renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law, arguing the plaintiff lacked evidence of causation. What is the plaintiff's strongest procedural response?
Answer A: The motion is proper because any losing party may file renewed JMOL after a verdict
Answer B: The motion is improper because the defendant did not make a Rule 50(a) motion before the case went to the jury
Answer C: The motion is proper only if the defendant also files a new complaint
Answer D: The motion is improper because no post-trial motion may ever challenge a jury verdict
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 50(b) renews a pre-submission sufficiency motion; it is not a first opportunity to raise JMOL after losing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Renewed JMOL depends on a prior Rule 50(a) motion that preserved the issue before submission. C: A post-verdict motion in the same action does not require a new complaint. D: Properly preserved renewed JMOL and new-trial motions can challenge verdicts.
lb_rule_reference: A party generally may make a renewed Rule 50(b) motion only on grounds raised in a Rule 50(a) motion before the case was submitted to the jury.
