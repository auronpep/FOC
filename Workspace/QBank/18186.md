Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Mary owned a townhouse. She signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey the townhouse to my daughter Lydia.” Mary had only one daughter, whose full legal name was Lydia Miriam. No other Lydia was connected with Mary or the townhouse. Lydia Miriam accepted the deed. Who owns the townhouse?
Answer A: Mary, because the deed did not state Lydia Miriam's full legal name.
Answer B: Mary, until a court reforms the deed to add the full legal name.
Answer C: Miriam alone, because the deed omitted the second name from the grantee line.
Answer D: Lydia Miriam, because the deed identifies the grantee with reasonable certainty.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantee identity. Exact full legal names are not required when the instrument and surrounding identifying facts make the grantee reasonably certain. “My daughter Lydia” identifies Lydia Miriam, so title passes to her.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a full legal name is not mandatory when identity is clear. B: B is wrong because reformation is unnecessary when the deed already identifies the grantee. C: C is wrong because Miriam is part of Lydia Miriam's name, not a separate grantee.
lb_rule_reference: Exact legal name not required / grantee identity
