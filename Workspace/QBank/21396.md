Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: An employee sues her employer in federal court under a federal discrimination statute and also asserts a state-law emotional distress claim based on the same termination meeting and the same supervisor statements. The state claim seeks $20,000, and both parties are citizens of the same state. May the court hear the state-law claim?
Answer A: Yes, because it shares a common nucleus of operative fact with the federal claim
Answer B: No, because the state claim does not exceed $75,000
Answer C: No, because the parties are nondiverse
Answer D: Yes, but only if the employer consents to federal jurisdiction
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal claim supplies original jurisdiction, and Section 1367(a) reaches the related state claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Supplemental jurisdiction has no independent amount-in-controversy threshold. C: Diversity is unnecessary when the state claim is supplemental to a federal-question anchor. D: Subject matter jurisdiction is statutory and cannot be supplied or withheld by consent.
lb_rule_reference: Section 1367(a) grants supplemental jurisdiction over claims sharing a common nucleus of operative fact with a claim within original jurisdiction.
