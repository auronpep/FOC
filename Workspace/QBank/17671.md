Subtopic: Inchoate Crimes
Outline: Attempt
Question: For years, Adrian has violently abused Morgan. One afternoon, Adrian tells Morgan, "When I get home from work tomorrow, you will regret everything." Adrian then leaves for the airport for an overnight business trip. Several hours later, while Adrian is hundreds of miles away, Morgan destroys Adrian's car brakes so he will crash when he returns. If Morgan is charged with attempted murder, what is the best analysis of self-defense?
Answer A: Self-defense succeeds because Adrian's abuse history made Morgan's fear reasonable
Answer B: Self-defense succeeds because the threatened harm involved serious bodily injury
Answer C: Self-defense likely fails because the threat was not imminent when Morgan acted
Answer D: Self-defense fails only because Morgan used a mechanical device
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Morgan may reasonably fear Adrian generally, but self-defense requires immediate necessity. Adrian was away for the night, and Morgan set up a later injury rather than responding to an imminent attack. The doctrine does not justify preemptive deadly force against future harm.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Abuse history can help explain reasonableness, but it does not erase the requirement of imminent danger. Adrian was away and threatened future harm. B: The threat level is not enough. The serious harm also must be imminent when defensive force is used. D: The central defect is lack of imminence. The mechanical method reinforces the premeditated nature but is not the only reason self-defense fails.
lb_rule_reference: Abuse history may inform reasonableness, but self-defense still requires an imminent threat when force is used
