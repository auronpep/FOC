Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Cause in fact (actual cause)
Question: An apartment tenant developed lung disease after exposure to old industrial paint. Five manufacturers sold paint in the city during the relevant period, but their products had different chemical formulas, warnings, and toxicity levels. The tenant cannot identify which paint was used in the apartment. Is market-share causation likely available?
Answer A: Yes, because any product-identification problem is solved by market share
Answer B: Yes, because all paint is treated as legally identical once it is sold in the same city
Answer C: No, because the products were not fungible and did not present the same risk profile
Answer D: No, because causation can never be shown in toxic exposure cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Market-share causation is limited and is a poor fit for materially different products.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Market-share liability is not a general substitute for product identification. B: The materially different formulations matter. D: Toxic exposure cases can satisfy causation with appropriate proof.
lb_rule_reference: Market-share causation is exceptional and is generally inappropriate for nonfungible products with materially different risks or formulations.
