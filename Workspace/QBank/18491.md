Question: Deborah contracted with a merchant seller to buy two medical scanners for $200,000. The seller also agreed to provide one afternoon of training on how to use the scanners. The written price was almost entirely for the scanners. When a formation dispute arose, Deborah argued that common law controlled because training services were included. Which law most likely controls the formation issue?
Answer A: Common law, because the agreement includes any service component.
Answer B: Common law, because medical equipment is not movable at the time of sale.
Answer C: Common law, because Article 2 governs only transactions with no human assistance of any kind.
Answer D: Article 2, because the predominant purpose of the transaction is the sale of movable goods and the training is incidental.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The source-law classification turns on the dominant character of the mixed transaction. The medical scanners are movable goods and the price is overwhelmingly for the scanners. The training is incidental. Article 2 most likely controls the formation issue.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an incidental service component does not automatically displace Article 2. B: B is wrong because scanners are movable goods. C: C is wrong because Article 2 can apply even when a goods sale includes incidental service or assistance.
lb_rule_reference: UCC_goods
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats any service component as enough to displace Article 2.
