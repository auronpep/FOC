MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Nuisance
Question: A research lab in a residential area used a rare toxic gas in an outdoor test. Even with careful equipment, a small shift in wind could move the gas beyond the lab. During one test, gas drifted onto a neighbor's property, killing garden plants and forcing the neighbor to leave home overnight because of the fumes. The lab had used industry-best precautions and complied with all permits. Is the neighbor likely to prevail on a strict liability claim against the lab for the gas drift?
Answer A: No, because the lab used industry-best precautions and complied with all permits
Answer B: No, because odor and temporary evacuation are nuisance harms, not strict-liability harms
Answer C: Yes, because the toxic-gas drift caused the kind of property and occupancy harm that made the activity abnormally dangerous
Answer D: Yes, because strict liability makes the lab responsible for every injury occurring during the test
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Outdoor use of rare toxic gas in a residential area creates a high, uncommon risk not eliminated by care. Plant damage and evacuation from drifting gas fall within that abnormal risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Reasonable care does not defeat strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity when the harm arises from the abnormal risk. B: The same facts can support nuisance-like damages and strict liability if the activity is abnormally dangerous and the injury comes from the abnormal toxic-gas risk. D: Strict liability is not boundless. It applies only to harms within the abnormal risk, not every injury temporally connected to the activity.
lb_rule_reference: Strict liability for an abnormally dangerous activity reaches only harms that arise from the very abnormal risk that makes the activity subject to strict liability.
