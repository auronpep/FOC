Question: Ruth hired Jacob to paint her house. The signed writing stated the price, paint color, and completion date. It did not contain a merger clause and did not address cleanup. During negotiations, Jacob orally promised to remove all empty paint cans and drop cloths after finishing. After Jacob leaves the materials on Ruth's porch, Ruth offers the oral cleanup promise. Assuming the court finds the writing final as to the included terms but not complete and exclusive, how should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because the cleanup promise is a consistent additional term that supplements a partial integration.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because any prior oral promise is barred once a writing is signed.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence because cleanup relates to the same general transaction as the painting.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if the written agreement is first found void.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A final writing may be only a partial integration. A consistent additional term may supplement a partial integration if it does not contradict the written terms. The oral cleanup promise does not negate the price, color, or completion date.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because final writing does not automatically mean complete and exclusive writing. C: C is wrong because relatedness to the same transaction is not enough to bar a consistent additional term in a partial integration. D: D is wrong because the evidence supplements the agreement; it does not require voiding the writing.
lb_rule_reference: Partial integration / consistent additional term
lb_student_diagnostic: Student assumes every final writing is complete and exclusive.
