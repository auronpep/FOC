Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Moses sues Solomon for breach of a written lease. The only disputed issue is whether Solomon paid the final month's rent. Solomon offers evidence that he regularly donates food to poor families in his neighborhood. Moses objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the evidence because Solomon's charitable conduct does not make payment of the final month's rent more or less probable.
Answer B: Admit the evidence because charitable people are generally less likely to breach contracts.
Answer C: Admit the evidence because any favorable fact about a party is relevant in a civil case.
Answer D: Exclude the evidence only if Moses proves that Solomon's donations were made after the lawsuit began.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Evidence is relevant only if it has some tendency to prove a fact of consequence. Solomon's charitable donations do not logically bear on whether he paid a particular month's rent. The evidence is a moral-halo distraction, not a probative link to the disputed fact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because it uses impermissible character-style reasoning and still lacks a focused link to rent payment. C: C is wrong because relevance requires connection to a consequential fact, not merely a favorable impression. D: D is wrong because the timing of the donations does not fix the missing connection to the rent-payment dispute.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Confusing favorable background evidence with a consequential proof link.
