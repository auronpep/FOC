Outline_code: 73020700
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Rape
Question: The defendant met a girl in a bar where both were drinking. Because the defendant was too drunk to drive, the girl offered him a ride home. In the girl's car, the girl consented to intercourse. A statute provided that it was unlawful to engage in sexual intercourse with a female under the age of 18 years, and the girl was 17 years old. If the defendant believed that the girl was over the age of 18 years, is he guilty of statutory rape?
Answer A: No, because he believed the girl to be over the age of 18 years.
Answer B: No, if the reasonable person who was not intoxicated would have believed the girl to be over the age of 18 years.
Answer C: Yes, unless the girl assured him that she was over the age of 18 years.
Answer D: Yes, but only if the defendant realized that he was having intercourse.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Some courts say that statutory rape is a strict liability crime, requiring no intent at all; other courts say that it is a general intent crime requiring only the intent to have sexual intercourse. Under either view, this means that a defendant who has sexual intercourse with a female who is too young to consent is guilty if he was aware that he was engaging in intercourse. This is so even though he did not know that she was underage, even though the reasonable person would not have known it, and even though she told him that she was over the age of consent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A, B, and C are therefore incorrect.
