Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Jacob hired Leah to design a trade-show display for Jacob's new product launch. Before Leah repudiated, Jacob reasonably spent $9,000 on site measurements and display renderings tied to Leah's design. After receiving Leah's unequivocal repudiation, Jacob nevertheless ordered $14,000 of custom packaging usable only with Leah's design. Jacob cannot prove any launch profits with reasonable certainty. What is Jacob's reliance-damages recovery?
Answer A: $23,000, because Jacob spent both amounts because of the project.
Answer B: $9,000, because the later $14,000 expenditure was avoidable after repudiation.
Answer C: $14,000, because the custom packaging was the final reliance expense.
Answer D: Nothing, because Jacob failed to mitigate after repudiation.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is reliance under common law. The breach posture is repudiation, so Jacob may recover reasonable expenditures caused by the contract before repudiation but not avoidable expenditures made after clear notice of breach. The formula is $9,000 pre-repudiation reliance and $0 for the later avoidable packaging order. The limiting gate is mitigation, and the result scope is limited rather than forfeited. The answer does not award speculative launch profits.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it includes post-repudiation costs that should have been avoided, a WAL-REM-010 error. C: C is wrong because it awards only the avoidable post-repudiation amount and omits the recoverable pre-breach reliance. D: D is wrong because mitigation failure reduces avoidable losses; it does not eliminate all damages under WAL-REM-009.
lb_rule_reference: Common law reliance damages — exclude avoidable expenditures made after repudiation
