Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Paul prepared a deed stating that he conveyed his cottage to Timothy. The deed named Paul and Timothy, described the cottage, and used present words of transfer. Paul forgot to sign or mark the deed and did not direct anyone to sign for him. James and John signed the deed as witnesses, and a notary acknowledged their signatures. Paul delivered the paper to Timothy, who accepted. Who owns the cottage?
Answer A: Timothy, because two witnesses signed the deed.
Answer B: Timothy, because the notary acknowledgment supplied the missing signature.
Answer C: Timothy, because Paul delivered the paper with present intent.
Answer D: Paul, because the deed lacked Paul's signature or authorized authentication.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is grantor signature or authorized authentication. The deed had parties, description, present words, and delivery, but Paul did not sign, mark, or direct another person to sign for him. Witness and notary signatures do not substitute for the grantor's signature, so title remains with Paul.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because witness signatures do not replace the grantor's signature. B: B is wrong because acknowledgment proves or certifies a signature; it does not create a missing grantor signature. C: C is wrong because delivery intent cannot cure the missing grantor-authentication gate.
lb_rule_reference: Grantor signature cannot be replaced by witnesses
