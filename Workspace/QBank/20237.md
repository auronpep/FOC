Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Sarah died owning no land. At Sarah's death, her only heirs were Isaac and Rebekah. Later, Jacob signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey my field to the heirs of Sarah.” Isaac and Rebekah accepted the deed. Jacob later argued that the word “heirs” made the deed invalid. Who owns the field?
Answer A: Jacob, because the word “heirs” can never identify grantees in a deed.
Answer B: Jacob, because Sarah herself was dead when the deed was delivered.
Answer C: Sarah's estate, because a deed to heirs must pass through the deceased person's estate.
Answer D: Isaac and Rebekah, because Sarah's heirs were ascertainable when the deed was delivered.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is whether the grantees are reasonably ascertainable. Although a living person has no heirs, Sarah was already dead, so her heirs could be identified as Isaac and Rebekah. The deed uses the heirs as grantees by description, and title passes to Isaac and Rebekah.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because “heirs” can identify a class when the class is fixed and ascertainable. B: B is wrong because the deed is not attempting to convey to Sarah; it conveys to her heirs. C: C is wrong because Sarah's estate is not the grantee when the deed names the heirs as purchasers.
