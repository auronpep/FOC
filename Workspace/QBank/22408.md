


BARMATRIX Q#: 22408
Outline_code: 75070000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: The police believed a woman was selling drugs from her hotel room. When the woman left the room to go out, the manager gave police officers a key and showed them into the woman's room. Inside, the officers saw a large amount of cocaine sitting in the middle of the hotel bed in clear plastic bags. When the woman returned, she was placed under arrest. At trial, the woman moved to have the cocaine ruled inadmissible. Should the court admit the cocaine into evidence?
Answer A: A: No, because the woman's reasonable expectation of privacy was violated.
Answer B: B: No, because the arrest was unlawful.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the manager let the officers into the hotel room.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the cocaine was in plain view.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Because a person has a reasonable expectation of privacy in a hotel room the police need a warrant to search it. Because the police didn't have a warrant, the search was unreasonable, and the cocaine should be excluded.B is incorrect because the evidence should be suppressed based on the violation of the woman's privacy rights, not because of anything to do with the arrest. C is incorrect because the manager could not consent to a search of the hotel room. D is incorrect because the officers were unlawfully in the hotel room when they saw the cocaine in plain view.
