Question: Moses owned Lot 1 and Lot 2. By recorded deed, Moses granted “to Ruth, as owner of Lot 1, and to Ruth's successors as owners of Lot 1, a permanent right to cross the lane on Lot 2 to reach the public road.” Ruth later sold Lot 1 to Boaz. Boaz's deed did not repeat the crossing language. What is the best classification of the crossing right?
Answer A: A revocable license because the grant first names Ruth.
Answer B: An easement in gross because every easement naming a person is personal.
Answer C: An easement appurtenant because the right benefits Lot 1 and passes with the dominant parcel.
Answer D: A profit because crossing Lot 2 increases the value of Lot 1.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement appurtenant benefits land rather than merely a person. The grant ties the crossing right to Ruth as owner of Lot 1 and to successors as owners of Lot 1. Lot 1 is the dominant parcel, so the right is appurtenant and generally passes with Lot 1.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a recorded deed granting a permanent right is not merely revocable permission. B: B is wrong because naming a person does not make the right in gross when the grant identifies benefited land. D: D is wrong because a profit is a right to remove resources from land, not a right to cross.
lb_rule_reference: Easement appurtenant / dominant parcel
