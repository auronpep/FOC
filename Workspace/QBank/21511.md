Question: Daniel is on trial for burglary. Before the arresting officer testifies, the prosecutor says the officer will repeat Daniel's oral confession. Daniel objects that the confession was obtained during custodial interrogation without warnings and asks for a hearing before the jury hears the confession. What is Daniel's best procedural objection?
Answer A: The court must conduct a hearing on the confession's admissibility so that the jury cannot hear it.
Answer B: The jury must hear the officer's testimony and decide whether the confession was lawfully obtained.
Answer C: The confession is inadmissible unless it was recorded in writing and signed by Daniel.
Answer D: Daniel must wait until after the officer answers and then move to strike only the nonresponsive part.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A hearing on the admissibility of a confession must be conducted so that the jury cannot hear it. The objection concerns the court's preliminary admissibility role before the jury hears the confession.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the jury does not decide the threshold admissibility of the confession. C: C is wrong because an oral confession is not automatically inadmissible merely because it was not written or signed. D: D is wrong because Daniel is objecting before the testimony; the issue is a hearing, not cleanup of a volunteered answer.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(c) / confession hearing outside jury
