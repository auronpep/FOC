Question: A neighborhood newsletter stated that a contractor's license was suspended in March for unsafe electrical work. The license was actually suspended in April for the same unsafe electrical work. The contractor sued for defamation based only on the wrong month. What is the best defense?
Answer A: Substantial truth, because the sting of the statement was true despite the wrong month
Answer B: No defense, because any factual mistake defeats truth
Answer C: Absolute privilege, because all neighborhood newsletters are protected
Answer D: Opinion, because license status is subjective
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The defamatory sting was license suspension for unsafe work, and that was true.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Substantial truth tolerates minor inaccuracies that do not change the gist. C: There is no automatic absolute privilege for a newsletter. D: License suspension is factual and verifiable; the defense is substantial truth.
lb_rule_reference: Minor inaccuracies do not create defamation liability when the gist or sting of the statement is substantially true.
