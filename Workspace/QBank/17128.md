Question: David held a recorded easement to drive across Esther's lot to reach his parcel. Esther began parking construction equipment across the only usable portion of the easement route every weekday. David could sometimes pass on weekends, but weekday access to his parcel was effectively blocked. What is David's best remedy?
Answer A: No remedy, because Esther owns the land beneath the easement and may use it however she wishes.
Answer B: An injunction preventing unreasonable interference with David's access easement.
Answer C: Automatic fee title to the easement strip by adverse possession.
Answer D: Termination of the easement because Esther's obstruction shows that the easement is inconvenient.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Although the servient owner retains ownership and may make reasonable use of the land, the servient owner may not unreasonably interfere with the easement holder's use. Blocking the only usable route on weekdays materially interferes with access, supporting injunctive relief.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because servient ownership is limited by the easement. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because David seeks enforcement of a use right, not title. D: D is wrong because obstruction by the servient owner does not terminate the easement.
lb_rule_reference: Servient interference / injunction
lb_student_diagnostic: The repair signal is recognizing that servient ownership remains, but it is burdened by the easement.
