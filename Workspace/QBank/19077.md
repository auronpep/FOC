Question: For 12 years, Sarah openly crossed a dirt path over Abraham's vacant parcel to reach her garden lot. The jurisdiction's prescriptive period is 10 years. Sarah never asked Abraham for permission, and Abraham saw the use several times but did nothing to stop it. Sarah now seeks the right to continue using the path, but she does not claim ownership of the land under the path. Which is the best result?
Answer A: Sarah has a prescriptive easement because her use was open, continuous, adverse, and lasted longer than the statutory period.
Answer B: Sarah has title to the path by adverse possession because all long-term land use transfers ownership.
Answer C: Sarah has only a revocable license because an easement can never arise from use alone.
Answer D: Sarah has no right because she did not exclude Abraham from the path.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A prescriptive easement may arise from open, notorious, continuous, and adverse use for the statutory period. Sarah used the path openly for 12 years without permission, exceeding the 10-year period, and she seeks only a use right.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because Sarah claims a use right, not title; adverse possession is the title route. C: C is wrong because use alone can create a prescriptive easement when the elements are met. D: D is wrong because exclusivity is generally not required for a prescriptive easement.
lb_rule_reference: Prescriptive easement / open adverse continuous use
lb_student_diagnostic: The recurring trap is converting a use right into title or importing adverse-possession exclusivity.
