


BARMATRIX Q#: 22839
Outline_code: 97100300
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Verdicts and Judgments
Outline: Issue and Claim Preclusion
Question: A truck driver was in an accident with two cars, one driven by a man and one by a woman. The man sued the truck driver for negligence in federal district court. Although the man's attorney put forth an appropriate case, the jury ruled in the truck driver's favor and found that he was not negligent in the crash. The woman was unaware of the man's lawsuit. Consequently, she sued the truck driver and the man for negligence in federal district court. The truck driver argued that the woman's claim against him was barred by res judicata. May the woman assert her claim against the truck driver?
Answer A: A: No, because the issue of the truck driver's negligence has already been fully and fairly litigated.
Answer B: B: No, because the woman's claim is based on the same transaction or occurrence.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the woman was not a part of the earlier case.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the decision was made by a jury, not a judge.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the rules of res judicata or claim preclusion, a party who is not represented in a prior party's unsuccessful litigation is not barred from asserting the same claim in a subsequent lawsuit. Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793 (1996). Here, the woman was unaware of the man's lawsuit, and the woman is now suing both the man and the truck driver (so the man's and the woman's interests were clearly not aligned in the first suit). Therefore, A and B are incorrect. D is incorrect because there is no applicable rule that makes a distinction between a jury and a judge's decision.
