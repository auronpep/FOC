Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Peter sues Esther's restaurant after slipping at 7:15 p.m. on water near the entry. Esther offers testimony from a host that she inspected the same entry at 7:05 p.m. and saw it was dry. Peter objects that the host did not inspect the floor at the exact time of the fall. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the testimony because the floor's condition ten minutes before the fall has some tendency to prove its condition at the time of the fall.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because only conditions observed at the precise moment of injury are relevant.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony unless Esther proves no customer entered during the ten-minute interval.
Answer D: Admit the testimony only if the host can identify the person who later spilled the water.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Condition evidence shortly before the event can be probative of the condition at the relevant time. The ten-minute interval and possible later spill affect weight, not threshold admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because exact timing is not required for relevance. C: C is wrong because Esther need not exclude every possible intervening event before admission. D: D is wrong because the identity of the spiller is not required for the evidence to be relevant.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Over-demanding precision for condition evidence.
