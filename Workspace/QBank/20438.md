Question: In a federal civil trial, Abigail testifies for the plaintiff. The defendant offers Abigail's felony embezzlement conviction to impeach her. The conviction occurred 14 years ago, but Abigail was released from confinement for that conviction eight years ago. Abigail objects that the conviction is more than ten years old. How should the court rule on that objection?
Answer A: Sustain it because the conviction date alone controls the ten-year limit.
Answer B: Sustain it unless the defendant gave written notice and proves the conviction's probative value substantially outweighs prejudice.
Answer C: Overrule it because the ten-year period runs from the later of conviction or release from confinement, and release occurred eight years ago.
Answer D: Overrule it only if Abigail is a criminal defendant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 609(b) applies only if more than ten years have passed since conviction or release from confinement for it, whichever is later. Abigail was released eight years ago, so the conviction is not treated as an old conviction under Rule 609(b).
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the release date can control when it is later. B: B is wrong because the stricter old-conviction standard is not triggered. D: D is wrong because criminal-defendant status is not relevant to this timing question.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(b) / ten-year clock measured from conviction or release, whichever later
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests timing-lock discipline for old convictions and prevents conviction-date-only analysis.
