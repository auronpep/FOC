Outline: Scope
Question: Rachel gave Daniel a written right “personal to Daniel, and not to his heirs or assigns, to walk to the pond on Rachel's land and fish there on weekends.” Daniel later sold the purported right to Nathan, who began entering Rachel's land to fish. May Rachel exclude Nathan?
Answer A: No, because all written easements are freely transferable.
Answer B: No, because a fishing right is automatically appurtenant to any land Nathan owns.
Answer C: Yes, but only if Rachel proves Nathan physically damaged the pond.
Answer D: Yes, because the grant created a personal in-gross right that was not assignable to Nathan.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: An easement in gross benefits a person rather than a parcel. Personal, noncommercial easements in gross are generally not transferable when the grant shows the right was intended to be personal. The grant expressly made the right personal to Daniel and not assignable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every written easement is freely transferable. B: B is wrong because the right is not tied to a dominant parcel. C: C is wrong because Rachel need not prove physical damage to exclude an unauthorized assignee. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Personal easement in gross / nonassignability
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is applying commercial-transfer rules to a personal recreational right.
