Outline: Personal knowledge
Question: In a federal premises-liability case, Leah claims the store had notice of water near the entrance. Ruth testifies that she stood beside the manager when Jeremiah said, “There is water on the floor by the entrance.” Ruth did not personally see the water. The store objects that Ruth lacks personal knowledge of the water. The statement is offered only to show notice to the manager. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because Ruth did not personally see the water.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony because any out-of-court statement about a dangerous condition is inadmissible hearsay.
Answer C: Admit the testimony for the limited purpose of showing that the warning was made to the manager.
Answer D: Admit the testimony to prove that water was actually on the floor.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Ruth has personal knowledge that Jeremiah made the warning in the manager's presence. Because the statement is offered to show notice to the manager rather than the truth that water was present, Ruth need not have seen the water. The court should limit the purpose if requested.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the relevant foundation is Ruth's perception of the warning, not the condition. B: B is wrong because the statement is not offered for truth. D: D is wrong because the testimony is not admissible for the truth of the condition absent another route.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 / personal knowledge of statement for notice
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the difference between personal knowledge of the utterance and personal knowledge of the asserted fact.
