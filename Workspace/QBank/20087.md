MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A storage company owned a fenced lot beside an apartment complex. Children often slipped through a broken section of fence to play near an old pump house. The company knew this. The pump house had an unlocked hatch covering a deep mechanical pit, and replacing the latch would have cost little. A nine-year-old opened the hatch, fell into the pit, and was injured. The child sues the company for negligence. Is the child likely to establish a land-possessor duty in the negligence claim?
Answer A: No, because trespassers are never owed a duty regarding land conditions
Answer B: Yes, because the company knew children trespassed near a serious artificial hazard that was cheap to secure
Answer C: Yes, because children are always treated as invitees on any land they enter
Answer D: No, because the company did not personally invite the child to open the hatch
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The company knew children entered, the unlocked hatch covered a serious artificial danger, a child might not appreciate the risk, and a latch was a slight burden.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Child trespasser rules can impose a duty for dangerous artificial conditions when the possessor knows children are likely to trespass and the burden of protection is low. C: Children are not automatically invitees. The duty depends on the child-trespasser artificial-condition doctrine, not a status conversion. D: The doctrine does not require express invitation. Knowledge of likely child trespass and a serious artificial condition can be enough.
lb_rule_reference: A possessor may owe reasonable care for a dangerous artificial condition when children are likely to trespass, the risk is serious, children do not appreciate it, and the burden of protection is slight.
