Question: A parent knows that his teenager has repeatedly driven a dirt bike recklessly through neighborhood sidewalks. The parent nevertheless gives the teenager the keys and tells him to ride to a friend's house through the same area. The teenager loses control and injures a pedestrian. What is the strongest theory against the parent?
Answer A: Automatic vicarious liability because the teenager is the parent's child
Answer B: Direct liability for negligent entrustment based on giving the bike to a known reckless rider
Answer C: Strict liability because all recreational vehicles are abnormally dangerous
Answer D: No liability is possible because the teenager's negligence is a superseding cause as a matter of law
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The parent's own negligent act can support liability.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Parent status alone is not enough. C: The facts point to negligent entrustment, not strict liability. D: The teenager's foreseeable reckless driving is the risk that made the entrustment negligent.
lb_rule_reference: Although a parent is not automatically liable for a child's tort, the parent may be directly liable for negligent supervision or negligent entrustment.
