Question: John borrowed $24,000 from Mary, agreeing to repay it in 24 monthly installments of $1,000. The agreement contained no acceleration clause. John missed the March and April payments but told Mary he would make future payments. Mary sued immediately for the entire unpaid balance. What may Mary recover on this record?
Answer A: The entire unpaid balance, because missing two installments is automatically a total breach.
Answer B: Only the matured unpaid installments, absent an acceleration clause, repudiation, or other basis for treating the breach as total.
Answer C: Nothing until all 24 installments become due, because suing now would split the cause of action.
Answer D: The reasonable value of the loan, rather than the contract installments.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When a debtor misses installment payments and there is no acceleration clause or total repudiation, the creditor ordinarily may recover only the matured unpaid installments. The missed payments are partial breaches, not automatic acceleration of the entire debt.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because missed installments do not automatically accelerate the full debt. C: C is wrong because the creditor may sue for matured installments. D: D is wrong because the contract installment schedule controls the matured payment obligation.
lb_rule_reference: Common-law partial breach / installment payments
