Question: Gabrielle was indicted for armed robbery. Three days after the indictment, police conducted a lineup without notifying Gabrielle's appointed attorney. The victim identified Gabrielle in the lineup. At trial, the prosecution called the victim to testify. The victim testified both that she had identified Gabrielle at the lineup and that she recognized Gabrielle in the courtroom as the robber. Defense counsel objected to both identifications. The trial court overruled both objections. Which of the following best describes the correct evidentiary rulings?
Answer A: Both identifications are admissible because eyewitness identification is inherently reliable when the witness is confident
Answer B: Both identifications are inadmissible as fruit of the uncounseled lineup
Answer C: The lineup identification is inadmissible, but the in-court identification may be admitted if supported by an independent source
Answer D: The lineup identification is admissible because Gabrielle impliedly waived her right to counsel by not requesting it before the lineup
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under *United States v. Wade*, a post-indictment lineup is a critical stage. Absence of counsel renders the lineup identification inadmissible. The in-court identification is subject to an independent-source inquiry: if the prosecution can show by clear and convincing evidence that the witness's identification derives from her observations at the crime scene rather than from the tainted lineup, the in-court identification is admissible. The trial court must conduct that hearing before allowing the in-court identification to go to the jury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The post-indictment lineup was a critical stage. Absent counsel, the lineup identification is per se inadmissible. Witness confidence is irrelevant to the Sixth Amendment analysis. B: The lineup identification is inadmissible, but the in-court identification may survive if the prosecution proves by clear and convincing evidence that it has an independent source — i.e., that the witness's in-court identification is based on observations at the crime scene rather than on the tainted lineup. The court must make that determination; the in-court identification is not automatically excluded. D: Waiver of a Sixth Amendment right must be knowing, voluntary, and intelligent. Mere silence or failure to request counsel is not a knowing waiver. Police had an obligation to notify counsel; they could not proceed and then claim waiver from the defendant's ignorance of the scheduled lineup.
lb_rule_reference: A post-indictment lineup is a critical stage of prosecution; absence of counsel renders identification evidence inadmissible unless an independent source for the in-court identification is established
