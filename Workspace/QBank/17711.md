Question: A neighborhood association followed a zoning lawsuit from the day it was filed. After summary judgment briefing closed and trial was scheduled, the association moved to intervene to present arguments it could have raised earlier. Existing parties object that adding the association now would require reopening discovery and delaying trial. What is the strongest basis for denying intervention?
Answer A: Intervention must be denied because only original parties may raise zoning arguments
Answer B: The association has no possible interest because it is not the property owner
Answer C: The motion is untimely because the association waited despite knowing about the case, and late intervention would prejudice existing parties
Answer D: Intervention is required because zoning decisions affect the public
Correct Answer: C
lb_rule_reference: Timeliness is required for both intervention of right and permissive intervention; a court may deny intervention when a late motion would prejudice existing parties or disrupt the case.
