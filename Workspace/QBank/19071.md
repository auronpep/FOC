MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Judicial Review
Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: A citizen challenges a federal statute regulating passports, arguing that it violates a specific constitutional limit. The government argues that the case is nonjusticiable solely because passports relate to foreign affairs. The court has ordinary legal standards for deciding the constitutional claim. Should the court dismiss solely because the case touches foreign affairs?
Answer A: Yes, all foreign-affairs cases are political questions
Answer B: Yes, because federal courts may never interpret passport statutes
Answer C: No, the court must test for textual commitment, lack of standards, and related factors
Answer D: No, because political-question doctrine applies only to state courts
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The doctrine requires more than a foreign-affairs label; the court looks for the recognized political-question features.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Political-question doctrine is not triggered by foreign-affairs context alone. B: Courts can decide legal questions involving passports when standards exist and no political-question bar applies. D: Political-question doctrine is a federal justiciability doctrine.
lb_rule_reference: A case is not a political question merely because it involves foreign affairs; the court asks whether there is a textual commitment to another branch, no manageable standard, or other political-question factor.
