Question: In a federal medical-malpractice trial, Hannah calls Ezra, a hospital billing clerk. Before asking anything about Ezra's training, Hannah's lawyer asks, “In your opinion, did the surgeon's technique cause the infection?” The surgeon objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because hospital employees may give any medical opinion.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because no foundation shows Ezra is qualified to give a medical-causation opinion.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because causation is always a lay issue.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Ezra has never seen a hospital bill.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Medical causation is specialized opinion testimony. Without a foundation showing Ezra is qualified to give such an opinion, the question is objectionable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because employment in a hospital does not qualify a billing clerk to give medical causation opinions. C: C is wrong because medical causation often requires expert foundation. D: D is wrong because billing experience does not address medical-causation expertise.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 702/FRE 611 / expert foundation objection
