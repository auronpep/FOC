Question: Solomon told Leah, “You may walk across my meadow to visit Rachel.” Leah used the meadow path a few times. She made no improvements, spent no money, and changed no position in reliance on Solomon's statement. Solomon then told Leah to stop using the path. What is the best result?
Answer A: Leah has an easement by estoppel because every oral license is irrevocable once used.
Answer B: Leah has a prescriptive easement because she crossed the meadow openly.
Answer C: Leah owns the path because Solomon allowed her to use it.
Answer D: Leah has only a revocable license because she had permission but no substantial reliance or prescriptive period.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A license is permission to use land and is generally revocable unless estoppel or another doctrine makes it irrevocable. Leah made no expenditures, improvements, or other substantial reliance, and no prescriptive period is present.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because use alone does not make every oral license irrevocable. B: B is wrong because permission defeats adversity and the facts do not show a statutory period. C: C is wrong because permission to walk does not transfer title. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: No estoppel without substantial reliance
lb_student_diagnostic: The repair signal is failing to demand actual reliance before applying estoppel.
