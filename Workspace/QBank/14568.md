Question: A prosecutor presented to a federal grand jury the testimony of a witness in order to secure a defendant's indictment for theft of government property. The prosecutor did not disclose to the grand jury that the witness had been convicted four years earlier of perjury. The grand jury returned an indictment, and the defendant pleaded not guilty. Shortly thereafter, the prosecutor took the case to trial, calling the witness to testify before the jury. The prosecutor did not disclose the witness's prior perjury conviction until the defense was preparing to rest. Defense counsel immediately moved for a mistrial, which the court denied. Instead, the court allowed the defense to recall the witness for the purpose of impeaching him with this conviction, but the witness could not be located. The court then allowed the defense to introduce documentary evidence of the witness's criminal record to the jury before resting its case. The jury convicted the defendant. The defendant has moved for a new trial, arguing that the prosecutor's failure to disclose the witness's prior conviction in a timely manner violated the defendant's right to due process of law. If the court grants the defendant's motion, what will be the most likely reason?
Answer A: A: The defendant was unable to cross-examine the witness about the conviction.
Answer B: B: The prosecutor failed to inform the grand jury of the witness's conviction.
Answer C: C: The court found it reasonably probable that the defendant would have been acquitted had the defense had timely access to the information about the witness's conviction.
Answer D: D: The court found that the prosecutor had deliberately delayed disclosing the witness's conviction to obtain a strategic advantage.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. Had the prosecution disclosed the impeachment evidence earlier, there would have been a reasonable probability that the defendant would have been acquitted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The constitutional violation here rests on the untimely disclosure of the witness's prior conviction and the prejudice that resulted, not on the defendant's ability to cross-examine the witness, which the court did not limit. B is incorrect. The prosecution did not violate the defendant's rights on this basis because it is not constitutionally required to present the grand jury with evidence that is favorable to the defense. D is incorrect. The issue here is whether the delayed disclosure created a reasonable probability of a different outcome, irrespective of whether the prosecutor deliberately delayed disclosure of the impeachment information.
