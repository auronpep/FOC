Question: During a large document production, a defendant accidentally produces one privileged legal-advice email despite using keyword screening and attorney review. Two days later, defense counsel discovers the mistake, notifies opposing counsel, and asks for return or destruction of the email. Has the privilege most likely been waived?
Answer A: Yes, because any production to an opposing party waives privilege
Answer B: No, because the disclosure was inadvertent, the defendant used reasonable screening, and counsel promptly tried to rectify the error
Answer C: No, because inadvertent disclosure can never waive privilege
Answer D: Yes, unless the privileged email was prepared after the complaint was filed
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts satisfy the main Rule 502(b) conditions for avoiding waiver.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 502(b) prevents waiver for qualifying inadvertent disclosures. C: Protection depends on reasonable precautions and prompt corrective steps. D: Waiver of attorney-client privilege under Rule 502(b) does not turn on filing date.
lb_rule_reference: Under Federal Rule of Evidence 502(b), inadvertent disclosure does not waive privilege if the holder took reasonable steps to prevent disclosure and promptly took reasonable steps to rectify the error.
