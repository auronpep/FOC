Question: A corporation incorporated in State B challenges a State A law that charges higher filing fees to out-of-state corporations seeking construction permits. The corporation asserts only Article IV privileges and immunities. What is the best response to the Article IV claim?
Answer A: The claim fails because corporations are not citizens protected by Article IV privileges and immunities
Answer B: The claim succeeds because corporations count as citizens for every constitutional provision
Answer C: The claim succeeds automatically because construction affects interstate commerce
Answer D: The claim fails only if the corporation has no shareholders in State B
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Article IV protects natural citizens of other states, not corporations.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Article IV privileges and immunities is specifically limited to citizens, and corporations are not citizens for this purpose. C: The question asks only Article IV; DCC and Article IV have different plaintiff rules. D: Shareholder citizenship does not turn the corporation into an Article IV citizen.
lb_rule_reference: Article IV privileges and immunities protects citizens of other states, not corporations.
