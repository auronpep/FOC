Question: A developer sold Lot 1 to Lydia by deed with no land-use restriction. At that time, the developer had no recorded subdivision declaration and had made no representations about a common plan. One year later, the developer recorded a declaration stating that all lots in the development were limited to residential use. The developer then sold Lot 2 to John, whose deed referred to the recorded declaration. Lydia later began building a small store on Lot 1. John sues Lydia to enforce the residential restriction. Which is the best result?
Answer A: John can enforce the restriction because Lydia had notice after the developer later recorded the declaration.
Answer B: John can enforce the restriction because any restriction on one lot automatically creates a common scheme for all earlier-sold lots.
Answer C: John can enforce the restriction because zoning law favors residential use.
Answer D: John cannot enforce the restriction against Lydia because the burden was not attached to Lot 1 when Lydia took title.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A land-use burden generally must attach to the burdened land through an effective restriction, common scheme, or other recognized source. A developer usually cannot unilaterally impose a new burden on a lot already conveyed without restriction. Lydia bought before any declaration, restriction, or common-plan representation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because later notice does not create a burden that did not attach when Lydia acquired title. B: B is wrong because a common scheme requires more than a later restriction on other lots. C: C is wrong because zoning preferences do not create private covenants.
lb_rule_reference: Burden attachment / late-recorded declaration
