Outline: Embezzlement
Question: Police suspected Randall of embezzlement. Before any charges were filed, Randall retained an attorney, who sent the police department a letter stating that Randall would not speak to officers without counsel present. Two days later, detectives arrested Randall without a warrant in a public place based on probable cause. After administering Miranda warnings, they questioned Randall without notifying his attorney. Randall, who had forgotten his attorney's advice, answered questions and made incriminating admissions. Randall moves to suppress the statements under the Sixth Amendment. Should the court suppress Randall's statements on Sixth Amendment grounds?
Answer A: Yes, because Randall had retained counsel and clearly invoked his right to have counsel present
Answer B: Yes, because any statement taken from a represented defendant without counsel present is automatically inadmissible
Answer C: No, because the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had not yet attached at the time of the interrogation
Answer D: No, because Randall waived his right by answering questions after receiving Miranda warnings
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches only at the initiation of formal adversarial judicial proceedings — indictment, information, arraignment, or preliminary hearing. At the time of questioning, no charges had been filed, so no Sixth Amendment right existed. The interrogation is governed solely by Miranda. Because Miranda warnings were given and Randall (having forgotten counsel's advice) chose to answer, the Fifth Amendment was not violated either.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The Sixth Amendment right had not yet attached because no formal adversarial proceedings had commenced. Retaining counsel before indictment does not trigger Sixth Amendment protections. The letter to police was effective only for Miranda/Fifth Amendment purposes if Randall personally invoked during custodial interrogation. B: There is no blanket rule barring statements from represented defendants before formal proceedings begin. The Sixth Amendment applies only after formal charges. Civil representation and pre-charge retention do not invoke the Sixth Amendment. D: The more fundamental reason is that the Sixth Amendment had not attached at all. Framing the answer as a waiver implies the right existed, which misstates the law. The correct basis for admissibility is non-attachment, not waiver.
lb_rule_reference: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel does not attach until formal adversarial proceedings begin; pre-indictment questioning is governed by Miranda and the Fifth Amendment only
