Question: Hannah sold Lot 8 to Samuel. The deed stated that the conveyance was “except and subject to the existing recorded utility easement across the north ten feet of Lot 8.” The utility easement had been validly granted years earlier. After closing, Samuel argued that he owned Lot 8 free of the utility easement because the deed did not newly grant anything to the utility. What is the best result?
Answer A: Samuel takes free of the utility easement because every easement must be newly granted in each later deed.
Answer B: Samuel takes subject to the existing utility easement because the deed recognized an already-created burden on the land.
Answer C: The utility company receives fee title to the north ten feet because the deed used the word “except.”
Answer D: The utility easement becomes a revocable license because Samuel did not personally sign the original utility grant.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: An existing easement can burden land when the servient estate is later conveyed. The deed's exception or subject-to language recognizes that the buyer takes the land with the existing recorded utility burden. The utility's right need not be re-granted in every later deed.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because valid easements can bind later owners without being recreated in each deed. C: C is wrong because the referenced interest is a utility easement, not fee title. D: D is wrong because successors may take subject to existing property interests even though they did not sign the original grant.
lb_rule_reference: Exception / existing easement preserved
