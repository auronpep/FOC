Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: MarbleGate Motors is incorporated in North Carolina and has its principal place of business in North Carolina. It sells cars through independent dealers in all 50 states, including $12 million in annual sales in Franklin. A plaintiff injured in North Carolina by a car bought in North Carolina sued MarbleGate in Franklin state court. The plaintiff argues Franklin has general jurisdiction because MarbleGate does substantial business there. Should Franklin treat MarbleGate as subject to general jurisdiction for this unrelated North Carolina accident?
Answer A: Yes, because substantial sales in a state always make a corporation at home there
Answer B: Yes, because the plaintiff is allowed to choose any state where the defendant sells products
Answer C: No, because MarbleGate is not incorporated or headquartered in Franklin and the claim is unrelated to Franklin sales
Answer D: No, but only because federal courts can never exercise general jurisdiction
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Franklin is not an at-home forum. The North Carolina accident does not arise out of or relate to Franklin contacts, so the forum sales do not support specific jurisdiction either.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: General jurisdiction requires an at-home forum, usually incorporation or PPB. Ordinary substantial sales do not create all-purpose jurisdiction. B: Plaintiff choice does not create general jurisdiction. D: Federal courts can exercise general jurisdiction when authorized and consistent with due process.
lb_rule_reference: A corporation is ordinarily subject to general jurisdiction where incorporated or where it has its principal place of business; exceptional contacts are required elsewhere.
