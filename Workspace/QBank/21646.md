Question: Noah ordered 200 market tents from Paul. The signed purchase order stated, “Tents to conform to attached Spec Sheet A,” and the agreement contained a merger clause. Spec Sheet A required flame-resistant fabric. Paul delivered tents made from ordinary fabric and argued that Spec Sheet A was inadmissible parol evidence because it was not separately signed. If Spec Sheet A was attached and incorporated as stated in the purchase order, what is the likely legal effect?
Answer A: Paul wins because an unsigned attachment is always excluded by the parol evidence rule.
Answer B: Noah may treat the tents as nonconforming because the incorporated specifications are part of the agreement's content.
Answer C: Noah may rely on Spec Sheet A only if it stated a condition precedent to the contract's effectiveness.
Answer D: Paul wins because a seller's objection in the current dispute erases any incorporated specification.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a document incorporated by reference into a signed agreement becomes part of the contract's content. The trigger facts are the purchase order's express reference to attached Spec Sheet A and the sheet's flame-resistant-fabric requirement. The decisive lock is term status: the specification is incorporated, not merely prior parol evidence. If Paul delivered tents that did not meet that incorporated specification, Noah can treat them as nonconforming.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because an attached, incorporated specification is not excluded merely because it has no separate signature. C: C is wrong because the spec defines a performance term, not a condition to effectiveness. D: D is wrong because a current objection cannot erase a term that was already incorporated into the agreement.
