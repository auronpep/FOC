Outline: Nonconforming use
Question: Peter orally promised Abigail, “You will always have a view of the river across my land, and I will never build anything that blocks it.” Abigail paid Peter $500 but received no deed, covenant, or other writing. Abigail made no improvements in reliance on the promise. Peter later began building a lawful house that would block the view. Abigail claims an express easement protecting the view. What is the best answer?
Answer A: Abigail has an express easement because oral promises are enough to create permanent view rights.
Answer B: Abigail has no express easement because a permanent land-use restriction generally requires a writing or another recognized source such as estoppel.
Answer C: Abigail has a prescriptive easement because she previously enjoyed the view openly.
Answer D: Abigail owns the airspace above Peter's land because she paid money for the promise.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A permanent easement or comparable land-use restriction generally must satisfy the statute of frauds unless another doctrine, such as estoppel, applies. Abigail received only an oral promise and made no reliance improvements. Prior enjoyment of a view does not create a prescriptive or express easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because oral promises ordinarily do not create permanent easements. C: C is wrong because merely enjoying a view is not adverse use of Peter's land. D: D is wrong because payment alone does not convey ownership of airspace or create a written property interest.
lb_rule_reference: Statute of frauds / oral negative-use promise
