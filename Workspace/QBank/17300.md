Question: In a federal slip-and-fall trial, Matthew calls the store manager, Esther, only to establish that the store owned the entrance where Matthew fell. On cross-examination, the store's lawyer asks Esther to describe all cleaning procedures used on the morning of the fall. Matthew objects that the question exceeds the scope of direct and is not about credibility. How should the court rule under the federal default?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because cross-examination may always cover every relevant issue.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because a manager may be questioned without limits.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because ownership testimony bars all cross-examination.
Answer D: Sustain the objection unless the court permits inquiry into the new matter as if on direct examination.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the federal default, cross-examination should not go beyond the subject matter of direct examination and credibility, though the court may permit additional inquiry as if on direct. Cleaning procedures go beyond the narrow ownership direct and are not credibility by themselves.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because federal cross is not automatically wide open. B: B is wrong because a manager's status does not eliminate scope limits. C: C is wrong because cross-examination is not barred entirely; it is limited unless the court expands it.
