Question: A hospital negligently called a patient family and reported that the patient had died, even though the patient was alive and recovering in another unit. The patient's spouse suffered severe emotional distress from the notice. The jurisdiction recognizes erroneous death notice as a special NIED category. Is the spouse's emotional-distress claim likely viable?
Answer A: Yes, under the jurisdiction's special category for erroneous death notice
Answer B: Yes, because any negligent misinformation creates emotional-distress liability
Answer C: No, because the spouse was not physically endangered by the hospital
Answer D: No, because the patient was alive, so the spouse cannot be distressed as a matter of law
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts fit the stated special NIED category, so ordinary zone-of-danger or bystander requirements do not control.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: The claim is viable because the notice falls within a recognized special category, not because every negligent statement creates NIED liability. C: The jurisdiction recognizes erroneous death notice as a special category. D: The wrongfully delivered death notice itself can be the basis for the recognized special category.
lb_rule_reference: A jurisdiction may recognize negligent erroneous death notice as a special emotional-distress category distinct from ordinary bystander or zone-of-danger rules.
