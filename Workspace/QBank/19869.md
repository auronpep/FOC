MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal-Question Jurisdiction
Question: A plaintiff files a state-court action asserting only state-law breach-of-contract claims against a software company. The complaint does not reference any federal law. Eighteen months after the original filing, the plaintiff files an amended complaint adding a federal copyright infringement claim under the Copyright Act, 17 U.S.C. § 501. Within 30 days of receiving the amended complaint, the defendant files a notice of removal, citing federal-question jurisdiction based on the copyright claim. The plaintiff moves to remand, arguing that the one-year outer limit for removal has been exceeded. The governing forum is initially state court, now removed, under the MBE baseline. Is the removal timely?
Answer A: No, because the action was commenced more than one year ago in state court, and § 1446(c)(1) bars removal of any action more than one year after commencement regardless of the basis for removal
Answer B: Yes, because the removal is timely — the defendant filed the notice of removal within 30 days of the amended complaint, and the one-year outer limit does not apply to federal-question removal under § 1331
Answer C: No, because even for federal-question cases, a defendant must remove within one year of the date the case became removable — not merely within 30 days of a triggering paper
Answer D: No, because the plaintiff's original state-law claims remain in the case alongside the new copyright claim, and mixed state-federal cases cannot be removed on federal-question grounds alone
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Section 1446(c)(1)'s one-year outer limit applies only to diversity-based removals under § 1332. It does not restrict federal-question removals under § 1331. The defendant's removal is based on the Copyright Act, which is a federal cause of action creating § 1331 jurisdiction. The defendant filed within 30 days of receiving the amended complaint — the first paper from which removability was apparent. Both timing requirements for § 1331 removal are satisfied; the one-year bar does not apply. Removal was timely.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1446(c)(1) provides that a case "may not be removed under subsection (b)(3) on the basis of jurisdiction conferred by section 1332 more than 1 year after commencement." The one-year limit expressly applies only to § 1332 diversity removals. Federal-question removal under § 1331 is not subject to the one-year outer limit. The defendant timely removed within 30 days of the paper disclosing the federal claim. C: For federal-question removal, the only timing requirement is the 30-day window under § 1446(b)(3) — 30 days from receipt of the initial pleading showing removability or from the paper that first makes the case removable. There is no one-year backstop for federal-question cases. The defendant here removed within 30 days of the amended complaint, which is timely. D: Under § 1441(c), when a complaint includes a federal claim, the entire action may be removed. A defendant is not required to have a purely federal complaint to remove. Additionally, supplemental jurisdiction under § 1367 would likely cover the state-law contract claim if it shares a common nucleus with the copyright claim — or the court can sever and remand the state claims. The presence of state claims does not prevent federal-question removal.
lb_rule_reference: 28 U.S.C. § 1446(c)(1) — the one-year outer limit for removal applies only to diversity-based removals; federal-question cases are subject only to the 30-day window and no absolute outer limit.
