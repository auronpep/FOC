Question: In a federal negligence trial, Joshua calls his own friendly witness, Phoebe. Joshua's lawyer asks, “You would agree that Joshua was careful and did nothing wrong, correct?” The defendant objects. What is the best objection?
Answer A: The question is hearsay.
Answer B: The question is proper because it summarizes the desired testimony.
Answer C: The question is leading on direct examination and also pushes the witness toward a legal conclusion.
Answer D: The question is proper because witnesses must state the final result of the case.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The question suggests the desired answer to a friendly witness on direct and asks for a broad conclusion about care and wrongdoing. The leading/form objection is the best immediate objection.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the question does not ask for an out-of-court statement. B: B is wrong because summarizing the desired result is exactly why the question is leading. D: D is wrong because witnesses do not decide the final legal result.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(c) / leading on direct as best objection
