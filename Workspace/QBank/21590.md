Question: Moses posts a notice: “I will pay $500 to anyone who finds and returns my missing tool chest.” Ruth sees the notice and sends Moses a message: “I promise that I will search for it tomorrow.” Before Ruth begins searching, Moses revokes the reward offer. Is Ruth's promise enough to form a contract before revocation?
Answer A: Yes, because a promise to search is consideration for the reward offer.
Answer B: Yes, because Moses's public notice became irrevocable as soon as Ruth read it.
Answer C: No, because the offer invited acceptance only by performance—finding and returning the tool chest—not by a promise to search.
Answer D: No, but only because Ruth's promise was too vague to identify the tool chest.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A reward offer ordinarily invites acceptance by completed performance. Ruth promised to search but did not perform the requested act before revocation. Her promise did not accept the unilateral offer.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the offer asked for completed performance, not a promise to try. B: B is wrong because merely reading a reward notice does not make the offer irrevocable. D: D is wrong because the decisive issue is not vague wording; it is that the offer required performance rather than a return promise.
lb_rule_reference: common_law
lb_student_diagnostic: Student converted a unilateral reward offer into a bilateral promise contract.
