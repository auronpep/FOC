Subtopic: Defenses to Enforceability
Outline: Mistake / Lack of Mutual Assent
Question: David agrees to buy a small building from Sarah for use as a bakery. Before signing, both parties rely on an official zoning letter stating that bakery use is allowed. Unknown to both, the zoning office had revoked that permission two days before the contract was signed. The building cannot lawfully be used as a bakery, and Sarah made no risk allocation in the contract. David seeks to avoid the contract. What is the best analysis?
Answer A: David cannot avoid the contract because zoning restrictions are matters of law, not fact.
Answer B: David cannot avoid the contract because Sarah did not cause the zoning problem.
Answer C: David can avoid the contract only if Sarah guaranteed future profits.
Answer D: David can avoid the contract because both parties were mistaken about an existing fact that was basic to the bakery-use bargain and materially affected the exchange.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The mistaken belief existed at the time of contracting: the parties believed the property could lawfully be used as a bakery when that permission had already been revoked. That use was basic to the bargain, materially affected the exchange, and was not assigned to David by the contract.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the operative error is the existing legal-use status of the property at contract formation, not a later legal change. B: B is wrong because mutual mistake does not require fault. C: C is wrong because the issue is suitability for the contracted use, not a guarantee of profits.
lb_rule_reference: Mutual mistake / existing legal-use fact
