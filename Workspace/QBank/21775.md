Outline: Mode and order of witnesses
Question: Mary sues Boaz for breach of contract in federal court. Mary asks to call Boaz as her first witness because his testimony will identify the contract and narrow the issues. Boaz objects that Mary must call her own friendly witnesses first. How should the court rule?
Answer A: The court may allow Mary to call Boaz first as a reasonable order-of-proof decision.
Answer B: The court must bar Boaz from testifying until Mary has called all nonparty witnesses.
Answer C: The court must allow Boaz to refuse to testify because he is the opposing party.
Answer D: The court may allow Boaz first only if Boaz agrees to the order.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The trial judge may reasonably control the order of proof. Calling an adverse party early to organize or narrow the issues can be permitted, subject to ordinary rules of examination and fairness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because there is no requirement that friendly witnesses come first. C: C is wrong because a civil party is not generally exempt from testifying as a witness. D: D is wrong because the judge's control of witness order does not depend on the opposing party's consent.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / adverse party first witness
