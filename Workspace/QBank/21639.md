Question: A public high school expels a student for the rest of the year after a contested allegation of selling drugs at school. The school gives only a one-sentence notice and no opportunity to present the student's side before the expulsion begins. What is the likely due process problem?
Answer A: No process is due because school discipline is always informal
Answer B: The student likely needed meaningful notice and a chance to respond before a year-long expulsion
Answer C: The student is entitled to a full criminal jury trial before any school discipline
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies because education is always a fundamental right
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The deprivation is significant and the facts are contested, increasing the value of procedure.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Expulsion is a more serious deprivation and generally requires more meaningful procedures. C: School discipline requires due process, but not necessarily criminal trial procedures. D: The question concerns process for school discipline, not a substantive fundamental-right classification.
lb_rule_reference: Longer school exclusions such as expulsions generally require more robust notice and opportunity to respond than brief suspensions.
