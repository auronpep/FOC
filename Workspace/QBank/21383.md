Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Personal knowledge
Question: In a federal negligence trial, Miriam testifies that she saw Caleb's wagon enter the intersection while the light was red. On voir dire, Miriam says she was standing across the street, facing the intersection, and had an unobstructed view. Caleb objects that Miriam was too far away to be certain. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because Miriam's testimony is enough to support a finding that she had personal knowledge; distance affects weight.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because only an expert can estimate what a witness could see from across a street.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because personal knowledge must be corroborated by another witness.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if Miriam can prove the traffic light's timing records.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: FRE 602 requires evidence sufficient to support a finding that the witness has personal knowledge, and the witness's own testimony may supply that foundation. Miriam says she saw the intersection from an unobstructed position. The distance may be explored on cross-examination but does not defeat foundation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because no expert is needed for ordinary visual perception. C: C is wrong because corroboration is not required. D: D is wrong because traffic-light records are not necessary to show Miriam perceived the event.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 602 / personal knowledge through witness testimony
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the personal-knowledge threshold and the tendency to turn credibility weaknesses into admissibility bars.
