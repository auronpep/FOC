Question: In a federal negligence trial, Jeremiah claims that Eve ran a stop sign and hit him. Eve admits she had an expired driver's license, but there is no claim that she lacked driving skill, that the expiration affected her driving, or that licensing status caused the collision. Jeremiah offers the expired-license evidence to prove negligence. Eve objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence because any violation of a licensing rule proves negligence in a traffic case.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence because, without a link to driving skill, causation, or the collision, the expired license does not make running the stop sign more probable.
Answer C: Admit the evidence because the jury may punish Eve for driving with expired documents.
Answer D: Exclude the evidence only if Eve renews the license before trial.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The expired license is not logically connected to whether Eve ran the stop sign, drove negligently, or caused the accident under the stated facts. A regulatory status fact is not automatically relevant to negligence.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A overstates the effect of licensing violations. C confuses punishment with relevance to a fact of consequence.
