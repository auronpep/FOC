MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Complaint
Question: A freelance writer sued a magazine in federal court claiming federal civil rights violations. The court believed the claims made in the writer's complaint put the magazine on notice and supported a possible claim, but not a plausible one.&#10;&#10;Does the writer's complaint fulfill the applicable requirements of the federal rules?
Answer A: A: Yes, because it put the magazine on notice.
Answer B: B: Yes, because it supported a possible claim.
Answer C: C: No, because it did not contain detailed assertions of the facts underlying the claim.
Answer D: D: No, because it did not support a plausible claim.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The federal rules generally require only that a complaint put the other side on notice of the claim and they generally do not require detailed assertions of the facts underlying the claim. However, under Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544 (2007) and Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662 (2009) the facts in the complaint need to support a plausible claim, not just a possible one.
Wrong Answer Explanations: Therefore, A, B, and C are incorrect.
