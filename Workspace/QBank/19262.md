Question: In a federal contract trial, Peter and Lydia sign a written stipulation that Lydia owned the warehouse on the date of the fire. At trial, Peter tries to call three witnesses solely to prove that Lydia owned the warehouse. Lydia objects. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because a party may always prove a stipulated fact with additional witnesses.
Answer B: Sustain the objection in the court's discretion because the stipulation has removed ownership from dispute and additional proof would be cumulative.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because stipulations are not evidence of any kind.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the three witnesses have documentary evidence.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A formal stipulation or judicial admission can remove a fact from dispute. The court may exclude needless cumulative proof of that fact and rely on the stipulation.
