Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: CorpPlex International SA is a Swiss holding company incorporated under Swiss law. CorpPlex's global headquarters and executive suite — where its CEO, board, and all senior officers work — are physically located in the State of Vantrill, where CorpPlex leases an entire office tower. CorpPlex moved its executive operations to Vantrill 12 years ago and has never returned any operations to Switzerland. CorpPlex conducts 0% of its operations in Switzerland beyond maintaining its legal registration there. CorpPlex's annual reports describe Vantrill as "our global operational home." The CEO has stated publicly that Vantrill is "where we run everything." CorpPlex has no operations outside Vantrill and Switzerland, and 95% of its activity occurs in Vantrill. A foreign investor sues CorpPlex in Vantrill federal court on a breach-of-contract claim. CorpPlex is incorporated in Switzerland; its PPB under the nerve-center test would be Vantrill. Does Vantrill have general personal jurisdiction over CorpPlex?
Answer A: No, because CorpPlex is incorporated in Switzerland, and incorporation is one of exactly two permissible bases for general jurisdiction under Daimler
Answer B: No, because even extensive operations in Vantrill cannot satisfy the Daimler at-home test unless the corporation is incorporated or has its PPB there — extensive operations are never enough
Answer C: Yes, because Vantrill is CorpPlex's principal place of business under the Hertz nerve-center test — CorpPlex's officers direct all corporate activity from Vantrill — making Vantrill one of its home states for general jurisdiction, even though CorpPlex is incorporated in Switzerland
Answer D: Yes, because CorpPlex's 95% Vantrill activity and public statements about Vantrill as "our global operational home" establish exceptional circumstances under Daimler that allow a third state to supply general jurisdiction beyond incorporation and PPB
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Daimler* requires that the corporation be "at home" in the forum — either through incorporation or PPB. *Hertz* defines PPB as the nerve center: where high-level officers direct, control, and coordinate corporate activities. CorpPlex's CEO, board, and all senior officers work from Vantrill; 95% of activity occurs there; no operations exist elsewhere except a Swiss legal registration. Vantrill is clearly the nerve center under *Hertz*, making it the PPB and one of CorpPlex's home states for *Daimler* purposes. General jurisdiction in Vantrill is proper.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: This answer ignores both the PPB prong and the *Hertz* nerve-center test. CorpPlex's nerve center — where its officers direct and coordinate all activity — is in Vantrill. Under *Hertz*, Vantrill is CorpPlex's principal place of business for jurisdictional purposes. That makes Vantrill one of CorpPlex's two home states, giving Vantrill general jurisdiction. There is no need to reach the exceptional-circumstances question because the PPB prong is directly satisfied. B: CorpPlex's entire executive operation is in Vantrill. Under *Hertz*, that makes Vantrill the PPB — which directly satisfies *Daimler*'s at-home test without requiring any exceptional-circumstances analysis. The *Daimler* rule does not say operations are irrelevant — it says operations alone (without being the nerve center) are insufficient. When the nerve center is in Vantrill, the PPB prong is met directly. D: This answer reaches the right result through the wrong analysis. There is no need to invoke exceptional circumstances here because Vantrill directly satisfies the PPB prong under *Hertz*'s nerve-center test. Exceptional circumstances is a residual, narrow category for situations where neither incorporation nor PPB lies in the forum. Here, the PPB analysis answers the question directly. Reaching exceptional circumstances when the standard analysis suffices is analytically imprecise.
lb_rule_reference: General jurisdiction over a corporation normally exists only in its state of incorporation and its principal-place-of-business state. *Daimler AG v. Bauman* (2014) left open the possibility that in "exceptional" circumstances — e.g., a foreign corporation whose operations are so pervasively centered in the forum that it is essentially "at home" there in a structural sense — a third state might also supply general jurisdiction. Lower courts have rarely found exceptional circumstances.
