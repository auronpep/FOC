Question: A small nonparty accounting firm receives a subpoena demanding every client file created during the last decade, even though the lawsuit concerns one client's single transaction. Compliance would require weeks of review and would expose unrelated client information. The firm moves to quash or modify. What is the strongest basis for relief?
Answer A: A subpoena can never be served on a nonparty
Answer B: The subpoena should be enforced in full because all subpoena commands are automatically proportional
Answer C: The subpoena may be quashed or modified because it imposes undue burden and sweeps far beyond the transaction at issue
Answer D: The subpoena is valid only if the accountant is added as a defendant first
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The request is broad, burdensome, and directed at a nonparty with many unrelated files.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 45 subpoenas can be directed to nonparties. B: Subpoenas remain subject to limits, including undue burden protection. D: A nonparty can be subpoenaed without being joined as a party.
lb_rule_reference: A court must quash or modify a subpoena that subjects a person to undue burden, and nonparty status can matter in the burden analysis.
