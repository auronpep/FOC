Outline: Common law
Question: Joseph promised his son Benjamin that, if Benjamin bought a used cart from any seller within the next month, Joseph would pay that seller the first $3,000 of the price. Benjamin later bought a cart from Hannah. Joseph then refused to pay Hannah, arguing that Hannah was not named and did not know about the promise when Joseph made it. May Hannah enforce Joseph's promise?
Answer A: Yes, because Hannah falls within the intended class of sellers to whom Joseph promised direct payment.
Answer B: No, because a beneficiary must be specifically named in the contract at the time it is made.
Answer C: No, because Hannah did not know of Joseph's promise when it was made.
Answer D: Yes, but only as Benjamin's assignee.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Hannah may enforce as an intended class beneficiary. A third-party beneficiary need not be individually named or aware at formation if the contract manifests an intent to benefit the class and the claimant later becomes a member of that class.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because specific naming is not required when the intended class is clear. C: C is wrong because awareness at formation affects vesting, not intended status. D: D is wrong because Hannah is enforcing a promise made for her class, not an assigned right from Benjamin.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — future or class beneficiary
