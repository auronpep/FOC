Question: Daniel signed and delivered a deed conveying his orchard to Esther. In a separate note signed the same day, Esther promised to pay Daniel twenty thousand dollars within six months. The deed itself was unconditional and used present words of transfer. Esther accepted the deed but later failed to pay the note. What is the best statement of the title result?
Answer A: Esther owns the orchard; Daniel's claim for the unpaid note is separate from the deed-validity lock.
Answer B: Daniel owns the orchard until the note is paid in full.
Answer C: Daniel owns the orchard because the separate note shows that the deed was only a contract to convey later.
Answer D: Esther owns the orchard only if she records the deed before the six-month payment date.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is present execution and delivery, not later performance of a separate payment promise. Daniel delivered an unconditional deed with present transfer words, and Esther accepted. Failure to pay the separate note may create a contract remedy, but it does not by itself defeat title.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because payment of the note was not made a condition of title passage. C: C is wrong because the deed itself used present conveyance language and was delivered. D: D is wrong because recording is a priority device, not a prerequisite to the deed's validity between Daniel and Esther.
lb_rule_reference: Separate promise to pay does not defeat deed validity
