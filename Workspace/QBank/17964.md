Question: A city prohibits adult theaters from operating within 500 feet of schools or residential zones. The city relies on studies about traffic, late-night noise, and crime around such businesses. The ordinance does not ban adult theaters citywide and leaves multiple commercially available locations. What is the best classification of the ordinance?
Answer A: It is automatically content based because adult theaters contain expressive material
Answer B: It is content neutral if justified by secondary effects and leaves reasonable alternatives
Answer C: It is valid even if it leaves no realistic locations for adult theaters
Answer D: It receives no First Amendment review because zoning is not speech regulation
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The city points to location-based secondary effects rather than disagreement with the message and leaves adult theaters other places to operate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Secondary-effects zoning may be treated as content neutral when aimed at noncommunicative effects. C: A content-neutral location rule must leave adequate alternative channels. D: Zoning that burdens expressive businesses still receives First Amendment analysis.
lb_rule_reference: A zoning rule targeting secondary effects of adult businesses may be analyzed as content neutral if it is justified without reference to suppressing expression and leaves adequate alternatives.
