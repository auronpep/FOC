MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A newspaper published an article about a public corruption trial and accurately quoted a publicly filed court exhibit showing that Witness had once declared bankruptcy. The bankruptcy record was embarrassing to Witness but was part of the public court file and relevant to the trial story. Witness sues for public disclosure of private facts. Is Witness likely to prevail?
Answer A: Yes, because the bankruptcy was embarrassing
Answer B: Yes, because the article reached many readers
Answer C: No, because the article truthfully reported newsworthy information from a public record
Answer D: No, because public disclosure requires the report to be false
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The bankruptcy information was part of a public court file and relevant to a public trial. That defeats the private-facts claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The information was in a public court record and was newsworthy in the trial coverage. B: Publicity alone is not enough. The facts must be private and non-newsworthy. D: Public disclosure can involve true facts. This case fails because the information is public-record and newsworthy.
lb_rule_reference: Public disclosure of private facts generally does not impose liability for truthful reporting of newsworthy information drawn from public records.
