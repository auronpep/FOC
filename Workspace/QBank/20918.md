Outline: Rule of completeness
Question: Rachel sues Samuel for fraud. Rachel introduces Samuel's recorded statement from Monday: “I never told Rachel about the leaking roof.” Samuel offers a separate text message he sent to his brother Nathan on Wednesday saying, “I am an honest seller.” Samuel argues that the text must be admitted under the rule of completeness. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the text because any later statement by Samuel about the same sale completes the Monday recording.
Answer B: Admit the text because Samuel's credibility is now important.
Answer C: Exclude the text because it is a separate self-serving statement that does not explain or qualify the Monday statement.
Answer D: Exclude the text only if Rachel promises not to argue that Samuel is dishonest.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Completeness is not a general right to introduce separate helpful statements. The text was made on a different day and does not explain or qualify the specific recorded statement Rachel introduced. Its general claim of honesty does not correct a misleading impression from the quoted Monday statement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because related subject matter is not enough; the statement must fairly complete the portion introduced. B: B is wrong because credibility importance does not create a completeness right. D: D is wrong because the ruling does not depend on Rachel's future argument.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 106 / separate statement not completion
