Outline: Statute of Frauds
Question: Hannah orally agreed to sell 600 baskets to Jonah for $2 each. No writing was signed and no goods were delivered, paid for, specially manufactured, or admitted. When Hannah sued Jonah for breach, Jonah asserted the Statute of Frauds. Hannah argues that because they clearly reached an oral agreement, the Statute of Frauds cannot matter. What is the best answer?
Answer A: Hannah is correct because the Statute of Frauds prevents formation only when the parties did not agree.
Answer B: Hannah is correct because oral goods contracts are always enforceable if the parties remember the same terms.
Answer C: Jonah is wrong because the Statute of Frauds can be used only by plaintiffs, not defendants.
Answer D: Jonah is correct; even if an oral agreement was reached, a sale-of-goods contract for $500 or more is not enforceable by action or defense absent a sufficient writing or UCC exception.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The Statute of Frauds does not require the court to find that no oral agreement occurred. It bars enforcement of certain agreements unless the writing requirement or an exception is satisfied. A 600-basket sale at $2 each exceeds $500 and lacks a writing or exception.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because SOF is an enforceability defense, not simply a no-agreement rule. B: B is wrong because agreement on oral terms does not satisfy UCC §2-201 for goods over $500. C: C is wrong because the UCC SOF can defeat enforcement by way of action or defense.
