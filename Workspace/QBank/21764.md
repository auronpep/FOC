Question: A driver negligently ran a red light and struck a child in a crosswalk. The child's father was standing nearby, saw the collision as it happened, and immediately suffered severe distress with physical symptoms. The father was not personally in danger. The jurisdiction recognizes bystander NIED for close family members who contemporaneously observe the injury. Can the father recover for emotional distress?
Answer A: No, because he was not physically touched
Answer B: No, because only the injured child may recover any damages
Answer C: Yes, because he contemporaneously observed serious injury to his child
Answer D: Yes, because any person who sees an accident may recover
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The father satisfies the stated close-family and contemporaneous-observation requirements for bystander NIED.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The supplied bystander rule does not require physical impact on the bystander. B: The jurisdiction recognizes bystander recovery when close-family and contemporaneous-observation requirements are met. D: The rule is limited; the father recovers because of close family and contemporaneous observation, not mere witness status.
lb_rule_reference: Bystander NIED is commonly limited to plaintiffs who contemporaneously perceive serious injury to a close family member.
