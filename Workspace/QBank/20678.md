Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Mary agreed to design a set of architectural plans for John for $12,000. Mary completed and delivered the plans. John refused to pay after learning that similar plans might have cost only $9,500 from another designer. The plans met the contract requirements. What is Mary's proper recovery?
Answer A: $9,500, because damages for services are limited to reasonable market value.
Answer B: $2,500, the difference between the contract price and market value.
Answer C: $12,000, the contract price for Mary's completed performance.
Answer D: Nothing, because John found cheaper similar plans elsewhere.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: After full performance of a valid services contract, the direct expectation measure is the unpaid contract price. Reasonable value does not replace the agreed price merely because the buyer later finds a lower market rate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it substitutes restitution for the contract price. B: B is wrong because the market-price difference is not Mary's loss after full performance. D: D is wrong because a cheaper alternative discovered after performance does not erase John's payment duty.
lb_rule_reference: Common law expectation damages; full service performance
