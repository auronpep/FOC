Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Discretionary exclusion (undue prejudice, confusion, waste of time)
Question: In a federal negligence trial, Sarah calls four witnesses who each testify that the warehouse floor was wet before she fell. Sarah then seeks to call eight more witnesses to say the same thing from the same vantage point. The defendant objects under Rule 403. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because relevant evidence can never be excluded for being cumulative.
Answer B: Overrule the objection unless the eight witnesses are lying.
Answer C: Sustain the objection only if the defendant stipulates that the floor was wet.
Answer D: Sustain the objection if the additional testimony would be needlessly cumulative or waste time compared with its added probative value.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 403 permits exclusion when probative value is substantially outweighed by needlessly presenting cumulative evidence or wasting time. Once several witnesses have made the same point, more identical testimony may add little probative value.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 403 expressly includes cumulative evidence and waste of time. B: B is wrong because the issue is marginal probative value and time, not whether the witnesses are lying. C: C is wrong because a stipulation may affect probative need, but it is not the only basis for excluding cumulative testimony.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 403
lb_student_diagnostic: Student treats relevance as an absolute right to repeat proof.
