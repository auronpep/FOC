Question: Jacob sues Eve after slipping in Eve's market. Eve asks the court to take judicial notice that the floor was dry at the time of Jacob's fall because a local newspaper article quoted a customer saying, “The floor looked dry to me.” Jacob objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Refuse judicial notice because whether the floor was dry at the time of the fall is subject to reasonable dispute and the newspaper quote is not a source whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned for that fact.
Answer B: Take judicial notice because newspapers are always accurate sources for adjudicative facts.
Answer C: Take judicial notice because the statement was printed before trial.
Answer D: Take judicial notice unless Jacob proves the floor was wet by a preponderance of the evidence.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A disputed case-specific fact, such as the condition of a floor at the time of an accident, is not proper for judicial notice merely because a newspaper quoted someone about it. The fact is subject to reasonable dispute and the source does not conclusively establish it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B and C are wrong because publication in a newspaper does not make a disputed adjudicative fact indisputable. D is wrong because the party opposing notice does not bear a burden to disprove a fact that does not qualify for notice.
