Question: Abraham promised Ruth that he would pay Mary $1,000 if Ruth delivered ten shelves to Abraham by June 1. Ruth told Mary about the promise. Mary, relying on the promised payment, turned down another temporary job. Ruth delivered only six shelves by June 1 and never delivered the rest. Mary sued Abraham for the $1,000. What is the likely result?
Answer A: Mary wins because her reliance vested her rights and therefore Abraham cannot assert any contract condition.
Answer B: Mary wins because beneficiaries are protected from all defenses between the original parties after vesting.
Answer C: Mary loses because, although her rights vested, she can enforce only the promise Abraham made, and Abraham's duty to pay Mary was conditioned on Ruth's timely delivery of ten shelves.
Answer D: Mary loses because reliance can never vest a beneficiary's rights.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Mary's reliance may vest her rights against later modification, but vesting does not give her rights greater than the contract provides. Abraham promised to pay Mary only if Ruth delivered ten shelves by June 1. Because that condition was not satisfied, Abraham's payment duty did not mature.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because vesting blocks later modification; it does not erase conditions in the original promise. B: B is wrong because an intended beneficiary takes subject to contract defenses and limits. D: D is wrong because reasonable reliance can vest intended-beneficiary rights.
