Question: Sasha put poison in a tea cup intending to kill her business partner, Leo. Before Leo returned, Leo's assistant mistakenly drank the tea and died. Sasha was charged with murder of the assistant. Is Sasha guilty of murder of the assistant?
Answer A: No, because Sasha intended to kill Leo, not the assistant
Answer B: No, because poison requires the intended victim to consume it
Answer C: Yes, because Sasha's intent to kill Leo transfers to the assistant's completed homicide
Answer D: Yes, but only of involuntary manslaughter because the assistant drank by mistake
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Sasha intended to kill Leo and used poison for that purpose. The assistant died from the poisoned tea. Transferred intent supplies malice for the assistant's murder.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: In completed homicide, intent to kill the intended victim transfers to the actual victim who dies. B: The plan failed as to Leo but succeeded in causing a death. The intent transfers to the actual victim. D: Accidental victim identity does not reduce a completed intentional homicide to manslaughter.
lb_rule_reference: Intent to kill transfers to an unintended victim in a completed homicide.
