Outline: Rule of completeness
Question: In a murder trial, the prosecutor introduces part of Reuben's recorded statement: “The knife was mine.” The prosecutor stops the recording there. The next sentence says, “I sold it to Levi six months before the stabbing.” Reuben immediately asks that the second sentence be played. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Deny the request because the second sentence is hearsay and Reuben may not introduce it for its truth.
Answer B: Grant the request if the second sentence is necessary to correct the impression that Reuben admitted owning the knife at the time of the stabbing.
Answer C: Deny the request because the jury must decide ownership from the excerpt alone once it is admitted.
Answer D: Grant the request only as impeachment of the prosecutor's witness, not as context for the recorded statement.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The excerpt could mislead the jury into thinking Reuben admitted contemporaneous ownership. The next sentence directly qualifies the timing of ownership. If fairness requires it, Rule 106 permits the completing statement to be introduced at the same time, including over a hearsay objection when necessary.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 106 can allow a completing statement over a hearsay objection. C: C is wrong because the rule is designed to prevent the jury from being misled by an incomplete excerpt. D: D is wrong because the completing sentence is offered to give fair context to the recorded statement, not merely to impeach a witness.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 106 / misleading ownership admission
