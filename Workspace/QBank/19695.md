Question: Leah contracted with Samuel to sell Samuel a small printing press. Samuel promised Leah that he would pay Leah's father Jacob $4,000 after Leah delivered the press. Leah never delivered the press. Jacob sues Samuel for the $4,000. What is the likely result?
Answer A: Jacob recovers because the payment promise was made for his benefit.
Answer B: Jacob does not recover because Leah's delivery of the press was a constructive condition to Samuel's duty to pay Jacob.
Answer C: Jacob recovers because delivery of the press matters only between Leah and Samuel.
Answer D: Jacob does not recover only if he personally promised to deliver the press.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Jacob may have been an intended beneficiary, but Samuel's duty to pay was tied to Leah's delivery of the printing press. Because the promisee did not perform the constructive condition that made Samuel's payment duty mature, Samuel may assert that defense against Jacob.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because intended-beneficiary status does not override the performance sequence. C: C is wrong because the beneficiary takes the payment promise subject to conditions on Samuel's duty. D: D is wrong because Jacob need not be personally responsible for the nonperformance for Samuel's duty not to mature.
