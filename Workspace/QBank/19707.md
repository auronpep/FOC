Question: Samuel sends Jacob a written offer: “I will pay you $4,000 if you send me a written promise by Friday to build the stone wall next month. I will accept only your written promise, not beginning work.” Jacob receives the letter on Monday, buys stones on Tuesday, and starts clearing the site on Wednesday. He does not send a promise. On Thursday, Samuel revokes. Did Jacob accept before revocation?
Answer A: Yes, because beginning work always accepts any offer for services.
Answer B: Yes, because Jacob's purchase of stones was a vague but sufficient promise.
Answer C: No, because the offer unambiguously required a written promise and Jacob did not send one before revocation.
Answer D: No, but only because Jacob did not sign a formal contract prepared by a lawyer.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An offeror may prescribe the permitted method of acceptance. Samuel clearly required a written promise and excluded beginning work as acceptance. Jacob's preparations and site work did not accept before Samuel revoked.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A: beginning work is not always sufficient when the offer clearly requires another mode of acceptance.
