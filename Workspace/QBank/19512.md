Question: Final judgment is entered on March 1. On April 20, the losing party files a motion labeled "reconsideration" raising the same legal arguments the court already rejected. The party argues that any reconsideration motion automatically tolls the appeal time. Is that correct?
Answer A: Yes, every reconsideration motion tolls appeal time
Answer B: Yes, because Rule 60 is a substitute for appeal
Answer C: No, an untimely or nonqualifying post-judgment motion does not automatically toll the notice deadline
Answer D: No, because appeal deadlines never matter
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The appeal clock is affected only by timely qualifying motions.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Tolling depends on timing and rule classification, not labels alone. B: Rule 60 is not a routine appeal substitute. D: Notice deadlines are jurisdictionally significant in many federal appeals.
lb_rule_reference: Tolling depends on the type and timing of the post-judgment motion.
