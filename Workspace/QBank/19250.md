Question: Solomon bought a grain sorter from Hannah. The contract stated, “Payment is due only if Buyer is satisfied that the sorter processes 500 bushels per hour.” The sorter processed 520 bushels per hour in repeated tests. Solomon honestly disliked the sound of the machine and refused to pay, saying he was not satisfied. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: Solomon may refuse payment because any satisfaction clause gives unlimited subjective discretion.
Answer B: Solomon may refuse payment if he honestly dislikes any aspect of the machine.
Answer C: Hannah can recover only in restitution because the express condition failed.
Answer D: Solomon's satisfaction is judged by an objective commercial standard, and the payment condition is satisfied if the sorter met the required commercial performance.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A satisfaction clause involving commercial utility or mechanical performance is generally judged by objective reasonableness. The machine met the specified processing capacity, so Solomon's dislike of its sound does not prevent the payment duty from maturing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not all satisfaction clauses are purely subjective. B: B is wrong because honest dislike is not enough when the condition concerns commercial utility. C: C is wrong because the condition did not fail under the proper objective standard.
lb_rule_reference: Satisfaction condition / objective commercial utility
