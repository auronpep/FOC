Subtopic: Homicide
Outline: Manslaughter
Question: At a house party, Greg struck Lena in the head with a full bottle, knocking her unconscious. Lena regained consciousness approximately forty-five seconds later. Disoriented and in severe pain, she immediately picked up the broken bottle from the floor and stabbed Greg in the neck, killing him. There was no prior history of violence between them, and Lena had not anticipated the attack. What is the most serious homicide offense for which Lena can properly be convicted?
Answer A: First-degree murder, because Lena made a conscious decision to grab the bottle before stabbing Greg
Answer B: Second-degree murder, because the brief period of unconsciousness constituted a cooling-off period
Answer C: Voluntary manslaughter, because a serious physical battery is legally adequate provocation and Lena acted immediately upon regaining consciousness with no opportunity to cool
Answer D: No homicide, because Lena was acting in self-defense — she reasonably believed she was in continued danger from Greg
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Voluntary manslaughter requires: (1) adequate provocation, (2) actual passion, (3) no cooling-off period, and (4) a causal connection between provocation and killing. A serious physical battery — being struck unconscious with a bottle — is one of the paradigmatic categories of legally adequate provocation at common law. Lena was in fact provoked. She acted within seconds of regaining consciousness (no reasonable cooling-off period elapsed; the unconscious interval does not count). The killing was causally connected to the provocation. All four elements are satisfied. Voluntary manslaughter is the correct charge.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Premeditation requires prior cool reflection about the killing — a decision formed after deliberation. Lena regained consciousness forty-five seconds after a surprise assault and acted in immediate response. The act of grabbing a nearby object during a frenzied, pain-filled recovery from unconsciousness is not cool reflection. There is no premeditation on these facts. B: A cooling-off period must be a period during which a reasonable person could and would cool. Unconsciousness is not a cooling-off period — Lena was incapacitated, not regaining composure. She acted immediately upon regaining awareness, with no opportunity for her passions to subside. The cooling-off requirement is evaluated on the objective possibility of cooling, and an unconscious person cannot cool off. D: The facts do not establish an ongoing threat at the moment of the stabbing. Greg struck Lena once and she was unconscious; there is no indication Greg was continuing to attack when Lena regained consciousness. Self-defense requires a reasonable belief of imminent threat at the moment of the defensive act. A single completed blow — without any indication of an ongoing attack — likely does not support a reasonable belief in continued imminent threat when Lena was the one who then escalated. Self-defense is not established on these facts.
lb_rule_reference: A serious physical battery is one of the paradigmatic categories of legally adequate provocation at common law; when the defendant acts immediately upon regaining consciousness without a cooling-off period, voluntary manslaughter is the correct charge
