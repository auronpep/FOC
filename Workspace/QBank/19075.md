Subtopic: Other Crimes
Outline: Robbery
Question: After arresting Nia for robbery, officers placed her in the back of a patrol car without Miranda warnings. Knowing that Nia was worried about her younger brother, one officer said loudly to another, "If we cannot find the gun, her little brother might pick it up and get hurt." The officers had already searched the scene and knew the gun was not accessible to anyone. Nia immediately said, "It is under my aunt's porch." Should Nia's statement about the gun be suppressed from the prosecution's case-in-chief?
Answer A: No, because the officer did not ask Nia a direct question
Answer B: No, because Nia volunteered the location on her own
Answer C: Yes, because the officer's remark was the functional equivalent of interrogation while Nia was in custody
Answer D: Yes, because any statement about physical evidence automatically suppresses the evidence too
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Nia was under arrest and in custody. The officer exploited a known concern to prompt disclosure of the gun's location when no real emergency existed. That was interrogation without warnings, so the statement is excluded from the case-in-chief.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Interrogation includes the functional equivalent of questioning. The officers used a remark they should have known was likely to elicit a response from Nia. B: A statement is not volunteered when it is produced by police words designed or reasonably likely to elicit it. D: The call asks about Nia's statement. Physical fruits of a voluntary unwarned statement are a separate remedy question.
lb_rule_reference: Interrogation includes express questioning and its functional equivalent: police words or actions reasonably likely to elicit an incriminating response from the suspect.
