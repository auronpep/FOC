Question: A state licensing board adopts a rule requiring online tutors to obtain a license before offering paid lessons to state residents. The rule takes effect in 30 days, and the board has sent warning letters saying unlicensed tutors will face civil penalties. An online tutor who teaches state residents stops advertising there and sues before any penalty is imposed. Is the challenge likely ripe?
Answer A: No, because a plaintiff must violate the rule and be penalized before any pre-enforcement challenge is ripe
Answer B: Yes, because the rule is final enough for review and the tutor faces credible enforcement threats and immediate business costs
Answer C: No, because ripeness is impossible unless standing is absent
Answer D: Yes, solely because online tutoring involves speech
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The rule is concrete, enforcement is credibly threatened, and the tutor is already changing conduct. Fitness and hardship support ripeness.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Pre-enforcement review does not always require violating the law first. Credible threat and present compliance burdens can make the dispute ripe. C: They are distinct threshold doctrines. A plaintiff can have standing and still need to show the dispute is ripe. D: The ripeness answer turns on credible threat and present hardship, not merely the subject matter.
lb_rule_reference: Pre-enforcement review can be ripe when the issue is fit for decision and the plaintiff faces a credible threat of enforcement or immediate compliance costs.
