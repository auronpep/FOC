


BARMATRIX Q#: 22108
Outline_code: 85030100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Contracts for Land
Outline: Land-Sale Contracts
Question: A buyer bought a tract of land from the seller, agreeing to an installment contract whereby the buyer would pay the seller $1,000 a month for 14 months. After the final payment, the seller would furnish title to the buyer. During those 14 months, the seller gave the buyer permission to live on the land. Ten months into the contract, the buyer discovered that there was a mortgage on the land. The buyer also discovered that the seller was in financial trouble due to several business failures. The buyer asked the seller about the mortgage, and the seller said he'd pay it off sometime within the next four months. Because the buyer had reason to believe the seller would not be able to pay off the mortgage in four months, the buyer decided to withhold further installment payments until the seller paid off the mortgage. May the buyer withhold further installment payments?
Answer A: A: No, because the seller only had a duty to furnish marketable title when the buyer made his final payment.
Answer B: B: No, because the buyer was living on the land while making the installment payments.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the mortgage made the title unmarketable at the time of the contract.
Answer D: D: Yes, because the buyer reasonably believed that the seller would be unable to deliver marketable title.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: If a buyer buys land through an installment contract, the seller only has a duty to deliver marketable title when the buyer makes his or her final payment. Here, since the buyer still has four more payments to make, the seller does not have to deliver marketable title yet, and the buyer cannot withhold his installment payments. Thus, C and D are incorrect. B is incorrect because the fact that the buyer was living on the property does not change the analysis.
