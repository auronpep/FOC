Question: A company's manager emails in-house counsel asking whether a planned termination would violate employment law. The email includes the dates of the employee's warnings and attaches performance records. In later federal litigation, the employee requests the dates and records. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The employee may discover the underlying dates and records if otherwise discoverable, but not the confidential request for legal advice itself
Answer B: The employee may not discover the dates or records because sending them to counsel made them privileged
Answer C: The entire email and attachments must be produced because in-house counsel are business employees
Answer D: Privilege applies only if the manager copied outside counsel on the email
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Privilege protects the confidential legal communication, not the underlying nonprivileged facts or business records.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: A party cannot shield discoverable facts simply by telling them to a lawyer. C: Confidential communications seeking legal advice from in-house counsel can be privileged. D: Attorney-client privilege can apply to in-house counsel communications for legal advice.
lb_rule_reference: Attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications for legal advice, but it does not protect the underlying facts merely because they were communicated to a lawyer.
