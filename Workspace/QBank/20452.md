Question: In a federal assault trial, Samuel testifies that he has no connection to David, the alleged victim. The defense offers a lease signed by Samuel and David showing that they have shared an apartment for six years. The prosecution objects that the lease is extrinsic evidence on a collateral matter. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the lease because all extrinsic evidence about credibility is collateral.
Answer B: Exclude the lease unless Samuel first testifies about David's character for truthfulness.
Answer C: Admit the lease only to prove that Samuel is generally untruthful.
Answer D: Admit the lease because it tends to show Samuel's possible bias in favor of David.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The lease is not offered merely to show a collateral contradiction; it tends to show a relationship that may give Samuel a motive to favor David. Extrinsic proof of bias is generally permitted, subject to ordinary controls such as relevance and Rule 403.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because bias is not automatically collateral. B: B is wrong because character-for-truthfulness testimony is not required. C: C is wrong because the proper purpose is bias, not general untruthfulness.
lb_rule_reference: Bias impeachment / collateral matter limit does not bar central bias
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests collateral-bar overread when extrinsic evidence directly proves bias.
