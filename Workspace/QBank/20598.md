MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Other Torts
Outline: Invasion of privacy
Question: A beverage company hired an actor to imitate a well-known singer's distinctive hairstyle, voice, catchphrase, and stage outfit in a commercial. The ad never used the singer's real name or photo, and the singer did not consent. Viewers widely understood the actor to be evoking the singer to sell the drink. The singer sues for appropriation. Is the singer likely to prevail?
Answer A: No, because the ad did not use the singer's exact name or photograph
Answer B: No, because the commercial was flattering
Answer C: Yes, because the company used the singer's distinctive identity for commercial advantage without consent
Answer D: Yes, but only if the commercial stated a false fact about the singer
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The ad deliberately evoked the singer to sell a product. Unauthorized commercial identity use can support appropriation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Appropriation can cover identity use through a distinctive lookalike or imitation used for commercial advantage. B: Appropriation does not require reputational harm or insult. D: Appropriation does not require a false factual assertion.
lb_rule_reference: Appropriation can occur through unauthorized commercial use of a person's identity, including a distinctive lookalike or imitation that clearly evokes the plaintiff.
