Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Summary judgment motion
Question: Defendant moves for summary judgment one month after the complaint is filed, before any depositions. Plaintiff submits a Rule 56(d) declaration identifying three documents and two witnesses controlled by defendant that are needed to oppose the motion on causation. Plaintiff explains why those facts are unavailable without discovery. What may the court do?
Answer A: Grant summary judgment because Rule 56 never allows delay for discovery
Answer B: Defer or deny the motion to allow discovery if the Rule 56(d) showing is specific and justified
Answer C: Deny the request because a party must complete trial before summary judgment can be decided
Answer D: Convert the motion to a default judgment
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Plaintiff identified needed facts, why they matter, and why they cannot yet be presented.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 56(d) expressly allows relief when the nonmovant needs facts unavailable without discovery. C: Summary judgment is pretrial; Rule 56(d) concerns discovery needed to oppose it. D: Default has nothing to do with a discovery-based Rule 56(d) request.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 56(d) permits deferral or denial of summary judgment when the nonmovant shows by affidavit or declaration specific reasons it cannot present facts essential to oppose.
