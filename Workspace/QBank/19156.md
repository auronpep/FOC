Outline: Judicial notice
Question: In a federal breach-of-contract case, Timothy asks the court to take judicial notice that the national bird is the bald eagle. The fact is not subject to reasonable dispute, but neither party's claim or defense depends on it. Lydia objects that the noticed fact has no role in the case. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Refuse to take judicial notice because even an indisputable fact should not be noticed for jury use when it is not relevant to any fact of consequence in the case.
Answer B: Take judicial notice because every indisputable fact must be placed before the jury.
Answer C: Take judicial notice and instruct the jury to decide whether the national bird matters.
Answer D: Take judicial notice only in a criminal case, because civil juries are not allowed to receive noticed facts.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Judicial notice is a substitute for proof of qualifying adjudicative facts; it is not a device for placing irrelevant facts before the jury. If the fact has no bearing on any issue in the case, the court should not notice it for jury use merely because it is indisputable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because Rule 201 does not require courts to notice every indisputable fact. C: C is wrong because the jury should not be given irrelevant facts and asked to find a use for them. D: D is wrong because judicial notice is available in civil cases, and the civil instruction is conclusive when the fact is properly noticed.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 201 / relevance and adjudicative consequence
