Outline: Larceny
Question: Jon finds a wallet on a restaurant table. The wallet contains $300, a driver's license, and a business card with the owner's phone number. Jon immediately decides to keep the cash and throw away the wallet. Is Jon likely guilty of larceny of the cash?
Answer A: Yes, because the wallet contained clear clues to the owner and Jon intended to keep the cash when he took it
Answer B: No, because all lost property belongs to the finder
Answer C: No, because cash can never be the subject of larceny
Answer D: No, unless Jon first promised the owner he would return it
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The owner was identifiable at the moment Jon formed the intent to keep the property.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Lost property is not automatically free to keep when owner identity is reasonably knowable. C: Personal property, including cash, can be stolen. D: Larceny can occur without a promise when the finder takes identifiable lost property with intent to steal.
lb_rule_reference: A finder who takes lost property with intent to keep it can commit larceny when there are sufficient clues to the owner's identity at the time of taking.
