Question: A state provides full welfare benefits to long-term residents but limits new residents, for their first year, to the lower benefit amount they would have received in their prior state. A new resident challenges the rule. What is the strongest Equal Protection argument?
Answer A: The rule may penalize the fundamental right to interstate travel and trigger strict scrutiny
Answer B: Rational basis applies because public benefits are always discretionary
Answer C: Strict scrutiny applies only if the new resident crossed an international border
Answer D: The rule is valid because states may always favor long-term residents
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Disadvantaging new residents because they recently moved burdens the right to travel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Benefit rules can still burden fundamental rights. C: The constitutional right at issue is interstate travel. D: Penalizing new residents for moving states raises right-to-travel concerns.
lb_rule_reference: Laws penalizing new residents for exercising the right to interstate travel can trigger strict scrutiny.
