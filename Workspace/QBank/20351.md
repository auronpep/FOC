Outline: Mistake / Lack of Mutual Assent
Question: Naomi sells an old warehouse to Jacob. The contract states that Jacob buys the building “as is, including all hidden structural defects, whether known or unknown.” Both parties believe the roof beams are sound. Before signing, however, the beams had already been badly weakened by insects. Jacob later discovers the defect and seeks rescission for mutual mistake. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Grant rescission because both parties were mistaken about the beams.
Answer B: Grant rescission because hidden structural defects are always outside an as-is clause.
Answer C: Deny rescission because Jacob expressly assumed the risk of hidden structural defects.
Answer D: Deny rescission only if Naomi proves that Jacob inspected every beam personally.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Even a genuine mutual mistake does not permit avoidance when the adversely affected party bears the risk of the mistake. The contract expressly assigned unknown hidden structural defects to Jacob. That risk allocation defeats Jacob's mutual mistake defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because mutual mistake requires checking risk allocation before granting rescission. B: B is wrong because a properly framed as-is clause can allocate hidden-defect risk. D: D is wrong because Jacob's contractual assumption of risk controls without requiring proof that he inspected every beam.
