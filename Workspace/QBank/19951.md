Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Defense of property
Question: After several thefts from his storage shed, Pavel rigs a shotgun to fire when the shed door opens. He posts no warning. A teenager opens the shed intending to steal tools and is shot in the leg. Pavel is charged with aggravated battery. Is the spring gun justified as defense of property?
Answer A: Yes, because the teenager was committing theft
Answer B: Yes, because the device protected property while Pavel was absent
Answer C: No, because deadly force cannot be used solely to protect unattended property
Answer D: No, because defense of property applies only to homes, never sheds
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Pavel used a device capable of inflicting death or serious bodily harm to protect tools in an unattended shed. Defense of property does not justify that level of force where no person is threatened.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Theft of property does not justify deadly or seriously dangerous force. The property owner could not personally shoot a nonviolent thief solely to protect tools. B: A mechanical device cannot use more force than the owner would be privileged to use personally. Deadly force for unattended property is not allowed. D: Defense of property is not limited to homes. The problem is the deadly force, not the property's location.
lb_rule_reference: A property owner may not use a spring gun or similar device to inflict deadly force solely to protect property
