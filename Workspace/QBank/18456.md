Outline: Larceny by trick
Question: Using a fake driver's license, Pat rents a camera from a photography shop for one weekend. Pat signs a rental agreement promising to return the camera Monday, but he already plans to sell it online that night. What is Pat's best theft offense?
Answer A: False pretenses, because Pat used a false ID
Answer B: Larceny by trick, because Pat obtained possession by fraud while intending to steal
Answer C: Embezzlement, because Pat lawfully possessed the camera after the rental
Answer D: No offense, because the shop voluntarily handed him the camera
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The shop intended only a temporary rental. Pat's fraud induced possession, and he had intent to permanently deprive when he obtained it.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The shop transferred possession for the weekend, not title. That points to larceny by trick. C: Because Pat fraudulently obtained possession with intent to steal at the outset, larceny by trick is the better offense. D: Fraud vitiates clean consent to possession for larceny-by-trick purposes.
lb_rule_reference: When deception causes the owner to transfer possession but not title, the offense is larceny by trick if the defendant intended to steal at the time of the transfer.
