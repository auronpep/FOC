Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Mode and order of witnesses
Question: In a federal bench trial, Jacob has one remaining witness, Hannah, who will provide the only testimony about damages. At 4:55 p.m., the judge announces, “Court ends at 5:00, and no evidence will be taken tomorrow. I am excluding Hannah solely because I do not want to stay late.” Jacob objects and says Hannah can testify in fifteen minutes the next morning. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The exclusion is automatically proper because a judge has unlimited control over the trial schedule.
Answer B: The exclusion is likely improper because reasonable control over presentation does not permit arbitrary exclusion of important evidence solely for convenience.
Answer C: The exclusion is automatically proper because the trial is to the judge rather than a jury.
Answer D: The exclusion is proper unless Jacob proves Hannah's testimony would be undisputed.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Although the judge has broad control over the mode and order of presentation, that discretion must be reasonable. Excluding the only damages witness solely for convenience, when a short continuation is available, is likely an abuse of that control.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because trial-control discretion is not unlimited. C: C is wrong because a bench trial does not eliminate the need for a fair opportunity to present evidence. D: D is wrong because important contested testimony is not excluded merely because it may be disputed.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / arbitrary cutoff as abuse
