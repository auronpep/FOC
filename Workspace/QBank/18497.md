Question: During a state legislative committee hearing on contractor fraud, a witness falsely accused a contractor of falsifying invoices. The accusation was relevant to the committee's investigation. The witness made the statement because he disliked the contractor. The contractor sues for defamation. What is the likely privilege result?
Answer A: The statement is likely absolutely privileged because it was relevant testimony in a legislative proceeding
Answer B: The privilege is only qualified and is lost by ill will
Answer C: There is no privilege because the statement was false
Answer D: Absolute privilege applies only in newspapers
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Absolute privilege protects relevant legislative testimony even if the speaker acted with spite.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Ill will can matter for qualified privilege, but not for this absolute legislative privilege. C: Privilege can protect false statements in covered proceedings. D: Absolute privilege is tied to certain settings, not media format.
lb_rule_reference: Statements made in legislative proceedings are generally absolutely privileged when relevant to the proceeding, even if made with spite.
