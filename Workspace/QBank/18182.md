Question: A district court denies a defendant's ordinary motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim. The defendant seeks a writ of mandamus, arguing that trial will be expensive and that the judge probably made a legal mistake. Is mandamus likely available on those facts?
Answer A: Yes, because mandamus is available whenever a party thinks an interlocutory ruling is wrong
Answer B: Yes, because litigation expense alone creates mandamus jurisdiction
Answer C: No, because mandamus requires a clear and indisputable right and no adequate alternative remedy
Answer D: No, because federal courts abolished mandamus in civil cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The defendant can usually challenge ordinary legal error after final judgment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Mandamus is not a general error-correction device. B: Ordinary litigation burden is usually not enough for mandamus. D: Mandamus exists, but only in extraordinary circumstances.
lb_rule_reference: Mandamus is an extraordinary remedy requiring a clear and indisputable right and no adequate alternative means of relief.
