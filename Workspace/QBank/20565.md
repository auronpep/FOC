Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Laying a Foundation
Question: In a federal products-liability case, the plaintiff offers a loose metal bolt found in the courthouse parking lot the morning of trial. No witness identifies it, no markings connect it to the product, and no evidence shows where it came from. The plaintiff argues that the jury can decide whether it matters. The defendant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the bolt because there is no evidence sufficient to support a finding that it is connected to the product or the accident.
Answer B: Admit the bolt because any physical object may be considered by the jury.
Answer C: Admit the bolt because the opponent can argue about its weight.
Answer D: Exclude the bolt only if the defendant proves it came from another product.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Before a physical object is admitted, the proponent must present evidence sufficient to support a finding that it is what the proponent claims or is connected to the case. Here there is no witness, marking, or circumstance connecting the bolt to the product or accident.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because relevance and authentication require some connection. C: C is wrong because weight arguments come after a threshold foundation exists. D: D is wrong because the opponent need not prove an alternate source.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 901 / insufficient foundation for unidentified object
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests when the foundation gap is total rather than merely a weight issue.
