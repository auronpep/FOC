Outline: Clergy and penitent
Question: Ezra is both a pastor and a certified accountant. Deborah meets Ezra at his accounting office and asks how to classify income on her tax return. The meeting is billed as accounting work, not spiritual counseling. In a later federal tax case, Deborah objects to Ezra's testimony on clergy-penitent grounds. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection, because Ezra was acting as a secular accounting adviser rather than as clergy providing spiritual counseling.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because Ezra is ordained clergy regardless of the role he was serving.
Answer C: Sustain the objection, because tax advice always involves moral judgment.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the tax advice was negligent.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Clergy privilege turns on the capacity in which the person received the communication. Ezra was acting as an accountant in a secular business meeting, not as spiritual adviser. The decisive axis is role at the time of communication.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because This is spiritual-title shortcut. Clergy status alone does not privilege every conversation. C: C is wrong because A moral dimension does not transform accounting advice into penitential counseling. D: D is wrong because Negligence is irrelevant to whether the communication was made to clergy in spiritual capacity.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / clergy acting outside spiritual role
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is treating the speaker's title as dispositive and missing the secular-advice boundary.
