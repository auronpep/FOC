Subtopic: Performance, Breach, and Discharge
Outline: Conditions
Question: Miriam agreed to buy Noah's printing press. The contract stated, “This purchase is subject to Buyer's satisfactory inspection of the press by May 10.” Miriam inspected the press only briefly on May 5, then told Noah, “I am satisfied; I will close.” Noah later refused to deliver the press, arguing that a full inspection by May 10 never occurred. Who is most likely correct?
Answer A: Noah, because an express condition must occur exactly as written.
Answer B: Noah, because substantial performance cannot satisfy an express condition.
Answer C: Miriam, but only if she paid Noah new consideration to waive the inspection condition.
Answer D: Miriam, because the inspection condition was inserted for her protection and she could waive it.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The inspection condition protected Miriam as buyer. The party for whose benefit a condition is inserted may generally waive it and proceed with the contract. Noah cannot use Miriam's waived inspection condition as an excuse for his own refusal to deliver.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the nonbeneficiary cannot invoke a condition that the protected party has waived. B: B is wrong because the issue is waiver by the protected party, not substantial performance of the condition. C: C is wrong because waiver of a protective condition need not be supported by new consideration in this posture.
