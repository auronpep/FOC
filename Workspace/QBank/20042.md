Outline: Judicial notice
Question: In a federal case challenging a city ordinance, the judge reviews academic studies about whether curfews reduce late-night crime. The judge considers the studies while deciding the constitutional standard to apply. Caleb argues that Rule 201 requires the judge to instruct the jury to accept the studies as conclusive. Is Caleb correct?
Answer A: Yes, because all facts considered by a judge are adjudicative facts under Rule 201.
Answer B: Yes, because studies about crime are always generally known.
Answer C: No, but only because judicial notice is unavailable in constitutional cases.
Answer D: No, because Rule 201 governs judicial notice of adjudicative facts, not broad legislative or policy facts used in legal reasoning.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 201 governs judicial notice of adjudicative facts—case-specific facts about the parties and events. Broad social or policy material used in legal reasoning is legislative in nature and is not governed by Rule 201's jury-instruction command.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 201 does not cover every fact a judge considers. B: B is wrong because academic studies are not automatically generally known adjudicative facts. C: C is wrong because the issue is the type of fact, not a categorical bar in constitutional cases.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 201(a) / legislative fact outside rule
