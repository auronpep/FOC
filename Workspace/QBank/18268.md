Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Leah agreed in a signed contract to buy “standard crates” from Isaac. The contract contained a merger clause. In the trade, “standard crates” could mean either 20-inch crates or 24-inch crates. Leah offers negotiation emails showing that both parties used “standard crates” to mean 24-inch crates. How should the court treat the emails?
Answer A: Exclude the emails because a merger clause bars all outside evidence for every purpose.
Answer B: Admit the emails to explain the ambiguous term “standard crates.”
Answer C: Admit the emails only if Leah first proves Isaac breached the contract.
Answer D: Exclude the entire written contract because the term “standard crates” is ambiguous.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Extrinsic evidence may be used to explain an ambiguous term. The emails are not offered to add a new obligation but to clarify the meaning of an existing term. A merger clause does not automatically bar evidence used to interpret ambiguity.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because merger clauses do not bar all interpretive evidence. C: C is wrong because admissibility to interpret a term does not depend on first proving breach. D: D is wrong because ambiguity calls for interpretation, not automatic invalidation.
lb_rule_reference: Ambiguity explanation despite integration
lb_student_diagnostic: Student applies four-corners or merger-clause absolutism to explanatory evidence.
