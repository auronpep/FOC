Question: In a federal negligence trial, Paul testifies for Peter. On cross-examination, the defendant shows that Paul is Peter's brother and will receive part of any recovery. Peter then calls Lydia to testify that Paul has a reputation for truthfulness. The defendant objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because any credibility attack opens the door to truthful-character evidence.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because bias and untruthfulness are the same form of impeachment.
Answer C: Sustain the objection because evidence of family and financial bias does not by itself attack Paul's character for truthfulness.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Paul admitted that he is Peter's brother.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Bias impeachment shows a motive to shade testimony; it is not necessarily an attack on character for truthfulness. Rule 608(a) permits truthful-character support only after an attack on character for truthfulness. The bias evidence did not open that door.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not all credibility impeachment is truthfulness-character impeachment. B: B is wrong because bias and character for truthfulness are different credibility theories. D: D is wrong because the result does not turn on Paul's admission of the relationship.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 608(a) / bias attack does not open truthful-character support
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests purpose routing: bias does not automatically authorize opinion/reputation truthfulness rehabilitation.
