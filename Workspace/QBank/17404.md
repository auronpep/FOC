Question: A delivery company requires every driver to complete a standard accident report before leaving the scene of any collision, even when no claim is expected. Driver James completed the routine form after a minor crash. No lawyer had been contacted, and no litigation threat had been made. Is the report protected as work product?
Answer A: Yes, because accidents sometimes lead to litigation.
Answer B: Yes, because the company later sent the report to its lawyer.
Answer C: Yes, because the report contains facts helpful to trial preparation.
Answer D: No, because it was created in the ordinary course of business rather than because of anticipated litigation.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Work product protects materials prepared in anticipation of litigation or for trial. A routine business report required for every accident before litigation was anticipated is not work product merely because it later becomes useful. The decisive axis is purpose at creation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Potential litigation in the abstract is not enough; the report was routine. B: B is wrong because Sending a preexisting business document to a lawyer does not transform it into work product. C: C is wrong because Usefulness in litigation is not the same as creation because of litigation.
lb_rule_reference: FRCP 26(b)(3) / anticipation of litigation requirement
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is using later litigation value instead of the creation-purpose test.
