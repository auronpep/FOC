Question: After Levi is indicted for robbery, police place him in a live lineup with five fillers. Each participant repeats the robber's phrase while the victim listens from behind glass. Levi's counsel is not notified and no waiver is obtained. The victim identifies Levi by voice. What is Levi's strongest Sixth Amendment argument?
Answer A: The live voice lineup was a post-attachment corporeal identification procedure requiring counsel or waiver
Answer B: Counsel was unnecessary because the witness identified a voice rather than a face
Answer C: Counsel was unnecessary because photo arrays do not require counsel
Answer D: Counsel was unnecessary because robbery is a property offense
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The procedure was a live confrontation arranged for witness identification after attachment. Counsel protections apply to that critical stage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: This was not merely collecting an exemplar for analysis; it was a live identification confrontation with a witness. C: This was a corporeal lineup, not a photo display. D: The counsel rule turns on attachment and the identification procedure, not whether the crime is property-related.
lb_rule_reference: After attachment, a live lineup or similar corporeal confrontation for witness identification is a critical stage requiring counsel or a valid waiver.
