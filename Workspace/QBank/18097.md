Question: A former employer received a confidential reference request from one prospective employer. Instead of responding only to the requester, the former employer emailed the same false accusation of theft to 300 companies in the industry "so everyone would know." The employee sues for defamation. What is the best privilege analysis?
Answer A: Any qualified privilege may be lost because the employer excessively published beyond the reference purpose
Answer B: The employer has an absolute privilege for all job-reference statements
Answer C: The employer keeps the privilege because the first recipient had a legitimate interest
Answer D: The privilege is lost only if the statement was made in court
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A job-reference privilege is limited to proper recipients and purposes; blasting the accusation broadly abuses the privilege.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Job references are usually qualified, not absolute. C: Excessive publication to unrelated recipients can defeat the privilege. D: Court statements may be absolutely privileged; this is a qualified-privilege abuse issue.
lb_rule_reference: A qualified privilege can protect proper-purpose communications, but it can be lost through excessive publication beyond the privilege's scope.
