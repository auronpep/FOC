Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Materiality and probative value
Question: Rebecca sues a theater after a ceiling tile fell during a show. Rebecca offers testimony that, one week after the accident, several tiles in the same ceiling area were sagging and water-stained. The theater objects that the later sagging does not prove the ceiling looked the same on the night of the accident. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because later condition evidence is always irrelevant.
Answer B: Exclude the testimony unless Rebecca proves no water entered the ceiling after the accident.
Answer C: Admit the testimony because a later similar condition in the same area may tend to show an ongoing condition; the possibility of change affects weight.
Answer D: Admit the testimony only if the theater denies ownership of the ceiling.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A later condition can be relevant to an earlier condition when the nature of the condition suggests continuity and the evidence concerns the same location. Possible changes after the event affect the strength of the inference.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because later condition evidence is not categorically irrelevant. B: B is wrong because the proponent need not eliminate every possible later change at the threshold stage. D: D is wrong because ownership is not the only issue for which ceiling-condition evidence may matter.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: Over-excluding later condition evidence because it is not identical-in-time proof.
