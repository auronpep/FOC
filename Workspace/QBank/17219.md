Question: Naomi signed a two-year agreement with a supplier for “all replacement HVAC filters required for Naomi's hotel renovations,” with an estimate of 8,000 filters. The supplier also agreed to perform ordinary filter installation, but the price schedule allocated most of the total price to the filters. Naomi later bought 2,500 replacement filters from a competitor for the same renovations and argued that the agreement was unenforceable because it included installation services and no exact quantity. Which statement best identifies the contract-content result?
Answer A: The agreement is governed by services law because installation was included, so the UCC requirements rule cannot apply.
Answer B: The agreement is unenforceable because a requirements contract must state an exact quantity.
Answer C: Article 2 likely applies because the predominant purpose was the sale of goods, and the requirements term can supply quantity through Naomi's good-faith renovation needs.
Answer D: Naomi may buy from competitors because an estimate is not the same thing as an exclusive source promise.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A mixed transaction is routed by its predominant purpose, and a goods requirements term can supply quantity through the buyer's actual good-faith requirements. The trigger facts are the filter-centered price allocation, incidental installation service, all-required-filters language, and Naomi's competitor purchases for the same renovations. The decisive lock is source law first, then quantity: Article 2 likely governs, and the requirements term is not invalid merely because it lacks a fixed number.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because incidental installation does not defeat Article 2 when the sale of filters predominates. B: B is wrong because Article 2 recognizes requirements quantity. D: D is wrong because the all-requirements language obligates Naomi to source covered requirements from the supplier in good faith even though the estimate is not a minimum.
lb_rule_reference: Predominant-purpose source-law lock; UCC 2-306(1) requirements quantity
