Question: Joshua signed and recorded an instrument granting Caleb, the owner of adjoining Lot 1, “an easement prohibiting Joshua and Joshua's successors from excavating below the marked support line along Lot 2's boundary.” The instrument gives Caleb no right to enter Lot 2. What is the best classification of Caleb's right?
Answer A: An affirmative easement because any easement must allow entry onto the servient land.
Answer B: An express negative easement because Caleb may prevent a specified use of the burdened land.
Answer C: A revocable license because Caleb has no right to enter Lot 2.
Answer D: A profit because the restriction concerns excavation beneath the surface.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A negative easement gives the holder the right to prevent the servient owner from using land in a specified way. The recorded instrument restrains excavation below a support line rather than allowing Caleb to enter Lot 2.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every easement authorizes entry. C: C is wrong because a recorded easement restriction can create a property right even without an entry privilege. D: D is wrong because a profit is a right to remove resources, not a right to stop excavation.
lb_rule_reference: Negative easement / restraint on servient use
