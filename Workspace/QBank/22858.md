


BARMATRIX Q#: 22858
Outline_code: 94010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Question: A logging corporation filed suit in State B federal court against an unincorporated environmental association after members of the association destroyed several logging trucks in State D. The destruction of the trucks caused $80,000 in damages. The logging corporation was incorporated in State A and had its principal place of business in State B. The environmental association had its headquarters in State C, and all of its officers were State C citizens. The environmental association had members who were citizens of State A, State B, State C, and State D. Is the State B federal court the correct court to hear the claim?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the association is a citizen of State C, where its headquarters are located.
Answer B: B: Yes, because all of the association's officers are State C citizens.
Answer C: C: No, because there is insufficient diversity between the parties.
Answer D: D: No, because the trucks were destroyed in State D.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: For unincorporated associations, the citizenship of the association is the citizenship of any of its members. Here, some of its members are State A and State B citizens, while the logging corporation is a citizen of where it is incorporated, State A, and its principal place of business, State B. Consequently, there is no diversity between the parties to support federal jurisdiction. A and B are incorrect because the association's citizenship is based on the citizenship of all of its members. D is incorrect, because the issue here is the lack of diversity between the parties.
