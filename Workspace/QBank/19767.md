Outline: Robbery
Question: After indictment for robbery, police approach Lena in jail, give Miranda warnings, and ask if she is willing to talk without her lawyer present. Lena says she understands and signs a written waiver. She then confesses to the charged robbery. Lena moves to suppress solely because police initiated the interview after indictment. What is the best answer?
Answer A: Suppression is automatic because police initiated contact after indictment
Answer B: The statement may be admissible because Lena knowingly and voluntarily waived counsel before questioning
Answer C: The statement must be suppressed because Miranda warnings can never support a Sixth Amendment waiver
Answer D: The statement is admissible only if Lena's lawyer gave written permission
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The right had attached, but Lena received warnings and affirmatively waived counsel. A valid waiver can permit questioning about the charged offense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A represented or charged defendant can waive the Sixth Amendment right if the waiver is valid. Police initiation alone is not an automatic bar. C: Although distinct, warnings and a waiver can establish a valid waiver of the Sixth Amendment right to counsel. D: The defendant can personally waive the right if the waiver is valid.
lb_rule_reference: After attachment, a defendant may waive the Sixth Amendment right during police-initiated questioning if the waiver is knowing, voluntary, and intelligent.
