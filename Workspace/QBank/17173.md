Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Esther fully performed a services contract for Jonah at a contract price of $18,000. The reasonable value of Esther's services was $25,000. Jonah refuses to pay. Esther seeks $25,000, arguing that she relied on the contract by doing work worth that amount. What is Esther's proper contract recovery?
Answer A: $18,000, the agreed contract price, because she fully performed and expectation is readily measured.
Answer B: $25,000, because reliance damages always use reasonable value of services.
Answer C: Nothing, because full performance eliminates reliance damages.
Answer D: $7,000, the difference between reasonable value and contract price.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: When a valid contract has been fully performed by the plaintiff and the agreed price is due, the ordinary contract recovery is the contract price. Reasonable value is a restitution concept and should not be used to exceed the agreed price simply by calling it reliance.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because reasonable value is not the reliance measure and would override the agreed price. C: C is wrong because Esther can recover the contract price. D: D is wrong because there is no separate recovery for the excess of reasonable value over the bargain price.
lb_rule_reference: Expectation available / reliance measure not substitute for higher reasonable value
