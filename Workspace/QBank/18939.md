Outline: Prior inconsistent statement
Question: In a federal civil trial, Timothy testifies on Monday and is excused subject to recall. On Wednesday, after Timothy has left the state for emergency surgery and cannot return, the defendant discovers an audio recording in which Timothy said the opposite of his Monday testimony. The defendant offers the recording for impeachment. The plaintiff objects that Timothy has not yet had an opportunity to explain or deny the statement. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The recording must be excluded in all circumstances because Timothy did not first explain or deny it.
Answer B: The recording must be admitted automatically because Timothy is unavailable.
Answer C: The recording may be admitted only if the court orders otherwise under Rule 613(b), considering the circumstances and fairness.
Answer D: The recording is substantive evidence simply because Timothy cannot return.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 613(b) generally requires that the witness receive an opportunity to explain or deny a prior inconsistent statement before extrinsic evidence is admitted, but the rule includes an unless-the-court-orders-otherwise safety valve. Timothy's unavailability after later discovery of the recording does not create automatic admission or substantive use; it creates a discretionary procedural question for the court.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Rule 613(b) expressly allows the court to order otherwise. B: B is wrong because unavailability does not make extrinsic impeachment automatic. D: D is wrong because unavailability does not convert impeachment material into substantive evidence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 613(b) / court orders otherwise exception
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the court-ordered exception to the prior opportunity requirement under current Rule 613(b).
