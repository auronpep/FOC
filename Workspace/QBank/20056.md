Question: A state-run nursing school admits only women. In litigation, the state argues for the first time that women are "naturally more caring," though the admission rule was adopted decades earlier for no documented reason. What is the strongest Equal Protection objection?
Answer A: The state may rely on any conceivable rationale because gender classifications receive rational basis
Answer B: The justification rests on gender stereotypes and appears post hoc, which is not enough under intermediate scrutiny
Answer C: The school is valid because education programs may always separate by sex
Answer D: Strict scrutiny applies because gender is identical to race for tier purposes
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Overbroad stereotypes and post hoc rationalizations do not satisfy the required justification.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Sex classifications receive intermediate scrutiny and require an exceedingly persuasive justification. C: Sex separation by a state actor still requires constitutional justification. D: The usual tier for sex classifications is intermediate scrutiny.
lb_rule_reference: A sex classification requires an exceedingly persuasive justification; the government cannot rely on overbroad generalizations or purely post hoc rationalizations.
