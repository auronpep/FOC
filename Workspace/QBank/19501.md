Question: After indictment for forgery, police obtain a court order requiring Mira to write sample signatures. No eyewitness is present. An expert later compares the samples to a forged check. Mira argues the expert comparison must be excluded because counsel was absent when the samples were taken. What is the best answer?
Answer A: The counsel-at-lineup rule does not apply because this was physical exemplar collection, not a witness confrontation
Answer B: The evidence must be excluded because every identification-related procedure requires counsel after indictment
Answer C: The evidence must be excluded because handwriting is testimonial
Answer D: The evidence is admissible only if the expert was present when Mira was charged
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Sixth Amendment lineup rule protects counsel at live identification confrontations. Taking handwriting exemplars for expert comparison is not that kind of critical stage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Attachment does not make every evidence-gathering step a critical stage. C: Handwriting samples generally reveal physical characteristics, not testimonial admissions. D: Expert presence at charging has nothing to do with the lineup-counsel doctrine.
lb_rule_reference: Counsel-at-lineup doctrine applies to post-attachment corporeal confrontations with witnesses, not ordinary collection or display of physical exemplars for expert comparison.
