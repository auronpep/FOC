Question: In a federal robbery trial, the prosecutor offers a small canvas bag containing dye-stained bills. Ruth testifies that she saw Caleb run from the bank, drop a canvas bag beside a tree, and continue running. An officer found a canvas bag with dye-stained bills beside that same tree less than two minutes later. Caleb objects that Ruth did not keep her eyes on the bag until the officer arrived. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the bag because the prosecutor must prove by a preponderance that it is the exact bag Caleb dropped.
Answer B: Exclude the bag because any gap in continuous observation destroys relevance.
Answer C: Admit the bag if the evidence is sufficient for a reasonable juror to find that it is the bag Caleb dropped; the observation gap affects weight.
Answer D: Admit the bag only if Caleb admits that he dropped it.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The bag's relevance depends on a conditional fact: whether it is the same bag Caleb dropped. The proponent need only introduce evidence sufficient to support a finding of that fact. Ruth's observation, the location, and the short time gap support admission; the defense can argue weight.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because conditional relevance does not require the judge to be persuaded by a preponderance. B: B is wrong because a minor observation gap does not automatically defeat admissibility. D: D is wrong because a party admission is not required; circumstantial proof can connect the object to the event.
