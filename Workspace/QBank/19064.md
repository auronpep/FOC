Outline: Responsibility
Question: Felix takes a prescription sleep medication. The label warns in bold print: "May cause hallucinations and impaired judgment. Do not drive or handle firearms." Felix reads the label, takes double the prescribed dose for recreation, hallucinates, and fires a gun through a neighbor's window. He claims involuntary intoxication. Is Felix's intoxication likely involuntary?
Answer A: Yes, because the medication was prescribed
Answer B: Yes, because hallucinations were an unusual side effect
Answer C: No, because he knowingly accepted the intoxicating risk and misused the medication
Answer D: No, because involuntary intoxication is never recognized
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Felix read a clear warning about hallucinations and impaired judgment, then took double the dose recreationally. The intoxication is treated as voluntary, so he cannot use the broader involuntary-intoxication defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Felix knowingly ignored a clear warning and doubled the dose for recreation. That is not involuntary intoxication. B: The hallucination risk was expressly warned, and Felix read the warning. The effect was legally foreseeable to him. D: Involuntary intoxication is recognized in proper cases, such as coerced or unknowing ingestion. Felix simply lacks those facts.
lb_rule_reference: Intoxication from medicine is not involuntary when the defendant knowingly takes it after clear warnings of intoxicating effects
