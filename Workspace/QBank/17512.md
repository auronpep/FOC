Subtopic: Defenses to Enforceability
Outline: Mistake / Lack of Mutual Assent
Question: John buys a delivery cart from Peter. The final written contract says nothing about free maintenance. After the contract is signed, John says he assumed maintenance was included because other sellers sometimes include it. Peter never discussed maintenance with John. John asks the court to reform the contract to add a free-maintenance term. Should the court reform the contract?
Answer A: Yes, because reformation can add any term the buyer expected.
Answer B: No, because reformation corrects a writing that fails to reflect the parties' actual agreement; it does not add a term never agreed to.
Answer C: Yes, because silence in a contract always creates ambiguity.
Answer D: No, but only because delivery carts are goods.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Reformation is available to correct a writing that does not express the parties' actual agreement because of mistake. It is not a device to add a term that one party merely assumed but that was never agreed upon.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a party's expectation alone is not enough for reformation. C: C is wrong because contractual silence does not automatically create an ambiguity requiring an added term. D: D is wrong because the remedy limit applies regardless of whether the subject is goods or services.
lb_rule_reference: Reformation denied absent prior agreement
