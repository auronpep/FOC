Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Ruth contracted to design a stage for Naomi's theater. Before Naomi repudiated, Ruth spent $6,000 buying software and drafting preliminary plans. Naomi never received the plans and obtained no benefit from Ruth's work. Ruth cannot prove lost profits with reasonable certainty. Which recovery best fits Ruth's claim?
Answer A: Reliance damages for Ruth's reasonable preparation expenditures, subject to any appropriate offsets.
Answer B: Restitution measured by the benefit Naomi received from the plans.
Answer C: The full contract price, even though Ruth did not complete performance.
Answer D: No recovery because Naomi received no benefit.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages reimburse expenditures made in preparation for performance when expectation is uncertain. Restitution is based on a benefit conferred on the defendant. Naomi received no benefit, so restitution is not the right route, but Ruth may recover reasonable reliance expenditures.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because Naomi received no benefit from the plans. C: C is wrong because Ruth did not complete performance and cannot simply recover the full contract price. D: D is wrong because lack of benefit defeats restitution, not necessarily reliance damages.
