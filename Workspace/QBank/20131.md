Question: At midnight, Shane hears a burglar in his living room. Shane grabs a handgun and shouts. The burglar drops a tablet computer and runs out the front door with empty hands. After the burglar is outside and sprinting down the sidewalk, Shane fires and kills him. Shane is charged with murder. Does castle doctrine most likely justify Shane's shooting?
Answer A: Yes, because a person has no duty to retreat inside his home
Answer B: Yes, because burglary is a serious felony
Answer C: No, because the burglar had fled and no imminent person-safety threat remained
Answer D: No, because deadly force is never allowed inside a dwelling
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Castle doctrine affects the duty to retreat in the home; it does not erase the requirements of necessity, imminence, and proportionality. Shane fired after the burglar had dropped the property, left the home, and was running away. The shooting was not necessary to protect persons from imminent danger.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: No duty to retreat does not mean unlimited deadly force. By the time Shane fired, the burglar had fled and posed no imminent threat to persons. B: The facts at the moment of force matter. Once the burglar was outside, unarmed, and fleeing, deadly force was not necessary to prevent imminent death or serious injury. D: Deadly force may be justified inside a dwelling when the defendant reasonably faces imminent death, serious bodily harm, or qualifying violent intrusion. The problem is that those facts had ended.
lb_rule_reference: Castle doctrine may remove a retreat duty in the home, but it does not authorize disproportionate deadly force when the threat to persons has ended
