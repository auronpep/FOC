


BARMATRIX Q#: 22816
Outline_code: 95070705
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Pretrial Procedures
Outline: Preliminary injunctions, permanent injunctions, and temporary restraining orders
Question: An internationally famous artist entered into a five- year lease in a city building as part of the city's downtown revitalization project. One year into the lease, the artist informed the city that she was moving out of the building because the city had not completed promised renovations. In response, the city sued the artist in federal court. The city asked for a permanent injunction to prevent the artist from breaking the lease. In her answer, the artist included a counterclaim for losses caused by the city's failure to do the renovations. The artist demanded a jury trial on her counterclaim. Both the artist and the city moved that their claim should be heard first. How should the court rule?
Answer A: A: The city's claim should be heard first.
Answer B: B: The artist's counterclaim should be heard first.
Answer C: C: The court should schedule a jury trial of both claims at the same time.
Answer D: D: The court should schedule a nonjury trial of both claims at the same time.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: In civil actions, the usual form of legal relief is money damages. However, a party can request the equitable remedy of an injunction to either prohibit the other party from doing something or to order the other party to do something. Importantly, a court may only award equitable relief if legal relief is inadequate under the circumstances. Here, the court should first determine whether the artist's request for legal relief (money) is an adequate remedy before ruling on the equitable remedy of a permanent injunction to prevent the artist from breaking the lease. Thus, A is incorrect. C is incorrect because the city did not request a jury trial on its claim under Fed. R. Civ. P. 38(b). D is incorrect because the artist did request a jury trial on its claim under Fed. R. Civ. P. 38(b).
