Subtopic: General Principles
Outline: Duress
Question: Kara's ex-partner threatens to hurt her unless she delivers stolen packages by midnight. He leaves her alone for six hours with her phone and car. A police station is two blocks away. Kara is afraid but does not call police or leave; she delivers the packages and is charged with theft. Is Kara likely to establish duress?
Answer A: Yes, because the threat involved serious harm
Answer B: Yes, because fear alone makes alternatives unreasonable
Answer C: No, because she had a reasonable opportunity to seek help or escape before committing the crime
Answer D: No, because duress applies only to threats made at gunpoint
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Even assuming the threat was serious, duress fails when the defendant has a reasonable legal alternative. Kara was left alone for six hours with practical means to get help.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Duress also requires no reasonable legal alternative. Kara had hours, a phone, a car, and nearby police. B: Fear does not automatically eliminate legal alternatives. The question asks whether a reasonable safe alternative existed. D: Duress does not require a gun. It requires imminent death or serious bodily harm and no reasonable legal alternative.
lb_rule_reference: Duress requires no reasonable legal alternative; a safe opportunity to escape or contact authorities defeats the defense
