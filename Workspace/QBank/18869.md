Outline: Form and scope of examination
Question: In a federal products-liability trial, Daniel testifies on direct only that he saw a ladder collapse. On cross-examination, the defendant asks Daniel about a separate conversation with the manufacturer's engineer concerning a different ladder design. The question does not bear on Daniel's credibility. The plaintiff objects that the question is beyond the scope of direct. What is the best ruling?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because cross-examination may cover any relevant issue as of right.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because cross-examination may never go beyond the words used on direct.
Answer C: The court may restrict the question as beyond direct and not credibility, or allow it in its discretion as if on direct examination.
Answer D: The court must allow the question only if it is phrased without leading words.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Cross-examination should not go beyond the subject matter of direct and credibility, but the court may allow inquiry into additional matters as if on direct. The new ladder-design conversation is new substantive matter, not credibility, so the court has discretion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A overstates cross-examination as wide open as of right. B understates the court's discretion to allow additional matters. D confuses scope with leading form. B: A overstates cross-examination as wide open as of right. B understates the court's discretion to allow additional matters. D confuses scope with leading form. D: A overstates cross-examination as wide open as of right. B understates the court's discretion to allow additional matters. D confuses scope with leading form.
