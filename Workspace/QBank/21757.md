Outline: Marital Communications Privilege
Question: While Jacob and Rachel were engaged but not yet married, Jacob privately told Rachel, “I used a false name on the loan papers.” They married a month later and divorced before trial. In a federal bank-fraud prosecution, Jacob objects to Rachel repeating the statement under the marital communications privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because Jacob and Rachel later married.
Answer B: Sustain the objection, because the communication was private and romantic.
Answer C: Overrule the objection, because the communication was not made during a marriage.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only because the later divorce ended every spousal privilege.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Marital communications privilege protects confidential communications made during a valid marriage. A statement made while the parties were engaged does not become privileged merely because they later marry. The decisive axis is timing of the communication.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Later marriage does not retroactively convert premarital communications into marital communications. B: B is wrong because Privacy alone is insufficient; the privilege is tied to the marital relationship at the time of communication. D: D is wrong because This gives the wrong reason. Divorce does not necessarily end the communication privilege, but this statement never qualified.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / timing of marital communication
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is focusing on privacy and later relationship status instead of when the protected communication was made.
