Question: Sarah contracted with Peter to remodel Sarah's house. Peter promised Sarah that he would also build a wheelchair ramp at Mary's home because Mary had loaned Sarah money for the project. Peter refused to build the ramp. Mary filed suit against Peter to enforce the ramp promise. Two days after Mary filed suit, Sarah and Peter signed a release eliminating the ramp obligation. What is the effect of the release on Mary's claim?
Answer A: The release bars Mary's claim because original contracting parties can always rescind a beneficiary promise.
Answer B: The release does not bar Mary's claim because Mary's rights vested when she filed suit before the release.
Answer C: The release bars Mary's claim unless Mary paid Peter separate consideration.
Answer D: The release automatically turns Mary into Sarah's assignee.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Filing suit is a classic vesting event for an intended beneficiary. Because Mary filed suit before Sarah and Peter released the ramp obligation, the later release cannot cut off Mary's vested right without her consent.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the original parties' modification power is limited after vesting. C: C is wrong because Mary's enforcement right does not depend on her separate consideration. D: D is wrong because vesting does not convert beneficiary status into assignment.
lb_rule_reference: Common law third-party beneficiary — vesting by suit
