Subtopic: Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions
Outline: Attorney-client
Question: Corporate counsel Deborah interviews Mark, a plant supervisor at Shepherd Supply, Inc., during an internal investigation. Deborah tells Mark that she represents the company, that she is gathering information to give legal advice to the company, and that the interview concerns actions within Mark's job duties. Deborah never agrees to represent Mark personally. After Mark leaves the company, he wants to disclose the interview over the company's objection. Who controls the attorney-client privilege for Mark's interview with Deborah?
Answer A: The corporation controls the privilege because Mark's communication to corporate counsel was made for the purpose of obtaining legal advice for the entity, and Mark was not personally represented.
Answer B: Mark controls the privilege because he was the person who made the statements during the interview.
Answer C: Deborah controls the privilege personally because she conducted the interview and recorded the legal-advice information.
Answer D: No attorney-client privilege can cover the interview because Mark was a supervisor rather than a member of the board or senior management.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Corporate attorney-client privilege can protect employee communications to corporate counsel when the communications are made so counsel can give legal advice to the entity, concern matters within the employee's corporate duties, and the employee understands the legal-advice purpose. The holder is the corporation, not the employee communicator, absent facts showing personal representation. The decisive axis is holder control in an entity-client setting.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the communicator is not necessarily the privilege holder in a corporate investigation. Mark supplied information for counsel to advise the company. C: C is wrong because the lawyer may assert privilege for the client but does not personally own the privilege. D: D is wrong because corporate privilege is not limited to board members or senior management; lower and middle employees may possess information counsel needs to advise the corporation.
lb_rule_reference: Corporate attorney-client privilege and holder control
lb_student_diagnostic: Student fails to separate the speaker, the lawyer, and the entity holder.
