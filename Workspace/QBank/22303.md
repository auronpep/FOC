Outline_code: 75110100
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Criminal Law and Procedure
Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Eighth Amendment
Question: The defendant was convicted of writing a bad check, a crime that carried a fiveyear jail sentence. However, because it was his seventh nonviolent felony conviction in 15 years, he was sentenced to life imprisonment without possibility of parole under a state recidivist statute. The defendant's life sentence could be commuted by the state governor. The defendant argued that his sentence was unconstitutional. Was the defendant's sentence constitutional?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the defendant had committed six prior felonies.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the life sentence could be commuted by the state governor.
Answer C: C: No, because the primary crime only carried a five- year sentence.
Answer D: D: No, because the sentence was grossly disproportionate to the crimes committed.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A criminal sentence violates the Eighth Amendment's prohibition against cruel and unusual punishment if the sentence is grossly disproportionate to the crime committed. Solem v. Helm, 463 U.S. 277 (1983). A life sentence without the possibility of parole is grossly disproportionate to the crime of writing seven bad checks. While writing a bad check was a felony, the felony was a nonviolent one. A is incorrect because the sentence was grossly disproportionate to the crime. B is incorrect because the possibility of the governor commuting the sentence would not make the life sentence itself constitutional. C is incorrect because the sentence carried by the primary crime would not change the analysis.
