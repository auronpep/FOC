Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Refreshing recollection
Question: In a federal civil trial, Lydia cannot remember the number of boxes she delivered to a store. Her lawyer shows her a delivery slip. After reading it silently, Lydia says, “That refreshes my memory. I delivered 40 boxes.” She then testifies from present memory. The opponent objects that Lydia is merely reading hearsay from a document. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because a witness may never use a writing to revive memory.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless the delivery slip itself is admitted as a business record.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because Lydia's refreshed present memory, not the slip's contents, is the evidence.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the opponent is forbidden from inspecting the slip.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness may use a writing to refresh memory. If the refresh succeeds, the testimony comes from the witness's present recollection. The writing is not automatically admitted for the truth of its contents, although the adverse party may have Rule 612 rights if the writing was used while testifying.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because refreshing memory is permitted. B: B is wrong because the testimony does not depend on admitting the delivery slip. D: D is wrong because Rule 612 gives the adverse party inspection rights when the writing is used while testifying.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 612 / present memory after refresh
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the difference between refreshed present recollection and document-content proof.
