Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: Miriam sues under a written lease and quotes several lease provisions in her complaint. The defendant attaches the complete lease to a Rule 12(b)(6) motion. Miriam argues the court must convert the motion to summary judgment because the lease was attached by the defendant. Must the court convert solely for considering the complete lease?
Answer A: Yes, every attachment to a motion requires conversion
Answer B: Yes, because leases are never considered at pleading stage
Answer C: No, if the lease is integral to and incorporated by the complaint
Answer D: No, because courts may weigh all evidence on Rule 12(b)(6)
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The complaint relies on the lease; considering the complete document may remain within the pleading-stage record.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Incorporated or integral documents can be considered without conversion. B: The category is not automatically excluded. D: The court may not turn 12(b)(6) into evidence weighing without conversion safeguards.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 12(d) conversion is triggered by outside matters considered, but incorporated or integral documents may be considered in limited circumstances.
