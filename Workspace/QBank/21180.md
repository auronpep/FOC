Question: A state enacts a statute providing that new state residents are not eligible for the state's general public assistance program until they have resided in the state for 12 continuous months. The stated purpose is to reduce the fiscal burden of new residents on the state's welfare system and to deter "welfare migration" — moving to the state specifically to access its more generous benefit levels. A family that recently moved from another state to take advantage of lower housing costs — not for welfare reasons — is denied benefits during their first year solely based on the durational residency requirement. Under Equal Protection analysis, what standard applies to the durational residency requirement for welfare benefits, and how should the state's fiscal concerns be evaluated?
Answer A: Rational basis, because the state has broad authority over public benefits eligibility, and fiscal conservancy is a legitimate state interest
Answer B: Intermediate scrutiny, because the durational residency requirement affects interstate migration, which is a quasi-fundamental right warranting an elevated but not strict level of review
Answer C: Strict scrutiny, because the durational residency requirement burdens the fundamental right to interstate travel; the state's fiscal concerns and anti-welfare-migration interest, while real, are not compelling interests served by narrowly tailored means
Answer D: The durational residency requirement is constitutional because the state may limit benefits to residents with stronger ties to the state, and encouraging civic integration before benefit receipt is a compelling interest
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The Supreme Court has held that durational residency requirements for welfare benefits burden the fundamental right to travel — specifically, the right to be treated as a full member of a state's community upon arrival. The Court has struck down one-year residency requirements for welfare because the state's fiscal and deterrence interests do not constitute compelling interests sufficient to justify penalizing new residents for having recently exercised the right to travel.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Durational residency requirements for public benefits burden the fundamental right to interstate travel. When a state withholds benefits from new residents that it provides to established residents, it effectively penalizes the exercise of the right to travel. Strict scrutiny applies, not rational basis. B: The right to interstate travel is a recognized fundamental right, not a quasi-fundamental right. It receives strict scrutiny. The Supreme Court specifically struck down one-year durational residency requirements for welfare benefits as violating the fundamental right to travel. There is no intermediate-scrutiny tier for fundamental rights under Equal Protection. D: The Supreme Court has specifically rejected fiscal concerns and benefit-deterrence as compelling interests that justify durational residency requirements for welfare. The interest in discouraging welfare migration is not a compelling interest; it is itself constitutionally suspect because it seeks to deter the exercise of the right to travel.
lb_rule_reference: The right to travel interstate is fundamental. Durational residency requirements for public benefits burden that right and trigger strict scrutiny under the Equal Protection Clause.
