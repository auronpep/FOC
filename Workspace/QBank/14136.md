


BARMATRIX Q#: 14136
Outline_code: 92080202
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Renewed Motion for Judgment as a Matter of Law
Question: A homeowner filed a diversity action against a plumber in federal court in State A. The homeowner alleged that the plumber failed to finish performing work on a $150,000 service contract concerning the installation of a bathroom in the homeowner's home. The plumber disputed the contract's validity at trial, testifying before the jury that the plumber and homeowner never had a contract and that the parties were joking when referring to a "contract" in their exchanged emails. The plumber offered undisputed evidence that he is a contractor by profession, who specializes in bathroom installation. After the close of evidence, the homeowner's attorney filed a motion for judgment as a matter of law, which the court rejected. The jury then returned a verdict for the plumber. May the homeowner file a renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law and motion for a new trial?
Answer A: A: No, the homeowner may only file a motion for a new trial, not a renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law.
Answer B: B: No, the homeowner may only file one of these motions, but not both.
Answer C: C: Yes, the homeowner may file both a renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law and a motion for a new trial.
Answer D: D: Yes, the homeowner may combine these motions with a motion for relief from judgment.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. Pursuant to the Federal Rule of Civil Procedure (FRCP), parties may file a renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law in conjunction with a motion for a new trial. A renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law may be granted if the judgment was based upon a verdict that reasonable persons could not have reached, and the moving party has sought a judgment as a matter of law at the close of evidence. A motion for a new trial is granted because of an error during the trial, because the verdict is against the weight of the evidence, because of juror misconduct or because the verdict is excessive or inadequate. Because each motion has a different basis, they may be brought together. Here, the homeowner may therefore submit both a renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law and a motion for a new trial.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. Pursuant to the FRCP, parties may make a renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law. This renewed motion can only be made if a party has previously made a motion for judgment as a matter of law. Because the homeowner previously made a motion for judgment as a matter of law, the homeowner may now file a renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law in addition to a motion for a new trial. B is incorrect. As stated above, the FRCP allow parties to make a renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law in conjunction with a motion for a new trial. D is incorrect. As stated above, a renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law and a motion for new trial are available to the homeowner, who may file both. Those motions do not have to be combined with a motion for relief from judgment.
