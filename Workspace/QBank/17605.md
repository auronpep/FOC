internal_id: TR-004

MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Battery
Question: During a workplace training session, an employee pulled a chair away just as a coworker started to sit. The employee meant it as a joke and expected the coworker to land on a padded mat behind the chair. The coworker instead hit the floor and bruised his hip. The coworker sues the employee for battery. Is the coworker likely to prevail in a battery claim against the employee?
Answer A: No, because the employee intended a joke rather than an injury
Answer B: No, because the coworker was not apprehensive before the fall
Answer C: Yes, because the employee intentionally caused contact that was harmful or offensive
Answer D: Yes, but only if the employee's conduct was unreasonable under negligence principles
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Pulling the chair was an intentional act substantially certain to cause bodily contact. The resulting fall was harmful contact, so battery is established.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Battery does not require intent to injure. The employee intentionally created the contact or was substantially certain the coworker would make bodily contact with the ground. B: Apprehension is an assault element, not a battery element. Battery turns on harmful or offensive contact. D: Unreasonableness is not the controlling element for battery. Intentional harmful or offensive contact is the controlling issue.
lb_rule_reference: Intent to cause contact or substantial certainty that contact will result can satisfy battery; intent to injure is not required.
