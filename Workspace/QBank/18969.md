Question: In a civil enforcement action, a witness is asked under oath whether he falsified invoices that are also the subject of an ongoing criminal investigation. The witness refuses to answer, invoking the Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination. How should the court rule on the refusal?
Answer A: Allow it because the answer could be testimonial and incriminating, even though the proceeding is civil.
Answer B: Reject it because the Fifth Amendment may be invoked only by criminal defendants at criminal trials.
Answer C: Reject it because the question is relevant to the civil case.
Answer D: Allow it only if the government has already filed an indictment.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The Fifth Amendment protects a person from compelled testimonial self-incrimination. It may be invoked in civil proceedings when an answer could reasonably expose the witness to criminal liability. The decisive axis is testimonial/incriminating risk, not case label.
