Question: A utility company negligently damaged its own switching station, causing a power outage in a shopping district for six hours. A bookstore suffered no property damage and no physical injury to anyone, but lost sales during the outage. The bookstore sues the utility in ordinary negligence for lost profits only. No special information-supply relationship or contract is alleged. Is the bookstore likely to recover its lost profits in ordinary negligence?
Answer A: Yes, because lost profits were foreseeable once power was interrupted
Answer B: Yes, because negligence always compensates every loss caused in fact
Answer C: No, because the claim seeks pure economic loss without injury, property damage, or a special relationship
Answer D: No, because businesses can never recover lost profits in tort
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The bookstore alleges only lost profits in ordinary negligence. The economic-loss rule limits open-ended liability for that kind of loss.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Foreseeability alone usually does not overcome the economic-loss limit for pure financial loss in ordinary negligence. B: Negligence liability also requires duty and legally recoverable damages; pure economic loss may be outside the duty scope. D: Lost profits may be recoverable when tied to property damage, personal injury, or a recognized tort duty. The issue is pure economic loss here.
lb_rule_reference: In ordinary negligence, a plaintiff who suffers only economic loss without personal injury, property damage, or a recognized special relationship is often barred by the economic-loss rule.
