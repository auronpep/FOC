Question: Moses sues Aaron in federal court over damage to a delivery truck. During trial, Aaron argues that Moses has not proved that the courthouse is located in Jericho County, even though the court sits in Jericho County and the fact is generally known within that district. No party has asked the court to take judicial notice. May the court take judicial notice of the courthouse's county location on its own?
Answer A: Yes, because a court may take judicial notice on its own of an adjudicative fact that is generally known within the trial court's territorial jurisdiction.
Answer B: Yes, but only if Moses first offers a witness to testify that the courthouse is in Jericho County.
Answer C: No, because judicial notice is available only when a party formally requests it before trial.
Answer D: No, because the jury must decide every geographic fact without judicial assistance.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A court may take judicial notice on its own. The courthouse's location in the county where the federal court sits is an adjudicative fact generally known within the trial court's territorial jurisdiction. No party request or witness is required for that kind of noticed fact.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because judicial notice dispenses with ordinary proof for facts that qualify. C: C is wrong because a request is not required; the court may act sua sponte. D: D is wrong because Rule 201 allows the court to notice certain adjudicative facts rather than requiring witness proof for every fact.
