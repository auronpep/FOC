Question: At night in a parking lot, Vance points what appears to be a real handgun at Nora and shouts, "Give me your wallet now." Nora has a licensed handgun and shoots Vance once. The object in Vance's hand is later discovered to be a realistic toy gun. Nora is charged with aggravated assault. Is Nora likely entitled to self-defense?
Answer A: No, because Vance did not actually have a real gun
Answer B: No, because deadly force can be used only after the attacker fires first
Answer C: Yes, because Nora reasonably believed she faced imminent deadly force
Answer D: Yes, but only because Vance demanded money
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Vance pointed what appeared to be a real gun while demanding property. Nora's belief that she faced imminent deadly force was reasonable even though the object later turned out to be a toy. A reasonable mistake can support self-defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Self-defense asks whether Nora reasonably believed deadly force was imminent. A realistic gun pointed during a robbery threat can support that belief. B: The law does not require the defendant to wait to be shot. Reasonable belief in imminent deadly force is enough. D: A demand for money alone does not justify deadly force. The apparent gun and imminent threat of deadly force are the decisive facts.
lb_rule_reference: Deadly self-defense can be justified by a reasonable belief of imminent deadly force, even if the belief later turns out to be mistaken
