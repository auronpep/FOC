Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Moses owned a small vineyard. On a sheet of ordinary notebook paper, he wrote, “I convey my vineyard at 12 Jordan Road to Aaron.” Moses, who could not write his name, placed an X at the bottom intending it as his signature, handed the paper to Aaron, and said, “The vineyard is yours now.” Aaron accepted. No money was paid, and the paper was not notarized. Who owns the vineyard?
Answer A: Moses, because a deed must be on a formal printed deed form.
Answer B: Moses, because Aaron paid no consideration.
Answer C: Aaron, because the writing identified the grantor, grantee, land, present transfer, and Moses authenticated it by his mark.
Answer D: Aaron only if the paper is later notarized and recorded.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is minimal deed formalities. The writing identified the grantor, a living grantee, the property, present words of transfer, and a grantor signature through Moses's intended mark. Formal paper, consideration, acknowledgment, and recording were not required for validity between Moses and Aaron.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because ordinary paper can satisfy the writing requirement. B: B is wrong because consideration is not required for a valid delivered deed. D: D is wrong because notarization and recording affect proof, recordability, or priority, not the baseline validity lock between the parties.
