Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Mode and order of witnesses
Question: In a federal civil fraud trial, John is the only witness who claims that Esther signed the disputed contract. On direct examination, John identifies the signature and says he watched Esther sign. When Esther's lawyer rises to cross-examine, the judge says, “This case has taken long enough; no cross-examination of John will be allowed.” Esther objects. Which statement is most accurate?
Answer A: The ruling is proper because the judge has complete discretion over the order and mode of examination.
Answer B: The ruling is proper because cross-examination is limited to credibility only.
Answer C: The ruling is likely improper because reasonable control cannot eliminate meaningful examination of a key witness on a contested fact.
Answer D: The ruling is proper unless Esther first makes an offer of proof about every question she would ask.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Although the court has broad authority to control the mode and order of examining witnesses, that control must remain reasonable and effective for determining the truth. Completely barring meaningful cross-examination of the key witness on a contested fact is likely improper.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the court's discretion is not absolute. B: B is wrong because cross may address the subject matter of direct and credibility. D: D is wrong because the immediate problem is the denial of meaningful cross-examination, not a failure to proffer excluded evidence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 611(a) / abuse by denying meaningful examination
