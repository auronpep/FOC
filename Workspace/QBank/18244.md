Question: Paul is prosecuted in federal court for tax evasion. The prosecution subpoenas Paul's former lawyer for a billing ledger showing only the dates and amounts of Paul's fee payments. The ledger contains no legal advice, litigation strategy, or description of confidential communications. Should the billing ledger be excluded on attorney-client privilege grounds?
Answer A: Yes, because any document held by a lawyer is privileged.
Answer B: Yes, because fee payments reveal that Paul sought legal advice.
Answer C: No, because the fee amounts and payment dates are not confidential legal-advice communications on these facts.
Answer D: No, but only because criminal defendants have no attorney-client privilege against the prosecution.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Fee amounts, payment dates, and ordinary billing records usually are not privileged because they are not communications seeking or providing legal advice. No special fact suggests that the ledger would reveal privileged substance. The decisive axis is communication content, not lawyer possession.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because This is lawyer-possession shortcut. A record is not privileged merely because it is in a lawyer's file. B: B is wrong because This overreads the inference from representation. The fact of payment is not itself the substance of confidential legal advice. D: A is wrong because This is lawyer-possession shortcut. A record is not privileged merely because it is in a lawyer's file. B is wrong because This overreads the inference from representation. The fact of payment is not itself the substance of confidential legal advice. C is wrong because Correct answer D is wrong because This states a false rule. Criminal defendants can assert attorney-client privilege, but the ledger is not privileged on these facts.
