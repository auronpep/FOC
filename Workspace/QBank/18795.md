MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Civil Procedure
Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Omicron Freight Solutions Corp. ("Omicron") is incorporated in Delaware and maintains its principal place of business in Atlanta, Georgia. Omicron operates a large regional hub in the State of Larkin, employing 2,400 workers there, maintaining a fleet of 800 trucks registered in Larkin, and generating approximately 18% of its annual revenue from Larkin operations. Omicron also holds long-term leases on three warehouse facilities in Larkin. A Larkin resident was struck by an Omicron truck in the State of Venton while traveling for work. The resident returned home to Larkin and sued Omicron in Larkin federal court, asserting only claims arising from the Venton accident. Omicron concedes it is subject to jurisdiction in Delaware, Georgia, and Venton but moves to dismiss the Larkin suit for lack of personal jurisdiction. Should the Larkin federal court grant Omicron's motion to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction?
Answer A: No, because Omicron's 2,400 employees, 800 trucks, and 18% revenue share make Larkin a surrogate home state for purposes of general jurisdiction
Answer B: No, because the plaintiff is a Larkin resident and courts routinely exercise general jurisdiction to benefit forum residents
Answer C: Yes, because the claims arise from a Venton accident, and Larkin has no specific jurisdiction over claims unrelated to Omicron's Larkin contacts
Answer D: Yes, because Omicron is incorporated in Delaware and headquartered in Georgia, and is therefore not "at home" in Larkin, making general jurisdiction unavailable regardless of the extent of Omicron's Larkin operations
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The claims arise from a Venton accident, so specific jurisdiction over those claims in Larkin is unavailable — they have no relationship to Omicron's Larkin contacts. General jurisdiction over Omicron requires that Larkin be Omicron's "home" state under *Daimler*. Omicron's home states are Delaware (incorporation) and Georgia (PPB). Larkin's long-arm statute may reach Omicron's Larkin-based operations, but those are specific-PJ contacts; they do not render Omicron "at home" in Larkin for purposes of all-claims general jurisdiction. The motion should be granted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Daimler* rejected the continuous-and-systematic standard for corporations, holding that the proper inquiry is whether the corporation is "at home" in the forum. A corporation is at home only where it is incorporated or where it has its principal place of business — unless an exceptional circumstance applies (e.g., wartime registration). Omicron is incorporated in Delaware and headquartered in Georgia; extensive Larkin operations do not make Larkin a home state. B: Personal jurisdiction is determined by the defendant's contacts with the forum, not the plaintiff's residence or convenience. The plaintiff's domicile in Larkin is irrelevant to whether Larkin may exercise general jurisdiction over Omicron. Due process protects the defendant's liberty interest; plaintiff residence does not supply that foundation. C: The answer identifies the right jurisdictional category but draws the wrong conclusion. The reason Larkin cannot exercise general jurisdiction is not that the claims arose elsewhere — that goes to specific PJ relatedness. The reason is that Omicron is not "at home" in Larkin under *Daimler*. The answer confuses the general-PJ inquiry with the specific-PJ relatedness inquiry.
lb_rule_reference: General personal jurisdiction over a corporation requires that the forum be the corporation's "home" — its state of incorporation or the state where its principal place of business is located. Extensive operations in a state that is neither do not suffice for general jurisdiction. *Goodyear Dunlop Tires Operations, S.A. v. Brown* (2011); *Daimler AG v. Bauman* (2014).
