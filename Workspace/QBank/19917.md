Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Noah agreed to cater Rachel's banquet for $18,000. Noah expected to spend $13,000 on food and staff. Before Noah bought food or hired staff, Rachel wrongfully canceled the banquet. Noah had no other losses and avoided all costs. What is Noah's proper expectation recovery?
Answer A: $18,000, because Rachel canceled the contract.
Answer B: $13,000, because Noah expected to spend that amount.
Answer C: $5,000, because Noah recovers his expected profit after deducting costs saved.
Answer D: Nothing, because Noah had not yet begun performance.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Noah should be put in the position he would have occupied if the banquet contract had been performed. Because he avoided the $13,000 performance cost, his recoverable expectation is his $5,000 expected profit.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it ignores the costs Noah saved. B: B is wrong because costs Noah did not incur are not his loss. D: D is wrong because a repudiation before performance can still cause lost profit.
lb_rule_reference: Common law expectation damages; avoided costs
