Question: In a federal burglary trial, the prosecutor proves that Jacob pawned a rare lamp two hours after it was stolen from Ruth's home. Jacob asks the court to instruct the jury that evidence of possession after theft is only character evidence and cannot be considered as proof of burglary. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Give the instruction because possession after theft is character evidence.
Answer B: Give the instruction because circumstantial evidence is usable only to impeach.
Answer C: Refuse the instruction because possession soon after theft is circumstantial evidence the jury may consider for whatever weight it deserves.
Answer D: Refuse the instruction only if an eyewitness saw Jacob enter the house.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Possession of stolen property shortly after the theft can be circumstantial evidence. It is not automatically character evidence and does not require an eyewitness to be usable.
