Question: A man contacted his lawyer regarding his right to use a path that was on his neighbor's vacant land. Fifteen years ago, after part of a path located on his land and connecting his cabin to the public highway washed out, the man cleared a small part of his neighbor's land and rerouted a section of the path through the neighbor's land. Twelve years ago, the neighbor leased her land to some hunters. For the next 12 years, the hunters and the man who had rerouted the path used the path for access to the highway. A month ago, the neighbor discovered that part of the path was on her land. The neighbor told the man that she had not given him permission to cross her land and that she would be closing the rerouted path after 90 days. The man's land and the neighbor's land have never been in common ownership. The period of time necessary to acquire rights by prescription in the jurisdiction is 10 years. The period of time necessary to acquire title by adverse possession in the jurisdiction is 10 years. What should the lawyer tell the man concerning his right to use the rerouted path on the neighbor's land?
Answer A: A: The man has fee title by adverse possession of the land included in the path.
Answer B: B: The man has an easement by necessity to use the path.
Answer C: C: The man has an easement by prescription to use the path.
Answer D: D: The man has no right to use the path.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. The man has used the path for the past 15 years without the neighbor's permission, openly and continuously, and has acquired an easement by prescription.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The man is not claiming to own the land, and thus acquired an easement instead of title by adverse possession. B is incorrect. No easement by necessity could be found here because the properties were never under common ownership. D is incorrect. The man has acquired an easement by prescription from his use of the path.
