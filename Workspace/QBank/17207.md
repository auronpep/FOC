MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Abnormally Dangerous Activities
Question: A contractor used explosives to remove rock for a private driveway in a quiet residential neighborhood. The contractor obtained permits, warned neighbors, and used the safest available charge size. The blast still sent vibrations through a nearby house and cracked several windows. The homeowner sued the contractor for strict liability. Is the homeowner likely to prevail on the strict liability claim for the cracked windows?
Answer A: No, because the contractor used the safest available blasting methods
Answer B: Yes, because blasting vibrations cracked the windows, which is the kind of harm that makes blasting abnormally dangerous
Answer C: No, because property damage is not recoverable without personal injury in strict liability
Answer D: Yes, because strict liability would also cover any unrelated injury at the site that day
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Blasting creates a high risk of serious property damage that cannot be eliminated by reasonable care and is uncommon in a residential area. The cracked windows resulted from that very risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Strict liability for abnormally dangerous activity does not turn on negligence. Care is not a complete defense when the harm comes from the abnormal blast risk. C: Strict liability for abnormally dangerous activities covers property damage caused by the abnormal risk. D: The reason is too broad. Strict liability reaches only harms within the abnormal risk, not every injury near the blasting site.
lb_rule_reference: Blasting is commonly treated as abnormally dangerous, and strict liability applies to harm caused by the blast's concussive or vibration risk even when the defendant used reasonable care.
