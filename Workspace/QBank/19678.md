MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: False imprisonment
Question: A store owner believed a shopper had taken headphones two days earlier after reviewing blurry security footage. When the shopper returned, the owner locked the shopper in a storage room for forty minutes and demanded payment for the headphones before allowing him to leave. The owner had not seen the shopper take anything that day. The shopper sues the store owner for false imprisonment. Can the store owner defeat the shopper's false imprisonment claim by relying on shopkeeper privilege or recapture of chattels?
Answer A: Yes, because merchants may detain anyone they suspect of prior theft
Answer B: No, because the detention was delayed, coercive, and broader than the shopkeeper or recapture privilege allows
Answer C: Yes, because recapture is always allowed if the owner honestly believes property was taken
Answer D: No, because stores can never detain suspected shoplifters
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The owner acted two days after the suspected event, locked the shopper in a storage room, and demanded payment. Those facts exceed reasonable shopkeeper detention and recapture limits.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The privilege has timing, manner, and reasonableness limits. A locked forty-minute detention over a two-day-old suspicion is outside the narrow privilege. C: Recapture of chattels is narrowly limited, especially by prompt pursuit and peaceful or reasonable means. Honest belief alone is insufficient. D: Stores may have a privilege for reasonable, prompt, limited detention based on reasonable suspicion. This detention exceeded those limits.
lb_rule_reference: Shopkeeper and recapture privileges are limited by reasonable suspicion, prompt timing, reasonable manner, and narrow purpose; delayed or coercive detention falls outside the privilege.
