Outline: Marital Communications Privilege
Question: Joseph and Mary were married when Joseph privately told Mary, “I forged the purchase order.” They later divorced. In a federal civil fraud action against Joseph, the plaintiff subpoenas Mary to repeat Joseph's statement. Joseph asserts marital communications privilege. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection, because the statement was a confidential communication made during the marriage and the communication privilege survives divorce.
Answer B: Overrule the objection, because Joseph and Mary are no longer married at the time of testimony.
Answer C: Overrule the objection, because spousal privileges apply only in criminal prosecutions.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Mary also refuses to testify.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The marital communications privilege protects confidential communications made between spouses during marriage, and the protection can survive divorce. This item asks Mary to repeat Joseph's private marital words. The decisive axis is confidential communication during marriage, not current relationship status.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because This applies the current-marriage rule for adverse testimonial privilege to the separate communications privilege. C: C is wrong because The adverse testimonial privilege is criminal-focused, but the marital communications privilege can apply in civil litigation. D: D is wrong because Mary's willingness does not eliminate Joseph's ability to assert the communication privilege on these facts.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 501 / confidential marital communication
lb_student_diagnostic: Student is importing the testimonial privilege's current-marriage rule into marital communications.
