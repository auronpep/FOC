Question: Benjamin owned Lot 1 in fee simple. Lot 1 benefited from a recorded easement over Lot 2. Later, Benjamin received only a life estate in Lot 2, while Elijah retained the remainder in Lot 2. Benjamin used the easement route during his life tenancy. Elijah now argues that Benjamin's life estate in Lot 2 terminated the easement by merger. Is Elijah's merger argument correct?
Answer A: Yes, because any interest in the servient estate held by the dominant owner terminates the easement.
Answer B: No, because merger generally requires unity of ownership in coextensive dominant and servient estates, not merely a life estate in the servient parcel.
Answer C: Yes, because a life tenant has the same estate as a fee simple owner for merger purposes.
Answer D: No, but only because recorded easements are categorically immune from merger.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Merger requires unity of ownership of the relevant estates, generally coextensive interests in the dominant and servient estates. Benjamin owned the dominant estate in fee but only a life estate in the servient estate, with Elijah holding the remainder. That incomplete unity does not extinguish the easement.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because partial or noncoextensive interests do not automatically create merger. B is correct for the reasons above. C: C is wrong because a life estate is not the same estate as fee simple ownership. D: D is wrong because recorded easements can terminate by merger if complete unity exists.
lb_rule_reference: Merger requires coextensive estates
lb_student_diagnostic: The repair signal is requiring same-owner, same-estate unity before calling merger.
