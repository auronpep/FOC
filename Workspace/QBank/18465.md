Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: An officer lawfully stands on a public sidewalk and sees a stolen sculpture through the window of a home. Without a warrant or exigent circumstances, the officer opens the front door, enters, and seizes the sculpture. Does plain view justify the seizure?
Answer A: Yes, because the officer could see the sculpture from a lawful public vantage point
Answer B: No, because the officer lacked lawful access to enter the home and seize the sculpture
Answer C: Yes, because stolen property receives no Fourth Amendment protection
Answer D: No, but only because the officer did not first arrest the homeowner
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The officer was lawfully positioned outside, but plain view did not supply authority to enter the home.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Plain view also requires lawful access to the object. Seeing into a home does not authorize entry. C: Incriminating character does not erase the lawful-access requirement. D: The problem is lack of entry authority, not absence of arrest.
lb_rule_reference: Plain view permits seizure only when officers have lawful vantage, lawful access to the object, and immediately apparent incriminating character.
