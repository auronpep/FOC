Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: Sarah alleges that a store employee spilled oil in aisle three at 2:00 p.m., that the store left it there for two hours despite customer complaints, and that Sarah slipped on the oil. She does not attach witness statements or photographs. The store moves to dismiss, arguing the complaint lacks evidence. Should the complaint be dismissed for lack of attached proof?
Answer A: Yes, because a complaint must attach evidence proving each element
Answer B: Yes, because negligence claims always require heightened pleading
Answer C: No, because the complaint alleges specific facts making negligence plausible
Answer D: No, because legal conclusions alone are always enough
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The pleaded facts identify conduct, notice, location, and injury plausibly supporting the claim.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 8 requires plausible factual allegations, not evidentiary exhibits. B: Ordinary negligence generally uses Rule 8, not Rule 9(b). D: Bare conclusions are not enough; this complaint survives because it alleges facts.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 8 requires enough factual matter to state a plausible claim; it does not require proof in the complaint.
