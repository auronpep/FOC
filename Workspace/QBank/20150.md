Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: Police obtained a valid warrant to search Erin's apartment for stolen cameras. The warrant was supported by probable cause and described the apartment and cameras with particularity. When executing it, officers failed to knock and announce before entering. Inside, they found the stolen cameras in a closet. Erin moves to suppress solely because of the knock-and-announce violation. Should the cameras be suppressed on that basis alone?
Answer A: Yes, because every Fourth Amendment violation requires suppression
Answer B: Yes, unless officers can prove the cameras would have been discovered inevitably
Answer C: No, because suppression is generally unavailable solely for a knock-and-announce violation when the search warrant itself was valid
Answer D: No, because knock-and-announce is only a police custom and never part of Fourth Amendment analysis
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The warrant validly authorized the search and seizure of the cameras. The knock-and-announce defect does not automatically trigger suppression of the evidence found.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The exclusionary rule is not automatic. A knock-and-announce violation alone generally does not require suppression of evidence found under a valid warrant. B: The better route is the limited remedy for knock-and-announce violations, not a required inevitable-discovery showing. D: Knock-and-announce can be a constitutional execution rule. The issue is the remedy, not whether the rule exists.
lb_rule_reference: The exclusionary remedy is limited; suppression is generally not required solely because officers violated the knock-and-announce rule during execution of a valid search warrant.
