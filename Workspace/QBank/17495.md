Outline: Robbery
Question: A federal grand jury returned a single indictment against Frost charging (1) armed bank robbery occurring on March 1, (2) use of a firearm during a crime of violence arising from the same March 1 robbery, and (3) a separate bank robbery committed on March 15, using the same modus operandi and the same weapon. Frost moved to sever all three counts, arguing that a single trial on three charges would unfairly prejudice the jury against him. Was the joinder of all three counts in a single indictment proper under the Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure?
Answer A: No, because each robbery is a separate criminal act and may not be joined in a single indictment unless the defendant consents
Answer B: No, because the March 1 and March 15 robberies are not part of a single transaction and cannot be joined without satisfying the identity-of-evidence test
Answer C: Yes, because the counts are either based on the same act or transaction or are of the same or similar character, satisfying Rule 8(a)
Answer D: Yes, because joinder of offenses is always proper when a single defendant is charged
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under Fed. R. Crim. P. 8(a), two or more offenses may be charged in the same indictment if they are (1) of the same or similar character, (2) based on the same act or transaction, or (3) connected with or constitute parts of a common scheme or plan. Counts 1 and 2 arise from the same March 1 transaction. Count 3, a separate robbery using the same weapon and modus operandi, is of the same or similar character as Count 1. All three grounds for joinder are met for at least some counts, and the joinder is proper. Whether to sever for fairness is a separate Rule 14 question.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 8(a) does not require defendant consent for joinder. Offenses may be joined when they are of the same or similar character or are connected as part of a common scheme. Multiple bank robberies using the same weapon and modus operandi are paradigmatically "same or similar character" offenses. B: Rule 8(a) provides three independent bases for joinder. Same-transaction is one; "same or similar character" is another. There is no "identity-of-evidence test" in Rule 8(a). Two robberies using the same weapon and technique qualify as same-or-similar-character offenses even if they are separate transactions. D: The identity of the defendant is not the test; the relationship among the offenses is. Rule 8(a) requires a substantive connection — same character, same transaction, or common scheme. Charging the same defendant with unrelated crimes of different types would not satisfy Rule 8(a).
