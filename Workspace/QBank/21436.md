Question: David contracted with Abraham to buy Abraham's tools. As part of the bargain, David promised Abraham that he would pay Abraham's daughter Leah's school tuition for the coming term. Abraham told Leah of the promise. Leah then declined another scholarship that required her to attend a different school. David and Abraham later rescinded the tools contract. Can Leah enforce the tuition promise?
Answer A: Yes, because Leah's rights vested when she materially relied by declining the other scholarship.
Answer B: No, because rescission by the contracting parties always eliminates third-party rights.
Answer C: No, because Leah's scholarship decision was not consideration to David.
Answer D: Yes, but only against Abraham, because David did not contract directly with Leah.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Leah was an intended beneficiary of the tuition promise. Her rights vested when she materially and reasonably relied on that promise by declining another scholarship. The later rescission by David and Abraham cannot cut off her vested right.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because rescission after vesting is ineffective against the beneficiary without consent. C: C is wrong because reliance is a vesting trigger, not consideration for the underlying contract. D: D is wrong because the promisor, David, may be liable to the intended beneficiary.
