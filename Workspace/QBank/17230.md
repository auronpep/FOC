Question: In a federal civil trial, Sarah testifies for the defendant. The plaintiff seeks to impeach Sarah with a fraud conviction. Sarah was released from confinement for the conviction 12 years ago. The plaintiff gave no written notice before trial. Sarah objects. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the conviction because fraud always involves dishonesty.
Answer B: Admit the conviction because the ordinary Rule 403 standard applies to old convictions.
Answer C: Exclude the conviction because more than ten years have passed since release and the proponent failed to satisfy the special old-conviction requirements, including reasonable written notice.
Answer D: Exclude the conviction only if Sarah is a criminal defendant.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: For convictions more than ten years old, measured from conviction or release from confinement, whichever is later, FRE 609(b) allows admission only if the probative value supported by specific facts and circumstances substantially outweighs prejudicial effect and the proponent gives reasonable written notice. The notice requirement is missing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because dishonest-act convictions still face Rule 609(b) when old. B: B is wrong because old convictions use a stricter standard than ordinary Rule 403. D: D is wrong because Rule 609(b) applies to all witnesses.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 609(b) / convictions more than ten years old
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the ten-year rule, the later-of-conviction-or-release date, and written-notice requirement.
