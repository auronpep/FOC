Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Peter hired Ruth to arrange a professional conference. Peter repudiated after Ruth had reasonably paid $10,000 in nonrefundable speaker and venue deposits. After the repudiation, Ruth paid a $3,000 cancellation charge that avoided a $15,000 venue liability. Ruth also spent $5,000 printing conference programs after learning that Peter had repudiated. Expected conference profits are too uncertain to prove. What is Ruth's proper reliance-related recovery?
Answer A: $18,000, because all three expenditures were connected to the conference.
Answer B: $10,000, because no post-repudiation payment can ever be recovered.
Answer C: Nothing, because Ruth continued spending after repudiation.
Answer D: $13,000, consisting of the pre-repudiation deposits plus the reasonable cancellation charge.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is reliance under common law, with a mitigation expense included because it reasonably reduced loss after repudiation. The formula is $10,000 pre-repudiation deposits + $3,000 reasonable cancellation cost = $13,000; the $5,000 program printing was avoidable new spending after repudiation and is excluded. The limiting gate is mitigation, and the result scope is limited. The answer does not award speculative conference profits.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it includes avoidable post-repudiation printing, a WAL-REM-010 error. B: B is wrong because reasonable post-repudiation mitigation expense may be recoverable even though avoidable new reliance is not. C: C is wrong because mitigation failures reduce avoidable amounts; they do not forfeit the entire claim under WAL-REM-009.
lb_rule_reference: Common law reliance damages — include reasonable mitigation expense, exclude avoidable new reliance after repudiation
