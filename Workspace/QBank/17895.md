Outline: Erie Doctrine
Question: A diversity case turns on an unsettled issue of state insurance law. The state has a certification procedure, but the federal judge concludes existing state appellate decisions provide enough guidance to predict the answer. A party argues that certification is mandatory whenever uncertainty exists. Is certification mandatory?
Answer A: Yes, every unsettled state-law issue must be certified
Answer B: Yes, because federal courts may never interpret state law
Answer C: No, the federal court may predict state law without certification when appropriate
Answer D: No, because unsettled state law is replaced by federal law
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Certification can be used for controlling unsettled issues, but it is not mandatory in every case.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Certification is generally discretionary, not an automatic Erie requirement. B: Federal courts in diversity routinely interpret and predict state law. D: Unsettled state law remains state law; the federal court predicts it rather than replacing it.
lb_rule_reference: A federal court may certify a controlling unsettled state-law question when authorized, but it can also make an Erie prediction when certification is unavailable or unwarranted.
