Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Solomon signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey my west field to the person I will name after next year's planting contest.” No person was named in the deed, and the deed gave no objective method for identifying the grantee at delivery. After the contest, Solomon announced that Elijah should receive the field, but Solomon signed no new deed. Who owns the west field?
Answer A: Elijah, because Solomon later announced that Elijah should receive the field.
Answer B: The contest judges, until they certify the result.
Answer C: Solomon, because the deed identified no present ascertainable grantee and the later announcement was not a deed.
Answer D: Elijah, because recording would relate the later announcement back to the original deed.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is an ascertainable grantee at delivery. The deed did not name a grantee or provide an objective way to identify one when delivered. Solomon's later announcement, without a new deed, does not transfer title. Solomon keeps the west field.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because later oral selection does not cure the missing grantee in the delivered deed. B: B is wrong because the judges were not named as grantees. D: D is wrong because recording cannot relate an oral later selection back to an invalid deed.
