


BARMATRIX Q#: 22207
Outline_code: 33040101
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Hearsay and Circumstances of Its Admissibility
Outline: Hearsay rule
Question: A man and a woman were charged with the kidnapping and murder of a professor that owed them money. The man pleaded guilty and went to prison, while the woman went to trial. At the woman's trial, the woman's lawyer called the man's father as a witness. The man's father testified that after the man went to prison he told his father that he was glad he was in prison because the kidnapping and murder was all his idea and the woman wasn't involved at all. Should the court admit the father's testimony?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the man's statement was the statement of a co- conspirator.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the man's statement is a statement against penal interest.
Answer C: C: No, because the man's statement is hearsay.
Answer D: D: No, because the man is available to testify on his own behalf.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Under the circumstances, the father's statement is hearsay not within any exception. A is incorrect because under FRE 801(d)(2)(E), the statement of a co- conspirator can be admitted against other co- conspirators if the statement is made during or in furtherance of the conspiracy. Here, the statement was made after the man went to prison, so the conspiracy was over when the statement was made. Additionally, there was no conspiracy charged or established by the man's confession. B is incorrect because under FRE 804(b) (3), a statement that subjects the declarant to criminal punishment, civil liability, or invalidates the declarant's claim against another can be admitted as an exception to the hearsay rule. Here, the man is already in prison, and FRE 804 requires the declarant to be unavailable, which the man is not (since he's in prison). D is incorrect because the fact the man is available to testify does not change the analysis.
