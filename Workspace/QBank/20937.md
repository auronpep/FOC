Outline: Nonresponsive answers
Question: In a federal theft trial, the prosecutor asks Miriam, “What did Eve tell you when you arrived at the store?” Miriam answers, “Eve said Daniel stole the purse.” Daniel moves to strike the answer on the ground that it was nonresponsive. How should the court rule on that motion?
Answer A: Grant the motion because all statements repeating what someone else said are nonresponsive.
Answer B: Grant the motion because every answer containing hearsay must be labeled nonresponsive.
Answer C: Grant the motion because Daniel did not like the answer.
Answer D: Deny the motion as stated because Miriam answered the question asked; any hearsay objection would be a separate ground that had to be raised specifically and timely.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The answer directly responded to the question asking what Eve told Miriam. The problem may be hearsay, but it is not nonresponsiveness. A party must state the specific ground for objection or motion to strike unless apparent from context.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because repeating a statement can be responsive to a question asking what was said. B: B is wrong because hearsay and nonresponsiveness are different objections. C: C is wrong because dislike of the answer is not a ground to strike.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 103(a)(1); FRE 611(a) / responsive answer vs wrong objection
