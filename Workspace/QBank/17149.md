Question: Rae walks up behind a pedestrian and yanks a necklace from the pedestrian's neck. The chain catches, cuts the pedestrian's skin, and breaks only after Rae pulls hard a second time. Rae runs away with the necklace. Is Rae guilty of robbery?
Answer A: No, because purse-snatching and necklace-snatching are always only larceny
Answer B: No, because Rae did not threaten the pedestrian before taking the necklace
Answer C: Yes, because Rae used force against the person to take property from the person
Answer D: Yes, but only because Rae successfully escaped
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The necklace was on the victim's body, and Rae used physical force to remove it. That is robbery rather than mere stealth larceny.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Here Rae used force to overcome the necklace's attachment and injured the victim. That force supports robbery. B: Robbery can be committed by force or intimidation. A threat is not required when force is used. D: The robbery elements were satisfied when force was used to take the necklace. Escape is not a separate element.
lb_rule_reference: A taking from the person by force, even force used to pull property from the victim's body, can satisfy robbery.
