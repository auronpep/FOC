Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motion for judgment on the pleadings
Question: A complaint alleges that the defendant signed the contract on April 1, delivered no goods, and caused $30,000 in damages. The defendant's timely answer denies damages but says nothing about signing the contract or failing to deliver goods. No amended answer is filed. What is the effect of the answer's silence?
Answer A: All allegations are denied because the defendant filed an answer
Answer B: The damages allegation is admitted because it was denied too generally
Answer C: The signing and nondelivery allegations are admitted because they were not denied
Answer D: No allegation can be admitted unless the plaintiff moves for summary judgment
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A responsive pleading was required, and the answer did not deny the signing or nondelivery allegations. Those non-damages allegations are admitted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: An answer must admit or deny. Silence on non-damages allegations can operate as admission. B: The defendant denied damages. The problem is silence on non-damages allegations. D: Rule 8(b) itself gives effect to unanswered allegations in pleadings. No summary-judgment motion is needed to create the admission.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 8(b) requires admissions and denials; an allegation other than one relating to damages is admitted if a responsive pleading is required and the allegation is not denied.
