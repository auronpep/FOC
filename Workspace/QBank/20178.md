Question: Abigail hired Benjamin's firm to perform a “white-glove migration package” for her accounting data. The agreement included a small hardware token for secure access, but most of the price was allocated to consulting, transfer, and testing work. After Benjamin moved the files but did not perform post-cutover reconciliation, Abigail claimed the package included one reconciliation review. Which additional fact would most strengthen Abigail's Content/Meaning argument?
Answer A: The hardware token was a movable item, so Article 2 automatically supplies every missing service term.
Answer B: Before signing, Benjamin said, “This will be painless,” but the final agreement did not repeat that phrase.
Answer C: In the data-migration services market, “white-glove migration package” ordinarily includes one post-cutover reconciliation review, and Benjamin regularly sells that service package.
Answer D: Abigail had to pay her bookkeeper overtime after the reconciliation was not performed.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that source law must be locked before selecting the meaning source. A mixed transaction that is predominantly services is interpreted under common-law/custom principles rather than automatic Article 2 gap-filling. The trigger facts are the small incidental hardware token, the consulting-heavy price allocation, and the technical phrase “white-glove migration package.” The decisive locks are source law and purpose. C directly defines the service phrase through market usage; the other facts do not.
