Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Fourth Amendment
Question: An anonymous caller tells police that "a man in a red jacket near the park has a gun." The caller gives no name, basis of knowledge, predictive information, or emergency details. Officers arrive, see a man in a red jacket calmly sitting on a bench, and immediately stop and frisk him. Was the stop and frisk justified?
Answer A: No, because the anonymous tip lacked sufficient indicia of reliability and the officers observed no suspicious conduct
Answer B: Yes, because any gun tip allows police to stop and frisk the described person
Answer C: Yes, because parks are public places with reduced privacy
Answer D: No, because police may never frisk for weapons unless they have probable cause
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: A bare anonymous description plus allegation, without corroborated reliability or suspicious conduct, does not provide reasonable suspicion for a stop and frisk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Reasonable suspicion still requires reliability. A bare anonymous gun tip is not automatically enough. C: Public location does not eliminate the Fourth Amendment requirement for a stop and frisk. D: A frisk can be based on reasonable suspicion that the person is armed and dangerous. The problem is that this tip did not supply it.
lb_rule_reference: A Terry stop requires reasonable suspicion based on specific, articulable facts; an anonymous tip lacking predictive detail or corroboration is usually insufficient.
