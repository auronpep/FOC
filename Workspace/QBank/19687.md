Question: A sailor was caught in a sudden violent storm and tied his boat to a private dock to avoid sinking. The dock owner ordered him to leave, but leaving would have put the sailor in serious danger. The boat remained tied until the storm passed and damaged several dock boards. The dock owner sues for trespass and property damage. What is the likely result?
Answer A: The sailor was privileged to remain during the emergency but must pay for the actual dock damage
Answer B: The sailor is liable for trespass because private necessity is never a privilege
Answer C: The sailor owes nothing because necessity always eliminates all liability
Answer D: The dock owner could lawfully cut the boat loose during the storm because the dock was private
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Private necessity prevents liability for the entry as a trespass, but it is an incomplete privilege requiring payment for actual damage.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Private necessity can privilege emergency entry or use. C: That confuses private necessity with public necessity. Private necessity usually requires payment for actual damage. D: A property owner may not eject a privileged private-necessity entrant into danger.
lb_rule_reference: Private necessity can privilege entry or temporary use of another's property to protect private interests, but the actor usually remains liable for actual damage caused.
