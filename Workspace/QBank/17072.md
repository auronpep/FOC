Subtopic: Jurisdiction and Venue
Outline: Specific and General Jurisdiction
Question: Lumex Wellness Co. is a health-supplement company incorporated in Nevada with its principal place of business in Las Vegas. Lumex ran a 6-month digital advertising campaign specifically targeting residents of the State of Grandview — using Grandview ZIP codes in its ad-targeting parameters, displaying Grandview-specific promotional offers, and retargeting Grandview IP addresses. Through this campaign, Lumex collected 1,800 email addresses of Grandview residents who expressed interest in its products. However, Lumex does not sell its products to consumers directly — it sells only through national pharmacy chains, none of which operate in Grandview. None of the 1,800 interested Grandview residents could actually purchase Lumex products in Grandview. A Grandview resident who obtained Lumex supplements on a trip to a neighboring state and suffered an adverse reaction sues Lumex in Grandview federal court. Grandview's long-arm statute reaches defendants that "solicit business from residents of this state." Does the Grandview court have personal jurisdiction over Lumex?
Answer A: Yes, because Lumex's targeted advertising campaign in Grandview constitutes soliciting business from Grandview residents, satisfying both the long-arm statute and purposeful availment
Answer B: Yes, because Lumex's 1,800 Grandview email leads constitute a commercial relationship with 1,800 forum residents, satisfying minimum contacts
Answer C: No, because although the long-arm statute may cover Lumex's solicitation, Lumex's advertising campaign did not result in any Grandview transactions — Lumex cannot sell in Grandview — and without completed forum transactions, Lumex did not purposefully avail itself of the Grandview market in the constitutionally required sense
Answer D: No, because Lumex is a Nevada company and Nevada corporations are not subject to personal jurisdiction in states where they have no physical establishment
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The long-arm statute covers solicitation, so the statutory step is potentially satisfied by the advertising campaign. However, due process requires that Lumex could "reasonably anticipate being haled into court" in Grandview. Lumex sells only through pharmacy chains, none of which operate in Grandview; Grandview residents cannot purchase Lumex products there. Lumex deliberately targeted Grandview for advertising but knew it could not complete any Grandview sales. Without commercial activity consummated in the forum, the purposeful-availment showing is insufficient. The advertising campaign created leads but no forum-state commercial entanglement. The motion to dismiss should be granted.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Although the long-arm statute's "solicitation" language may cover the advertising campaign, the due-process step requires that Lumex purposefully availed itself of the privilege of conducting activities in Grandview such that it could reasonably anticipate being haled into court there. Lumex cannot actually sell to Grandview residents — there are no authorized pharmacy partners in Grandview. The advertising campaign collected email leads but resulted in zero Grandview transactions. A court would scrutinize whether mere solicitation with no ability to transact satisfies purposeful availment. The weight of authority suggests that advertising which generates no forum transactions is insufficient. B: Email leads generated through an advertising campaign are not commercial transactions or contractual relationships. Purposeful availment requires that the defendant derive some economic benefit from or create some binding connection with the forum. Collecting email addresses of potential customers who could not actually purchase from Lumex in Grandview does not constitute the kind of deliberate commercial entanglement with the forum that purposeful availment requires. D: There is no categorical rule restricting personal jurisdiction to a corporation's state of incorporation. The state of incorporation is a basis for general jurisdiction, not a ceiling on where the corporation can be sued. Specific jurisdiction may attach in any state where the corporation purposefully availed itself of the forum, regardless of where it is incorporated.
lb_rule_reference: Advertising directed at a forum state's residents constitutes a deliberate act of targeting the forum and is relevant to purposeful availment. When combined with resulting sales to forum residents, advertising strongly supports specific jurisdiction. Advertising alone in a forum where the defendant makes no sales is a closer question. *Hanson v. Denckla* (1958); *World-Wide Volkswagen Corp. v. Woodson* (1980).
