Question: In a federal burglary prosecution, the government plans to call an officer to testify that Samuel confessed after being arrested. Before the officer testifies, Samuel objects that he was interrogated without Miranda warnings and asks for a hearing outside the jury's presence. How should the court proceed?
Answer A: Deny the request because a confession by a party-opponent is not hearsay.
Answer B: Grant the request because the admissibility of the confession must be determined outside the jury's hearing.
Answer C: Allow the officer to testify before the jury and instruct the jury to disregard the confession if it finds no warnings were given.
Answer D: Submit the Miranda issue to the jury because credibility and voluntariness are always jury questions.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court decides the admissibility of a confession as a preliminary matter, and a hearing on the admissibility of a confession must be conducted outside the jury's hearing.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because party-opponent status does not bypass constitutional and admissibility gates. C: C is wrong because the jury should not hear the confession before the court resolves its admissibility. D: D is wrong because the court decides the admissibility gate; the jury weighs the confession only if admitted.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(c)
