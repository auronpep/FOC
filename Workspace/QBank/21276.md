Question: Naomi bought 100 “field-ready pump kits with orange housings” from Isaac's supply company. The signed goods contract stated that it was the complete and exclusive statement of the parties' agreement. The contract did not define “field-ready pump kit.” Naomi offers trade-usage evidence that, in the irrigation-equipment trade, a “field-ready pump kit” includes intake screens. Isaac offers a prior oral statement that he could substitute black housings if orange housings were unavailable. Which evidence should be admitted?
Answer A: Both the intake-screen usage and the black-housing substitution statement.
Answer B: Only the intake-screen usage.
Answer C: Only the black-housing substitution statement.
Answer D: Neither, because the writing is complete and exclusive.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that a UCC final writing, even a complete writing, may be explained by established trade usage if the usage is reasonably consistent with the express terms, but it may not be contradicted by prior or contemporaneous terms. The trigger facts are a goods contract, an undefined technical phrase, an express orange-housing requirement, and two different offered uses. The decisive lock is purpose. The intake-screen usage explains what “field-ready pump kit” includes; the black-housing statement contradicts the express orange-housing term.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it fails to split the two pieces of evidence and admits a contradictory substitution term. C: C is wrong because the substitution term negates an express color requirement. D: D is wrong because complete integration does not automatically bar UCC trade usage used to explain a technical term.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-202 / technical term and contradiction split
