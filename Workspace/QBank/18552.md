Question: A tenant sues a landlord seeking damages for breach of a lease. A different tenant under a similar lease is absent. The landlord argues the absent tenant is required because a later court might interpret the same lease form differently. Is that risk enough by itself to make the absent tenant required?
Answer A: Yes, because any risk of inconsistent legal reasoning makes an absentee required
Answer B: No, because the risk described is only inconsistent adjudication, not a substantial risk of multiple or inconsistent obligations
Answer C: Yes, because every person affected by the same form contract is required
Answer D: No, because Rule 19 applies only to property disputes
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Mere precedential or persuasive effects do not automatically make the absentee required.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 19 focuses on inconsistent obligations, not merely inconsistent precedent or reasoning. C: Required-party status is not based on broad practical interest alone. D: Rule 19 can apply beyond property disputes.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 19 required-party analysis considers substantial risk of multiple or inconsistent obligations, not merely the risk that a later court may reach a different result.
