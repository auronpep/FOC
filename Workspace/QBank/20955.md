MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A driver negligently hit a pedestrian, breaking the pedestrian's arm. At the hospital, a doctor negligently set the arm, worsening the fracture. The driver argues that the doctor's malpractice supersedes the driver's liability for the worsened arm injury. Is the doctor's malpractice likely a superseding cause?
Answer A: Yes, because any later negligence cuts off the first actor's liability
Answer B: Yes, because the driver did not personally treat the arm
Answer C: No, because negligent medical treatment is a foreseeable consequence of causing a physical injury
Answer D: No, because doctors are strictly liable for every bad result
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The need for medical care is a foreseeable result of the collision, and negligent treatment is ordinarily within the scope of that risk.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Negligent medical treatment after an accident is generally foreseeable and does not supersede the original tortfeasor's liability. B: Proximate cause is not limited to the last actor. Foreseeable medical negligence can remain within the original risk chain. D: The issue is whether the driver's liability is cut off. The answer does not depend on strict liability for doctors.
lb_rule_reference: Negligent medical treatment of an injury caused by the defendant is generally a foreseeable intervening act and not a superseding cause.
