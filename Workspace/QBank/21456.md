Question: A former employee sues a company in federal court for breach of contract. The complaint seeks compensatory damages for unpaid commissions and also asks for an injunction requiring the company to return confidential files. The employee makes a timely jury demand. Which statement best describes the civil jury right?
Answer A: No jury is allowed because the complaint includes a request for an injunction
Answer B: A jury right exists for the legal damages claim, while equitable issues may be tried to the judge
Answer C: A jury decides only equitable issues because injunctions are more important than damages
Answer D: A jury is available only if both parties consent after the complaint is filed
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Damages for breach of contract are legal relief, while injunction relief is equitable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The equitable request does not erase the jury right on legal damages claims. C: Equitable issues are generally for the judge, not the jury. D: A preserved Seventh Amendment jury right does not depend on opponent consent.
lb_rule_reference: The Seventh Amendment preserves a jury right for legal claims seeking legal remedies such as damages, but not for purely equitable relief.
