


BARMATRIX Q#: 22186
Outline_code: 31010106
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Evidence
Subtopic: Presentation of Evidence
Outline: Judicial notice
Question: A woman sued a man for negligence after the two were in a car accident. The woman asked the court to take judicial notice that sunset was at 5:30 P.M. on the day of the accident. The court took judicial notice as requested by the woman. The man sought to introduce evidence that sunset was actually much later, since part of his defense was that it was bright daylight out and the woman should have taken actions to avoid the accident. May the man introduce evidence of the later sunset?
Answer A: A: Yes, because it was part of his defense.
Answer B: B: Yes, because he had the burden of persuasion on that issue.
Answer C: C: No, because the man did not object when the woman made her request.
Answer D: D: No, because the judicial notice created an irrebuttable presumption.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: In a civil case, if a court takes judicial notice of a fact, the jury must accept the judge's finding on that fact as conclusive and the presumption is irrebuttable. FRE 201(f). Here, since the presumption is irrebuttable, the man cannot introduce contrary evidence. A and B are incorrect because the man cannot introduce evidence contrary to the judicially- noticed fact. C is incorrect because the man's failure to object does not change the analysis.
