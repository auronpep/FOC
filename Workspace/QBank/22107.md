


BARMATRIX Q#: 22107
Outline_code: 82010000
MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Real Property
Subtopic: Ownership
Outline: Nature and Ownership of Land
Question: After several heat deaths in the state, the state passed a law that required leased premises to have air conditioners capable of maintaining a temperature of at least 75 degrees in any residential lease. A woman leased an apartment from a landlord. The lease agreement contained nothing about air conditioning or heat. When the woman turned on the apartment's air conditioner the first summer of her lease, it was only capable of cooling the apartment to 85 degrees. The woman took a picture of the thermostat at 85 degrees, sent it to the landlord, and vacated the apartment. She then sued the landlord for violating the covenant of quiet enjoyment. Should the court rule in the woman's favor?
Answer A: A: Yes, because the woman was constructively evicted.
Answer B: B: Yes, because the woman was actually evicted.
Answer C: C: No, because the woman was only entitled to a rent abatement.
Answer D: D: No, because the lease agreement contained nothing about air conditioning or heat.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Constructive eviction is used where the landlord has an obligation and continually breaches that obligation in a way that makes the premises virtually uninhabitable for the intended use. Here, state law gave the landlord an obligation to provide a working air conditioner capable of maintaining the property at 75 degrees. The landlord violated this obligation, constructively evicting the woman and violating the covenant of quiet enjoyment. B is incorrect because an actual eviction occurs when the landlord intentionally breaches his or her obligation. Here, there was no indication the non- working air conditioner was intentional. C is incorrect because the woman was constructively evicted and moved out. As such, she was entitled to withhold her entire rent, not just to receive a rent abatement. D is incorrect because the fact that the lease did not include anything about the state law does not change the analysis.
