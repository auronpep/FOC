Question: In a federal assault trial, Miriam testifies on direct for the prosecution that she saw Joseph holding a bottle after a fight. On cross-examination, Joseph's lawyer asks, “You never saw Joseph swing the bottle, did you?” The prosecutor objects that the question is leading. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because leading questions are forbidden whenever they suggest the answer.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Miriam is first declared hostile.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because leading questions are ordinarily allowed on cross-examination.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the question concerns Miriam's reputation for truthfulness.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Leading questions are ordinarily allowed on cross-examination. The question tests the witness's observation about the same incident she described on direct. The leading form is not improper merely because it suggests the desired answer.
