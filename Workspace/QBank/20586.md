Question: A state prohibits physicians from prescribing a particular hormone treatment to minors for one diagnosis, while allowing the same medication for different diagnoses. The statute applies to all minors seeking the prohibited use and does not classify patients as male or female on its face. What is the key Equal Protection threshold question?
Answer A: Whether the law classifies by medical use and age, rather than by sex as such
Answer B: Whether the policy is medically wise, because poor policy automatically triggers strict scrutiny
Answer C: Whether the same medication is used elsewhere, because that fact alone automatically proves sex discrimination
Answer D: Whether minors have the same constitutional rights as adults in every medical context
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The tier turns on the classification actually drawn. Medical-use and age classifications generally receive rational-basis review absent a sex classification or suspect status.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Courts do not select strict scrutiny simply because a medical-policy choice is disputed. C: Different medical-use treatment is not automatically a sex classification. D: The Equal Protection question starts with the classification drawn by the law, not an across-the-board adult-rights rule.
lb_rule_reference: Identify the classification actually drawn; a medical-use or age classification generally receives rational-basis review unless the law classifies by suspect status or sex as such.
