Outline: Proximate (legal) cause
Question: A bicyclist negligently clipped a pedestrian's shoulder while riding on a crowded sidewalk. A minor bruise was foreseeable. Because the pedestrian had a rare bone condition, the impact caused a severe shoulder fracture requiring surgery. What is the best proximate-cause analysis?
Answer A: The bicyclist is liable only for the bruise that an average person would have suffered
Answer B: The bicyclist is not liable because the rare condition was unforeseeable
Answer C: The bicyclist may be liable for the full fracture because he takes the pedestrian as found
Answer D: The bicyclist is strictly liable because the pedestrian was unusually fragile
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The type of harm, physical injury from a sidewalk collision, was foreseeable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The eggshell rule rejects limiting damages to ordinary severity once the harm type is foreseeable. B: Unforeseeable extent does not defeat liability for a foreseeable physical impact. D: The rule affects damage scope after negligence and causation, not strict liability.
lb_rule_reference: If the type of physical impact is foreseeable, the defendant takes the plaintiff as found and is liable for the full extent of unusually severe injury.
