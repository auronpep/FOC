


BARMATRIX Q#: 22541
subject_display: Contracts
topic: Third-Party Rights
subtopic: Intended beneficiaries
difficulty: 53070100
Stem / Fact Pattern: The homeowner hired the defendant to maintain his lawn, agreeing to pay him $50 per month for certain specified services. Two months later, the homeowner sold his house to the plaintiff. At the closing of title, the homeowner handed the plaintiff the original memorandum of his agreement with the defendant and executed an assignment to the plaintiff of his rights thereunder. The defendant continued rendering services as agreed for an additional six months, receiving a check for $50 from the plaintiff each month. Then, the defendant notified the plaintiff that he would no longer be able to work on the lawn. The plaintiff sued the defendant for breaching the agreement contained in the memorandum. Is the plaintiff entitled to enforce the agreement?
Answer A: A: No, because the payment by the plaintiff did not result in a novation.
Answer B: B: No, because his only agreement was with the homeowner.
Answer C: C: Yes, because he owned the home that was the subject of the agreement.
Answer D: D: Yes, as assignee of the agreement between the homeowner and the defendant.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The assignee of contract rights is entitled to enforce them to the same extent the assignor would have been. The defendant’s breach of his contract with the homeowner gives the plaintiff, as the homeowner’s assignee, a cause of action against the defendant. A and B are therefore incorrect. C is incorrect because mere ownership of the home would not give the plaintiff the right to enforce the agreement.
