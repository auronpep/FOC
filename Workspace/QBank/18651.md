Question: A seller orally agreed to sell 400 jars of oil to Lydia. The seller delivered 150 jars. Lydia inspected them, kept 80 jars for resale, and seasonably rejected the other 70 jars as defective. No writing was signed, and no payment had been made. For what quantity does the received-and-accepted exception make the contract enforceable?
Answer A: 80 jars, because Lydia received and accepted only those jars.
Answer B: 150 jars, because Lydia physically received all 150 delivered jars.
Answer C: 400 jars, because any delivery under an oral goods contract validates the full deal.
Answer D: None, because defects in part of the delivery eliminate the received-and-accepted exception.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: UCC §2-201 allows enforcement with respect to goods that have been received and accepted. Physical receipt alone is not enough if the buyer seasonably rejects. Lydia accepted 80 jars and rejected 70, so enforcement under this exception is limited to 80 jars.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the 70 rejected jars were not accepted. C: C is wrong because partial acceptance does not validate the entire 400-jar oral contract. D: D is wrong because defects in rejected goods do not erase acceptance of the 80 retained jars.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 / received and accepted goods
