Question: A manufacturer appointed Miriam as the exclusive dealer for its commercial ovens in three counties. The signed agreement listed oven models, dealer discounts, territory, and two required training visits by the manufacturer, and it contained a merger clause. The agreement did not state whether Miriam had to maintain a demonstration oven. After Miriam made no showroom demonstrations, the manufacturer offered evidence that exclusive commercial-oven dealers customarily keep one working demonstration unit on display. Miriam objects. Should the court admit the usage evidence?
Answer A: No, because the merger clause bars all evidence of duties not stated in the writing.
Answer B: Yes, if the usage is established and reasonably consistent with the exclusive-dealer relationship.
Answer C: No, because the manufacturer's training visits make the transaction predominantly services rather than goods.
Answer D: Yes, because trade usage controls even if it cannot be reconciled with express terms.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: In a goods transaction, established trade usage may explain or supplement the agreement, and exclusive dealing supplies best-efforts content unless the writing provides otherwise. The trigger facts are the exclusive dealership for ovens, the territory and discount terms, the silence about demonstration units, and usage evidence in the same commercial-oven trade. The decisive locks are source law and offered purpose: the usage explains the dealer's effort obligation and does not contradict an express term.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a merger clause does not automatically bar consistent UCC usage evidence. C: C is wrong because ordinary training visits are incidental to selling ovens. D: D is wrong because usage must be reasonably consistent and cannot override unreconcilable express terms.
lb_rule_reference: UCC 2-306(2) exclusive dealing; UCC 1-303 usage of trade; UCC 2-202 explanation/supplementation
