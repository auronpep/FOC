Outline: Possession of Animals
Question: A rancher's cattle escaped through a gate and wandered onto a neighbor's vegetable farm, trampling rows of crops. The jurisdiction follows the traditional rule imposing strict liability for trespassing livestock that damage another's land. The neighbor sues the rancher for the crop damage. Is the rancher strictly liable under the stated rule?
Answer A: No, because cattle are domestic animals and domestic animals never create strict liability
Answer B: Yes, because the jurisdiction imposes strict liability for trespassing livestock crop damage
Answer C: No, unless the cattle had known vicious propensities
Answer D: Yes, because all domestic-animal owners are strictly liable for every kind of harm
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The cattle trespassed and damaged crops, and the facts supply a livestock-trespass strict-liability rule.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The stated jurisdiction imposes strict liability for livestock trespass damaging land or crops. C: The claim is for livestock trespass damaging crops under a stated strict-liability rule, not for a dangerous propensity attack. D: The correct reason is the jurisdiction's livestock-trespass rule, not universal domestic-animal strict liability.
lb_rule_reference: Some jurisdictions impose strict liability for trespassing livestock that damage another's land or crops, even though livestock are domestic animals.
