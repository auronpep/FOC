Question: Joshua is tried in federal court for transporting stolen goods in interstate commerce. Evidence shows that he drove from City A in State A to City B in State B. The court properly takes judicial notice that State A and State B are different states. Which instruction is proper?
Answer A: The jury must find that the interstate element is satisfied if it finds Joshua drove from City A to City B.
Answer B: The burden shifts to Joshua to prove that the route was not interstate.
Answer C: The jury may, but is not required to, accept the noticed geographic fact as conclusive.
Answer D: The court may not take judicial notice of geography in a criminal case.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: In a criminal case, the court must instruct the jury that it may or may not accept a noticed adjudicative fact as conclusive. Judicial notice does not require the jury to find an element or shift the burden to the defendant.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it makes the noticed fact mandatory in a criminal case. B: B is wrong because judicial notice cannot shift the prosecution's burden to Joshua. D: D is wrong because judicial notice may be used in criminal cases, subject to the proper jury instruction.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 201(f) / criminal geography instruction
