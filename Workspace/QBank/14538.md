Question: A seller borrowed $5,000 from a bank. Soon thereafter the seller filed for bankruptcy, having paid nothing on his debt to the bank. Five years after the debt had been discharged in bankruptcy, the seller contracted to sell certain goods to a buyer for $5,000. The contract provided that the buyer would pay the $5,000 to the bank. The only debt that the seller ever owed the bank is the $5,000 debt that was discharged in bankruptcy. The seller delivered the goods to the buyer, who accepted them. If the bank becomes aware of the contract between the seller and the buyer, and the buyer refuses to pay anything to the bank, is the bank likely to succeed in an action against the buyer for $5,000?
Answer A: A: No, because the buyer's promise to pay the bank was not supported by consideration.
Answer B: B: No, because the seller's debt was discharged in bankruptcy.
Answer C: C: Yes, because the bank was an intended beneficiary of the contract between the buyer and the seller.
Answer D: D: Yes, because no consideration is required to support a promise to pay a debt that has been discharged in bankruptcy.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: C is correct. The bank will recover $5,000 from the buyer because the contract between the buyer and seller explicitly designated performance (the payment of $5,000) to run to the bank, which establishes the bank as an intended third- party beneficiary. A
Wrong Answer Explanations: A is incorrect. The buyer's promise to pay the bank is enforceable because one party (the buyer) is making a third party (the bank) the beneficiary of its performance under a contract with another party (the seller) that is supported by consideration. B is incorrect. The bank was an intended beneficiary of an enforceable contract between the buyer and the seller, and the prior discharge in bankruptcy is irrelevant. D is incorrect. The bank will succeed, but not because of the discharge of the seller's debt in bankruptcy. Rather, the bank can enforce its rights under the contractual agreement between the buyer and the seller because it was an intended third-party beneficiary, irrespective of the bankruptcy proceedings.
