Question: Mary owned Lot 1, which benefited from an access easement over Lot 2. After a new public road was built to Lot 1, Mary wrote to the owner of Lot 2, “I am permanently giving up the old easement.” Mary then built a stone wall on Lot 1 across the only entrance to the easement route. Ten years later, Mary tried to use the easement again. What is the best result?
Answer A: The easement was likely abandoned because Mary's express statement and permanent wall showed intent and conduct inconsistent with keeping the easement.
Answer B: The easement still exists because nonuse alone can never matter.
Answer C: The easement still exists because abandonment requires a signed deed in every case.
Answer D: The easement was converted into fee title in Lot 2 because Mary once had access rights.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Abandonment requires intent to relinquish the easement plus conduct showing that intent. Mary expressly stated she was permanently giving up the easement and built a stone wall blocking her own use of the route, which supports abandonment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because this case includes more than nonuse. C: C is wrong because abandonment can occur through intent and conduct even apart from a formal release. D: D is wrong because an easement is a use right, not fee title.
lb_rule_reference: Abandonment / intent plus inconsistent act
lb_student_diagnostic: The repair signal is distinguishing bare nonuse from conduct that objectively shows relinquishment.
