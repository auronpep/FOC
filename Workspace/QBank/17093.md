Question: In a federal arson trial, before any witness has testified that there was a second fire, the prosecutor asks Caleb, “When you returned to start the second fire, what did you carry?” Caleb objects that the question assumes facts not in evidence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because the prosecutor may include any fact she expects to prove later.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because the question assumes a disputed fact not yet established in the record.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because assumed facts affect only the weight of the answer.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Caleb first denies starting any fire.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The court may control examination to prevent misleading or unfair questions. A question that assumes a disputed fact not in evidence—Caleb returned to start a second fire—may be objectionable in that form.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A overstates counsel's ability to embed unproved facts. C misclassifies a defective question form as mere weight. D adds an unnecessary denial prerequisite.
