Question: Samuel orally agreed to buy 800 plain wool blankets from Hannah for $2 each. Hannah immediately pulled 800 identical blankets from her regular inventory and stacked them in the warehouse for Samuel. The blankets were the same kind Hannah sold daily to other buyers. Samuel canceled before delivery and asserted the Statute of Frauds. Does the specially manufactured goods exception apply?
Answer A: Yes, because Hannah identified the blankets to Samuel's order.
Answer B: Yes, because Hannah began preparing for performance before Samuel canceled.
Answer C: No, because the blankets were ordinary inventory suitable for sale to others in Hannah's regular business.
Answer D: No, because the specially manufactured goods exception applies only to merchants on both sides.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The specially manufactured goods exception requires goods made for the buyer and not suitable for sale to others in the seller's ordinary course. Ordinary inventory set aside for a buyer is not specially manufactured merely because the seller prepared it for shipment.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because identification to the contract is not enough. B: B is wrong because ordinary preparation is not substantial beginning of specially manufactured goods. D: D is wrong because the issue is the character of the goods, not merchant status on both sides.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-201 / generic inventory not specially manufactured
