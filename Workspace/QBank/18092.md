Question: In a federal assault trial, Abigail testifies that she did not see Samson strike the victim. The prosecution asks Abigail whether Samson threatened her the night before trial by saying, “If you testify against me, your family will regret it.” Samson objects that the threat is unfairly prejudicial character evidence. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because threats always show character and never credibility.
Answer B: Sustain the objection because bias may be shown only by financial interest.
Answer C: Overrule the objection because the alleged threat tends to show Abigail may be afraid to testify against Samson.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the prosecution offers the threat to prove Samson committed the assault.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Evidence that a witness is afraid of a party may show bias, pressure, or motive to shade testimony. The threat is offered to explain Abigail's possible motive to protect Samson, not simply to prove Samson's bad character or guilt of the charged assault.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because threats can be relevant to witness bias. B: B is wrong because bias is not limited to financial interest. D: D is wrong because the valid route is witness bias, not substantive proof that Samson committed the assault.
