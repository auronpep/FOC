Question: In federal litigation, the plaintiff serves 40 interrogatories on the defendant and 10 interrogatories on a nonparty eyewitness. The plaintiff did not obtain a stipulation or court order expanding the limit. Which objection is strongest?
Answer A: Interrogatories may be served on any person with relevant information, but only 25 may be served on each person
Answer B: The plaintiff exceeded the presumptive 25-interrogatory limit and improperly served interrogatories on a nonparty
Answer C: The interrogatories are proper because there is no numerical limit in federal court
Answer D: The interrogatories are improper only if they ask for legal contentions
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Rule 33 limits interrogatories to parties and presumptively caps them at 25 including discrete subparts.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Interrogatories are served on parties, not nonparties. C: Rule 33 includes a presumptive numerical limit. D: Contention interrogatories are not categorically barred, and the problem here is number and target.
lb_rule_reference: Interrogatories may be served only on parties and are presumptively limited to 25, including discrete subparts, unless otherwise stipulated or ordered.
