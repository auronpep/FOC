Outline: Roles of judge and jury
Question: In a federal civil case, Esther seeks to call Nathan to testify about a conversation he overheard between Abigail and Abigail's attorney. Abigail objects on attorney-client privilege grounds. Esther argues that the jury should hear the conversation and decide whether it was privileged. Who determines whether the communication is privileged?
Answer A: The jury, because privilege turns on disputed facts.
Answer B: The judge, because privilege is a preliminary question of admissibility.
Answer C: The jury, unless the communication was written.
Answer D: The judge, but only after the jury first hears the communication.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Privilege is a preliminary question for the court. The judge decides whether the facts establish a privilege before the jury hears the protected communication.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because disputed preliminary facts do not automatically make privilege a jury question. C: C is wrong because the oral/written form does not transfer the privilege determination to the jury. D: D is wrong because the jury should not first hear allegedly privileged material.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(a), 501
