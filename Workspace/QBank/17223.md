MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Constitutional Law
Subtopic: Judicial Review
Outline: Constitutional limitations and justiciability
Question: A speaker alleges that a private social-media platform removed her posts after federal officials publicly criticized misinformation. She sues the officials and seeks an injunction barring them from criticizing the platform. The platform is not a party, and it submits a declaration stating that it independently enforces its own content rules and would not restore the posts even if the officials were enjoined. Which Article III element is most vulnerable?
Answer A: Redressability, because the requested injunction against officials is unlikely to restore posts controlled by an independent nonparty platform
Answer B: Redressability exists because any judicial order might pressure the platform to change its mind
Answer C: Standing automatically exists because speech claims are never dismissed on threshold grounds
Answer D: The case is moot because the officials deny coercing the platform
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The relief sought would bind the officials, not the platform. If the platform independently refuses restoration, the injunction is unlikely to redress the speaker's injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Redressability requires likely relief, not speculation that a nonparty may voluntarily respond. C: Article III requirements apply to speech claims too. D: Mootness concerns whether a once-live dispute ended. The problem here is whether the requested relief is likely to redress the injury.
lb_rule_reference: Redressability is missing when the requested court order would not likely remedy the injury because the decisive actor is an independent nonparty.
