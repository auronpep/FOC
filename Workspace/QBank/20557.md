Question: A state suspends a driver's license for six months after an accident report, without notice or any chance to contest fault. State law allows suspension only when the driver caused the accident and failed to satisfy a judgment. What is the best due process analysis?
Answer A: No process is due because a driver's license is a privilege, not property
Answer B: The license interest triggers due process before or around suspension
Answer C: Strict scrutiny applies because driving is a fundamental right
Answer D: Process is due only after the six-month suspension ends
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The driver has a protected interest in continued licensing, and fault is contestable.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A license protected from suspension except for cause can be a property interest. C: Driving is not treated as a fundamental right; the claim is procedural due process. D: Review after the entire deprivation may be too late when the state can provide timely contest procedures.
lb_rule_reference: A state-issued license that may be suspended only for cause can be a protected property interest, so the government must provide appropriate process before or promptly after suspension depending on context.
