Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Joseph contracted with Elizabeth for booth space at a craft fair. Elizabeth failed to provide the booth. Joseph's expected fair profits are too speculative. After contracting, Joseph reasonably spent $3,000 on display materials usable only at Elizabeth's fair. Joseph also bought a $45,000 delivery van that he planned to use for many future fairs; after Elizabeth's breach, the van remained worth $45,000 and was fully usable in Joseph's business. What may Joseph recover as reliance damages?
Answer A: $48,000, because Joseph bought both the materials and the van after contracting.
Answer B: $45,000, because the van was the largest expenditure made in reliance on the fair.
Answer C: $3,000, because only the fair-specific materials produced a net reliance loss.
Answer D: Nothing, because the expected fair profits are speculative.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The remedy interest is reliance under common law. The recoverable loss is the net reasonable expenditure caused by the breached contract. The fair-specific $3,000 display materials are a reliance loss; the van remains fully valuable and usable, so awarding its price would overcompensate Joseph. The limiting gates are causation, reasonableness, and offset, and the result scope is limited. The answer does not award expected fair profits.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it includes a retained asset with no net loss, producing WAL-REM-008 overcompensation. B: B is wrong because it chooses the largest expenditure without applying causation and offset, a WAL-REM-002 wrong-gate error. D: D is wrong because speculative profits do not bar proven reliance damages.
lb_rule_reference: Common law reliance damages — recover net reasonable reliance loss, not fully retained assets
