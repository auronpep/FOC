Question: A city building inspector writes a required inspection report as part of her job. The report criticizes a contractor's safety practices. After supervisors discipline her for the report's contents, she argues she was speaking as a private citizen because the report addressed public safety. What is the first key issue?
Answer A: Whether she spoke pursuant to her official job duties
Answer B: Whether public employees lose all First Amendment rights when hired
Answer C: Whether the contractor is a private company
Answer D: Whether safety is always a public concern that automatically protects the report
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Speech made as part of official duties generally is not treated as citizen speech even if the topic matters to the public.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Public employees retain some speech rights, but the official-duty threshold matters. C: The speech claim is against the public employer, and the employee's role is the key threshold. D: Official-duty speech can fall outside citizen-speech protection even when it addresses an important topic.
lb_rule_reference: When a public employee speaks pursuant to official duties, the speech is generally not protected as citizen speech under the First Amendment.
