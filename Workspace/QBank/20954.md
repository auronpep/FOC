Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Statements and Confessions
Question: After Miranda warnings, Selena said, "I do not want to talk." Detectives immediately stopped and returned Selena to a holding cell. Three hours later, a different detective gave fresh Miranda warnings and asked only about a separate robbery. Selena waived and confessed to that robbery. Is the later confession automatically barred by Selena's earlier silence invocation?
Answer A: Yes, because any invocation of silence permanently bars all police questioning
Answer B: Yes, because a different detective cannot cure the earlier invocation
Answer C: No, because police stopped questioning, waited, gave fresh warnings, and obtained a later waiver for a limited interview
Answer D: No, because silence invocations have no legal effect unless the suspect also requests counsel
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The detectives honored Selena's initial choice to stop. The later questioning followed a meaningful break and fresh warnings, so the earlier invocation does not automatically bar the later confession.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Silence invocations must be scrupulously honored, but they do not create an absolute permanent bar to all later questioning. B: The key facts are that questioning stopped, time passed, fresh warnings were given, and the later interview was limited. D: Silence invocations do have legal effect. They were simply honored on these facts.
lb_rule_reference: A clear invocation of silence bars immediate continued interrogation, but later questioning can be valid after a meaningful break, fresh warnings, and a limited renewed interview.
