Question: For 12 years, Deborah walked across a narrow footpath on Ezekiel's land to reach a garden. She never asked permission. The jurisdiction's prescriptive period is 10 years. After the 12th year, Deborah bought a truck and began driving it over the same path, widening the path and damaging nearby shrubs. What is the best result?
Answer A: Deborah may use the path for any purpose because a prescriptive easement has no scope limits.
Answer B: Deborah owns the path because she used it for more than the statutory period.
Answer C: Deborah has no right to walk because later vehicle use destroys all prior prescriptive rights.
Answer D: Any prescriptive easement is limited by the nature and extent of the historical use, so vehicle use may exceed the footpath easement's scope.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The scope of a prescriptive easement is measured by the character and extent of the use during the prescriptive period. Deborah's historical use was walking on a narrow footpath, so later vehicle use that widens the path and causes damage may exceed the easement's scope.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because prescriptive easements have scope limits. B: B is wrong because prescription creates a use right, not title. C: C is wrong because later misuse does not automatically destroy the historically acquired walking easement. D is correct for the reasons above.
lb_rule_reference: Scope of prescriptive easement measured by historical use
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is proving prescription and then reading the easement as unlimited.
