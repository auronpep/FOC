Question: Daniel told the prosecutor, “I might let you read my emails with my lawyer if you offer a better plea.” Daniel never produced, summarized, or quoted any attorney-client communication. The prosecutor later argues that Daniel waived privilege by offering to cooperate. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Daniel did not waive privilege merely by offering possible disclosure without actually revealing significant privileged content.
Answer B: Daniel waived privilege because waiver depends only on an intent to disclose.
Answer C: Daniel waived privilege because plea discussions are never confidential.
Answer D: Daniel waived privilege as to all communications with his lawyer in the case.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: Waiver generally requires actual disclosure of privileged communication or conduct that puts the privileged content at issue. A bargaining statement that Daniel might disclose later does not itself reveal privileged material. The decisive axis is completed disclosure versus intent to waive.
