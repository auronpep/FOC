Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Duty of reasonable prudent person in prevailing circumstances
Question: A driver was traveling at the speed limit when a cabinet fell from an unsecured pickup truck directly ahead. The driver had less than two seconds to react and swerved into a parked car rather than hit the cabinet head-on. The owner of the parked car sues the driver for negligence. How should the emergency affect the standard of care in the parked-car owner's negligence claim?
Answer A: The driver is automatically not negligent because any emergency excuses all mistakes
Answer B: The driver is judged as a reasonably prudent person facing the same sudden emergency
Answer C: The driver is held to a subjective standard based on what she personally thought was safest
Answer D: The driver is strictly liable because she intentionally turned the wheel into the parked car
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Because the emergency was not of the driver's making, the factfinder considers the limited reaction time and danger when applying the objective reasonable-person standard.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Emergency circumstances are considered in applying reasonable care, but they do not create complete immunity for every response. C: The standard remains objective. The circumstances include the emergency, but the question is what a reasonably prudent person would do. D: A deliberate evasive maneuver in an emergency is judged under negligence principles, not strict liability merely because the steering was intentional.
lb_rule_reference: A sudden emergency not of the defendant's making is considered as part of the circumstances for objective reasonable care, but it does not create automatic immunity.
