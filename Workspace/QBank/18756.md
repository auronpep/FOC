MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Intentional Torts
Outline: Trespass to land
Question: A homeowner invited a repair technician inside to inspect a sink. After the inspection, the homeowner told the technician to leave because the price was too high. The technician refused, sat at the kitchen table for fifteen minutes, and said he would not leave until the homeowner signed the estimate. The homeowner sues the technician for trespass to land. Is the homeowner likely to prevail in a trespass to land claim against the technician?
Answer A: No, because the technician entered the house with permission
Answer B: Yes, because the technician intentionally remained after consent to be on the property was revoked
Answer C: No, because the technician caused no physical damage
Answer D: Yes, but only if the technician intended to trespass
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The technician's initial license ended when the homeowner ordered him to leave. His intentional refusal to leave was a continuing trespass.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Consent can be limited and revoked. Intentionally remaining after permission ends can be trespass. C: Trespass to land does not require physical damage. Unauthorized presence is enough. D: The defendant need only intend the physical presence after consent ends. He need not know the legal label or intend wrongdoing.
lb_rule_reference: A person who initially enters with permission may become a trespasser by intentionally remaining after consent is revoked.
