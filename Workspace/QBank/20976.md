Question: In a federal extortion trial, Miriam testifies that she received a threatening call from a person who used a phrase that Isaiah often used and knew a private nickname only Isaiah and two others knew. Miriam says she is not completely certain the caller was Isaiah. The prosecutor offers Miriam's testimony to identify Isaiah as the caller. Isaiah objects that Miriam cannot identify the voice with certainty. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Exclude the testimony because voice identification is admissible only if the witness is completely certain.
Answer B: Admit the testimony if there is evidence sufficient to support a finding that Isaiah was the caller; Miriam's uncertainty goes to weight.
Answer C: Exclude the testimony unless the prosecutor produces the original recording of the call.
Answer D: Admit the testimony only if Isaiah first testifies and denies making the call.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The identity of the caller is a conditional fact. If there is evidence sufficient to support a finding that Isaiah was the caller, the testimony may be admitted and the jury decides identity and weight. Complete certainty is not required for admissibility.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because uncertainty usually affects weight, not threshold admissibility. C: C is wrong because the witness is testifying from personal perception of the call, not proving the contents of a recording. D: D is wrong because the opponent need not testify before the proponent may offer identification evidence.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 104(b) / voice identification and conditional relevance
