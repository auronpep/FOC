Outline: Homicide
Question: After a bitter business dispute, Vance told Leah, "Someday soon, I will make you pay." Two days later, Leah saw Vance sitting unarmed at a cafe. Vance did not approach or threaten her. Leah, fearing he would eventually attack, pulled a gun and shot him. Vance died. Does Leah have a complete self-defense justification for the killing?
Answer A: Yes, because Vance had threatened her in the past
Answer B: Yes, because self-defense depends only on Leah's honest fear
Answer C: No, because there was no imminent threat justifying deadly force
Answer D: No, because self-defense is never available in homicide cases
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Vance was unarmed, not approaching, and not making an immediate threat when Leah shot him. A future or speculative danger does not satisfy the imminence requirement for deadly self-defense.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Deadly self-defense requires imminent danger. A vague future threat made days earlier, with Vance sitting unarmed and not approaching, does not justify immediate deadly force. B: The belief must be honest and reasonable, and deadly force requires imminent threat of death or serious injury. Leah's fear was not reasonable on these facts. D: Deadly self-defense can justify homicide when its elements are met. The defense fails here because imminence and reasonableness are missing.
lb_rule_reference: Deadly self-defense requires an honest and reasonable belief in imminent unlawful deadly force or serious bodily harm; fear of future harm is not enough.
