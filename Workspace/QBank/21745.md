Outline: Personal knowledge
Question: In a federal contract case, Isaac testifies that he personally heard Martha say on a conference call, “I accept the amended price of 300 shekels.” Unknown to Isaac at the time, the conference service recorded the call. Martha objects that Isaac cannot testify to the exact words unless the recording is produced. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because exact words must always be proved by a recording if one exists.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Isaac first proves the recording was lost.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if the recording is also admitted as a duplicate.
Answer D: Overrule the objection because Isaac is testifying from personal knowledge of the conversation, not proving the contents of the recording.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A witness with personal knowledge may testify to words he personally heard. The original-writing rule does not require the recording unless the proponent is trying to prove the contents of the recording itself.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because exact words heard live may be proved by a percipient witness. B: B is wrong because no loss showing is required when the rule is not triggered. C: C is wrong because the testimony does not depend on admission of the recording.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 1002; personal knowledge
lb_student_diagnostic: Exact-words false positive: student assumes exact phrasing automatically triggers the recording rule.
