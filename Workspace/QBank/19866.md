Question: Jacob wanted to cross Leah's land to reach a well. Jacob wrote and signed a memorandum stating, “I have a permanent easement over Leah's land to reach the well.” Leah never signed a deed, memorandum, or other writing granting Jacob a right to cross. Leah allowed Jacob to cross twice as a courtesy, but Jacob made no improvements in reliance on that permission. Jacob claims an express easement. What is the best result?
Answer A: Jacob has an express easement because his signed memorandum describes the route he wants to use.
Answer B: Jacob has an express easement because Leah allowed two crossings and did not immediately object.
Answer C: Jacob does not have an express easement because the burdened owner did not sign a writing granting the land-use right.
Answer D: Jacob has an express easement unless Leah proves that the memorandum was not recorded.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: An express easement burdens land and generally must satisfy the statute of frauds through a writing signed by the owner of the burdened land or that owner's authorized agent. Jacob's unilateral memorandum cannot grant him a property interest in Leah's land.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the benefited user's own writing does not burden another person's land. B: B is wrong because brief courtesy use is permission, not an express easement. D: D is wrong because recording is not the missing creation element; the missing element is Leah's written grant.
lb_rule_reference: Statute of frauds / burdened owner signature
