Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Elijah owned one 80-acre farm on the Jordan Road. He signed and delivered a deed stating, “I convey the north 40 acres of my 80-acre farm on the Jordan Road to Elisha.” A surveyor could identify the north 40 acres from the boundaries of the farm. Elisha accepted. Who owns the north 40 acres?
Answer A: Elijah, because a deed conveying part of a tract is always invalid.
Answer B: Elijah, because the deed did not attach the surveyor's map.
Answer C: Elisha, because the deed provides an objective way to identify the portion conveyed.
Answer D: Elisha only if Elijah later stakes the boundary line.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is whether the described portion can be identified with reasonable certainty. A deed may convey part of a tract if it provides an objective description. “The north 40 acres” of a known 80-acre farm can be located, so title to that portion passes to Elisha.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because deeds can convey portions of larger tracts. B: B is wrong because an attached map is not required when the description itself supplies an objective way to identify the land. D: D is wrong because later staking is not a validity condition when the portion is already ascertainable.
