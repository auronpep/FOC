Question: Noah privately told his lawyer, Mary, “I lied on last year's tax return, and I need advice about defending myself if I am charged.” Noah did not ask Mary to hide records, create false documents, or continue the lie. Is Noah's statement privileged?
Answer A: No, because communications admitting crimes are never privileged.
Answer B: Yes, because a confidential communication seeking legal advice about past wrongdoing remains privileged absent a future crime or fraud purpose.
Answer C: No, because tax crimes are an exception to attorney-client privilege.

Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A client may seek legal advice about past wrongdoing, including possible criminal exposure. The crime-fraud exception targets advice sought to further future or ongoing misconduct, not a confidential request for defense advice about completed conduct. The decisive axis is past wrongdoing versus future misuse of counsel.
