Question: A clinic bought a diagnostic scanner from a manufacturer under a signed agreement that included installation and staff training. Most of the price was allocated to the scanner. The agreement stated that training would be “as described in attached Schedule 2,” and Schedule 2 listed four onsite training sessions. The agreement also stated that support calls were limited to business hours. Before signing, the manufacturer orally promised a 24-hour support hotline for the first month. The clinic seeks to prove both the four-session training schedule and the 24-hour hotline promise. Which evidence should the court consider as part of the contract content?
Answer A: Both the attached Schedule 2 and the prior oral 24-hour hotline promise.
Answer B: Neither item, because the final writing is the only source of contract content.
Answer C: Schedule 2 only.
Answer D: The 24-hour hotline promise only.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The narrow rule is that material incorporated into or attached to a signed writing is part of the writing, while a prior oral promise that contradicts an express term is generally excluded. The trigger facts are the agreement's reference to attached Schedule 2 and the express business-hours support term. The decisive locks are integration and term status. Schedule 2 is written contract content; the hotline promise contradicts the support-hours limit.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it fails to split incorporated content from a contradictory prior oral term. B: B is wrong because Schedule 2 is not outside the writing once attached and referenced. D: D is wrong because it selects the contradictory prior oral term while excluding the incorporated schedule.
lb_rule_reference: Incorporation by reference / contradictory prior term
