Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Moses contracted to repair Ruth's barn for $100,000. Moses expected the full job to cost $80,000, leaving a $20,000 profit. After Moses spent $30,000 on labor and materials, Ruth wrongfully ordered Moses to stop and refused to pay anything. Moses avoided the remaining $50,000 in performance costs. What is Moses's proper expectation recovery?
Answer A: $30,000, because Moses may recover only costs already incurred.
Answer B: $50,000, because Moses recovers costs incurred plus expected profit.
Answer C: $80,000, because that was Moses's expected cost of performance.
Answer D: $100,000, because Ruth repudiated the contract.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: When an owner wrongfully stops a construction or service contract after partial performance, the nonbreaching contractor's expectation recovery is commonly costs already incurred plus expected profit, with costs saved excluded. Moses spent $30,000 and expected $20,000 profit, so his recovery is $50,000.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it omits Moses's expected profit. C: C is wrong because total expected cost is not the loss caused by Ruth's breach. D: D is wrong because full contract price would overcompensate Moses by ignoring the $50,000 he did not have to spend.
