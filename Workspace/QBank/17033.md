Subtopic: Hearsay and Circumstances of Its Admissibility
Outline: Hearsay rule
Question: In a federal robbery prosecution, the prosecutor knows before trial that Caleb will deny hearing the defendant confess. Caleb's alleged earlier statement was not under oath and is not otherwise admissible for its truth. The prosecutor calls Caleb solely so that, after Caleb denies hearing any confession, the prosecutor can have another witness repeat Caleb's earlier accusation that “the defendant confessed to me.” The defendant objects that the impeachment use is a pretext to place inadmissible hearsay before the jury. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Overrule the objection because FRE 607 always allows a party to impeach any witness without limitation.
Answer B: Overrule the objection because any prior inconsistent statement is automatically substantive evidence.
Answer C: Sustain the objection or limit the evidence because the court may prevent impeachment from being used as a pretext to put otherwise inadmissible hearsay before the jury.
Answer D: Sustain the objection only if Caleb is the defendant's relative.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Although FRE 607 allows a party to impeach its own witness, that permission does not make impeachment a license to place otherwise inadmissible hearsay before the jury as a pretext. The court may use its control and balancing authority to exclude or limit impeachment material when its impeachment value is substantially outweighed by the danger that the jury will misuse it for its truth.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because the right to impeach one's own witness is not unlimited. B: B is wrong because an ordinary unsworn prior inconsistent statement is not automatically substantive evidence. D: D is wrong because the issue is improper use of impeachment material, not family relationship.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 607 and 403 / impeachment material as backdoor hearsay
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests the advanced subterfuge problem: impeachment material cannot be used primarily as a backdoor substitute for inadmissible substantive proof.
