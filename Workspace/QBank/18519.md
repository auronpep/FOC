Subtopic: Motions
Outline: Motion for more definite statement
Question: A complaint alleges that the defendant breached a written supply contract by failing to deliver specified equipment on June 1. It attaches the contract and identifies the missed delivery. The defendant moves under Rule 12(e) for a more definite statement, arguing that the complaint does not list every witness or attach all delivery emails. How should the court rule on the Rule 12(e) motion?
Answer A: Grant it because complaints must include all trial evidence
Answer B: Grant it because every contract claim must plead every email exchanged
Answer C: Deny it only if the plaintiff proves the breach by affidavit
Answer D: Deny it because the complaint is clear enough for the defendant to respond
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: The complaint names the contract, equipment, date, and alleged breach. Missing evidentiary detail is not the same as a vague or ambiguous pleading under Rule 12(e).
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Rule 12(e) is not discovery. Pleadings do not need to list all evidence or witnesses. B: The rule requires enough clarity to respond, not every supporting document. The complaint identifies the contract and breach. C: Rule 12(e) asks whether the defendant can reasonably respond to the pleading. The plaintiff need not prove the claim by affidavit.
lb_rule_reference: Rule 12(e) permits a motion for a more definite statement when a pleading is so vague or ambiguous that the responding party cannot reasonably prepare a response.
