Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Samuel agreed to install a new roof on Hannah's house for $40,000. Before beginning work, Samuel wrongfully refused to perform. Hannah promptly hired another qualified roofer to do the same work for a reasonable price of $46,000. What is Hannah's proper expectation recovery from Samuel?
Answer A: $6,000, the reasonable substitute price minus the contract price.
Answer B: $40,000, the original contract price.
Answer C: $46,000, the price Hannah paid the substitute roofer.
Answer D: Nothing, because Samuel breached before beginning performance.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: For a service provider's refusal to perform, the injured owner's direct expectation measure is the reasonable cost of substitute performance minus the original contract price. Hannah had to pay $6,000 more to obtain the promised roof.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because the contract price is what Hannah expected to pay, not her loss. C: C is wrong because it ignores the $40,000 Hannah would have paid Samuel. D: D is wrong because breach before performance can still cause a substitute-price differential.
lb_rule_reference: Common law expectation damages; cost of substitute performance
