Question: A plaintiff files a federal civil rights claim and a related state negligence claim in federal court. After discovery, the court grants summary judgment to the defendant on the federal claim. Only the state negligence claim remains, and no diversity exists. What may the court do with the state claim?
Answer A: It must retain the state claim because supplemental jurisdiction once attached forever
Answer B: It must dismiss the whole case for lack of original jurisdiction retroactively
Answer C: It may decline supplemental jurisdiction because all claims within original jurisdiction have been dismissed
Answer D: It may retain the state claim only if the defendant consents
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Dismissal of all anchor claims is an express discretionary-decline ground.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Section 1367(c) allows discretionary decline in this posture. B: Original jurisdiction existed when the federal claim was filed. D: The statute gives the court discretion; party consent is not the hook.
lb_rule_reference: Section 1367(c) permits the district court to decline supplemental jurisdiction after dismissing all claims over which it had original jurisdiction.
