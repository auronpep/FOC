Outline: Reliance damages
Question: Nathan contracted with Rebecca to provide catering for Rebecca's banquet. Rebecca repudiated two weeks before the banquet. Before the repudiation, Nathan reasonably spent $6,000 on nonrefundable permits and special supplies. After the repudiation, Nathan spent another $5,000 preparing food for the cancelled banquet even though he knew Rebecca would not hold it and could have avoided the expense. If Nathan cannot prove expected profits, what reliance amount may he recover?
Answer A: $11,000, because all amounts were spent for the banquet.
Answer B: $5,000, because only food preparation is true performance.
Answer C: Nothing, because Rebecca repudiated before Nathan fully performed.
Answer D: $6,000, because the later $5,000 was an avoidable post-repudiation expense.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: Reliance damages do not include avoidable expenditures made after the injured party knows the other side will not perform. Nathan may recover the $6,000 incurred before repudiation but not the $5,000 spent after repudiation despite knowledge of cancellation.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because it includes avoidable post-repudiation costs. B: B is wrong because post-repudiation food preparation is the excluded expense. C: C is wrong because full performance is not required for reliance recovery.
lb_rule_reference: Reliance damages / avoidable continuation after breach
