Subtopic: Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons (Criminal Procedure)
Outline: Identifications
Question: After indictment, police schedule a live lineup. Officers tell Omar that he has the right to have counsel present at the lineup and that the lineup can be delayed until counsel arrives. Omar says he understands and signs a written waiver. The victim identifies Omar. Is the identification automatically excluded because counsel was absent?
Answer A: Yes, because counsel at a post-indictment lineup can never be waived
Answer B: No, if Omar's waiver was knowing, voluntary, and intelligent
Answer C: Yes, because waivers are valid only for trial, not lineups
Answer D: No, but only because the victim made an accurate identification
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Police advised Omar of the lineup counsel right and he signed a waiver. A valid waiver permits the uncounseled lineup.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The right exists, but a defendant can waive it knowingly, voluntarily, and intelligently. C: A defendant can waive counsel at other critical stages as well. D: The counsel issue is resolved by waiver, not by after-the-fact accuracy.
lb_rule_reference: A defendant may waive counsel at a post-attachment lineup if the waiver is knowing, voluntary, and intelligent.
