Outline: Requirements for valid deed
Question: Jacob signed a writing that accurately described his house and named Leah. The writing stated, “I plan to give this house to Leah next month if I still feel the same way.” Jacob handed the writing to Leah, and Leah thanked him. Jacob died the next week before signing any other document. Leah claims the house under the writing. Who owns the house?
Answer A: Leah, because Jacob signed a writing describing the house and naming her.
Answer B: Jacob's estate, because the writing did not contain present operative words of transfer.
Answer C: Leah, because no consideration was required.
Answer D: Leah, because delivery of any signed writing about land transfers title.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The title source claimed is a deed, and the decisive validity gate is present operative words of conveyance. Jacob's writing expressed a future plan conditioned on his later state of mind; it did not presently convey title. Consideration and delivery decoys do not cure the missing present-transfer language, so the house remains in Jacob's estate.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because a signed land description and named grantee are not enough without present words of transfer. C: C is wrong because the no-consideration rule applies only after there is an otherwise valid conveyance. D: D is wrong because delivery cannot turn a future promise into a present deed.
lb_rule_reference: Operative words of conveyance / future promise
