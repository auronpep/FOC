Outline: Arson
Question: Angry at a neighbor, Felix lights a pile of wet newspapers on the neighbor's concrete porch. The newspapers smoke heavily and blacken the exterior wall, but the wall, porch, and trim are not scorched, charred, or burned. Has Felix completed common-law arson of the dwelling?
Answer A: Yes, because smoke marks on a house are enough burning
Answer B: Yes, because Felix intended to damage the dwelling
Answer C: No, because smoke discoloration alone is not the burning required for common-law arson
Answer D: No, because arson requires the entire dwelling to be destroyed
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: The facts show smoke staining only. Without charring or scorching of the structure, completed common-law arson is not established.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Common-law arson requires burning of the structure. Smoke discoloration without charring or scorching is not enough. B: Intent does not replace the required burning result for completed arson. D: Total destruction is not required. Charring a small structural part can be enough.
lb_rule_reference: For common-law arson, scorching or charring of the structure can satisfy burning, but smoke discoloration alone does not.
