Question: Solomon conveyed Farmacre “to Esther and her heirs, but if Esther stops farming Farmacre before Esther turns 40, then to Nathan and his heirs.” Nathan later validly released all of his rights in Farmacre to Esther. Esther then stopped farming before she turned 40. What is the effect of Esther's later decision to stop farming?
Answer A: Farmacre automatically shifts to Nathan because the original deed named him.
Answer B: Esther keeps Farmacre in fee simple absolute because Nathan's executory interest was released to her before the condition occurred.
Answer C: Farmacre reverts to Solomon because the third-party gift-over was released.
Answer D: Esther keeps possession only until Solomon exercises a right of entry.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Esther keeps Farmacre in fee simple absolute. Nathan held the only divesting executory interest, and he validly released that interest to Esther before the condition occurred. Once the divesting future interest was extinguished in the present owner, Esther's estate was no longer subject to Nathan's gift-over.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because Nathan released his interest before the condition occurred. C: C is wrong because Solomon did not retain a possibility of reverter or right of entry. D: D is wrong because no right of entry was reserved to Solomon, and the divesting third-party interest was extinguished.
lb_rule_reference: Release of divesting future interest / fee becomes absolute
