Question: In a federal premises-liability trial, Naomi offers a photograph of a broken stair. Naomi testifies that she took the photograph the morning after she fell and that it fairly shows the stair as it appeared then. The defendant objects that the actual stair, not a photograph, is required. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because only the actual stair is real evidence.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless the photograph is the only available proof of the stair.
Answer C: Overrule the objection if Naomi's testimony is sufficient to authenticate the photograph as a fair depiction.
Answer D: Overrule the objection only if the photograph is treated as hearsay.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A photograph may be authenticated by testimony that it fairly depicts what it claims to show. The proponent need not bring the actual stair into court.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A overrequires the real object. B wrongly treats photographs as secondary proof that requires necessity.
