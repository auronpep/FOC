Outline: Lay opinion testimony
Question: In a federal assault case, Caleb testifies that he was standing near Hannah just after a loud argument. Caleb is asked whether Hannah appeared frightened. Hannah's opponent objects that the question calls for speculation about Hannah's mental state. How should the court rule?
Answer A: Sustain the objection because no witness may describe another person's apparent emotion.
Answer B: Sustain the objection unless Hannah first testifies that she was frightened.
Answer C: Overrule the objection only if Caleb is a psychologist.
Answer D: Overrule the objection if Caleb's opinion is based on Hannah's observed appearance, voice, and conduct.
Correct Answer: D
Correct Answer Explanation: A lay witness may describe another person's apparent emotional condition when the opinion is rationally based on observed appearance, conduct, tone, or demeanor and is helpful to the jury. The opinion is not barred merely because it describes an apparent emotion.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because apparent emotional condition can be described through lay opinion. B: B is wrong because Hannah need not testify first. C: C is wrong because Caleb is describing ordinary observed appearance and conduct, not giving a psychological diagnosis.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 701 / appearance and emotional condition
lb_student_diagnostic: Tests apparent emotional state as admissible lay shorthand.
