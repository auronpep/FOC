Subtopic: Strict Liability and Products Liability
Outline: Strict Products Liability
Question: A consumer bought a small countertop blender designed for food preparation. Ignoring its purpose, the consumer removed the jar, attached a homemade metal disk to the exposed motor shaft, and used the base as a workshop grinder. The disk shattered and injured the consumer. Nothing was wrong with the blender during ordinary kitchen use. What is the manufacturer's strongest defense?
Answer A: The consumer's unforeseeable misuse caused the injury
Answer B: Misuse never matters in strict products liability
Answer C: The manufacturer is liable because any injury involving the product proves defect
Answer D: The manufacturer is liable unless it warned against every possible nonfood use
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The consumer transformed a kitchen blender into a homemade grinder. That use was outside ordinary and reasonably foreseeable product use and caused the injury.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: Product defenses include unforeseeable misuse and substantial alterations that cause the harm. C: The injury came from an unforeseeable transformation, not a defect in ordinary product design or manufacture. D: Manufacturers need not warn against every bizarre, unforeseeable misuse.
lb_rule_reference: Unforeseeable misuse that causes the injury can defeat a products-liability claim.
