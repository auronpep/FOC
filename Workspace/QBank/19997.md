Question: A state statute provides that only U.S. citizens may be employed in any position within a state or local government agency. A lawfully admitted permanent resident applies for a position as a state laboratory technician at a public health department, processing water quality samples. She is denied solely because of her non-citizen status. She challenges the blanket government-employment restriction under the Equal Protection Clause. Which of the following most accurately describes how a court should analyze the blanket citizenship requirement applied to a water-laboratory technician position?
Answer A: The blanket citizenship requirement survives under the political function exception because the laboratory technician is employed by a government agency, which categorically qualifies for the exception
Answer B: The blanket citizenship requirement is unconstitutional as applied because it applies to all state employees indiscriminately, including those in positions that do not implicate representative self-governance
Answer C: The blanket citizenship requirement is constitutional under strict scrutiny because public health is a compelling state interest and restricting government health positions to citizens is narrowly tailored to ensuring civic commitment to the state's public welfare
Answer D: The court applies rational basis because the state's preference for citizen employees in government agencies is a reasonable expression of civic sovereignty
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: A blanket state ban on alien public employment fails Equal Protection because it sweeps in positions that do not go to the heart of representative government. A water-quality laboratory technician processes samples; she does not exercise coercive authority or formulate public policy. The political function exception may justify citizen-only requirements for some positions (police, probation officers, elected officials), but a blanket ban — applied categorically without regard to whether each individual position implicates self-governance — is not narrowly tailored and fails strict scrutiny as applied to positions like laboratory technician.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: The political function exception is narrow. It applies to positions that go to the heart of representative government — positions requiring the exercise of governmental authority, coercive power, or broad policy-making judgment. Technical, ministerial, or service positions within government agencies do not qualify. A water-quality laboratory technician does not exercise governmental authority or policy-making power. C: The fact that the agency serves the public health does not mean all positions within it require civic commitment sufficient to satisfy narrow tailoring. Narrow tailoring requires the state to use means that are specifically calibrated to the interest. A blanket citizenship requirement for every government employee — including those with no policy role — is not narrowly tailored. D: Alienage classifications by states receive strict scrutiny. The political function exception is a narrow carve-out that permits certain citizen-only requirements without lowering the scrutiny tier. Where the exception does not apply — as here, to a ministerial technical position — strict scrutiny governs and the government must satisfy it.
lb_rule_reference: Under the political function exception to strict alienage scrutiny, states may restrict to citizens positions that go to the heart of representative government — including police officers, probation officers, and public school teachers — but not purely technical or service-oriented public positions.
