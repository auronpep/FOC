Outline: Expectation damages
Question: Caleb contracted to buy 1,000 lamps from Naomi for $40 each. At the time of contracting, Caleb told Naomi that he had a signed resale contract to sell the lamps to a buyer for $55 each. Naomi failed to deliver, and comparable lamps were unavailable. Caleb proves the resale contract and his saved handling costs with certainty. What lost-profit damages may Caleb recover from Naomi?
Answer A: Nothing, because resale profits are always too remote in a goods contract.
Answer B: Only the contract price of $40,000.
Answer C: The foreseeable resale profit, reduced by any costs Caleb saved.
Answer D: The full resale revenue of $55,000 without subtracting Caleb's purchase cost or saved expenses.
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: A buyer's consequential damages can include losses from requirements or needs the seller had reason to know at the time of contracting and that could not reasonably be prevented by cover. Caleb disclosed the resale contract at formation, could not cover, and can prove net profit with certainty.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because foreseeable resale profits may be recoverable if the UCC consequential-damages gates are met. B: B is wrong because the contract price is Caleb's cost, not the lost-profit measure. D: D is wrong because lost profits are net, not gross resale revenue.
lb_rule_reference: UCC §2-715 / consequential lost resale profits
