Subtopic: Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence
Outline: Probative value
Question: Aaron is charged with burning his own storage building. The prosecution offers evidence that Aaron increased the fire-insurance coverage on the building two weeks before the fire. Aaron objects that the insurance evidence does not prove he set the fire. Assuming no separate exclusion applies, how should the court rule?
Answer A: Admit the evidence, because the increased coverage has some tendency to show motive.
Answer B: Exclude the evidence, because insurance can never be relevant.
Answer C: Exclude the evidence, because motive evidence must prove the charged act by itself.
Answer D: Admit the evidence only if the prosecution first proves the insurer paid Aaron.
Correct Answer: A
Correct Answer Explanation: The recent increase in coverage tends to show a possible financial motive for burning the building. The evidence is not conclusive, but it satisfies the low relevance threshold if offered for motive.
Wrong Answer Explanations: B: B is wrong because insurance evidence may be relevant for a purpose such as motive; the question assumes no separate exclusion applies. C: C is wrong because motive evidence need not prove the charged act on its own. D: D is wrong because the relevance lies in the incentive, not only in payment after the fire.
lb_rule_reference: FRE 401 / FRE 402
lb_student_diagnostic: The trap is treating a policy-sensitive category as categorically irrelevant or demanding direct proof.
