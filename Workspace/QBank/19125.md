MBE Subjects (1 of 7): Torts
Subtopic: Negligence
Outline: Foreseeable and unreasonable risk
Question: A factory owner knew neighborhood children often entered a vacant lot behind the factory to play. The owner left an unlocked artificial drainage pump there with exposed moving gears. A six-year-old child entered the lot, could not appreciate the gear danger, and was injured. A simple locked cover would have prevented access at minimal cost. Is the child's trespasser status a complete bar to negligence liability?
Answer A: Yes, because trespassers can never recover for land conditions
Answer B: Yes, because the pump was visible
Answer C: No, because the facts support an attractive-nuisance duty for a dangerous artificial condition
Answer D: No, because landowners are strictly liable for all injuries to children
Correct Answer: C
Correct Answer Explanation: Children were likely to trespass, the artificial pump posed a serious risk, the child could not appreciate it, and a low-burden precaution was available.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: Child trespasser and attractive nuisance rules can create a duty for dangerous artificial conditions. B: The issue is whether this young child could appreciate the risk, not merely whether the condition was visible. D: Attractive nuisance is a negligence duty rule with elements, not strict liability for every child injury.
lb_rule_reference: A land possessor may owe a duty for an artificial condition that poses an unreasonable risk to child trespassers when children are likely to trespass, the child cannot appreciate the risk, and reasonable precautions are not unduly burdensome.
