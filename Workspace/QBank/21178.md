Outline: Commerce power
Question: Congress makes it a federal offense to knowingly defraud a lender, but only when the loan proceeds have moved in interstate commerce or the lender is federally insured. A defendant argues the statute is invalid because his loan application was submitted at a neighborhood branch in one state. What is the best response?
Answer A: The statute is automatically invalid because every loan application is local
Answer B: The jurisdictional element helps sustain the statute by requiring a connection to interstate commerce in each case
Answer C: The statute is valid only if the defendant personally crossed state lines
Answer D: The statute is invalid unless it is also enacted under the Spending Clause
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: The statute does not reach all local fraud; it requires a commerce-linked lender or funds.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A case-specific interstate commerce element can connect the conduct to federal commerce power. C: The commerce connection can be through the loan, lender, instrumentality, or market, not only defendant travel. D: Commerce power can supply the authority if the commerce connection is present.
lb_rule_reference: A jurisdictional element requiring a case-specific connection to interstate commerce can support regulation of otherwise local conduct under the Commerce Clause.
