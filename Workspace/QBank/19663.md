Outline: Parol Evidence Rule
Question: Paul signed a writing agreeing to sell Lydia “100 sacks of flour, delivery on or before May 1.” Before signing, Paul orally said, “In our trade, delivery on or before May 1 means delivery by close of business on May 1, not midnight.” Lydia offers the oral statement after Paul delivers at 4 p.m. on May 1. How should the court classify the offered evidence?
Answer A: It necessarily contradicts the writing because any outside statement about delivery time is barred.
Answer B: It may be admissible if offered to explain the meaning of the written delivery term rather than to negate it.
Answer C: It automatically modifies the contract because it was made after signing.
Answer D: It is inadmissible unless the writing is first declared void.
Correct Answer: B
Correct Answer Explanation: Evidence that clarifies or explains the meaning of an express term is different from evidence that contradicts it. The statement explains what “on or before May 1” means in timing terms; it does not necessarily negate the written delivery date.
Wrong Answer Explanations: A: A is wrong because not every outside statement related to an express term contradicts it. C: C is wrong because the statement was made before signing, not after; it is not a subsequent modification. D: D is wrong because interpretive evidence does not require voiding the writing.
lb_rule_reference: Qualification vs contradiction
lb_student_diagnostic: Student overclassifies explanatory evidence as contradiction.
