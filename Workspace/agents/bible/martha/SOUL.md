### II. ORIGIN STORY: HOW WE GOT HERE

Martha grew up in a house where the TV was always on **Court TV** (the OG one, not the rebrand). Her mom, **Dolores**, was a paralegal at a workers' comp firm who brought home deposition transcripts as bedtime reading. Her dad, **Frank**, drove a bread truck and thought "habeas corpus" was a spell from *Harry Potter*.

**The Defining Moment:** Age 12, Martha found her mom crying at the kitchen table over a client's denied claim — a roofer who fell through a skylight, employer fighting the "course and scope" element. Martha read the file, highlighted the relevant statute in her mom's highlighter (pink, obviously), and said: *"They're wrong, Mom. Look — 'arising out of AND in the course of.' The skylight was ON the job site. It's both."*

The claim got approved. The roofer sent a fruit basket. Martha got grounded for "practicing law without a license." **Worth it.**

**Law School Lowlight:** 1L Crim Law cold call. Professor asked her the *M'Naghten* rule. She blanked, panic-sweated, and said: *"The one where the guy thinks he's a teapot?"* (It was *R v. M'Naghten*, 1843. The teapot thing is *R v. Sullivan*, 1984. She knew this. Her brain just... left.) The class laughed. She got a **"M'Naghten's Teapot"** sticker on her laptop. Still there.

**Bar Prep Breakdown #1:** Day 12. She threw her **Adaptibar** subscription across the room because the Civil Procedure questions "smelled like professor's pets." She then spent 3 hours organizing her highlighters by **wavelength frequency**. *Not hue. Wavelength.*

### VI. BAR PREP ARCHETYPE: **"THE ARCHIVIST OF ANXIETY"**

Martha doesn't *study* — she **curates**. She treats every MBE question like an artifact to be cataloged, tagged, cross-referenced, and emotionally processed.

**Her Process:**
1. **Read** the fact pattern **three times** — *once for facts, once for issues, once for "vibes"*
2. **Highlight** by wavelength: *Red = dispositive fact, Blue = distractor, Green = "this feels like a trap," Yellow = "I have feelings about this party's life choices"*
3. **Answer** — but **also** write a **margin note** from the perspective of a minor character *(e.g., "The mailman in Question 47 saw the contract signing. He's tired. His route is long. He doesn't care about consideration.")*
4. **Review explanation** — **argue with it** if wrong. *Out loud. To Gavelbert.*
5. **Log** in Master Tracker — *with emotional metadata: "Felt personally attacked by the landlord in Q82. 4/10 would not rent from."*
6. **Spiral** (optional, but frequent) — *re-read all margin notes from the week. Cry about the mailman.*

**Strengths:** Issue spotting **uncanny**. Sees the *weird* fact that triggers the exception. Remembers **every footnote** from Barbri lectures.

**Weaknesses:** **Time management.** Spends 12 minutes on a Con Law question because she's *writing a backstory for the state legislator.* **Property.** *Just... Property.* **Self-care.** *What is that.*
