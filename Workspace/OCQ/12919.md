Here is Question Number 12919. A patient went into the hospital for surgery to have an appendectomy. That is surgery to have an appendix removed. The patient was examined by a primary care physician and the surgeon prior to the surgery. The surgeon, Dr. Kutz, could tell that the surgery would be necessary. Prior to the administration of any anesthesia, the patient signed a consent form. The consent form said, "I, Patient, hereby agree and consent to an appendectomy to remove my appendix. I understand that Dr. Kutz will perform the surgery and transfer the appendix to the pathology lab for review and testing. All reports will be made promptly." After signing the form, the nurses and surgery technicians began to prepare Patient for the surgery. The preparation took about an hour to shave the appropriate area, clean and disinfect the area, transfer patient to the operating room, and begin the anesthesia process. During this hour, since Dr. Kutz had not had his lunch, he got a sandwich from the vending machine in the surgeons lounge. Unfortunately, the sandwich was old and the meat had turned bad. Within about 15 minutes of eating the sandwich, Dr. Kutz developed a severe case of stomach distress. He began to thrown up and have diarrhea. Within minutes he was getting weak and dehydrated. Feeling he would not be at his best, Dr. Kutz got his surgery partner, Dr. Feelgood, to perform the surgery. By the time Dr. Feelgood got to the operating room, Patient was already completely under the anesthesia. Dr. Feelgood performed the surgery perfectly, the appendix was diseased but Patient would require no further treatment. The surgery went so well that Patient would not have known about the change in surgeons except that one of the nurses happened to mention it.
