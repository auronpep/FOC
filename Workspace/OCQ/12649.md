Here is Question Number 12649. A newspaper published in the city published a series of articles about unlawful drug-dealing at the high school. The reporter, an employee of the newspaper, wrote the articles, furnished the photographs used to illustrate them, and wrote captions for the photographs. One of the photographs furnished by the reporter showed the plaintiff, a student at the high school, in conversation with another student in the schoolyard. The caption that the reporter provided with the photograph read, "Drug deal in progress at the high school." Although the plaintiff was not involved in the use or sale of unlawful drugs, the reporter sincerely believed that she was. When the photograph and caption were published in the newspaper, black squares were superimposed over the faces in the picture so that they could not be identified. Neither the caption nor the article named the plaintiff as one of the people in the photograph. Because she wore a distinctive dress with an unusual floral design, however, many high school students recognized the plaintiff upon seeing the photograph. If the plaintiff asserts a claim against the reporter and the newspaper for invasion of privacy, under which of the following theories would she be most likely to succeed? Answer Choices: A. Appropriation of identity, because the photograph that appeared in the newspaper was a publication of her likeness without her permission. B. Intrusion, because the photograph that appeared in the newspaper was taken without her permission. C. False light, because the photograph and caption that appeared in the newspaper suggested that she unlawfully used or sold drugs. D. Public disclosure, because her identity was revealed by the publication of her photograph in a distinctive dress with an unusual floral design.
