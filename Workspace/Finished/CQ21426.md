## 6. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** Naomi's fear was honest, but the outside facts make the fear unreasonable. Peter was walking away toward the bus stop, and several bystanders said he made no move to turn back. **Gold Key:** complete self-defense requires both an honest and objectively reasonable belief that deadly force is immediately necessary. An honest but unreasonable belief does not acquit on a murder charge; it reduces the offense to voluntary manslaughter under imperfect self-defense. **Silver Key:** use the three-rung ladder. Honest plus reasonable means acquittal; honest plus unreasonable means voluntary manslaughter; no honest fear means murder.

## 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** The verbal threat makes danger feel real. The breaker is timing and imminence: when Naomi used deadly force, Peter was walking away and made no move to return. A past verbal threat does not carry the whole self-defense test.

**C.** The stab from behind is a powerful prosecution fact. It shows why Naomi's belief was unreasonable, but it does not erase her genuine fear. **Silver Key:** use both facts. The walking-away/back-stabbing fact defeats full self-defense; the honest-fear fact keeps the case from staying at murder.

**D.** This is the dominant trap. It honors Naomi's genuine terror and treats subjective fear as enough for acquittal. **Gold Key:** complete self-defense requires the belief to be both honest and reasonable. Honest but unreasonable fear points to voluntary manslaughter, not acquittal.

When the stem gives both honest fear and facts showing the threat had dissipated, use the three-rung ladder: honest plus reasonable means acquittal; honest plus unreasonable means voluntary manslaughter; no honest fear means murder.

## review_truth
