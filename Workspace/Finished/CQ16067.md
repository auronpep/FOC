# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

The defendant was invited to a party at the home of the homeowner. Because he wanted to help make the party a success, the defendant purchased fireworks and brought them to the party. A state statute requires that any person engaging in the use of fireworks have a license and provides that the license may be issued only upon successful completion of a safety course conducted by the fire department. Although the defendant had never completed the safety course and had no license to engage in the use of fireworks, he believed that he was competent to do so without causing any danger. During the party, the defendant set off some of the fireworks in the homeowner's backyard with the homeowner's consent. Although the defendant acted reasonably, one of them exploded prematurely, causing a fire that completely destroyed the homeowner's home. If the defendant is charged with arson, he should be found:

A. not guilty, because the homeowner consented to the defendant's use of the fireworks.
B. guilty, because he violated the statute requiring a license for the use of fireworks.
C. not guilty, because the defendant did not intend damage to the homeowner's home.
D. guilty, because the fire resulted from his conduct.

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A defendant, without a license, reasonably used fireworks with the homeowner's consent, but they prematurely exploded and destroyed the home. Is he guilty of arson?

## 3. Final answer choices

A. not guilty, because the homeowner consented to the defendant's use of the fireworks.
B. guilty, because he violated the statute requiring a license for the use of fireworks.
C. not guilty, because the defendant did not intend damage to the homeowner's home.
D. guilty, because the fire resulted from his conduct.

## 4. Correct answer

C. Original key: D.

## 6. Full right-answer explanation

C is the best answer. **Gold Key:** arson requires intentional or reckless burning. The defendant did not intentionally burn the home, nor was he reckless; he acted reasonably and believed there was no danger. **Silver Key:** the absence of the necessary mens rea for arson makes him not guilty.

## 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice incorrectly equates consent to use fireworks with consent to burn the home. The home owner did not consent to the fire.

**B.** This choice incorrectly assumes that a statutory violation (no license) automatically satisfies the mens rea for arson. A statutory violation is not sufficient on its own.

**D.** This choice incorrectly suggests strict liability for the fire. Arson is not a strict liability offense; it requires the necessary mens rea.

## 8. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

Arson is the intentional or reckless burning of the dwelling of another. The defendant lacked the intent to burn the home and, because he acted reasonably, he also lacked the recklessness required for arson.

Authority floor: Model Penal Code § 220.1(1); Restatement (Second) of Torts § 500 (recklessness); Commonwealth v. Almeida (general arson principles).

## 9. Rigor/difficulty note

The item remains core because the student must distinguish between a statutory violation (negligence) and the specific mens rea required for arson.
