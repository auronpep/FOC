# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question
In January, a vineyard owner sold a large parcel to a merchant for $100,000. The merchant paid $50,000 at the deed transfer, and executed a note for the balance secured by a mortgage. Neither deed nor mortgage was recorded. The merchant defaulted on mortgage payments. In March, the vineyard owner purported to sell the parcel to a laborer for $100,000. In April, after the owner contacted the merchant regarding missing payments, the merchant reconveyed title back to the owner. In June, the laborer learned of the earlier sale and the reconveyance. If the laborer brings an action against the vineyard owner to quiet title, should the court rule in the laborer's favor?

A) No, because the owner did not hold title when he made the sale to the laborer.
B) Yes, because the owner is estopped from denying the laborer's title.
C) No, because there is no indication the laborer recorded the owner’s deed to him.
D) Yes, because the owner’s sale to the laborer was fraudulent.

## 2. Distilled Core Question
Is a grantor estopped from denying the validity of a deed conveyance if they later acquire the interest they lacked at the time of the conveyance?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
Under the doctrine of estoppel by deed, one who conveys realty in which he or she has no interest is estopped from denying the validity of the conveyance if he or she then acquires the realty. Here, the owner is estopped from denying the sale to the laborer because the owner later acquired the land through the merchant’s reconveyance.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
A: This ignores the doctrine of estoppel by deed, which applies when a grantor later acquires the title they lacked.
C: Compliance or non-compliance with recording statutes does not relate to a grantee’s rights against the grantor.
D: Fraud alone is not sufficient to result in a ruling that title has passed; estoppel by deed is the correct doctrine.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification
The doctrine of estoppel by deed prevents a grantor from denying validity of a conveyance if they later acquire the interest they lacked at the time of conveyance. The owner's acquisition through reconveyance makes him estopped from denying the laborer’s title.
