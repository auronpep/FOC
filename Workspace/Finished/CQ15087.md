## 1. Final question — stem + call

Barnabas built a stone storehouse for his grain at the edge of his field. Unknown to him,
one corner of the storehouse crossed about three feet over the boundary onto the adjoining
field owned by Naomi. Some time later, Barnabas sold his field to Stephen, who likewise did

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**B is correct.** Trespass to land needs only the intent to enter — to do the act that
intrudes upon — that particular piece of land. Barnabas meant to build the storehouse exactly
where he built it; that intentional act of putting a structure on that ground is all the
intent the tort requires. He did not need to intend to trespass or to know where the boundary
ran, and his mistaken belief that he was building entirely on his own land is no defense. The
physical invasion is established by the encroaching corner, and damage need not even be shown.
**Gold Key:** the only intent trespass requires is intent to enter that spot, not intent to
trespass or knowledge of the line — a reasonable mistake about the boundary is no defense.
**Silver Key:** the suit is against Barnabas, so cut any choice that turns on the buyer's
knowledge or on Barnabas's later sale — neither bears on whether he committed the trespass.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**D — "didn't know it crossed the line when he built it." (dominant trap)** It sells the
deepest fairness instinct in the set: an honest builder who had no idea he crossed onto a
neighbor's land feels blameless, so "No" feels right. The breaker: the intent element asks
only whether he intended to enter that ground, which he plainly did; mistake as to the
boundary, however reasonable, is no defense. **Gold Key:** intent to enter the spot — not
intent to trespass or knowledge of the line — is the whole requirement.

**A — "Barnabas no longer owns or possesses the storehouse."** It sells the idea that
liability follows the structure, so once he sold the field the problem became someone else's.
The breaker: the trespass was complete when he built across the line; selling the property
afterward does not undo a wrong already committed. **Silver Key:** a completed wrong is not
erased by a later sale — cut a "No" that rests on the defendant no longer owning the thing.

**C — "Stephen did not know of the encroachment when he bought the field."** It reaches the
correct "Yes," which makes it feel safe, and dresses it up with a fact about the buyer. The
breaker: Naomi is suing Barnabas, and the buyer's state of mind has nothing to do with whether
Barnabas intended to enter the land — it answers a question about the wrong party. **Silver
Key:** cut any choice that hinges on a different person's knowledge than the defendant's.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

**Tested rule (unchanged).** The prima facie case for trespass to land requires (i) a physical
invasion of the plaintiff's land, (ii) intent to enter that land, and (iii) causation. The
intent element is satisfied by the intent to enter the particular parcel; the defendant need
not intend to trespass or know the boundary. Restatement (Second) of Torts § 158 (liability
for an intentional intrusion regardless of harm); Restatement (Second) of Torts § 164 (one who
intentionally enters land is a trespasser though acting under a mistaken belief, however
reasonable, that he is not trespassing or has a right to be there). Damage is presumed —
nominal damages lie on the trespass alone.

**Application.** Barnabas intended to place the storehouse exactly where it stands, and one
corner in fact crossed onto Naomi's field — a physical invasion he intended (to enter that
ground), satisfying intent and causation even though he was mistaken about the line. His later
sale to Stephen does not erase the completed trespass, and Stephen's knowledge is immaterial
to Barnabas's liability. The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome (Yes, the
landowner prevails) are **unchanged** from the source item (original key C). `attorney_review:
false` for the authority line — §§ 158 and 164 are confidently real and directly on point.

## Gold Key(s)

- **GK-TORTS-TRESPASS-INTENT-01 (distinction).** For trespass to land the only intent required
  is intent to enter that particular parcel — not intent to trespass or knowledge of the
  boundary; a reasonable mistake about the line is no defense, and liability for the completed
  trespass is not erased by later selling the property. *Unlocks* the mistake "No" (D) and the
  divestment "No" (A). *Authority (lawyer layer):* Restatement (Second) of Torts §§ 158, 164.

## Silver Key(s)

- **SK-TORTS-WRONGPARTY-01 (cut).** The suit names the defendant-builder, so cut any choice
  that hinges on a different person's knowledge (the buyer's).
- **SK-TORTS-PASTWRONG-02 (cut).** A completed wrong is not undone by a later transfer — cut a
  "No" that rests on the defendant no longer owning the thing that caused the harm.
