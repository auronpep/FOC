At a Christian homeschool convention, Mary stopped at Stephen’s booth and saw a hand-carved olivewood nativity set displayed for sale. ## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The source row is Q14721, a Criminal Law / Other Crimes item keyed to no guilt because the defendant subjectively believed the property belonged to her, despite admitting the false representation and acquisition of the property. The row also supplies the focus-group rates, with the false-statement-and-reliance answer as the highest wrong-answer trap.  The verified outline code is **73030300**, “Crimes against Property > False pretenses.” 

The tested rule is stable: false pretenses requires obtaining title to property by misrepresentation with intent to defraud. Cornell’s Wex summary states that false pretenses involves obtaining title through misrepresentations with intent to defraud, and distinguishes it from larceny by trick because title, not merely possession, passes. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) The California Supreme Court’s *People v. Ashley* states the same core elements: a false pretense or representation, intent to defraud, and actual defrauding/reliance by the owner. ([Scocal][2]) A good-faith claim of right negates theft intent; the California Supreme Court describes theft intent as an intent to permanently deprive “without a good faith claim of right.” ([Supreme Court of California][3])

The variation preserves the legal issue and credited outcome. Mary’s false statement and Stephen’s reliance are not enough because the dispositive mental-state fact is her belief that the nativity set belonged to her.

* Larceny by trick: deception may obtain possession rather than title.
* False pretenses: deception obtains title but still requires fraud intent.
* Claim of right: ownership belief attacks criminal intent.
* Future-promise fraud: no present intent to pay can matter, but only if fraud intent exists.

## review_truth

False pretenses requires intent to defraud, and a genuine belief that the property is yours defeats that intent.
