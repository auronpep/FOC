## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**A is correct.** The offered evidence is generic prior-robbery proof. It does not show a special identity marker, a plan, a motive, a preparation step, or any other fact-bridge beyond “Peter robbed before, so Peter probably robbed here.”

**Gold Key:** Other-act evidence cannot use “he did it before, so he did it this time.” A listed purpose like identity or intent needs a real non-propensity bridge; same general crime is not enough.

**Silver Key:** Purpose first. The evidence changes status only if the offered purpose changes and the stem supplies facts supporting that purpose. Here, the purpose label has no bridge, so unfair-prejudice exclusion is the responsive endpoint.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B.** This choice sounds technical because reputation and opinion are familiar character-proof methods. The breaker is layer order: proof method matters only after the court has a valid admissible character route, and this evidence fails earlier at the admissibility-purpose layer.

**C.** This is the dominant trap. It uses the most tempting words in the array: **intent** and **identity**. Peter is denying identity, so the label feels responsive. **Gold Key:** identity is not a magic word; the prior robberies need a real non-propensity link, and the stem gives only generic similar wrongdoing.

**D.** This choice says the forbidden inference out loud. It treats prior misconduct as admissible proof that Peter acted the same way this time. **Gold Key:** “he did it before, so he did it now” is the blocked purpose.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification + right-answer legal reasoning

Pass-1 step (1): the tested rule is the prior-bad-acts/other-acts rule. Federal Rule of Evidence 404(a)(1) bars character evidence to prove conduct in conformity, and Rule 404(b)(1) separately bars other crimes, wrongs, or acts to prove character in order to show same-occasion conformity. Rule 404(b)(2) allows other-act evidence for real non-character purposes such as motive, intent, preparation, plan, knowledge, identity, absence of mistake, or lack of accident. ([Legal Information Institute][1])

Rule 403 permits exclusion of relevant evidence when probative value is substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice, confusion, misleading the jury, delay, waste, or cumulative proof. Here, once the prior robberies lack a non-propensity bridge, any proper probative value is weak and the unfair-prejudice danger is high. ([Legal Information Institute][2])

Rule 405 addresses methods of proving character; it does not make inadmissible character evidence admissible. Its reputation/opinion method rule is therefore a later layer, not the admissibility gate. ([Legal Information Institute][3])

Huddleston confirms that Rule 404(b) evidence may be admissible for a proper non-character purpose, but that is not the same as allowing generic other-crime evidence for propensity. The supplied key is not contradicted by the authority. ([Justia Law][4])
