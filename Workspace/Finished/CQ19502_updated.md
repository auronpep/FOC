### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Article I, Section 8 gives Congress power to lay and collect taxes, duties, imposts, and excises to pay debts and provide for the general welfare, subject to the uniformity requirement for duties, imposts, and excises. ([Congress.gov][1]) A facial tax is not invalid merely because it has regulatory or burdensome economic effects; *Sonzinsky v. United States* states that a tax may burden or restrict the taxed activity and still fall within Congress’s taxing power. ([Justia Law][2]) For indirect taxes, uniformity means geographical uniformity, not equal treatment of every possible activity category. ([Justia Law][3]) Tax classifications also receive broad rational-basis deference when no suspect classification or fundamental right is implicated. ([Justia Law][4])
