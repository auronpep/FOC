## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

Lydia owns a private showroom that sells Scripture-engraved garden stones. As a promotional display, she kept a full-grown leopard whose front claws had been removed in a locked glass habitat in a back room. A lightning strike cracked the showroom roof and warped the habitat latch, allowing the leopard to roam into the showroom. Timothy, who had come to help carry out water-damaged display crates after the lightning strike, turned a corner and saw the leopard loose. In Timothy's strict-liability tort action against Lydia to recover for the wrist injury, will Timothy prevail?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A lawful cleanup helper sees an escaped wild animal, reasonably flees, and is injured while fleeing. Does strict liability attach even though the animal escaped because of a force of nature and the owner may have used care?

D. No, because Timothy should have expected injury risks while helping with the cleanup.

### 4. Correct Answer

### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct.

**Gold Key:** A keeper of a wild animal is strictly liable for foreseeable harm tied to the animal's dangerous nature, even when the animal gets loose because of a force of nature or despite careful confinement. The leopard did not have to bite Timothy. It was foreseeable that a lawful visitor who suddenly saw a loose leopard, with no reason to know about the removed claws, would run and get hurt while escaping.

**Silver Key:** Split the answer array by conclusion first, then split the two "yes" choices by reason. The call asks strict liability, so the winning "yes" reason must point to the wild animal risk, not to inadequate care.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A.** This is the dominant trap because it reaches the right "yes" result with familiar negligence language. The breaker is the call: Timothy sued in strict liability. The reason is not that Lydia failed to use enough care; the reason is that keeping a wild animal creates strict liability for foreseeable fear-and-flight harm when the animal escapes. **Silver Key:** between the two "yes" choices, choose the one that explains strict liability rather than negligence.

**C.** This choice makes the lightning strike feel like the true legal cause. The breaker is the animal rule. A force of nature can explain how the leopard got out, but it does not defeat strict liability for the foreseeable harm caused by a loose wild animal. **Gold Key:** the wild-animal keeper takes the risk that a force of nature or other innocent event will put the animal out of control.

**D.** This choice tries to turn cleanup work into consent to the risk. The breaker is the specific-risk fact. Timothy had no reason to know that a leopard would be loose in the showroom, so he did not knowingly and voluntarily accept the leopard risk merely by helping with water-damaged crates.

### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested legal rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row. Lawyer confirmation: Restatement (Second) of Torts section 507 imposes strict liability on a possessor of a wild animal for harm done by the animal even when the possessor exercised utmost care to confine it. Section 510 confirms that strict liability can remain even when an innocent third-person act, another animal, or a force of nature is part of the causal chain. Section 515 confirms that ordinary failure to discover or avoid the animal is not a defense; only knowing and voluntary acceptance of the specific risk would matter.
