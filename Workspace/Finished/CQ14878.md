### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**A is correct.** In a slander case where the alleged slander is a bad-character accusation, the plaintiff's character trait is not background color. It bears directly on whether the defendant's statement was true, and it also bears on how much reputational harm the plaintiff can claim. Barnabas's testimony is reputation evidence about the same trait Ruth allegedly stated. That makes it admissible for both purposes.

**Gold Key:** When the defamation statement itself accuses the plaintiff of a character trait, that trait can matter twice: truth and damages.

**Gold Key:** FRE 405 does not make specific acts the only method. When character evidence is admissible, reputation and opinion evidence are allowed, and when the trait is an essential element, specific acts are also allowed.

**Silver Key:** When the answer set splits two admissible purposes and one answer combines them, test whether both purposes are live before choosing a half-answer.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**B** sells the clean damages instinct. A student sees reputational injury and thinks the testimony is only useful to show that Mary's reputation was already weak. That misses the second live purpose. In a slander case built around a bad-character accusation, the same reputation evidence can also help prove the statement true. The damages-only limit is the half answer.

**C** sounds technical because character is an essential element, and specific acts are one permitted way to prove character in that setting. The breaker is the word "must." **Gold Key:** FRE 405 does not make specific acts exclusive; reputation and opinion evidence remain valid character-proof methods.

**D** captures the truth-defense side but cuts off damages. That is another half answer. If Mary already has the reputation Ruth described, that fact can reduce or refute the claimed reputational harm. **Silver Key:** A choice that admits the evidence for only one of two live purposes is not the residual answer when another choice preserves both.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The credited answer is consistent with the Federal Rules of Evidence and the source key. FRE 405(a) permits reputation or opinion testimony when character evidence is admissible; FRE 405(b) permits proof by relevant specific instances when the trait is an essential element of a charge, claim, or defense. FRE 803(21) separately recognizes a hearsay exception for reputation concerning a person's character. Those rule numbers satisfy the authority floor for this item.

In defamation, truth is a defense, and the plaintiff's existing reputation can affect reputational damages. When the alleged slander is that the plaintiff has a dishonest trait, reputation evidence about that trait is relevant both to truth and to damages. The transformed question preserves the same legal issue, tested rule, answer substance, and credited outcome: the reputation testimony is admissible to prove the trait and to reduce or refute damages.
