### 7. Full right-answer explanation
A is correct. Claim preclusion does not arise just because an earlier case existed. Gold Key: claim preclusion requires a final judgment on the merits; a dismissal without prejudice normally is not a merits judgment and leaves the claim available to refile. Silver Key: in a preclusion question, separate the "same claim" box from the "final merits judgment" box. Here, the same contract appears twice, but the first case ended without prejudice for nonpayment of the filing fee, so the missing merits-judgment box controls.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
B is attractive because it spots the real same-claim feature: both suits involve the same delivery contract. The breaker is that sameness is only one box in claim preclusion; Silver Key: the student must also check for a final merits judgment, and the first dismissal was without prejudice.

C is attractive because it reaches the correct "No" result and tries to explain it with a broad category rule. The breaker is the overclaim: contract claims can be precluded when the preclusion elements are present, including a final merits judgment between the proper parties.

D is attractive because it treats the word "dismissed" as if it automatically closes the courthouse forever. The breaker is the absolute word "every"; Gold Key: dismissals without prejudice generally do not operate as merits judgments for claim-preclusion purposes.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Civil Procedure, Judgments and Verdicts, Issue and Claim Preclusion, and the verified outline code is `97100300`. Claim preclusion generally requires the same claim, the same parties or their privies, and a valid final judgment on the merits. A dismissal labeled without prejudice ordinarily does not bar a later action on the same claim because it is not a merits judgment. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 41(b) confirms the merits-judgment distinction by providing that certain involuntary dismissals operate as adjudications on the merits unless the order states otherwise, while excluding specified nonmerits dismissals; Rule 41(a) also recognizes dismissals without prejudice. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 41(a)-(b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_41. The Supreme Court has described "without prejudice" as permitting the plaintiff to return later with the same underlying claim. See *Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp.*, 531 U.S. 497, 505-06 (2001). Because the first action was dismissed without prejudice for failure to pay the filing fee, it was not a qualifying final merits judgment, so claim preclusion does not bar Mary's refiled contract claim. The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source.
