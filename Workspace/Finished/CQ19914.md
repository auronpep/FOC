## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (D)

**D is correct.** Hannah did not give Daniel the fee simple. She gave him a life estate plus a power to choose the future owner only by will. Daniel used a lifetime deed, not a will. That deed could transfer no more than Daniel had during his life, and it did not exercise the will-only appointment power.

**Gold Key:** when the donor says a power of appointment is exercisable by will, a lifetime deed is not the required instrument. If the powerholder dies without validly exercising the power and no default gift controls, the appointive property returns to the donor's estate.

**Silver Key:** lock the instrument before reading the family facts. The stem says "by his will"; the tempting answers talk about a deed and intestacy. The deed/intestacy facts are decoys unless the will-only power was actually exercised.

Because Daniel died intestate without exercising the power by will, Psalm Hill reverted to Hannah's estate. Lydia therefore did not own the fee simple when she contracted to sell Psalm Hill to Peter. A seller who lacks title cannot convey marketable title. Peter is entitled to refuse the deed and recover his deposit.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### A. "Lydia, because Daniel's deed to Lydia was a valid exercise of the power of appointment created by Hannah's will." (Dominant trap - 34% predicted.)

This choice sells the most attractive shortcut: Daniel had a power, Daniel picked Lydia, and Daniel signed a deed. The breaker is the instrument. Hannah limited Daniel's appointment power to exercise "by his will." Daniel's deed was a lifetime conveyance, not a will, so it did not exercise the power. **Gold Key:** a will-only power of appointment is not exercised by an inter vivos deed. **Silver Key:** "deed" and "will" are the clash words; once they do not match, this choice cannot carry Lydia to fee simple title.

### B. "Peter, because Lydia's tendered deed did not contain a covenant of general warranty."

This choice gets the buyer result but uses the wrong reason. A general warranty covenant could give a buyer a later contract or deed-covenant remedy if the conveyed title failed, but it does not make title marketable at closing. The defect here is not the label on Lydia's deed. The defect is that Lydia had no fee simple to convey because Daniel never validly exercised Hannah's will-only power. B is a remedy wrapper, not the ownership answer.

### C. "Lydia, because Lydia was the only person entitled to inherit from Daniel under intestacy."

This choice feels natural because the stem says Daniel died intestate and Lydia was his only survivor. The breaker is ownership source. Lydia can inherit property Daniel owned, but the fee simple remainder in Psalm Hill was not Daniel's property. Daniel held a life estate, which ended at his death, and a power of appointment, which he failed to exercise by will. Intestacy does not pull Hannah's unappointed property into Daniel's estate.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

**The rule.** A power of appointment lets a powerholder designate who will receive the appointive property, but the power must be exercised in the manner required by the donor. A power limited to appointment "by will" is testamentary. A lifetime deed is not a will and does not satisfy a will-only appointment clause. When the powerholder dies without a valid exercise and no default gift controls, the appointive property returns to the donor's estate under the common-law MBE rule tested here. If the person contracting to sell the land does not own the fee simple, the person cannot convey marketable title.

**Authority (lawyer-confirmation layer only).**

- Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers §§ 19.1 and 19.3 are the governing Restatement sections for requisites for exercise of a power of appointment and disposition of appointive property. The section titles are reflected in the published Restatement table of contents, which lists "Requisites for Exercise of a Power of Appointment" and "Disposition of Appointive Property." Source: [https://3668083.app.netsuite.com/core/media/media.nl?_xt=.pdf&c=3668083&h=d37b03f8628028562bd1&id=1970780](https://3668083.app.netsuite.com/core/media/media.nl?_xt=.pdf&c=3668083&h=d37b03f8628028562bd1&id=1970780).
- Uniform Powers of Appointment Act § 301 states the same core exercise requirement: the exercising instrument must manifest intent and satisfy the donor-imposed requirements of exercise. Source: [https://www.sos.ms.gov/content/documents/pol_res/Powers%20of%20Appointment/2013AM_UPAA_As%20approved.pdf](https://www.sos.ms.gov/content/documents/pol_res/Powers%20of%20Appointment/2013AM_UPAA_As%20approved.pdf).
- Virginia's enacted Uniform Powers of Appointment Act § 64.2-2713 likewise states the exercise requirement: the instrument's terms must manifest intent and satisfy donor-imposed requirements of exercise. Source: [https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title64.2/chapter27/](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title64.2/chapter27/).
- CALI's *The Law of Trusts* explains the same default-taker concept and states the common-law result used by the official explanation: if the donor names no default taker and the donee fails to exercise the power, the property reverts to the donor's estate. Source: [https://lewislawoftrusts.lawbooks.cali.org/chapter/power-of-appointments/](https://lewislawoftrusts.lawbooks.cali.org/chapter/power-of-appointments/).
- Cornell Wex defines marketable title as title free from ownership claims, disputes, or litigation threat, and states that a seller of property impliedly promises to deliver marketable title. Source: [https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/marketable_title](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/marketable_title).

## distilled_core_question

A life tenant has a will-only power to appoint the remainder, signs a lifetime deed to his only heir, then dies intestate. Can that heir later convey marketable title to a buyer?

## call_and_prediction

The call asks who wins litigation between seller and buyer. Predict a title-source answer. If Lydia did not own the fee simple, Peter wins. If Daniel validly gave Lydia the fee simple, Lydia wins.

## trigger_facts

- Hannah's will gave Daniel a life estate.
- The remainder could go only to the person Daniel designated by will.
- Daniel signed a deed to Lydia.
- Daniel died intestate.
- Lydia promised marketable title.
- The deed tendered at closing lacked a general warranty covenant, but that fact is a trap unless it explains ownership.

## governing_c3_lane

**Lane:** Future interests and power of appointment.

**Gold Key lane:** donor's required appointment method controls.

**Silver Key lane:** match the required instrument to the instrument used before letting heirship facts decide.

## choice_by_choice_walkthrough

### A. Lydia, because Daniel's deed to Lydia was a valid exercise of the power of appointment created by Hannah's will.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The stem says "by his will"; the answer says Daniel's deed worked.
2. **Student label:** will-only power treated as lifetime deed power.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** Lydia could win on this theory if Daniel had validly exercised the power by will, or if Hannah had given Daniel the fee simple outright.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** A donor-imposed method of exercising a power of appointment controls. Daniel's inter vivos deed did not satisfy a will-only power. This is the dominant trap.

### B. Peter, because Lydia's tendered deed did not contain a covenant of general warranty.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The answer changes the reason from title ownership to deed warranties.
2. **Student label:** remedy wrapper mistaken for title cure.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** A true version would say Peter wins because Lydia lacked title, not because the deed omitted a general warranty covenant.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Title covenants can matter after deed delivery, but they do not make an otherwise defective title marketable at closing.

### C. Lydia, because Lydia was the only person entitled to inherit from Daniel under intestacy.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The answer changes the source of title from Hannah's will to Daniel's intestacy.
2. **Student label:** correct heir to the wrong estate.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** Lydia's intestacy status would matter for Daniel's own property, not for property Hannah kept subject to a will-only appointment power.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Daniel's life estate ended at death. His unexercised power did not place Hannah's fee simple remainder into his estate.

### D. Peter, because Psalm Hill reverted to Hannah's estate when Daniel died.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This answer matches "by will" with "died intestate" and gives the buyer a title-source reason.
2. **Student label:** will-only appointment not exercised; donor estate owns the remainder.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** This is true and responsive as written.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Under the tested rule, failure to exercise a will-only power leaves the property in the donor's estate when no default gift controls, so Lydia could not convey marketable title.

## residual_answer

After cutting B and C and resolving the A/D clash, **D** is the only true and responsive answer. Peter wins because Lydia did not own the fee simple and could not convey marketable title.

## remediation_card

**Card ID:** RC-REAL_PROPERTY-19914-WILL_ONLY_POWER

**Title:** By Will Means By Will

**Signal:** A power of appointment clause names a required instrument.

**Student move:** Underline the required instrument. Then compare it to the instrument actually used.

**Tiny rule:** A deed cannot exercise a power that the donor made exercisable only by will.

**Trap:** deed plus sole heir feels like title.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-REAL_PROPERTY-WILL_ONLY_POWER-01:** When a power of appointment says it is exercisable by will, a lifetime deed is not the required instrument; if the power is not validly exercised and no default gift controls, the property returns to the donor's estate.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-REAL_PROPERTY-INSTRUMENT-MATCH-01:** Before deciding who wins, match the instrument the donor required to the instrument the powerholder used; if they do not match, family-heir facts cannot fix the appointment.
