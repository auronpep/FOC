# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question
A warrant authorized officers to search "all property owned by Priscilla and seize all items related to any wrongdoing." The warrant did not list an address, item category, offense, or limiting description. Officers executed it at Priscilla’s workshop and seized inventory ledgers. Priscilla moves to suppress. Can officers rely on the good-faith exception?

A) Yes, because a magistrate signed the warrant.
B) Yes, because ledgers are evidence officers expected to find.
C) No, because the warrant was so facially deficient that objective reliance was unreasonable.
D) No, because inventory ledgers can never be seized with a warrant.

## 2. Distilled Core Question

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
A warrant authorizing searches of all property and all items related to any wrongdoing lacks particularity. The good-faith exception does not save reliance on that facial defect.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
A: A facially deficient warrant cannot be saved merely because a magistrate signed it; a reasonable officer should know it lacks particularity.
B: Officer expectation cannot cure a warrant that fails to specify what may be searched and seized.
D: Inventory ledgers can be seized under a particular warrant; the issue here is the warrant's invalid, overly broad scope.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification
Officers cannot reasonably rely on a warrant that fails on its face to describe the place to be searched or items to be seized with sufficient particularity. The lack of particularity here is a fatal facial defect.
