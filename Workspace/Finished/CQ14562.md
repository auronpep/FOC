### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**B** is the answer. The stop and frisk were valid because the officers had reasonable suspicion and the frisk was limited to the outer clothing for weapons. Miranda warnings were not required merely because the officers asked brief questions during a Terry stop. **Gold Key:** Terry reasonable suspicion permits a brief stop and protective frisk, and Berkemer teaches that ordinary temporary roadside-style detention is not automatically Miranda custody. **Silver Key:** the answer array is a two-axis grid: Fourth Amendment violation yes/no and Miranda violation yes/no. ### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This answer is the dominant trap because it concedes the Terry stop but treats questioning during the stop as Miranda custody. The breaker is custody. **Gold Key:** a brief Terry detention plus ordinary questioning does not by itself trigger Miranda warnings.

**C.** This answer attacks the Fourth Amendment axis and assumes probable cause was needed. The breaker is the stated finding: reasonable suspicion was enough for a Terry stop and protective frisk.

**D.** This answer combines both wrong axes. The breaker is double overclaim. The stop/frisk was valid under Terry, and the questioning was not custodial for Miranda purposes.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are preserved. The original and this variation both ask whether a confession following a reasonable-suspicion stop, outer-clothing frisk, and brief questioning without Miranda warnings must be suppressed.

Under **Terry v. Ohio, 392 U.S. 1 (1968)**, officers may briefly stop a person on reasonable suspicion and may conduct a limited frisk for weapons when justified by safety concerns. Probable cause is not required for that limited stop and frisk. Under **Berkemer v. McCarty, 468 U.S. 420 (1984)**, a temporary investigative detention does not automatically create Miranda custody; Miranda applies to custodial interrogation. Because there was reasonable suspicion and no custodial interrogation, there was neither a Fourth Amendment violation nor a Miranda violation.

The outline placement is **75080101**, listed in the local outline under statements and confessions. The item also intersects Fourth Amendment Terry doctrine, but the suppression target is the confession and Miranda-custody axis.
