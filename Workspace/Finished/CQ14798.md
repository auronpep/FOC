## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

A plaintiff has brought a civil suit against a defendant for injuries arising
out of a shoving incident in a livestock barn at the county fair. The day
after the incident, an investigator with the state Department of Agriculture
interviewed the plaintiff, the defendant, and a fellow exhibitor, and prepared
an official incident report. At trial, the plaintiff seeks to introduce from
the properly authenticated incident report a statement attributed to the
fellow exhibitor, who is unavailable to testify at trial, that "Boaz shoved
Ruth first." Should the court admit the statement from the report?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Civil suit · an official incident report by a public agency · an inner
out-of-court statement by an unavailable eyewitness offered for the truth · no
exception fits the inner statement. Is the inner statement admissible?

- **C.** No, unless the entire report is also introduced into evidence.

### 7. Right-Answer Explanation (exam register)

**A is correct.** The statement sits inside another out-of-court statement,
so FRE 805 (hearsay within hearsay) applies: each layer must independently
fit a hearsay exception. The outer report may well fit FRE 803(8) — it is a
record of a public office setting forth matters observed pursuant to duty, or
factual findings from an investigation made under authority of law. But the
inner statement is the fellow exhibitor's out-of-court assertion that "Boaz
shoved Ruth first." That layer is offered for the truth of the matter asserted
and is unexcused hearsay: the exhibitor had no business or public duty to
report accurately, the statement was made the day after the incident (so it
is neither a present sense impression, FRE 803(1), nor an excited utterance,
FRE 803(2)), and no FRE 804 exception is in play. One layer's exception does
not save the other; the statement is not admissible.

> **Silver Key (SK-EVI-MULTI-01):** When you see a report being offered AND
> a quote from a witness inside that report, run FRE 805 on EACH layer
> independently. A public record's 803(8) status covers the report itself, not
> the witness's quote inside it.

### 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations (diagnostic register)

**B is incorrect — DOMINANT TRAP.** B is the answer that pulls hardest. The
trap sells the move "public record + civil case = admissible." A student who
half-remembers FRE 803(8)(iii) (factual findings admissible in civil actions)
will map "official agency report offered in a civil suit" onto "yes, comes
in," and forget the multi-layer step. The breaker is the **Silver Key
(SK-EVI-MULTI-01)**: 803(8) covers the report itself; FRE 805 still requires
the inner witness statement to fit a separate exception, and none does.

**C is incorrect.** C reads like a fairness move — "give the jury the whole
report so they can see the statement in context." The trap is that the
*entire* report is not the cure; introducing more report does not give the
inner statement an exception. The inner statement remains an out-of-court
assertion offered for the truth, and FRE 805 still controls. Rule of
completeness (FRE 106) addresses fairness in completing a recorded statement,
not whether a layer of hearsay is exempt.

**D is incorrect.** D conflates two rules: FRE 602 (a witness must have
personal knowledge) and FRE 801-805 (hearsay). A statement can be based on
personal knowledge AND still be hearsay. The hearsay problem is that the
out-of-court statement was not subject to cross-examination, and the
declarant might not have told the truth about the event. Firsthand knowledge
is necessary; it is not sufficient to make the statement admissible.

### 9. Black-Letter Verification and Right-Answer Legal Reasoning

The tested rule is **Federal Rule of Evidence 805** (Hearsay Within Hearsay):
"Hearsay within hearsay is not excluded by the rule against hearsay if each
part of the combined statements conforms with an exception to the rule." The
rule was last amended April 26, 2011 (eff. Dec. 1, 2011) — stylistic only,
with no intent to change any result. (Source: Advisory Committee Notes on
the 2011 Amendment, verified against the official text.)

The legal issue is multiple hearsay: the report (outer) quotes a witness's
statement (inner), and the proponent offers the inner statement for its
truth. The credited outcome — the inner statement is **not** admissible —
flows directly from FRE 805: the outer report may satisfy FRE 803(8) (public
records) or FRE 803(6) (records of a regularly conducted activity), but the
inner witness statement does not satisfy any exception. The day-after timing
defeats FRE 803(1) (present sense impression) and FRE 803(2) (excited
utterance); no FRE 804 unavailability-based exception is in play; the
witness had no business or public duty. Therefore the inner statement fails
the controlling rule.

The credited outcome is **unchanged** from the original Q#14798 (B was
correct; the new correct answer A carries the same legal substance
re-skin-narrative-only). No constitutional, establishment, or First
Amendment issues are introduced — this is a garden-variety civil battery
suit, not a church-property or clergy-penitent matter, and the report is
from a state agency, not a religious body.

controlling rule is FRE 805: each layer of a combined out-of-court statement

#### choice_by_choice_walkthrough

**Choice A (correct).** **4-part block.**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* Two layers; only the outer is plausibly
   exempt. The inner is hearsay with no exception. Multi-hearsay fails.
2. *Student label:* true and responsive.
3. *What a true/responsive version would look like:* It already is — A
   directly answers the call and states the multi-hearsay conclusion.
4. *Lawyer confirmation:* FRE 805 controls. The outer report may fit
   803(8) or 803(6); the inner witness statement fits nothing. Day-after
   timing defeats 803(1) and 803(2); no 804 exception is in play; the
   witness had no business or public duty.

**Choice B (dominant trap).** **4-part block.**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* "Public agency's report + civil case =
   admissible" — but the multi-hearsay anchor says: two layers, two
   exceptions. The outer layer's exception does not save the inner layer.
2. *Student label:* false: public record exception is not a magic key that
   absorbs inner hearsay.
3. *What a true/responsive version would look like:* "Yes, because the
   OUTER report is admissible as a public record; the inner witness
   statement must independently fit an exception, and no exception applies
   — therefore the inner statement is not admissible." (That sentence is
   what choice A already says.)
4. *Lawyer confirmation:* FRE 803(8) lets the *report* in; FRE 805 still
   requires the inner witness statement to fit a separate exception. None
   does. This is the dominant trap; 23% of original test-takers fell for
   it.

**Choice C (misfit).** **4-part block.**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* The student is offered a procedural
   workaround ("introduce the rest of the report"). Multi-hearsay asks
   about *exceptions*, not *completeness*. Adding more report does not
   give the inner statement an exception.
2. *Student label:* false: introducing the rest of the report does not
   fix the inner hearsay.
3. *What a true/responsive version would look like:* "No, because the
   inner statement remains hearsay and no exception applies — adding the
   rest of the report does not change that." (Effectively the same
   conclusion as A, framed as a completeness rebuttal.)
4. *Lawyer confirmation:* FRE 805 controls. FRE 106 (rule of
   completeness) addresses fairness in completing a recorded statement; it
   does not create a hearsay exception for the inner statement.

**Choice D (half-truth).** **4-part block.**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* "Firsthand knowledge" reads like a
   FRE 602 personal-knowledge move. But hearsay is a different test:
   whether the out-of-court statement was subject to cross-examination.
   Firsthand knowledge is necessary, not sufficient.
2. *Student label:* false: firsthand knowledge is necessary but not
   sufficient — it does not make the statement non-hearsay.
3. *What a true/responsive version would look like:* "Yes, but only if
   firsthand knowledge were a hearsay exception — and it is not. The
   statement is still hearsay, and no exception applies."
4. *Lawyer confirmation:* FRE 602 (personal knowledge) and FRE 801-805
   (hearsay) are separate rules. A statement can be based on personal
   knowledge AND still be hearsay.

**A.** No, because the eyewitness's out-of-court statement is hearsay and
no exception applies to it.

**Rationale.** Each layer of the combined statement must independently fit
an exception. The outer report may fit 803(8); the inner statement fits
nothing.

#### remediation_card

- **Card ID:** RC-MULTIHEARSAY-01
- **Title:** Multiple Hearsay — Each Layer, Each Exception
- **Signal:** Out-of-court statement inside another out-of-court statement
  (often a report quoting a witness).
- **Student move:** List every layer. For each, ask: does this layer
  independently fit a hearsay exception? If any layer fails, the whole
  statement is excluded.
- **Tiny rule:** FRE 805 — hearsay within hearsay is admissible only if
  each part conforms with an exception.
- **Trap:** Treating one layer's exception as absorbing the other. A
  public record's 803(8) status does not save an inner witness quote.
