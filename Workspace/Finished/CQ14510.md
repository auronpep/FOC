## 1. Final question

Timothy ran a for-profit storytelling booth that used Bible-themed puppet sets at county fairs. He ordered from Lydia’s Workshop a new fold-up Noah’s Ark puppet-stage kit. In an action by Lydia’s Workshop against Timothy for breach of contract, which of the following is **NOT** a proper measure of Lydia’s Workshop’s damages?

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is the answer.** The call asks for the measure that is **NOT** proper. The contract price is not an ordinary damages measure here because Timothy rejected the kit and returned it. Lydia’s Workshop is not stuck with goods it cannot resell, and the facts do not say Timothy accepted the goods or that they were lost or damaged after risk shifted.

**Gold Key:** An action for the price is narrow. It needs accepted goods, goods lost or damaged after risk shifted, or identified goods that the seller cannot reasonably resell.

**Silver Key:** Because the call says **NOT**, cross out the measures that are proper seller remedies. The remaining measure is the answer.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. The profit Lydia’s Workshop would have made on Timothy’s purchase.**
This choice sells the thought that profit is too generous because Lydia’s Workshop got the kit back. The unused-capacity fact makes this the lost-volume seller lane: Lydia could have made Timothy’s sale and another sale, so lost profit is a proper measure. **Gold Key:** A lost-volume seller can recover lost profit when the ordinary market formula undercompensates. That makes A a proper measure, so it fails the **NOT** call.

**C. The difference between the contract price and the market price of the puppet-stage kit.**
This is the dominant trap because it is the familiar default seller-damages formula. The attraction is legal fluency plus a missed **NOT**. **Silver Key:** Invert the call first. A valid market-damages formula answers the positive version of the question, not the question asked.

**D. The difference between the contract price and the price obtained from a proper resale of the puppet-stage kit.**
This choice follows the returned-goods fact and feels fact-matched. That is exactly why it is tempting. A proper resale formula is a proper seller remedy, so it cannot be the measure that is **NOT** proper.

A seller who receives rejected goods back may not recover the contract price unless the goods were accepted, lost or damaged after risk shifted, or cannot reasonably be resold.
