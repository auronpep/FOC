### 7. Full right-answer explanation
A is correct. The defense is not offering Esther to prove anything about the arson. It is offering her only to contradict Lydia on a side issue: whether Lydia ever entered a chili cook-off. Gold Key: Rule 613(b) gives conditions for using extrinsic proof of a prior inconsistent statement, but it does not force the court to admit extrinsic contradiction on a collateral point. Silver Key: ask whether the contradiction helps decide the charged event or only creates a mini-trial about a side fact. Because the cook-off point is collateral and would distract from the arson issue, the court should exclude Esther's testimony.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
B is attractive because a student may treat any false denial on cross as proof that the witness is generally dishonest. The breaker is that entering a chili cook-off is not a truthfulness-specific act, and the offered testimony is still extrinsic proof of a side point rather than evidence bearing on the arson.

C is attractive because it sounds like a tidy procedural foundation rule: show the flyer first, then call the contradicting witness. The breaker is that a cook-off flyer is not the decisive requirement; the problem is collateral contradiction, not missing-document foundation.

D is attractive because it remembers the prior-inconsistent-statement lane and then turns it into an absolute. Gold Key: extrinsic prior-inconsistent-statement proof remains subject to the collateral-matter limit and ordinary trial-control principles, so the word "any" overclaims the rule.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Evidence, Presentation of Evidence, Prior inconsistent statement, and the verified outline code is `31010502` - `Presentation of Evidence > Impeachment > Prior inconsistent statement`. Federal Rule of Evidence 613(b) governs timing and opportunity requirements for extrinsic evidence of a witness's prior inconsistent statement; it does not create an unconditional right to prove collateral contradictions through another witness. Federal Rules of Evidence 403 and 611(a) support exclusion or trial-control limits when proposed proof would confuse issues, waste time, or divert the trial into side disputes. Federal Rule of Evidence 608(b) also confirms that extrinsic proof is restricted when a party tries to use specific conduct only to attack truthfulness. Current rule text was checked in the United States Courts Federal Rules of Evidence PDF effective December 1, 2024: https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-evidence-dec-1-2024_0.pdf. The variant keeps the legal issue, tested rule, and credited outcome unchanged: the cook-off statement is collateral and offered only to contradict, so exclusion is proper.
