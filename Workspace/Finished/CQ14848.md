### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

D is correct. Stephen is a witness, and the question attacks Stephen's own credibility by asking about a prior dishonest act. **Gold Key:** FRE 608(b) allows cross-examination about a specific act if the act is probative of the witness's truthfulness, though the examiner may not prove the act with extrinsic evidence just to attack truthfulness. **Silver Key:** Do not stop at the proper/improper label; the because-clause must identify the right target. Here the target is Stephen's credibility, not Stephen's skill at judging Lydia's reputation.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A sells the familiar character-evidence warning. The breaker is that the question is not offered as civil propensity evidence; it is a cross-examination question about Stephen's own dishonest conduct, used to attack credibility under the truthfulness rule.

B is attractive because the witness did give reputation testimony, so "standard for judging reputation" sounds connected to the job Stephen performed. **Silver Key:** The call asks why this question is proper, and the question is aimed at Stephen's credibility, not at calibrating his reputation standards.

C leans on a fake shield around impeachment witnesses. The breaker is that an impeaching witness is still a witness, so that witness's credibility may be attacked.

### 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

Federal Rule of Evidence 608(b) provides the governing authority. It bars extrinsic evidence offered solely to prove specific conduct attacking or supporting truthfulness, but it permits the court, on cross-examination, to allow inquiry into specific conduct if probative of the witness's character for truthfulness or untruthfulness. See FRE 608(b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_608.

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Stephen testified as a witness. The question asks Stephen about his own allegedly false reimbursement form. A false mileage claim is probative of truthfulness. The lawyer is asking on cross-examination, not offering an outside document to prove the act. Under FRE 608(b), the question is proper in the court's discretion because it bears on Stephen's credibility.
