## 1. Final Question

Paul was indicted for burglary after a locked storeroom at a family-owned Christian bookstore was entered during a hymnbook inventory night. Police later arranged an in-person lineup without notifying Paul's attorney. The only eyewitness was Lydia, a volunteer who had seen a hooded figure through a rippled glass office panel from thirty-five feet away for about two seconds before the hallway lights failed. Lydia had never met Paul before. At the lineup, Lydia identified Paul. At trial, when asked to identify the person from the storeroom, Lydia again pointed to Paul. Paul's attorney objected and moved to suppress both identifications. What is the correct ruling on Paul's motion?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

After indictment, police hold an uncounseled lineup, and the witness had only a brief weak view of a stranger before later identifying the defendant in court. Should both identifications be suppressed?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

A. Suppress both identifications and dismiss the burglary charge because the prosecution cannot proceed without the eyewitness.

B. Suppress both identifications because the uncounseled post-indictment lineup tainted the lineup identification and the courtroom identification has no independent source.

C. Suppress only the lineup identification; permit the courtroom identification because it is independent of the lineup.

D. Deny the motion as to both identifications because an honest police mistake in arranging a lineup does not trigger suppression.

## 5. Correct Answer

B (original key C).

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is the best answer. A post-indictment in-person lineup is a critical stage at which the accused is entitled to counsel. Gold Key: after formal charges, an uncounseled lineup suppresses the lineup identification, and a later in-court identification survives only if the prosecution can show an independent source. Here Lydia had only a two-second view through rippled glass from thirty-five feet away, in failing light, and she had never met Paul. That weak original view does not give the prosecution a clean independent source for the courtroom identification. Silver Key: the final clash is not "lineup suppressed or not"; it is "does the courtroom identification have a source outside the lineup?" Because it does not, both identifications are suppressed.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A sells the remedy overreach move: if both identifications are gone, the case feels finished. Suppression removes the tainted identification evidence, but it does not automatically dismiss the charge; the prosecution may try to proceed with other proof.

C sells the half-right move. It correctly suppresses the lineup identification, but it treats the courtroom identification as automatically independent. Gold Key: the later in-court identification needs an independent source, and Lydia's weak original view of a stranger does not provide one.

D sells a familiar Fourth Amendment good-faith frame. The problem is not a warrant mistake; it is a Sixth Amendment critical-stage lineup after indictment. Silver Key: when the call names an indicted defendant and a lineup without notice to counsel, do not import the Leon warrant-good-faith lane.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. In United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), the Supreme Court treated a post-indictment lineup as a critical stage requiring counsel and required scrutiny of later courtroom identifications for an independent origin. Gilbert v. California, 388 U.S. 263 (1967), likewise treated testimony about an uncounseled lineup as subject to exclusion and required a determination that in-court identifications were not tainted by the illegal lineup. Kirby v. Illinois, 406 U.S. 682 (1972), confirms that the Wade/Gilbert right is tied to the start of adversary judicial proceedings, such as indictment or formal charge. The variation keeps that posture: Paul has already been indicted, the lineup was in person, counsel was not notified, and the witness's original observation was too weak to purge the taint. The correct result remains suppression of both the lineup identification and the in-court identification, not dismissal.
