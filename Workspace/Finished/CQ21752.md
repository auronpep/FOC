### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is the best answer.** It ties the claim to the fact that the business had the walkway-safety duty in the first place. **Gold Key:** when the law imposes a nondelegable safety duty on a hirer, the hirer may remain responsible even though an independent contractor performed the negligent work. **Silver Key:** do not turn this array into “contractor equals employee” or “contractor means no liability.” Look for the middle route: the duty stays with the business.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This sells a direct-act frame: the business loses only if its own hand created the danger. The breaker is the Gold Key: a nondelegable duty can keep responsibility with the business even when the outside contractor created or failed to remove the hazard.

**B.** This sells the usual independent-contractor default. The breaker is the word “possible”: hiring an outside company can be relevant, but it does not erase a nondelegable statutory safety duty.

**D.** This sells a shortcut to liability by converting every outside contractor into an employee. The breaker is the overclaim: nondelegable duty is a narrower doctrine than treating every contractor as staff.
