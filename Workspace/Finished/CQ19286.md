### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**C** is the calibrated sanction framework. The stem gives the Rule 37(e) gateway facts: preservation duty, lost ESI, failure to take reasonable steps, inability to restore or replace, and prejudice. It also withholds the fact needed for severe sanctions: intent to deprive.

**Gold Key:** Lost ESI has a sanction ladder. Prejudice without intent supports only measures no greater than necessary to cure prejudice; adverse-inference, presumption, dismissal, or default require intent to deprive.

**Silver Key:** Read the answer array by sanction severity. No intent in the stem knocks out the unfavorable-presumption and case-ending tracks, leaving the cure-the-prejudice track.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This sells the strongest intuitive reaction: “the notes vanished, so the jury should assume they hurt the company.” The breaker is the missing intent gate; **Gold Key:** an unfavorable inference is an intent-based sanction, not the prejudice-only track.

**B.** This sells a clean escape hatch: if electronic project notes are outside discovery, the court has no sanction problem to solve. The breaker is the fabricated category; ESI can be discoverable when it falls within ordinary discovery scope.

**D.** This sells punishment pressure: any lost ESI feels serious enough to end the case. The breaker is the absolute wording; “must” and “whenever any” collapse the sanction ladder into an automatic case-ending penalty.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37(e) governs lost ESI. If ESI should have been preserved in anticipation or conduct of litigation, is lost because reasonable preservation steps were not taken, and cannot be restored or replaced, the court may, on a finding of prejudice, order measures no greater than necessary to cure the prejudice. The rule permits adverse presumptions, adverse-inference jury instructions, dismissal, or default only on a finding that the party acted with intent to deprive another party of the ESI’s use in litigation. ([United States Courts][1])

The variation preserves the original tested issue and credited outcome: prejudice exists, ESI cannot be restored, and intent to deprive is absent. That is the Rule 37(e)(1) track, not the Rule 37(e)(2) track. The advisory committee’s explanation also confirms that negligence or gross negligence does not justify the adverse-inference logic reserved for intentional deprivation. ([Legal Information Institute][2])
