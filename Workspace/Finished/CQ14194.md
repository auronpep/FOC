# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

FaithForge, Inc., a private company that produces Bible-memory card decks, sued Stephen, a former layout designer, in federal court. FaithForge alleged that Stephen violated a confidentiality agreement by taking unreleased deck layouts to a rival devotional-card seller. Immediately after filing its complaint, but before Stephen was served, FaithForge filed a motion asking the court for a preliminary injunction barring Stephen from using the layouts. The motion relied only on the allegations in the complaint and did not submit affidavits, declarations, or other evidence addressing irreparable harm, likely success, the balance of equities, or the public interest.

Is the court likely to grant FaithForge's motion for a preliminary injunction?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

Can a federal court grant a preliminary injunction before the adverse party is served when the movant offers only a complaint and no injunction-factor showing?

D. No, because FaithForge requested a preliminary injunction, not a temporary restraining order, without making the showing required for that relief.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

D is correct. FaithForge asked for a preliminary injunction. That request requires notice to the adverse party and a real showing that preliminary relief is justified. The motion did not supply the injunction record: irreparable harm, likely success on the merits, balance of equities, and public interest. Silver Key: lock the requested remedy first. A preliminary injunction is not the same procedural lane as an emergency TRO. Gold Key: under FRCP 65, an ex parte TRO has its own narrow affidavit or verified-complaint path, but a preliminary injunction requires notice and an injunction-factor showing.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because filing feels official, public, and court-supervised. The breaker is that a public filing is not the same thing as notice to the adverse party for a preliminary injunction.

B is attractive because a well-pleaded complaint can make the underlying claim sound plausible. The breaker is that pleading a violation is not the same as proving the preliminary-injunction factors. Gold Key: a preliminary injunction needs an injunction showing, not just a cause-of-action pleading.

C is the dominant trap. It gets the bottom-line "No" right and points to the obvious service problem. The breaker is that it treats service timing as the whole reason. Service is not the only possible way notice can be given, and the motion also lacks the required injunction showing. Silver Key: when two "No" choices survive, pick the answer that matches the requested remedy and the full procedural burden.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: a federal court is unlikely to grant a preliminary injunction where the adverse party has not been put into the preliminary-injunction lane and the movant supplies only complaint allegations rather than a developed injunction showing.

Lawyer-confirmation layer: Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 65(a)(1) provides that a court may issue a preliminary injunction only on notice to the adverse party. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 65(b)(1) separately permits a temporary restraining order without notice only if specific facts in an affidavit or verified complaint clearly show immediate and irreparable injury before the adverse party can be heard, and counsel certifies notice efforts and reasons notice should not be required. The Supreme Court's preliminary-injunction standard in Winter v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 555 U.S. 7 (2008), requires likely success on the merits, likely irreparable harm without preliminary relief, favorable balance of equities, and consistency with the public interest.

Sources checked: Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, Rule 65, United States Courts PDF, https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-civil-procedure-dec-1-2024_0.pdf; Winter v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 555 U.S. 7 (2008), https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/555/7/.
