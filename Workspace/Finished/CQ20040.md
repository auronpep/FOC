## 1. Final Question
After a Christmas craft bazaar in a church fellowship hall, Ruth and Barnabas robbed a jewelry booth at gunpoint. As they sprinted to the van, Barnabas fired toward a volunteer who was chasing them. The shot missed the volunteer but killed Naomi, a passerby carrying folding chairs. Ruth did not fire any weapon and did not know Barnabas would shoot. Under the common-law agency theory, is Ruth guilty of felony murder for Naomi's death?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
C is right because robbery is a qualifying felony and Barnabas, a co-felon, fired while the pair were still in immediate flight. Under the agency theory, the killing is attributable to participants in the robbery when it is committed in furtherance of the felony transaction or escape. Ruth does not escape liability just because she did not pull the trigger.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
A is attractive because it makes liability sound like it depends on a direct command to shoot. The breaker is that agency-theory felony murder does not require proof that Ruth ordered the killing; participation in the robbery plus the co-felon's in-furtherance killing during flight is enough.

B is attractive because it focuses on the intended target and treats the missed target as if it controls the homicide analysis. The breaker is that felony murder does not require intent to kill the particular victim; the death of a bystander during immediate flight still counts.

D is attractive because it treats personal firing as the key to murder liability. The breaker is that felony murder can reach a participant when a co-felon causes the death in furtherance of the robbery; personal discharge of the fatal shot is not required.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning
The credited rule is unchanged: under the agency theory, felony murder can attach when the killing is committed by the defendant or a co-felon acting in furtherance of the felony during the transaction or immediate flight. Ruth helped commit an armed robbery, Barnabas fired while they were still escaping, and Naomi was killed in that flight. That fits the agency rule even though Ruth did not fire the shot and did not know in advance that Barnabas would shoot.

Authority used for verification:
- [Commonwealth v. Redline, 391 Pa. 486 (1958)](https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/supreme-court/1958/391-pa-486-0.html)
- [Cornell LII: Felony murder rule](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/felony_murder_rule)

Redline states that, under common-law felony-murder principles, the killing must be by the defendant, an accomplice, or another person acting in furtherance of the felonious undertaking. The Cornell overview likewise explains that felony murder commonly reaches co-felons for killings during the felony transaction or immediate flight. This is why Ruth is guilty even though Barnabas fired the shot and Naomi, not the intended chasing volunteer, died.
