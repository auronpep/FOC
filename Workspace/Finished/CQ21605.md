### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation
Depraved-heart murder (a form of second-degree murder at common law) does not require a specific intent to kill. Instead, it requires "malice aforethought" in the form of an extreme indifference to human life, demonstrated by conduct that creates an obvious and unjustifiable risk of death. Voluntary intoxication is a defense only to specific-intent crimes (where it may negate the required purpose). However, it is generally not a defense to general-intent crimes or crimes based on recklessness, such as depraved-heart murder. Therefore, Silas’s inability to "perceive the risk" due to his self-induced intoxication does not bar his conviction.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
- **A:** This choice is an overclaim. While voluntary intoxication can negate specific intent (like the premeditation required for first-degree murder), it does not negate all forms of malice. Specifically, it does not negate the extreme recklessness required for depraved-heart murder.
- **C:** This is incorrect because voluntary intoxication *can* be relevant to specific-intent crimes (e.g., larceny, burglary, or first-degree premeditated murder). The reason it fails here is not that it is "never relevant," but that it does not apply to the reckless mental state of depraved-heart murder.
- **D:** This is the dominant trap. It incorrectly labels depraved-heart murder as a specific-intent crime. Murder is generally a "malice" crime, and depraved-heart murder specifically relies on a "reckless indifference" standard rather than a "purpose to kill." If a student incorrectly believes depraved-heart murder requires specific intent, they will wrongly conclude that intoxication is a valid defense.

### 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification
**Authority:** *People v. Register*, 60 N.Y.2d 270 (1983); MPC § 2.08(2) (if a person, due to voluntary intoxication, is unaware of a risk of which they would have been aware had they been sober, such unawareness is immaterial).
The common law and modern statutes (including the Model Penal Code) strictly hold that voluntary intoxication cannot negate the recklessness required for a conviction. Because depraved-heart murder is defined by a reckless extreme indifference to human life, the defense is unavailable.
