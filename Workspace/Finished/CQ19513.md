### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

**B**. In an ordinary § 1404(a) convenience transfer from a proper plaintiff-chosen forum, the transferee court applies the transferor's state choice-of-law rules. A defendant should not get a change in governing law merely by obtaining a convenience transfer from a proper forum. The Supreme Court's leading statement of the rule is in Van Dusen v. Barrack, 376 U.S. 612 (1964). **Gold Key:** § 1404(a) transfer from a proper plaintiff-chosen forum carries the transferor's state law; the rule is different for § 1406(a) transfer to cure a defective venue.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The supplied row tests QID 19513, a Civil Procedure item on the transferor-law carryover rule for § 1404(a) convenience transfers. The official key is A: State A's choice-of-law rules apply.

In Van Dusen v. Barrack, 376 U.S. 612 (1964), the Supreme Court held that a § 1404(a) transfer from a proper plaintiff-chosen forum carries the transferor's state choice-of-law rules; the transferee court must apply them. The rule prevents a defendant from using a § 1404(a) motion to obtain a change in the governing substantive law merely by seeking a more convenient federal forum. The rule is different for § 1406(a) transfers to cure a defective venue, where the transferee court applies its own forum's choice-of-law rules. The Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws § 145 and Klaxon Co. v. Stentor Electric Manufacturing Co., 313 U.S. 487 (1941), provide the broader framework that the federal court sitting in diversity applies the choice-of-law rules of the state in which it sits. The transformed outcome is identical: the transferee court applies the transferor's (State A / Georgia) choice-of-law rules.
