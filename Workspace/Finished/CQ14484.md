# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

In a single writing, Lydia, a Christian stained-glass restorer, contracted with Martha to restore three matching stained-glass windows in Martha's private prayer room for $1,500 each. The contract provided for Martha's payment of $4,500 upon Lydia's completion of the restoration work on all three windows. Lydia did not ask for any payment when the first window was fully restored, but she demanded $3,000 after restoring the second window.

Is Martha obligated to make the $3,000 payment?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A service contract prices three parts separately but requires payment only when all three are completed. After finishing two parts, is the service provider entitled to payment for those two parts?

## 3. Final Answer Choices (A-D, post-shuffle)

- **A:** Yes, because the contract is divisible.
- **B:** Yes, because Lydia has substantially performed the entire contract.
- **C:** No, because Lydia waived her right, if any, to payment on a per-window basis by failing to demand $1,500 upon completion of the first window.
- **D:** No, because Martha has no duty under the contract to pay anything to Lydia until all three windows have been restored.

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**D is correct.** The contract made completion of all three windows the trigger for payment. **Gold Key:** In a service contract where one party's performance takes time, that performance is ordinarily due before the other party's payment duty unless the language or circumstances make the performances due in parts. Here, the writing did not call for $1,500 after each window. It called for $4,500 upon completion of all three windows. Lydia has completed only two, so Martha does not yet owe the $3,000 payment.

**Silver Key:** Do not let separate arithmetic turn a whole-job payment clause into progress payments. First ask what event the contract uses as the payment trigger.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A - "Yes, because the contract is divisible."** This is the dominant trap because "$1,500 each" makes the job look like three mini-contracts. The breaker is the payment trigger: the writing says payment comes upon completion of all three windows, not after each window. **Gold Key:** A contract is divisible only when the paired parts of performance are properly treated as agreed equivalents. Separate pricing helps, but it does not overcome an express whole-job payment condition.

**B - "Yes, because Lydia has substantially performed the entire contract."** This choice sells the intuition that two out of three is close enough. But the job was to restore three matching windows, and one window remains unrestored. Even if substantial performance could sometimes trigger a payment duty subject to damages, this stem gives a cleaner payment condition: completion of all three windows.

**C - "No, because Lydia waived her right, if any, to payment on a per-window basis."** This reaches the right bottom-line word, but it uses the wrong route. The issue is not waiver. Lydia had no per-window payment right to waive because the contract never made $1,500 due after each completed window. **Silver Key:** When the call asks whether payment is due now, anchor on the contract's due-date language before chasing doctrines like waiver.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The credited outcome is unchanged: Martha is not obligated to pay before completion of the whole job. Under Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 234, when one party's promised performance requires a period of time, that performance is generally due before the other party's return performance unless the language or circumstances indicate otherwise. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 237 also treats the other party's remaining duties as conditioned on the earlier-due performance being rendered without an uncured material failure, subject to the part-performance rule in § 240. Section 240 applies only when the performances can be apportioned into corresponding pairs of part performances that are properly regarded as agreed equivalents.

Those rules fit the variant exactly. Lydia's restoration work takes time; Martha's payment is the return performance. The contract's language points even more strongly toward a whole-job condition because payment is due only upon completion of all three windows. Although the contract lists a per-window price, it does not pair each window with an installment payment. Therefore the contract is not divisible in the way Lydia needs, and Martha's duty to pay has not yet become due.
