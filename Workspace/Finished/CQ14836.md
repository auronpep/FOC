### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

**A is correct.** Federal Rule of Evidence 608(b) flatly bars extrinsic evidence to prove specific instances of a witness's conduct for the purpose of attacking (or supporting) the witness's character for truthfulness. The Rule carves out only one channel: the court may, on cross-examination, allow inquiry into such specific instances if they are probative of truthfulness. Here, the prosecutor did not stay on cross — she called a separate witness (the loan officer) to relate out-of-court facts about the friend's prior application statement. That is the textbook definition of extrinsic evidence offered for the truthfulness attack, and FRE 608(b) shuts the door on it. The fact that the friend's credibility is consequential to the case does not change the rule; the bar is structural, not balancing. The proper remedy was either (i) to ask further questions on cross about the loan application statement, or (ii) to use a different impeachment channel (e.g., a prior inconsistent statement under FRE 613 if the friend were the declarant of the application as a party-opponent admission, but the loan application is the friend's own statement, not a hearsay-predicate statement, and FRE 613 also has its own foundation requirements). The witness's testimony is therefore inadmissible.

> **Gold Key (GK-EVID-EXTRINSIC-01):** *FRE 608(b) bars extrinsic evidence of specific instances to attack truthfulness — cross-examination inquiry is the only door, and even that is left to the judge's discretion.*
> See FRE 608(b); the Rule's "but the court may, on cross-examination, allow them to be inquired into" language is the entire allowance; everything else is closed.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B is incorrect** (admissible, in the judge's discretion, because the friend's credibility is a fact of major consequence to the case). This is the dominant trap. The cognitive move it sells: "credibility is at issue and consequential, so the judge gets to weigh it in." That sounds like a 403 balancing call — but FRE 608(b) is not a balancing rule. The bar on extrinsic evidence of specific instances for truthfulness is structural; it does not yield to a "major consequence" or "judicial discretion" exception. The judge has discretion about whether cross-examination *inquiry* into the specific instance is allowed; the judge has no discretion to admit a *separate witness's testimony* about the specific instance. The fact that the friend is the lynchpin of an alibi defense makes the temptation to admit stronger — and the rule's purpose (to avoid mini-trials on collateral matters) more important, not less. **Gold Key:** FRE 608(b) is a structural bar, not a 403 balancing test.

**D is incorrect** (admissible, as a matter of right, because the friend "opened the door" by her denial on cross-examination). The cognitive move: "she lied, so she opened the door to proof she lied." Half-right, half-wrong. The friend's denial did open one door — to *further cross-examination* about the loan application statement, including questions about the two-year gap. It did not open the door to extrinsic evidence. "Opening the door" in evidence law is a narrow concept: it removes a specific objection (often hearsay or relevance) so the examiner can complete the picture the witness has painted. It does not let a party bypass the structural bars in FRE 608(b). Calling a separate witness to prove the contents of the loan application is exactly what 608(b) prohibits; the friend's denial did not, and could not, waive that bar. The remedy was more cross-examination, not new evidence.

**C is incorrect** (inadmissible, because the misstatement by the friend could have been caused by a misunderstanding of the application form). This is the right conclusion for the wrong reason. The testimony *is* inadmissible — but the reason is FRE 608(b)'s extrinsic-evidence bar, not the speculative possibility that the friend simply misunderstood the form. Two C3-visible problems: (i) the stated reason is a colloquial fairness appeal ("maybe she just got confused") rather than the legal test, and (ii) the reason undercuts the impeachment purpose: if the friend *could have* misunderstood the form, then the prior statement has *less* impeachment value, not more — so this reasoning, taken seriously, would *support* admissibility, not defeat it. **Silver Key (SK-EVID-ARRAY-01):** *When two answers share the same bottom-line verdict ("inadmissible"), CLASH on the stated reason — the right answer names the actual rule, not a common-sense guess.*

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

**Governing rule (verbatim from the current Federal Rule of Evidence 608(b), as amended through December 1, 2023):**

> *Specific Instances of Conduct.* Extrinsic evidence is not admissible to prove specific instances of a witness's conduct in order to attack or support the witness's character for truthfulness. But the court may, on cross-examination, allow them to be inquired into if they are probative of the character for truthfulness or untruthfulness of: (i) the witness; or (ii) another witness whose character the witness being cross-examined has testified about.

**Application to the right answer (A).** The prosecutor is offering extrinsic evidence (the loan officer's testimony) about a specific instance of the friend's conduct (a false statement on a loan application) for the purpose of attacking the friend's character for truthfulness. The first sentence of FRE 608(b) is a flat bar. The exception in the second sentence is limited to *cross-examination inquiry*, not extrinsic proof. The loan officer's testimony is therefore inadmissible.

**Verification of the transform.** The legal substance of the question is unchanged from Q14836:

- *Legal issue:* the same — admissibility of extrinsic evidence attacking a witness's character for truthfulness after a denial on cross.
- *Credited outcome:* the same — inadmissible.

**Authority floor.** The governing rule is FRE 608(b). The Rule text itself is the authority for the bar; no case citation is required to ground a clean bar. The Rule's "may … on cross-examination" carve-out is the textual source of the cross-examination-only channel. The bar is structural and does not depend on a balancing test. The transform is consistent with the original's official answer and explanation.

**Doctrinal-currency check.** FRE 608(b) has not been substantively amended in the relevant respects. The 2023 FRE amendments (Daubert-related, Rule 702; rule of completeness, Rule 106; expert basis confrontation, Smith v. Arizona) do not touch Rule 608(b). The Rule is current as drafted.
