### 6. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. Samuel is a licensed real estate agent, not a licensed lawyer. In the majority approach tested here, a nonlawyer real estate agent may conduct a real estate closing, but he may not give legal advice or answer the buyer's legal questions as though he were an attorney. Samuel's real-estate experience and law-school graduation do not make him licensed to practice law. Gold Key: separate a permitted closing role from legal advice; the first can be done by a nonlawyer in the majority approach, while the second requires a licensed lawyer. Silver Key: read the answer set as a two-by-two grid, asking separately whether Samuel may conduct the closing and whether he may answer legal questions.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B.** This choice is attractive because Samuel is not a licensed lawyer and Lydia wants legal answers. The breaker is that it overextends the unauthorized-practice concern. The legal-advice part is barred, but the closing role itself is not barred under the majority approach. Gold Key: the closing task and the legal-advice task split apart.

**C.** This choice is attractive because it notices Samuel's law-school background and might feel like a compromise: no closing authority, but some legal-answer authority. The breaker is that the permissions are backward. A nonlawyer agent may conduct the closing in the majority approach, but may not answer legal questions as legal advice.

**D.** This choice is attractive because Samuel has 22 years of real estate experience, a good relationship with Lydia, and a law degree. The breaker is licensure. Experience, trust, and law school do not authorize a nonlawyer to provide legal advice at the closing.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested issue remains Real Property, Titles, Deeds, and Conveyancing, and the verified outline code is `84040000`. That code appears verbatim in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md`; no deeper closing-specific node is listed under Titles, Deeds, and Conveyancing.

The source item tests the majority-style split between a nonlawyer's permitted closing/settlement role and the unauthorized practice of law. State law varies on real estate closings, but the official key is consistent with the common exam framing: a non-attorney may conduct the closing in jurisdictions following the majority approach, but may not give legal advice. In re Opinion No. 26 of the Committee on the Unauthorized Practice of Law, 139 N.J. 323, 654 A.2d 1344 (1995), is a leading state high-court authority allowing nonlawyer participation in residential real estate closings while protecting the boundary against legal advice. Source: https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/opinion-no-26-of-886397604

Nebraska Supreme Court Rule section 3-1004(A)(5) similarly identifies real-estate closing document work that licensed brokers, title insurers, and title insurance agents may perform when connected to a sale or loan closing, while section 3-1004(A)(7) preserves the ban on counseling or advising about legal rights or legal effects. Source: https://supremecourt.nebraska.gov/supreme-court-rules/chapter-3-attorneys-and-practice-law/article-10-unauthorized-practice-law-guidelines

Those authorities confirm the source answer's legal split. Samuel is a licensed real estate agent and may conduct the closing under the tested majority approach. But Samuel was never admitted to practice law. Lydia's questions about deed, title, and closing documents are legal questions; answering them as legal advice would be unauthorized practice. The credited outcome is unchanged: yes to conducting the closing, no to answering legal questions. No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is triggered.

**Tiny rule:** A nonlawyer may be allowed to conduct the closing, but legal advice requires a licensed lawyer.

**Trap:** Treating unauthorized practice as all-or-nothing.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-REAL_PROPERTY-NONLAWYER-CLOSING-01** - A nonlawyer real estate agent may perform a permitted closing or settlement role in the majority approach, but may not give legal advice or opinions about legal rights unless licensed to practice law.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-REAL_PROPERTY-CLOSING-2X2-01** - Turn the answer set into a two-by-two grid: yes/no on conducting the closing, and yes/no on giving legal advice. The correct cell is yes to the closing role and no to legal advice.
