## 7. Right-Answer Explanation (Choice B)

None of the three theories of homicide liability is established on these facts.

**Unlawful act doctrine (Choice C fails):** Under the misdemeanor-manslaughter rule, a person may be guilty of involuntary manslaughter if a death results from commission of a crime that is malum in se or inherently dangerous. Removing a temperature-pressure relief valve is a regulatory violation — the act is malum prohibitum (wrong only because a statute prohibits it), not malum in se (inherently and morally wrongful). Nor is the act inherently dangerous. Removing a dripping valve is not the kind of conduct that is intrinsically perilous. The doctrine does not apply to regulatory misdemeanors of this type.

**Voluntary manslaughter (Choice A fails):** Voluntary manslaughter requires intent to kill or cause great bodily harm — not merely knowledge that a type of hazard could cause death. Priscilla had no intent to harm Naomi. Awareness that loose gas fittings can be fatal does not establish the kind of culpable mental state voluntary manslaughter demands.

**Culpable negligence (Choice D fails):** Culpable or criminal negligence requires more than ordinary negligence. Depending on the jurisdiction, it requires either unreasonable conduct in the face of a foreseeable risk, willful disregard of a known risk, or conduct creating a high degree of risk of death under circumstances the defendant was aware of. Priscilla worked carefully. She accidentally disturbed the gas fitting without knowing she had done so, and she did not know she had created a specific risk. This is at most ordinary negligence, not the gross deviation or willful disregard required for criminal culpability.

**Gold Key GK-CRIMINAL-MSMR-MALUM-01:** The misdemeanor-manslaughter rule applies only to crimes that are malum in se (inherently and morally wrong) or inherently dangerous. A regulatory prohibition — wrong only because a statute says so — is malum prohibitum and does not trigger the doctrine, even if the defendant's conduct was a but-for cause of death.

**Silver Key SK-CRIMINAL-MSMR-MALUM-01:** When the stem describes the defendant's underlying crime as a statutory regulatory requirement ("must have device X"), ask: is this the kind of act that is morally wrong or dangerous in itself, or is it only wrong because the legislature prohibited it? Environmental, safety, and building code regulations are almost always malum prohibitum. The unlawful act choice collapses when the underlying crime is a regulatory violation.

## 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice A — "guilty of voluntary manslaughter, because Priscilla knew there was a possibility that death could result from a loose gas fitting."**

Voluntary manslaughter typically arises from an intentional killing in the heat of passion upon adequate provocation — reducing what would otherwise be murder to a lesser offense. It requires, at minimum, intent to kill or cause serious bodily harm. Priscilla had no intent to harm Naomi. The choice tries to build voluntary manslaughter out of awareness of risk: she "knew there was a possibility" of death. But knowing that a type of hazard can be fatal is categorically different from intending to kill or cause serious injury. This choice states a false rule: awareness of a risk does not constitute the mental state for voluntary manslaughter.

**Choice C — "guilty of involuntary manslaughter under the unlawful act doctrine, because Naomi's death would not have occurred but for Priscilla's removal of the temperature-pressure relief valve."**

This is the dominant trap. Priscilla did commit a misdemeanor (removing a required safety valve), and but-for that act Naomi would not have died. The unlawful act doctrine sounds like it fits: crime + death = manslaughter. But the doctrine carries a critical limitation: the underlying crime must be malum in se or inherently dangerous. Removing a regulatory safety device is not inherently wrong in a moral sense; it is prohibited only by statute. That makes it malum prohibitum. The choice correctly identifies but-for causation but omits the essential requirement that makes the doctrine applicable. **Gold Key GK-CRIMINAL-MSMR-MALUM-01:** But-for causation is necessary, not sufficient. The type of misdemeanor controls — and regulatory violations don't qualify.

**Choice D — "guilty of involuntary manslaughter, because a water heater is a dangerous instrumentality and Priscilla was culpably negligent."**

Culpable negligence as a theory of involuntary manslaughter is real — and the choice correctly identifies it as the applicable standard. The trap is that the facts do not support it. The stem says Priscilla worked carefully and disturbed the fitting accidentally without knowing it. She did not know she had created a gas leak. Criminal or culpable negligence requires more than this: typically either unreasonable conduct facing a known foreseeable risk, willful disregard of a recognized danger, or conduct that the defendant knew created a high probability of death or serious injury. None of those apply here. The "dangerous instrumentality" reasoning — that working on a water heater is inherently dangerous enough to establish culpable negligence — is too thin. Working carefully on a potentially dangerous object and accidentally creating an unknown hazard is ordinary negligence at most.

## 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule 1 — Misdemeanor-manslaughter (unlawful act doctrine):** A death resulting from the commission of a misdemeanor gives rise to involuntary manslaughter only if the underlying crime is malum in se (inherently and morally wrong) or is inherently dangerous in how it was committed. A regulatory violation that is malum prohibitum — wrong only because a statute says so, not because the act is inherently immoral or dangerous — does not trigger the rule. The removal of a mandated regulatory device is malum prohibitum.

**Tested rule 2 — Voluntary manslaughter:** Voluntary manslaughter is an intentional killing, reduced from murder by provocation and heat of passion, or alternatively involves intent to kill or cause serious bodily harm. Knowledge that a category of hazard can cause death does not establish this intent. No provocation or heat of passion exists here.

**Tested rule 3 — Involuntary manslaughter (culpable negligence):** Culpable or criminal negligence requires a gross deviation from the standard of care of a reasonable person — either recklessness (conscious disregard of a substantial and unjustifiable risk) under the MPC, or at common law, unreasonable conduct in the face of a foreseeable risk, willful disregard of a known danger, or creation of a high probability of death that the defendant was aware of. Priscilla's careful work and lack of knowledge that she disturbed the fitting defeat culpable negligence; ordinary negligence, at most, is present.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-CRIMINAL-MSMR-MALUM-01 — Misdemeanor-Manslaughter Rule: Malum Prohibitum Does Not Qualify**

The misdemeanor-manslaughter rule (unlawful act doctrine) applies only when the underlying crime is malum in se — inherently and morally wrong, like battery or assault — or inherently dangerous in the manner committed. A regulatory violation that is merely malum prohibitum — wrong only because a statute says so, not because the act is inherently immoral or dangerous — does NOT trigger the rule. But-for causation alone is not enough; the character of the misdemeanor controls.
