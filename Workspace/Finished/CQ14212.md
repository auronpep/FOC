## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** The court should grant Ruth's motion.

**Gold Key — GK-CIVPRO-DEFAULT-01:** Under FRCP 55, once the clerk enters default against a party who failed to plead or otherwise defend, the court may enter a default judgment against that party. This procedure operates entirely independently of any co-defendants: pending litigation against Barnabas's Ink Works does not delay or preclude the default judgment against Stephen's Gilded Parchment, and no allocation of fault between the two defendants can shield Stephen's from a default judgment.

**Silver Key — SK-CIVPRO-MULTIDEF-ISOLATE-01:** When a multi-defendant question asks about the defaulting party, evaluate that defendant's procedural posture in isolation. Anything about the non-defaulting co-defendant — its trial status, its possible liability, its defenses — is procedurally irrelevant to the default judgment ruling.

Stephen's Gilded Parchment failed to plead or otherwise defend. Ruth had the clerk enter default against it. After entry of default, Ruth properly applied to the court for default judgment. The FRCP 55 sequence is complete; the motion should be granted.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A — No, because the case against Barnabas's Ink Works has not yet gone to trial.**

The appeal here is that all claims against both defendants should be resolved together — that a plaintiff shouldn't collect a judgment against one while the other's liability is still undetermined. That intuition has nothing to do with FRCP 55.

**Gold Key — GK-CIVPRO-DEFAULT-01** defeats this: there is no trial-first requirement in FRCP 55. A default judgment may be entered as soon as a party fails to plead or defend and the clerk enters default — whether the case against co-defendants is at the pleading stage, mid-discovery, or hasn't been set for trial at all. Choice A invents a procedural prerequisite that does not exist.

**C — No, because the fact finder might determine that Barnabas's Ink Works alone was responsible for the damage to Ruth's scrolls.**

This is the dominant trap. It pulls on causation and fairness: if a fact-finder later decides Barnabas's Ink Works was solely responsible for the damage, then Stephen's Gilded Parchment did nothing wrong — so why hold it liable now?

**Gold Key — GK-CIVPRO-DEFAULT-01** defeats this: a potential allocation of sole fault to a co-defendant does not block, delay, or invalidate a default judgment against the party that failed to appear. Stephen's Gilded Parchment had the opportunity to appear and raise exactly this defense. It chose not to. That choice, not the fact-finder's future verdict, is what governs. The procedural consequence of non-appearance is default judgment.

**Silver Key — SK-CIVPRO-MULTIDEF-ISOLATE-01:** The moment you analyze the defaulting defendant's posture in isolation, the co-defendant's possible sole liability disappears from the analysis. FRCP 55 does not require the court to speculate about how fault will ultimately be allocated.

**D — Yes, because Ruth provided Stephen's Gilded Parchment with seven days' advance notice of the hearing.**

This choice gets the outcome right (yes, grant the motion) but offers a false premise as the reason. The 7-day advance notice requirement under FRCP 55(b)(2) applies only when the party against whom default judgment is sought has previously *appeared* personally or by representative.

**Gold Key — GK-CIVPRO-DEFAULT-NOTICE-02:** The 7-day notice threshold under FRCP 55(b)(2) is triggered only by a prior appearance. Stephen's Gilded Parchment never appeared — it failed to plead or otherwise defend at all. A non-appearing defendant is not entitled to any prior notice of the default judgment hearing. Notice is a non-issue here; it does not justify the result.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Rule tested:** Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 55 — Entry of Default and Default Judgment.

**FRCP 55(a):** When a party against whom a judgment for affirmative relief is sought has failed to plead or otherwise defend, and that failure is shown by affidavit or otherwise, the clerk must enter the party's default. ✓ Ruth presented the affidavit; default was entered.

**FRCP 55(b)(2):** In cases where the sum is not certain, the non-defaulting party must apply to the court for default judgment. "If the party against whom a default judgment is sought has appeared personally or by a representative, that party or its representative must be served with written notice of the application at least 7 days before the hearing." Stephen's Gilded Parchment never appeared — the notice requirement is not triggered. ✓

**Independence from co-defendants:** FRCP 54(b) permits the court to enter judgment against fewer than all parties. Nothing in FRCP 55 conditions a default judgment on the trial of co-defendants or on any finding about how fault will be allocated between defendants. The official key — Yes, default judgment is proper — is confirmed. No doctrinal conflict. No doctrinal-currency hotlist issues. Verdict: **PASS**.

**Credited outcome unchanged:** The default judgment is proper under FRCP 55 because Stephen's Gilded Parchment failed to plead or otherwise defend and default was properly entered. Identical to the original item's credited outcome. ✓

#### § 3 — question_data

Ruth, an illuminated-manuscript artist, sued Barnabas's Ink Works and Stephen's Gilded Parchment in federal court, alleging that both companies delivered materials so defective that they destroyed her commissioned Scripture scrolls and caused her substantial financial losses. Barnabas's Ink Works answered Ruth's complaint, denying that its inks were defective and denying that its products were capable of causing the type of damage Ruth described. Stephen's Gilded Parchment failed to appear or otherwise defend. Ruth then presented the appropriate affidavit to the court clerk to enter default against Stephen's Gilded Parchment. Two months later, Ruth applied to the court for a default judgment against Stephen's Gilded Parchment.

Is the court likely to grant Ruth's motion for default judgment against Stephen's Gilded Parchment?

**A:** No, because the case against Barnabas's Ink Works has not yet gone to trial.
**B:** Yes, because courts may enter default judgments when parties fail to plead or otherwise defend and the default has been entered.
**C:** No, because the fact finder might determine that Barnabas's Ink Works alone was responsible for the damage to Ruth's scrolls.
**D:** Yes, because Ruth provided Stephen's Gilded Parchment with seven days' advance notice of the hearing.

#### § 12 — remediation_card

**Card:** CARD-CIVPRO-DEFAULT-55 — FRCP 55 Default Judgment: Three Traps

**Signal:** Federal court; two (or more) defendants; one fails to appear; clerk enters default; motion for default judgment.

**Three Traps to Neutralize:**
- *Trap 1 — Trial-First Myth:* FRCP 55 has no trial-first requirement. Default judgment against a non-appearing defendant may be entered regardless of whether the case against co-defendants has gone to trial.
- *Trap 2 — Co-Defendant Shield:* The potential allocation of sole fault to a co-defendant does not block, delay, or invalidate a default judgment. The defaulting party's procedural choice not to appear governs.
- *Trap 3 — Notice Misfire:* The 7-day notice requirement under FRCP 55(b)(2) applies only when the defaulting party has previously appeared. A party that never appeared at all is not entitled to prior notice.

**Student Move:** Apply GK-CIVPRO-DEFAULT-01 to kill Traps 1 and 2. Apply GK-CIVPRO-DEFAULT-NOTICE-02 to kill Trap 3.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-CIVPRO-DEFAULT-01 — FRCP 55: Default Judgment Operates Independently of Co-Defendants**

Under FRCP 55, once the clerk enters default against a party who failed to plead or otherwise defend, the court may enter a default judgment against that party. Pending litigation against co-defendants does not delay or preclude the default judgment, and no allocation of fault to a co-defendant shields the defaulting party.

- **Type:** rule
- **Unlocks:** Distractors A (trial-first) and C (co-defendant shield)
- **Trigger:** Federal court; multiple defendants; one fails to appear; clerk enters default; motion for default judgment
- **Tested choice:** C (dominant trap)
- **Lawyer confirmation:** FRCP 55(a), 55(b)(2); FRCP 54(b)
- **Last-minute review:** Yes

---

**GK-CIVPRO-DEFAULT-NOTICE-02 — FRCP 55(b)(2): 7-Day Notice Threshold Is Appearance-Triggered**

The 7-day advance notice requirement under FRCP 55(b)(2) is triggered only if the party against whom default judgment is sought has already appeared personally or by representative. A defendant who never appeared at all is not entitled to any prior notice of the default judgment hearing.

- **Type:** threshold
- **Unlocks:** Distractor D (7-day notice misapplication)
- **Trigger:** Multiple defendants; one never appeared; motion for default judgment; answer choice mentions 7-day notice
- **Tested choice:** D
- **Lawyer confirmation:** FRCP 55(b)(2) — "if the party against whom a default judgment is sought has appeared personally or by a representative"
- **Last-minute review:** Yes
