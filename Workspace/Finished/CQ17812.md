### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. In a notice recording jurisdiction, a later purchaser for value without notice can prevail over a prior conveyance that is not effectively recorded. A recorded instrument must be in the purchaser's chain of title to give constructive record notice. When a deed is recorded outside a grantor-grantee chain, it is commonly treated as a wild deed and does not give constructive notice to a later protected purchaser.

Lawyer-confirmation authority:

- In **Sabo v. Horvath**, 559 P.2d 1038 (Alaska 1976), the Alaska Supreme Court applied the majority rule that a deed recorded outside the chain of title under a grantor-grantee index did not provide constructive notice to later purchasers. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/alaska/supreme-court/1976/2682-1.html
- In **Spradling v. May**, 65 So. 2d 494 (Ala. 1953), the Alabama Supreme Court treated deeds outside the claimant's chain as insufficient to impose constructive notice on a later grantee. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/alabama/supreme-court/1953/65-so-2d-494-1.html
- Colorado's notice-recording statute and annotations give a representative statutory frame: unrecorded conveyances do not defeat a later good-faith purchaser without notice, and proper recording gives constructive notice when the instrument is in the effective record path. Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/2021/title-38/article-35/part-1/section-38-35-109/

Those authorities support the official outcome. Barnabas had received a deed from Lydia, so he could convey whatever interest he had to Ruth. But because Barnabas's incoming deed was never recorded, Ruth's recorded outgoing deed from Barnabas was not discoverable through the ordinary grantor-grantee chain for the parcel. Timothy's deed from Ruth had the same chain problem. Esther's strongest argument is therefore that the Barnabas-to-Ruth deed was outside the chain of title and, for priority purposes, should be treated as unrecorded against Esther.
