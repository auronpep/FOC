## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

Naomi stopped at a bus depot after a women's Bible-study retreat. A man she did not know offered to sell her a shrink-wrapped case of premium study Bibles with a bookstore shipping label partly scraped off. The case looked new and was worth several hundred dollars, but the man asked only $35 cash and said he had to leave before security made another round.

Naomi thought, "That case is probably stolen, and asking questions will only make me responsible." She paid the $35 and walked away without asking where the case came from, planning to say later that she never knew it was stolen.

The case was, in fact, stolen. At Naomi's trial, the prosecution can prove the case was stolen and that Naomi recognized a strong likelihood that it was stolen. The prosecution cannot prove that Naomi had actual certainty that it was stolen.

Is Naomi guilty of receiving stolen property under the common-law standard?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Is a buyer guilty of receiving stolen property when she strongly suspects the goods are stolen and deliberately avoids confirming that fact so she can deny knowledge later?

D. No, because Naomi only suspected the case might be stolen, and suspicion alone is insufficient for the knowledge element.

### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. Receiving stolen property requires knowledge that the property was stolen. Naomi did more than passively notice a suspicious bargain. She recognized a strong likelihood that the case was stolen, refused to ask because she wanted deniability, and bought it anyway. That is willful blindness.

Gold Key: Willful blindness can count as knowledge when the defendant is aware of a high probability of the fact and deliberately avoids confirming it.

Silver Key: In this answer array, do not fight over the word "suspicion" in isolation. Split the choices on whether the buyer merely suspected the fact or deliberately avoided confirming a high-probability fact.

The breaker is the mental-state standard: common-law receiving stolen property requires knowledge, and a negligence-style "reasonable buyer would have known" theory is not enough by itself.

### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The transformed item preserves the tested rule, legal issue, trap structure, and credited outcome. Receiving stolen property requires receipt or purchase of stolen property with knowledge that the property was stolen and the intent to deprive the owner. The supplied source rule also treats willful blindness as satisfying the knowledge element.

The willful-blindness rule is grounded in real authority. In Global-Tech Appliances, Inc. v. SEB S.A., 563 U.S. 754 (2011), the Supreme Court described the criminal-law willful-blindness doctrine as requiring that the defendant subjectively believe there is a high probability that a fact exists and take deliberate steps to avoid learning that fact. Source: [Justia, Global-Tech Appliances, Inc. v. SEB S.A.](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/563/754/). Model Penal Code Section 2.02(7) likewise treats knowledge of a fact as established when a person is aware of a high probability of its existence, unless the person actually believes it does not exist. Source: [UMKC Law, Model Penal Code selected provisions](https://www1.law.umkc.edu/suni/crimlaw/mpc_provisions/model_penal_code_default_rules.htm). Cornell's Wex summary describes receiving stolen property as knowingly possessing property known to be stolen with intent to permanently deprive the owner. Source: [Cornell Wex, receiving stolen property](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/receiving_stolen_property).

Naomi's facts satisfy that rule. The case was stolen; she bought it; she recognized a strong likelihood that it was stolen; and she deliberately avoided asking so she could preserve a denial. That is not merely objective suspicion or negligence. It is the deliberate-ignorance substitute for knowledge. No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is triggered, and the researched authority does not contradict the supplied official key.
