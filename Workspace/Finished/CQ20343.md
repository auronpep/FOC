## 7. Full right-answer explanation

D is the best answer. The court should admit the testimony.

**Gold Key:** Evidence of an organization’s routine practice may be admitted to prove that the organization acted in accordance with that routine on a particular occasion. FRE 406 expressly provides that such evidence may be admitted "regardless of whether it is corroborated or whether there was an eyewitness." Lydia's testimony describing the insurer's standard barcode-print-scan-and-outgoing-mail process, combined with the company record showing that Barnabas's specific notice received the outgoing-mail scan, is sufficient to support a reasonable inference that the notice was mailed, even though Lydia has no personal recollection of processing this particular notice.

**Silver Key:** Lock the call. The objection is to the foundation for proving mailing; the question is whether the routine-practice evidence plus record lays an adequate foundation for the inference. The call is not "does anyone remember this exact envelope?" or "must the post office testify?"

The facts give a clear, repeated organizational process tied to a contemporaneous business record for this very notice. That combination is classic, admissible routine-practice foundation under the rule.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. Exclude the testimony because Lydia lacks memory of Barnabas's particular notice.**
This is the dominant trap. It feels intuitively fair and "fairness"-based that the witness should remember the specific mailing. Students see "Rachel/Lydia does not personally remember this notice" and treat personal memory as a hard prerequisite for any testimony about mailing. **Gold Key:** FRE 406 routine-practice evidence exists precisely for situations in which no one has a specific memory of the particular instance; the regularity of the organization's process itself is what permits the inference. The rule text says the evidence may be admitted "regardless of whether... there was an eyewitness."

**B. Exclude the testimony because routine practice is character evidence.**
This sells a category-confusion story. Students who have just studied FRE 404 character evidence hear "routine" or "habit" and immediately map it onto propensity reasoning. **Silver Key:** Distinguish the rules. FRE 406 is a relevance rule that allows specific, repeated conduct in a particular situation (here, the mailroom's barcode-scan-mail process) to prove what the organization did on one occasion. It is analytically and textually distinct from character evidence under FRE 404. The answer is a flat misclassification of the evidence category.

**C. Admit the testimony only if the postal worker who collected the mail testifies.**
This sounds procedurally rigorous and "chain-of-custody"-like. Students imagine that proving something entered the mail stream requires the person who physically took the envelope from the mailbox. **Gold Key:** FRE 406 does not impose an external-corroboration or third-party requirement. The organization's own routine-practice testimony plus its internal business record is enough. Requiring the postal worker would turn a simple foundation question into an impossible and unnecessary hurdle the rule was written to avoid.
