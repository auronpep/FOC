This is a claim of racial abridgment of voting rights in the structure of municipal elections. The Fifteenth Amendment is the constitutional provision whose text directly addresses that claim.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The Fifteenth Amendment, Section 1 states: "The right of citizens of the United States to vote shall not be denied or abridged by the United States or by any State on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude." Section 2 gives Congress enforcement power.

The Supreme Court has long recognized that the Fifteenth Amendment applies to schemes that abridge minority voting power on account of race, including certain racial gerrymanders and changes to electoral structures. See Gomillion v. Lightfoot, 364 U.S. 339 (1960) (striking down a racially motivated reconfiguration of city boundaries that removed Black voters from the city under the Fifteenth Amendment). Although modern vote-dilution litigation frequently proceeds under the Fourteenth Amendment's Equal Protection Clause (often in conjunction with the Voting Rights Act § 2), the source question explicitly states that "No state or federal statutes are applicable to the resolution of this suit" and asks for the "most obvious basis" of the plaintiffs' claim as framed in the stem. The stem describes a claim by "minority racial groups" that the election system "diminishes the ability of voters who are members of minority racial groups to affect the outcome of the city elections." The Fifteenth Amendment's text is the most direct and obvious match for that claim. The Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause (and Privileges or Immunities Clause) do not contain parallel explicit language protecting the right to vote on racial grounds.
