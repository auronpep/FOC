### 6. Full right-answer explanation

D is correct. Lydia did not merely react in the moment. She selected Stephen's predictable Saturday route, arrived before him, concealed herself, waited for ninety minutes, kept the weapon ready, and struck from a position of advantage. Those facts support first-degree murder because lying in wait is circumstantial evidence of premeditation and deliberation.

Gold Key: A planned ambush can prove premeditation without a confession or other direct statement of intent. The law can use conduct - concealment, waiting, positioning, and surprise attack - to prove the killer thought first and acted after that reflection.

Silver Key: In a charge-plus-reason array, do not stop when two choices name the same charge. The winning choice must give the legally correct reason for the degree.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

A. This choice sells the idea that first-degree murder needs direct proof of a plan. That is tempting because Lydia said she was "waiting to confront" Stephen, not "waiting to kill" him. The breaker is that premeditation may be proved circumstantially. Gold Key: lying-in-wait facts - concealed purpose, waiting, and a surprise attack from advantage - are enough to support premeditation and deliberation.

B. This choice reaches the right charge but gives the wrong basis. A deadly weapon helps show intent to kill, but weapon use alone does not make a killing first-degree murder. The degree comes from the planned ambush facts, not from the letter opener by itself. Silver Key: when the call asks for the most serious charge, the reason attached to the charge still matters.

C. This choice turns ordinary anger into heat-of-passion mitigation. Anger over being reported may explain motive, but it is not the sudden legally adequate provocation that reduces murder to voluntary manslaughter. The waiting, travel, concealment, and weapon preparation move the case away from heat of passion and toward deliberate killing.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. The item tests whether lying-in-wait facts support first-degree murder by proving premeditation and deliberation through circumstantial evidence.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: California Penal Code section 189 treats murder by lying in wait, and other willful, deliberate, and premeditated killings, as first-degree murder. California CALCRIM No. 521 describes lying in wait as concealment of purpose, waiting and watching for an opportunity to act, and a surprise attack from a position of advantage; it also explains that the waiting need only be substantial enough to show a state of mind equivalent to deliberation or premeditation. Sources checked: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=189 and https://www.justia.com/criminal/docs/calcrim/500/521/

The variant facts satisfy that rule. Lydia knew where Stephen would be, arrived before him, hid in the loading bay, waited ninety minutes, kept the letter opener ready, and attacked by surprise when Stephen arrived. That is evidence of advance thought and cool execution. The official key remains first-degree murder on a lying-in-wait / premeditation theory.

Attorney-review note: The MBE common-law frame uses the standard first-degree murder distinction between willful, deliberate, premeditated murder and lesser homicide.
