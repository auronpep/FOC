### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam-register)

**C is correct.** The keeper of a wild animal is strictly liable for injuries that result from the animal's characteristic dangerous propensity. A venomous snake's ability to envenomate is precisely that kind of inherent, species-typical danger. The plaintiff does not need to show that this particular snake ever bit anyone before, nor that the keeper was negligent. Strict liability attaches because the animal is wild and the harm flows from the very danger that makes the species dangerous.

**Gold Key:** Wild-animal strict liability turns on the *species' characteristic dangerous propensity*, not on the individual animal's track record. Prior harm is irrelevant.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Rule:** The keeper of a wild animal is strictly liable for injuries caused by the animal's characteristic dangerous propensity. The plaintiff need not prove negligence, nor that the individual animal had previously caused harm.

**Authority:** Restatement (Second) of Torts §§ 506–513 (Chapter 20: Liability of Possessors and Harborers of Animals). Section 507 provides that the possessor of a wild animal is subject to strict liability for harm done by the animal, even though the possessor has exercised the utmost care to prevent harm. Section 510 confirms that the possessor is liable for harm caused by the animal's characteristic dangerous propensity regardless of whether the particular animal has previously exhibited dangerous behavior.

#### Wrong-Answer Recovery Paths

**If a student picked A (dominant trap):** The student conflated wild-animal strict liability with the domestic-animal scienter rule. Recovery: review the wild vs. domestic classification. Wild animals = strict liability regardless of prior harm. Domestic animals = scienter required (knew/should have known of viciousness). The tiny anchor: "Wild = strict, domestic = scienter."

**If a student picked B (negligence bait):** The student reframed the case as negligence. Recovery: the stem explicitly says "strict liability." When the theory is strict liability, the keeper's knowledge of defects is irrelevant. The tiny anchor: "Strict liability = no fault required."

**If a student picked D (paid-work colloquialism):** The student substituted a fairness intuition for the legal rule. Recovery: the victim's employment status has no bearing on strict liability. The tiny anchor: "Who the victim is doesn't change the keeper's strict liability for wild animals."
