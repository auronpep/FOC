## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Stephen, an indigent defendant, was convicted in state court of felony burglary after breaking into Lydia's privately owned Bible-book bindery after closing. The state gives every felony defendant one appeal as of right to an intermediate appellate court.

Stephen asks the trial court for appointed appellate counsel and a free copy of the trial transcript so that he can pursue that appeal. The court denies both requests under a new budget directive and tells Stephen that he may file a handwritten pro se brief using the public docket sheet.

Stephen argues that the denial violates the Constitution. Which of the following correctly analyzes his claims?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

An indigent felony defendant receives a first appeal as of right but is denied both appointed appellate counsel and a free trial transcript. Does the Constitution require both for meaningful access to that appeal?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. Stephen has no valid constitutional claim because the Constitution does not require a state to provide criminal appeals, so the state may impose whatever conditions it chooses on its optional appellate process.

B. Stephen has a valid claim to a free transcript, but not to appointed counsel, because the constitutional right to counsel ends after trial.

C. Stephen has a valid claim to appointed appellate counsel, but not to a free transcript, because preparation of the appellate record is a matter of state procedure rather than constitutional entitlement.

D. Stephen has valid claims to both a free transcript and appointed appellate counsel for his first appeal as of right under the Due Process and Equal Protection Clauses.

### 4. Correct answer

D (original key: B)

### 6. Full right-answer explanation

D is correct. A state does not have to create an appellate system for criminal convictions. But once it gives a first appeal as of right, it may not make that appeal meaningfully available only to defendants who can pay.

Gold Key: No constitutional duty to create an appeal does not mean no constitutional rules for an appeal the state chooses to provide. For the first appeal as of right, an indigent criminal defendant must receive the basic tools needed for meaningful review: appointed appellate counsel and an adequate record.

Silver Key: Read the answer array as a 2x2 grid. The question asks whether Stephen gets counsel, transcript, both, or neither; first appeal as of right makes "both" the only complete answer.

Stephen is pursuing the state's first appeal as of right. Under the Fourteenth Amendment due process and equal protection principles applied in Griffin v. Illinois and Douglas v. California, the state must provide indigent defendants with meaningful access to that appeal. That includes an adequate appellate record, such as a free transcript when needed, and appointed counsel for the first appeal as of right. Stephen therefore has valid claims to both.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

#### A

This choice is attractive because its first sentence is true: the federal Constitution does not require states to provide appellate review at all. The breaker is the leap from that premise to "whatever conditions." Once the state creates a first appeal as of right, the Fourteenth Amendment limits how the state administers it. Gold Key: no duty to create an appeal does not let the state make the created appeal usable only by defendants with money.

#### B

This choice is attractive because the Sixth Amendment right to counsel is usually taught as a trial right, and the choice gives Stephen one useful tool: the transcript. The breaker is that appointed counsel on the first appeal as of right comes from the Fourteenth Amendment access-to-appeal line, not from simply extending the Sixth Amendment trial rule. Douglas requires counsel at that first appellate tier.

#### C

This choice is attractive because it gives Stephen counsel and treats the transcript as an administrative record issue. The breaker is that the appellate record is not a mere convenience when the state has made appeal available. Griffin requires the state to give an indigent defendant an adequate record for meaningful appellate review. Silver Key: the 2x2 answer array punishes half-credit answers; counsel-only leaves out the record tool that makes the appeal reviewable.

The credited outcome is therefore correct. Stephen is not asking for discretionary review or collateral review; he is invoking the state's first appeal as of right.
