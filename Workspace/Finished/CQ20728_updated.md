Lydia owns a privately run Christmas craft hall called **Star & Stable Market**. For the season, she leased Stall 7 to Timothy to sell handmade Nativity candles. ## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** Lydia accepted Peter’s rent check in his own name and made no objection before later trying to terminate the lease. **Gold Key:** A landlord who knows of an unauthorized assignment or sublease and accepts rent without promptly reserving rights generally waives the right to terminate for that transfer. **Silver Key:** When the answer set pits the original lease clause against later landlord conduct, do not stop at the clause; lock the later acceptance-with-no-protest fact.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** The pull is the owner-versus-tenant hierarchy. That sounds like a property answer, but the call is not asking who owns the building; it asks whether Lydia’s later conduct preserved or lost the right to terminate.

**C.** The pull is timing: it tries to characterize Lydia’s later termination as a timely objection. The breaker is the stem’s earlier fact: Lydia deposited Peter’s rent check and said nothing before changing her mind.

**D.** The pull is the lease clause. The clause matters at the start, but **Gold Key:** acceptance of rent after knowing the assignment, without prompt objection or reservation, is the rule that kills the original-clause trap.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

The tested rule is unchanged: a lease may restrict assignment or subletting, but a landlord who knows of a breach and accepts rent without preserving the objection may waive the right to declare forfeiture for that breach. A property-law source confirms that lease-transfer restrictions are enforceable when the lease provides them, but they operate against a background preference for transferability. ([FLASH Archive][1]) A California jury instruction and its sources state the rent-acceptance waiver principle: acceptance of rent after knowledge of a lease violation can waive the right to evict or declare forfeiture, including in assignment/sublease contexts when the landlord has notice and then accepts rent. ([Justia][2]) Weisman v. Clark also confirms that an assignment violating a consent covenant is not automatically void; it is voidable at the landlord’s option, and waiver requires knowledge of the assignment. ([Justia][3])

The variation preserves the same legal issue and credited outcome. Lydia’s conduct tracks the original landlord’s conduct: she accepts rent from the assignee without protest, then tries to terminate because she dislikes the assignee. That keeps the waiver rule intact.
