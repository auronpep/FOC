## 7. Right-Answer Explanation (Choice C)

FRE 615 provides that at a party's request, the court must order witnesses excluded so they cannot hear other witnesses' testimony. The modal force is mandatory — this is not a matter of discretion. However, FRE 615 expressly enumerates persons who may not be excluded. Among them: a party who is a natural person. Paul is a natural person and the named defendant. He has an absolute right to be present throughout his own trial. Esther's motion must be granted as to all ordinary, nonexempt witnesses — but Paul cannot be removed no matter how persuasive the tailoring argument, because the rule says so without qualification.

**Gold Key GK-EVIDENCE-FRE615-NATPERSON-01:** FRE 615 makes sequestration mandatory on request — but it expressly protects natural-person parties from exclusion. Being "expected to testify" does not cost a natural-person party this protection. The rule does not say "unless the party will testify." If a party is a natural person, that party stays, period.

**Silver Key SK-EVIDENCE-FRE615-NATPERSON-01:** When the person targeted for exclusion is identified in the stem as an individual party-defendant (not a corporate officer or expert), that single fact is your clash-solver. Ask: is the target a natural-person party? If yes, the natural-person exception blocks exclusion — cut any choice that says "exclude" that person.

## 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice A — "Deny the exclusion request entirely because witness sequestration in civil cases is always within the court's discretion."**

Sequestration sounds discretionary because judges often have procedural flexibility, and students who have not memorized FRE 615's modal language may assume it works like most evidentiary rulings — within the judge's control. But FRE 615 says the court "must order" witnesses excluded at a party's request. It is not permissive; it is mandatory. Choosing A gets the modal force exactly backwards and would wrongly deny Ruth's valid and rightfully granted sequestration order for all nonexempt witnesses.

**Choice B — "Exclude Paul only if Esther first demonstrates that Paul has previously given false testimony in another proceeding."**

This choice invents a precondition for exclusion that FRE 615 does not contain. Nothing in the rule requires proof of a prior dishonest act before a witness may be sequestered, and nothing creates a prior-perjury gateway to excluding a natural-person party — who cannot be excluded at all, not even with such proof. D invents a rule from scratch. When a choice attaches a specific factual showing requirement to a rule that imposes no such requirement, the mold is fabricated: it sounds plausible but has no doctrinal basis.

**Choice D — "Exclude Paul along with all other witnesses because any witness who is expected to testify may be sequestered on request."**

It is true that witnesses who are expected to testify may be sequestered on request. That is what FRE 615 is for. The trap here is that the choice says "any witness" — which sounds like a clean, broad application of a rule the student knows. But FRE 615 carves out specific exemptions, and one of them is dispositive: a party who is a natural person may not be excluded. Paul is such a party. The rule being applied is true as far as it goes; the exemption that defeats it for Paul is simply missing. **Gold Key GK-EVIDENCE-FRE615-NATPERSON-01:** "Expected to testify" does not override the natural-person party exemption. The exemption is categorical, not conditional.

## 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** FRE 615 (Excluding Witnesses). "At a party's request, the court must order witnesses excluded so that they cannot hear other witnesses' testimony." The following persons may not be excluded: (a)(1) a party who is a natural person; (a)(2) an officer or employee of a party that is not a natural person designated as its representative by its attorney; (a)(3) a person whose presence a party shows to be essential to presenting the party's claim or defense; (a)(4) a person authorized by statute to be present. The rule is mandatory on request. The natural-person party exemption (a)(1) is categorical and unconditional — it does not depend on whether the party will testify, how prejudicial sequestration would be, or any other showing.
