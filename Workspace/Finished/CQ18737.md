9. **Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer**

The tested rule is unchanged: where the conspiracy offense does not require an overt act, the conspiracy is complete when the parties agree with the required criminal purpose. Later withdrawal does not negate the completed conspiracy offense, though it may limit liability for later acts or affect a statute-of-limitations defense.

United States v. Shabani, 513 U.S. 10, 13-17 (1994), confirms that when a conspiracy statute adopts the common-law conspiracy footing and does not require an overt act, the government need not prove an overt act beyond the act of conspiring. Smith v. United States, 568 U.S. 106, 110-12 (2013), confirms that withdrawal does not negate an element of the conspiracy; it presupposes the offense and normally limits later consequences rather than erasing guilt for the conspiracy. Research URLs: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/513/10 and https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/11-8976.
