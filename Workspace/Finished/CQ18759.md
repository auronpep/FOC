## 1. Final Question

At a private Bible-memory retreat, Daniel placed six identical paper cups on a table for a "courage challenge." Five cups held grape juice. One cup held a clear cleaning solvent that Daniel knew could kill a person if swallowed. During a break, Daniel urged Stephen to choose one cup and drink it, telling the group that anyone who backed out was "all talk and no courage." Stephen, wanting to impress the others, chose a cup, drank it, and died within minutes. Daniel had prepared the cups and pushed Stephen to take the dare.

What is the most serious homicide offense for which Daniel can be convicted?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

If a defendant knowingly creates a one-in-six lethal dare, pressures the victim to take it, and the victim dies after making the final choice, is the offense murder or only manslaughter/no crime?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

D. Murder (depraved-heart), because Daniel consciously disregarded an extreme and unjustifiable risk to Stephen's life by engineering and pressuring the lethal dare.

## 8. Full Black-Letter Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The source question tests depraved-heart murder: an unintentional killing committed with malice in the form of conscious disregard of an extreme and unjustifiable risk to human life. The credited theory remains murder, not no crime, not involuntary manslaughter, and not intent-to-cause-serious-bodily-harm murder.

Authority floor: Model Penal Code section 210.2(1)(b) treats criminal homicide as murder when it is committed recklessly under circumstances manifesting extreme indifference to the value of human life. The H2O Open Casebook reproduction of MPC Article 210 states that murder includes homicide committed "recklessly under circumstances manifesting extreme indifference to the value of human life": https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/3185-tanaka-criminal-law-casebook/resources/9.1.2-mpc-article-210-criminal-homicide/. Commonwealth v. Malone, 354 Pa. 180, 47 A.2d 445 (1946), affirmed a second-degree murder conviction for a Russian-roulette-style shooting and states that malice can be shown by an intentional, uncalled-for act done in callous disregard of its likely harmful effects: https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/supreme-court/1946/354-pa-180-47-a-2d-445.html. People v. Commonwealth v. Stephen's participation does not break the homicide analysis because Daniel's conduct was a substantial cause of the death and the very risk Daniel created occurred. The official key is confirmed.
