Hannah is a master potter who commissioned Paul, a well-known ceramics designer, to design a custom kiln and glazing studio for her pottery business. Hannah told Paul she was hiring him because she trusted his unique artistic vision. ### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** Specific performance is an equitable remedy that requires the breaching party to perform as promised. However, courts will not order specific performance of a contract for personal services. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 367(1). The rationale is twofold: (1) forced personal service resembles involuntary servitude, and (2) courts cannot adequately supervise the quality of compelled personal performance. Here, the remaining design work involves Paul's personal creative services — his unique artistic vision was the very reason Hannah hired him. Because the unperformed obligations are personal services, specific performance is unavailable. Hannah's remedy lies in damages for breach of contract, not in compelling Paul to continue working.

**Gold Key:** *Specific performance is categorically unavailable for personal services contracts — the remedy is damages, not compulsion, no matter how unique or irreplaceable the service provider.*

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Rule 1 — Specific performance unavailable for personal services.**
Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 367(1): "A promise to render personal service will not be specifically enforced." This is a categorical bar. Courts will not compel performance of personal services because (a) forced service approximates involuntary servitude, and (b) effective supervision of the quality of compelled personal performance is impracticable. *See also* Restatement (Second) § 360 (factors affecting adequacy of damages — but the personal-services bar operates independently as a per se rule).

**Rule 2 — Anti-assignment clauses: right vs. power.**
Under the majority rule, a contractual provision prohibiting assignment destroys the *right* to assign (making the assignor liable for breach of contract) but does not destroy the *power* to assign (the assignment remains effective between assignor and assignee). *See* Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 322(2); UCC § 2-210(3) (goods context, same principle). The non-assigning party's remedy for an unauthorized assignment is damages, not voiding the assignment.

**Application to this item:**
The call asks whether the court should grant specific performance. The remaining work involves Paul's personal creative services. Under Rule 1, specific performance is categorically unavailable. Rule 2 (the anti-assignment clause) is irrelevant to the specific-performance question — it gives Hannah a separate damages claim but does not change the personal-services bar. The credited answer (C) is correct.
