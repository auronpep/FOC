## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are preserved: necessity generally is not a complete common-law defense to intentional homicide. The leading common-law authority is R v. Dudley and Stephens, 14 Q.B.D. 273 (1884), commonly taught for rejecting necessity as a justification for murder when defendants intentionally killed an innocent person to survive. The University of Minnesota Law Library summarizes the case as standing for the rule that homicide is not justified by necessity except in self-defense: https://lawlibrarycollections.umn.edu/classic-cases-criminal-regina-v-dudley-stephens.

That authority supports the credited result. Lydia intentionally killed Stephen to save others. Her emergency was severe, and her motive was rescue, but those facts do not make necessity a complete common-law defense to murder. The modern Model Penal Code's choice-of-evils provision, MPC section 3.02, is not the common-law rule tested here.
