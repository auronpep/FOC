### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The supplied official explanation states the governing rule: courts treat a rescuer’s arrival as foreseeable, and one who unreasonably creates danger to any human being, including himself or herself, creates a risk to a potential rescuer and owes that rescuer reasonable care.  The leading formulation is *Wagner v. International Railway Co.*, where Cardozo wrote that “Danger invites rescue” and that a wrongdoer who imperils life is accountable to a rescuer even if the wrongdoer did not foresee the specific deliverer. ([Justia][1]) New York authority also recognizes that the doctrine can apply where the culpable party placed himself or herself in peril, which preserves the original item’s self-rescue setup. ## crossover_intersection_map

| Crossover                               | Why it matters                                                       |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Torts negligence duty × proximate cause | Rescue is both duty-relevant and foreseeability-relevant.            |
| Remedies/restitution × tort duty        | Automatic indemnity is a remedy-frame trap.                          |
| Issue-Sense wrong element × Gold Key    | Actual awareness is the wrong element once the rescue rule is known. |

## review_truth

Danger invites rescue: negligent danger to human life can make the rescuer a foreseeable plaintiff.
