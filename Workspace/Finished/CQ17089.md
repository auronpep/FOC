### 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. The copy is a certified copy of a public-office record, and it bears the office seal and a certification that it is correct. Gold Key: under FRE 902(4), a properly certified copy of a public record is self-authenticating, so the proponent does not need a live records custodian just to authenticate it. Silver Key: when one answer names the self-authentication route and the other answers demand extra foundation, make the extra-foundation answers identify a rule that still requires the witness. They cannot do that here.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

B is attractive because a stipulation would make authenticity easy. The breaker is that the rules do not make the opponent's agreement a condition of authentication; the certification itself supplies the authentication route.

C sells the idea that firsthand observation is the safest foundation. The breaker is that Lydia's personal observation is not the authentication requirement for a certified public-record copy. The rule authenticates the copy through certification by the public office, not through the proponent's memory.

D is the dominant trap because live custodian testimony feels like the normal foundation move for records. Gold Key: FRE 902(4) is the exception that removes that ordinary witness need for properly certified public-record copies. Silver Key: when D demands extra foundation and A names a specific self-authentication route, test the rule route before accepting the witness-demand answer. A licensing-office employee may be useful if the certification is defective or another objection is raised, but not merely to authenticate this certified copy.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are preserved: a properly certified copy of a public record is self-authenticating for authentication purposes, so live custodian testimony is not required.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: FRE 902 is the governing rule. FRE 902(4) covers a copy of an official record, or a copy of a document recorded or filed in a public office as authorized by law, when the copy is certified as correct by the custodian or another authorized certifier, or by a compliant certificate. FRE 902 also states that self-authenticating evidence requires no extrinsic authenticity proof. A certified copy of a city licensing permit on file with the licensing office fits that rule. Therefore, the authentication objection based only on the absence of a live licensing-office employee should be overruled.

Doctrinal-currency check: no listed hotlist issue is implicated. Current Rule 902(4) confirms the same outcome; recent 2026 evidence-rule materials concern other Evidence Rules and do not change the certified-public-record-copy rule used here.

Authorities checked: FRE 902(4) and Rule 902's self-authentication text, Cornell LII, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_902; United States Courts Federal Rules of Evidence, amended to December 1, 2024, https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-evidence-dec-1-2024_0.pdf; Supreme Court proposed 2026 Evidence amendment package for Rule 801, https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/frev26_da3i.pdf.

- Hearsay: Authentication does not decide whether a statement inside the permit is hearsay or fits an exception.
- Relevance: Authentication does not decide whether the permit matters to a disputed issue.
- Public records: Certified public records can raise both authentication and hearsay questions, but this call asks only authentication.

## review_truth

A certified copy of a public record is self-authenticating when properly certified, so live custodian testimony is not needed for authentication.
