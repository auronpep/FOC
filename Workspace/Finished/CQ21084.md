## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
C is right because depraved-heart murder turns on extreme recklessness and conscious disregard of a grave risk to human life, not on a purpose to hit a particular person. Lydia fired multiple bullets into a hall she knew was crowded, which shows indifference to human life even if she later denied aiming at anyone.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
A is attractive because it treats lack of a chosen target as if it lowers the offense to negligence-level homicide. The breaker is that depraved-heart murder does not require an intent to hit a specific person; consciously spraying bullets into an occupied room is far beyond involuntary manslaughter.

B is attractive because it leans on the idea that you must be able to see the victim before homicide can attach. The breaker is that the law does not require visibility of the individual victim when the defendant knows the space is occupied and fires into it anyway.

D is attractive because it sounds like a bright-line rule and uses the presence of a gun death to push the offense upward. The breaker is that gun use alone does not automatically make a homicide first-degree; the facts support depraved-heart murder, not necessarily premeditated or statutorily elevated first-degree murder.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning
The credited rule is unchanged: recklessly creating a grave risk of death under circumstances showing extreme indifference to human life is murder, even without a chosen target. That is exactly what Lydia did when she fired multiple bullets into a crowded hall from outside.

Authority used for verification:
- Model Penal Code section 210.2(1)(b)
- [Arave v. Creech, 507 U.S. 463 (1993)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/507/463)
- [Tison v. Arizona, 481 U.S. 137 (1987)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/481/137)

The Model Penal Code treats reckless killing under circumstances manifesting extreme indifference to the value of human life as murder. Arave and Tison both recognize that extreme recklessness and indifference to human life can satisfy murder-level blameworthiness. Here, Lydia knew the hall was crowded and still fired multiple rounds through the glass wall. A specific target is unnecessary because the conduct itself created the grave risk.
