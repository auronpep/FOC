## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
B is correct. Lydia lost title through a completed foreclosure sale before she obtained the money to buy the property back. Gold Key: equitable redemption is the borrower's pre-sale safety valve; after the foreclosure sale is complete, a former owner gets title back only if a statute creates a post-sale redemption period and the owner complies with it. Silver Key: draw the time line at the foreclosure sale. Because Lydia's money arrived after that line, the winning reason must be statutory redemption, not equitable redemption.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
A is attractive because "exoneration" sounds like a technical property remedy that could rescue an owner from mortgage debt. The breaker is that exoneration belongs to debt-allocation problems involving inherited or devised property, not a living mortgagor trying to undo a completed foreclosure sale.

C is attractive because the stem deliberately says Lydia lived at Selah House, and residential status can feel protective. The breaker is that residence alone is not a title-recovery rule. It matters only if a redemption statute makes residential use a condition or gives homeowners a protected period.

D is the dominant trap because it names the familiar redemption doctrine and matches Lydia's desire to pay after default. The breaker is timing. Gold Key: an equitable right of redemption exists before foreclosure is complete; after a completed foreclosure sale, the surviving post-sale path must come from statute.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Mortgages, Right to Redemption, and the verified outline code is `83050602`. The black-letter split is between equitable redemption and statutory redemption. A mortgagor may redeem in equity before foreclosure is complete by paying the secured obligation. After a completed foreclosure sale, however, the common-law equitable right is cut off; any right to recover title from the foreclosure purchaser depends on a jurisdiction's post-sale statutory redemption scheme.

The statutory side is real. Iowa Code section 628.3 provides that a debtor may redeem real property within one year from the day of sale, and Iowa Code section 626.95 describes a purchaser's deed or certificate depending on whether the property remains subject to redemption. Nebraska Revised Statutes section 25-1530 likewise frames redemption around foreclosure sale and confirmation, and states that mortgagor interests are terminated by the sale subject to the statutory redemption/confirmation rules. Cornell's Wex summary of "equity of redemption" separately reflects the same exam distinction: equitable redemption protects the borrower before foreclosure, while some jurisdictions add a statutory post-sale right. Research source URLs: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/628.3.pdf, https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/626.95.pdf, https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=25-1530, and https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/equity_of_redemption.

No researched authority contradicted the supplied official key. The bank's refusal to resell voluntarily is not decisive; Lydia recovers only if the jurisdiction gives her an enforceable statutory right of redemption and she complies with it. Authority floor satisfied; attorney_review: false.
