## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
B is correct. The statute protects only a later purchaser for value who takes without notice. Gold Key: open, notorious possession by someone other than the seller can charge a later purchaser with inquiry notice of that possessor's rights, even if the purchaser did not actually know the unrecorded deed existed. Silver Key: in a recording-act question, do not stop at value, deed type, and recording; run the statute's notice slot last. ## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Titles, Recording Acts, Types of notice, and the verified outline code is `84040404`. The statute in the item is a notice recording act: an earlier unrecorded conveyance is ineffective against a subsequent purchaser for value only if that purchaser takes without notice. A later buyer who has actual, record, or inquiry notice is not protected.

The key notice rule is that open possession by a person other than the seller gives the later buyer a duty to inquire into that possessor's rights. If reasonable inquiry would disclose the possessor's claim, the buyer is charged with that knowledge and cannot be a bona fide purchaser without notice. Asisten v. Underwood, 183 Cal. App. 2d 304 (1960), states that possession of real property is constructive notice to an intending purchaser of the rights that inquiry would disclose. Martinez v. Affordable Housing Network, Inc., 123 P.3d 1201 (Colo. 2005), similarly explains that possession can put an interested person on inquiry notice of legal or equitable claims held by the person in open, notorious, and exclusive possession.

That authority confirms the supplied key. Timothy's deed was earlier, and although it was unrecorded, Timothy's possession and use of Cedar Ridge charged Barnabas with inquiry notice. Barnabas therefore was not a protected subsequent purchaser "without notice." The credited outcome is unchanged from the original: the later buyer loses, and the prior possessor-grantee can quiet title and stop the extraction activity. Research checked: Asisten v. Underwood at https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2d/183/304.html and Martinez v. Affordable Housing Network, Inc. at https://www.coloradojudicial.gov/sites/default/files/2024-05/04SC421.pdf. No researched authority contradicted the supplied official key.
