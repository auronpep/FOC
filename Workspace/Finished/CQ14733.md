## 1. Final Question

At a private Christian music shop, Stephen asked to examine a valuable brass trumpet that Lydia kept behind the counter. While chatting with Lydia, Stephen falsely said that he was the music director for a well-known chapel choir. After Lydia placed the trumpet on the counter, Stephen asked whether he could carry it into the courtyard for a short sound check before deciding whether to buy it. Lydia agreed, saying, "I can trust a chapel musician with my instrument." Stephen walked out through the courtyard gate with the trumpet and did not return.

One week later, Lydia saw Stephen carrying the trumpet at a hymn-sing picnic. She told him that he had to pay for it or give it back. Stephen said, "Keep pressing me and you will regret it." Frightened, Lydia left him alone.

The following list of crimes is in descending order of seriousness. What is the most serious crime Stephen committed?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. Stephen committed larceny by trick, which is treated as larceny. Lydia gave him only temporary possession of the trumpet for a sound check. She did not transfer ownership. Stephen used a present lie to get the trumpet, walked away with it, and kept it with intent to steal. Gold Key: the title-versus-possession distinction decides the theft label. Silver Key: read the answer array as four neighboring property crimes and ask which element the facts actually complete.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A. False pretenses is attractive because Stephen lied and Lydia voluntarily handed over the trumpet. The breaker is title: Lydia let him test the trumpet, but she never transferred ownership, so the Gold Key points away from false pretenses and toward larceny by trick.

B. Embezzlement is attractive because Lydia allowed Stephen to hold the trumpet before he kept it. The breaker is the quality of possession: a short testing permission obtained by fraud is not the lawful possession that makes a later conversion embezzlement.

D. Robbery is attractive because Stephen later threatened Lydia when she demanded the trumpet back. The breaker is timing: the threat came a week after the taking, so it did not supply force or fear for the original taking.

## 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

The credited outcome remains larceny. Common-law larceny requires a trespassory taking and carrying away of another's personal property with intent to permanently deprive. Larceny by trick satisfies the trespassory-taking idea when the victim is tricked into giving only possession or custody, not title. Model Penal Code section 223.2 covers theft by unlawful taking or control, and section 223.3 covers theft by deception; those rule numbers support the modern theft framing while the MBE distinction stays common-law. Cornell Wex's larceny-by-trick and false-pretenses entries confirm the exam distinction: possession only is larceny by trick; title transfer is false pretenses. Robbery requires force or threat in the taking, and the later picnic threat does not make the earlier shop taking robbery. Embezzlement requires lawful possession before conversion, which Stephen did not have because his temporary control was procured by fraud.
