### 7. Full right-answer explanation
C is correct. When a defendant offers a character witness to testify to the defendant's reputation (or opinion) for a pertinent trait, Federal Rule of Evidence 405(a) lets the prosecutor rebut on cross-examination by inquiring into relevant specific instances of the defendant's conduct, and Federal Rule of Evidence 608(b) bars extrinsic evidence of those specific instances. The prosecutor therefore cannot call a separate witness to prove the specific dishonest act; the form of proof is wrong. The fact that the bull is a different asset and the specific act is a fraudulent breeding record does not move the rule. Gold Key: when the defendant offers a character witness, the prosecutor's two rebuttal channels are reputation/opinion testimony and cross-examination of the character witness — nothing else. Calling a separate witness to prove a specific act uses the wrong form of proof and is excluded.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
D is attractive because "opening the door" feels intuitively right. The defense put on character, so the prosecution should be able to attack it. The trap is that the student over-reads the doctrine. Opening the door does let the prosecutor call a reputation or opinion witness, and it does let the prosecutor cross-examine the character witness about specific acts, but it does not let the prosecutor call a separate witness to prove the specific acts. The form of proof is still controlled by FRE 405(a) and 608(b). Gold Key: even when the door is open, the rebuttal must travel through the right channel.

A is attractive because "dishonesty or false statement" sounds like a known FRE rule. The student borrows the language of FRE 609(a)(2) (impeachment by prior conviction of a crime of dishonesty or false statement) and assumes any dishonest act is admissible as character rebuttal. The breaker is that 609 governs the admissibility of prior convictions of a witness, not the form of proof a prosecutor may use to attack a defendant's character on the merits through an extrinsic witness. The specific-act rebuttal still has to use the 608(b) channel — cross-examination of the character witness — or be excluded.

B is attractive because the student's eye is on the underlying charge (did Paul steal the bull?) and treats the rebuttal as if it had nothing to do with the case. The breaker is that Paul made honesty relevant by putting his reputation for honesty in issue. Once the defendant opens the character door, a specific dishonest act is probative on that trait; the evidence is excluded for form, not for lack of relevance. Silver Key: the reason for exclusion is the form of proof, not the relevance of the act.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Evidence, Character Evidence, Cross-examination and rebuttal of character witnesses, and the verified outline code is `32020405` (verified verbatim in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md` at "Relevance > Character Evidence > Cross-examination and rebuttal of character witnesses"). Federal Rule of Evidence 405(a) provides that, when character evidence is admissible, it may be proved by reputation or opinion testimony, and that on cross-examination of the character witness the court may allow an inquiry into relevant specific instances of the person's conduct. Federal Rule of Evidence 608(b) provides that, except for a criminal conviction under Rule 609, extrinsic evidence is not admissible to prove specific instances of a witness's conduct in order to attack or support the witness's character for truthfulness, but the court may, on cross-examination, allow them to be inquired into if they are probative of the character for truthfulness of (1) the witness, or (2) another witness whose character the witness being cross-examined has testified about. The character witness here falls within Rule 608(b)(2) (a witness whose character the witness being cross-examined has testified about). Reading 405(a) and 608(b) together, when a criminal defendant offers a character witness who testifies to the defendant's reputation for honesty, the prosecution may rebut by calling its own reputation or opinion witness, or by cross-examining the character witness about specific instances of conduct, but may not call a separate witness to prove the specific acts. The Cornell LII version of FRE 608 and the Advisory Committee's Note to Rule 405 confirm this structure. The official key remains correct after the transform, and the credited outcome is unchanged.
