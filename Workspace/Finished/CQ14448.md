## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Ruth owns a company that sells equipment to Christian print shops. She mailed Daniel, a repeat customer who prints Bible-study packets, an offer to sell him a commercial laminator for $14,800. Two days later, Daniel mailed Ruth a letter stating, "I accept your offer on the condition that you provide me with a warranty that the laminator is merchantable." After reading Daniel's letter, Ruth called Daniel and said that the offer was no longer open. Ruth promptly sold the laminator to Mary for $15,900.

If Daniel sues Ruth for breach of contract, is Daniel likely to prevail?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A merchant seller offers goods; the buyer says yes while asking for a merchantability warranty; the seller then tries to revoke and sells the goods to someone else. Did a contract form?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. No, because Ruth's sale to a buyer who paid value ended Daniel's claim against Ruth.

B. Yes, because Daniel's letter accepted the offer; a merchantability warranty was already implied in the sale.

C. Yes, because Ruth's mailed offer was a firm offer that Ruth could not revoke for a reasonable time.

D. No, because Daniel's letter demanded a new term and therefore operated as a counteroffer.

### 5. Correct answer

B (original key letter: C).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Daniel's response accepted Ruth's offer because the requested merchantability warranty did not add a new bargain term. Gold Key: in a merchant's sale of goods, the warranty of merchantability is already supplied by law unless excluded or modified. Once Daniel accepted, Ruth could not undo the contract by announcing that the offer was no longer open or by selling the laminator to Mary.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A attracts the student who lets the later sale pull the question away from the actual lawsuit. The breaker is the call: Daniel is suing Ruth for breach, so Mary's later purchase does not answer whether Ruth and Daniel already had a contract.

C attracts the student who sees a merchant, a written offer, and a later attempted revocation. Gold Key: a UCC firm offer requires a signed record that gives assurance the offer will be held open. Ruth's offer named a deal; it did not promise to keep the offer open.

D is the dominant trap because the phrase "on the condition" sounds like a counteroffer trigger. The breaker is that the supposed condition was not a new or different term at all; merchantability already came with this merchant sale.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: a buyer's acceptance of a merchant seller's offer for goods is not converted into a counteroffer merely because the buyer asks for the warranty of merchantability when that warranty is already implied by law.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: UCC section 2-314, as codified for example at D.C. Code section 28:2-314, implies a warranty that goods are merchantable in a contract for sale when the seller is a merchant with respect to goods of that kind unless the warranty is excluded or modified. UCC section 2-207 recognizes that a definite and seasonable expression of acceptance can operate as acceptance even though it states additional or different terms, unless acceptance is expressly conditional on assent to additional or different terms; here, the merchantability warranty is not an additional or different term because Article 2 already supplies it. UCC section 2-206 treats an offer to make a contract as inviting acceptance in any reasonable manner and medium unless the offer clearly indicates otherwise. UCC section 1-103 preserves consistent supplemental contract-law principles, and the mailbox rule treats an invited mailed acceptance as effective when dispatched unless the offer provides otherwise. UCC section 2-205 confirms why the firm-offer distractor fails: a merchant firm offer requires a signed record that gives assurance the offer will be held open.

Authorities checked: D.C. Code sections 28:2-314, 28:2-207, 28:2-206, 28:1-103, and 28:2-205; Cornell Wex mailbox-rule summary citing Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 63. Clean URLs: https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/28%3A2-314; https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/28%3A2-207; https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/28%3A2-206; https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/28%3A1-103; https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/28%3A2-205; https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/mailbox_rule.
