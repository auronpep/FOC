## 8. Black-Letter Legal Verification

**Rule:** In federal court, the adverse spousal testimonial privilege (spousal immunity) belongs exclusively to the witness-spouse in a criminal case. The witness-spouse may choose whether to testify against the defendant-spouse and cannot be compelled to do so. The defendant-spouse has no privilege to prevent the witness-spouse from testifying.

**Authority:**
- **Fed. R. Evid. 501** — Privileges in federal court are governed by federal common law. The spousal privileges are recognized under this rule.
- **Trammel v. United States, 445 U.S. 40 (1980)** — The Supreme Court held that the adverse spousal testimonial privilege belongs only to the witness-spouse, not the defendant-spouse. The Court rejected the older rule that the defendant-spouse could prevent the witness-spouse from testifying, reasoning that the privilege's purpose is to protect the witness-spouse's choice, not the defendant's interest in silence.
- **LII / Cornell Law Institute**, "Marital Privilege" (Wex): "In a minority of jurisdictions, the defendant spouse holds the privilege to prevent the witness spouse from testifying, regardless of the witness spouse's willingness. In federal courts, the witness-spouse alone holds the privilege."

#### Wrong Answer Recovery Paths

**If a student picked A:** You thought the defendant-spouse controls the privilege. Re-read the Gold Key: the privilege is the WITNESS-spouse's. The defendant cannot block the witness from testifying. Next time, ask: which spouse is the witness? That's who holds the privilege.

**If a student picked C:** You applied the general rule (witnesses must testify) and forgot the exception (spousal privilege). The adverse spousal privilege exists precisely to override the general compulsion rule. When you see a spouse testifying against a spouse, think privilege first.

#### Crossover Intersection Map

- **Criminal Law / Confrontation Clause:** Spousal privilege can intersect with the right to confront witnesses, but the privilege is an independent ground for exclusion.
- **Fifth Amendment:** A spouse's immunity (as in this fact pattern) removes the Fifth Amendment privilege but does not affect the spousal privilege.
- **Marital Communications Privilege:** The other spousal privilege — covers confidential communications, held by both spouses in most jurisdictions. Students often confuse the two.

#### Review Truth

In federal court, only the witness-spouse holds the adverse spousal privilege and may choose whether to testify against the defendant-spouse; the defendant-spouse cannot block the witness-spouse from testifying.
