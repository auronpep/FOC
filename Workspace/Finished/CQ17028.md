# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

After indictment in a state criminal case arising from an incident at a private Christian bookstore, the prosecutor emailed Martha’s lawyer a formal plea offer that would cap Martha’s sentence at 18 months. The lawyer accidentally archived the email in a folder for a church youth-retreat schedule and never told Martha about it. The offer expired. Martha went to trial, was convicted, and received six years. Martha proves that she would have accepted the offer. What is Martha’s strongest constitutional claim?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A lawyer fails to tell a defendant about a formal plea offer; the defendant proves she would have accepted it and later receives a harsher sentence. What is the constitutional claim?

## 3. Final answer choices

A. She has no claim because effective assistance of counsel applies only at trial, not during plea discussions.
B. She is entitled to relief automatically without proving that she would have accepted the offer.
C. She may have an ineffective-assistance claim because counsel failed to communicate a formal plea offer during plea bargaining.
D. She has no claim because she received a trial after the offer expired.

## 5. Correct answer

**C**. Original key letter: **A**.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Martha’s lawyer failed to communicate a formal plea offer during plea bargaining. **Gold Key:** the Sixth Amendment right to effective assistance applies during plea bargaining, and defense counsel generally must communicate formal prosecution offers that may be favorable to the accused. Martha also proves the core prejudice fact supplied by the stem: she would have accepted the offer. **Silver Key:** when the stem gives a formal offer, counsel silence, expiration, trial conviction, and a worse sentence, do not let the later trial become the whole question.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice is the trial-only overclaim. It treats effective assistance as if it applies only at trial. **Gold Key:** effective assistance applies at critical stages of the criminal prosecution, including plea bargaining.

**B.** This choice makes the lawyer’s miss do too much. **Gold Key:** ineffective assistance is not automatic. The defendant must show deficient performance and prejudice; in a lost-plea-offer case, prejudice focuses on whether the plea process would probably have been different.

**D.** This is the dominant trap. It sounds plausible because Martha did receive a trial. The breaker is that a later trial does not automatically wipe out plea-bargaining prejudice. **Gold Key:** plea bargaining is a critical stage for effective-assistance purposes, so the lost formal offer remains constitutionally relevant.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The source row is Q17028, a Criminal Law / Constitutional Protection of Accused Persons item keyed to the ineffective-assistance claim. The verified outline code is **75090400**, “Sixth Amendment—Other Rights of the Accused > Right to Counsel.”

The tested rule is stable. *Strickland v. Washington* supplies the general ineffective-assistance framework: deficient performance plus prejudice. *Missouri v. Frye* holds that, as a general rule, defense counsel has a duty to communicate formal prosecution plea offers that may be favorable to the accused. *Lafler v. Cooper* confirms that a later fair trial does not automatically erase prejudice from ineffective assistance during plea bargaining. The variation preserves those facts: a formal offer, counsel’s failure to convey it, expiration, a later harsher sentence, and proof that Martha would have accepted the offer.[1][2][3]
