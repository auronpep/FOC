## 7. Full right-answer explanation (C)

Deny the request unless Peter supplies admissible evidence from which the court could find the completing words were spoken. Rule 106 reaches oral statements and can let a completing portion in even over a hearsay objection, but the party seeking completion must first give the court a basis to find that the completing statement was actually made and that fairness requires adding it. Lydia denies the words; Peter offers no witness, recording, or other admissible proof. **Gold Key:** no admissible basis that the completing words exist → no completion. **Silver Key:** three choices speak in absolutes or order a strike; only C is conditioned on proof — lock the conditional one.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A (dominant trap).** The 2023 amendment really did loosen Rule 106 — it reached oral statements and let completing material in over a hearsay objection — so "removes every foundation requirement" feels like the natural next step. **Gold Key:** the amendment never erased the threshold that the completing words were actually said; the absolute "removes *every* requirement" is the overclaim tell, and the court still needs a basis to find Peter spoke them.

**B.** Striking Lydia's testimony reads as the clean fix when the rest of the conversation is contested — purge the disputed evidence. A dispute over the unproven remainder goes to weight, not admissibility; it never erases the words the witness actually heard. **Silver Key:** a choice that strikes the heard portion answers a different question than "should the court order completion."

**D.** "Any accused person is entitled to complete an accusation against him" sounds like basic fairness — let the defendant tell the whole story. The word "any" makes completion automatic, which Rule 106 never is; completion turns on proof the words were said and on fairness, not on the speaker's status.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

**Tested rule (unchanged).** FRE 106 (rule of completeness). When a party introduces part of a statement, an adverse party may require introduction of any other part "that in fairness ought to be considered at the same time." The **Dec. 1, 2023 amendment** made two changes relevant here: (a) Rule 106 now expressly covers **oral statements** (not just writings/recordings), and (b) a completing statement may be admitted **over a hearsay objection**. The amendment did **not** dispense with the threshold that the completing statement was in fact made; the proponent must give the court a basis to find the remainder exists and that fairness requires it. With the witness denying the words and no admissible proof offered, the court need not order completion.

**Credited outcome (unchanged).** Deny unless the proponent supplies an admissible basis. C is true and responsive; it breaks neither filter.
