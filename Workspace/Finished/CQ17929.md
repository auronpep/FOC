## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
B is right because the jurisdiction expressly requires safe retreat before deadly force outside the home, and Eli had a safe way out by driving away. Once safe retreat was available, complete self-defense is not available on these facts. The knife makes the threat serious, but it does not erase the retreat requirement.

Silver Key: When the facts are outside the home and a safe escape route exists, cut the castle-doctrine bait first and ask whether the retreat rule controls.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
A is attractive because it treats the knife as if it automatically authorizes deadly force. The breaker is that the retreat rule still matters outside the home, and Eli had a safe way to leave by driving away.

C is attractive because it stretches the castle doctrine to any place a person lawfully occupies. The breaker is that the facts limit the exception to the home, so a parking lot is not covered.

D is attractive because it makes the knife itself feel like the entire justification inquiry. The breaker is that a knife can support deadly defensive force only when retreat is not safely required and available; here, retreat was available, so the rule fails.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning
The credited rule is unchanged: in a jurisdiction imposing a duty to retreat before using deadly force, the defendant must retreat if safely possible unless an exception such as the home applies. Eli was outside the home in a parking lot and could have driven away safely, so the complete self-defense justification fails.

Authority used for verification:
- [Brown v. United States, 256 U.S. 335 (1921)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/256/335)
- [Beard v. United States, 158 U.S. 550 (1895)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/158/550)
- [Cornell Wex: duty to retreat](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/category/criminal-law-and-procedure?page=27)
- [Cornell Wex: castle doctrine](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/castle_doctrine)

Brown describes the retreat principle as requiring retreat so long as it can be done safely. Beard recognizes the home-based exception that removes the retreat obligation in the dwelling context. The Wex summaries track the same basic rule and exception structure. Because Eli was in a parking lot and had a safe avenue of escape, the home exception does not apply and the self-defense claim is incomplete.
