## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**D is correct.** The crime of conspiracy requires an agreement between two or more persons to commit an unlawful act. The agreement is the core of the offense — without it, there is no conspiracy, no matter how much assistance a person secretly provides. Daniel never agreed with Paul and Timothy to kill James. He privately decided to help, but a private, uncommunicated decision to assist is not an agreement. Because the agreement element is missing, Daniel cannot be guilty of conspiracy.

Daniel might well be guilty of murder as an accomplice — he knowingly aided the crime with the intent that it succeed. But the charge here is conspiracy, and the facts do not support that charge. The absence of an agreement is fatal to the conspiracy count.

> **Gold Key:** Conspiracy = agreement + intent. Secret help without agreement ≠ conspiracy. The agreement can be tacit or implied, but there must be a meeting of the minds — a person who silently decides to help, without any communication or mutual understanding with the planners, has not agreed.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### Choice A (Original D) — "No, because one of the young men effectively withdrew"

**What makes it attractive:** Students know that withdrawal can be a defense to conspiracy, and the facts clearly state that Timothy "decided not to go through with the plan and left." This triggers the withdrawal reflex.

**The breaker:** Withdrawal is a defense that applies to someone who was *part of* a conspiracy and then took affirmative steps to abandon it. But Daniel was never part of any conspiracy in the first place — there was no agreement for him to withdraw from. The withdrawal defense presupposes the existence of the conspiracy, which is precisely what is at issue. Timothy's withdrawal is irrelevant to Daniel's liability because Daniel was never a co-conspirator.

### Choice B (Original A) — "Yes, because he knowingly aided and abetted"

**What makes it attractive:** This is the dominant trap. Daniel clearly aided the crime — he disabled the brake-warning light, knowing about the plan and hoping it would succeed. Aiding and abetting is a well-established basis for criminal liability, and students who recognize Daniel's accomplice liability may select this answer without carefully reading the charge.

**The breaker:** The question charges Daniel with *conspiracy*, not with murder. Aiding and abetting makes a person liable as an accomplice to the *underlying crime* (here, murder), but it is not the same as conspiracy. Conspiracy requires an agreement; aiding and abetting requires assistance with knowledge and intent. Daniel might be guilty of murder as an accomplice, but that is not what he is charged with. The answer is legally true but not responsive to the call.

> **Gold Key:** Aiding and abetting ≠ conspiracy. One makes you liable for the principal crime; the other requires an agreement to commit it. Always match the answer to the charged offense.

### Choice C (Original B) — "Yes, because he committed an overt act in furtherance of an agreement"

**What makes it attractive:** Many jurisdictions do require an overt act in addition to an agreement for conspiracy. Daniel clearly committed an overt act — disabling the brake-warning light. Students who know the overt act requirement may think this answer is correct.

**The breaker:** The overt act requirement only applies *after* an agreement has been established. It is an additional element, not a substitute for the agreement. Daniel disabled the warning light, but he never agreed with Paul and Timothy to commit the crime. Without the agreement, the overt act is legally irrelevant to the conspiracy charge. The answer fabricates an agreement where none existed.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing rule:** Criminal conspiracy at common law requires (1) an agreement between two or more persons (2) to commit an unlawful act, with (3) intent to enter the agreement and (4) intent to achieve the unlawful objective. Many modern jurisdictions add a fifth element: (5) an overt act in furtherance of the conspiracy by at least one conspirator. *See* 18 U.S.C. § 371 (federal conspiracy); Model Penal Code § 5.03; *United States v. Shabani*, 513 U.S. 10 (1995) (holding that the federal drug conspiracy statute, 21 U.S.C. § 846, does not require an overt act, but noting that the general conspiracy statute, § 371, does).

**The agreement element is the defining feature of conspiracy.** The agreement need not be formal or express — it may be tacit or inferred from conduct. *See* *United States v. Jimenez Recio*, 537 U.S. 270 (2003). However, a person who secretly decides to help a known criminal plan, without any communication or mutual understanding with the planners, has not entered into an agreement. *See* *People v. Foster*, 198 N.E.2d 763 (N.Y. 1964) (mere knowledge of a plan and secret assistance, without agreement, does not establish conspiracy).

**Aiding and abetting (accomplice liability)** is a distinct theory of liability. A person who knowingly aids, counsels, or encourages the commission of a crime by another is liable as an accomplice to that crime. *See* 18 U.S.C. § 2; *Rosemond v. United States*, 572 U.S. 65 (2014). Accomplice liability requires that the underlying crime be committed and that the defendant assisted with knowledge and intent — but it does not require an agreement.

**Withdrawal** from conspiracy requires that the defendant have been a member of the conspiracy and that he take affirmative steps to renounce or defeat the conspiracy's objectives. *See* *United States v. United States Gypsum Co.*, 438 U.S. 422 (1978). Withdrawal is irrelevant where no conspiracy ever existed.

**Verdict:** The credited answer (C in the original, D in the variant) is correct. The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the original. Daniel's secret assistance without agreement does not satisfy the agreement element of conspiracy. **PASS.**
