7) Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

The crime of kidnapping requires unlawful removal (asportation) of a person from one place to another or unlawful confinement in a place of isolation for a substantial period. Here, Paul forced Ruth to accompany him to his car and kept her there for about 15 minutes before releasing her; that movement and period of isolation satisfy kidnapping for Ruth (one kidnapping). The other persons were ordered to lie down but were not moved to a different place of confinement; that restraint is false imprisonment, not kidnapping. Therefore, Paul may be convicted of one kidnapping.

8) Full wrong-answer explanations

B (Zero). Attractive move: treats every coerced, stationary restraint as kidnapping. Breaker: kidnapping requires asportation or confinement in a place of isolation for a substantial period — merely ordering someone to lie down without moving them does not meet the asportation element.

C (Two). Attractive move (dominant trap): counts the teller and the hostage as kidnapped because both were restrained by force. Breaker: the essential distinction is asportation — only the person moved to the car underwent the required unlawful removal; temporary on-the-floor restraint without movement is false imprisonment, not kidnapping.

D (Six). Attractive move: overcounts every person present as a separate kidnapping because all were threatened and restrained. Breaker: overbroad assumption — kidnapping requires moving or isolating victims; mere simultaneous threats to multiple persons create multiple offenses (robbery, false imprisonment) but not multiple kidnappings unless each was moved or isolated as defined.
