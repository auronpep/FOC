Ruth's wording was evaluative opinion from actual use, and Timothy's company had no canceled order, lost buyer, or other specific money loss. ### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

B. This choice reaches the right "No" result but sells a fake party limit. Trade libel is not confined to business competitors; the claim fails here because the review was evaluative opinion and no specific pecuniary loss was shown.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule and credited outcome are preserved. Injurious falsehood/trade libel requires a false disparaging statement that harms another's pecuniary interests, plus the required fault and proof of pecuniary loss. Restatement (Second) of Torts sections 623A and 626 state the false-disparagement basis for liability; section 633 addresses proof of pecuniary loss. Milkovich v. Lorain Journal Co., 497 U.S. 1 (1990), confirms the opinion/fact distinction: a statement that cannot reasonably be read as stating actual facts is not actionable as a false factual assertion. See https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-second/torts and https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/1/ .

Applying that rule, Ruth's "feels jittery" and "not worth a corner of your craft table" language is evaluative review language based on actual use. Timothy's company also cannot identify a canceled order, lost buyer, or other specific pecuniary loss. The official no-liability outcome is therefore correct.
