## 1. Final Question

Martha owned a converted grain-barn building with several private workshop bays. She leased the north bay to Peter for a 30-month term so Peter could run a Scripture-calligraphy studio. Peter paid the rent for 18 months. He then assigned the remaining 12 months of his lease to Lydia, sending Martha a signed note that Lydia would pay the rent from that point forward.

For the next four months, Lydia paid the rent directly to Martha. Lydia then moved out of the workshop bay and stopped paying rent. If Martha asserts a claim against Peter for the unpaid rent, which of the following arguments would be most effective in Peter's defense?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. Peter's best defense is that Martha's conduct amounted to a novation: Peter assigned the remaining lease term to Lydia, told Martha in writing that Lydia would pay rent from then on, and Martha knowingly accepted rent directly from Lydia for four months before the default. A novation substitutes a new obligor for the original obligor and discharges the original duty. Here, the strongest defense is that Martha accepted Lydia as the new tenant in Peter's place.

Gold Key: consent to assignment is not the same thing as release by novation. The defense needs the release/substitution move, not merely permission for Lydia to take over the lease.

Silver Key: in a best-defense call, rank answers by what they accomplish. The answer that could discharge Peter beats an answer that merely says the assignment was allowed.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because accord and satisfaction is a real contract-discharge phrase, and the facts involve a later payment pattern. The breaker is that no new obligation was imposed on Peter and then satisfied; Martha simply received ordinary rent from Lydia after the assignment. That is not an accord and satisfaction.

B sounds like a property-law objection that could protect transferability of the lease. The breaker is that Peter is not defending by challenging an anti-assignment clause; Martha is suing him for unpaid rent after the assignee defaulted. Even if assignment restraints are disfavored or strictly construed, that does not release the original tenant from rent liability.

D is the dominant trap. It is attractive because Martha accepted rent directly from Lydia while knowing that Peter had assigned the lease, so a student can reasonably infer consent to the assignment. The breaker is the Gold Key: consent to assignment only says Lydia may take the lease; it does not say Martha released Peter from his existing rent obligation. The defense needs a novation, not just consent.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, credited outcome, and trap structure are unchanged. The item tests the difference between an assignment that the landlord accepts and a novation that substitutes the assignee for the original tenant and releases the original tenant.

Governing authority: Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 279 defines a substituted contract as a contract accepted by the obligee in satisfaction of the obligor's existing duty; the substituted contract discharges the original duty. Comment a to section 279 states that when a substituted contract brings in a new party, it is called a novation. Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 280 defines a novation as that new-party substituted contract. The American Law Institute identifies the Restatement (Second) of Contracts as a modern common-law contracts authority. Sources checked: ALI Restatement page, https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-second/contracts; Restatement section 279 open text, https://advance.lexis.com/open/document/openwebdocview/-279-Substituted-Contract/; Fay Corp. v. Bat Holdings I, Inc., 646 F. Supp. 946, 951 (W.D. Wash. 1986), https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/646/946/1750378/; Cornell Wex novation summary, https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/novation.

Applied here, Peter's assignment alone would not necessarily release him. The ordinary assignment/lease-transfer rule leaves the original tenant exposed unless the landlord agrees to release him. But the call asks for the most effective argument in Peter's defense, not for a guaranteed winning defense. Martha knew of the assignment, accepted Lydia's direct rent payments for four months, and dealt with Lydia as the rent payer. Those facts make novation the only answer that could discharge Peter from Martha's later rent claim.

The other answers do not do that work. Accord and satisfaction requires a different agreement-plus-performance structure. Implied consent to assignment may be true, but consent to assignment is not release. A restraint-against-alienation argument may affect a restriction on assignment; it does not answer why Peter is discharged from the rent covenant. No doctrinal-currency hotlist item changes this rule, and no researched authority contradicted the supplied official key.

## review_truth

An assignment does not release the original obligor unless the obligee agrees to substitute the new obligor and discharge the old one; that release/substitution is the novation move.
