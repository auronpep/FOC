### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

Timothy has a **shifting executory interest**. A conveyance "to A and his heirs, but if [a third person meets a condition], then to [the third person] and his heirs" creates a fee simple subject to an executory limitation in A, with a future interest in the third person that will divest A's estate if the condition occurs. The future interest is **executorily** rather than a remainder because it cuts short a prior grantee's estate (rather than waiting for a natural termination); it is **shifting** because it is created in a third party (rather than the grantor) and operates between the grantees. Because Timothy's interest is created in Daniel's original deed, no subsequent conveyance from Barnabas is required for the interest to exist. The condition has not yet occurred (Timothy has not yet graduated and is still 22), so the interest is **contingent** — it will become possessory only if Timothy graduates before turning 25. **Gold Key:** the cutting-short axis is what makes it executory; the third-party axis is what makes it shifting. A future interest in a third party that cuts short a prior grantee's estate is a shifting executory interest.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. A vested remainder subject to open.**

This is the dominant trap. Students who remember "remainder" and "vested" reach for this answer. The breaker is that a **remainder** must wait for the natural termination of a prior possessory estate (typically a life estate). Here, Barnabas received a **fee simple** (subject to an executory limitation), not a life estate. There is no life estate to support a remainder; the future interest must therefore be **executory**, not a remainder. **Gold Key:** a remainder follows a life estate (or other estate of years); an executory interest follows a fee simple and **cuts short** the prior estate. The "subject to open" sub-class adds a further mismatch (it applies to class-based remainders, not to a single named individual), but the threshold defect is that there is no remainder at all.

**B. A possibility of reverter.**

This is the "grantor's retained interest" trap. A possibility of reverter is the future interest retained by a grantor who conveys a fee simple determinable. The marker is durational language ("so long as," "while," "during") that ties the determinable quality to the grant. Here, the future interest is in a **third party** (Timothy, not Daniel), so it cannot be a possibility of reverter. **Gold Key:** a possibility of reverter is the **grantor's** interest in a fee simple determinable; a right of entry is the grantor's interest in a fee simple subject to condition subsequent. When the future interest is in a **third party** that cuts short a prior grantee, it is a shifting executory interest, not a grantor-retained interest.

**D. No interest unless Barnabas later signs another deed.**

This is the "no interest at all" trap. Timothy's interest was created in Daniel's original deed; the deed is the dispositive instrument that creates the future interest, and no subsequent conveyance from Barnabas is required. The breaker is that the language "if Timothy graduates ... then to Timothy and his heirs" is exactly the structure of a shifting executory interest — the executory interest is created in the original deed, becomes possessory if the condition occurs, and shifts the prior grantee's estate to Timothy. **Silver Key:** read the original deed for the source of the future interest; if the original deed creates the executory interest, no later deed is required.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The supplied row tests QID 18077, a Real Property item involving a conveyance to one grantee "and his heirs" with a shifting condition in favor of a third party. The official key is **C**: a shifting executory interest.

The classification of future interests in the grantor and in third parties tracks the categories set out in the standard property curriculum and the Restatement (Third) of Property. A "remainder" is a future interest created in a transferee that becomes possessory, if at all, upon the natural termination of the prior possessory estate — typically a life estate. A "reversion" is the future interest retained by the grantor when the grantor conveys an estate of lesser duration than the grantor's own (e.g., grantor conveys a life estate and keeps the reversion). A "possibility of reverter" is the future interest retained by the grantor after a fee simple determinable. A "right of entry" (or power of termination) is the future interest retained by the grantor after a fee simple subject to condition subsequent. An "executory interest" is a future interest in a transferee (third party) that **cuts short** a prior estate — that is the cutting-short axis that distinguishes executory interests from remainders. An executory interest is "shifting" when it cuts short an interest in a **third party** (rather than reverts to the grantor) and "springing" when it cuts short the grantor's interest.

On these facts, the conveyance is "to Barnabas and his heirs" (a fee simple in Barnabas, but subject to an executory limitation because of the cutting-shifting language) "but if Timothy graduates ... then to Timothy and his heirs." The future interest in Timothy cuts short Barnabas's fee simple on a condition subsequent (graduation before 25). It is therefore an **executory** interest (cuts short), and it is **shifting** (operates between two grantees; no return to the grantor). The classification is "shifting executory interest." Timothy's interest is contingent because the condition (graduation before 25) has not yet been satisfied; if the condition never occurs, the executory interest is extinguished and Barnabas's fee simple becomes absolute. The transformed outcome is identical to the original: Timothy has a shifting executory interest.

## program_frame

This is a Real Property question that runs a future-interest classification. The two axes are (1) cutting-short vs. natural termination (executory vs. remainder) and (2) third party vs. grantor (shifting vs. springing). The call asks for the future-interest category, and the four choices name four different categories (or a non-category).

## choice_by_choice_walkthrough

### A. A vested remainder subject to open.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This is the dominant trap. A remainder must wait for the natural termination of a prior possessory estate (typically a life estate). Barnabas received a fee simple, not a life estate. There is no life estate to support a remainder; the future interest must be executory, not a remainder. The "subject to open" sub-class adds a further mismatch (Timothy is a single named individual, not an open class), but the threshold defect is that there is no remainder at all.
2. **Student label:** Vested-remainder-subject-to-open (dominant trap).
3. **True/responsive version:** "A vested remainder subject to open MIGHT be true if there were a life estate and a class-based remainder, but Barnabas received a fee simple, not a life estate, and Timothy is a single named individual, not a class. The future interest must be executory, not a remainder."
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Restatement (Third) of Property: a remainder follows a life estate; an executory interest follows a fee simple and cuts short the prior estate.

### B. A possibility of reverter.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** Wrong-holder cut. A possibility of reverter is the grantor's retained interest in a fee simple determinable. The future interest here is in a third party (Timothy), not the grantor (Daniel).
2. **Student label:** Possibility-of-reverter (wrong holder).
3. **True/responsive version:** "A possibility of reverter MIGHT be true if the future interest were in the grantor (Daniel), but the future interest is in a third party (Timothy). A future interest in a third party that cuts short a prior grantee's estate is a shifting executory interest, not a possibility of reverter."
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Restatement (Third) of Property: a possibility of reverter is the grantor's retained interest; a right of entry is the grantor's interest in a fee simple subject to condition subsequent.

### C. A shifting executory interest.

4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Restatement (Third) of Property: a future interest in a transferee that cuts short a prior estate is an executory interest; an executory interest in a third party is shifting.

### D. No interest unless Barnabas later signs another deed.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** False-source cut. Timothy's interest was created in Daniel's original deed; no subsequent deed from Barnabas is required. The Silver Key tells the student to read the original deed for the source of the future interest.
