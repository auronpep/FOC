# CQ17226 — The Quarrel of the Cookbooks

## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question (stem + call)

Naomi, a citizen of State A, was the recipe editor of *Loaves & Fishes Kitchen*, a cookbook
company incorporated and with its principal place of business in State A. A rival publisher,
*Bread of Life Press*, incorporated and with its principal place of business in State B, printed
several mocking "kitchen notes" in its latest cookbook ridiculing Naomi by name. Both companies
sold their cookbooks nationally, and each shipped about 9,000 copies a month into State C's
bookstores.

Naomi sued Bread of Life Press for libel in the federal district court in State C, because State C
was the only state in the nation where the libel statute of limitations had not yet run. The
federal district court in State C dismissed the claim, finding that State C lacked jurisdiction.

**Is the federal district court's decision correct?**

### 2. Distilled Core Question

An out-of-state publisher regularly sells thousands of copies of its content in the forum, and the
libel claim arises from that content. Does the forum have personal jurisdiction over the publisher
even though the plaintiff has no contacts with the forum?

### 3. Final answer choices (A–D, post-shuffle)

- **A.** Yes, because Bread of Life Press did not have sufficient minimum contacts with State C.
- **B.** Yes, because Naomi did not have sufficient minimum contacts with State C.
- **C.** No, because Bread of Life Press marketed and sold its cookbooks in State C.
- **D.** No, because State C was the only forum still open to Naomi.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

The dismissal is wrong, so the answer is **No** — and the reason is the defendant's own market
conduct. Personal jurisdiction in a specific-jurisdiction case turns on the **defendant's**
purposeful contacts with the forum. Bread of Life Press chose to sell roughly 9,000 cookbooks a
month into State C; by deliberately exploiting that market it could reasonably anticipate being
haled into State C's courts for a libel claim arising from the very books it sold there. That is
minimum contacts. **Gold Key:** a publisher that regularly circulates a substantial volume of its
content in a state has purposefully availed itself of that market and is subject to personal
jurisdiction there for a defamation claim arising from that content — and the plaintiff's own lack
of forum contacts is irrelevant. **Silver Key (answer-array):** A and C are the matched pair on the
single axis "did the *defendant* have minimum contacts?" — resolve them by the stem's substantial-
sales fact, which makes "marketed and sold in State C" the true side. Choice **C** states exactly
that, so it is credited.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

- **A — Yes, because Bread of Life Press did not have sufficient minimum contacts (DOMINANT TRAP).**
  This sells the right test — defendant's contacts — then quietly raises the bar, treating "just
  selling books there" as too thin to count and implying the press would need to be incorporated or
  domiciled in State C. The breaker is the quantum the law actually requires: **Gold Key** —
  regularly selling a substantial volume into the forum *is* purposeful availment, full stop, so a
  steady 9,000 copies a month is more than enough. The contacts exist; the conclusion is false.

- **B — Yes, because Naomi did not have sufficient minimum contacts.** This is attractive because
  it borrows the correct magic words ("minimum contacts") and pins them to the person the reader is
  sympathizing with. It answers the wrong question: specific jurisdiction measures the
  **defendant's** contacts, never the plaintiff's. **Silver Key (cut):** any personal-jurisdiction
  choice that analyzes the *plaintiff's* forum contacts is cuttable on sight — the plaintiff's
  contacts are non-dispositive.

- **D — No, because State C was the only forum still open to Naomi.** This reaches the right
  conclusion for a fairness reason that feels just: she would otherwise have nowhere to sue. But
  necessity is not a jurisdictional test — the fact that every other state's limitations period has
  run does not manufacture contacts with State C. It substitutes "it would be unfair to leave her
  with no court" for the minimum-contacts analysis, so the reason is not the law.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification & legal reasoning (lawyer-confirmation layer)

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are **unchanged** from the source. A forum may exercise
specific personal jurisdiction over a non-resident publisher that continuously and deliberately
circulates a substantial number of copies of its publication in the forum, where the defamation
claim arises out of that publication; the plaintiff's own contacts with the forum are not required.

- *Keeton v. Hustler Magazine, Inc.*, 465 U.S. 770 (1984) — New Hampshire had personal jurisdiction
  over a magazine that sold 10,000–15,000 copies per month there in a libel suit, even though the
  plaintiff and defendant were not New Hampshire citizens and had no other forum contacts; "regular
  monthly sales of thousands of magazines" is purposeful availment, and the plaintiff's lack of
  contacts is irrelevant.
- *International Shoe Co. v. Washington*, 326 U.S. 310 (1945) — minimum-contacts / "traditional
  notions of fair play and substantial justice" framework under the Fourteenth Amendment Due
  Process Clause.
- *Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court*, 592 U.S. 351 (2021) — currency check:
  the claim must "arise out of or relate to" the defendant's forum contacts. Satisfied here: the
  libel arises directly from the cookbook content sold in State C. Same outcome under current
  framing.

Doctrinal-currency hotlist: the only PJ entry is *Mallory v. Norfolk Southern* (2023) on
consent-by-registration general jurisdiction — not implicated by this specific-jurisdiction item.
Keeton remains controlling. Authority floor satisfied (leading cases + constitutional provision).
Research **agrees** with the supplied official key (orig A → new C). Verdict available: **PASS**.
