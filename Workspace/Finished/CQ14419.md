## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

On April 2, a private Bible-memory camp received a signed letter from Timothy, a camping-supply merchant, offering to sell the camp 900 rechargeable lanterns suitable for its cabins. Timothy's letter stated that the offer would remain open until April 25, but that the camp's acceptance must be received on or before that date. On April 21, Lydia, the camp director, mailed a letter accepting the offer. On April 22, Timothy sent Lydia a message saying that he was revoking the offer. Lydia received the message on April 22, but Timothy did not receive Lydia's acceptance letter until April 26.

As of April 27, which of the following is a correct statement?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A merchant's signed offer to sell goods says it stays open until a date and that acceptance must be received by that date; the buyer mails acceptance before the date, receives a revocation before the date, and the seller receives the acceptance after the date. Was the attempted acceptance effective?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. The offer could be revoked at any time because Lydia gave no consideration to keep it open.

B. Under the terms of Timothy's offer, Lydia's attempted acceptance was ineffective.

C. Timothy's notice revoking the offer became effective when Lydia received it.

D. A letter was the only authorized method for Timothy to revoke the offer.

### 5. Correct answer

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Timothy's offer was a merchant firm offer for goods, so his April 22 revocation did not end the offer before April 25. But the offer also stated that acceptance had to be received on or before April 25. Lydia's mailed acceptance did not reach Timothy until April 26, so the attempted acceptance came too late. Gold Key: A firm offer can block revocation without consideration, but it does not erase the offeror's stated received-by acceptance condition. Silver Key: Separate the revocation question from the acceptance-timing question; the revocation can fail and the acceptance can still fail.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because it imports the familiar option-contract instinct: no consideration, no locked offer. The breaker is the Gold Key: under the UCC firm-offer rule, a merchant's signed assurance to hold a goods offer open can be irrevocable without consideration.

C is attractive because revocation is usually effective when received, and Lydia received Timothy's message before Timothy received her acceptance. The breaker is that Timothy's signed merchant firm offer was not revocable during the stated period. Silver Key: do not let the earlier revocation message distract from the separate received-by acceptance deadline.

D is attractive because the facts use letters and messages, so the answer sounds like a communication-method rule. The breaker is that the offer did not make mail the exclusive method of revocation, and during the firm-offer period Timothy had no effective revocation method at all.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. This is a Contracts formation question about a merchant firm offer and an offeror-prescribed received-by deadline for acceptance.

Authority layer: UCC 2-205 provides that an offer by a merchant to buy or sell goods in a signed record that gives assurance it will be held open is not revocable, for lack of consideration, during the stated time, subject to the three-month cap. See NY UCC 2-205, https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/UCC/2-205. Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 60 states that when an offer prescribes the place, time, or manner of acceptance, those terms must be complied with to create a contract. Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 63 also frames the mailbox rule as applying unless the offer provides otherwise. See https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/15383-contract-law/resources/2.4.1.7-a-note-and-some-questions-about-the-mailbox-rule/ and https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/1st%2BSemester/LAW%2B505-002%2B%E2%80%93%2BContracts%2BI/R2C%2B%C2%A7%2B60.

Timothy is a merchant offering to sell goods in a signed letter. The letter says the offer will remain open until April 25, so his April 22 revocation is ineffective. But the same letter says the camp's acceptance must be received on or before April 25. Lydia's acceptance was not received until April 26. The attempted acceptance therefore did not form a contract. The credited answer remains B.
