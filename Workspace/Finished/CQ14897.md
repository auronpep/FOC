### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Daniel is charged with a violent act and claims self-defense. His peacefulness is a pertinent character trait, so he may offer reputation testimony about that trait. Lydia's testimony is in a permitted reputation form. Truthfulness is different. Daniel is not on trial for lying, and the stem does not say his character for truthfulness as a witness has been attacked. The court should admit the peacefulness reputation evidence and exclude the truthfulness reputation evidence.

**Gold Key:** Split the two traits. Peacefulness fits a violent battery charge; truthfulness fits witness credibility only after a truthfulness attack.

**Silver Key:** When one witness offers two reputation traits, do not rule on the witness as a package. Rule trait by trait.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells the mirror-image split. It treats truthfulness as the trait the trial needs and treats peacefulness as character-propensity clutter. The breaker is that battery is a violent-conduct charge, so peacefulness is the pertinent defendant trait; truthfulness is only a witness-credibility trait, and no truthfulness attack has opened that door.

**B.** This choice sells the general ban on propensity character evidence. That ban is real, but it has a criminal-defendant exception. Daniel may offer reputation evidence of his own pertinent trait, and peacefulness is pertinent to whether he committed a violent battery or acted in self-defense.

**D.** This is the dominant trap. It notices that both offered traits are positive reputation traits and admits the whole package.
