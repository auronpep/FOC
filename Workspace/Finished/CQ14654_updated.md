### 8. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The original rule and answer are unchanged. The original item tests whether a defendant who **knows the act is illegal and wrong** but suffers from an **irresistible compulsion** can be acquitted under M’Naghten, ALI/MPC, either, or no recognized defense. The official key is B. 

M’Naghten is the right-wrong cognitive test: the defendant must have such a defect of reason from disease of the mind that the defendant did not know the nature and quality of the act or did not know that the act was wrong. ([Law Library Digital Collections][1]) The ALI/MPC test states that a person is not responsible if, as a result of mental disease or defect, the person lacks substantial capacity either to appreciate criminality/wrongfulness or to conform conduct to law. ([Legal Information Institute][2]) On these facts, the experts say Mary knew the act was illegal and wrong, so the M’Naghten cognitive route is unavailable. They also say her mental disease left her unable to resist the compulsion, which maps to the ALI/MPC conform-conduct branch. The Supreme Court has also recognized that due process does not impose one canonical insanity formulation on the states, which supports treating “abolished insanity defense” as a no-defense threshold rather than a hidden route to acquittal. ([Supreme Court][3])
