The legal verification is stable. A representative conveyancing statute states that a conveyance of a freehold or similar land estate must be in writing, subscribed, and delivered by the conveyor or an authorized agent. ([Texas.Public.Law][1]) General signature law also recognizes that a “signature” can include a mark by a person unable to write. ([Justia][2]) Delivery and acceptance are the operative transfer acts: title does not pass by signing alone, and acceptance by the grantee is also required. ([OpenYLS][3]) Lack of consideration does not defeat an otherwise valid deed; one representative statute states that a deed otherwise valid does not fail for want of payment or recital of consideration. ([FindLaw][4]) Recording is a priority/notice system rather than the transfer event between the parties; representative recording statutes treat unrecorded instruments as valid between the parties or those with notice. **Student move:** Check the minimum elements before adding formal extra gates.
**Tiny rule:** Writing + parties + land + present transfer + grantor authentication + delivery + acceptance is enough between the parties.
**Trap:** Contract and courthouse instincts add conditions.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-REAL_PROPERTY-MINIMAL-DEED-FORMALITIES-01**
A deed can be valid between grantor and grantee if it is a writing that identifies the grantor, grantee, land, and present transfer, is signed or otherwise authenticated by the grantor, and is delivered and accepted. No printed deed form, consideration, notarization, or recording is required for baseline validity between them.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-REAL_PROPERTY-EXTRA-GATE-ARRAY-01**
When three deed answers add formal extra gates and one answer lists the minimal transfer elements, treat the array as an extra-gate sweep after the deed-formality anchor.
