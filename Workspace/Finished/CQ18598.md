### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Peter was indicted, so the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had attached. But attachment is not the whole question. Gold Key: the post-charge counsel rule applies to corporeal confrontations such as lineups, not to photographic displays where the accused is not present. Silver Key: separate "right has attached" from "this event is a counsel-required critical stage." A nonsuggestive six-photo array conducted outside Peter's presence did not require counsel.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A sells the strongest defense instinct: once indictment happens, counsel feels required for everything police do in the case. The breaker is the event type. Attachment is necessary, but the Sixth Amendment still asks whether this event is a critical stage requiring counsel; a photo display is outside the counsel-required lineup rule.

B sells a timing reset. It sounds simple because trial testimony is an obvious lawyer-required setting. The breaker is that the Sixth Amendment right attaches when adversary judicial proceedings begin, such as indictment; the reason there is no violation is not late attachment, but the photo-array format.

D sells a due-process photo-array worry. It is attractive because photo identifications can be suggestive in some cases. The breaker is the call and the facts: the question asks about Sixth Amendment counsel, and the stem says the array was not otherwise suggestive.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches after the initiation of adversary judicial proceedings, including indictment, but counsel is required only at critical stages where the accused needs legal assistance against the prosecutorial confrontation.

United States v. Ash, 413 U.S. 300 (1973), is the direct authority: the Sixth Amendment does not grant an accused the right to have counsel present when the government conducts a post-indictment photographic display containing the accused's picture for witness identification. United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), supplies the adjacent contrast: a post-indictment corporeal lineup is a critical stage requiring counsel. Kirby v. Illinois, 406 U.S. 682 (1972), confirms that the Sixth Amendment right attaches only at or after initiation of adversary judicial proceedings. Here, the right attached at indictment, but the procedure was a photo array, not a lineup or other corporeal confrontation. The photo array was also not otherwise suggestive, so the due-process distractor does not change the Sixth Amendment answer.
