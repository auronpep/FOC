## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are preserved. The question asks for the best constitutional argument supporting a presidential order that sends U.S. military forces abroad for noncombat rescue work while Congress is away. The correct answer is the Commander-in-Chief argument because Article II, Section 2, Clause 1 makes the President Commander in Chief of the Army and Navy. That constitutional provision is the closest listed authority for an order directing military units.

Authority: U.S. Const. art. II, Sec. 2, cl. 1. Constitution Annotated identifies the Commander-in-Chief Clause as the source of the President's role in commanding the armed forces. See https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artII-S2-C1-1-11/ALDE_00013473/ and https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-2/.

The foreign-affairs choice remains weaker because the Constitution's express foreign-affairs hooks include treaty-making, appointments of ambassadors, and receiving ambassadors, not a freestanding substitute for the Commander-in-Chief Clause when the order controls troops. See U.S. Const. art. II, Sec. 2, cl. 2 and art. II, Sec. 3; Constitution Annotated background at https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artII-S1-C1-8/ALDE_00013797/.
