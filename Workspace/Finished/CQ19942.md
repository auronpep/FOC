# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question — Stem + Call

Lydia sued Barnabas in federal district court, on diversity jurisdiction, over a contract for the sale and delivery of a flock of sheep. The parties disputed what had been promised when they struck the deal — whether Barnabas had agreed to deliver the flock by the start of the lambing season and whether he had warranted that the ewes were sound. At trial the judge used a special verdict form. The jury answered written questions about what each party said and agreed to during the negotiations, but the jury did not state which party should win. After receiving the answers, the judge applied the contract-law instructions to those findings and entered judgment.

**What is the proper division of labor between the judge and the jury?**

## 2. Distilled Core Question

In a federal jury trial using a special verdict, the jury answers written factual questions but does not name a winner, and the judge then applies the law to those findings. Who does what?

## 3. Final Answer Choices (A–D, post-shuffle)

**B.** The verdict must be returned to the jury, because a special verdict is invalid unless it names the prevailing party.

**C.** The parties, not the court, apply the governing law once the jury has answered the questions.

**D.** The court must set the jury's written answers aside and decide the case itself in a new bench trial.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam register)

**A is correct.** The stem describes exactly how a special verdict works, and A restates it: the jury returns only written findings on the disputed facts of the negotiation, and then the **court** applies the governing contract law to those findings and enters judgment. That is the defining division of labor for a special verdict.

**Gold Key:** Under a special verdict (FRCP 49(a)), the jury answers only factual questions and does **not** declare a winner; the court supplies the law. The verdict that names the prevailing party is a *general* verdict (49(b)) — so a special verdict's silence on the winner is correct, not a defect.

**Silver Key:** The stem already narrates a procedure that worked — the judge received the answers and entered judgment. The "proper division of labor" is the choice that *restates* that narration; every other choice contradicts a fact the stem already gives you.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**B — special verdict invalid unless it names the winner (dominant trap).** This choice trades on the intuition that a "verdict" must announce who won, and that a jury that stays silent on the winner has done something incomplete. The breaker is the special-vs-general verdict line: naming the prevailing party is the hallmark of a *general* verdict; a special verdict is *supposed* to return only factual findings and leave the legal conclusion to the court. **Gold Key:** the jury's silence on the winner is the special verdict working as designed, not a ground to send it back.

**C — the parties apply the law.** This choice is attractive because parties do argue the law and propose judgments. The breaker is direction: the stem itself says the *judge* applied the contract-law instructions to the findings, and that is the rule — the court, not the parties, applies the governing law to the jury's findings and enters judgment.

**D — set the answers aside and hold a new bench trial.** This choice sounds like a tidy cure if something seems off about a verdict that names no winner. The breaker is that the written answers are the whole point of a special verdict; the court uses them, it does not discard them and retry the facts itself. Nothing in the stem warrants a do-over.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Right-Answer Legal Reasoning (lawyer-confirmation layer)

**Tested rule (unchanged from the original):** the special-verdict division of labor under **Fed. R. Civ. P. 49(a)**. The court may require the jury to return only a special verdict — written findings on each issue of fact — and the court then applies the law to those findings and enters judgment.

**Why A is correct under the governing rule.** A special verdict separates fact-finding (jury) from law-application (court). The jury's job is to resolve the disputed facts of the negotiation in writing; the court's job is to apply the governing contract law to those facts and enter judgment. A says exactly this, so it is true and fully responsive to the "division of labor" call.

**Authority (real, kept out of the student layer):**
- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 49(a)** — special verdict: the court may require a jury to return only "a special written finding on each issue of fact"; the court gives the instructions needed for the jury to make those findings and then applies the law.
