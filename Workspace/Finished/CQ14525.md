**A.** This choice is attractive because it gives the reversal result and invokes foreseeability, a familiar contract-damages limiter. The breaker is the reason: the defect in the instruction is the categorical new-business-profit bar, not a breach-time foreseeability test. **Silver Key:** in an appellate result-plus-reason array, a correct result with the wrong because-clause still fails.

**D.** This choice leans on the familiar idea that juries decide damages. The breaker is the absolute word “solely.” A jury may find damages facts, but an appellate court can review whether the legal instruction misstated the damages rule.

Black-letter verification: contract lost profits are limited by reasonable certainty, not by a categorical “new business” ban. The credited outcome is unchanged: reversal is proper because the jury instruction used an outdated categorical bar.
