## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

**A is correct.** Esther's statement is offered against Timothy, her alleged principal; if Esther was acting as Timothy's agent on a matter within the scope of her authority, the statement is non-hearsay as an admission by a party-opponent under **FRE 801(d)(2)(D)**. The relevance of Hannah's testimony to the existence of a contract turns on whether an agency relationship existed. When the relevance of evidence depends on a fact, **FRE 104(b)** requires only that the proponent introduce *evidence sufficient to support a finding* that the fact exists; the court may admit the proposed evidence on the condition that the proof be introduced later. The court does **not** have to find the foundational fact by a preponderance of the evidence before letting the testimony in — that would be the FRE 104(a) standard, which does not apply to questions of conditional relevance. The jury then decides whether the agency relationship existed. **Gold Key (GK-EVIDENCE-AGENCY-ADMISSION-01):** *Conditional relevance (FRE 104(b)) uses a "sufficient to support a finding" bar, not a preponderance bar.* **Silver Key (SK-EVIDENCE-AGENCY-ADMISSION-01):** *Two answers say admissible (different standards), two say inadmissible (different reasons) — lock the hearsay classification first (agent admission = non-hearsay), then the CLASH on the standard resolves to "sufficient to support a finding."*

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations (diagnostic register; one per distractor)

That puts it inside **FRE 801(d)(2)(D)** — the party-opponent exemption for agent admissions. The statement is *not hearsay* at all, so the "not within any exception" framing collapses. **C is incorrect.** *This is the dominant trap.* C engineers the move every MBE student wants to make: agency must be shown, and "agency must be shown" sounds like the court needs to be *sure* before letting the statement in. The breaker: when relevance depends on a foundational fact, **FRE 104(b)** sets the bar at *evidence sufficient to support a finding* — a much lower standard than a *preponderance of the evidence*. The court admits the testimony now and lets Hannah connect the agency dots later; the jury makes the final call. C's "by a preponderance of the evidence" framing smuggles in the FRE 104(a) standard, which the rules reserve for a different category of preliminary question. The **Gold Key (GK-EVIDENCE-AGENCY-ADMISSION-01)** kills the trap: conditional relevance is "sufficient to support a finding," not "preponderance."

**D is incorrect.** The trap D engineers is the "missing-witness" reflex: the student assumes that an out-of-court statement requires the declarant to take the stand. Because Esther's statement is offered as an admission by a party-opponent under FRE 801(d)(2)(D), it is not hearsay, and Esther's availability is irrelevant to its admissibility. ## 9. Black-letter verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited answer (A in the variant) is correct under the same black-letter rule the original answer (B) was tested on. The chain:

1. **Hearsay threshold (FRE 801(c)).** A "statement, other than one made by the declarant while testifying at the current trial or hearing, offered into evidence to prove the truth of the matter asserted." Esther's oral statement to Hannah is an out-of-court statement offered to prove that the offer was accepted. On its face, it is hearsay.
2. **Party-opponent exemption (FRE 801(d)(2)(D)).** A statement is *not* hearsay if it "is offered against an opposing party and was made by the party's agent or employee on a matter within the scope of that relationship and while it existed." Esther is alleged to be Timothy's agent; the statement concerns a transaction within the scope of her role as venue coordinator (accepting an event-services offer on behalf of the venue). The contents of the statement itself may be considered as one piece of evidence on the agency question, but they are not the only piece — the proponent cannot rest on the statement alone to prove agency.
3. **Conditional relevance (FRE 104(b)).** When the relevance of evidence depends on whether a fact exists, "proof must be introduced sufficient to support a finding that the fact does exist. The court may admit the proposed evidence on the condition that the proof be introduced later." The agency relationship is the foundational fact. The judge does **not** decide agency by a preponderance of the evidence (that is the FRE 104(a) standard, which governs a different class of preliminary questions such as privilege and witness qualifications). The judge lets the testimony in subject to later proof; the jury then decides whether Esther was Timothy's agent.
4. **Authoritative floor.** Fed. R. Evid. 801(d)(2); Fed. R. Evid. 104(a)–(b). See also *Bourjaily v. United States*, 483 U.S. 171 (1987) (judge resolves preliminary fact questions under FRE 104, and the party-opponent framework is a hearsay exemption, not a true exception). The substantive law is unchanged from the source row.

No superseding authority on these rules; no doctrinal-currency concern triggered.
