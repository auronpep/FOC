### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The credited scenario is murder based on intent to inflict serious bodily injury followed by death. At common law, murder is an unlawful killing with malice aforethought, and malice includes intent to cause grievous or serious bodily harm when death results. Texas Penal Code section 19.02(b)(2) gives a modern statutory formulation of the same tested idea: murder may be committed by intending to cause serious bodily injury and committing an act clearly dangerous to human life that causes death. Peter's bat strike was intended to inflict serious injury, and Stephen died, so the murder charge can be sustained even without proof that Peter intended death.

The distractors preserve their original charge-specific defects. Attempt requires intent to commit the target offense and a substantial step strongly corroborating that intent, reflected in Model Penal Code section 5.01. The arson scenario lacks malicious burning. The manslaughter scenario lacks the recklessness or criminal negligence hook needed for involuntary manslaughter.
