### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Criminal, Homicide, Murder, and the verified outline code is `74020101`. The official answer is consistent with common-law transferred intent. In *State v. Seba*, 305 Kan. 185, 380 P.3d 209 (2016), the Kansas Supreme Court held that when a homicidal act is directed at one person but another person dies, the defendant's intent to kill the target can prove first-degree premeditated murder of the unintended victim. *Ruffin v. United States*, 642 A.2d 1288 (D.C. 1994), states the same basic rule: one who intends to kill one person and kills another is deemed to have committed whatever homicide would have been committed had the intended victim died. That is exactly what happens here. Peter formed the deliberate plan to kill Martha, placed the bomb, and set the lethal mechanism in motion. Lydia's death therefore inherits the same premeditated intent, so the homicide is first-degree murder. The security officer's later warning efforts may affect the factual story, but they do not undo Peter's mens rea or break the completed homicide chain. The source explanation's transferred-intent reasoning matches the real authorities, so the credited answer stays unchanged.
