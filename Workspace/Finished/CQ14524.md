### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**B is correct.** Under UCC § 2-718(2), when a seller justifiably withholds delivery because of the buyer's breach, the buyer is entitled to restitution of any amount by which the buyer's payments exceed the greater of (a) the liquidated-damages amount (here, none) or (b) 20% of the total performance value or $500, whichever is smaller. Here, the $1,000 deposit exceeds the $500 floor, so Timothy has a restitution right.

However, § 2-718(3) subjects that right to offset: the seller may deduct damages recoverable under other UCC provisions. Under § 2-708(2), a lost volume seller — one who could have made both the original sale and the resale — recovers the profit it would have earned on the lost sale. Peter is a high-volume goat breeder with multiple buyers waiting; he would have sold a goat to Timothy *and* to the second buyer. His lost profit on the Timothy contract is $3,000 − $1,800 = $1,200.

**Gold Key (Lost Volume Seller Offset):** A defaulting buyer's restitution right under § 2-718 is *not* eliminated by the breach — it is merely offset by the seller's provable damages, including lost profits under § 2-708(2) for a lost volume seller. The buyer recovers the deposit minus the seller's lost profit.

Timothy recovers: $1,000 − $1,200 = $0 (the offset cannot make Timothy pay *extra*; the recovery is floored at $0). The answer choice correctly states the *formula*: $1,000 minus lost profit.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is wrong** — but it's the most attractive trap. Timothy's repudiation did cost Peter a second sale, and Peter *is* entitled to that lost profit. But the inference runs the wrong way: the lost-volume-seller doctrine is an *offset* to the buyer's restitution claim, not a complete bar. The buyer still gets the deposit back minus the lost profit. **Gold Key:** Lost volume seller damages are an offset under § 2-718(3), not a forfeiture rule.

**C is wrong** — Timothy is the party in default, but UCC § 2-718(2) expressly gives a defaulting buyer a right to restitution of the deposit exceeding the statutory floor. The old common-law rule that a defaulting buyer forfeits the deposit has been superseded by the Code. The blanket-forfeiture answer overclaims.

**D is wrong** — The $500 figure in § 2-718(2)(b) is the *floor* below which the seller can always retain (the smaller of 20% of contract price or $500), not the measure of the seller's offset. Here, the seller's actual damages ($1,200 lost profit) exceed $500, so the offset is the actual damages, not the statutory minimum. This choice confuses the floor with the ceiling.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing authority:** UCC § 2-718(2)–(3) (restitution and offset); UCC § 2-708(2) (lost volume seller profits).

**§ 2-718(2):** Where the seller withholds delivery due to the buyer's breach, the buyer is entitled to restitution of payments exceeding the greater of (a) liquidated damages or (b) 20% of total performance or $500, whichever is smaller.

**§ 2-718(3):** The buyer's restitution right is subject to offset for (a) the seller's damages under other UCC provisions and (b) benefits received by the buyer.

**§ 2-708(2):** If the measure in § 2-708(1) (contract price minus market price) is inadequate to put the seller in as good a position as performance, the measure is the profit the seller would have made from full performance, including reasonable overhead and incidental damages, due credit for payments or proceeds of resale.

**Lost volume seller doctrine:** A seller qualifies as a lost volume seller if it had the capacity and demand to make both the original sale and the resale. The seller's damages are the per-unit profit margin on the lost sale. *See* J. Breen, *The Lost Volume Seller and Lost Profits Under U.C.C. § 2-708(2)*, 50 U. Miami L. Rev. 779 (1996); *see also* the Harris test (seller would have solicited and completed the additional sale and had capacity to perform both contracts).

**Application:** Peter is a high-volume goat breeder with multiple buyers. He sold the goat to a second buyer for the same $3,000. He would have sold *both* goats. His lost profit = $3,000 − $1,800 = $1,200. Timothy's $1,000 deposit is subject to a $1,200 offset. Timothy recovers $0 under the formula (the offset wipes out the deposit, but Timothy owes nothing beyond losing the deposit). Answer B correctly states the formula: $1,000 minus lost profit.

**Invariant layer confirmed:** The tested rule (UCC § 2-718 restitution + § 2-708(2) lost volume offset), the credited outcome (buyer recovers deposit minus lost profit), and all three trap mechanics are unchanged from the original.
