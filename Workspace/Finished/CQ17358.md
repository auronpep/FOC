At a private Christian bookstore's Saturday Bible-trivia night, Naomi and Stephen argue over a reserved table. ### 6. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. Stephen faced, at most, ordinary nondeadly physical force: an open-hand slap to the shoulder and a bump into a rolling cart. He answered with deadly force by striking Naomi in the head with a heavy brass display stand and killing her. Self-defense is limited by proportionality. Gold Key: deadly force is justified only when the defender reasonably believes it is necessary to prevent imminent death or serious bodily harm. Silver Key: when the choices split between "some force was allowed" and "deadly force was too much," name the force-level axis before picking. Because Naomi's conduct did not threaten death or serious bodily harm, Stephen's complete self-defense claim fails.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because Naomi did make intentional physical contact, and that contact could support some defensive response. The breaker is the word "any." Physical contact does not unlock every level of force. Nondeadly contact may justify proportionate nondeadly defense, but it does not justify a killing blow.

C is attractive because it reaches the same bottom-line result: Stephen's claim fails. The breaker is the reason. The stem says Naomi made the first physical contact. Stephen's problem is not that he started the fight; it is that he escalated a nondeadly encounter into deadly force.

D is the dominant trap. It uses Stephen's stated fear of another slap or bump, so it feels responsive to self-defense. The breaker is force level. Gold Key: fear of ordinary physical contact is not the same as a reasonable belief in imminent death or serious bodily harm. That fear might explain some defensive movement, but it cannot justify deadly force.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested legal issue is unchanged: whether a defendant may claim complete self-defense after responding to nondeadly force with deadly force. The credited outcome is unchanged: the claim is invalid because the response was disproportionate.

The lawyer-confirmation authority floor is Model Penal Code § 3.04. Section 3.04(1) states the general self-protection rule in terms of force the actor believes immediately necessary to protect against unlawful force. Section 3.04(2)(b) then limits deadly force: deadly force is not justified unless the actor believes it necessary to protect against death, serious bodily harm, kidnapping, or forced sexual intercourse. Public text reproductions: H2O Open Casebook, Model Penal Code § 3.04, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/6141-tanaka-criminal-law-casebook/resources/12.2.1-model-penal-code-mpc-304-use-of-force-in-self-protection/ and Criminal Law Web, Model Penal Code §§ 3.04-3.07, https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/3_04-3_07.htm.

That rule matches the common MBE proportionality point. Deadly force is reserved for deadly or seriously injurious threats. Naomi's slap and bump were unlawful or offensive physical contact, but the source facts and the variant facts both deny the aggravating facts that would make deadly force reasonable: no visible weapon, no serious injury, and only one nondeadly physical act. The credited answer remains the same legal substance: self-defense fails because deadly force was disproportionate to nondeadly force.
