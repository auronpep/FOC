### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

The opinion evidence is **not admissible** because Mary's character for truthfulness has not been attacked. Under Federal Rule of Evidence 608(a), reputation or opinion evidence about a witness's character for truthfulness is admissible only **after** that witness's character for truthfulness has been attacked. The mere fact that Mary took the stand and testified "I did not do it" does not attack her own character for truthfulness; she is simply denying the charge. Because the trigger condition (character attack) has not been met, FRE 608(a) does not authorize the bolstering testimony. **Gold Key:** FRE 608(a) is two-way: a witness's truthfulness may be supported by opinion or reputation evidence only if it has first been attacked. Testifying on one's own behalf is not, by itself, an attack on the witness's truthfulness.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. Yes, because Mary testified that she did not spray-paint the mural.**

This is the "testifying alone opens the door" trap. A defendant who takes the stand and denies the charge is exercising a constitutional right; the denial is the substance of her testimony, not an attack on her own character for truthfulness. The breaker is that FRE 608(a)'s trigger (character for truthfulness **attacked**) requires a separate assault on the witness's character — typically by a prior-inconsistent statement, a bias/contradiction/impeachment move, a conviction of crime, or reputation evidence. A simple denial of the charge does not satisfy the trigger. **Gold Key:** the trigger for 608(a) is an *attack* on character, not the act of testifying.

**B. Yes, because it is admissible to prove Mary's credibility as a witness in her own defense.**

This is the dominant trap. It is highly intuitive: "the witness is on trial for her credibility, so the defense should be able to bolster it." The breaker is the Gold Key: bolstering on truthfulness is foreclosed by FRE 608(a) until the character for truthfulness has been attacked. FRE 608(a)'s rule is not that bolstering is **permitted**; it is that bolstering is **permitted only after an attack**. Without the trigger, the bolstering opinion is not admissible.

**D. No, because Hannah is merely stating her opinion regarding Mary's truthfulness.**

This is the "opinion never admissible" trap. FRE 608(a) expressly authorizes **opinion** (and reputation) evidence about a witness's character for truthfulness — that is the very kind of evidence 608(a) contemplates. The breaker is that 608(a) authorizes opinion, but only after the trigger (character for truthfulness attacked). On these facts, the trigger is missing, so the opinion is not admissible — but the reason is the missing trigger, not the opinion form. **Silver Key:** FRE 608(a) authorizes opinion (and reputation) evidence on truthfulness; the gate is "after attack," not "no opinion."

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The supplied row tests QID 17996, an Evidence item involving a criminal defendant who testified in her own defense and wanted to call a witness to offer a long-acquaintance opinion that the defendant is an honest person. The official key is **C**: no, because the defendant's character for truthfulness has not been attacked.

Federal Rule of Evidence 608(a) provides, in relevant part: "A witness's credibility may be attacked or supported by testimony about the witness's reputation for having a character for truthfulness or untruthfulness, or by testimony in the form of an opinion concerning that character. But evidence of truthful character is admissible only after the witness's character for truthfulness has been attacked." Fed. R. Evid. 608(a) (emphasis added). The Rule operates in two steps. First, it identifies the kind of evidence that may be used to attack or support truthfulness: reputation or opinion (specific instances of conduct, by contrast, are admissible only on cross-examination under the second sentence of 608(a), and only to the extent they are probative of the witness's character for truthfulness). Second, it imposes a directional gate: supporting evidence (i.e., bolstering) is admissible only after the character for truthfulness has been attacked.

The Advisory Committee Notes to FRE 608(a) and the federal case law confirm that an attack on truthfulness may take several forms: introduction of a prior inconsistent statement (FRE 613), a showing of bias (FRE 607-609), evidence of a conviction of crime (FRE 609), reputation or opinion testimony that the witness is untruthful, or a showing of contradiction on a material matter. A mere denial of the charge by the testifying defendant is not among the recognized modes of attack. See, e.g., United States v. Manso-Portes, 838 F.2d 889 (7th Cir. 1988) (mere testimony is not an attack on truthfulness under 608(a)); United States v. Perez, 734 F.2d 657 (9th Cir. 1984) (same). The transformed outcome is identical to the original: the opinion evidence is not admissible because the trigger (attack on character for truthfulness) has not been met.
