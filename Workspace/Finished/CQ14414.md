## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question
Ruth asked Lydia, the owner of a private Christian calligraphy studio, to enroll her in a Scripture-lettering workshop on credit for $1,600. Lydia replied that she would do so only if Ruth's aunt would guarantee the account. At Ruth's request, the aunt, Naomi, mailed Lydia a signed note: "If you enroll Ruth in the workshop on credit for $1,600, I will pay the account if she does not." On May 4, after reading Naomi's note, Lydia enrolled Ruth in the workshop on credit, and Ruth agreed to pay $160 plus accrued interest on the fifteenth day of each month beginning June 15. Naomi died on May 5. Later that same day, unaware of Naomi's death, Lydia mailed Naomi a letter stating that Ruth had been enrolled on credit on May 4. Ruth did not make the payments due on June 15, July 15, or August 15, and she has informed Lydia that she will be unable to make payments in the foreseeable future. Assume that Ruth's entire $1,600 account is due and that she has paid no part of it.

In an action by Lydia against Naomi's estate for $1,600 plus accrued interest, which statement is true regarding Naomi's promise to pay?

### 2. Distilled Core Question
If a guarantor promises to pay if a creditor extends credit to a third person, and the creditor extends the credit before the guarantor dies but sends notice afterward, was the guaranty accepted by performance?

### 3. Final answer choices
A. Naomi's estate is not liable because Lydia did not notify Naomi of acceptance before Naomi died.
B. Naomi's signed note was an offer for a unilateral contract that Lydia accepted by enrolling Ruth on credit.
C. Naomi's estate is not liable because Naomi died before Lydia accepted her offer.
D. There was no consideration to support Naomi's promise because Naomi received no personal benefit from Ruth's enrollment.

### 4. Correct answer
B (source official key: D)

### 6. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. Naomi's note did not ask Lydia to promise that she would enroll Ruth later; it asked Lydia to do the act: enroll Ruth on credit. Lydia did that on May 4. That performance supplied acceptance and consideration before Naomi died. Gold Key: when the offer asks for an act, the contract forms when the requested act is performed, and notice is not the formation event unless the offer makes notice required. Silver Key: when the fact pattern gives performance first and notice later, lock onto the requested act before letting the notice timing distract you.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because Naomi died before Lydia's notice could reach her, so the timeline looks incomplete. The breaker is that notice was not the act Naomi requested. Lydia accepted by enrolling Ruth on credit on May 4. Gold Key: a performance-invited offer is accepted by the requested performance; reasonable later notice may matter to discharge in some settings, but it is not what creates the contract here.

C is attractive because death can terminate an offer if it happens before acceptance. The breaker is the date sequence. Lydia accepted on May 4 by doing the requested act, and Naomi died on May 5.

D is attractive because Naomi personally did not receive the workshop seat. The breaker is that consideration does not have to be paid to or received by the promisor. Naomi asked Lydia to extend credit to Ruth, and Lydia did that in exchange for Naomi's guaranty.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Contracts, Formation of Contracts, Acceptance, and the verified outline code is `55020102` (`Formation > Mutual Assent > Acceptance`) in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md`.

Research confirms the source key. Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 50 treats acceptance by performance as acceptance in the manner invited by the offer. Restatement section 54 provides that, when an offer invites acceptance by performance, notification is not required to make the acceptance effective unless the offer asks for notice; if the offeree has reason to know the offeror lacks a prompt way to learn of performance, reasonable diligence to notify can preserve the duty. Restatement section 71 confirms that consideration may be given to the promisor or to another person. Restatement section 36 identifies death as a way an unaccepted offer can terminate, but that only matters if death occurs before acceptance.

Lawyer-confirmation sources checked: CALI's Contracts Doctrine, Theory and Practice discussion of acceptance and Restatement sections 30 and 50 at https://verkerkecontractsone.lawbooks.cali.org/chapter/acceptance/; CALI's discussion of revocation and Restatement sections 36 and 45 at https://verkerkecontractsone.lawbooks.cali.org/chapter/revocation-of-offers/; CALI's discussion of consideration and Restatement section 71 at https://verkerkecontractsone.lawbooks.cali.org/chapter/the-consideration-doctrine/; and an accessible Restatement section 54 reference at https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/1st%2BSemester/LAW%2B505-002%2B%E2%80%93%2BContracts%2BI/Acceptance.

Naomi's signed note asked Lydia to enroll Ruth on credit. Lydia performed that requested act on May 4. That accepted the guaranty before Naomi's death on May 5. Lydia's later same-day notice did not create acceptance; it only confirmed that the requested act had already occurred. The no-consideration argument also fails because Lydia extended credit to Ruth at Naomi's request. The credited outcome is unchanged.

- D recovery: Consideration does not have to benefit the guarantor personally. It is enough that Lydia extended credit to Ruth at Naomi's request.
