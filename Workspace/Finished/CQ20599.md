### 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. The signed agreement says it is the complete and exclusive statement of the parties’ agreement. Martha is offering a prior oral add-on inside the same writing/rehearsal deal. **Gold Key:** a complete-and-exclusive integration bars prior or contemporaneous consistent additional terms within its scope; “it does not conflict” is not enough. **Silver Key:** clash “not conflicting” against “complete and exclusive,” then let the integration phrase decide.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B.** The trap sells the attractive move: “Nothing in the writing says no handbells, so the promise does not conflict.” The breaker is that consistency only helps when the writing is not complete and exclusive; here the signed writing says it is complete and exclusive. **Gold Key:** complete integration blocks the prior oral add-on even if it is consistent. **Silver Key:** do not stop at contradiction; first ask whether the writing is complete.

**C.** The trap sells the idea that leaving out a promised feature damages the signed document itself. The breaker is object control: Martha offered the oral promise, so the court rules on that evidence, not by excluding the signed contract.

**D.** The trap sells a sales-of-goods frame because UCC parol-evidence language is familiar. The breaker is that merger clauses and complete integrations are not limited to goods contracts; this is a common-law services contract, and complete integration still matters.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule and outcome are unchanged. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 210 defines a completely integrated agreement as one adopted as the complete and exclusive statement of the agreement; § 213 provides that a binding completely integrated agreement discharges prior agreements within its scope; and § 216 states that evidence of a consistent additional term is admissible unless the court finds the agreement completely integrated. ([Open Casebook][1]) The variation remains a common-law services contract, so the oral handbell promise is excluded for the same reason the original fountain promise was excluded.

### 10. Why the variation preserves rigor / difficulty

The legal test is identical: prior oral consistent add-on + signed complete-and-exclusive writing. The dominant trap survives: students who stop at “does not contradict” pick B. The difficulty is preserved because the decisive distinction is still complete integration versus mere consistency.
