# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

Daniel asked Lydia to lend him something he could use to get into Peter's padlocked storage trailer at a private Christian music retreat so Daniel could steal Peter's portable keyboard. Lydia handed Daniel a heavy tent-stake puller. Daniel took it, but then found a battery grinder that Peter had left beside the trailer. Using the grinder, Daniel cut the padlock, entered the trailer, and took the keyboard. Later that evening, Daniel used the keyboard to play hymns at Lydia's backyard Bible study. Lydia was surprised and pleased by the music. Burglary in the jurisdiction applies to any structure or building, and there is no nighttime element. Lydia has been charged as an accomplice to burglary and larceny. Of which crimes, if any, is she guilty?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

If Lydia purposely gave Daniel a tool to help him break into Peter's locked trailer and steal the keyboard, is she liable for burglary and larceny even though Daniel used a different tool?

## 3. Final answer choices

A. Burglary, but not larceny, because Lydia intended to assist only in opening the trailer.

B. Neither burglary nor larceny, because Lydia's tent-stake puller was not actually used.

C. Burglary and larceny.

D. Larceny, but not burglary, because Lydia gave no actual help in opening the trailer but later enjoyed the music.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Lydia purposely supplied Daniel with a tool for the planned trailer break-in and keyboard theft. Gold Key: accomplice liability attaches when the helper purposely aids, agrees to aid, or attempts to aid the offense; the principal's choice to complete the crimes with a different tool does not erase the helper's purposeful assistance. Silver Key: do not let the tool swap split the crimes; track Lydia's purpose through the whole planned break-in-and-theft package.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because it notices that Lydia handed over a tool aimed at opening the locked trailer. The breaker is that the planned opening was only the means to the planned theft, so her purpose reached both the burglary and the larceny.

B is attractive because Daniel did not use Lydia's exact tool, which makes her help feel factually wasted. The Gold Key kills that trap: purposeful aid or attempted aid is enough for accomplice liability when the crimes are committed, even if the principal uses a different instrumentality.

D is attractive because Lydia later enjoyed the keyboard performance, which sounds like the theft side has a separate benefit hook. The breaker is that later pleasure is not the basis for liability; her earlier purposeful tool assistance for the planned entry and theft makes her liable for both crimes.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule is accomplice liability for purposeful assistance to another person's crimes. Model Penal Code section 2.06(3)(a)(ii) states the core formulation: a person is an accomplice when, with the purpose of promoting or facilitating the offense, the person aids, agrees to aid, or attempts to aid another in planning or committing it. The same concept appears in modern accomplice statutes such as 18 Pa. Cons. Stat. section 306(c). Here, Lydia gave Daniel a tool with the purpose of helping him break into the locked trailer and steal the keyboard. That purpose covered both the unlawful entry and the theft object. Daniel's later use of Peter's grinder instead of Lydia's tent-stake puller does not change the legal issue or credited outcome. The tested rule, issue, and answer substance are unchanged from the source: the accomplice is guilty of both burglary and larceny.
