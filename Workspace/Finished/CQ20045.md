7) Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

Federal Rule of Evidence 602 requires evidence sufficient to support a finding that the witness has personal knowledge, and a witness's own testimony may supply the foundation. Nathan's testimony that he was present, looking through the glass, and that he saw the carts collide supplies the threshold foundation. Challenges based on distance, rain, or reflections attack credibility and accuracy and are proper subjects for cross-examination and jury weighing, not automatic exclusion. The court should therefore overrule the objection and admit Nathan's testimony; the adversary may expose perceptual weaknesses on cross-examination.

8) Full wrong-answer explanations

B (Sustain). Attractive move (dominant trap): treats imperfect perception as a legal bar to testimony. Breaker: Foundation requires sufficient evidence of perception, which Nathan's direct testimony provides; imperfections affect weight, not admissibility.

C (Photo required). Attractive move: demands corroboration as a gatekeeping shortcut. Breaker: Rule 602 allows the witness's own testimony to provide the foundation; requiring a photograph invents a prerequisite not supported by the rules.

D (Jury decides first). Attractive move: confuses conditional relevancy thresholds (Rule 104(b)) and jury role. Breaker: The judge screens threshold foundation under Rule 104(b); the jury later assesses credibility and weight.
