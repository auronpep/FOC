## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Naomi, as representative of Hannah's estate, has brought a products liability action against a defendant, the manufacturer of a three-wheel electric delivery cart that Hannah was driving after a Bible-study coat drive when she was fatally injured in a tip-over on a sloped service road. Naomi claims that a design defect in the cart caused it to tip over. The defendant claims that the cause of the accident was Hannah's driving at excessive speed during a sleet storm. Witnesses leaving the fellowship hall gave contradictory estimates about the cart's speed just before it tipped. It is also disputed whether Hannah was killed instantly.

Which of the following items of offered evidence is the court most likely to admit?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

In a products case over a fatal vehicle tip-over, which offered item is most likely admissible when speed and whether the driver died instantly are disputed?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. Evidence offered by the defendant that Hannah had received two citations for speeding in the previous three years.

B. Photographs taken at the accident scene and during the autopsy that would help Naomi's medical expert explain to the jury why she concluded that Hannah did not die instantly.

C. A videotape offered by the defendant of a test conducted by the defendant showing that a three-wheel electric delivery cart of the same model Hannah was driving did not tip over when driven by a professional driver on a dry proving lane at the top speed testified to by the witnesses.

D. A videotape offered by Naomi of a television news program about three-wheel electric delivery carts that includes footage of accident scenes in which the carts had tipped over.

### 5. Correct answer

B (original key letter: D).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is most likely to be admitted. The photographs are tied to a disputed fact: whether Hannah died instantly. They also serve a concrete expert-explanation purpose. The court can limit the jury's use of the photographs to that purpose, and the evidence is less likely than the other choices to confuse the product-defect and speed issues.

Gold Key: Under Rule 403, even disturbing photographs may be admitted when they help an expert explain a disputed, consequential issue; exclusion requires unfair prejudice or confusion to substantially outweigh probative value.

Silver Key: Start with the offered purpose. The choice that names a disputed fact and an expert's explanatory need has a cleaner fit than dramatic videos or propensity material.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because the defendant's theory is excessive speed, and prior speeding citations feel like they point in the same direction. The breaker is that the citations are being used to show Hannah probably drove fast on this occasion, which is propensity reasoning barred by the character-evidence rule in this civil setting.

C is the dominant trap. It is attractive because it uses the same model and the disputed top speed, so it looks like a scientific recreation. Gold Key: An experiment or demonstration gets traction only when the testing conditions are sufficiently similar to the litigated event; here the professional driver, dry proving lane, and missing sleet conditions make the test a poor fit and a likely source of confusion.

D is attractive because the footage shows the same broad kind of product tipping over, which feels emotionally and visually persuasive. The breaker is fit. General news footage about other carts and other accidents has weak probative value on this cart, this road, this weather, and this driver, while carrying a serious risk of unfair prejudice and jury confusion.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

Legal research checked the current Federal Rules of Evidence. Rule 401 defines relevance by whether evidence tends to make a consequential fact more or less probable. Rule 403 allows exclusion when probative value is substantially outweighed by dangers such as unfair prejudice, confusing the issues, or misleading the jury. Rule 404(a) and 404(b)(1) bar character or other-act evidence offered to prove action in accordance with character on a particular occasion. Current Rule 702, as amended, allows expert opinion when the proponent shows it is more likely than not that the expert's specialized knowledge will help the trier of fact and rests on sufficient facts, reliable methods, and reliable application.

Authorities checked: FRE 401, FRE 403, FRE 404, and FRE 702, using the current LII Federal Rules pages at https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_401, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_403, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_404, and https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_702.

The credited item is unchanged in legal substance. The photographs are relevant because they help the medical expert explain a conclusion about a disputed consequential fact: whether the decedent died instantly. That expert-explanation use is stronger than the other offered items because it connects directly to a disputed issue and can be controlled with limiting instructions. The other video evidence has weak fit and high confusion risk, and the prior citations are prohibited propensity evidence.
