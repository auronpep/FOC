### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

**Verified rule:** Evidence derived from unlawful police conduct is generally subject to exclusion as fruit of the poisonous tree, but the inevitable discovery doctrine permits admission when the prosecution proves by a preponderance of the evidence that the same evidence would inevitably have been discovered through lawful means.

**Governing authority:** *Nix v. Williams*, 467 U.S. 431 (1984), recognized the inevitable-discovery doctrine and approved admission where an organized search would have discovered the evidence without the unlawful interrogation. See Justia, https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/467/431/ and the Library of Congress U.S. Reports PDF, https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep467/usrep467431/usrep467431.pdf. *Wong Sun v. United States*, 371 U.S. 471 (1963), supplies the fruit-of-the-poisonous-tree baseline that the exception modifies.

Under *Nix*, that is enough to admit the knife despite the unlawful statement.
