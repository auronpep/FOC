Collection on the note would become barred by the statute of limitations on August 31. After receiving advice from a nonlawyer cousin in her prayer group, Lydia worried about the legal effect of the June 1 writing. Full right-answer explanation:
B is correct. Daniel did more than repeat, "I will pay what I already owe." He promised to keep the 7% interest running through February 28, even though Lydia was agreeing not to sue before then and the limitations deadline would pass on August 31. Gold Key: the preexisting-duty rule cuts off a promise to do only what is already legally required; it does not cut off a meaningful extension term that gives the creditor extra interest or gives up the debtor's ability to end interest earlier by paying. Silver Key: keep the call locked on Lydia's early lawsuit. The question asks whether the June 1 agreement blocks that suit, so the answer must say Yes if the agreement is supported by consideration.

Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer:
The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The issue is whether the debtor's promise gave consideration for the creditor's promise to forbear suit. The answer remains Yes because the extension agreement includes more than a bare promise to perform an existing duty: Daniel promised to pay interest through a later agreed payment date while Lydia promised not to sue before then.

Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 71 states the basic bargain rule: a return promise or performance is consideration when it is bargained for, and forbearance can be a bargained-for performance. Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 73 supplies the preexisting-duty boundary: performing a legal duty is not consideration unless the performance differs from the required duty in a meaningful way. The section 73 illustrations specifically recognize that extending a matured interest-bearing debt can be binding because the debtor gives up the ability to stop interest by paying earlier. Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 75 supports the promise-for-promise exchange frame, and section 82 is an adjacent doctrine for promises to pay antecedent debt; it does not contradict the credited answer because the call is whether the forbearance agreement is an effective defense to the creditor's premature suit.
