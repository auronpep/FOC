## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The credited answer is correct under the common-law classification of defeasible fees and future interests. A conveyance using durational language such as "for so long as," "while," or "until" creates a fee simple determinable. If the future interest after that determinable estate is retained by the grantor, the retained future interest is a possibility of reverter. A right of entry follows a fee simple subject to a condition subsequent and requires the grantor to act. A reversion follows a prior estate that is certain to terminate. A possibility of reverter follows an estate that might never terminate.

Authority floor: Restatement (First) of Property section 44 describes a fee simple determinable as an estate that expires automatically on a stated event; section 45 distinguishes the fee simple subject to a condition subsequent; section 154(3) defines a possibility of reverter; and section 374, including its commentary, treats grantor-retained interests as outside the Rule Against Perpetuities. Cornell Wex summarizes the same exam rule: a possibility of reverter is retained by the grantor of a fee simple determinable, and the property returns automatically if the condition occurs. See https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/possibility_of_a_reverter.
