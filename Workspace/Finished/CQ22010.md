## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Paul owned a small cedar vineyard in fee simple absolute. In a valid will, he devised the vineyard to "those of my children who survive me, to hold in equal shares, as joint tenants and not as tenants in common, with full right of survivorship." At the time Paul signed the will, his adult children, Ruth and Timothy, were his only living children.

A few months later, Timothy borrowed money from a credit union to launch a private Bible-memory tutoring business. As security for the note, Timothy signed a mortgage describing the cedar vineyard. Before repaying the loan, Timothy died in a climbing accident. The next year, Paul died, survived only by Ruth.

The jurisdiction has a statute providing that a mortgagee of real property holds equitable title to the mortgaged real property. Which statement best describes Ruth's interest in the vineyard after Paul's death?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

If a will devises land only to children who survive the testator, can a child who dies before the testator mortgage that land before the testator dies?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. Ruth is the sole owner of the vineyard, subject to the credit union's lien for the unpaid amount on Timothy's note.

C. Ruth holds the vineyard as a tenant in common with the credit union.

D. Ruth holds the vineyard as a joint tenant with the credit union.

### 5. Correct answer

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Paul's will did not transfer the vineyard when he signed it. It operated at Paul's death and gave the land only to children who survived Paul. Timothy died before Paul, so Timothy never received an interest in the vineyard. Gold Key: a will is not a present transfer; a survival-conditioned devise gives nothing to a person who does not survive the testator. Silver Key: before ranking joint tenancy, tenancy in common, and lien answers, ask whether the borrower ever owned any interest for the mortgage to attach to.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because it avoids making the credit union a co-owner and keeps the mortgage as a lien. The breaker is earlier than lien theory. Timothy had no present or later-acquired interest because the will gave only to children who survived Paul, so the credit union had no lien on Ruth's vineyard.

C is attractive because the statute says a mortgagee holds equitable title, and "tenant in common" sounds like the safer co-ownership label when survivorship is broken. The breaker is that title theory only matters if Timothy had mortgaged an interest he owned. He never owned any part of the vineyard.

D is attractive because Paul's will used joint-tenancy and survivorship words. The trap imports the credit union into the will's ownership structure. The credit union was not a surviving child, and Timothy had no interest to pass into a joint tenancy.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is unchanged: Ruth takes sole ownership free of the credit union's claimed mortgage because Timothy predeceased Paul and never received an interest under a devise limited to children who survive the testator. The tested issue is whether a purported mortgage can attach to property when the mortgagor had no present interest and never later acquired one under the will.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers section 1.2 states the baseline that a will transfers property at the testator's death, not when it is executed. Uniform Probate Code section 2-603 also treats a class member who failed to survive the testator as a deceased devisee for anti-lapse purposes, which confirms that survival is measured against the testator's death. Because the source gives no facts about substitute descendants and asks only about Timothy's attempted mortgage, the title-theory mortgage statute does not create an interest where Timothy had none. Title theory affects the mortgagee's interest in mortgaged property; it does not supply ownership to a mortgagor who never owned the land.

Current-law note: modern anti-lapse statutes can create substitute gifts for descendants of some deceased devisees. The source row gives no descendant facts and the answer choices do not present a substitute-descendant outcome, so that doctrine does not disturb the supplied key.
