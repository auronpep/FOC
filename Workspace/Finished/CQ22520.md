# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question (Chosen Variation)

Timothy hired Paul, a skilled woodworker, to build custom bookshelves for Timothy's home office. They entered into a valid written contract fixing the price at $3,000 and providing that the finished work would be "fully satisfactory to Timothy." Because Timothy wanted to give his son Daniel the $3,000 as a college graduation gift, a clause in the contract directed Paul to bill Daniel directly for the full amount upon completion. Paul finished building the bookshelves and sent Timothy a bill for $3,000. When Timothy inspected the work, he called Paul and complained that the shelves were uneven and not "satisfactory" as the contract required. Timothy said, "I'm not paying full price for this. I'll give you $2,400 and we'll call it even." Paul, who needed the money for materials on another job, agreed. Timothy paid Paul the $2,400 directly. Paul did not give any portion of the money to Daniel. Daniel first learned about the contract between Timothy and Paul after Paul had already received the $2,400 payment from Timothy.

Which of the following most accurately describes Daniel's rights?

A: Daniel is entitled to collect $2,400 from Paul.
B: Daniel is not entitled to collect anything from either party.
C: Daniel is entitled to collect $3,000 from Timothy.
D: Daniel is entitled to collect $3,000 from Timothy or $2,400 from Paul, but not both.

A contract directs payment to a third-party donee beneficiary. Before the beneficiary learns of the contract, the parties modify it — reducing the price and redirecting payment away from the beneficiary. The beneficiary discovers the contract only after the modification and payment. Can the beneficiary recover?

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**B is correct** because Daniel's rights as a donee beneficiary never vested. A donee beneficiary's rights vest when the beneficiary (1) learns of the contract, (2) materially relies on it, (3) assents to it in the manner requested, or (4) sues to enforce it. Here, Daniel did not learn of the contract until after Timothy and Paul had already modified it — reducing the price from $3,000 to $2,400 and redirecting payment from Daniel to Paul directly. Because Daniel's rights had not vested at the time of modification, Timothy and Paul were free to modify the contract without Daniel's consent and without incurring any liability to him. Daniel cannot enforce either the original or the modified terms.

**Gold Key (GK-CONTRACTS-DONEe-01):** A donee beneficiary's rights vest upon learning of the contract *or* upon material reliance — whichever comes first. Until vesting, the original parties can modify or cancel the benefit freely. The moment of modification relative to vesting is the entire ballgame.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (Daniel gets $2,400 from Paul) — DOMINANT TRAP.** This choice is attractive because it correctly identifies that Daniel is a donee beneficiary with potential enforcement rights, and it picks a real dollar amount from the modified contract. The trap: it ignores that the contract was modified *before* Daniel's rights vested. A student who knows "donee beneficiary can enforce" but forgets the vesting timing will gravitate to this choice because it names a concrete recovery. **Gold Key GK-CONTRACTS-DONEe-01** defeats this: until Daniel knew of the contract, his rights hadn't vested, and the parties were free to modify. The modified contract extinguished the original payment direction entirely.

**C (Daniel gets $3,000 from Timothy).** This choice is attractive because it identifies the correct original contract amount and the correct party (the promisor/homeowner). The trap: it treats the original $3,000 contract term as still enforceable, ignoring that the parties validly modified the contract before Daniel's rights vested. A student who spots that Daniel is a donee beneficiary but doesn't apply the vesting analysis will default to the original contract terms. **Gold Key GK-CONTRACTS-DONEe-01** defeats this: pre-vesting modification is effective; the original $3,000 term was extinguished before Daniel could enforce it.

**D (Daniel gets $3,000 from Timothy OR $2,400 from Paul, not both).** This choice is attractive because it offers two recovery paths, creating an illusion of careful analysis. The trap: it compounds the vesting error of A and C by giving the student two ways to be wrong. A student who recognizes Daniel has *some* rights but isn't sure which contract terms apply might pick this as the "safest" answer. **Gold Key GK-CONTRACTS-DONEe-01** defeats this: Daniel has no enforceable rights against either party because his rights never vested.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Rule:** Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§ 133, 142, 143.

An intended third-party beneficiary may enforce a contract only after the beneficiary's rights have vested. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 133 divides intended beneficiaries into donee beneficiaries (gift intent) and creditor beneficiaries (debt satisfaction intent). A donee beneficiary's rights vest when: (1) the beneficiary manifests assent to the promise in the manner requested by the parties; (2) the beneficiary materially changes position in justifiable reliance on the promise; or (3) the beneficiary brings suit to enforce the promise. *See also* Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 142 (vesting by assent or reliance). Some jurisdictions additionally hold that a donee beneficiary's rights vest upon learning of the contract. *See* LII/Wex, Third-Party Beneficiary, law.cornell.edu/wex/third-party_beneficiary.

Before vesting, the promisor and promisee retain full power to modify or discharge the contract without the beneficiary's consent and without liability to the beneficiary. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 143.

**Application:** Daniel is a donee beneficiary — Timothy directed payment to Daniel as a gift (college graduation), not to satisfy any debt Timothy owed Daniel. Daniel did not learn of the contract until after Timothy and Paul modified it. Under any vesting test (knowledge, reliance, assent, or suit), Daniel's rights had not vested at the time of modification. Therefore, Timothy and Paul validly modified the contract, and Daniel has no enforceable rights against either party.

**Authority:** Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§ 133, 142, 143. LII/Wex, Third-Party Beneficiary, https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/third-party_beneficiary.
