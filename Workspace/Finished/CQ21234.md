### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Miranda warnings are required before custodial interrogation. Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966), requires procedural warnings before statements from custodial interrogation may be used in the prosecution's case-in-chief. Berkemer v. McCarty, 468 U.S. 420 (1984), frames Miranda custody by asking how a reasonable person in the suspect's position would understand his situation. Thompson v. Keohane, 516 U.S. 99 (1995), states the ultimate custody inquiry as whether there was formal arrest or restraint on freedom of movement of the degree associated with formal arrest. Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318 (1994), confirms that custody turns on objective circumstances, not the officers' hidden subjective views.

Those authorities support the official key. Stephen was not merely at a stationhouse interview. Detectives removed his phone, locked the door, expressly said he was not leaving, and questioned him for two hours. Those objective restraints made the interview custodial. Because the detectives interrogated him and obtained an admission without Miranda warnings, the admission should be suppressed from the prosecution's case-in-chief. The transform preserves the same Miranda custody rule, the same issue, and the same credited outcome.
