## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

The deputies had a serious felony in motion: an armed robbery report, a matching fleeing suspect, and an uninterrupted chase into a dwelling. The Fourth Amendment normally treats the home as the highest-protection space, but it also recognizes exigent circumstances.

**Gold Key GK-CRIMINAL-HOT-PURSUIT-01:** Immediate, continuous hot pursuit of a fleeing felon is a warrant exception that allows officers to enter a dwelling to apprehend the suspect. Armed robbery is the kind of serious felony that keeps the emergency real.

**Silver Key SK-CRIMINAL-HOT-PURSUIT-CALL-01:** Match the call to the justification, not the property label. The question asks whether to suppress the pistol and cash, so the decisive lane is whether the warrantless entry was justified when the deputies crossed the threshold.

Choice A gives the correct justification and stays within the entry-for-capture lane. The motion to suppress should be denied.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice B - "Yes, because Peter kept a Fourth Amendment privacy claim in Hannah's rowhouse even though he did not own it."**

This choice sells the student on a real-looking privacy frame: home entry feels protected, and ownership is not always the whole Fourth Amendment story. The breaker is responsiveness. The motion turns on hot-pursuit exigency, not on using Hannah's property status as an automatic suppression ticket. **Silver Key SK-CRIMINAL-HOT-PURSUIT-CALL-01:** do not let a privacy-label answer replace the emergency-entry question.

**Choice C - "No, because Hannah consented to police entry when she let Peter run inside."**

This choice reaches the right suppression result, then attaches the wrong exception. Letting Peter run into the rowhouse is not voluntary consent to government entry. Consent must come from a person with authority and be directed to the officers. The correct reason is hot pursuit, not implied consent.

**Choice D - "Yes, because the Fourth Amendment permits no warrantless entry into a private home."**

This choice leans on the strongest baseline rule in the fact pattern: the home normally requires a warrant. The breaker is the absolute word "no." Warrantless home entry is sometimes permitted when exigent circumstances make delay unreasonable. **Gold Key GK-CRIMINAL-HOT-PURSUIT-01:** hot pursuit of a fleeing armed felon is one of those exceptions.

## 9. Full Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

Black-letter rule: Police may enter a dwelling without a warrant when they are in immediate, continuous hot pursuit of a fleeing felony suspect and the circumstances create an urgent need to prevent escape, protect safety, or preserve evidence. Warden v. Hayden, 387 U.S. 294 (1967), upheld warrantless entry and search where officers pursued a suspected armed robber into a house he had entered minutes earlier; the Court treated the armed-felon pursuit as an exigency. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/387/294/

Doctrinal currency check: Lange v. California, 594 U.S. ___ (2021), rejects a categorical hot-pursuit rule for misdemeanants, but it does not undermine the armed-felony pursuit rule tested here. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/594/20-18/

The legal issue and credited outcome are preserved: the question tests whether officers in uninterrupted pursuit of a fleeing armed-robbery suspect may enter a private residence without a warrant and avoid suppression of the weapon and cash. The answer remains no suppression because the entry was justified by exigent hot pursuit.

**PASS-1 rule and invariant layer:** The rule is the exigent-circumstances exception for immediate, continuous hot pursuit of a fleeing armed-felony suspect.
