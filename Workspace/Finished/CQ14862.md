### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Evidence, Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions, Psychotherapist-patient, and the verified outline code is `35030204`. In federal criminal court, privilege is governed by Federal Rule of Evidence 501, which directs federal courts to apply common-law privilege principles as interpreted in light of reason and experience. The Supreme Court in *Jaffee v. Redmond*, 518 U.S. 1 (1996), recognized a federal psychotherapist-patient privilege for confidential communications between a licensed psychotherapist and patient in the course of diagnosis or treatment, and extended that protection to licensed social workers providing psychotherapy. See FRE 501, https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title28%2Ftitle28a%2Fnode232%2Farticle5; *Jaffee v. Redmond*, https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/518/1/. The variant keeps the same legal issue and credited outcome: the defendant's statement to a nurse was not a confidential psychotherapy communication made for diagnosis or treatment, so the federal privilege does not bar the nurse's testimony.
