## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Esther owned a small brick workshop where she designed Bible-study maps for a private online store. Esther signed a promissory note for $275,000 to Cedar Bank, secured by a mortgage on the workshop. The note stated that Esther would have no personal liability for the mortgage debt.

Eight days later, Timothy Credit obtained a judgment against Esther for $64,000 and filed the judgment in the county where the workshop was located. At the time the judgment was filed, Timothy Credit had no actual notice of Cedar Bank's mortgage. Three weeks after that filing, Cedar Bank recorded its mortgage on Esther's workshop.

The recording act of the jurisdiction provides: "Unless the same be recorded according to law, no conveyance or mortgage of real property shall be good against subsequent purchasers for value and without notice or against judgment creditors without notice."

Timothy Credit sued to enforce its judgment lien against Esther's workshop. Cedar Bank intervened, contending that the judgment lien was a second lien on the workshop and that Cedar Bank's mortgage was a first lien.

Is Cedar Bank's contention correct?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Under a recording act that protects judgment creditors without notice, does an unrecorded mortgage beat a later judgment lien that was filed before the mortgage was recorded?

### 6. Full right-answer explanation

D is correct. The statute expressly protects judgment creditors without notice against unrecorded mortgages. Timothy Credit filed its judgment in the county land records before Cedar Bank recorded the mortgage, and Timothy Credit had no actual notice of that mortgage. Cedar Bank therefore cannot claim first priority merely because its mortgage was signed earlier. Silver Key: when the recording act names "judgment creditors without notice," do not default to first-in-time; line up the filing dates and the notice fact.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice is attractive because the note says Esther has no personal liability, which sounds like a serious defect in the debt. The breaker is that a nonrecourse note does not make the mortgage void. A mortgage can secure a debt against the land even when the borrower has no personal liability. The priority problem is recording and notice, not personal liability.

**B.** This is the dominant trap. It sells the ordinary first-in-time instinct: Cedar Bank's mortgage was signed before Timothy Credit filed its judgment. The breaker is the quoted recording act. Silver Key: the answer must honor the statute's protected class, and the statute protects judgment creditors without notice when the mortgage was not yet recorded.

**C.** This choice is attractive because "relation back" sounds like a technical recording rule that could rescue an earlier mortgage. The breaker is that recording gives constructive notice from recording, not retroactive notice from the date the note was signed. Cedar Bank recorded too late to defeat Timothy Credit's already-filed judgment lien.
