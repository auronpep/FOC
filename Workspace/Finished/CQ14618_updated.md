## ORIGINAL MBE QUESTION (DO NOT EDIT)

**Question:**
A defendant was charged with felony murder because of his involvement in a bank robbery. The evidence at trial disclosed that a friend invited the defendant to go for a ride in his new car, and after a while asked the defendant to drive. As the friend and the defendant drove around town, the friend explained to the defendant that he planned to rob the bank and that he needed the defendant to drive the getaway car. The defendant agreed to drive to the bank and to wait outside while the friend went in to rob it. As they approached the bank, the defendant began to regret his agreement to help with the robbery. Once there, the friend got out of the car. As the friend went out of sight inside the bank, the defendant drove away and went home. Inside the bank, the friend killed a bank guard who tried to prevent him from leaving with the money. The friend ran outside and, finding that his car and the defendant were gone, ran down an alley. He was apprehended a few blocks away. The defendant later turned himself in after hearing on the radio that the friend had killed the guard.

The jurisdiction has a death penalty that applies to felony murder.

**Consistent with the law and the Constitution, the jury may convict the defendant of:**

A. felony murder and impose the death penalty. 
B. felony murder but not impose the death penalty. 
C. bank robbery only.
D. no crime.

## RESEARCH & ANALYSIS

---

### Applied Legal Rules

1. **Felony Murder Rule Scope** – A defendant is liable for felony murder when a killing occurs during the commission or attempted commission of an inherently dangerous felony, regardless of malice aforethought, so long as the killing is a proximate outgrowth of the felony.

2. **Accomplice/Accessory Liability** – One who knowingly aids and abets a felony is liable for crimes committed by co-felon(s) that are foreseeable and in furtherance of the common plan.

3. **Constitutionally-Proportional Punishment Under the Eighth Amendment** – The death penalty cannot be imposed for felony murder unless the defendant either:
   - Actually killed,
   - Attempted to kill,
   - Intended killing to occur, OR
   - Was a **major participant** in the felony who acted with **reckless indifference to human life** (Enmund v. Florida, 458 U.S. 782 (1982); Tison v. Arizona, 481 U.S. 137 (1987); Arizona Judicial Branch, Enmund/Tison Requirement).


### Fact Pattern Mapping

| Element | Finding | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Underlying Felony:** Bank robbery | Established | Inherently dangerous felony |
| **Accomplice Behavior:** Defendant drove friend to bank, waited outside knowing the plan | Established | Knew of plan and knowingly facilitated by providing getaway logistics; this is foreseeable participation in furtherance |
| **Victim Homicide:** Friend killed bank guard during robbery | Established | Homicide occurred during and in furtherance of robbery |
| **Defendant Participation:** Present, aided, but **did not enter bank**, left before homicide, later turned himself in | Minor participant status | Left prior to killing; no evidence of major participation, no intent to facilitate killing, or reckless indifference |
| **Culpable Mental State of Defendant** | **None proven** | Defendant expressed regret; left before homicide occurred |

### Why the Rule Yields "Felony Murder Only" 

- The defendant’s presence, willingness to aid, and facilitation that enabled the robbery satisfy the felony murder *actus reus* requirement (assisting the intended felony).
- The killing was a probable and foreseeable consequence of a bank robbery, thus falling within the felony murder rule. Therefore, conviction for felony murder is constitutionally sound.
- **However**, the Eighth Amendment bars the death penalty in this scenario. The defendant was merely a getaway driver who demonstrated no intent to kill, no intent that a killing occur, no awareness of the specific use of deadly force planned, and renounced participation before the killing happened. Under **Enmund/Tison** standards, such a minor participant without reckless indifference to human life cannot face capital punishment for felony murder.

  1. Actually killed? ❌  
  2. Attempted to kill? ❌  
  3. Intended that a killing occur? ❌ (evidence shows regret and withdrawal)

Use the **"Distance" heuristic** when evaluating accomplice felony-murder death penalty issues:

- **Close:** Defendant is shooter, major planner, or active enabler of violence – death penalty in play.  
- **Far:** Defendant is peripheral facilitator who left early or repudiated – death penalty forbidden.
