## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** Stephen promised to deliver 240 etched wooden tiles by April 20 and tendered only 226. A short tender is not the tender promised by the contract. Under the UCC perfect tender rule, Ruth may reject the whole tender when the goods or delivery fail in any respect to conform to the contract.

Gold Key: under UCC Article 2, a buyer who rightfully rejects nonconforming goods may recover the price paid and still pursue buyer remedies for the seller's breach; rejection is not a remedy waiver. Silver Key: read the answer array on two axes: goods disposition and remedy scope. The answer that says "reject" still fails if it shrinks the remedy to refund only.

Because Ruth prepaid the full $432, rightful rejection lets her recover that payment. Because Stephen breached by tendering fewer goods than promised, Ruth may also recover legally available damages caused by the breach, such as cover, market, incidental, or consequential damages if proven.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### A. Acceptance-waives-damages trap - 9% predicted

**What makes it attractive.** This choice sounds like a clean election: if Ruth keeps the short delivery, she should be stuck with that bargain. It appeals to the idea that acceptance means forgiveness.

**The breaker.** The UCC does not treat acceptance of nonconforming goods as an automatic waiver of all breach remedies. If Ruth accepted nonconforming goods and gave the required notice, she could still pursue damages for the nonconformity. Gold Key: acceptance changes what happens to the goods, but it does not erase Article 2 remedies when notice is preserved.

### C. Duty-to-accept trap - 16% predicted

**What makes it attractive.** This choice treats 226 out of 240 tiles as close enough and tries to solve the shortage with damages after forced acceptance. That feels efficient because the event is the next day.

**The breaker.** Perfect tender is stricter than substantial performance for this single-delivery goods sale. Ruth is not required to accept a tender that comes up short on the contracted quantity.

### D. Refund-only rejection trap - 29% predicted

**What makes it attractive.** This is the dominant trap because it gives Ruth the correct power to reject and gives back the money she paid. It feels complete because a refund looks like it restores the starting point.

**The breaker.** The refund is not the outer limit of Ruth's remedy. Gold Key: UCC section 2-711 lets a rightfully rejecting buyer recover the price paid and also pursue buyer damages. ## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer

The transformed item preserves the source's legal issue, tested rule, credited outcome, and answer-array structure. The credited outcome is unchanged: the buyer may reject the short tender and sue for both return of the advance payment and damages resulting from the seller's breach.

The governing rule is UCC Article 2's perfect tender rule. UCC section 2-601 provides that, subject to exceptions not triggered here, if the goods or tender fail in any respect to conform to the contract, the buyer may reject the whole, accept the whole, or accept commercial units and reject the rest. Stephen's tender of 226 tiles did not conform to a contract for 240 tiles, so Ruth may reject the tender.

UCC section 2-711 provides that when a buyer rightfully rejects, the buyer may cancel, recover so much of the price as has been paid, and also use buyer damages remedies such as cover under UCC section 2-712 or non-delivery damages under UCC section 2-713. UCC sections 2-607 and 2-714 also confirm the opposite side of the array: acceptance of nonconforming goods does not itself wipe out all Article 2 remedies if the buyer gives the required notice. The official key is consistent with these authorities.
