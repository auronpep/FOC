## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** A facially neutral law is not insulated from Equal Protection review when the record supports discriminatory purpose. Here, the ordinance does not name a protected class, but the town departed from its normal mailed-notice process, ignored the planning director's recommendation, and council members made direct statements that Haitian families "do not belong here" before voting. Those facts support the argument that discriminatory purpose motivated the ordinance. Once that purpose is shown, ordinary land-use deference no longer controls and heightened scrutiny can apply.

> **Gold Key:** Facial neutrality is not a safe harbor. A challenger can prove discriminatory purpose with surrounding evidence such as official statements, irregular sequence, and departures from normal procedure.

> **Silver Key:** Do not stop at the text of the ordinance. When the stem gives procedure plus official statements, compare the "neutral text" answer against the "purpose evidence" answer.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is attractive** because students may think Equal Protection protection turns on whether every person burdened by the law fits the same protected class. The breaker is that discriminatory-purpose analysis does not require every affected person at Cedar Gate to share the same trait; the question is whether forbidden purpose was a motivating factor in the government action.

**B is attractive** because zoning and land-use decisions often receive deferential rational-basis review, and "local land use" sounds like the classic low-scrutiny category. The breaker is the word "always." Discriminatory purpose can move a facially neutral land-use law out of ordinary rational-basis treatment.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Rule statement:** Under the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment, a facially neutral law is not unconstitutional merely because it has a disproportionate impact. But discriminatory purpose can be proven through direct and circumstantial evidence, including the impact of the decision, the historical background, the sequence of events, departures from normal procedures, substantive departures, and contemporary statements by decisionmakers. If discriminatory purpose involving a suspect classification such as race or national origin is shown, heightened scrutiny may apply.

**Authority:** U.S. Const. amend. XIV. In **Washington v. Davis**, 426 U.S. 229 (1976), the Court held that disproportionate impact alone does not establish an Equal Protection violation without discriminatory purpose. In **Village of Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp.**, 429 U.S. 252 (1977), the Court identified the evidentiary sources used to infer discriminatory purpose, including impact, historical background, sequence of events, procedural or substantive departures, and contemporary statements. Cornell's Constitution Annotated likewise summarizes that a facially neutral classification can be subject to heightened scrutiny when it is a pretext for discrimination on a forbidden basis. Sources checked: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/426/229/ ; https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/429/252/ ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution-conan/amendment-14/section-1/facially-neutral-laws-implicating-a-racial-minority .

**Application to this item:** The credited answer (C / original B) is correct. The ordinance is facially neutral, but the record includes direct anti-Haitian statements by voting council members plus procedural irregularities and rejection of planning-staff advice. Those facts are exactly the kind of purpose evidence Arlington Heights makes relevant.

**Verification of invariance:** The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: facial neutrality does not bar an Equal Protection claim when discriminatory purpose can be inferred from statements and departures from normal procedure.
