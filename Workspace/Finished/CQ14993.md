### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**A is correct.** The phrase "so long as it is used for tutoring classes" creates a fee simple determinable in the academy. That estate ends automatically when the academy stops using Cedar Lot for tutoring classes. Martha retained a possibility of reverter. When Martha died, the probate statute made that descendible property interest devisable, and her will gave all of her estate to Ruth. So when the tutoring use stopped, the future interest that Martha had devised to Ruth became possessory.

**Gold Key:** A "so long as" deed is not just a purpose statement; it creates an automatically ending estate. **Gold Key:** once the stem tells you a retained interest is descendible and therefore devisable, the will beats the heir. **Silver Key:** run the title chain in order: limited estate, automatic ending, then who owns the retained interest.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

**Tested rule and issue:** Durational deed language such as "so long as" creates a fee simple determinable in the grantee and leaves a possibility of reverter in the grantor. When the stated use ceases, the grantee's estate terminates automatically and the retained future interest becomes possessory in its holder. If the retained future interest is devisable, it passes by will rather than by intestacy.

**Authority floor:** In *Mahrenholz v. County Board of School Trustees of Lawrence County*, 417 N.E.2d 138 (Ill. App. Ct. 1981), the court treated school-purpose limitation language as creating a fee simple determinable followed by a possibility of reverter, and explained that a holder of a possibility of reverter becomes owner by operation of law when the condition is broken. The opinion cites Restatement (First) of Property secs. 44, 45, and 153 for the defeasible-fee framework. Cornell Wex likewise describes a possibility of reverter as the grantor's retained future interest after a fee simple determinable and says the property returns automatically when the condition occurs: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/possibility_of_a_reverter. The supplied probate statute controls the devise point in this item; as a modern primary-law analog, Ga. Code Ann. sec. 44-5-40 provides that future interests or estates are descendible, devisable, and alienable: https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/2021/title-44/chapter-5/article-2/section-44-5-40/.

**Application:** Martha's deed gave the academy a fee simple determinable. Martha retained the possibility of reverter. Martha died before the use stopped, but the statute made any descendible property interest devisable. Because Martha's will left all her estate to Ruth, Ruth held the possibility of reverter. When the academy stopped using the lot for tutoring classes, the academy's estate ended automatically and Ruth's interest became present title. Barnabas took nothing by quitclaim because the academy had no title left to convey, and Timothy took nothing because the will displaced intestacy.
