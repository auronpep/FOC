### Correct-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

The Fourteenth Amendment's Equal Protection Clause demands that similarly situated persons be treated alike, and classifications based on sex trigger intermediate scrutiny — but only when the classification is genuinely sex-based. Under *Geduldig v. Aiello*, 417 U.S. 484 (1974), a classification tied to pregnancy is not automatically a sex-based classification for constitutional Equal Protection purposes. The state program here draws a line between pregnancy-related disabilities and all other disabilities. The plaintiff has brought solely an Equal Protection claim and offers no evidence that the legislature acted with a sex-stereotyping purpose. Without proof that the classification is a pretext for sex discrimination, or a statutory hook (like Title VII / the Pregnancy Discrimination Act), the court applies rational-basis review, not intermediate scrutiny. The claim does not automatically trigger heightened scrutiny — **C is correct**.

**Gold Key:** On the MBE, a pregnancy classification ≠ automatic sex classification under the constitutional Equal Protection Clause (*Geduldig v. Aiello*). Congress can statutorily treat it as sex discrimination (Pregnancy Discrimination Act, Title VII), and proof of sex-stereotyping purpose can change the constitutional analysis — but the bare classification, without more, is reviewed rationally.

### Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A was attractive because** a student may believe Equal Protection obligates the government to treat every condition identically — a "universality" instinct. **The breaker:** Equal Protection requires only that the state have a rational basis for treating groups differently; it never mandates that every benefit program cover every condition. A legislature may permissibly prioritize some disabilities over others for fiscal or administrative reasons.

**B was attractive because** pregnancy is obviously linked to sex, and students are trained to associate sex classifications with intermediate scrutiny — so the inference "pregnancy = sex = intermediate scrutiny" feels automatic. **The breaker (Gold Key):** Under *Geduldig v. Aiello*, the Court held that a pregnancy-based classification is not for that reason alone a sex-based classification under the Equal Protection Clause. Only proportionality/C-level students will have the *Geduldig* Gold Key; without it, this is the trap.

**D was attractive because** bodily autonomy is a real constitutional value (substantive due process / privacy), and students may conflate the strict-scrutiny framework applicable to fundamental-rights Due Process claims with the Equal Protection framework. **The breaker:** Strict scrutiny applies to Equal Protection sex classifications — but only when the classification is actually sex-based. Pregnancy status alone does not trigger it, and the stem offers no foundation for a fundamental-rights claim.

### Black-Letter Legal Verification and Right-Answer Legal Reasoning

**Governed by:** U.S. Const. amend. XIV, § 1 (Equal Protection Clause).

**Controlling authority:** *Geduldig v. Aiello*, 417 U.S. 484 (1974). California's State Disability Insurance program excluded pregnancy-related disabilities from coverage. The Supreme Court held, 6-3, that this exclusion did not constitute sex discrimination under the Equal Protection Clause:

> "While the first group [pregnant women] is exclusively female, the second [nonpregnant persons] includes members of both sexes... There is no risk from which men are protected and women are not, and no risk from which women are protected and men are not... It does not follow that every legislative classification concerning pregnancy is a sex-based classification." 417 U.S. at 496–97 n.20.

The Court applied rational-basis review and sustained the program. *See also General Electric Co. v. Gilbert*, 429 U.S. 125 (1976) (reaching the same conclusion under Title VII's statutory framework, later superseded by the Pregnancy Discrimination Act of 1978, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e(k), which **statutorily** treats pregnancy discrimination as sex discrimination — but that is a Title VII point, not a constitutional one).

**Credible supplementary framing:** The Pregnancy Discrimination Act (PDA), 42 U.S.C. § 2000e(k), amended Title VII to define sex discrimination to include pregnancy. But the PDA is a **statutory** protection. On an MBE question that says "brings only a federal Equal Protection claim," the PDA does not apply — it is a Title VII statute, not a Fourteenth Amendment rule. *Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs*, 538 U.S. 721 (2003), relied on sex-stereotyping evidence in upholding the FMLA's family-care provision under Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment — proof of legislative purpose matters in the stereotyping analysis, and the question says no such proof was offered.

**Confirmed:** The tested rule (pregnancy ≠ automatic sex classification under constitutional EP), the credited outcome (no automatic intermediate scrutiny), and the legal issue are all unchanged from the original.

**Verdict: PASS.** The residual (a pregnancy classification alone does not automatically trigger intermediate scrutiny under the Equal Protection Clause) is true, responsive, and faithful to *Geduldig*.
