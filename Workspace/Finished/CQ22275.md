## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**C is correct.** The warrant named one thing: the courthouse weathervane. The officers found it immediately in the showroom, which exhausted the warrant's purpose. Once the object of a warrant is located, the authority to keep searching is spent — so opening the springhouse afterward was a search the warrant no longer authorized, and the carriage lamps it produced must be suppressed. **Gold Key:** a search warrant's authority ends when officers find the specific item it names; continued searching is unwarranted, and what it yields is excluded. **Gold Key:** a "premises" warrant *does* reach outbuildings on the property — so the springhouse's being a separate structure neither saves the search nor dooms it; the only thing that decides the case is that the search continued past its purpose. **Silver Key:** when two choices argue about *where* the cache was (separate shed vs. on the property), location is a decoy — the premises warrant already covered the springhouse; the deciding axis is *when* the search happened.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. No, because the lamps were relevant to show that Stephen knew his shop contained stolen property.** This pairs a true observation — the lamps really do tend to prove guilty knowledge — with the comfortable instinct that relevant evidence comes in. The breaker: a suppression motion tests *how* evidence was obtained, not whether it is probative; relevance cannot cure an unlawful search.

**B. The breaker: the warrant's authority had already ended when the officers found the weathervane, so the springhouse search was no longer authorized no matter where the springhouse sat. **Gold Key:** authority terminates once the named item is found.

**D. Yes, because the lamps were in a separate springhouse.** This trades on the feeling that a freestanding outbuilding sits outside a warrant aimed at the shop, making separateness look like grounds to suppress. The breaker: a "premises" warrant reaches outbuildings on the property, so the springhouse's separateness is not why the lamps are excluded — the search's continuation past its purpose is. **Gold Key:** a premises warrant covers outbuildings.

## 9. Black-letter verification + affirmative legal reasoning (lawyer-confirmation layer)

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are UNCHANGED from QID 22275. Governing authority:

- **Scope and duration of a warrant search:** *Horton v. California*, 496 U.S. 128, 140 (1990) — a search must be "confined in area and duration by the terms of the warrant"; if the search exceeds what the warrant's terms permit, the subsequent seizure is unconstitutional. Once the described object is found, continued searching is no longer within the warrant's terms.
- **Particularity / no general search:** *Marron v. United States*, 275 U.S. 192, 196 (1927) — the particularity requirement prevents general, exploratory searches; the warrant's described objects bound what officers may do.
- **Premises includes outbuildings (kills D, baits B):** a warrant to search a "premises" extends to structures and outbuildings within the curtilage of the described property (2 Wayne R. LaFave, *Search and Seizure* § 4.10 (treatise support)). This is secondary support for the place-scope point only; the dispositive tested rule rests on *Horton*'s duration limit.

Here the officers were lawfully present executing a valid premises warrant, immediately found the single named object (the weathervane), and only then searched an outbuilding — exceeding the warrant's duration. Credited answer = grant the motion / suppress. No authority contradicts the official key. Doctrinal-currency check: warrant-scope/duration is current and not on the hotlist. **No NEEDS_HUMAN trigger.**
