### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

The book club's strongest constitutional defense is that **the Equal Protection Clause does not reach purely private discrimination without state action**. The Fourteenth Amendment's Equal Protection Clause is, by its terms, a constraint on government action — it prohibits any State from denying to any person the equal protection of the laws. The doctrine of state action requires that the discriminatory conduct be attributable to the State, either because the State itself engaged in the discrimination (e.g., a state-run club) or because a recognized state-action theory applies (e.g., a private actor performing a traditional public function, acting jointly with the State, or operating under a statutory or contractual right granted by the State that makes the discrimination state action). The Civil Rights Cases, 109 U.S. 3 (1883), establish that the Equal Protection Clause does not reach purely private discrimination; other constitutional provisions (e.g., Thirteenth Amendment for racial discrimination) and federal civil-rights statutes (e.g., 42 U.S.C. § 1981 for racial discrimination in contractual relations) may reach some private conduct, but the Equal Protection claim itself fails at the state-action threshold.
