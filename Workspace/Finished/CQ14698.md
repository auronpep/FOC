## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged: the facts prove attempted robbery, not completed robbery and not assault. Under the Model Penal Code attempt formulation, attempt liability requires culpability for the target crime and conduct constituting a substantial step strongly corroborative of the criminal purpose. ([Penn Carey Law][1]) Robbery, at common law, requires the unlawful taking of another’s property by force or threat of force with intent to permanently deprive. ([Legal Information Institute][2]) Assault requires an intentional act placing another in reasonable apprehension of imminent harmful or offensive contact; federal criminal-law guidance likewise frames apprehension assault around reasonable apprehension of immediate force. ([Legal Information Institute][3])

Paul’s conduct satisfies attempted robbery because he intended to take the cashbox by threat and moved into execution by reaching for the latch. It does not satisfy completed robbery because he took nothing. The official row’s assault rule is preserved: the only threat signal is verbal, so the assault add-on does not change the conviction answer.
