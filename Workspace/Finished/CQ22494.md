### 1. Final Question

Daniel owned a mobile espresso trailer called Daily Bread Coffee that he used for weekend Christian retreats. When Daniel's employer transferred him from Phoenix to the Pacific Northwest, the employer promised to reimburse his relocation expenses, including any brokerage commission he might owe for selling the trailer.

Daniel contacted Lydia, a business-equipment broker, and signed a written agreement with her on October 1. The agreement stated that if the trailer was sold to any buyer who made an offer during the following eight weeks, Daniel would pay Lydia, upon transfer of title, a commission equal to 6 percent of the actual selling price. In return, Lydia promised to use reasonable efforts to find a buyer at a price of $120,000.

On October 18, after Lydia showed the trailer to Peter, Peter offered to buy it for $111,000 on condition that title would transfer on or before January 15. On October 20, Daniel accepted Peter's offer. On October 21, Peter paid Daniel a $14,000 deposit.

What can Lydia collect if she institutes a claim against Daniel for her commission?

### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**B is correct.** Lydia cannot collect a commission because the written agreement made Daniel's duty to pay conditional. **Gold Key:** When a commission promise says payment is due only upon a specified sale or transfer of title, producing an accepted offer is not enough. The stated condition must occur unless it is excused.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested issue is unchanged from the source item: whether a broker can recover a commission when the contract expressly conditions payment on a qualifying sale and closing or title transfer, and the buyer produced by the broker backs out before that event occurs. The credited outcome is unchanged: the broker recovers nothing.

Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 224 defines a condition as an event that must occur before contractual performance becomes due unless the nonoccurrence is excused. Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 225 provides the effect: a duty subject to a condition does not become due unless the condition occurs or is excused. See the H2O Open Casebooks reproductions of Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 224 and section 225:

- https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/11720-bruckner-howard-law-contracts-2024/resources/11.1.1-restatement-second-of-contracts-224/
- https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/11720-bruckner-howard-law-contracts-2024/resources/11.1.2-restatement-second-of-contracts-225/

Those rules support the official key. Daniel's duty to pay Lydia was not triggered merely because Lydia found Peter or because Daniel accepted Peter's offer. The parties expressly chose narrower payment conditions: a sale to a buyer who made an offer during the window and payment upon transfer of title. Peter's purchase failed before title transferred. Ruth's later contract used the target price but did not satisfy the offer-window buyer condition. No fact shows Daniel prevented the condition from occurring or that the condition was excused.
