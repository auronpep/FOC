## 1. Final question — stem + call

Stephen had been arraigned on a charge of stealing sheep from the fold of a shepherd, Hannah. The day before Stephen's trial, he was told that he was to stand in a lineup. Stephen asked for his lawyer to be present at the lineup but was told that he had no right to his lawyer's presence. When he refused to take part, the officers said, "It will go hard for you if you refuse." Stephen then stood in the lineup. Five other men stood with him, all of approximately the same height, build, and complexion as Stephen. ## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

D is correct. The objection is to an identification produced by a lineup held after Stephen had been arraigned — after formal charges. **Gold Key:** once adversary judicial proceedings have begun, the accused has a Sixth Amendment right to counsel at a corporeal (in-person) lineup, and a lineup conducted after counsel is requested and denied is constitutionally infirm. Stephen asked for his lawyer and was refused, so the lineup identification cannot come in. **Gold Key:** nor does Hannah's courtroom identification cure it — a later in-court identification survives only if it rests on a source independent of the tainted lineup, and here her testimony identifies Stephen only as "the man she had identified at the lineup," a pure repetition with no independent basis. The objection is sustained. D states the dispositive ground — the denial of counsel — and is the only choice that does.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

- **C (dominant trap).** C is the sophisticated lure: it invokes a real rule — a tainted lineup can be cleansed when the in-court identification has an independent source — and the courtroom identification feels like a fresh, second look that washes the first one out. **Gold Key:** but the independent-source exception requires exactly that, an independent source, and Hannah's in-court identification is nothing more than a repetition of the lineup pick (she names Stephen as "the man she identified at the lineup"). A repeat of the tainted identification is not purged of the taint; it carries it into the courtroom.
- **B.** B is attractive because a lineup stacked with five look-alikes is precisely what a fair, non-suggestive lineup is supposed to be, so it seems to answer the objection. Lineup fairness goes to suggestiveness — a different question entirely; even a flawlessly composed lineup is unconstitutional if it was conducted after charges without the counsel the accused requested. The choice answers the suggestiveness question while the dispositive defect is the denial of counsel.
- **A.** A is tempting because the officers did lean on Stephen — "it will go hard for you" — and coercion feels like a reason to throw the identification out. Being compelled to stand in a lineup is not a ground to exclude the identification; a lineup is non-testimonial, an accused may be required to appear, and no one must resist a violation to preserve it — the suggestion that failing to resist waives the right is simply not the law. The choice reaches the right result on a rule that does not exist.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

Tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are **unchanged** from the source item: the Sixth Amendment right to counsel at a post-charge corporeal lineup; denial of counsel renders the lineup identification inadmissible; an in-court identification is admissible only on an independent source, and a mere repetition of the lineup identification is not purged; the objection is **sustained**.

Affirmative legal reasoning: after the initiation of adversary judicial proceedings (here, arraignment), a lineup is a critical stage at which the accused is entitled to the presence of counsel; conducting the lineup after counsel was requested and refused violates that right, and the resulting identification is excluded. A subsequent in-court identification may be received only if the prosecution shows an origin independent of the illegal lineup; where, as here, the witness's courtroom identification merely repeats the lineup pick, no independent source exists and the taint is not purged.

Lawyer-confirmation authority (not part of the student path): U.S. Const. amend. VI; *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218 (1967) (right to counsel at post-indictment lineup; in-court identification admissible only on an independent source); *Gilbert v. California*, 388 U.S. 263 (1967) (per se exclusion of the lineup identification taken without counsel); *Kirby v. Illinois*, 406 U.S. 682 (1972) (right attaches only after the initiation of adversary judicial proceedings). Research confirms rather than contradicts the official key, so the verdict is PASS (no NEEDS_HUMAN). The matter is not on the doctrinal-currency hotlist; the Wade/Gilbert/Kirby framing remains the controlling test.
