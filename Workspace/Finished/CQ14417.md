### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Ruth accepted a permanent full-time job, but the stem gives no fixed duration, no just-cause limit, no separate promise to stay for a minimum period, and no special term limiting resignation. Gold Key: a permanent job with no definite duration or termination limit is ordinarily employment at will. Either side may end the relationship at any time. Silver Key: do not grade Ruth's reason for leaving until you know whether the job contract created a duty to stay. Here it did not, so Barnabas is not likely to prevail for breach.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice is attractive because the second job really is different: tour logistics manager is not the same work as puzzle writer. The breaker is call focus. Barnabas is not suing Ruth for failing to mitigate damages after a breach. The first question is whether Ruth had a contractual duty to report and stay at all, and the at-will answer defeats that duty.

**B.** This choice sounds businesslike because salary matching feels fair when a worker leaves for more money. The breaker is the invented term. Gold Key: an ordinary permanent employment relationship does not imply a right of first refusal for the first employer to match another offer.

**D.** This is the dominant trap. It turns the word "permanent" into a reasonable-time promise, which feels like a compromise between lifetime employment and instant quitting. The breaker is the Gold Key: without a definite term or a termination limit, "permanent" ordinarily points to at-will employment, not a reasonable-time duty.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row: the original employer is not likely to prevail because the job was described as permanent but had no fixed duration or other termination limit.

The governing rule is the common-law employment-at-will default. The American Law Institute's Restatement of the Law, Employment Law lists section 2.01 as the "Default Rule of an At-Will Employment Relationship" and section 2.03 for agreements with definite or indefinite terms. [ALI][1] A current legal synopsis of the Restatement describes the default as allowing either party to terminate the employment relationship with or without cause unless statute, public policy, or contract limits that right. [Judicature][2] NCSL's employment-at-will overview likewise states that an employee is free to leave a job at any time for any or no reason, and that a contract can modify the default by providing a specific term or cause-only limit. [NCSL][3]

Older common-law permanent-employment cases state the MBE default even more directly: absent additional duration terms or separate consideration, a contract for permanent employment is treated as an indefinite hiring terminable at will. [Open Casebook][4] Modern authorities recognize that some indefinite employment promises may be enforceable when facts show a contractual limit on termination or reliance-based agreement, but this stem supplies no such facts. Ruth merely accepted a permanent job and then promptly declined to report. Under the at-will default, Barnabas has no breach claim for that departure.

**Student move:** Ask whether any fact limits either party's power to end the relationship.  
**Tiny rule:** No definite duration or just-cause limit usually means at will.  
**Trap:** reasonable-time permanence  
**Confidence:** ANCHOR_ASSISTED

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-CONTRACTS-ATWILL-PERMANENT-01:** A permanent job with no definite duration or just-cause limit is ordinarily at will; either side may end the relationship at any time.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-CONTRACTS-ATWILL-CALL-FOCUS-01:** In an employment-at-will question, decide whether the job created a duration duty before grading the employee's reason for leaving.
