### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam register)

B is correct. In a criminal jury trial, the jury must make the guilty-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt finding. A judge may grant judgment for a defendant when the evidence is legally insufficient, but the judge may not direct a guilty verdict for the prosecution, even when the evidence appears overwhelming.

Gold Key: In a criminal jury trial, the judge may not direct a guilty verdict for the prosecution. The jury itself must find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.

Silver Key: When answer choices pair an appellate outcome with a reason, do not stop at "reverse" or "affirm." Pick the reason that identifies the trial actor who took the jury's job.

Here, the judge told the jury to return a guilty verdict after the prosecution rested. That act denied Paul's constitutional right to have the jury decide guilt. The late objection does not make the directed guilty verdict valid. The appellate court should reverse.

### 9. Full Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

Governing rule: The Sixth Amendment gives a criminal defendant the right to trial by jury in serious criminal prosecutions, and the jury must make the ultimate guilty-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt determination. The Supreme Court's doctrine treats the reasonable-doubt requirement and jury verdict requirement as interrelated. A judge may direct an acquittal when evidence is legally insufficient, but may not direct a guilty verdict for the prosecution.
