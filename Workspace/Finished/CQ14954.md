## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** Timothy's Porchworks is seeking to repossess the inserts, not the fixed frames. The facts separate the two: the inserts are designed to lift out by hand, without tools, for seasonal changes and cleaning. That makes the removable inserts personal property rather than fixtures that passed into Mercy Bank's mortgage collateral.

**Gold Key:** do not merge the removable insert into the frame that holds it. A seasonal insert designed to come out by hand stays in the personal-property lane unless the facts make that insert itself part of the realty.

**Silver Key:** split the object being repossessed from the object attached to the house. The bank's stronger argument reaches the fixed frame; Timothy's Porchworks is after the removable inserts.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**C.** The attraction is the clean recording-priority story: Mercy Bank's mortgage existed and was recorded before Timothy's Porchworks filed. The breaker is the Gold Key. **D.** This is the dominant trap. The attraction is the ordinary-house intuition: panels and screens feel like parts of the home, and the bank's mortgage seems to cover the home. The breaker is the answer's overclaim - "regardless of their design." The design is the point: these inserts lift out by hand, so the choice ignores the fact that controls the fixture question.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

Black-letter verification: UCC 9-102(a)(41) defines fixtures as goods that have become so related to particular real property that an interest in them arises under real-property law. UCC 9-334 governs priority of security interests in fixtures, including purchase-money security interests and readily removable goods. UCC 9-604 allows a secured party with priority in fixtures, after default, to remove collateral from real property, subject to reimbursement for physical injury caused by removal. See [UCC 9-102](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-102), [UCC 9-334](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-334), and [UCC 9-604](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-604).

The right answer is even cleaner because the item asks about removable inserts rather than fixed frames. The frames may plausibly be fixtures, but the inserts are designed to be removed without tools for seasonal use and cleaning. The security interest attached to those inserts, and the mortgagee cannot block repossession simply by pointing to the earlier recorded mortgage. The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: the secured seller prevails because the items sought are removable inserts, not fixtures absorbed into the realty.
