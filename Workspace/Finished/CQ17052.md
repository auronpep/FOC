1. Final question — the revised question stem + call.

Three friends—Daniel, Aaron, and Peter—agreed to rob a small-town pharmacy. All three carried loaded, fully automatic rifles into the store. To frighten people, Aaron fired roughly thirty rounds into a tin ceiling above the sales counter. A bullet ricocheted and struck a customer, killing them. The jurisdiction does not apply felony-murder. What is the most serious crime for which Daniel and Peter (who did not fire) should be convicted?

A. Depraved-indifference murder

7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register).

Depraved-indifference murder requires a mental state showing an extreme indifference to human life — reckless conduct so severe it amounts to implied malice. When multiple participants bring and use extremely dangerous weapons in a shared criminal venture, accomplice liability can attach if the non-shooters shared or adopted the actor's reckless indifference. Here, the defendants jointly armed themselves with fully automatic rifles and entered a confined space with a tin ceiling, creating a high risk of ricochet. Aaron's act of firing into the ceiling manifests depraved indifference; Daniel and Peter are accomplices because they participated in and encouraged the enterprise that created the risk. Therefore, depraved-indifference murder is the most serious offense for the non-shooters.

8. Full wrong-answer explanations — one per distractor.

B (Conspiracy): Attractive because the defendants agreed to commit robbery; however, conspiracy is an inchoate agreement offense and is less serious than a homicide conviction. The question asks for the most severe crime for which the non-shooters may be convicted, and conspiracy does not account for accomplice liability to the killing.

C (Involuntary manslaughter): Attractive to students who focus on reckless negligence causing death; but involuntary manslaughter typically addresses criminal negligence or unlawful act manslaughter, not the extreme recklessness constituting depraved-indifference murder. The participants' concerted use of fully automatic weapons in a confined space supports a higher mental-state finding.

D (Voluntary manslaughter): Attractive when a killing results from sudden passion or adequate provocation. No provocation or heat-of-passion facts are present; the shooting was deliberate and reckless without mitigating provocation.

9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer.

Rule: Depraved-indifference murder is established where a defendant's conduct manifests extreme indifference to human life (MPC § 210.2(1)(b); common-law variants). Accomplice liability applies when a person aids or abets another in the commission of a crime with the requisite mental state (Model Penal Code § 2.06; common-law aider-and-abettor doctrines). Applying these principles, accomplices who share the reckless indifference necessary for depraved-indifference murder may be held liable for the homicide even absent direct participation in the lethal act. Legal authority: Model Penal Code § 210.2 cmt.; see cases recognizing depraved-heart murder where multiple participants bring dangerous weapons into a crime and a co-defendant's extreme recklessness causes death (authority note: jurisdictional variations exist; attorney review recommended for specific case law citations).
