### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Dr. Timothy is a treating psychiatrist offering a specialized opinion about Martha's mental condition at the time of the fall. That opinion would help the trier of fact decide whether the insurer has proved intentional self-injury. Gold Key: the ultimate-issue rule does not automatically bar helpful expert testimony in civil cases; the mental-state bar is the criminal-defendant exception, not a civil-insurance rule. Silver Key: keep the call tied to this civil policy dispute before letting the mental-state language pull you into the neighboring criminal exception.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because the insurer's defense turns on Martha's mental state, so the answer sounds like it protects the jury from being told how to decide the case. The breaker is that the specific mental-state bar in FRE 704(b) applies in criminal cases to a defendant's mental state for a charged crime or defense, not to a civil insurance dispute. Gold Key: criminal-defendant mental state is the narrow exception; civil ultimate-issue help remains admissible if Rule 702 is satisfied.

B is attractive because expert testimony often has foundations, and a basis objection sounds procedural and cautious. The breaker is FRE 705: unless the court orders otherwise, an expert may state the opinion and reasons before first laying out the underlying facts or data, with the basis available on cross-examination.

D is attractive because mental-state testimony can feel like a backdoor endorsement of Martha's story. The breaker is that Dr. Timothy is not saying Martha is truthful on the stand; he is giving a psychiatric opinion about her condition at the time of the fall, which bears on intent.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged. FRE 702 permits a qualified expert to testify in opinion form if the proponent shows, more likely than not, that the specialized knowledge will help the trier of fact, the opinion rests on sufficient facts or data, and the opinion reflects reliable principles and application. FRE 704(a) provides that an opinion is not objectionable just because it embraces an ultimate issue. FRE 704(b) creates a specific criminal-case exception for an expert opinion about whether a defendant did or did not have the mental state constituting an element of the crime charged or a defense. FRE 705 allows the expert to state an opinion and reasons without first testifying to the underlying facts or data unless the court orders otherwise.

This is a civil insurance case, not a criminal prosecution. Dr. Timothy's opinion is not offered to decide Martha's credibility and is not barred merely because it bears on the ultimate fact of intentional self-injury. The current expert-testimony rule is also accounted for: after the December 1, 2023 amendment to FRE 702, the proponent must make the admissibility showing by a more-likely-than-not standard, and the source facts say the witness is a treating psychiatrist giving a helpful mental-state opinion.
