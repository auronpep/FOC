### 1. Final question

Several years ago, Olive & Ink Press, a Bible-bookbinding company, planned to run a buried fiber-optic conduit from its print shop to a Scripture-gift storage barn several miles away. To do that, the company negotiated for rights to place and maintain the conduit across several privately owned parcels. In return for a cash payment, Martha, then the owner of a lavender farm, executed a right-of-way deed allowing Olive & Ink Press to install and maintain the underground conduit across the farm. The right-of-way deed was properly recorded.

The farm later passed through several recorded intermediate conveyances. None of those deeds mentioned the right of way. About 15 years after the right-of-way deed was recorded, Peter acquired the farm. Two years later, Peter agreed in a written contract to sell the farm to Ruth, and the contract required Peter to furnish Ruth with an abstract of title. Peter hired Cedar Abstract, a reputable abstract company, to prepare the abstract. Cedar Abstract prepared the abstract and delivered it to Peter, but the abstract omitted any mention of Olive & Ink Press's recorded right-of-way deed. Peter delivered the abstract to Ruth.

After reviewing the abstract, Ruth paid the full purchase price. Peter conveyed the farm to Ruth by a deed containing covenants of general warranty and against encumbrances. At the closing, Peter, Ruth, and Cedar Abstract were all unaware of Olive & Ink Press's right-of-way deed. Nearly a year later, Olive & Ink Press notified Ruth that it planned to begin installing the underground conduit on its right of way across the farm. Ruth then asserted a claim against Cedar Abstract for damages caused by the existence of the right of way.

### 6. Full right-answer explanation

Ruth wins against Cedar Abstract. Peter hired Cedar Abstract to prepare the abstract Peter was contractually required to furnish to Ruth before closing. Silver Key: Keep the defendant locked. The claim is against the abstract company, so the winning reason must connect Cedar Abstract's contract or duty to Ruth.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. Cedar Abstract, because the conduit right-of-way deed was outside the chain of title.**  
This choice sells a chain-of-title shortcut: later deeds did not mention the right of way, so the abstract company supposedly did not have to find it. The breaker is that the right-of-way deed was properly recorded while Martha owned the farm and before the later conveyances, so the omission cannot be excused as an outside-the-chain instrument.

**C. Ruth, because Peter's deed to her contained a covenant against encumbrances.**  
This choice is attractive because a recorded right of way is an encumbrance, and Peter's deed covenant gives Ruth a real title-covenant route against Peter. The breaker is the call: Ruth asserted a claim against Cedar Abstract, and Peter's deed covenant does not create Cedar Abstract's liability. Silver Key: a true covenant point loses when it answers the wrong party.

**D. Cedar Abstract, because it did not know about the conduit right-of-way deed.**  
This choice sells the common fault instinct: no awareness, no liability. The breaker is that an abstractor's job is to find and report recorded title matters with reasonable care, and the buyer's enforcement theory does not depend on Cedar Abstract's actual knowledge. Gold Key: the intended-user contract route makes the abstractor's promised title work enforceable by the buyer.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source: a purchaser may recover against an abstract company when the seller hired the abstract company to furnish the title abstract required for the purchaser's closing, the abstract omitted a properly recorded interest, and the purchaser was the intended beneficiary of that abstracting contract.

- Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 302 treats a beneficiary as intended when recognizing a right to performance is appropriate to effectuate the parties' intent and the circumstances show the promisee intended to give the beneficiary the benefit of the promised performance. Source: https://advance.lexis.com/open/document/openwebdocview/-302-Intended-and-Incidental-Beneficiaries/
- Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 304 provides that a promise creates a duty to an intended beneficiary, who may enforce that duty. Source: https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/2nd%2BSemester/LAW%2B506-002%2B%E2%80%93%2BContracts%2BII/R2C%2B%C2%A7%2B304

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/supreme-court/1984/63136-0.html

The recorded right-of-way deed was not a wild or outside-chain instrument in the exam sense. It was granted by the then-owner of the land and recorded before the later conveyances. Peter's covenant against encumbrances may give Ruth a separate claim against Peter, but it does not answer why Ruth may recover from Cedar Abstract.
