## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam-register)

Yes. Duress is available as an affirmative defense when the defendant committed a nonhomicide offense because of an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm from a human coercer, directed at the defendant or a close family member, and the defendant had no reasonable legal alternative. Here, Naomi was threatened with the imminent death of her son Daniel — a close family member. The threat came from a human agent (the masked man), was immediate (thirty-second deadline with no time to alert police), and left no reasonable legal alternative (locked office, no escape, no time). Embezzlement is a nonhomicide financial crime; duress is not categorically barred for such offenses. All elements are satisfied.

**Gold Key:** Duress can be based on an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm to the defendant *or a close family member* — the threat does not need to be directed at the defendant personally.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing rule:** Duress is an affirmative defense at common law and under the Model Penal Code (MPC § 2.09). The standard MBE formulation requires: (1) an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm, (2) made by a human agent, (3) directed at the defendant or a close family member, (4) that a reasonable person in the defendant's position would have been unable to resist, and (5) the defendant had no reasonable legal alternative. Duress is not a defense to intentional homicide (common law and MPC), but it IS available for all other offenses, including nonhomicide financial crimes like embezzlement.

**Authority:**
- MPC § 2.09(1): "It is an affirmative defense that the actor engaged in the conduct charged to constitute an offense because he was coerced to do so by the use of, or a threat to use, unlawful force against his person or the person of another, that a person of reasonable firmness in his situation would have been unable to resist."
- MPC § 2.09(2): "The defense provided by this Section is not available if the actor recklessly placed himself in a situation in which he would be subjected to such coercion... [or] if he was negligent in placing himself in such a situation."
- Common law rule: Duress is not a defense to intentional murder. *See, e.g.*, Commonwealth v. Vasquez, 456 Mass. 350 (2010) (duress not a defense to intentional homicide but may be considered for guilt reduction).
- MBE rule: Duress may be based on an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm to the defendant or a close family member, if no reasonable legal alternative exists. (Source row lb_rule_reference.)
