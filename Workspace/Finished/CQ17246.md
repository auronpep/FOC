Daniel has a severe psychotic disorder. During an episode at a gospel-music fair, he takes a boxed set of brass handbells from Ruth's Christian music booth without paying. ## 6. Right-Answer Explanation (exam register)

**Gold Key: MPC substantial-capacity insanity** - The MPC/ALI test does not require the defendant to have zero abstract knowledge that a crime is illegal. It asks whether, because of mental disease or defect, the defendant lacked substantial capacity either to appreciate the criminality or wrongfulness of the conduct or to conform conduct to law.

**Silver Key: named-test clash** - When the stem names the MPC/ALI test, do not let a choice quietly import a stricter knowing-right-from-wrong shortcut. Lock the named test, then ask whether the facts meet that test's capacity language.

Daniel's expert testimony ties his severe psychotic disorder to a lack of substantial capacity to appreciate the criminality of taking the handbells. That is the MPC/ALI cognitive prong. His ability to recite an abstract rule against shoplifting does not automatically defeat the defense, because the test is substantial appreciation in the charged conduct, not perfect legal ignorance. The claim may therefore succeed.

## 7. Wrong-Answer Explanations

### B - Automatic success because any psychotic disorder satisfies the MPC test

**What makes it attractive:** The student sees a serious diagnosis and assumes the legal defense follows from the label. The choice feels sympathetic and uses the right general doctrine.

**The breaker:** **Gold Key: MPC substantial-capacity insanity** - A mental disease or defect is necessary, but it is not enough by itself. The disorder must cause a lack of substantial capacity to appreciate criminality or conform conduct to law. This choice skips the causal-capacity link and turns a required gateway into an automatic result. (Filter: NOT_TRUE - half_truth.)

### C - Failure because planned conduct can never qualify under the MPC test

**What makes it attractive:** The student treats purposeful movement as proof of legal responsibility. The choice sounds disciplined because Daniel left with a specific item and a definite purpose.

**The breaker:** Planning is not an automatic bar under the MPC/ALI insanity test. A person can act with a visible purpose while still lacking substantial capacity to appreciate criminality because of mental disease or defect. The choice's "can never" language overclaims the rule and ignores the named test. (Filter: NOT_TRUE - tiered_absolute.)

### D - Failure because Daniel had abstract awareness that shoplifting is illegal

**What makes it attractive:** This is the dominant trap. It imports the familiar right-from-wrong instinct: if Daniel can say shoplifting is illegal, the defense must fail.

**The breaker:** **Silver Key: named-test clash** - The stem uses the MPC/ALI test, not a strict zero-knowledge shortcut. Under that test, abstract awareness that theft is illegal does not end the analysis if the disorder left Daniel without substantial capacity to appreciate the criminality of this conduct. The answer fights the wrong test. (Filter: NOT_RESPONSIVE - bait_doctrine.)

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Tested rule:** Under the MPC/ALI insanity test, a defendant is not responsible if, at the time of the conduct and as a result of mental disease or defect, the defendant lacks substantial capacity either to appreciate the criminality or wrongfulness of the conduct or to conform conduct to the requirements of law. The test requires a causal link between the disease or defect and the lack of substantial capacity. It does not require complete ignorance that the conduct is illegal.

**Authority:** Model Penal Code § 4.01 is the governing rule number. The U.S. Department of Justice's archived Criminal Resource Manual quotes MPC § 4.01 as requiring lack of substantial capacity to appreciate criminality or conform to law and lists federal cases that used ALI/MPC variants before the federal statutory standard changed: https://www.justice.gov/archives/jm/criminal-resource-manual-636-insanity-prior-law. Harvard's H2O Open Casebook reproduction of Model Penal Code § 4.01 states the same rule text: https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/981-balloberman-crim-law-casebook/resources/10.5.5-model-penal-code-sec-401/. The American Law Institute also notes that Kahler v. Kansas discussed state insanity-defense variation and cited MPC § 4.01; that confirms the rule's identity without changing the MBE-style jurisdictional premise here: https://www.ali.org/news/articles/us-supreme-court-cites-model-penal-code-2020.
