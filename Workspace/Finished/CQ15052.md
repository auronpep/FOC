### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** The call asks what will determine who receives the sale proceeds. The answer is the governing land-sale rule in the jurisdiction. **Gold Key:** after a binding land-sale contract with no unmet contingency, jurisdictions that apply equitable conversion treat the buyer as the equitable owner of the land and the seller as owning the right to the purchase money. **Silver Key:** when the call asks what determines the proceeds, choose the rule-selector answer before chasing the will paragraph, closing date, or probate mechanics.

If the jurisdiction recognizes equitable conversion, Lydia’s remaining interest at death is treated as personalty: the purchase money. That makes the residue side of the estate the relevant destination. If the jurisdiction does not recognize it, the allocation can change. So the determinant is whether the jurisdiction recognizes equitable conversion.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** The probate-court-order answer sounds official because an estate representative may need authority to complete a deed. That is the wrong frame. The call is not asking who had authority to sign the deed; it is asking what determines who receives the money after the sale. **Gold Key:** equitable conversion, if adopted, decides whether Lydia’s interest was land or purchase money at death.

**C.** The closing-date answer is attractive because title formally changes at closing. That timing detail is not the determinant. Under the Gold Key, the operative shift can occur at the binding land-sale contract, before deed delivery. The relevant question is not whether the scheduled closing came before or after death; it is whether the jurisdiction uses the conversion rule.

**D.** The will-wording answer is the dominant trap. It pulls the student into comparing a specific land gift with a general “all my real property” gift. **Silver Key:** do not start with the will text when the call asks what determines the proceeds. First decide whether the seller’s interest was still land or had been converted into purchase money. **Gold Key:** if equitable conversion applies, the real-property gift does not control the sale proceeds because the seller’s remaining interest is treated as personalty.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are preserved. The governing doctrine is equitable conversion in a land-sale contract. A Maryland appellate decision states that when a vendor and vendee contract for a land sale, equity treats the purchaser as owner of the land and the vendor as owner of the purchase money; because the conversion occurs when the land-sale contract is made, a seller who dies before execution leaves legal title passing as real estate but the purchase-money interest as personal property. ([Justia Law][1]) A New Hampshire decision applies the same rule: an executory land-sale contract ordinarily converts the vendor’s interest into personal property and the vendee’s into real property; if the seller dies before execution, legal title passes as real estate but the purchase-money interest passes as personal property. ([Justia Law][2]) The Ninth Circuit likewise states that if the seller dies while the contract remains in force, the seller’s interest passes to the personal representative as personal property, not to heirs as realty. ([Justia Law][3])
