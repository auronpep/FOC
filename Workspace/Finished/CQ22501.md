## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

On March 2, Lydia, who operated a private Christian bookstore, ordered from Barnabas Supply 1,000 wooden journal stands for a spring Bible-study display. The order required each stand to be 12 inches wide, with delivery by March 14.

Barnabas Supply delivered the stands on March 14. Peter, Lydia's store manager, received them and told Lydia the same day that the stands had arrived but were only 10 inches wide. Lydia intended to notify Barnabas Supply immediately, but she was preparing for a large weekend event and forgot to do so.

On April 18, Lydia received Barnabas Supply's invoice for the stands, but she did not pay the invoice or communicate with Barnabas Supply in any way. On July 10, Barnabas Supply filed a claim against Lydia for the price of the stands. Lydia defended on the ground that the stands did not conform to the contract.

Is Barnabas Supply entitled to the price of the stands?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Does a merchant buyer who discovered a goods defect at delivery but never seasonably notified the seller have to pay the contract price when the seller sues months later?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. No, because the stands did not conform to the contract of sale.

B. Yes, because a merchant buyer who accepts nonconforming goods is always bound to pay for them at the contract price.

C. No, because a merchant seller is not entitled to the price of nonconforming goods if a reasonable inspection before shipment would have disclosed the nonconformity.

D. Yes, because Lydia failed to inform Barnabas Supply that the stands were only 10 inches wide.

### 5. Correct answer

D (original key A).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

D is correct. Lydia learned on the delivery date that the stands were not the promised width, but she did not seasonably notify Barnabas Supply. Gold Key: under the UCC, a buyer who accepts goods must pay the contract rate, and a buyer who accepts nonconforming goods must give timely notice of breach or lose remedies for the nonconformity. Silver Key: separate two questions: did the buyer accept, and did the buyer preserve a nonconformity remedy? Lydia loses both.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because the stands were truly nonconforming. The breaker is notice: nonconformity alone is not a defense after the buyer accepts the goods and then waits months without notifying the seller.

B is attractive because accepted goods do trigger a duty to pay the contract rate. The breaker is the word "always." A buyer who accepts nonconforming goods may still preserve remedies or a setoff by giving timely breach notice; Lydia loses because she gave no timely notice.

C is attractive because the seller may have been able to catch the width problem before shipment. The breaker is that the UCC does not make the seller's pre-shipment inspection failure a price bar after the buyer accepts and fails to notify.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is unchanged: Barnabas Supply is entitled to the price. UCC section 2-606 provides that acceptance occurs when a buyer, after a reasonable opportunity to inspect, fails to make an effective rejection. UCC section 2-602 makes rejection ineffective unless the buyer seasonably notifies the seller within a reasonable time after delivery or tender. UCC section 2-607 requires a buyer to pay the contract rate for accepted goods and requires a buyer who accepted a tender to notify the seller of breach within a reasonable time after discovering it or be barred from any remedy. Lydia discovered the short stands on the day of delivery, kept silent through the invoice, and raised the defect only after suit months later. That silence made any rejection ineffective, left the goods accepted, and barred her nonconformity remedy.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: UCC sections 2-602, 2-606, and 2-607 state the operative rules: timely seller notice is required for rejection, failure to make an effective rejection after inspection is acceptance, accepted goods must be paid for at the contract rate, and failure to give breach notice after acceptance bars buyer remedies.
