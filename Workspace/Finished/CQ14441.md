**Gold Key:** One generous prior payment does not by itself establish a controlling course of dealing.

8. **Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer**

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. When an express contract term conflicts with course-of-performance evidence, course-of-dealing evidence, or usage of trade, the express term receives greater weight and controls. Trade usage may help explain or supplement an agreement, but it does not override a clear inconsistent express term. A single generous prior payment also does not create a course of dealing strong enough to displace the written percentage in the renewed contract.

Authority floor: Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 203(b) states the hierarchy of interpretive preferences, with express terms receiving greater weight than course of performance, course of dealing, and usage of trade. Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§ 222 and 223 define usage of trade and course of dealing. As a parallel codified hierarchy, UCC § 1-303(e) provides that express terms prevail over course of performance, course of dealing, and usage of trade when they cannot reasonably be read consistently; UCC § 1-303(b) defines course of dealing as a sequence of previous conduct, and § 1-303(c) defines usage of trade. Research sources consulted: https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/11720-bruckner-howard-law-contracts-2024/resources/6.4-restatement-second-of-contracts-203/ and https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/1/1-303.

Applied here, Naomi and Paul signed a renewed services agreement that incorporated the 18% term. The 12% custom is inconsistent with that express term, and the 23% prior payment is one prior act rather than a controlling course of dealing. No researched authority contradicts the supplied key. No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is implicated.
