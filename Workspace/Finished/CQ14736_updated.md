## Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. Paul forced Hannah to keep driving under threat of a pistol, and the movement continued for several miles in the neighboring MPC state before the robbery and flight. Gold Key: under Model Penal Code section 212.1, kidnapping can be built on unlawful removal for a substantial distance when done to facilitate a felony or flight; it is not limited to the first state where the threat began. Silver Key: separate the travel from the later taking. The question is not asking where the episode started; it asks whether the movement inside the charging state is enough.

## Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because robbery often includes some restraint, and short restraint that merely helps the taking can be too small for kidnapping. The breaker is the distance and sequence: Paul forced Hannah to drive several miles across and inside the neighboring state before the remote stop, so this is more than a momentary hold at the robbery spot. Gold Key: substantial-distance movement to facilitate a felony or flight can satisfy MPC kidnapping.

B is attractive because the gun first appeared in Hannah's home state, so the student may freeze the crime at the first threat. The charge is in the neighboring state, and the facts still show forced movement there; the MPC movement element can be satisfied by the travel that occurred after the state line.

D is attractive because ransom is a real kidnapping purpose, and Paul did take money before releasing Hannah from his control. The answer uses the wrong lane. The wallet and cash were robbery proceeds, not the price of release, and the conviction does not depend on ransom because the substantial movement facilitated the robbery and flight.

## Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

Model Penal Code section 212.1 defines kidnapping to include unlawfully removing another from a place of residence or business, or a substantial distance from the vicinity where the person is found, or unlawfully confining another for a substantial period in a place of isolation, with listed purposes including facilitating commission of a felony or flight thereafter. The section also treats force, threat, or deception as making the removal or confinement unlawful. Cornell Wex summarizes the same MPC formulation at https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/kidnapping, and Criminal Law Web reproduces the section text at https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/212_1.htm.

The credited outcome is unchanged. The original item and this variation both test whether forced, gunpoint movement for several miles in the MPC charging state, followed by robbery and flight, is enough for kidnapping despite a distractor arguing the restraint was incidental to robbery. It is enough.
