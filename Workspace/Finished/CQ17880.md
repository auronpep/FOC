### 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The common-law rule is that necessity is not a defense to murder when the defendant intentionally kills an innocent person to save others. The leading common-law authority is Regina v. Dudley & Stephens, 14 Q.B.D. 273 (1884), commonly stated as rejecting necessity as a justification for homicide except self-defense. Source used: University of Minnesota Law Library, "Regina v. Dudley & Stephens," https://lawlibrarycollections.umn.edu/classic-cases-criminal-regina-v-dudley-stephens. The MPC is a different modern framework; MPC Section 3.02 uses a choice-of-evils formulation, but this item expressly asks for the common-law rule. Source used for the MPC contrast: Model Penal Code Section 3.02 text hosted at https://individual.utoronto.ca/dubber/web/website/genjust/Model_Penal_Code.htm.

Mary deliberately takes an act that she knows will certainly kill Stephen. Under common-law murder doctrine, that satisfies intent to kill. Necessity fails because the common law does not permit the deliberate sacrifice of an innocent person as a lesser evil. The credited answer remains B.
