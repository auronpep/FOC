## 1. Final question
In a federal bench trial in a diversity contract action, Ruth, the owner of Loaves of Hope—a Christian bakery that donates a percentage of its proceeds to support an orphanage—has just finished presenting all her evidence on liability in her breach-of-contract claim. Ruth contends that Peter, the owner of a regional trucking company, breached their flour-supply agreement by delivering a late and damaged shipment, causing her to lose a major order for a large church conference. After Ruth rests on the liability phase, Peter stands and tells the judge, “Your Honor, I move for JMOL because the plaintiff has failed to prove an essential element of her claim.”

What is the correct procedural mechanism?

## 2. Distilled Core Question
In a bench (nonjury) trial, after the plaintiff has been fully heard on an issue during her case-in-chief and the defendant claims she failed to prove an essential element, what is the correct motion the court may use to enter judgment against the plaintiff on that issue?

## 6. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)
The correct procedural mechanism is Rule 52(c) judgment on partial findings.  

In a bench (nonjury) trial, once a party has been fully heard on an issue, the court may enter judgment against that party on any claim or defense that can be maintained or defeated only with a favorable finding on the issue. Ruth has finished presenting her evidence on liability. Peter’s request therefore falls squarely under Rule 52(c).  

**Silver Key (navigation move):** Read the call for the precise trial posture (“bench trial”) and the exact timing (“plaintiff finishes presenting evidence on liability”). Match that posture to the rule whose text expressly uses “nonjury trial” and “after a party has been fully heard.” Everything else is eliminated by visible mismatch with the call.

## 7. Full wrong-answer explanations
A: Attractive because the case is already at trial and the movant is asking the judge to end the case on liability. Breaker: Rule 56 summary judgment is a pretrial device; once trial has begun, summary judgment is no longer available. The posture here is mid-trial after the close of a party’s case-in-chief on an issue.

B: Attractive because the plaintiff has “rested” and the defendant is asking for judgment. Breaker: Rule 55 default judgment is for failure to plead or otherwise defend; it has nothing to do with failure of proof at trial after evidence has been presented.

D (dominant trap): Attractive because “JMOL” is the familiar label students learn for mid-trial judgment motions. Breaker: Rule 50 judgment as a matter of law applies only in jury trials. The parallel mechanism for a bench trial is Rule 52(c) judgment on partial findings. The call explicitly states this is a bench trial.

## 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The credited answer is correct under the governing rule.  

FRCP 52(c) provides: “If a party has been fully heard on an issue during a nonjury trial and the court finds against the party on that issue, the court may enter judgment against the party on a claim or defense that, under the controlling law, can be maintained or defeated only with a favorable finding on that issue.”  

This is the exact posture: bench trial, plaintiff has finished presenting evidence on liability (i.e., has been fully heard on the issue), defendant claims failure to prove an essential element.  

By contrast, FRCP 50(a) is limited to jury trials: “If a party has been fully heard on an issue during a jury trial and the court finds that a reasonable jury would not have a legally sufficient evidentiary basis to find for the party on that issue…”  

Rule 56 governs summary judgment before trial. Rule 55 governs default for failure to appear or plead.  

The tested rule, legal issue (correct mid-trial motion label in bench trial after plaintiff rests on an issue), and credited outcome are identical to the source question. Authority: FRCP 52(c) (current text post-1991 amendment that aligned the nonjury mechanism with the jury-trial Rule 50 framework). No doctrinal-currency issue on the hotlist applies.
