### 7. Full right-answer explanation
D is correct. The deed gives Ruth a continuing nonpossessory use right in Daniel's land, but the benefit is not tied to Ruth's ownership or occupancy of a separate parcel. Gold Key: a no-dominant-parcel easement is not destroyed; it is classified as an easement in gross when it benefits the holder personally or commercially. ### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because recording sounds like it should make the easement a land-linked right. The breaker is that recording does not force the benefit to attach to a dominant parcel; it just gives notice of the recorded interest.

B is attractive because the stem loudly says Ruth owns no neighboring parcel, so the student may treat the missing parcel as a defect. Gold Key: the missing dominant parcel is the clue for an easement in gross, not a reason to downgrade the deeded right into a revocable license.

C is attractive because "profit" sounds like the ordinary word for money earned from the workshops. Gold Key: a property-law profit is about the right to enter and remove resources or substances from land, not about earning money from a display case.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Rights in Land, Non-Possessory Rights and Interests in Land > Easements > Nature and Type. The source row supplied `81020701`, which maps to zoning and nonconforming use; the actual tested rule maps to the verified outline code `81020101`.

An easement is a nonpossessory right to use land in another's possession. Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes Section 1.2 supplies that baseline definition, and Section 1.5 distinguishes appurtenant servitude benefits from benefits held in gross. A benefit is appurtenant when tied to ownership or occupancy of a parcel; it is in gross when it is not tied to a parcel. Section 2.6 also recognizes that a servitude benefit may be created in gross. The recorded deed here grants a continuing right to place and maintain structures on Daniel's land, and Ruth owns no benefited parcel, so the benefit is in gross. The right is not a license merely because there is no neighboring parcel, and it is not a profit because Ruth receives no right to take timber, minerals, crops, game, or other substances from Daniel's land.

Research check: the American Law Institute identifies the Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes as covering creation, benefits, burdens, enforcement, and related servitude doctrine: https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-third/property-servitudes. Courts and uniform-law comments confirm the same classification frame: M.P.M. Builders quotes Section 1.2's easement definition, Stowell v. Andrews quotes Section 1.5's appurtenant/in-gross distinction, Laverty applies Section 1.2 to profits, and the Uniform Easement Relocation Act comments distinguish licenses from easements. Sources: https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/supreme-court/volumes/442/442mass87.html; https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/5ba084f198a6200c7d9ded4e; https://law.justia.com/cases/alaska/supreme-court/2000/s-8951-1.html; https://webservices.ncleg.gov/ViewDocSiteFile/75842.
