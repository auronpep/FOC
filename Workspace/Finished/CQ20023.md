## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam-Register)

**D is correct.** Receipt of stolen property requires (1) receiving stolen property, (2) with knowledge it is stolen, (3) with intent to permanently deprive the true owner. ## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** Receipt of stolen property requires that the defendant gain dominion and control over the stolen property with knowledge of its stolen nature. A fleeting, momentary handling of an object without exercising dominion over it does not constitute receipt.

**Authority:**

- *Price v. State*, 1913 OK CR 118, 131 P. 1102, 9 Okl. Cr. 359 — receipt can be established by the exercise of dominion and control over the property regardless of whether the defendant has manual possession.
- *Hunsucker v. State*, 1970 OK CR 140, ¶ 17, 475 P.2d 618, 621 — same principle applied.
- *Palmer v. State*, 1951 OK CR 26, 228 P.2d 391, 93 Okl. Cr. 357 — receipt is a separate and independent crime from larceny; a thief cannot receive from himself.
- 18 U.S.C. § 2315 — federal statute covering receipt, possession, concealment, and storage of stolen goods, confirming that the offense extends to those who exercise control over stolen property even without having stolen it themselves.
- W. LaFave & A. Scott, *Criminal Law* § 93 (1972) — receipt requires gaining possession or dominion; momentary handling is insufficient.
