### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The verified issue is procedural due process under the Fourteenth Amendment, not incorporation of the Bill of Rights. The source row listed `44040201`, but the deepest matching outline node in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md` is `44040203` for Individual Rights > Due Process Clause > Procedural due process.

The governing constitutional provision is the Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause. Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564, 573-74 (1972), recognizes that when the government makes charges that seriously damage a person's good name or imposes a stigma or disability that forecloses employment opportunities, due process may require an opportunity to refute the charge. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/408/564

Paul v. Davis, 424 U.S. 693 (1976), supplies the reputation-alone limit: defamation by a state official, without more, is not itself a deprivation of liberty or property under the Due Process Clause. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/424/693/

Codd v. Velger, 429 U.S. 624, 627-28 (1977), confirms that where the required elements of stigmatization are present, the due process remedy is an opportunity to refute the charge and clear one's name, and the worker's denial of the charge matters. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/429/624

The Constitution Annotated summarizes the same stigma-plus line: Paul v. Davis distinguished cases where official stigma also deprived the person of a right or status previously recognized by state law. Source: https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt14-S1-5-2/ALDE_00013748/

Those authorities confirm the supplied official key. Esther was publicly accused of theft from patients, the accusation was reported to the licensing board, the board consequence barred her from paramedic work until cleared, and she denied the accusation. The likely constitutional requirement is a meaningful name-clearing opportunity.
