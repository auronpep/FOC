# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

**The Galilee Guesthouse Retreat Weekend**

Daniel, a resident of State B, booked a paid weekend calligraphy retreat at the Galilee Guesthouse, a privately owned retreat lodge incorporated and located in State A. After the lodge allegedly failed to provide the reserved room package, Daniel sued the lodge for breach of contract in a State B court. The lodge had no office, bank account, employee, or regular business operation in State B. It did, however, rent a highway billboard in State B, and its website promoted "easy retreat travel from State B," gave driving directions from several State B cities, highlighted that the lodge was just across the State B border, and accepted reservations and credit card payments from guests nationwide, including State B residents.

Is the Galilee Guesthouse subject to personal jurisdiction in State B?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

An out-of-state lodge has no office, bank account, employee, or regular operations in the forum, but it advertises there, gives forum-specific travel directions, promotes its proximity to the forum, and takes online reservations and payments from forum residents. Can the forum exercise personal jurisdiction over a contract claim from a forum resident's booking?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** A forum can exercise specific personal jurisdiction when the defendant purposefully directs business activity toward the forum and the claim arises out of or relates to those forum-directed contacts, so long as jurisdiction is fair. The Galilee Guesthouse did more than operate a passive website. It rented State B advertising, promoted travel from State B, gave directions from State B cities, highlighted its proximity to State B, and accepted online reservations and payments from State B residents. Daniel's contract claim arises from the same reservation business the lodge solicited from State B. Those facts support minimum contacts.

**Gold Key:** Specific jurisdiction does not require an office, employee, or bank account in the forum. The exam asks whether the defendant purposefully reached into the forum for this kind of business and whether the claim is tied to that outreach.

**Silver Key:** Do not isolate the billboard. In a personal-jurisdiction array, compare the whole contacts bundle against the single-contact and no-physical-office answers.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (was D) - "No, because the reserved retreat weekend and alleged breach occurred at the lodge in State A."**
This sells an event-location move: the retreat happened in State A, so State A sounds like the only court with power. The breaker is that specific jurisdiction focuses on the defendant's forum-directed contacts and their relation to the claim; the place of the breach does not erase the lodge's deliberate State B solicitation.

**C (was C) - "No, because the lodge had no office, bank account, employee, or regular business operation in State B."**
This is the dominant trap. It lists facts that would matter strongly to general jurisdiction or ordinary physical presence, but it leaves out the contacts that actually matter here: targeted State B advertising, State B travel directions, proximity marketing, and online reservations from State B residents. The Gold Key kills this trap: specific jurisdiction can rest on purposeful forum solicitation tied to the claim even without offices or employees in the forum.

**D (was B) - "Yes, because the lodge placed a highway billboard in State B."**
This sells the right side of the answer with too little proof. A billboard is a real contact, but the answer isolates one fact and treats it as automatically sufficient. The Silver Key steers away from it: the better "Yes" answer relies on the whole contacts bundle, not the billboard alone.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The governing rule is the Due Process minimum-contacts test. `International Shoe Co. v. Washington`, 326 U.S. 310 (1945), allows personal jurisdiction when the defendant has forum contacts such that suit does not offend fair play and substantial justice. `Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz`, 471 U.S. 462 (1985), confirms that specific jurisdiction can rest on defendant-created, purposeful forum-directed activity and that physical entry into the forum is not required. `Walden v. Fiore`, 571 U.S. 277 (2014), warns that plaintiff contacts alone are not enough; the defendant's own conduct must connect it to the forum. `Ford Motor Co. v. Montana Eighth Judicial District Court`, 592 U.S. 351 (2021), confirms the claim must arise out of or relate to the defendant's forum contacts.

The source row's hotel fact pattern also matches the logic of `Snowney v. Harrah's Entertainment, Inc.`, 35 Cal. 4th 1054, 112 P.3d 28 (Cal. 2005), where targeted hotel advertising, reservation channels, and California-directed solicitation supported specific jurisdiction for hotel-related claims. The transformation preserves that rule: the lodge's State B billboard, State B directions, State B proximity pitch, and interactive reservation-payment system show purposeful availment, and Daniel's contract claim relates to those contacts. No current doctrinal update contradicts the supplied official key.
