### 9. Black-Letter Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Rule:** The unauthorized practice of law (UPL) generally consists of exercising legal discretion or giving legal advice on behalf of another person. However, the preparation of standard, pre-approved legal documents — where the non-attorney merely fills in factual information on routine forms — does not constitute the practice of law, provided no legal judgment or advice is rendered.

**Authority:** This is a well-established principle in UPL jurisprudence. The Florida Bar v. Brumbaugh, 355 So. 2d 1186 (Fla. 1978), is a leading case holding that a non-lawyer may sell legal forms and complete them with information provided by the customer without engaging in UPL, so long as no legal advice is given. Similarly, Sperry v. Florida, 373 U.S. 379 (1963), recognized that routine document preparation in regulated contexts (there, patent practice before the USPTO) does not necessarily constitute the practice of law. The general principle across jurisdictions is that filling in blanks on standard mortgage forms — names, property descriptions, loan amounts, interest rates — is an administrative/clerical function, not a legal one.

**Application:** The bank employee is preparing standard mortgage documents for Peter and Ruth's home purchase. The employee is not selecting legal provisions, advising the couple on their rights, or customizing terms. The employee is completing routine paperwork in the regular course of the bank's lending business. This is not the practice of law. The bank may also charge a reasonable fee for this service.
