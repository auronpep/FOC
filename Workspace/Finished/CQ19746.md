## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Naomi brings a portable worship-music mixer to a private Christian makers' market and tells Timothy that it was stolen from a delivery van. Timothy trades her a set of used camera lenses for the mixer because he wants it for his own home Bible-study recordings. There is no evidence that Timothy agreed to help Naomi steal more equipment, resell stolen equipment, find other buyers, or hide her stolen-property operation.

Prosecutors charge Naomi and Timothy with conspiracy to traffic stolen property. What is Timothy's strongest argument against conspiracy liability?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

If a person knowingly takes stolen property in a one-time exchange for personal use, without agreeing to help the seller's broader stolen-property venture, what defeats conspiracy liability?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. The exchange shows only a knowing one-time buyer-seller transaction, not an agreement that Timothy join or further Naomi's stolen-property venture.

B. Timothy is guilty of conspiracy only because the item was worship equipment.

C. Timothy cannot be guilty of any crime because he was not the person who stole the mixer.

D. Knowingly trading for stolen property automatically proves a conspiracy with the seller.

### 5. Correct answer

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. Conspiracy requires an agreement to pursue or help a criminal objective. Timothy may have knowingly taken stolen property, but the facts show only a one-time exchange for his own use. They do not show that he agreed to join Naomi's stolen-property venture, help her steal more equipment, resell goods, recruit customers, or conceal the operation. Gold Key: a buyer-seller transaction, even a knowingly dirty one, is not itself conspiracy unless the buyer agrees to join or further a broader criminal plan. Silver Key: when the call asks for the strongest argument against conspiracy, look for the missing agreement, not for complete innocence.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

B is attractive only if the student treats the religious-flavored object as legally special. The breaker is that the item's worship use does not supply the missing agreement; conspiracy turns on the shared criminal plan, not the type of property.

C sells a clean innocence story: Timothy did not steal the mixer. That answers the wrong question. A person can commit receiving-stolen-property or another property offense without being the original thief, so non-thief status does not explain why conspiracy is missing.

D is the dominant trap because Timothy knowingly took stolen property, which feels like joining Naomi's wrongdoing. Gold Key: knowledge plus a completed exchange is not automatic conspiracy; the charge still needs an agreement that Timothy join or further the broader stolen-property venture.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The item tests the conspiracy agreement requirement in the buyer-seller setting: a knowing purchase or exchange involving illegal goods may support a substantive property offense, but it does not automatically prove the buyer agreed to join or further a broader trafficking scheme.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Model Penal Code section 5.03 defines conspiracy around acting with the purpose of promoting or facilitating a crime and agreeing that criminal conduct occur or agreeing to aid its planning or commission. See Model Penal Code section 5.03, https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/overtact.htm. Direct Sales Co. v. United States, 319 U.S. 703 (1943), confirms the buyer-seller boundary: knowledge of unlawful use is not the same as intent and agreement, while repeated, stimulated, interested cooperation can permit the step from knowledge to conspiratorial agreement. See https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/319/703/.

Those authorities confirm the supplied key. Timothy knowingly exchanged value for stolen property, but the stem expressly removes the facts that would show a joint trafficking objective: no agreement to steal more goods, resell, recruit buyers, or conceal Naomi's operation. The strongest argument is therefore that the facts show only a buyer-seller transaction, not conspiracy.
