# CQ14820 - Evidence - Divergent Variant

## Pass-1 Report

**Final Question:**
During a church board meeting, a man presented a reimbursement request form for construction materials that he claimed had been authorized by the church treasurer. The church administrator, who had worked closely with the treasurer for years and was intimately familiar with the treasurer's signature, immediately recognized the signature on the form as a forgery. The administrator challenged the man, who then grabbed the form from the table and fled. The man is later charged with attempted fraud. At trial, the prosecutor calls the church administrator to testify that the signature on the form was a forgery. Is the administrator's testimony admissible?

**Distilled Core Question:**
Can a lay witness testify to the authenticity of a signature if they have personal familiarity with the individual's handwriting?

**Final Choices (Post-Shuffle):**
A: Yes, because the church administrator is by occupation an expert on document authentication.
B: Yes, because the testimony is rationally based on the witness's perception and helpful to the trier of fact.
C: No, because the administrator failed to secure the original document, which is required for such testimony.
D: No, because a jury cannot determine the authenticity of a signature without expert testimony.

**Correct Answer:** B

**Letter Map:**
A: Distractor (Mechanic: Misidentification of expertise requirement)
B: Correct
C: Distractor (Mechanic: Invented foundational requirement)
D: Distractor (Mechanic: Misstatement of jury capability/authentication rules)

**Right-Answer Explanation:**
Under FRE 701, lay opinion testimony is admissible if it is rationally based on the perception of the witness and helpful to a clear understanding of the witness's testimony or to the determination of a fact in issue. Familiarity with an individual's handwriting, acquired other than for the purpose of litigation, is a proper basis for such testimony (FRE 901(b)(2)).

**Wrong-Answer Explanations:**
A: The administrator is not testifying as an expert; they are testifying as a lay witness based on personal familiarity.
C: There is no rule requiring the original document to be secured by the witness as a condition for lay opinion testimony.
D: A jury can determine authenticity based on admissible lay testimony regarding handwriting familiarity, and expert testimony is not the exclusive method for this determination.

**Black-Letter Verification with Authority:**
FRE 701 (Lay Opinion) and FRE 901(b)(2) (Authentication by non-expert with familiarity).

**Rigor/Difficulty Note:**
Core. Tests the fundamentals of lay opinion testimony and handwriting authentication.

*   **CLASH:** The clash is between the witness's lack of formal "handwriting expert" status and their personal, rationally-based perception of the signature's authenticity.
