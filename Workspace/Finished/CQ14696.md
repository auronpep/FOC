### 7. Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** Lydia expressed her intent to acquire smuggled goods and agreed to meet the agent in two weeks — but she did nothing more. At common law, criminal attempt requires two elements: (1) specific intent to commit the target offense, and (2) an overt act that goes meaningfully beyond mere preparation. Her words and agreement satisfy the intent prong, but the overt-act prong is completely empty. She contacted no supplier, arranged no transport, moved no funds, and took no physical step of any kind toward actually obtaining the fabric. That gap is fatal under common law. No act = no attempt.

**Gold Key (GK-CRIM-ATTEMPT-01):** At common law, criminal attempt requires BOTH specific intent AND an overt act beyond mere preparation. Words and agreements, however explicit, never satisfy the overt-act requirement on their own.

### 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A — "Yes, because of the agreement"**
The deal feels done — the meeting is set, both parties said yes. But an agreement is proof of intent, not proof of an act. At common law, even a clear mutual agreement to commit a crime does not constitute attempt. Conspiracy may attach, but attempted possession requires Lydia to have taken some step toward actually possessing the goods. She never did. Gold Key (GK-CRIM-ATTEMPT-01): The overt-act requirement is not satisfied by an agreement, no matter how explicit.

**C — "Yes, because of expressed intent"**
Lydia said plainly that she knew the fabric was smuggled and wanted to buy it. That looks decisive. But criminal intent, however clearly expressed, is only one of two required elements. Common law attempt demands something more — an overt act toward commission. Intent without action is not punishable as attempt. Gold Key (GK-CRIM-ATTEMPT-01): Intent alone, however clearly expressed, satisfies the starting condition, not the finish line for common law attempt.

**D — "No, because of the illegal arrest"**
D reaches the right bottom line ("No") but for a legally irrelevant reason, and that makes it a trap. The legality of an arrest does not determine guilt or innocence for the underlying charge. An illegal arrest does not make an innocent person guilty, and it does not make a guilty person innocent. The exclusionary rule may suppress evidence obtained through an unlawful search, but it does not function as a complete defense to the substantive crime charged. D fails because its reason — procedurally true though it may be — does not address whether Lydia committed attempt. Silver Key (SK-CRIM-ATTEMPT-01): When two "No" answers remain, CLASH on the reason — the reason grounded in the substantive elements of the crime wins over one grounded in procedure.

### 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Rule:** At common law, criminal attempt requires: (1) specific intent to commit the target crime, and (2) an overt act that goes beyond mere preparation — sometimes described as a "direct step" toward commission or "dangerous proximity." This two-element structure is consistent across American common law jurisdictions. For contrast, the Model Penal Code § 5.01(1)(c) uses the broader "substantial step" standard, but the question specifies a common law jurisdiction, making the stricter overt-act framework controlling.

**Key B is correct:** Lydia's words and agreement establish intent, but she took zero action toward actually obtaining the silk. That gap defeats the attempt charge under common law. The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the original question: in both, a defendant expressed intent and made an agreement, but took no overt act, in a common law jurisdiction.

**Distractor verification:** Agreement (A) is recognized as insufficient for attempt at common law — it supports conspiracy, a distinct offense. Expressed intent (C) is a necessary but not sufficient element. Illegal arrest (D) speaks to Fourth Amendment suppression, not a defense to the substantive charge.

**Authority:** Common law attempt doctrine (widely recognized); MPC § 5.01(1)(c) cited for contrast only. Rule stated plainly from first principles; attorney review not required.

#### Section 12 — remediation_card

**Card ID:** REM-CRIM-ATTEMPT-01
**Title:** Common Law Attempt — Two Elements

**Signal:** Charged with attempt; defendant only talked, agreed, or expressed intent.

**Student move:** Ask two questions:
1. Did the defendant have specific intent to commit the target crime?
2. Did the defendant take an overt act beyond mere preparation?

Both must be YES for common law attempt.

**Tiny rule:** Intent + Agreement ≠ Attempt. An overt act is required.

**Trap:** Students confuse conspiracy (which arises from agreement) with attempt (which requires an act). The dominant trap offers a procedurally correct reason (illegal arrest) for the right bottom line — test whether you can identify the *substantive* reason.
