### 1. Final question

The land records for a narrow city lot called the Harp Room still listed Martha as owner in fee simple. Martha had signed a contract to sell the lot, where she operated a private psalm-calligraphy studio, to Timothy. Twelve days after the contract, Timothy paid the agreed price and received Martha’s warranty deed. Neither the contract nor the deed was recorded. Five days after that closing, while Martha still kept her hymn-lettering presses in the studio, Daniel, a creditor, properly filed a money judgment against Martha. Daniel had no knowledge of Timothy’s interest.

A statute in the jurisdiction provides: “Any judgment properly filed shall, for 12 years from filing, be a lien on the real property then owned or subsequently acquired by any person against whom the judgment is rendered.”

The recording act of the jurisdiction provides: “No conveyance or mortgage of real property shall be good against subsequent purchasers for value and without notice unless the same be recorded according to law.”

Daniel brought an appropriate action to enforce his lien against the Harp Room in Timothy’s hands. If the court rules for Timothy, it most probably will be because:

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**A is correct.** The recording act protects “subsequent purchasers for value and without notice.” Daniel is a money-judgment creditor, not a purchaser for value. Timothy already received the deed before Daniel filed the judgment. The buyer wins because Daniel cannot use a purchaser-only recording act to defeat Timothy’s earlier unrecorded deed.

**Gold Key:** A purchaser-only recording act protects later purchasers for value without notice; a judgment creditor is not protected unless the statute expressly names judgment creditors or otherwise gives them that status.

**Silver Key:** Read the recording act from the later claimant’s side. The unrecorded deed loses only if the later claimant fits the protected class named in the statute.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B. Timothy was a purchaser without notice.**
This is the dominant trap because it uses the familiar buyer-without-notice sound. The breaker is actor direction: Timothy is not the person trying to use the recording act to defeat an unrecorded deed. Daniel is. **Gold Key:** the recording act’s protected class must cover Daniel, and it does not. **Silver Key:** run the statute on the later claimant, not on the earlier deed grantee.

**C. the doctrine of equitable conversion applies.**
This is attractive because the facts begin with a land-sale contract. The breaker is timing. Equitable conversion is the contract-to-closing doctrine; this dispute arises after Timothy paid and received the deed. A true/responsive version would matter if the lien arose between contract and closing.

**D. Martha’s continued possession gave Daniel constructive notice of Timothy’s interest.**
This is attractive because possession can sometimes create inquiry notice. The breaker is the person in possession. Martha’s visible possession points to Martha’s interest, not Timothy’s unrecorded deed.
