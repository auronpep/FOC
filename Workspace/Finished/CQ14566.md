## PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question
Lydia was subpoenaed to a grand jury investigating a killing that occurred outside a winter choir concert. After giving her name and address, she was asked where she had been at the time of the killing. Lydia asked to speak with her lawyer in the hallway, was allowed to do so, and returned saying that an answer might incriminate her. The prosecutor believes Lydia's nephew Peter committed the killing and falsely claims that he was with Lydia at the time. The prosecutor does not believe Lydia took part in the killing, but does believe her answer may expose her to other crimes.

Which step should the prosecutor take to compel Lydia to answer while causing the least harm to the prosecution's case?

### 2. Distilled Core Question
A grand-jury witness invokes the Fifth Amendment before answering an incriminating alibi question; what immunity is enough to compel the answer with the least prosecutorial cost?

### 3. Final answer choices

D. Request that the grand jury simply order Lydia to answer the question.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation
Choice B is the tight answer. Lydia may invoke the privilege because the question could expose her to criminal liability, but the prosecutor can compel testimony by giving use and derivative-use immunity. Silver Key: When the call asks for the least harmful step, prefer the remedy that is sufficient and no broader. Gold Key: A witness who invokes the Fifth Amendment can be compelled with use and derivative-use immunity; transactional immunity is broader than required.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
- A: The evidence-rule point sounds procedural and lawyerly, but it answers the wrong problem. The Fifth Amendment privilege applies even when ordinary evidence rules do not control the grand-jury room.
- C: The broad-immunity choice feels protective because it plainly would make the witness answer. The breaker is the call: transactional immunity gives away more prosecution power than the Constitution requires. Gold Key: use and derivative-use immunity is enough.
- D: A direct grand-jury order feels efficient because it commands the answer. The breaker is that compulsion without sufficient immunity leaves the privilege intact.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
A witness may not be forced to give potentially incriminating testimony unless the government supplies immunity coextensive with the Fifth Amendment privilege. Use and derivative-use immunity satisfies that requirement; transactional immunity is not constitutionally necessary. Authority: Kastigar v. United States, 406 U.S. 441 (1972). The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged.
