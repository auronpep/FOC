## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**Gold Key:** Solicitation requires specific intent that the solicited crime actually be committed. The solicitor must genuinely want the crime to occur; a request made without that intent — even if the words describe a crime — is not solicitation.

**Silver Key:** When the call asks for a "defense," the correct answer will name a missing element or justification, not assert a guilty verdict. If the stem says the defendant "did not want" the crime, the defense is lack of specific intent — never the victim's response or the property's value.

The correct answer is **C**. Solicitation is an inchoate crime that requires both (1) a communication requesting or encouraging another to commit a crime, and (2) the specific intent that the solicited crime actually be committed. The stem explicitly states that Barnabas "does not want Lydia to commit any crime." This negates the specific intent element. Because Barnabas lacks the mens rea required for solicitation, his strongest defense is that he lacked the specific intent that Lydia commit burglary. Words alone, however descriptive of a crime, do not satisfy the offense when the speaker does not genuinely desire the crime to occur.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice A — "Barnabas is guilty because the words requested a burglary."**
This choice sells the common misconception that words alone are enough for solicitation. It is a flat misstatement of the law. Solicitation requires both communication *and* specific intent that the crime occur. The stem explicitly says Barnabas did not want the crime committed, so the specific intent element is missing. **Gold Key:** Words without genuine criminal purpose are not solicitation.

**Choice B — "Barnabas is not guilty only because equipment sheds contain low-value property."**
This choice focuses on property value as the reason for not guilty, but property value is irrelevant to the specific intent requirement for solicitation. It attacks the wrong element. The correct reason for not guilty is Barnabas's lack of specific intent, not the value of the property in the equipment shed. **Silver Key:** When the call asks for a defense, look for a missing mens rea element, not a property-value distinction.

**Choice D — "Barnabas is guilty unless Lydia refused out loud."**
This choice sells the misconception that the recipient's response matters to solicitation liability. The recipient's agreement or refusal is irrelevant — solicitation is complete upon the request with specific intent, regardless of whether the solicitee agrees, refuses, or even hears the request. The missing element is the solicitor's intent, not the victim's response. **Silver Key:** The defendant's intent, not the victim's behavior, determines solicitation liability.

## 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** Solicitation requires (1) a communication requesting or encouraging another to commit a crime, and (2) the specific intent or purpose that the solicited crime actually be committed. Both elements are required; words alone are insufficient.

**Authority:** The Model Penal Code § 5.02(1) defines solicitation as requesting, commanding, or encouraging another person to commit a crime "with the purpose of promoting or facilitating its commission." At common law, solicitation similarly requires the specific intent that the person solicited commit the crime. See general authority at Lumen Learning, Criminal Law — Solicitation ("The criminal intent element required for solicitation is specific intent or purposely to induce another to commit any crime"), and Inazu-CrimLaw, Solicitation ("Both the MPC and the common law require the solicitor to have the specific intent for the person solicited to commit the crime. A woman who jokingly asks a man to steal a diamond ring for her does not have the requisite mens rea to constitute a solicitation, even if the man actually steals the ring.").

**Right-answer legal reasoning:** The credited answer (C) is correct because the stem explicitly negates the specific intent element. Barnabas "does not want Lydia to commit any crime." Under both the MPC and common law, a request made without the specific intent that the crime occur is not solicitation. The defendant's subjective purpose is dispositive.

### wrong_answer_recovery_paths

**Choice A recovery path:**
- **Why a student picks this:** Student thinks words alone create solicitation, ignoring the mens rea requirement.
- **Skipped move:** Checking whether the defendant had the specific intent that the crime be committed.
- **Recovery step:** Memorize that solicitation requires both communication and specific intent; words without intent are not solicitation. Drill: Read a stem where someone says words describing a crime but clearly jokes or role-plays. The answer is always "not solicitation because no specific intent."

**Choice B recovery path:**
- **Why a student picks this:** Student thinks property value affects the seriousness of the solicitation charge.
- **Skipped move:** Recognizing that property value is irrelevant to the specific intent element.
- **Recovery step:** Focus on the defendant's mental state, not the target property's characteristics. In solicitation, the only relevant mental state is the solicitor's specific intent.

**Choice D recovery path:**
- **Why a student picks this:** Student thinks the victim's refusal negates or mitigates the solicitation.
- **Skipped move:** Recognizing that the recipient's response is irrelevant to solicitation liability.
- **Recovery step:** Remember that solicitation is complete upon the request with specific intent, regardless of the recipient's response. The victim's behavior is never the dispositive element.
