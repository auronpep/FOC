### 1. Final question — revised stem and call

By a signed order, Lydia, a private maker of Scripture-themed ceramic mug sets, promised to supply Barnabas’s Christian bookstore with **640 boxed mug sets** for its November and December shelves. If Barnabas sues Lydia for breach of contract, is Barnabas likely to prevail?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A buyer in a UCC sale gives a required assortment choice one day late; the seller had all goods ready and was not affected by the delay. Can the seller refuse to perform?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** The delay was only one day, Lydia still had every design needed, and Lydia made no inventory commitments on either relevant day. That means the late assortment choice did not materially affect Lydia’s ability to perform.

**Silver Key:** Do not stop at “missed deadline.” Name the axis: did the late specification materially affect the seller’s performance? The inventory/no-commitment facts answer that axis.

**Gold Key:** In a UCC sale, leaving assortment particulars for one party to specify later does not invalidate the contract, and a late specification gives the other party stronger remedies only when the lateness materially affects performance.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice is attractive because it gives Lydia a practical-looking substitute: if Barnabas missed the choice date, Lydia can just pick the assortment herself. The breaker is that the “reasonable assortment” move is not an automatic duty here, because the late choice did not materially affect Lydia’s performance.

**C.** This choice is attractive because it treats the missing design breakdown as if the deal was still incomplete. The breaker is the signed order itself: the open assortment detail did not keep the UCC sale from existing.

**D.** This is the dominant trap. It is attractive because deadline language feels fatal, and the words “express condition” sound precise. The breaker is the Silver Key move: the deadline is not the end of the analysis; the fact that Lydia was not materially affected defeats the fatal-deadline frame.

### 9. Black-letter verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule is UCC § 2-311. An otherwise definite sale contract is not invalid merely because particulars of performance are left for later specification; assortment specifications are ordinarily at the buyer’s option; and if a needed specification is not seasonably made, the other party’s remedies depend on whether the late specification materially affects performance. The UCC text states that such an agreement “is not made invalid” by leaving performance particulars to later specification, and subsection (3) gives remedies where the specification would materially affect the other party’s performance and is not seasonably made. ([Legal Information Institute][1])

The credited outcome is unchanged. Barnabas made the assortment choice one day late. Lydia had all required goods and made no inventory commitments during the delay. The delay therefore did not materially affect Lydia’s ability to perform. Lydia’s refusal is a breach.

## review_truth

In a UCC sale, a late buyer selection defeats the buyer only when the lateness materially affects the seller’s performance.
