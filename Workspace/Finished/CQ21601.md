## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

A search incident to arrest does not require the formal arrest announcement to come first. What matters is: (1) officers had probable cause to arrest before the search, and (2) a lawful custodial arrest promptly followed as part of the same continuous encounter. Here, officers witnessed the drug sale — probable cause existed before the search. They searched Josiah's pockets, found the buy-money, and then immediately announced the arrest. The search and arrest were one continuous encounter. The search is valid.

**Gold Key:** For a search incident to arrest, the formal announcement need not precede the search. The test is: (1) probable cause to arrest existed before the search, AND (2) a lawful custodial arrest promptly followed as part of the same continuous encounter. Both were satisfied here.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**B (originally D):** "Police may search anyone they suspect before deciding whether to arrest." This overstates the rule. Officers need probable cause to arrest before the search — not mere suspicion. The search-incident-to-arrest doctrine requires PC + prompt arrest, not just a hunch.

**C (originally B) — DOMINANT TRAP:** "The words 'under arrest' must always be spoken before any search." This is the classic formalistic trap. Students think the magic words must come first. But the law looks at substance over form: if PC existed and the arrest promptly followed, the timing of the announcement doesn't matter. The Gold Key kills this trap.

**D (originally C):** "Drug offenses never permit searches incident to arrest." This is flat wrong. Any lawful custodial arrest — including for drug offenses — can support a search incident to arrest. There is no drug-offense exception.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing rule:** A warrantless search incident to a lawful custodial arrest is valid when: (1) the officer has probable cause to make the arrest before the search, and (2) the arrest follows promptly as part of the same continuous encounter. The formal announcement of arrest need not precede the search. *See* Rawlings v. Kentucky, 448 U.S. 98 (1980); United States v. Robinson, 414 U.S. 218 (1973); Rawlings at 111 ("Where the formal arrest followed on the heels of the challenged search of petitioner's person, we do not believe it particularly important that the search preceded the arrest rather than vice versa").

**Application:** Officers witnessed the drug sale → probable cause existed before the search. Search conducted, buy-money found, arrest announced seconds later → same continuous encounter. Search is valid. Official key A confirmed.
