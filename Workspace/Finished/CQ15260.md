### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested issue is strict products liability, specifically design defect versus failure to warn. The verified outline code is 63030100 (Products Liability > Strict Products Liability). The source row's displayed outline code, 63040100, points to abnormally dangerous activities, but the stem, call, official explanation, and credited answer test whether the product was defective. That source metadata mismatch is logged; the credited outcome remains unchanged.

Restatement (Third) of Torts: Products Liability Section 2 separates product defects into manufacturing defects, design defects, and inadequate instructions or warnings. Under Section 2(b), a design defect turns on whether foreseeable risks could have been reduced or avoided by a reasonable alternative design and whether omission of that alternative made the product not reasonably safe. Under Section 2(c), a warning defect turns on whether reasonable instructions or warnings would have reduced foreseeable risks and whether the omission made the product not reasonably safe. The American Law Institute describes the Restatement (Third) products liability volume as covering commercial product sellers and superseding the older Section 402A framework for modern product-defect categories. State formulations vary, but the same risk-utility frame appears in leading cases such as Barker v. Lull Engineering Co., which allows a design-defect finding where the design caused injury and the manufacturer fails to show that the design's benefits outweigh its risks.

Here, the product was not shown to contain a manufacturing defect, and the facts affirmatively state that it was as safe as possible for its educational use and that its benefits exceeded its risks. The missing warning concerns a dangerous outside-manual flare project, not the kit's intended educational use or a reasonable foreseeable use. The abnormal-activity answer invokes a separate strict-liability doctrine, commonly associated with Restatement (Second) of Torts Sections 519 and 520, but the manufacturer is being sued as a product seller. The third-party-negligence answer does not answer the product-defect question. The official key is confirmed.

Authority checked: Restatement (Third) of Torts: Products Liability Section 2(b)-(c); Restatement (Second) of Torts Sections 519-520; Barker v. Lull Engineering Co., 20 Cal. 3d 413 (1978); ALI, Restatement of the Law Third, Torts: Products Liability; Blue v.
