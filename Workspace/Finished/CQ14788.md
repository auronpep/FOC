### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**A is correct.** Barnabas testified that he saw Martha commit the burglary. His alleged jail statement says he did the break-in alone. That is a prior inconsistent statement. Martha is not using it to prove that Barnabas truly acted alone; she is using it to attack Barnabas’s credibility.

**Gold Key:** An unsworn prior inconsistent statement of a witness can be used to impeach that witness if the Rule 613 foundation is satisfied, but it is not substantive prior-statement evidence unless the prior statement was under oath at a qualifying proceeding or deposition.

**Silver Key:** Before choosing a hearsay label, lock the purpose. If the statement is offered to show the witness changed stories, the lane is impeachment, not truth of the statement.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B.** This choice hears that Barnabas’s statement admits his own crime and reaches for the corroboration rule. The breaker is purpose. Clear corroboration belongs to the statement-against-interest route, not to ordinary impeachment by prior inconsistent statement.

**C.** This is the dominant trap. It gets the admissibility result but uses the wrong reason. Barnabas is available and has been given the Rule 613 opportunity; Martha does not need the statement-against-interest exception. **Gold Key:** Rule 804(b)(3) is an unavailable-declarant route.

**D.** This choice correctly notices an unsworn out-of-court statement, then grades it as if it were offered for its truth. That is the wrong lane. **Silver Key:** the impeachment purpose matters first. The statement is offered to show Barnabas changed his story.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested issue and credited legal outcome match the source row: a witness’s prior inconsistent statement is admissible to impeach the witness, while the statement-against-interest and hearsay-for-truth routes are the wrong frames. The source explanation identifies the statement as admissible impeachment and rejects the declaration-against-interest, hearsay-only, and corroboration distractors. 

Under current Federal Rule of Evidence 613(b), extrinsic evidence of a witness’s prior inconsistent statement may not be admitted until after the witness is given an opportunity to explain or deny it and the adverse party has an opportunity to examine the witness, unless the court orders otherwise. The variation makes that foundation explicit. ([Legal Information Institute][1])

Rule 801(c) defines hearsay as an out-of-court statement offered for the truth of what it asserts, and Rule 801(d)(1)(A) treats a prior inconsistent statement as non-hearsay only when it was made under penalty of perjury at a qualifying proceeding or deposition. Barnabas’s alleged bunkmate statement is not substantive non-hearsay under that rule; it is admissible for impeachment. ([U.S. Code][2])

Rule 804(b)(3) applies only when the declarant is unavailable as a witness, and its corroboration requirement attaches to criminal-liability statements on that route. Barnabas is available, so the declaration-against-interest route is not the governing reason. ([Legal Information Institute][3])

## remediation_card

**Card:** REMED-EVIDENCE-PRIORS-PURPOSE-01 — Prior Statement Purpose Lock.

**Signal:** A witness testifies one way and someone else reports that the witness earlier said the opposite.

**Student move:** Ask whether the statement is offered for truth or to attack credibility.

**Tiny rule:** Impeachment use is different from substantive hearsay use.

**Trap:** Jumping to a hearsay exception label before locking purpose.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-EVIDENCE-613-IMPEACHMENT-01.** An unsworn prior inconsistent statement of a witness can be used to impeach that witness if the Rule 613 foundation is satisfied, but it is not substantive prior-statement evidence unless the prior statement was under oath at a qualifying proceeding or deposition.

**GK-EVIDENCE-804B3-UNAVAILABILITY-01.** A statement-against-interest route under Rule 804(b)(3) requires an unavailable declarant; the corroboration requirement belongs to that route, not to ordinary impeachment by prior inconsistent statement.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-EVIDENCE-PURPOSE-LANE-01.** Before choosing a hearsay label, lock the purpose. If the statement is offered to show the witness changed stories, the lane is impeachment, not truth of the statement.

## review_truth

A prior inconsistent statement offered only to impeach a testifying witness is admissible for credibility, not for the truth of the statement.
