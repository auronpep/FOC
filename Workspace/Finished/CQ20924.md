### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

Lydia's remedy is expectation through cover under UCC Article 2. Under §2-712(2), a buyer who properly covers may recover "the difference between the cost of cover and the contract price together with any incidental or consequential damages ... but less expenses saved." Lydia's cover was in good faith and without unreasonable delay — she bought identical substitute chairs from another craftsman.

The cover differential is: (400 × $62) − (400 × $50) = $24,800 − $20,000 = $4,800.

Under §2-715(1), Lydia also recovers her incidental damages — the $250 freight charge is a reasonable expense incident to effecting cover.

Under §2-715(2)(a), consequential damages (lost profits) are recoverable only if they result from "general or particular requirements and needs of which the seller at the time of contracting had reason to know." Peter had no reason to know about Lydia's special hotel-chain resale deal. The $3,500 is therefore excluded.

Total: $4,800 + $250 = **$5,050**.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing authority:** UCC §§2-712(2) and 2-715(1)–(2).
