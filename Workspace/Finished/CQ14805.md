## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C** is correct.

The notes are written assertions made outside court, and the government offers them to prove what happened at the launch huddles. That is hearsay.

**Gold Key:** A personal note made to help the witness remember is not automatically a business record or a recorded recollection. Business records require regular-practice foundation; recorded recollection requires current memory failure, a fresh record, and accuracy foundation.

**Gold Key:** Even when recorded recollection is founded, the record is ordinarily read into evidence and is received as an exhibit only if offered by an adverse party.

**Silver Key:** Do not let the word "notes" pick the exception. Ask why the notes were made and what foundation the witness actually supplied.

Lydia supplied authentication and a personal memory-aid motive. She did not supply a regular business practice. She did not testify that she now cannot recall the huddles fully and accurately or that the notes accurately reflected her knowledge when the matter was fresh. No hearsay exception fits, so the notes should not be admitted.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This is the dominant trap. Personal notes made to remember meetings look like recorded recollection because the words "notes" and "remember" point in that direction. The breaker is the missing foundation. **Gold Key:** recorded recollection requires the witness's present inability to testify fully and accurately and proof that the record accurately reflected the witness's knowledge when fresh; if admitted, it is read and not received as an exhibit unless offered by an adverse party.

**B.** This choice is attractive because Lydia's notes concern company huddles, so the business setting makes the exception sound plausible. The breaker is regularity. **Gold Key:** business records require a record kept in the course of a regularly conducted activity and made as a regular practice. Personal planner notes made on Lydia's own initiative do not satisfy that foundation.

**D.** This choice reaches the same bottom-line exclusion but sells the wrong reason. The immunity agreement may be useful impeachment material, but it does not make the earlier notes inadmissible under a Rule 403 prejudice theory. The notes are excluded because they are hearsay with no founded exception, not because the immunity agreement changes the notes' admissibility.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

Federal Rule of Evidence 801(c) defines hearsay as a statement the declarant does not make while testifying at the current trial or hearing and that a party offers to prove the truth of the matter asserted. Federal Rule of Evidence 802 bars hearsay unless a federal statute, the Evidence Rules, or other Supreme Court-prescribed rules provide otherwise. Lydia's notes are out-of-court written assertions, and the government offers them to prove what happened in the huddles, so the hearsay gate is triggered. ([U.S. Code][1])

Federal Rule of Evidence 803(5) covers recorded recollection only when the record concerns a matter the witness once knew about but now cannot recall well enough to testify fully and accurately, was made or adopted while fresh, and accurately reflects the witness's knowledge. If admitted, it may be read into evidence and may be received as an exhibit only if offered by an adverse party. Those foundation facts are missing, and the government is offering the notes themselves. ([U.S. Code][1])

Federal Rule of Evidence 803(6) requires a record made at or near the time by someone with knowledge, kept in the course of a regularly conducted activity, and made as a regular practice, with proper foundation and no shown untrustworthiness. Lydia's personal notes, made on her own initiative to help herself remember, do not show a business-record foundation. ([U.S. Code][1])

The notes are inadmissible hearsay because neither the business-records exception nor the recorded-recollection exception has been founded.
