### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The credited answer remains unchanged in substance. A statement obtained through custodial interrogation without Miranda warnings is generally inadmissible in the prosecution's case-in-chief. If the statement is voluntary, however, it may be used for the limited purpose of impeaching the defendant's inconsistent trial testimony. The rule does not apply to coerced or involuntary statements.

Authority floor: Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966), supplies the warning rule for custodial interrogation. Harris v. New York, 401 U.S. 222 (1971), permits impeachment with a voluntary Miranda-defective statement that is inadmissible in the prosecution's direct case. Oregon v. Hass, 420 U.S. 714 (1975), confirms the limited impeachment use of voluntary statements obtained in violation of Miranda. Sources checked: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/401/222, https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/420/714/, and https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/384/436/.
