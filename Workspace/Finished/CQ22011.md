## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Naomi owned a private olive-press lot in fee simple. When Naomi died, her will devised the lot to her husband, Barnabas, "for life, remainder to such person as Barnabas shall designate by his will."

Four months after Naomi's death, Barnabas married Lydia. As a celebration gift, Barnabas signed and delivered a deed purporting to convey the olive-press lot to Lydia in fee simple absolute. The next day, Barnabas died intestate, survived only by Lydia.

Lydia later agreed to sell the lot to Esther under a contract requiring Lydia to convey marketable title. The deed Lydia tendered at closing did not contain a covenant of general warranty. Esther refused to accept the deed and demanded return of her deposit.

In litigation between Lydia and Esther, should the court find in Esther's favor?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Can a seller give marketable title when her claimed ownership depends on a lifetime deed that attempted to exercise a power of appointment exercisable only by will?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. No, because Barnabas's deed to Lydia was a valid exercise of the power of appointment Naomi gave him.

B. No, because Lydia was the only person qualified to inherit from Barnabas under intestacy law.

C. Yes, because the lot reverted to Naomi's estate when Barnabas died.

D. Yes, because the deed Lydia tendered did not contain a covenant of general warranty.

### 5. Correct answer

C (original key B).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Naomi gave Barnabas a life estate and a power to choose the next owner only by will. Barnabas tried to transfer the lot by lifetime deed, then died without a will. Gold Key: a power of appointment must be exercised in the manner the donor required; if the donor says "by will," a lifetime deed does not do the job. Silver Key: separate the seller's deed problem from the earlier source-of-title problem. Lydia's deed did not fail because it lacked warranty words; it failed because Lydia did not own marketable title.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because Barnabas signed a deed that named Lydia and purported to give her fee simple absolute. The breaker is the required method. Naomi authorized appointment by Barnabas's will, not by Barnabas's lifetime deed.

B is attractive because Lydia was Barnabas's surviving spouse and only intestate heir. The appointive property did not pass through Barnabas's intestate estate. Barnabas had only a life estate plus a will-only power, and both failed to give Lydia title when he died intestate.

D is attractive because the contract required marketable title and a general warranty sounds like title protection. The breaker is that warranty covenants are promises about liability; they do not make a doubtful title marketable at closing.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is unchanged: Esther wins because Lydia could not convey marketable title. Barnabas held a life estate and a power of appointment that Naomi required him to exercise by will. His lifetime deed did not satisfy that requirement, and his later intestate death did not exercise the power. The property therefore returned to Naomi's estate, leaving Lydia without title to convey.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Uniform Powers of Appointment Act section 301 states that a power is exercised only if the instrument manifests intent and satisfies the donor-imposed requirements of exercise. Utah Code section 75A-4-304, an enacted version of the Uniform Act's formal-requirement rule, states that if the donor limited exercise to a validly executed will, substantial compliance may not include a trust or another document that is not a properly executed will. Virginia Code section 64.2-2713 similarly requires the exercising instrument to satisfy donor-imposed requirements. Marketable title means title free from reasonable doubt about validity; warranty covenants may create later contract or deed remedies, but they do not cure the seller's lack of title for marketability at closing.

Current-law note: substantial-compliance statutes can soften some formal defects, but the tested fact is a lifetime deed where the donor required appointment by will and the donee died intestate. That framing confirms, rather than contradicts, the supplied key.
