# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

Peter and Daniel were injured when a hay baler they were operating on Peter's farm suddenly jammed and then lurched forward, crushing Daniel's arm and injuring Peter's back. They sued AgriMax, the company that serviced the baler, claiming AgriMax had improperly repaired a safety clutch. According to both men, the baler's rotor seized and then released violently without warning. Before trial, Daniel was diagnosed with aggressive liver cancer. At trial, AgriMax argued that Peter and Daniel had disabled the safety guard before operating the baler. Peter offered into evidence Daniel's deposition testimony, in which Daniel stated: "The rotor just locked and then snapped free on its own — we never touched the safety guard, and Peter kept yelling that the brake wasn't responding." Is Daniel's deposition testimony admissible?

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** Under FRE 804(b)(1), the former testimony of an unavailable declarant is admissible as an exception to the hearsay rule when the testimony was given at a deposition or other hearing, and the party against whom the testimony is now offered (or a party in privity) had an opportunity and similar motive to examine the declarant at that time. Here, Daniel is dead — he is unavailable under FRE 804(a). His deposition was taken by AgriMax's attorneys, meaning AgriMax (the party against whom Peter now offers the testimony) had a full opportunity to examine Daniel. The requirements of the former testimony exception are satisfied, and the deposition testimony is admissible.

> **Silver Key (SK-EVIDENCE-FORMER-DEPO-01):** When you see a dead witness whose deposition was taken by the opposing party, think former testimony — the opposing party's own deposition opportunity is what makes it admissible against them.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (No — not challenged during deposition):**
This choice sells the misconception that the opposing party must have actually used the deposition to challenge or impeach the witness. The attraction is procedural-sounding rigor — it feels like a real requirement. But FRE 804(b)(1) requires only that the opposing party had the *opportunity* to examine the declarant. Whether AgriMax actually challenged Daniel's statements at the deposition is irrelevant. The opportunity itself satisfies the rule.

**B (No — inadmissible hearsay):**
This is the flat-hearsay denial. It's attractive to students who identify that Daniel's out-of-court statement is hearsay but then stop analyzing before reaching the exceptions. The former testimony exception at FRE 804(b)(1) exists precisely for this scenario — a deposition by an unavailable declarant, examined by the opposing party. Calling it "inadmissible hearsay" ignores the exception entirely.

> **Silver Key (SK-EVIDENCE-FORMER-DEPO-01) applies here:** The move that steers off this trap is recognizing that hearsay has exceptions, and former testimony at a deposition taken by the opposing party is one of them.

**D (Yes — because Daniel knew he was dying):** **[DOMINANT TRAP]**
This is the dying-declaration trap, and it's the most attractive wrong answer because Daniel really was dying. The cognitive move it sells: "dying person + statement = dying declaration = admissible." But FRE 804(b)(2) requires that the statement concern the *cause or circumstances of the declarant's own impending death*. Daniel died of liver cancer — not from the baler accident. His statement described the baler malfunction, not the circumstances of his cancer. The dying-declaration exception does not apply. The statement is admissible, but for a different reason (former testimony, not dying declaration).

> **Gold Key (GK-EVIDENCE-DYING-DECL-01):** A dying declaration under FRE 804(b)(2) requires that the declarant's statement concern the cause or circumstances of *his or her own impending death*. A declarant dying of cancer who describes an unrelated accident does not satisfy this requirement — even though the declarant is literally dying.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**FRE 804(b)(1) — Former Testimony Exception:**
Testimony given as a witness at another hearing of the same or a different proceeding, or in a deposition taken in compliance with law in the course of the same or another proceeding, if the party against whom the testimony is now offered (or, in a civil case, a predecessor in interest) had an opportunity and similar motive to develop the testimony by direct, cross-, or redirect examination. (Fed. R. Evid. 804(b)(1).)

**FRE 804(a) — Unavailability:**
A declarant is "unavailable" if the declarant is exempted from testifying by ruling of the court, persists in refusing to testify concerning the subject matter of the declarant's statement despite an order of the court to do so, testifies to not remembering the subject matter, is unable to be present or to testify at the hearing because of death or a then-existing physical or mental illness, or is absent from the hearing and the proponent of the declarant's statement has not been able, by process or other reasonable means, to procure the declarant's attendance. (Fed. R. Evid. 804(a).) Death is the paradigmatic ground of unavailability.

**FRE 804(b)(2) — Dying Declaration (distinguishing):**
In a prosecution for homicide or in a civil case, a statement that the declarant, while believing the declarant's death to be imminent, made about the cause or circumstances of what the declarant believed to be impending death. (Fed. R. Evid. 804(b)(2).) The statement must concern the *declarant's own death* — not an unrelated event the declarant witnessed.

**Verification:** The credited answer (former testimony, FRE 804(b)(1)) is correct. The legal issue, tested rule, and credited outcome are unchanged from the original. Daniel's death from cancer (unrelated cause) means the dying-declaration exception does not apply — the statement is admissible solely under the former testimony exception because AgriMax had the opportunity to examine Daniel at the deposition.

## Choice-by-Choice Walkthrough

### Choice A: "No, because Daniel's statement was not challenged during the deposition."

**Part 1 — Student-accessible C3 signal:**
FRE 804(b)(1) requires that the opposing party had the *opportunity* to examine the declarant. It does not require that the opposing party actually challenged or impecced the witness. This choice invents a requirement that does not exist in the rule.

**Part 2 — Student label:**
Invented challenge requirement.

**Part 3 — What a true/responsive version would look like:**
A true version would say: "No, because AgriMax did not have the opportunity to examine Daniel." But that's not what happened — AgriMax's attorneys took the deposition.

**Part 4 — Lawyer confirmation:**
FRE 804(b)(1) requires "an opportunity and similar motive to develop the testimony by direct, cross-, or redirect examination." The opportunity is the requirement, not the exercise of it.

### Choice B: "No, because Daniel's statement is inadmissible hearsay."

**Part 1 — Student-accessible C3 signal:**
The statement *is* hearsay — Daniel's out-of-court statement offered for its truth. But calling it "inadmissible hearsay" ignores the exceptions. The former testimony exception (FRE 804(b)(1)) exists precisely for this scenario: a deposition by an unavailable declarant, examined by the opposing party.

**Part 2 — Student label:**

**Part 3 — What a true/responsive version would look like:**
A true version would say: "No, because no hearsay exception applies." But the former testimony exception does apply.

**Part 4 — Lawyer confirmation:**
FRE 804(b)(1) provides a specific exception for former testimony when the declarant is unavailable and the opposing party had opportunity to examine. The statement is hearsay but admissible under the exception.

> **Silver Key (SK-EVIDENCE-FORMER-DEPO-01):** When you see a dead witness whose deposition was taken by the opposing party, think former testimony — the opposing party's own deposition opportunity is what makes it admissible against them.

**Part 2 — Student label:**

**Part 3 — What a true/responsive version would look like:**

**Verdict:** CORRECT — breaks neither filter.

### Choice D: "Yes, because Daniel made the statement knowing that he was dying." **[DOMINANT TRAP]**

**Part 1 — Student-accessible C3 signal:**
Daniel really was dying, and dying declarations are a real exception (FRE 804(b)(2)). But the dying declaration exception requires that the statement concern the *cause or circumstances of the declarant's own impending death*. Daniel died of liver cancer — not from the baler accident. His statement described the baler malfunction, not his cancer. The dying-declaration exception does not apply. The statement is admissible, but for a different reason (former testimony).

**Part 2 — Student label:**
Dying declaration trap — right outcome, wrong reason.

**Part 3 — What a true/responsive version would look like:**
A true dying-declaration version would require Daniel to say something like: "The cancer is killing me because..." — a statement about the cause or circumstances of his own death.

**Part 4 — Lawyer confirmation:**
FRE 804(b)(2): "a statement that the declarant, while believing the declarant's death to be imminent, made about the cause or circumstances of what the declarant believed to be impending death." Daniel's statement was about the baler, not his cancer. Exception does not apply.

> **Gold Key (GK-EVIDENCE-DYING-DECL-01):** A dying declaration under FRE 804(b)(2) requires that the declarant's statement concern the cause or circumstances of the declarant's own impending death.
