## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. A homicide is murder when it is committed recklessly under circumstances manifesting extreme indifference to the value of human life. Model Penal Code section 210.2(1)(b) states that murder includes reckless homicide under circumstances manifesting extreme indifference to human life. Model Penal Code section 2.08(2) treats self-induced intoxication as immaterial when recklessness is the culpability element and the actor would have been aware of the risk if sober.

The same result is supported by drunk-driving murder authorities. United States v. Fleming, 739 F.2d 945 (4th Cir. 1984), upheld second-degree murder where extremely intoxicated, exceptionally reckless driving supported malice. People v. Watson, 637 P.2d 279 (Cal. 1981), recognized that fatal drunk driving can support implied-malice murder when the driver acts with conscious disregard for life; prior DUI-related knowledge can matter because it helps prove awareness of the danger.
