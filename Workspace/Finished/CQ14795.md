## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

C is correct. The incident log is a record of an event or condition made in the regular course of the bookstore's activity. Timothy did not need to personally see the shelf collapse. FRE 803(6) permits a regular activity record when it was made at or near the time by, or from information transmitted by, someone with knowledge, kept in the course of a regularly conducted activity, and made as a regular practice. The facts supply those points: Timothy regularly made the store log entry, and the information came from a floor supervisor with knowledge of the event. The authenticated photocopy does not create a best-evidence problem because FRE 1003 allows duplicates unless authenticity or fairness is genuinely disputed. And the nested statement in the log does not defeat admissibility because FRE 805 is satisfied when each link in the statement chain fits an exception.

**Gold Key (GK-EVID-BIZ-RECORD-01):** A regular activity record can be made by one employee from information transmitted by another person with knowledge. The entrant's lack of personal observation does not defeat FRE 803(6) when the record was made and kept as regular practice.

**Silver Key (SK-EVID-RECORD-LANE-01):** When the call asks what "the document is," separate the lanes before answering: business-record admissibility, recorded recollection, duplicate/original-document rule, and nested hearsay are different doors.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A - inadmissible as a violation of the best evidence rule.**

The pull: A photocopy feels second-best, and the best evidence rule sounds like it demands the original. That is a classic overread of the original-document rule.

The breaker: FRE 1003 allows a duplicate to the same extent as the original unless there is a genuine authenticity dispute or unfairness in using the duplicate. The stem says the photocopy is properly authenticated and gives no fairness problem. The duplicate issue is therefore not the reason to exclude the log.

**B - inadmissible, because it is hearsay within hearsay.**

The pull: The log has two layers: Timothy's written notation and the floor supervisor's out-of-court report. A student who spots two layers may stop at "double hearsay" and exclude the document.

The breaker: FRE 805 does not automatically exclude hearsay within hearsay. It allows the combined statement if each part fits an exception. Here, the store log is a regular activity record, and the information came through the store's regular reporting channel from a person with knowledge. **Gold Key:** the business-record exception can cover a regular record made from information transmitted by someone with knowledge; the recorder need not be the eyewitness. This is the dominant trap.

**D - admissible as recorded recollection.**

The pull: Timothy cannot remember who gave him the information, so "recorded recollection" looks attractive. The answer borrows a real hearsay exception and places it next to memory failure.

The breaker: Recorded recollection is the wrong lane. FRE 803(5) applies when a witness once knew the matter, cannot now testify fully and accurately, and the record is used because of that witness's failed memory. The party is not using Timothy's memory to read a past recollection into evidence; Lydia is offering the store's regular incident log as the document itself. **Silver Key:** memory words in the stem do not automatically mean recorded recollection; first ask whether the document is being offered as a regular record or as a witness-memory substitute.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

**Tested rule:** FRE 803(6) admits records of regularly conducted activity when the record was made at or near the time by, or from information transmitted by, someone with knowledge; was kept in the course of a regular activity; was made as a regular practice; and is not shown untrustworthy. The rule expressly covers a business, organization, occupation, or calling, whether or not for profit. FRE 803(6), text reviewed at Cornell LII: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803.

**Nested-hearsay layer:** FRE 805 permits hearsay within hearsay when each part of the combined statement conforms to an exception. Here, the outer layer is the regular log entry, and the inner layer is information transmitted by a floor supervisor with knowledge through the regular reporting system. FRE 805, text reviewed at Cornell LII: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_805.

**Duplicate layer:** FRE 1003 allows a duplicate to be admitted to the same extent as the original unless authenticity is genuinely disputed or circumstances make admission unfair. The stem states that the photocopy is properly authenticated and supplies no unfairness. FRE 1003, text reviewed at Cornell LII: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1003.

**Recorded-recollection layer:** FRE 803(5) is not the governing exception. It covers a record used for a witness who once knew the matter but now cannot recall well enough to testify, and the record generally is read into evidence rather than received as an exhibit unless an adverse party offers it. This document is being offered as a regular activity record, not as Timothy's memory substitute. FRE 803(5), text reviewed at Cornell LII: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803.

**Applied:** Lydia's authenticated photocopy of the store incident log is admissible because the entry was made as part of the store's regular practice, from information transmitted by a person with knowledge, and no trustworthiness problem is stated. The legal issue, tested rule, credited outcome, and trap structure match the source item: regular activity record wins; recorded recollection, best-evidence exclusion, and hearsay-within-hearsay exclusion do not.
