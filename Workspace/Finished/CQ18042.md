## 7. Wrong-Answer Explanations

This choice is attractive because it is the cleanest attack on the statute. ### B - Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment

The breaker is the negative call: B is not the weakest invalidity argument because the classification gives Lydia Press a facial discrimination story. This is the dominant trap and the validation problem. The source explanation treats D as a plausible invalidity argument because the statute disadvantages out-of-staters. Current doctrine is more exacting: Article IV Privileges and Immunities protects citizens, not corporations, and a company generally cannot invoke it. Because Lydia Press is framed as a company, D may be even less likely than the official Contracts Clause answer. That conflict is why the item is marked NEEDS_HUMAN rather than PASS.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Verdict:** NEEDS_HUMAN. The official key is C after shuffle, but legal research creates a material key-validation concern.

**Contracts Clause authority:** U.S. Const. art. I, section 10, cl. 1 prohibits states from passing laws impairing the obligation of contracts. The modern test asks whether the state law substantially impairs a contractual relationship; if so, the state must show a significant and legitimate public purpose and reasonable, appropriate means. See *Energy Reserves Group, Inc. v. Kansas Power & Light Co.*, 459 U.S. 400 (1983), https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/459/400; Constitution Annotated, https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artI-S10-C1-4-1/ALDE_00013037/.

**Dormant Commerce Clause authority:** A law that discriminates against out-of-state goods or economic actors is generally subject to a virtually per se rule of invalidity unless narrowly justified. *Philadelphia v. New Jersey*, 437 U.S. 617 (1978), struck down a ban on importing out-of-state waste under the Commerce Clause. See https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/437/617/ and Constitution Annotated, https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artI-S8-C3-7-5/ALDE_00013311/.

**Equal Protection authority:** The Fourteenth Amendment bars a state from denying any person equal protection, and corporations have been treated as persons for Equal Protection purposes. See Constitution Annotated, https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt14-S1-8-9-1/ALDE_00000835/.

**Article IV Privileges and Immunities authority:** Article IV, section 2 protects citizens of each state, and the Supreme Court has long treated the clause as inapplicable to corporations. See Constitution Annotated, https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artIV-S2-C1-6/ALDE_00013782/.

**Why this matters:** The supplied explanation says the Contracts Clause applies only when a law is limited to altering contract rights and remedies. That is not the current formulation. And if the challenger is a company/corporation, Article IV Privileges and Immunities may be the weaker answer than Contracts Clause. The transformed item therefore preserves the source answer but cannot receive a clean PASS.
