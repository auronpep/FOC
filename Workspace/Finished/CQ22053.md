### 7. Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** Esther's only path to continued access is a prescriptive easement — the real property analog to adverse possession. A prescriptive easement requires use that is (1) actual and open, (2) hostile (without the owner's permission), and (3) continuous for the full statutory period. "Continuous" does not demand daily use; it demands regularity consistent with the nature of the easement claimed. **Gold Key (Continuity):** When use is so infrequent — roughly once a year, and not even every year — that it amounts only to an occasional trespass rather than a sustained assertion of right, the continuity element fails, even if the total span of years exceeds the statutory period. Esther dumped debris "most seasons, but not every year" for 35 years. That sporadic, sometimes-skipped pattern does not satisfy continuous use. **Silver Key (Gap Language):** The stem's phrase "most seasons, but not every year" is the continuity signal — lock on that gap before counting toward the 20-year period.

### 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A — No, because Daniel knew she was dumping there.**

**B — Yes, because Esther did this for 35 holiday seasons.**
B is the dominant trap. A student who checks "statutory period" sees 35 seasons against a 20-year statute and feels confident: she's clearly in the zone. The trap sells the idea that meeting the clock is enough. **Gold Key (Continuity):** A prescriptive easement requires more than satisfying the clock — the use itself must be continuous throughout. Sporadic use that amounts to no more than an occasional trespass fails continuity even when it spans the statutory period. B proves one necessary element (duration) while missing the dispositive one (quality of use). The 35 seasons satisfy the time requirement but not the continuity-of-use requirement.

**D — Yes, because Esther had no other place for the debris.**
D invokes need — and the need is real; the stem confirms Esther's lot has no room. But Esther's need alone does not create a property right. ### 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** Prescriptive easement (the real property analog to adverse possession). Elements under common law and the Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes: (1) actual use, open and notorious; (2) hostile — meaning without the owner's permission or consent; (3) continuous for the statutory period.

**Continuity:** "Continuous" does not require constant or daily use; it requires regular, uninterrupted use consistent with the nature of the easement claimed. Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes § 2.17 (2000) confirms that prescriptive use must be continuous in the sense of regular and consistent with the kind of use being claimed. Courts hold that infrequent, sporadic use — use that amounts to no more than a minor, occasional trespass — fails the continuous requirement even if the sporadic use spans many years. A pattern of "most years but not every year," each visit roughly once per season, is the paradigm case of insufficient continuity.

**Hostility:** A prescriptive claim is hostile when it is without the owner's permission. Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes § 2.16. Mere knowledge by the landowner does not constitute implied permission; only an express or implied grant of consent negates hostility. The stem confirms no permission was given.

**Easement by necessity:** Requires prior common ownership followed by severance. Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes § 2.15. No facts suggest these parcels were ever under common ownership; necessity is unavailable as a theory.

**Key agreement:** The official key (D → now C) is correct under all three analyses. No disagreement; no NEEDS_HUMAN flag.

#### 8. choice_by_choice_walkthrough

**Choice A — No, because Daniel knew she was dumping there.**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* A says "No" — right direction. But the "because" clause points to Daniel's knowledge. The stem says Daniel never gave permission but knew. A treats knowledge as defeating Esther's claim through the hostility element. But the stem explicitly says no permission was given — that settles hostility in Esther's favor. 2. *Student label:* Right direction, wrong element — backwards.
3. *True/responsive version would look like:* "No, because Daniel's knowledge without permission does not negate hostility, and Esther's use fails for a different reason."
4. *Lawyer confirmation:* Hostile means without the owner's permission. The owner's mere awareness of the use does not constitute a grant. Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes § 2.16. **Gold Key (Hostility):** Knowledge ≠ permission; only actual consent negates hostility.

**Choice B — Yes, because Esther did this for 35 holiday seasons.**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* B says "Yes" because 35 > 20-year statute. That part is true — duration is met. 2. *Student label:* True but incomplete — dominant trap.
3. *True/responsive version would look like:* "Yes, because Esther used the property openly, without permission, continuously, and for more than the 20-year period."
4. *Lawyer confirmation:* Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes § 2.17: prescriptive use must be continuous — regular and consistent with the kind of use being claimed. Sporadic use fails continuity even over a long span. **Gold Key (Continuity):** Duration ≠ continuity. Sporadic use fails even when it spans the statutory period.

**Choice C — No, because Esther only dumped her tree waste most seasons. ← CORRECT**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* C says "No" and points to the gap-language in the stem: "most seasons, not every year." That pattern — sometimes skipped, roughly once per season — is too sporadic to be continuous use. The element that defeats Esther's claim is continuity.

3. *True/responsive version:* This IS the true and responsive answer. It correctly identifies the dispositive element (continuity) and correctly applies the fact (sporadic, skipped-year use fails continuity).
4. *Lawyer confirmation:* Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes § 2.17. Use roughly once a year, sometimes not at all, amounts to occasional trespass rather than continuous prescriptive use. The statutory period (35 years) is met but the quality of use is not. **Gold Key (Continuity)** confirms C.

**Choice D — Yes, because Esther had no other place for the debris.**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* D says "Yes" because of need. Need is real — the stem confirms Esther's lot has no room. But need alone creates no legal right. D imports a doctrine that isn't available on these facts: the stem contains no facts about common ownership between the parcels.

#### 12. remediation_card

**Card ID:** REM-RP-PRESCRIPTIVE-01
**Title:** Prescriptive Easement: Duration Is Not Continuity
**Signal:** You see the statutory period is clearly met and want to mark "Yes."
**Student move:** Before counting years, ask: *Was the use regular and consistent throughout?* "Most years, some skipped" = sporadic = fails continuity — regardless of how many years passed.
**Tiny rule:** A prescriptive easement requires continuous use — meaning regular, not just long.
**Trap it defeats:** Confusing duration (time-span met) with continuity (quality-of-use throughout).
