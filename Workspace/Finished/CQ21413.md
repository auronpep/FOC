## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

Factual impossibility is not a defense to attempt. Paul had the specific intent to kill Timothy and took a substantial step toward that goal — aiming the crossbow from close range and pulling the trigger. The fact that the crossbow string had been removed is an unknown factual circumstance that prevented completion of the murder. But if the facts had been as Paul believed (a functional crossbow), pulling the trigger would have killed Timothy. That is enough for attempt. The broken crossbow is not a defense.

**Gold Key:** Factual impossibility — when the defendant's intended crime is impossible to complete due to unknown factual circumstances — is NOT a defense to attempt. The test is: if the facts were as the defendant believed, would the completed act be a crime? If yes, attempt liability attaches.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (originally C):** This choice claims attempted murder requires an actual injury. That is simply wrong. Attempt crimes by definition involve conduct that falls short of completion. Attempted murder requires specific intent to kill plus a substantial step — not injury. A student who knows the definition of attempt can cut this immediately.

**B (originally D):** This choice says "No, but only because Paul was standing close." Distance is not the dispositive element. That is attempted murder.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing rule:** Factual impossibility is not a defense to attempt. A defendant is guilty of attempt when: (1) the defendant has the specific intent to commit the target crime, and (2) the defendant takes a substantial step toward its completion. If the crime is impossible to complete solely because of unknown factual circumstances, attempt liability still attaches. The test is whether the completed act would be a crime if the facts were as the defendant believed them to be. *See* MPC § 5.01(1)(a); *People v. Dlugash*, 41 N.Y.2d 725 (1977); *State v. Moretti*, 52 N.M. 182 (1974).

**Application:** Paul had the specific intent to kill Timothy. He aimed a crossbow from ten feet and pulled the trigger. The crossbow string had been removed — an unknown factual circumstance. If the facts had been as Paul believed (functional crossbow), pulling the trigger would have killed Timothy. Factual impossibility is not a defense. Paul is guilty of attempted murder.
