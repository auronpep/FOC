## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

Mary and Lydia bought a small downtown calligraphy studio from a retiring art teacher. The deed conveyed the studio to both of them in equal shares and said nothing about which owner could use the studio or receive use payments. Lydia began using the whole studio to store and prepare her hand-lettered Bible-verse prints. Mary kept working from a separate rented office. The fair rental value of the studio was $32,000 a year. Mary demanded $16,000 in yearly rent from Lydia. Lydia refused to pay, but told Mary that she was welcome to bring her own supplies and use the studio with her whenever Mary wanted.

Is Mary entitled to receive rent from Lydia?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

Two equal cotenants own a building. One uses the whole building, while the other works elsewhere and demands half the rental value. The user refuses to pay rent but keeps the building open for the other cotenant. Does the user owe rent?

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Equal cotenants each have the right to possess and use the whole common property, subject to the equal possessory right of the other cotenant. **Gold Key:** an occupying cotenant does not owe the other cotenant rent for personal use unless the occupying cotenant ousts or excludes the other cotenant, agrees to pay rent, or receives third-party rents. **Silver Key:** when the answer choices fight over rent, look for the access fact, not just the square footage fact.

Lydia is using the whole studio, but she has not denied Mary's equal right to use it. She did the opposite: she told Mary she could bring supplies and use the studio with her whenever Mary wanted. That open-access fact defeats rent liability. Mary is not entitled to rent from Lydia.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A** is attractive because Lydia is physically using the entire studio, and the stem gives a clean annual rental value that makes Mary's half feel easy to calculate. The breaker is the Gold Key: use of the whole property is not rent-triggering unless Lydia excludes Mary from her equal right to possess and use it. The stem gives invitation, not exclusion.

**B** is attractive because equal shares sound like equal dollars. The breaker is that equal title gives Mary an equal right to possess the studio, not an automatic right to charge Lydia rent for Lydia's personal use. The missing fact is ouster or an agreement to pay.

**D** is attractive because it reaches the correct "No" answer and points to the ownership document. The breaker is responsiveness: the deed's silence about rental proceeds does not decide the default cotenant rule. If Lydia had excluded Mary, rent could be owed even though the deed was silent.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule is unchanged: a cotenant in possession may occupy and use the entire common property without paying rent to cotenants out of possession unless there is an agreement to pay rent, an ouster or exclusion, or third-party rent that must be accounted for. The source issue remains Real Property, Ownership, Rights and duties of co-tenants, with verified outline code `82010303`.

The authority floor is met by the leading cotenancy case **Spiller v. MacKereth, 334 So. 2d 859 (Ala. 1976)**. In that case, the court stated the general rule that, absent an agreement to pay rent or an ouster, a cotenant in possession is not liable to cotenants for the value of use and occupation. The court also emphasized that rent liability requires a denial of the other cotenant's right to enter, not merely one cotenant's occupancy of the whole property. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/alabama/supreme-court/1976/334-so-2d-859-1.html

That authority confirms the official key. Mary and Lydia are equal cotenants. Lydia's use of the entire studio is consistent with a cotenant's possessory right. Mary demanded rent, not entry, and Lydia affirmatively kept entry open by telling Mary she could use the studio too. No agreement to pay rent, no third-party rent, and no ouster appear in the stem. The credited outcome is unchanged: no rent is owed.

A cotenant in possession owes no rent to the other cotenants for personal use of the common property unless the cotenant excludes them, agrees to pay rent, or receives third-party rents.
