# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question (revised stem + call)

Barnabas owned a row of craftsmen's workshops and leased one of them to Lydia, a potter. Their signed lease covered the entire unit — both the front throwing room, where Lydia shaped her vessels, and the back kiln room, where she fired them. For several months Lydia worked the whole shop and paid her rent on time. Then Barnabas, who kept his own shop next door, grew irritated that smoke from Lydia's kiln drifted through his window. He bought a padlock, carried Lydia's drying racks out of the kiln room, locked its door, and told her she was no longer allowed inside it. Lydia said Barnabas was being unreasonable, but she kept making and selling pottery from the front throwing room. She did, however, stop paying any rent until Barnabas "came to his senses." Barnabas sued Lydia for the unpaid rent. The jurisdiction followed the majority rule.

**Should the court rule in Barnabas's favor?**

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**B is correct.** Barnabas locked Lydia out of the kiln room — a part of the premises her lease entitled her to occupy. That is a partial *actual* eviction by the landlord. **Gold Key:** under the majority rule, a landlord's actual eviction of the tenant from *any* portion of the leased premises suspends the tenant's **entire** rent obligation while the eviction lasts, no matter how small the portion and even though the tenant remains in possession of the rest. Because Lydia was actually evicted from the kiln room, she owed Barnabas no rent at all, and his suit for unpaid rent fails. **Silver Key:** two choices answer "No" — pick the one that names the operative doctrine (eviction), not the one that names a feeling (unreasonable).

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A — Yes, because Lydia is entitled only to a reduction in rent proportional to the value of the kiln room.** This sells the most intuitive remedy: she lost part of the space, so dock part of the rent. Proportional abatement *is* a real rule — but for partial *constructive* eviction or a partial eviction by someone with paramount title, not for the landlord's own actual eviction. **Gold Key:** the landlord's actual eviction from any part suspends the *whole* rent, not a slice of it; that is what makes A a misstatement of the controlling majority rule.

**C — No, because Barnabas's decision to padlock the kiln room was unreasonable.** This lands on the right outcome by way of fairness, which is exactly why it is tempting — Barnabas does look petty. But a court applying the eviction rule does not weigh the landlord's reasons; the legal trigger is the physical exclusion itself, not its motive. **Silver Key:** when two choices share the credited outcome, the fairness-worded twin ("unreasonable") is the cut, and the doctrine-worded twin ("actually evicted") is the keep.

**D — Yes, because Lydia did not vacate the workshop.** This trades on the muscle-memory rule that a tenant must move out to claim an eviction defense. That requirement belongs to *constructive* eviction. **Gold Key:** for an *actual* partial eviction the tenant need not vacate — staying in the rest of the premises rent-free is precisely the tenant's entitlement — so her continued use of the throwing room does not revive the rent.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer (lawyer-confirmation layer)

**Tested rule (unchanged from the original):** Under the majority/common-law rule, when a landlord wrongfully and actually evicts the tenant from any part of the leased premises, the tenant's entire obligation to pay rent is suspended for the duration of the eviction, even though the tenant retains possession of the remainder and even though the portion taken is small. The landlord may not apportion the rent to the part still occupied.

**Authority (lawyer-confirmation only):**
- Restatement (Second) of Property: Landlord & Tenant § 6.1 (landlord's conduct depriving the tenant of a part of the leased property; suspension of rent).
- *Smith v. McEnany*, 170 Mass. 26, 48 N.E. 781 (1897) (Holmes, J.) — leading case: a landlord's partial actual eviction suspends the entire rent; "The land is hired as one whole," and the landlord cannot apportion his own wrong.

**Confirmation:** the credited outcome (landlord loses his rent claim), the legal issue (effect of a landlord's partial actual eviction on the rent duty), and the tested rule (total suspension, not abatement, with no vacatur requirement) are identical to the source item. No element of the transform moves the credited result. Not on the doctrinal-currency hotlist; the partial-actual-eviction rule is stable common law. Authority floor satisfied (Restatement section + leading case).

- **GK-RP-PARTIAL-EVICTION-RENT-01** — A landlord's actual eviction of the tenant from any part of the leased premises suspends the tenant's ENTIRE rent obligation while it lasts (no apportionment, no abatement), regardless of the portion's size or that the tenant keeps the rest. *Unlocks A.*
- **GK-RP-ACTUAL-VS-CONSTRUCTIVE-02** — Actual partial eviction does NOT require the tenant to vacate; the must-move-out rule belongs to constructive eviction. *Unlocks D.*

## Silver Key(s)

- **SK-RP-TWO-NO-DOCTRINE-01** — With two same-outcome ("No") choices, cut the feeling-worded one ("unreasonable") and keep the doctrine-worded one ("actually evicted"). *Navigates the B-vs-C tension.*
