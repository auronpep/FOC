### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Daniel used his public office to obtain money that was not owed to him. The pressure was not an ordinary bargain; it was the misuse of official signing power. Gold Key: official-position extortion turns on a public official obtaining payment through the leverage of office, not on an immediate street-style taking. Silver Key: once the stem says the permit was approved and the officer will delay signing unless paid personally, the fight is between official leverage and the neighboring theft labels.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A sells the word "fear." That is attractive because Lydia paid to avoid a bad consequence. The breaker is the source and timing of the pressure: Daniel used official delay power to obtain money, not an immediate force taking from Lydia's person or presence.

B sells the false-statement frame. That is attractive because Daniel speaks about the permit status. The breaker is that the payment is caused by the threatened delay unless he is paid personally; the operative wrong is official leverage, not a title-transfer induced by a false present fact.

D sells the autonomy story. That is attractive because Lydia physically hands over the money. The breaker is that consent extracted through wrongful official pressure is not clean consent that erases the offense.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. A public official commits extortion when the official obtains property not owed by exploiting official position or threatening official action.

The authority floor is satisfied by 18 U.S.C. 1951(b)(2), which defines extortion to include obtaining property from another, with consent, under color of official right. Evans v. United States, 504 U.S. 255 (1992), confirms the public-official form follows the common-law official-right meaning and is complete when an official obtains a payment to which the official is not entitled in return for official action. Daniel's demand is even more direct than the minimum required under that rule: he expressly conditions signing the already-approved permit on a personal payment.
