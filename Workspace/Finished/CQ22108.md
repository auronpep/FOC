## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

Martha entered an installment land contract to buy a small wooded lot from Samuel for $18,000. She agreed to pay Samuel $250 each month for 24 months. After the final payment, Samuel promised to deliver a warranty deed. During the payment period, Samuel allowed Martha to live in a small caretaker cottage on the lot and to build a private prayer-garden arbor for a neighborhood women's Bible-study group.

Twenty months into the contract, Martha discovered that Samuel had mortgaged the lot to a bank and still owed $16,000. She also learned that Samuel's landscaping business had recently failed and that he was behind on several debts. When Martha asked about the mortgage, Samuel said he would pay it off sometime within the next four months, before the final payment came due. Martha had reason to believe Samuel would not be able to clear the mortgage in time. She stopped making installment payments until Samuel paid off the mortgage.

May Martha suspend further installment payments without breaching the contract?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A buyer under an installment land contract discovers a mortgage and seller financial trouble before final payment, doubts the seller can clear title, and withholds payments. May the buyer stop interim payments before the final-payment/title-delivery date?

### 3. Final Answer Choices (A-D, post-shuffle)

A. Yes, because a mortgage is an encumbrance that makes title unmarketable as soon as the buyer discovers it.

B. No, because Samuel's duty to deliver marketable title is not due until Martha makes the final payment.

C. Yes, because Martha reasonably believes Samuel will not be able to clear the mortgage before final payment.

D. No, because Martha has been living on the lot while making the installment payments.

### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** Martha is still in the installment-payment period. Samuel promised to deliver the warranty deed after the final payment, and the facts do not say he had to furnish marketable title earlier. A seller under an installment land contract generally does not have to deliver marketable title until the buyer makes the final payment, unless the contract fixes an earlier title-delivery time. Because Samuel's title-delivery duty is not yet due, Martha cannot unilaterally suspend her interim installment payments merely because she discovered a mortgage and doubts Samuel's finances.

**Gold Key:** In an installment land contract, the buyer's interim payment duty is not suspended just because title is not yet due; the seller's marketable-title obligation matures at the final payment.

Martha's concern is not irrelevant. A mortgage is an encumbrance that can make title unmarketable at the time title must be delivered. But Martha has not reached the title-delivery date. Her safer legal path would be to seek information, assurance, escrow, or another protective remedy if available; it is not a self-help right to stop paying.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A.** This is the dominant trap. A mortgage is a real encumbrance, and an un-discharged mortgage can make title unmarketable at closing. The missing element is timing. The question asks whether Martha may stop payments before final payment, not whether the mortgage would be a problem when Samuel must deliver the deed. **Silver Key:** a mortgage is a title problem only at the wrong time if the seller still has time and a duty to clear it before delivery.

**C.** This answer turns Martha's reasonable worry into a legal excuse. Reasonable fear that Samuel may not clear the mortgage later does not, by itself, let Martha withhold payments now. The seller has not refused to perform; he has said he will pay off the mortgage before final payment. **Gold Key:** insecurity is not the same as a present title-delivery default.

**D.** This answer reaches the same result as the correct answer, but the reason is not the legal test. Martha's possession or residence on the lot may show that she is using the land during the installment period, but it does not decide whether her payment duty is suspended. **Silver Key:** possession is a timing trap, not the reason Martha must keep paying.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Right-Answer Legal Reasoning

The black-letter rule is unchanged. In an installment land contract, the buyer pays in installments and the seller delivers title after the final payment. Unless the contract requires earlier title delivery, the seller's duty to furnish marketable title arises when the buyer makes the final payment.
