### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel does not attach simply because a suspect is in police custody. It attaches only when the government has initiated formal adversary judicial proceedings — such as a formal charge, indictment, information, arraignment, or preliminary hearing. *Rothgery v. Gillespie County*, 554 U.S. 191, 198 (2008). Here, Timothy was arrested and questioned before any of those proceedings had begun. His Sixth Amendment right had not yet attached, so the absence of counsel during the interview did not violate it.

**Gold Key:** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel and the Fifth Amendment Miranda right to counsel are triggered by completely different events. Miranda is triggered by custodial interrogation (a Fifth Amendment protection against self-incrimination). The Sixth Amendment is triggered by the initiation of formal adversary judicial proceedings. Custody alone does not trigger the Sixth Amendment.

**Silver Key:** When a question asks about the Sixth Amendment right to counsel, the first thing to check is whether formal proceedings have begun. If the fact pattern describes only an arrest and police questioning — with no indictment, information, arraignment, or preliminary hearing — the Sixth Amendment has not attached, and any answer claiming a Sixth Amendment violation is wrong.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

The cognitive move: "custody = rights kick in" feels right. But the breaker is that Miranda is a Fifth Amendment doctrine, and the Sixth Amendment has its own, different attachment trigger — formal adversary proceedings. Custody alone does not satisfy that trigger. *See* **Gold Key:** Miranda and Sixth Amendment counsel rights have different triggering events.

**B (original C).** This choice tempts students who focus on the seriousness of the crime rather than the procedural posture. The cognitive move: "armed robbery is a serious felony, so all constitutional protections must apply." But the severity of the offense has nothing to do with when the Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches. The rule turns entirely on whether formal judicial proceedings have begun, not on the gravity of the charge.

**D (original D).** This choice contains a kernel of truth — the Sixth Amendment did not apply here — but states the rule too broadly. The cognitive move: "the right didn't apply in this case, so it never applies pre-trial." The breaker is that once formal proceedings have begun, the Sixth Amendment *does* apply to pre-trial questioning, including post-charge interrogations. *Massiah v. ### 8. Black-Letter Law Verification & Legal Authority

**Rule:** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches at "the initiation of adversary judicial criminal proceedings — whether by way of formal charge, preliminary hearing, indictment, information, or arraignment." *Rothgery v. Gillespie County*, 554 U.S. 191, 198 (2008). Once attached, the right applies to all "critical stages" of the prosecution, including post-charge interrogations. *Massiah v. United States*, 377 U.S. 201 (1964).

**Authority:**
- *Rothgery v. Gillespie County*, 554 U.S. 191 (2008) — the Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches at the initiation of adversary judicial proceedings.
- *Massiah v. United States*, 377 U.S. 201 (1964) — post-indictment interrogation without counsel violates the Sixth Amendment.
- *Kirby v. Illinois*, 406 U.S. 682 (1972) — the right does not attach before formal proceedings begin.
