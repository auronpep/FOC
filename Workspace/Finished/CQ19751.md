## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

After indictment for robbing the delivery desk at Lydia's family-run Christian bookstore, police place Stephen in a live lineup without notifying his lawyer or getting a waiver. Mary identifies Stephen at the lineup. At trial, Mary also wants to identify Stephen in court, saying she watched the robber for several minutes under the store's bright front lights during the robbery.

What is the best statement of the remedy?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

After indictment, police run a live lineup without counsel or waiver, and the same witness later wants to identify the defendant in court from an independent view during the crime. What remedy applies?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. The lineup violation is cured if the witness was certain during the lineup.

B. Every later identification is automatically excluded once a lineup counsel violation occurs.

C. The lineup identification is vulnerable, but the in-court identification may be admitted if the prosecution proves an independent source.

D. The lineup identification is admissible because the in-court identification might have an independent source.

### 5. Correct answer

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is the best answer. The uncounseled post-indictment live lineup is the vulnerable event, so the lineup identification can be suppressed. But the later courtroom identification is not automatically gone. Gold Key: separate the tainted lineup from the later in-court identification; the prosecution may use the later identification only if it proves an independent source. Silver Key: split the remedy before picking the answer - one part attacks the lineup ID, the other asks whether the courtroom ID has its own source.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A sells the witness-certainty shortcut. A confident witness may matter to reliability, but certainty does not cure the absence of counsel or waiver at a post-indictment live lineup. The Gold Key kills this trap: a Sixth Amendment critical-stage violation is not repaired by the witness saying she was sure.

B sells the broad suppression reflex. It notices the lineup violation and then pushes the remedy too far. The independent-source doctrine can permit a later in-court identification if the prosecution proves it rests on the witness's crime-scene observations rather than on the unlawful lineup.

D sells a half-right independent-source idea in the wrong direction. Independent source can save the later courtroom identification; it does not make the uncounseled lineup identification admissible. The Silver Key would steer off this trap by separating the two pieces of identification evidence.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule is unchanged. After adversary proceedings have begun, a live lineup is a critical stage at which the accused has a Sixth Amendment right to counsel unless that right is waived. An uncounseled post-indictment lineup can require exclusion of the lineup identification. A later in-court identification by the same witness is not automatically excluded, but the prosecution must show an origin independent of the illegal lineup.

Authority floor: U.S. Const. amend. VI; United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/388/218/; Gilbert v. California, 388 U.S. 263 (1967), https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/388/263/; Constitution Annotated, Lineups and Other Identification Situations and Right to Counsel, https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt6-6-3-4/ALDE_00013440/. Wade and Gilbert confirm the post-indictment lineup right-to-counsel problem and the independent-source hearing concept for courtroom identifications. The supplied key matches that rule.
