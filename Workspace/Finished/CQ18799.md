## 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Market-share liability is a narrow causation shortcut. It is not triggered merely because several manufacturers sold products in the same region and the plaintiff cannot identify the maker. The shortcut is built for fungible or interchangeable products whose risk profiles are the same enough that market share can approximate causal responsibility.

Gold Key: market-share liability needs a fungible, interchangeable product. When the stem stresses different materials, designs, tests, load ratings, or warnings, ordinary product-identification proof still matters.

Silver Key: in a market-share array, cut the all/never answers first, then clash market presence against interchangeability. Market presence starts the exception; interchangeability decides whether the exception can work.

Lydia's bench was not part of an interchangeable product pool. The possible benches differed in woods, hinge hardware, load tests, weight ratings, and caution cards. Because those differences change the risk profile and make market-share apportionment unfair, market-share liability is not likely to supply causation.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is confirmed: market-share liability is unlikely to supply causation where the plaintiff cannot identify the maker of an old product and the possible defendants made materially different products with different risk profiles.

The leading case is Sindell v. Abbott Laboratories, 26 Cal. 3d 588, 607 P.2d 924 (1980), which allowed market-share liability for DES where the plaintiff joined manufacturers representing a substantial share of the relevant market and liability could be apportioned by market share. See https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/26/588.html. Skipworth v. Lead Industries Ass'n, 547 Pa. 224, 690 A.2d 169 (1997), describes the Sindell factors as including identical/fungible products, inability to identify the manufacturer through no fault of the plaintiff, and joinder of substantially all manufacturers, then refuses market-share liability for lead paint because differing formulas, toxicity, and time periods would distort liability. See https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/supreme-court/1997/547-pa-224-1.html. Cornell Wex likewise states that market-share liability requires an interchangeable product and is recognized only in limited jurisdictions. See https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/market_share_liability.

Those authorities support the source answer. Signal: The plaintiff cannot identify the maker, but the stem lists different designs, materials, tests, ratings, or warnings.

Student move: Ask whether market share would fairly approximate causal responsibility.

Tiny rule: Regional sales are not enough; market-share liability needs fungible or risk-interchangeable products.

Trap: Treating market presence as causation.

## Gold Key(s)

GK-TORTS-MARKET-SHARE-FUNGIBLE-01: Market-share liability is a narrow causation shortcut for products that are fungible or interchangeable in risk; materially different products keep the plaintiff on ordinary product-identification proof.

## Silver Key(s)

SK-TORTS-MARKET-SHARE-ARRAY-01: In a market-share array, cut the all/never answers first, then clash market presence against interchangeability. Market presence starts the exception; interchangeability decides whether the exception can work.
