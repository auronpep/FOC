## 7. Right-Answer Explanation

Personal knowledge under FRE 602 means the witness directly perceived the event — saw it, heard it, smelled it, felt it. Timothy did none of that. He read a social media post. That gives him information about the flooding, but information is not perception. The trial judge must sustain the objection because there is no evidence in the record sufficient to support a finding that Timothy has personal knowledge of the water level. The absence of personal knowledge is the end of the inquiry.

**Gold Key (GK-EVID-602-01):** FRE 602 personal knowledge means direct perception — not awareness gained through a secondary source. A social media post, even an accurate one, gives the reader informational awareness of the event, not personal knowledge. These are categorically different.

**Silver Key (SK-EVID-602-01):** On a "ruling on a witness testimony" question, check personal knowledge foundation before anything else. If the witness did not perceive the fact, the objection is sustained — source accuracy and topic relevance are irrelevant to that threshold question.

## 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A — Overrule the objection, because Timothy saw the social media post about the flooding.**

The pull: Timothy didn't just hear rumors — he read an actual account of the flooding. That sounds like it gives him a basis to testify. If he knows the water was deep because the post said so, why can't he say it?

The breaker: Knowing something from a secondary source is not the same as personally perceiving it. FRE 602 requires that the witness directly observed the matter she is testifying to. A social media post tells Timothy what others reported about the water level; it does not give him personal knowledge of the water level itself. The question of foundation is answered by whether he was there and perceived it — full stop.

**Gold Key (GK-EVID-602-01):** Personal knowledge means direct perception, not informational awareness from a secondary source. This is the dominant trap: students who equate "information about the event" with "knowledge of the event" select this choice.

**B — Sustain the objection only if Timothy admits that the social media post was inaccurate.**

The pull: If Timothy believes the post was accurate, doesn't that mean his testimony would reflect accurate information? Why exclude testimony based on an accurate source?

The breaker: The objection is about personal knowledge, not about source accuracy. Even a perfectly accurate social media post cannot make Timothy a percipient witness. The court doesn't evaluate whether the post was right or wrong — it evaluates whether Timothy personally perceived the event. Accuracy of the secondary source is simply not the legal test.

**Gold Key (GK-EVID-602-03):** Under FRE 602, the personal knowledge inquiry focuses on whether the witness directly perceived the event — not whether her source of information was accurate. A witness with an accurate secondary source still lacks personal knowledge. This choice invents an "inaccurate source" condition that the rule does not recognize.

**C — Overrule the objection, because the depth of flooding is always relevant in a water damage case.**

The pull: Of course flood depth matters in a flood damage case. It is directly relevant to how much harm occurred. If it is relevant, isn't the testimony admissible?

The breaker: Relevance (FRE 401) and personal knowledge (FRE 602) are separate, independent admissibility requirements. A fact can be highly relevant and still be excluded if the witness lacks personal knowledge of it. Establishing that flood depth is relevant to Hannah's claim does nothing to cure the foundational defect that Timothy never witnessed the flooding.

**Gold Key (GK-EVID-602-02):** FRE 602 personal knowledge is a foundation requirement, completely independent of FRE 401 relevance. Relevance cannot supply personal knowledge. A relevant topic testified to by a witness with no personal knowledge remains objectionable under FRE 602.

**Silver Key (SK-EVID-602-01):** Check personal knowledge foundation before relevance. If the witness wasn't there, the foundation objection is sustained regardless of whether the topic is relevant.

## 9. Black-Letter Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** Federal Rule of Evidence 602 provides: "A witness may testify to a matter only if evidence is introduced sufficient to support a finding that the witness has personal knowledge of the matter." Personal knowledge means the witness perceived the matter through her own senses — sight, sound, touch, smell. The rule does not permit testimony based on what the witness read, heard from others, or saw reported on secondary sources.

**Applied:** Timothy states he learned about the flooding only from a social media post and was not at the facility. There is no evidence in the record sufficient to support a finding that he has personal knowledge of the water level. The objection must be sustained under FRE 602.

**Why not overrule on relevance (C):** FRE 401 relevance and FRE 602 personal knowledge are independent requirements. Meeting one does not satisfy the other. See FRE 602 Advisory Committee Notes ("The rule requiring that a witness who testifies to a fact which can be perceived by the senses must have had an opportunity to observe, and must have actually observed the fact").

**Why not overrule because of the social media post (A):** FRE 602 requires perception, not information. A secondary source gives awareness, not personal knowledge. Courts apply FRE 602 to exclude lay testimony where the only basis is something the witness read or heard — not something the witness directly observed.

**Why not condition the ruling on source accuracy (B):** FRE 602 asks only whether the witness perceived the fact. Source accuracy is not part of the personal knowledge analysis. Even if the social media post was entirely accurate, Timothy still lacks personal knowledge. No case authority recognizes an "accurate secondary source" exception to FRE 602.

**Authority:** FRE 602 (text and Advisory Committee Notes); Christopher B. Mueller & Laird C. Kirkpatrick, Federal Evidence § 6:5 (treatise) (personal knowledge requires direct sensory perception). No fabricated case citations included; rule text is the primary authority.

#### Section 12 — Remediation Card

**Card:** EVID-602-01

**Title:** FRE 602: Personal Knowledge = Direct Perception

**Signal:** A witness admits he learned about an event only by reading or hearing about it and was not present.

**Student move:** Ask one question: Did the witness directly perceive this fact with her own senses? If no — sustain the foundation objection. Do not ask whether the topic is relevant (different rule) or whether the source was accurate (not the test).

**Tiny rule:** Personal knowledge = direct sensory perception, not awareness gained through secondary sources. Relevance does not cure a foundation defect. Source accuracy does not supply personal knowledge.

**Trap to watch:** "He saw the social media post" sounds like a basis for testimony. It is not. Reading a post about an event gives you information; it does not make you a percipient witness to the event.

---

## Gold Keys

**GK-EVID-602-01 — Personal Knowledge = Direct Perception, Not Secondary Source Awareness**
FRE 602 personal knowledge means direct perception of the event — seeing it, hearing it, touching it. A secondary source (newspaper, social media post, radio broadcast) gives the reader informational awareness, not personal knowledge. These are categorically different, and only direct perception qualifies.
*Type: distinction | Unlocks: A (dominant trap) | Trigger: witness with only a secondary source account is called to testify to a fact she did not personally observe*
*Authority (lawyer confirmation only): FRE 602 text and Advisory Committee Notes; Mueller & Kirkpatrick, Federal Evidence § 6:5*

**GK-EVID-602-02 — Relevance Cannot Cure a Personal Knowledge Defect**
FRE 602 personal knowledge is a foundation requirement independent of FRE 401 relevance. A fact can be highly relevant and still require exclusion if the witness lacks personal knowledge. Relevance cannot cure a foundation defect.
*Type: rule | Unlocks: C | Trigger: overrule answer citing relevance in response to a personal knowledge objection*
*Authority (lawyer confirmation only): FRE 602; FRE 401*

**GK-EVID-602-03 — Source Accuracy Is Irrelevant to FRE 602**
Under FRE 602, the personal knowledge inquiry focuses on whether the witness directly perceived the event — not whether her source of information was accurate. Even a perfectly accurate social media post does not supply personal knowledge. FRE 602 recognizes no "accurate secondary source" exception.
*Type: rule | Unlocks: B | Trigger: sustain-with-condition answer linking the ruling to source accuracy*
*Authority (lawyer confirmation only): FRE 602 text; Advisory Committee Notes*

## Silver Keys

**SK-EVID-602-01 — Foundation Before Relevance**
On a "ruling on objection to witness testimony" question, check personal knowledge foundation before relevance. If there is no evidence the witness directly perceived the fact, the foundation objection is sustained — source accuracy and topic relevance do not enter the analysis.
*Type: call_focus | Navigates: relevance distractor (C) and accuracy-condition distractor (B) | Trigger: overrule answer citing relevance, or sustain conditioned on source accuracy*
