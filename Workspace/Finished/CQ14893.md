### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** The defense is trying to use two prior trailer-safety incidents to make Paul look careless and therefore less believable about this collision. That is not the channel Rule 608(b) opens. Specific acts may be inquired into on cross-examination only when they are probative of the witness's character for truthfulness or untruthfulness. Towing with an unsecured coupler is careless, but it is not deceitful.

The prior incidents also do not contradict Paul's testimony. Paul said he latched the coupler and towed carefully on this trip. Two separate incidents from other trips do not make that statement false. **Gold Key:** carelessness is not veracity, and other-occasion carelessness is not contradiction. **Silver Key:** when a choice says "credibility," ask what kind of credibility attack it is; a broad credibility label is not enough.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. No, because there is no indication that the prior trailer-coupler citations led to convictions.**

This answer reaches the right bottom line by grabbing the wrong gate. The absence of a conviction is not what keeps the questions out. Rule 608(b) can allow inquiry into non-conviction conduct on cross-examination when the conduct is probative of truthfulness. The breaker is narrower and stronger: unsecured-coupler citations show carelessness, not truthfulness, and they do not contradict Paul's statement about this trip.

**B. Yes, because the two recent unsecured-coupler incidents tend to contradict Paul's claim that he towed carefully when the collision occurred.**

This answer is attractive because the prior incidents resemble the current safety dispute. The breaker is the **Silver Key:** contradiction must collide with the testimony given in this case. Paul did not say he had never towed unsafely; he said he latched the coupler and towed carefully on this occasion. Other-occasion carelessness does not contradict that statement.

**D. Yes, because towing with an unsecured coupler on other occasions bears on Paul's credibility, since he claims he secured the coupler before this collision.**

This is the dominant trap. It sells a broad "credibility" move: if Paul did the same careless thing before, maybe the jury should doubt him now. The breaker is the **Gold Key:** Rule 608(b) is limited to truthfulness character, not general carelessness, and Rule 404 blocks using prior careless acts to show conduct in conformity in a civil case. A prior unsecured-coupler citation may make Paul look like a careless tower, but that is exactly the propensity inference the rule forbids.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested issue remains Evidence, Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence, prohibited uses of character evidence. The verified outline code is `32020402`, which appears in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md` as `Relevance > Character Evidence > Prohibited uses of character evidence`.

Federal Rule of Evidence 404(a)(1) bars character evidence when offered to prove that a person acted in accordance with that character or trait on a particular occasion. Federal Rule of Evidence 404(b)(1) similarly bars other acts when offered to prove character and conduct in accordance with that character. Federal Rule of Evidence 608(b) allows inquiry into specific instances of conduct on cross-examination only if those acts are probative of the witness's character for truthfulness or untruthfulness.

Application to the credited answer: Paul's prior unsecured-coupler incidents are specific acts of carelessness. They are not acts of deceit, lying, fraud, or other conduct bearing on truthfulness. They also do not contradict Paul's testimony that he latched the coupler and towed carefully on the trip involved in this collision. The defense theory is therefore either impermissible propensity reasoning under FRE 404 or an overbroad credibility theory outside FRE 608(b). The court should not allow the questioning.
