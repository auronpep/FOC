## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

In federal proceedings, privilege is governed by Federal Rule of Evidence 501, which uses federal common law unless the Constitution, a federal statute, Supreme Court rules, or state-law rule-of-decision principles provide otherwise. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) Federal common law recognizes a confidential marital communications privilege; *SEC v. Lavin* states the core prerequisites as a communication, a valid marriage at the time, confidence, and no waiver. ([OpenJurist][2]) *Wolfle v. United States* recognized the privilege’s rationale as protection of the marital relationship and involved a letter to a wife; its admissibility result turned on the stenographer/third-party problem, not an oral-only limitation. ([Justia Law][3]) *Trammel* also distinguishes confidential marital communications from broader adverse spousal testimony; the wife’s willingness to testify does not erase protection for a confidential communication. ([Justia Law][4])
