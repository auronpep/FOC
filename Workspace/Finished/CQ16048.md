## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**Answer D is correct.** The defendant is guilty of rape.

*Rape* is sexual intercourse without the victim's consent. Here, the church member's compliance was compelled by the defendant's threat of death — her apparent "consent" was the product of overwhelming coercion and is no consent at all. The intercourse was therefore without her consent.

The defendant did not himself perform the sex act, but he is guilty of rape under **accomplice (principal) liability**. One who commands, induces, or procures another to commit a crime is liable as a principal. The defendant's gunpoint order to the pastor was the proximate cause of the rape, and the defendant possessed the requisite criminal intent.

**Gold Key — Solicitation Merger:** When a solicited crime is actually completed, solicitation merges into the substantive crime. The defendant cannot be convicted of both solicitation to commit rape and rape. The solicitation "disappears" into the completed offense.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### Answer A: "Both solicitation to commit rape and rape"

**What makes it attractive:** The defendant did solicit the pastor, and rape did occur. This feels like the most "complete" answer — it captures both the inchoate and the substantive crime. This is the **dominant trap** because students who see both elements often fail to apply merger.

**The breaker:** While the defendant's conduct satisfies the elements of solicitation, the merger doctrine bars separate conviction for solicitation once the crime is completed. The defendant is guilty of rape (via accomplice liability), but the solicitation charge merges and cannot stand alone. **Gold Key:** Solicitation merges into the completed crime.

### Answer B: "Neither solicitation to commit rape nor rape"

**What makes it attractive:** The defendant didn't personally have intercourse with the victim, and the pastor and member "complied" rather than physically resisting. A student who requires physical resistance for rape, or who thinks the defendant must personally commit the sex act, will pick this.

**The breaker:** Consent obtained by threat of death is no consent at all. And under accomplice liability, one who commands another to commit a crime with the requisite intent is guilty as a principal. The defendant is guilty of rape.

### Answer C: "Solicitation to commit rape only"

**What makes it attractive:** The defendant clearly *asked* (commanded) the pastor to commit rape, which looks like textbook solicitation. A student who spots the solicitation element but forgets merger will pick this.

**The breaker:** This choice says "solicitation *only*" — but the crime was completed. Under the merger doctrine, solicitation merges into the completed crime. The defendant is not chargeable with solicitation as a separate offense. **Gold Key:** Solicitation merges into the completed crime; you cannot be convicted of both.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

### Rape by Coercion

At common law, rape is unlawful sexual intercourse with a person without that person's consent by means of force or threat of force. Consent obtained through threats of death or serious bodily harm is legally no consent. The Model Penal Code (§ 213.1) similarly defines sexual assault to include compelling sexual intercourse by "force or threat of force" that would prevent resistance by a person of reasonable resolution. See MPC § 213.1(1)(a).

### Accomplice Liability (Commanding Another)

Under the common law and modern statutes, one who aids, abets, counsels, commands, induces, or procures the commission of a crime is liable as a principal. See 18 U.S.C. § 2(a) ("Whoever commits an offense against the United States or aids, abets, counsels, commands, induces or procures its commission, is punishable as a principal."). The Wex legal dictionary defines a principal in the second degree as one "present at the scene of a crime and aids, abets, or encourages the commission of the crime with the required criminal intent." *See* LII/Wex, "principal in the second degree."

The defendant was present, commanded the pastor at gunpoint, and possessed the requisite intent — satisfying all elements of accomplice liability for rape.

### Solicitation Merger

Under the majority/common-law rule, when a person solicits another to commit a crime and the solicited person actually commits it, the solicitation merges into the completed crime. The defendant cannot be separately convicted of both solicitation and the completed offense. *See* LawShelf, "Solicitation" ("a solicitation of a person to commit a crime 'merges' with the completed crime. A person cannot be convicted of both solicitation to commit a specific crime and the completed crime."); JRank, "Criminal Law — Merger" ("One who solicits another to commit a crime may not be convicted of both the solicitation and the completed crime.").

### Verdict

The tested rule (rape via accomplice liability + solicitation merger), the legal issue (whether a defendant who commands rape by coercion is guilty of rape and/or solicitation), and the credited outcome (rape only) are all **unchanged** from the original. Research confirms the official key.
