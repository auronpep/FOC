### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. The printer did more than generally challenge Lydia's story; the printer properly used a recent perjury conviction, which directly attacks Lydia's character for truthfulness. Gold Key: once a witness's character for truthfulness has been attacked, Federal Rule of Evidence 608(a) permits support by reputation or opinion testimony about truthful character. Hannah's opinion testimony is therefore a permitted rehabilitative form, so the objection should be overruled.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because students remember that specific bad acts can matter in witness-impeachment problems and may overcorrect by looking for a concrete event rather than a character-opinion witness. The breaker is the form rule: Gold Key: truthful-character support under Rule 608(a), after an attack, is by reputation or opinion testimony; specific truthful acts are not the required direct-support form.

C is attractive because it sounds like a foundation requirement: if Hannah will support Lydia after a perjury conviction, maybe Hannah must have seen the perjury episode. The breaker is that Hannah is not being offered as an eyewitness to the old perjury; she is giving an opinion about Lydia's character for truthfulness.

D is attractive because "improper bolstering" is a real danger before credibility has been attacked. The breaker is the absolute word "never": a conviction used to impeach truthfulness can be the attack that opens the door to truthful-character rehabilitation.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome remain unchanged from the source row. The issue is Evidence, Presentation of Evidence, Impeachment, Opinion or reputation, and the verified outline code is `31010505`.

Federal Rule of Evidence 608(a) permits a witness's credibility to be attacked or supported by reputation or opinion testimony about the witness's character for truthfulness or untruthfulness, but evidence of truthful character is admissible only after that character has been attacked. Federal Rule of Evidence 609(a)(2) provides that evidence of a conviction must be admitted for impeachment when the court can readily determine that the crime required proving, or the witness admitted, a dishonest act or false statement. Perjury is a classic false-statement crime. The Advisory Committee note to Rule 608 also treats misconduct, including conviction of crime, as an attack that can open truthful-character support. Authority checked: Fed. R. Evid. 608(a), Fed. R. Evid. 609(a)(2), and the Advisory Committee material reproduced at https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_608 and https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_609.

Because Lydia's perjury conviction attacked her character for truthfulness, Barnabas may rehabilitate her with Hannah's opinion testimony. The official key is confirmed.
