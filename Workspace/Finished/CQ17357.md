## 1. Final question

Lydia received a fenced one-acre desert parcel as payment for designing Scripture bookmarks for a private retreat center. Later, the state created a night-sky and tortoise-corridor preserve that required the parcel to remain untouched. The rule permanently barred Lydia from building a tiny Psalm Herbs stand, camping, growing herbs, grazing goats, leasing the ground, parking a food cart, or making any other economically productive use of the parcel. The state did not show that any proposed use was already barred by background nuisance or property principles. Lydia seeks compensation. Which is the best result?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

When a state regulation permanently bars every productive use of an owner's land and no background property or nuisance rule already barred those uses, is compensation likely required?

## 3. Final answer choices (A-D, post-shuffle)

- **A:** Lydia is not entitled to compensation because regulations protecting land in its natural condition can never be takings.
- **B:** Lydia is entitled to compensation only if the state takes possession of the parcel or authorizes someone else to physically enter it.
- **C:** Lydia is likely entitled to compensation because the regulation leaves the parcel with no economically beneficial or productive use.
- **D:** Lydia is not entitled to compensation unless she proves the parcel lost at least half its market value.

## 5. Correct answer

**C** is correct. The original key was **A**.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

C is correct. The regulation does not merely limit one planned project; it permanently leaves Lydia with no economically beneficial or productive use of the parcel. Gold Key: a total regulatory wipeout is a categorical taking unless the barred use was already outside the owner's title because background nuisance or property principles would have forbidden it anyway. Silver Key: do not let the physical-taking answer crowd out the separate total-regulatory-taking route.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A sells a comforting conservation shortcut: if the state is protecting land in its natural condition, no compensation is ever owed. The breaker is the word "never." Environmental purpose does not defeat compensation when a regulation wipes out all economically productive use and no background-principles defense applies.

B sells the dominant trap by remembering a real takings route: physical occupation or compelled access can require compensation. The breaker is that physical occupation is not the only route. Gold Key: Lucas also protects against a regulation that leaves land with no economically beneficial use, so this "only if" answer skips the tested route.

D is attractive because value-loss numbers sound like the ordinary regulatory-takings balancing world. The breaker is the invented threshold. The rule here turns on total economic wipeout, not a fixed 50-percent line.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

Authority floor: the Fifth Amendment Takings Clause requires just compensation when private property is taken for public use. [Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/505/1003), is the governing authority for this item: a regulation that deprives land of all economically beneficial or productive use is treated as a categorical taking unless the restriction merely reflects background principles of property or nuisance law. [Cedar Point Nursery v. Hassid, 594 U.S. ___ (2021)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/20-107_ihdj.pdf), confirms that physical appropriation is a separate per se takings category, but it does not make physical occupation the only way to get compensation. For non-total use restrictions, Penn Central balancing remains the ordinary framework; the Constitution Annotated summary of regulatory takings and [Penn Central](https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt5-9-6/ALDE_00013285/) confirms that the inquiry is not a mechanical percentage threshold. [Tyler v. Hennepin County, 598 U.S. 631 (2023)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/22-166_8n59.pdf), concerns retention of surplus tax-foreclosure value and does not alter this Lucas total-regulatory-taking result.

The supplied official key is A before shuffle; after the required choice shuffle, the credited answer is C. The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: a permanent regulation that eliminates every economically beneficial use of land is likely compensable unless background nuisance or property principles already barred the use. Doctrinal-currency check: the takings hotlist is triggered, but Cedar Point and Tyler do not displace Lucas on this total-use-deprivation fact pattern. Bank validation verdict: PASS.
