Daniel and Lydia agree to steal a boxed set of antique handbells from a private Christmas-market booth run by Barnabas. That evening, they carry out the plan and take the handbells from the booth. ### 7. Full right-answer explanation

D is correct. Gold Key: conspiracy is punished for the unlawful agreement, so it normally remains a separate offense even if the target crime is completed. Daniel and Lydia made an agreement to commit larceny, then completed the theft. If the prosecution proves both the agreement and the larceny, convictions for both may stand.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A pulls because the target crime is a theft of personal property. The breaker is that conspiracy non-merger does not depend on whether the target offense is a property crime.

B pulls because federal ownership sounds like a jurisdictional switch. The breaker is that the merger issue is not controlled by who owned the booth.

C pulls because merger is a real idea for some inchoate offenses. Gold Key: conspiracy is the major exception students must keep separate; it punishes the agreement and does not merge merely because the target crime was completed.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged. The general rule is that conspiracy and the completed substantive offense are separate crimes; conspiracy punishes the agreement and the distinct social danger created by concerted criminal action. In Iannelli v. United States, 420 U.S. 770 (1975), the Supreme Court described Wharton's Rule as an exception to the general principle that conspiracy and the substantive offense that is its immediate end do not merge upon proof of the completed offense. Pinkerton v. United States, 328 U.S. 640 (1946), likewise treats conspiracy as separate from substantive offenses committed in furtherance of it. Those authorities confirm the source key: Daniel and Lydia may be convicted of both conspiracy to commit larceny and the completed larceny if both are proved.
