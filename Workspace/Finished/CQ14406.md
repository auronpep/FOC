# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question — revised stem + call

Preparing for a fall youth-ministry expo, **Lydia Supply**, a private retreat-supply reseller, sent **Barnabas Crafts** a purchase order for **1,200 battery-powered Advent lantern kits**. Barnabas received the order by courier on **May 6**. The form called for delivery no earlier than **August 20** and no later than **September 10**, but a bold box above the signature line read: **“Lydia Supply reserves the right to cancel this order at any time before August 12.”** On **May 8**, Barnabas mailed Lydia Supply a signed reply stating, **“We accept your order,”** and Lydia Supply received it on **May 9**.

As of **May 10**, which statement is accurate as to whether a contract was formed?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

Buyer orders goods but reserves an unrestricted right to cancel before the delivery window; seller accepts; before the cutoff, was a contract formed?

## 3. Final answer choices

A. No contract was formed, because Lydia Supply’s purchase order was merely a revocable offer.

B. A contract was formed, but before August 12 it was an option contract cancelable only at Lydia Supply’s election.

C. No contract was formed, because Lydia Supply reserved the right to cancel the order.

D. A contract was formed, but before August 12 it was terminable at the election of either party.

## 5. Correct answer

**C**. Original key: **A**.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Lydia Supply did not merely state a delivery schedule or reserve an ordinary remedy. It reserved the right to cancel the order at any time before August 12, before performance was due. That means Lydia Supply had not made a real commitment to buy the lantern kits as of May 10.

**Gold Key:** A buyer’s apparent promise is illusory when the buyer reserves an unrestricted right to cancel before it must perform; the seller’s acceptance does not convert that escape hatch into an option contract.

**Silver Key:** Decide formation before labels. The answer set tries to pull the student into “revocable offer” and “option contract” labels, but the first question is whether the cancellation reservation leaves any binding buyer promise.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice attracts the student who sees that Lydia Supply’s order could have been withdrawn before acceptance and treats “revocable” as the end of the analysis. The breaker is the call date: Barnabas accepted and no cancellation fact appears, so revocability before acceptance is not the formation problem.

**B.** This is the dominant trap. It sees the one-sided cancellation power and gives it a familiar contract label: option contract. **Gold Key:** the one-sided cancellation escape belongs on the illusory-promise track, not the option-contract track; the label cannot create a contract when Lydia Supply’s own commitment is optional.

**D.** This choice makes the cancellation clause look balanced. The breaker is visible from the stem: only Lydia Supply reserved the right to cancel. Barnabas did not.

## 9. Black-letter verification and legal reasoning

The uploaded source row supplies QID 14406, the official key, the original pick rates, the stem, the four choices, and the official explanations. 

Article 2 allows a sales contract to be formed by any manner sufficient to show agreement, and an offer can generally invite acceptance by any reasonable medium; those rules explain why Barnabas’s written acceptance would normally matter. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) But UCC § 1-103(b) preserves supplementary principles of law and equity, including validating and invalidating contract doctrines, unless displaced by the UCC. ([Legal Information Institute][2]) The illusory-promise rule is such a supplementary doctrine: Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 77 states that an apparent promise is not consideration when the promisor reserves optional performance, and its comment explains that words making performance entirely optional do not constitute a promise. ([Open Casebook][3])

That confirms the credited outcome. Lydia Supply’s unrestricted pre-performance cancellation right makes its apparent promise to buy illusory as of May 10. The option/firm-offer contrast does not rescue the formed-contract answer: UCC § 2-205 concerns a merchant’s signed assurance that an offer will be held open, not a buyer’s reservation that it may cancel at will. ([Legal Information Institute][4])
