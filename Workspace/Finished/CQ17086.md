### 7. Full right-answer explanation
A is correct. Rule 609(a)(2) normally makes a conviction involving a dishonest act or false statement powerful impeachment material, but this stem gives the later fact that controls the result. Gold Key: under Rule 609(c)(2), a conviction that has been pardoned, annulled, or cleared by an equivalent procedure based on a finding of innocence is not admissible for impeachment under Rule 609. Silver Key: when the stem gives both a crime label and a later cleanup order, read the cleanup order last; it may decide admissibility before the crime label matters. Ruth's conviction was annulled because she was found innocent, so the court should exclude it.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
B is attractive because it sells the ordinary dishonesty-crime route: embezzlement sounds like exactly the kind of conviction that attacks truthfulness. The breaker is the Gold Key: an innocence-based annulment shuts down Rule 609 impeachment even when the original crime carried a dishonesty label.

C is attractive because conviction-impeachment rules often make students think about special protection for criminal defendants. The breaker is that the innocence-based exclusion is attached to the conviction itself, not to whether the witness is a criminal defendant.

D is attractive because "annulment" can sound like a sentencing or punishment cleanup that leaves credibility impeachment untouched. The breaker is that Rule 609(c) speaks directly to impeachment admissibility and makes the conviction inadmissible when the annulment rests on innocence.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Evidence, Presentation of Evidence, Impeachment, Conviction of crime, and the verified outline code is `31010504`. Federal Rule of Evidence 609(a)(2) covers convictions whose elements required proving, or the witness admitted, a dishonest act or false statement. That is the bait behind the embezzlement/fraud-style answer. But Federal Rule of Evidence 609(c)(2) controls the result: a conviction is not admissible for Rule 609 impeachment when it has been the subject of a pardon, annulment, or equivalent procedure based on a finding of innocence. The U.S. Courts December 1, 2024 Federal Rules of Evidence packet states Rule 609(c)(2) in that form, and Cornell LII's current Rule 609 text matches it. Sources checked: https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-evidence-dec-1-2024_0.pdf and https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_609. Current-rule drift check: the U.S. Courts pending amendments page lists Evidence Rule 609 in the December 1, 2027 pipeline, but the researched current rule used for this item still contains the innocence-based annulment exclusion; no researched source contradicted the supplied official key. Source checked: https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/pending-rules-and-forms-amendments.
