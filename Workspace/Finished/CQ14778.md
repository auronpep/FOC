If a hiring defendant offers a former supervisor's warning only to explain why he refused to hire the plaintiff, is the warning hearsay or admissible for the hiring reason?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. Peter is not offering Ruth's warning to prove that Naomi really could not handle the predawn route. He is offering it to show what he heard and why he refused to hire her. Gold Key: an out-of-court statement is hearsay only when offered for its truth; when the statement is offered to show its effect on the listener or the listener's reason for acting, the hearsay rule is not triggered. Silver Key: when the answer pair fights over "true fact" versus "reason for action," decide the purpose of the offer first.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because it reaches the word "admissible" and then tries to use Ruth's warning as proof that Naomi lacked the physical capacity for the route. The breaker is purpose: the statement is admissible to show Peter's reason, not to prove Naomi actually could not do the work.

B is the dominant trap because the warning is an out-of-court statement and "hearsay not within an exception" sounds like the Evidence reflex. The breaker is the Gold Key: no hearsay exception is needed when the statement is offered for its effect on Peter and his reason for refusing to hire Naomi, rather than for Ruth's assertion to be true.

D is attractive because Peter did not personally observe Naomi's work at the bindery. The breaker is the testimony target: Peter has personal knowledge that he heard Ruth's warning and that it affected his decision; he is not being asked to prove Naomi's actual lifting endurance from personal observation.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, trap structure, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. The governing rule is FRE 801(c): hearsay requires an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted. Ruth's warning is relevant under FRE 401 and FRE 402 because Peter's asserted nondiscriminatory reason for refusing to hire Naomi is a consequential fact in the age-discrimination dispute. Offered for that reason-purpose, the warning is not hearsay; it is admissible to show Peter's state of knowledge, motive, or reason for acting. FRE 602 does not defeat the testimony because Peter has personal knowledge that he received the warning and relied on it; the rule would matter if Peter were trying to prove Naomi's actual work capacity from facts he did not observe. Authority checked: Federal Rules of Evidence 801(c), 401, 402, and 602 in the current U.S. Courts Federal Rules of Evidence, December 1, 2024, https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-evidence-dec-1-2024_0.pdf.
