## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** Under FRE 608(b), a witness may be cross-examined about specific instances of conduct that are probative of the witness's character for truthfulness or untruthfulness. The prior false testimony in the divorce proceeding is an act of dishonesty directly probative of truthfulness, so the cross-examination question was proper. The fact that the questioning occurred on cross-examination is what makes this permissible — the questioner need not introduce extrinsic evidence and is bound by the witness's answer on the stand.

**Silver Key:** When you see a witness asked about a prior bad act on cross-examination, check the form first. If the question is asked directly on cross, it is allowed under FRE 608(b) as long as the act is probative of truthfulness. The trap answer will try to drag you into the extrinsic-evidence rule — but that rule only applies when someone tries to *prove* the bad act through documents or other witnesses, not when the question itself is asked on cross.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (originally C) — DOMINANT TRAP.** This is the most attractive wrong answer because it sounds like a real evidence rule. The "collateral issue" language triggers something students have heard about impeachment. But here's the breaker: the common-law collateral-issue rule only bars *extrinsic evidence* offered to impeach on a collateral matter. It does NOT bar cross-examination questions themselves. FRE 608(b) explicitly permits inquiry on cross-examination into specific instances of conduct probative of truthfulness. The transcript is extrinsic evidence, but the question on cross is not — and the question is what is being challenged here. The choice misapplies the collateral-issue ban to the cross-examination question itself, which is exactly the trap.

**Silver Key:** The collateral-issue rule is a rule about *extrinsic evidence*, not about *questions on cross*. If the stem says "asked on cross," the collateral-issue bar does not apply to the question — only to bringing in outside proof.

**C (originally D).** This choice says character for truthfulness cannot be attacked by specific instances of conduct. That is the opposite of what FRE 608(b) says. The rule *permits* exactly this kind of attack on cross-examination. The breaker is straightforward: FRE 608(b) was enacted specifically to allow inquiry into specific instances of conduct probative of truthfulness, so long as extrinsic evidence is not used to prove them.

**D (originally B).** This choice reaches the right outcome (admissible) but for the wrong reason. It says the transcript is admissible because it is self-authenticating documentary evidence. But the transcript is extrinsic evidence offered to prove a prior bad act — and FRE 608(b) flatly prohibits extrinsic evidence for that purpose. The question on cross is what is admissible, not the transcript. The choice confuses the admissibility of the cross-examination question with the admissibility of the extrinsic document.

## 8. Black-Letter Legal Verification

**Rule:** Federal Rule of Evidence 608(b) provides that a witness's character for truthfulness may be attacked (or supported) by testimony about specific instances of conduct, but only if the conduct is probative of truthfulness or untruthfulness. Extrinsic evidence is not admissible to prove such specific instances of conduct. The questioning party is bound by the witness's answer on cross-examination.

**Authority:**
- FRE 608(b): "Except for a criminal conviction under Rule 609, extrinsic evidence is not admissible to prove specific instances of a witness's conduct in order to attack or support the witness's character for truthfulness. But the court may, on cross-examination, allow them to be inquired into if they are probative of the character for truthfulness or untruthfulness of: the witness or another witness whose character the witness being cross-examined has testified about."
- *United States v. Goings*, 313 F.3d 423, 426–27 (8th Cir. 2002) (FRE 608(b) allows cross-examination about specific instances of conduct concerning truthfulness but forbids extrinsic evidence to prove the act).
- *United States v. Matthews*, 168 F.3d 1234, 1244 (11th Cir. 1999) (if witness denies conduct, it may not be proved by extrinsic evidence; the party must take the witness's answer).
- Advisory Committee Notes to FRE 608(b): extrinsic evidence offered for other grounds of impeachment (contradiction, bias, prior inconsistent statement) remains governed by Rules 402 and 403, not 608(b).
