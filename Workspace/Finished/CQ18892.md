## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam register)

**B is correct.** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel "attaches" — that is, becomes operational against the government — at the start of formal adversary judicial criminal proceedings. *Kirby v. Illinois*, 406 U.S. 682 (1972). Those proceedings are not limited to indictment or jury selection. They include an initial appearance before a judicial officer at which the defendant is told the charge against him and his liberty is restricted. *Rothgery v. Gillespie County*, 554 U.S. 191 (2008). On the facts given, the magistrate's hearing did exactly that: she read the formal theft charge, set bond, and committed Mark to jail when he could not post. That hearing is the kind of formal judicial proceeding that starts the adversary process, so the Sixth Amendment had already attached by the time Detectives Daniel and Barnabas showed up to question him. Their questioning therefore violated Mark's Sixth Amendment right to counsel, and Mark is likely correct.

> **Gold Key (Sixth Amendment attachment):** The right attaches the moment adversary judicial criminal proceedings begin — including an initial appearance where the charge is stated and liberty is restricted. It does **not** wait for indictment, jury selection, or trial.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations (one per distractor)

### A. "Yes, because an arrest by itself always attaches the Sixth Amendment."

**Why it is attractive.** Arrest is dramatic and custodial, and many students blur the Fifth Amendment's *Miranda* rule (which is triggered by custodial interrogation) with the Sixth Amendment's right to counsel. If Miranda attaches at custody-plus-interrogation, the easy slide is to assume the Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches at custody alone. The word "always" reinforces that overconfident slide.

**What kills it.** A bare arrest is investigative, not adversarial judicial process. *Kirby v. Illinois* held that the Sixth Amendment does not apply to identification procedures conducted before the initiation of formal adversary judicial proceedings, and that the right attaches only when the government has formally committed to prosecute. The split comes from a stem fact that is easy to miss if you stopped reading after "arrested": the magistrate's initial appearance. The added facts — charge read, bond set, defendant jailed because he could not post — are the formal judicial proceeding that actually triggered attachment. Strip those and the trap might land; with them in the stem, A is the classic 5th/6th Amendment confusion and is wrong. **Gold Key:** *Sixth Amendment attachment is a judicial-officer event, not a custodial event.*

### C. "No, because bail hearings are never part of criminal proceedings."

**Why it is attractive.** A student who thinks "bail hearing = pre-charge, pre-trial, not real court" can convince themselves that anything called a bail setting is administrative and outside the adversary process. The word "never" reads as a clean rule.

**What kills it.** A bail hearing before a judicial officer at which a charged defendant appears and liberty is restricted is part of adversary criminal process for Sixth Amendment purposes. The Supreme Court in *Rothgery* treated the initial appearance — which often includes the bail decision — as the attachment event. The categorical "never" is the giveaway: it is a tiered absolute that the case law simply rejects.

### D. "No, because the Sixth Amendment attaches only after a jury is sworn."

**Why it is attractive.** "The Sixth Amendment is the trial amendment" is a common student shorthand. From that shorthand it is a small step to assume the right cannot possibly exist before trial begins. "Only after a jury is sworn" sounds principled.

**What kills it.** *Kirby* rejected the idea that the Sixth Amendment is a trial-stage right for attachment purposes. The right attaches when the government commits to prosecution through formal judicial process, which is normally far earlier than jury selection. The "only after jury sworn" rule is a fabricated threshold, and "only" is a tiered absolute that the case law does not support. **Gold Key:** *Attachment tracks the start of adversary process, not the start of trial.*

## 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

**Tested rule.** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches at the start of formal adversary judicial criminal proceedings, including — and this is the key modern development — an initial appearance before a judicial officer at which the defendant is informed of the charge and liberty is restricted.

**Authority.** *Kirby v. Illinois*, 406 U.S. 682 (1972), is the foundational case. *Rothgery v. Gillespie County*, 554 U.S. 191 (2008), expressly held that the Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches at the initial appearance before a magistrate. *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), and *Massiah v. United States*, 377 U.S. 201 (1964), supply the related principle that, once attached, the right forbids the government from deliberately eliciting statements from the defendant outside the presence of counsel. Together these cases establish (1) the attachment event and (2) the consequence of attachment (no uncounseled interrogation on the charged offense). The Sixth Amendment itself supplies the constitutional text.

**Application to the credited answer.** Mark was arrested overnight. The next morning a magistrate told him the formal theft charge, set bond, and ordered him held when he could not post. That is an initial appearance before a judicial officer with both the charge-and-information element and the liberty-restriction element that *Rothgery* requires. The Sixth Amendment therefore attached at that initial appearance. By the time Detectives Daniel and Barnabas questioned him later that afternoon — without counsel — the right had attached, the questioning was "deliberate elicitation" of statements on the charged offense, and Mark's Sixth Amendment was violated. The legal substance of the original question, the legal issue, the cited rule, and the credited outcome are unchanged.

**Doctrinal-currency check.** The Sixth Amendment attachment rule has been stable since *Kirby* (1972) and was explicitly confirmed for initial appearances in *Rothgery* (2008). ### Choice A — "Yes, because an arrest by itself always attaches the Sixth Amendment."

1. **C3 signal (student-accessible).** "Always" is the giveaway. The Sixth Amendment does not attach at every step of the criminal process — it attaches at a specific event. 1. **C3 signal (student-accessible).** "Never" is the giveaway. The stem says the magistrate *did* set bond at Mark's initial appearance.
