## 1. Final Question
After a heated argument at the marketplace, Timothy told Peter, "You have not seen the last of me." Two days later, Peter saw Timothy sitting quietly on a stone bench in the marketplace. Timothy was unarmed and did not approach or threaten Peter. Fearing Timothy would eventually harm him, Peter drew a dagger and killed Timothy. Does Peter have a complete self-defense justification for the killing?

A) Yes, because Timothy had threatened him in the past.
B) Yes, because self-defense depends only on Peter's honest fear.
C) No, because there was no imminent threat justifying deadly force.
D) No, because self-defense is never available in homicide cases.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
Timothy was unarmed, not approaching, and not making an immediate threat when Peter killed him. A future or speculative danger does not satisfy the imminence requirement for deadly self-defense.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
A: Deadly self-defense requires imminent danger. A vague future threat made days earlier does not justify immediate deadly force when the threatener is passive.
B: The belief must be honest AND reasonable, and deadly force requires an imminent threat of death or serious injury. Peter's fear was not reasonable on these facts.
D: Deadly self-defense can justify homicide when its elements are met. The defense fails here solely because imminence and reasonableness are missing.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification
Deadly self-defense requires an honest and reasonable belief in imminent unlawful deadly force or serious bodily harm; fear of future harm is not enough. Peter's action lacked both imminence and a reasonable basis.
