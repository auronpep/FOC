B is correct. Paul has a strong felony-murder defense because Daniel, a non-felon, fired the fatal shot while resisting the robbery. Under the agency-theory limitation on felony murder, the killing must be committed by the defendant or by a co-felon acting in furtherance of the felony. Daniel was neither.

Gold Key: For felony murder under agency theory, identify who caused the death. A fatal shot by a victim, customer, police officer, or other non-felon resisting the crime is not the felon's killing.

### 8. Full Black-Letter Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule is the agency-theory limitation on felony murder. Under the common MBE-facing agency approach, felony murder does not attach when the fatal act is committed by a victim, police officer, customer, or other non-felon resisting the felony rather than by the defendant or a co-felon. The leading authority commonly associated with this limitation is *People v. Washington*, 62 Cal.2d 777, 402 P.2d 130 (Cal. 1965), which rejected felony-murder liability for a killing by a robbery victim resisting the crime. See https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/people-v-washington-24520. *Commonwealth v. Redline*, 391 Pa. 486, 137 A.2d 472 (1958), similarly refused to extend felony murder to a killing by a police officer in response to the felony. See https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/supreme-court/1958/391-pa-486-0.html.

The attempted-predicate point also preserves the source key. Felony murder commonly covers deaths during the commission or attempted commission of covered felonies; Model Penal Code section 210.2(1)(b) treats participation in the commission, attempted commission, or flight from robbery as a predicate for its extreme-indifference presumption. That confirms why C is not the best defense. The agency/proximate-cause split is real, and a minority proximate-cause jurisdiction may treat a foreseeable third-party shooting differently. The source item asks for the defendant's best argument, and the credited answer matches the agency-theory defense.

The tested rule, legal issue, answer-array geometry, and credited outcome are unchanged: the defendant attempted a robbery, a non-felon fired the fatal shot while resisting it, and the best felony-murder defense is non-responsibility for that non-felon's act.
