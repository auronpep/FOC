## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**Correct answer: C.** Truth is a complete defense to a libel claim. When the defendant pleads truth, the plaintiff's character for honesty is directly and substantively at issue — it is an "essential element" of the defense. Under FRE 405(b), when a person's character or character trait is an essential element of a charge, claim, or defense, the character may be proved by specific instances of conduct. Lydia's prior act of skimming cash from a previous employer is a specific instance of dishonest conduct directly pertinent to whether she is a person who takes what isn't hers. This evidence is admissible not merely for impeachment but as substantive evidence supporting Barnabas's defense that his letter was true.

**Gold Key (Drift Audit — see Block 3):** *When character itself is an essential element of a claim or defense, FRE 405(b) opens the door to specific instances of conduct as substantive proof. The "character in issue" exception is narrow: it applies only when character is a material element of the cause of action or defense, not merely because the witness's credibility is attacked.* This unlocks the impeachment-only trap in A.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. "Admissible, but only to impeach Lydia's credibility."**
*Why attractive:* A student who correctly identifies that the evidence is admissible — perhaps because they recall that character evidence can always be used to impeach a witness under FRE 608 — might seize on this half-right answer. It acknowledges admissibility but artificially limits the purpose. This is the most dangerous distractor because it contains a kernel of truth.
*The breaker:* The impeachment-only framing is incomplete. Here, Lydia's honesty is not just a credibility question — it is the very substance of Barnabas's truth defense. Under FRE 405(b), when character is an essential element, specific acts are admissible substantively, not just for impeachment. **Gold Key:** The "character in issue" exception permits specific instances of conduct as substantive proof of the trait itself.

**B. "Inadmissible, because character may not be shown by specific instances of conduct."**
*Why attractive:* The general rule — that character cannot be proved by specific instances — is one of the most heavily tested rules in evidence. Students drilled on FRE 405(a) (reputation and opinion only) may reflexively apply it here. The answer has a confident, rule-statement ring to it.
*The breaker:* The general rule has a major exception. FRE 405(b) specifically authorizes specific instances of conduct when character is "an essential element" of a claim or defense. In a libel case where truth is pleaded, the plaintiff's character for honesty is precisely such an element.

**D. "Inadmissible, because such evidence is more unfairly prejudicial than probative."**
*Why attractive:* FRE 403's balancing test is always in a student's mind, and evidence of prior bad acts feels like the kind of thing a judge might exclude for prejudice. This distractor tests whether the student can recognize when probative value is at its highest.
*The breaker:* When character is an essential element, the probative value of directly relevant specific acts is extremely high — it goes to the heart of the defense. A court would not find that such evidence's probative value is "substantially outweighed" by the danger of unfair prejudice under FRE 403 when it directly proves the very trait at issue.

## 9. Black-Letter Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing Rules:**

1. **FRE 404(a)(1)** — Character evidence generally prohibited to prove that a person acted in conformity with a character trait on a particular occasion.

2. **FRE 405(a) & (b)** — Methods of proving character:
   - (a) General rule: When character evidence is admissible, it may be proved by reputation or opinion testimony.
   - (b) **Exception:** When a person's character or character trait is **an essential element** of a charge, claim, or defense, the character or trait **may also be proved by relevant specific instances of conduct.**

3. **FRE 403** — Court may exclude relevant evidence if its probative value is substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice, confusion of the issues, etc.

**Application:**

In a defamation (libel) action, truth is an affirmative defense. *See Restatement (Second) of Torts § 581A (1977).* When the defendant pleads truth, the plaintiff's character for the trait asserted in the defamatory statement becomes a material element of the case. Under FRE 405(b), when character is an essential element, evidence may take the form of reputation, opinion, **or specific instances of conduct**. The plaintiff's prior act of skimming cash is a specific instance of the very character trait (dishonesty) that is placed in issue by the truth defense. It is admissible substantively — not merely for impeachment.

**Authority:** Fed. R. Evid. 404(a), 405(b); *see also* 2 McCormick on Evidence § 187 (8th character ed.) (when character is "in issue" as an essential element, all three methods of proof — reputation, opinion, and specific acts — are available); Restatement (Second) of Torts § 613 (1977) (truth as defense to defamation).

**Doctrinal-currency check:** No hotlist doctrine implicated. The FRE 405 exception for character-in-issue is settled, uncontroversial, and has not been subject to recent amendment or overruling.

**Unchanged from original:** The tested rule (character-in-issue/FRE 405(b)), the legal issue, and the credited outcome (admissible as substantively) are identical to the original. The variant tests the same point.

## Program Frame

This item tests the intersection of defamation law and character evidence rules — specifically, the FRE 405(b) exception that allows specific instances of conduct to prove character when it is an essential element of a claim or defense. The item sits within the BarMatrix "Red Zone Map" for Evidence > Character Evidence, targeting the common student error of applying the general bar on specific-act evidence (FRE 405(a)) while overlooking the "character in issue" exception (FRE 405(b)).

## Question Data

**Stem:** Barnabas runs a mail-order bookshop. After a customer dispute, Barnabas wrote to the shop's primary supplier — a wholesale distributor — stating that the supplier's bookkeeper, Lydia, had been pocketing cash from customer payments. Lydia sued Barnabas for libel. During her case-in-chief, Lydia testified truthfully that Barnabas had sent the letter. Barnabas then offers evidence that, two years earlier, Lydia skimmed cash from the register at a different bookshop where she previously worked.

**Call:** The evidence of Lydia's prior cash-skimming is:

**Choices:**
- **A.** admissible, but only to impeach Lydia's credibility.
- **B.** inadmissible, because character may not be shown by specific instances of conduct.
- **C.** admissible, as substantive evidence to prove that Lydia is a person who takes what isn't hers.
- **D.** inadmissible, because such evidence is more unfairly prejudicial than probative.

**Correct Answer:** C

## Distilled Core Question

In a libel suit where truth is the defendant's defense, is evidence of the plaintiff's prior specific dishonest act admissible substantively to prove the plaintiff's character?

## Choice-by-Choice Walkthrough

### Choice A: "Admissible, but only to impeach Lydia's credibility."

**1. Student-accessible C3 signal:** The word "only" is a red flag. When an answer says evidence is admissible for one purpose "only," ask: is there another purpose this evidence could serve? Here, Barnabas isn't just attacking Lydia's credibility — he's trying to prove his letter was true. That's a substantive use, not just impeachment.

**2. Student label:** The Half-Right Answer — it gets admissibility right but artificially limits the purpose.

**3. What a true/responsive version would look like:** A true-and-responsive version would say "admissible, both to impeach Lydia's credibility AND as substantive evidence of her character for honesty." But that's not an option — which means the "only" makes this choice wrong.

**4. Lawyer confirmation:** FRE 405(b) permits specific instances of conduct as substantive proof when character is an essential element. In a libel case where truth is pleaded, the plaintiff's character for honesty IS an essential element. The evidence is not limited to impeachment. **Gold Key:** When character is an essential element of a claim or defense, FRE 405(b) permits specific instances of conduct to prove the trait substantively — not just for impeachment.

---

### Choice B: "Inadmissible, because character may not be shown by specific instances of conduct."

**1. Student-accessible C3 signal:** Absolute language alert: "may not." The MBE loves to test exceptions to general rules. The general rule (FRE 405(a)) does bar specific instances — but is there an exception? Yes: FRE 405(b) when character is an essential element. This choice states the general rule as if it were absolute.

**2. Student label:** The General Rule Overclaim — it states a real rule but ignores the exception.

**3. What a true/responsive version would look like:** "Inadmissible, because character may not be shown by specific instances of conduct **unless** the character is an essential element of a claim or defense." The missing exception is what makes this choice wrong.

**4. Lawyer confirmation:** FRE 405(a) bars specific instances for proving character generally. FRE 405(b) creates an exception when character is an essential element. In this case, Lydia's honesty is an essential element of Barnabas's truth defense. The exception applies; the general bar does not.

---

### Choice C: "Admissible, as substantive evidence to prove that Lydia is a person who takes what isn't hers."

**1. Student-accessible C3 signal:** This choice correctly identifies both admissibility AND the correct purpose. It doesn't limit the evidence to impeachment. It doesn't invoke a false bar. It matches the analysis: Barnabas's truth defense puts Lydia's honesty in issue, and specific acts are admissible to prove her character.

**2. Student label:** The Correct Answer.

**3. What a true/responsive version would look like:** This IS the true-and-responsive version. It correctly states that the evidence is admissible and correctly identifies the substantive purpose.

**4. Lawyer confirmation:** FRE 405(b) permits specific instances of conduct when character is an essential element. Truth is a defense to libel. Lydia's character for honesty is therefore an essential element. Her prior cash-skimming is a specific instance of the trait at issue. Admissible substantively.

---

### Choice D: "Inadmissible, because such evidence is more unfairly prejudicial than probative."

**1. Student-accessible C3 signal:** FRE 403 is always a tempting answer, but ask: when is probative value at its HIGHEST? When the evidence goes to the very heart of the case. Here, the case IS about whether Lydia takes what isn't hers. The prior act proves exactly that. The probative value is enormous.

**2. Student label:** The Balancing Red Herring — it invokes a real rule (FRE 403) but misapplies it to facts where probative value clearly dominates.

**3. What a true/responsive version would look like:** "Inadmissible, because such evidence is more unfairly prejudicial than probative **when the evidence is only marginally relevant to a collateral issue.**" Here, the evidence is directly relevant to the central issue.

**4. Lawyer confirmation:** FRE 403 balancing rarely excludes evidence that goes to an essential element of a claim or defense. When character is in issue, the probative value of directly relevant specific acts is at its peak. A court would not exclude this evidence under FRE 403.

## Wrong-Answer Recovery Paths

**If a student picked A (impeachment-only):**
- **Skipped move:** Did not recognize that "character in issue" triggers FRE 405(b), which permits substantive use beyond impeachment.
- **Recovery step:** Review the Gold Key: when character is an essential element of a claim or defense, specific instances of conduct are admissible to prove the trait itself. In libel/truth defense, the plaintiff's character for the asserted trait IS the defense. The evidence is substantive, not just impeachment.

**If a student picked B (specific-instances bar):**
- **Skipped move:** Applied the general rule (FRE 405(a)) without checking for the exception (FRE 405(b)).
- **Recovery step:** Absolute bars on the MBE almost always have exceptions. When character is "in issue" as an essential element, FRE 405(b) specifically authorizes specific instances of conduct. Always ask: "Is someone's character itself what the case is about?"

**If a student picked D (403 balancing):**
- **Skipped move:** Did not recognize that probative value is at its highest when evidence goes to an essential element of the case.
- **Recovery step:** FRE 403 is a balancing test, but it rarely excludes evidence that directly proves an element of the claim or defense. When the case is "Did Lydia take what isn't hers?" — evidence that she DID take what wasn't hers at a prior job is about as probative as it gets.
