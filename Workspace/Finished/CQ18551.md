### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** Extortion is obtaining property by threat. Stephen obtained Martha's money by threatening to destroy her hymnals unless she paid him. The threat did not need to be real. The offense turns on the coercive demand: pay money or suffer threatened harm to property. Martha was not handing over temporary possession of the money, and Stephen had never been entrusted with the money. The better label is extortion.

**Gold Key:** Robbery requires force or threat of force against a person; extortion covers coercive threats, including threats to property or future harm, that induce payment. **Silver Key:** In a theft-label array, identify the threat target first: person-threat during the taking points to robbery; property/future-harm threat demanding payment points to extortion.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. Larceny by trick.** This choice is attractive because Stephen lied about the solvent. The breaker is what Martha gave up: she paid $1,400 permanently to avoid the threatened harm. Larceny by trick is built for a victim who is deceived into handing over possession while keeping title or expecting return. Martha did not loan, entrust, or temporarily part with possession of the money.

**B. Embezzlement.** This choice sounds like a broad theft word, but it starts in the wrong place. Embezzlement requires lawful possession or custody first, followed by criminal conversion. Stephen never had lawful custody of Martha's money or the hymnals. He got the money only because of the threat.

**D. Robbery.** This is the dominant trap because the stem contains a serious threat and immediate pressure to pay. The breaker is the target of the threat. Robbery requires force or threat of force against a person in the taking. Stephen threatened to damage property in a locked van, not to harm Martha or another person. **Gold Key:** a property-destruction threat demanding payment is extortion, not robbery, when the threat is not force against a person.

The tested rule and credited outcome are preserved, but the source row's outline placement was corrected. The source listed `73030400` for robbery. The tested doctrine is extortion, so the verified deepest outline placement is `73030600 Crimes against Property > Extortion`.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Model Penal Code section 223.4 defines theft by extortion as purposely obtaining another's property by threat, including threats to inflict injury, commit another criminal offense, or inflict other harm. The MPC framing confirms that a coercive property-harm threat demanding money belongs in the extortion lane. Robbery remains a different offense: *Stokeling v. United States*, 139 S.
