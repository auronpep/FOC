# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

Timothy borrowed a utility trailer from his friend Barnabas after helping haul folding chairs home from a county hymn sing. Later that afternoon, Timothy was arrested for reckless driving while towing the trailer. When officers inventoried the impounded trailer, they found a glass jar of methamphetamine inside a locked metal toolbox bolted to the trailer bed. Timothy was then charged with knowingly possessing methamphetamine.

Testifying on his own behalf at trial, Timothy said that he did not know the methamphetamine was in the toolbox. On cross-examination, after properly marking it for identification, the prosecutor showed Timothy a note that had been taken from his jacket pocket when he was booked. Timothy said that it was a note from his wife, Hannah. The prosecutor then showed it to Timothy's lawyer and offered it into evidence. Among other things, the note said, "That meth we used after the hymn sing last night kept us singing till sunrise." The jurisdiction recognizes the common-law spousal privilege.

If Timothy's lawyer objects to admission of the note, should the objection be sustained?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A defendant is charged with knowingly possessing contraband found in borrowed property. He denies knowing it was there. The prosecutor offers a note from the defendant's wife showing they used the same kind of contraband on a different occasion. Should the objection be sustained because the note is irrelevant to whether he knew this contraband was in this container?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

C. Yes, because the note is not relevant to a material issue.

D. Yes, because the note is hearsay not within any exception to the hearsay rule.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

Choice C is correct. The objection should be sustained because the note does not bear on the fact that matters in this case: whether Timothy knew the methamphetamine was in the locked toolbox on Barnabas's trailer. Hannah's note suggests that Timothy and Hannah used methamphetamine on another occasion after a hymn sing. That may show past drug use, but it does not tend to prove Timothy's knowledge of the jar hidden in this toolbox on this day.

Silver Key: when an evidence question baits you with privilege or hearsay labels, stop first and ask what fact the evidence is supposed to prove in this case. If the note does not move that fact, the evidence fails before the later doctrines matter.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### Choice A

Attractive move: the stem practically waves a privilege flag by telling you the jurisdiction recognizes the common-law spousal privilege, so it feels natural to treat the note as a spouse-based bar to admission.

Breaker: the best answer is not the detour doctrine the stem name-drops; it is the threshold doctrine the note actually fails. Silver Key: do not let a named privilege distract you from the first gate. Even if a spouse-based privilege could become part of the discussion, the note still does not tend to prove Timothy knew the methamphetamine was in the toolbox, so relevance is the dispositive reason the objection should be sustained.

### Choice B

Attractive move: the note sounds self-inculpatory because Hannah admits meth use, so students may correctly think of the statement-against-penal-interest exception and then leap to admissibility.

Breaker: a hearsay exception does not rescue evidence that never clears relevance. Even if the note could satisfy a penal-interest exception, it still says nothing about Timothy's knowledge of the jar in the toolbox, so the objection should still be sustained.

### Choice D

Attractive move: the note is plainly an out-of-court statement offered for its truth, and "hearsay with no exception" is a familiar exclusion answer.

Breaker: that answer overstates the hearsay problem. A statement admitting criminal drug use can qualify as a statement against penal interest, so the "no exception" claim is too broad. But even fixing the hearsay point does not help the prosecutor, because the note still fails relevance.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer

The transformed item preserves the original tested rule, issue, and credited outcome. The governing rule is relevance, not privilege.

- Fed. R. Evid. 401 defines relevant evidence as evidence having any tendency to make a fact more or less probable when that fact is of consequence in determining the action. Here, the fact of consequence is Timothy's knowledge that methamphetamine was in the locked toolbox. Hannah's note about meth use after a hymn sing does not tend to prove that knowledge. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_401
- Fed. R. Evid. 402 makes irrelevant evidence inadmissible. Once the note fails relevance, the objection should be sustained without needing privilege to do the work. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_402
- The hearsay detours match the original array. Fed. R. Evid. 804(b)(3) recognizes statements against penal interest when the declarant is unavailable, which is why the "no exception" distractor is weak and the penal-interest distractor feels plausible. But that doctrine still does not answer the relevance failure. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_804
- The spouse bait also tracks the original array. `Trammel v. United States`, 445 U.S. 40 (1980), describes the modern adverse-spousal-testimony privilege, but even assuming a spouse-based privilege issue is in the neighborhood, the official explanation's point remains the same: the note is excluded because it is not relevant to the material issue. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/445/40
