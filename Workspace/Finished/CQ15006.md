## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

**A is correct.** The deed gave Naomi a life estate and gave Stephen — a named, living, ascertained person — the rest: "to my cousin Stephen, his heirs and assigns." That is an **indefeasibly vested remainder in fee simple**. **Gold Key:** *"his heirs and assigns" are words of limitation describing the size of Stephen's estate (a fee simple), not a class gift to his heirs* — so the heirs took nothing under the deed, and Stephen owned the whole future interest. **Gold Key:** *a vested remainder is alienable, devisable, and descendible*, so when Stephen died devising "all of his estate," the vineyard interest passed under that will to Timothy. When Naomi's life estate ended, Timothy's already-vested remainder simply became possessory. **Silver Key:** *once you classify the remainder as vested, every "contingent / never vested / reversion / destructibility" answer falls as a bloc* — leaving the choice that honors the will.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B (destructibility fork) — incorrect.** It sounds rigorous: name a real future-interests doctrine, hedge it on a jurisdictional split, and you look like the careful student. The breaker is that destructibility only ever destroys a **contingent** remainder that fails to vest by the close of the prior estate; Stephen's remainder was vested from the moment of the deed, so the doctrine has nothing to operate on. **Gold Key:** *the destructibility rule never touches a vested remainder.*

**C (owner by reversion) — incorrect.** It feels safe because grantors so often keep a reversion, and "it never vested, so it bounced back" is a tidy story. The breaker is that Daniel conveyed his **entire** interest — a life estate plus a vested remainder in fee — leaving **no reversion**; and the remainder was vested, not contingent, so nothing "failed to vest." **Gold Key:** *a vested remainder is devisable and does not revert.*

**D (Levi the heir) — incorrect; dominant trap.** It pulls hardest because Levi is the bloodline heir and "the son inherits the family land" is the common-sense default. The breaker is sitting in the stem: Stephen died **devising his entire estate by will** to Timothy, so there was nothing left to pass by intestacy to Levi — and "his heirs and assigns" in the deed never gave the heirs a separate interest to begin with. **Silver Key:** *when the stem says someone died "devising his entire estate," cut any "because he is the heir" choice on sight.* **Gold Key:** *"his heirs and assigns" = words of limitation, not a gift to the heirs.*

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

**Tested rule (unchanged from the original):** A remainder created in a named, ascertained, living person, not subject to any condition precedent, is a **vested remainder**. The accompanying language "and his heirs and assigns" is the classic **words of limitation** marking a fee simple — it does not create an interest in the heirs (no condition precedent, no class gift), so the remainder is **indefeasibly vested**. A vested remainder is freely **alienable, devisable, and descendible**; it therefore passes under the remainderman's will. The **destructibility of contingent remainders** applies only to *contingent* remainders that remain unvested at the termination of the preceding freehold estate (and is abolished in most states); it is irrelevant to a vested remainder.

**Application:** Stephen held an indefeasibly vested remainder. His will ("all of his estate") carried the vineyard interest to Timothy. Stephen's death before the life tenant did not defeat the interest (no destructibility issue — the remainder was vested), and his sole heir Levi took nothing by intestacy because the will exhausted the estate. On Naomi's death the life estate ended and Timothy's vested remainder became possessory. **Title is in Timothy — credited answer A — exactly as the original credited the hospital under original-key B.**

**Authority (lawyer-confirmation layer only):** Restatement (First) of Property §157 (classification of remainders: indefeasibly vested, vested subject to open, vested subject to complete defeasance, contingent); common-law rule that vested remainders are alienable, devisable, and descendible; destructibility-of-contingent-remainders doctrine (common law; abolished in most jurisdictions). Authority class: Restatement section + settled common-law rule — within the authority floor; settled hornbook law, no attorney escalation required. `bank_validation_verdict: PASS` — research confirms the supplied key.
