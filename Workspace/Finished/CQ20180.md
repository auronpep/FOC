## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

Common-law burglary has four elements: (1) breaking and entering (2) the dwelling of another (3) at nighttime (4) with intent to commit a felony therein. All four must be satisfied. Here, two elements are missing. First, the cooperage workshop is not a dwelling — it is a detached work building never used for sleeping. Second, the entry occurred at noon, which is daytime, not nighttime. Because both the dwelling and nighttime elements fail, Ruth is not guilty of common-law burglary. The fact that she broke and entered with intent to steal is necessary but not sufficient — it does not cure the missing elements.

**Gold Key:** Common-law burglary requires ALL four elements: breaking + entering + dwelling of another + nighttime + intent to commit a felony therein. Missing ANY element means no common-law burglary.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (originally B):** This choice says intent to steal "satisfies the felony requirement" — as if intent alone can substitute for the missing elements. The trap is the backwards logic: intent is required IN ADDITION to the other elements, not INSTEAD OF them. The Gold Key makes this clear: all four elements are required. Intent to steal satisfies one element (felony intent) but does nothing to cure the missing dwelling and nighttime elements.

**B (originally D):** This choice claims that breaking a window is not a breaking. That is simply wrong as a matter of law. Any force used to create an opening — including prying open or breaking a window — satisfies the breaking element. This choice is a flat misstatement of the facts and law. A student who knows the definition of "breaking" can cut this immediately.

**D (originally A) — DOMINANT TRAP:** This is the most attractive wrong answer. Ruth DID break and enter. She DID intend to steal. Every fact stated in this choice is true. The trap is the half-truth: the choice states facts that are correct but omits the two elements that are missing (dwelling + nighttime). Students who focus on what IS present without checking what ISN'T will pick this. The Silver Key move — "check ALL four elements before concluding" — would steer a student off this trap.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing rule:** Common-law burglary requires: (1) breaking and entering (2) the dwelling house of another (3) in the nighttime (4) with intent to commit a felony therein. All four elements must be present. *See* 3 W. Blackstone, *Commentaries on the Laws of England* 224 (1768); *People v. Sparks*, 28 Cal. 4th 71 (2002); *State v. Howe*, 116 Wash. 2d 321 (1991).

**Application:** The cooperage workshop is a detached work building, not a dwelling house. A dwelling is a structure where someone regularly sleeps. A workshop used for making barrels does not qualify. Additionally, the entry occurred at noon — well outside the nighttime window (one hour after sunset to one hour before sunrise). Two of four elements are missing. Common-law burglary fails.
