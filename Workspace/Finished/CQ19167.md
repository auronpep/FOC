## 1. Final Question
At a youth-group picnic in a dry canyon preserve, Daniel illegally started a charcoal grill during a posted fire ban. The flames spread toward Daniel's van. To escape, Daniel broke into a ranger kiosk and stole the keys to a locked service gate. Daniel is charged with burglary and theft and claims necessity. What is the best response to Daniel's necessity claim?

## 2. Distilled Core Question
Does necessity excuse burglary and theft when the defendant recklessly created the emergency?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
A is right because necessity generally does not apply when the defendant created the emergency, especially by recklessly making the dangerous situation in the first place. Daniel illegally started a fire during a posted ban, then used burglary and theft to get out of the danger he helped create. That is not the kind of blameless emergency the defense is meant to excuse.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
B is attractive because it focuses on the real danger from the fire and treats that danger as enough by itself. The breaker is that necessity is conditional, and Daniel's own reckless conduct created the emergency that he later invoked as a justification.

C is attractive because it tries to separate necessity from duress by saying only human threats count. The breaker is that necessity covers natural or circumstantial dangers too; the real problem here is created danger, not the source type.

D is attractive because emergencies do sometimes soften criminal blame and the answer sounds compassionate. The breaker is that necessity is not automatic, and property crimes are not always excused just because the defendant was in danger.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning
The credited rule is unchanged: necessity is generally unavailable when the defendant created the emergency and had no lawful blameless position from which to invoke the defense. Daniel's own illegal fire started the crisis, so the later burglary and theft are not justified by necessity.

Authority used for verification:
- Model Penal Code section 3.02
- [Necessity defense, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/necessity_defense)
- [Commonwealth v. Morris M., 70 Mass. App. Ct. 688 (2007)](https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/court-of-appeals/volumes/70/70massappct688.html)

Under the MPC, the choice-of-evils defense is limited and depends on the actor's reasonableness and the absence of stronger contrary legislative policy; necessity doctrine generally does not protect someone who recklessly created the danger. Cornell's overview states the defense is for conduct that prevents a greater harm, and Massachusetts authority describing the defense adds that the danger must be imminent, no reasonable legal alternative may exist, and the defendant must not have substantially contributed to the emergency. Daniel's illegal fire ban violation supplied the emergency, so necessity fails.
