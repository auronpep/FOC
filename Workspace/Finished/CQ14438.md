## Final question

For a fall Bible-story festival, Lydia, who operated a private Christian gift shop, sent Martha, an artisan who made olive-wood nativity ornament kits, a signed order for 720 kits. Which of the following is an accurate statement as of June 6 after Lydia rejected the kits?

## Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** **Gold Key:** A promise that is freely cancelable until a stated deadline can be illusory during that window, but once the deadline passes unused, the promise can become binding if the other side has accepted.

Here, Lydia reserved only a temporary cancellation right: before May 1. She did not cancel before May 1. Martha had already accepted and later shipped conforming goods on May 12. As of June 6, after Lydia refused the conforming kits, the best statement is that Lydia’s order, even if initially illusory, had become a binding promise to accept and pay.

**Silver Key:** Sort the answer choices by time. The cancellation right mattered before May 1; the call asks for the legal status on June 6.

## Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This answer is attractive because it notices the cancellation clause and uses it to keep the deal in the “offer” stage. **Silver Key:** the date check steers away from it. The clause allowed cancellation only before May 1, while Martha shipped on May 12 and Lydia rejected on June 6.

**B.** This answer is the dominant trap because “condition subsequent” sounds like a precise Contracts label for a deadline that did not happen. **Gold Key:** the missed cancellation deadline is the illusory-promise cutoff; it is not a condition subsequent whose failure creates enforceability.

**D.** This answer is attractive because “option” is a familiar formation label and the facts include an order, acceptance, and shipment. The call asks for the parties’ status after the cancellation deadline passed and conforming goods arrived; it does not ask whether shipment created an option.

## Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested issue is unchanged from the source row: a buyer’s temporary right to cancel makes the buyer’s promise suspect during the cancellation window, but the promise can become binding after the right expires unused. The source explanation states that the cancellation right made the promise initially illusory and that the promise became consideration once the cancellation period passed unused. 

The authority floor is satisfied.
