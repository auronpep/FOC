### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. The signed writing says Lydia may choose among Daniel's brass handbell sets. Evidence that Daniel also promised her a choice of a bronze set would contradict that written term and is excluded. Evidence that Daniel promised a padded rolling case does not contradict the writing; it adds a side term. Gold Key: parol evidence cannot contradict a final writing, but a consistent additional term may supplement a partially integrated writing. Silver Key: split the answer array by term; do not decide the whole oral conversation as one bundle.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because students often remember the parol evidence rule as "no prior oral evidence once there is a signed writing." The breaker is the Gold Key: the rule bars contradictory terms, but it can allow consistent additional terms if the writing is only partial.

B is attractive because it notices that the oral promise was made before the writing and tries to admit some outside evidence. The breaker is that it admits the wrong part: the bronze-set option directly fights the written brass-set limitation, while the rolling case merely supplements the deal.

D is attractive because the rolling case feels harmless, and that can make the entire oral agreement seem admissible. The breaker is the Silver Key: split the two oral terms. The rolling case can supplement; the bronze option contradicts the brass-only choice term.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. This is a Contracts question under the parol evidence rule for a sale of goods. The issue is whether prior oral terms may be admitted when a signed writing appears final as to included terms but may not be complete as to every side term.

Authority layer: UCC 2-202 provides that terms in a writing intended as a final expression may not be contradicted by evidence of a prior agreement or contemporaneous oral agreement, but may be explained or supplemented by consistent additional terms unless the court finds the writing was intended as a complete and exclusive statement. See UCC 2-202, https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-202. Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 213 likewise treats an integrated agreement as discharging inconsistent prior agreements, and Restatement section 216 permits evidence of consistent additional terms unless the agreement is completely integrated. See Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 213, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/11720-bruckner-howard-law-contracts-2024/resources/7.3-restatement-second-of-contracts-213/ and section 216, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/11720-bruckner-howard-law-contracts-2024/resources/7.5-restatement-second-of-contracts-216/.

The written contract gives Lydia a choice among brass handbell sets. A prior oral option to choose a bronze set contradicts the written limitation and is excluded. The rolling case term adds an accessory and does not contradict the brass-set term. Because the writing does not clearly state that it is complete and exclusive, a court may admit the consistent additional rolling-case evidence if it finds the writing partially integrated. The credited answer remains C.
