### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

**Tested rule (unchanged from the source item):** A future interest in a transferee must be created in the person or class identified by the instrument. A remainder is contingent when the taker is unascertained or when the taker's right is subject to a condition precedent; a vested remainder requires an ascertained taker with no condition precedent to taking. A future-interest label does not attach to a person whom the instrument never selects.

**Application:** Lydia's will creates a term of years in Timothy, a contingent remainder in Hannah because Hannah must be living at the end of Timothy's estate, and an alternate contingent remainder in the single person who was Hannah's oldest child living when Lydia died. At Lydia's death, Stephen was that person. Ruth, the younger child, was not the niece, was not the oldest child living at Lydia's death, and could not later become the oldest child living at Lydia's death. Her legal position on the day after Lydia's death was therefore **no valid interest**.

**Authority (lawyer-confirmation layer only):** The governing classification is grounded in the common-law future-interests taxonomy reflected in Restatement (First) of Property Section 157, which classifies remainders including contingent remainders and vested remainders. Cornell LII's Wex entry for contingent remainder confirms that a contingent remainder is a non-vested future interest that becomes possessory at the expiration of the prior estate only if its condition is met, and its related entry on a remainder subject to a condition precedent describes the same concept.
