## 6. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Stephen's father's offer was for a unilateral contract—his promise to pay in return for Stephen's postponing the wedding. When Stephen postponed the wedding, he accepted his father's offer, and a contract was formed.

## 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This answer is attractive because death of an offeror is a classic way offers terminate. The breaker is timing: the stem shows the father died "soon after Stephen began the residency," which was after Stephen had already postponed the wedding and thereby accepted the offer by performance. Once accepted, the contract is formed and the offeror's death does not revoke it.

**B.** This is the dominant trap. It sells the overbroad memory that any parental attempt to influence the timing of a child's marriage is unenforceable on public policy grounds. The breaker is the scope of the rule: while some cases hold that an agreement never to marry violates public policy, there is no reason an agreement merely to postpone a marriage for a finite, one-year period tied to completing a training program would do so.

**D.** This answer is attractive because the stem mentions "completion of the first year of the residency" and Stephen ultimately chose not to continue. The breaker is the exact language of the father's promise: he offered the $1,000 and the second-year fee "if Stephen postponed his wedding plans until after the completion of his first year." The promise was conditioned on the postponement, not on Stephen's later decision to continue or register for the second year. The stem explicitly notes that Stephen told the administrator "although there would be no residency fee for a second year, he expected to be paid the $1,000."

## 8. Full black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

The tested rule is formation of a unilateral contract by performance of the requested forbearance, the effect of the offeror's death after acceptance, interpretation of the condition in the offer, and the limited scope of the public policy defense against agreements affecting marriage. The credited outcome (recovery) is unchanged from the source row.

Governing authority (lawyer-confirmation layer only):

- Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 45 (Option Contract Created by Part Performance or Tender): where an offer invites acceptance by rendering a performance and does not invite a promissory acceptance, an option contract is created when the offeree tenders or begins the invited performance.

- Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 48 (Death or Incapacity of Offeror or Offeree): the power of acceptance is terminated by the death or incapacity of the offeror or offeree, but only while the power remains unexercised. Once the offeree has accepted by beginning or completing the invited performance, a contract exists.

- Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 50 (Acceptance of Offer Defined; Acceptance by Performance; Acceptance by Promise): acceptance by performance requires that at least part of what the offer requests be performed or tendered.

- Restatement (Second) of Contracts Chapter 8 (Unenforceability on Grounds of Public Policy), especially the treatment of restraints on marriage: agreements imposing a general restraint on marriage (e.g., a covenant never to marry) are unenforceable as against public policy, but a promise conditioned on postponing marriage for a limited, reasonable time tied to a specific event (here, completion of a one-year program) does not constitute an unreasonable restraint and is enforceable.

The source row's official explanations align exactly with these authorities. The father's promise was accepted when Stephen performed the requested forbearance (postponing the wedding). The father's subsequent death is irrelevant. The language did not condition the $1,000 on Stephen actually completing or continuing the residency—only on the postponement. An agreement to postpone marriage for one year is not the kind of permanent or general restraint that triggers the public policy bar.

No doctrinal-currency hotlist item is implicated. No key disagreement exists.
