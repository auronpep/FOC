## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Lydia owns an unusually fire-safe mobile wood-fired pizza trailer that she rents for private Christian family gatherings at a $900 daily rate. The fee includes Lydia operating the oven, one helper, dough, toppings, and serving supplies. On June 3, Timothy agreed with Lydia that Timothy would have the trailer for a full-day Scripture-memory picnic for his relatives on June 20 for $900. Timothy paid a $350 advance deposit and signed an agreement stating that Lydia could keep the deposit as liquidated damages if Timothy canceled or failed to appear.

At 2 a.m. on June 20, the county fire marshal issued a red-flag emergency order prohibiting all portable open-flame cooking trailers of Lydia's size from operating anywhere in the county. The order remained in effect throughout the day. Timothy did not appear on June 20 because he heard the emergency order on the radio.

Which of the following is an accurate statement?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A paid event contract cannot legally be performed on the performance day because of a later government safety order, and the customer prepaid a deposit under a no-show liquidated-damages clause. Is the contract discharged, and does the customer get the deposit back?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. The contract is discharged because both parties were mistaken about an essential fact when they made the agreement, and Timothy is entitled to return of his deposit.

B. The contract is not discharged, and Timothy is not entitled to return of his deposit, because the liquidated-damages clause allocated the risk of the fire marshal's order to Timothy.

C. The contract is discharged because performance became impossible, and Timothy is entitled to return of his deposit.

D. The contract is not discharged, because performance was possible in light of Lydia's unusually fire-safe trailer, and Timothy is not entitled to return of his deposit.

### 4. Correct answer

C. The original credited choice was A.

### 6. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. The legally decisive fact is not whether Lydia's trailer is unusually safe. The decisive fact is that a later government order made the promised performance illegal for the whole performance day. Gold Key: a supervening event that makes performance impracticable or impossible without the performing party's fault discharges the duty when nonoccurrence of that event was a basic assumption of the deal. Silver Key: separate the outside event that blocks performance from the no-show clause; a no-show clause does not automatically turn an excused nonappearance into a breach. Because the contract is discharged and Timothy's deposit was a benefit paid in advance, Timothy is entitled to restitution of the deposit.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

A sells the right neighborhood but the wrong time. Mutual mistake looks backward to a mistaken basic fact at the moment of contracting. Here the emergency order happened later, so the better frame is supervening impossibility, not mutual mistake.

B is the dominant trap because it sounds like the written deposit clause already solved the whole problem. The clause lets Lydia keep the deposit if Timothy cancels or fails to appear in breach. It does not clearly allocate the risk that a later government order would make Lydia's promised performance illegal. Gold Key: a breach-triggered liquidated-damages clause is not the same thing as a clear assumption of the supervening-impossibility risk. Silver Key: read the trigger words in the clause before treating it as a risk-allocation clause.

D is attractive because the stem makes Lydia's trailer sound unusually safe. That fact does not answer the call. The government order covers all portable open-flame cooking trailers of Lydia's size, so legal performance is blocked even if this trailer is better built than most.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: a supervening government safety prohibition prevents the promised performance, discharges the contract duty, and supports restitution of the advance deposit.

Authority checked: Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 261 provides the supervening impracticability discharge rule when an event occurs after contract formation, without the party's fault, and nonoccurrence of the event was a basic assumption of the contract. Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 377 provides restitution when a party's duty does not arise or is discharged because of impracticability, frustration, nonoccurrence of a condition, or related excuse.
