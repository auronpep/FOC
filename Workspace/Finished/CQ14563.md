## 1. Final question

Police received a late-night report that a loud crack and a cry for help had come from a townhouse where Mary stored supplies for a Christian youth choir. When officers arrived, they looked through a front window and saw Stephen lying on the floor beside overturned music stands. The front door was unlocked. The officers entered and found that Stephen had recently been shot and was unconscious. They called an ambulance. While waiting, one officer made a quick walk through the townhouse to see whether anyone else was inside. No one else was found, but the officer saw on the kitchen table several clear bags of what he believed to be cocaine. The officer seized the bags, and later testing confirmed that they contained cocaine. After the ambulance arrived a few minutes later and took Stephen to the hospital, the officers opened cabinets and drawers throughout the townhouse looking for the gun used in the shooting. They did not find the gun, but in a dresser drawer in an upstairs bedroom they found marijuana and seized it. Mary, who lived in the townhouse, was later charged with unlawful possession of the cocaine and the marijuana. Mary moved to suppress both items because the entry and searches were warrantless. How should the court decide the motion?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

Police lawfully enter a home for an emergency, see contraband in plain view during a quick safety sweep, then later open drawers after the emergency has ended; which evidence is suppressed?

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

D is the best answer. The officers had an emergency reason to enter after seeing an unconscious shooting victim, and the brief walk through the townhouse was tied to safety and possible victims or suspects. The clear bags on the kitchen table were exposed while the officer was lawfully present, and their incriminating character was immediately apparent. Gold Key: emergency entry and plain view can save what officers see while they are lawfully inside, but the emergency does not authorize a later drawer-by-drawer evidence search. Silver Key: split the array by item, not by the word "warrantless." The cocaine survives; the marijuana does not.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A. This choice sells the broad idea that no warrant means no evidence. That move overcuts the emergency-entry and plain-view part of the fact pattern. The officer did not have to ignore clear bags of apparent cocaine sitting on the kitchen table during a lawful safety sweep.

B. This choice sells the opposite overread: once the officers were lawfully inside, everything found later is fair game. The breaker is timing and scope. Gold Key: a crisis entry is not a general search warrant, so opening bedroom drawers after the ambulance removed the victim exceeds the emergency.

C. This is the dominant trap because it flips the two evidence buckets. It treats the visible cocaine as suppressed while treating the hidden marijuana as admissible. Silver Key: the item in plain view during the safety sweep is the one that stays; the item inside the later drawer search is the one that goes.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged: emergency entry and a limited protective sweep support seizure of cocaine in plain view, but the later warrantless opening of drawers after the emergency ended requires suppression of the marijuana. Mincey v. Arizona, 437 U.S. 385 (1978), rejects a general homicide-scene exception and requires a warrant once the emergency no longer justifies the intrusion. Michigan v. Tyler, 436 U.S. 499 (1978), confirms that officials may enter to meet an emergency and seize evidence in plain view during that lawful presence, but later investigative searches need a warrant or another exception. Horton v. California, 496 U.S. 128 (1990), states the plain-view rule: lawful vantage point, immediately apparent incriminating character, and lawful access. Those authorities support denying suppression as to the cocaine and granting suppression as to the marijuana.
