## 1. Final Question

Mary sued Timothy for injuries she allegedly suffered when she slipped on the painted "Jordan River" bridge at Timothy's private Bible-themed mini-golf course. At trial, without asking that Stephen, the course's operations manager, be declared a hostile witness, Mary called Stephen solely to establish that Timothy owned the course where Mary fell. On cross-examination of Stephen, Timothy's attorney sought to establish that Timothy used inspection cards, warning cones, and nonskid mats to make the course safe for paying guests. Should the court permit Timothy's cross-examination over Mary's objection?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

If direct examination only proved property ownership, may cross-examination cover the owner's safety precautions without court permission?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. Stephen's direct examination covered only Timothy's ownership of the mini-golf course. The proposed cross-examination would move into a different merits topic: Timothy's safety precautions. Gold Key: under FRE 611(b), cross-examination should stay within the subject matter of direct examination and credibility unless the court allows additional inquiry as if on direct. Silver Key: compare the direct topic to the cross topic before asking whether the new topic is broadly relevant. Ownership and safety precautions are different topics, and the stem gives no court permission to expand the scope.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because the stem points out that Mary did not ask for a hostile-witness ruling. The breaker is that hostile status affects the use of leading questions, not the permissible scope of cross-examination. Gold Key: FRE 611(c) handles leading questions for hostile or adverse witnesses; FRE 611(b) handles the scope of cross-examination.

B is attractive because Stephen manages Timothy's business property and looks aligned with Timothy. The breaker is that a witness's relationship to a party does not create unlimited cross-examination. The same scope limit still applies unless the court expands the inquiry.

D is the dominant trap because Timothy's safety precautions are obviously relevant to the negligence dispute. The breaker is the word "entitled." Silver Key: relevance alone does not make cross-examination wide open under FRE 611(b); the new merits topic needs court permission when direct examination was limited to ownership.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested issue, legal rule, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. Federal Rule of Evidence 611(b) provides the governing rule: cross-examination should not go beyond the subject matter of direct examination and matters affecting credibility, though the court may allow additional matters as if on direct examination. Federal Rule of Evidence 611(c) separately addresses leading questions, including when a party calls a hostile witness, an adverse party, or a witness identified with an adverse party.

Here, the direct examination established only ownership of the premises. The attempted cross-examination about inspection cards, warning cones, and nonskid mats concerns reasonable safety precautions, a separate negligence-merits topic. The facts do not say that the court allowed inquiry into additional matters. Therefore the objection should be sustained and the cross-examination should not be permitted on that subject. This confirms the original key and supports the transformed key C.
