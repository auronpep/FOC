### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.** The sudden drop of an overhead prop during ordinary show use is the kind of abnormal event that can support an inference of negligence when the defendant retained operational responsibility, received inspection reports, and the injured guest did not contribute to the accident.

**Gold Key:** Res ipsa is an inference bridge. It can work when the plaintiff cannot name the precise defect; it does not require direct proof of the exact failure.

**Silver Key:** Read the array as an “inference vs. proof” fight. The answer that treats missing direct proof as the reason res ipsa is useful beats the answer that treats missing direct proof as fatal.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells the contractor shortcut: because Timothy’s rigging company serviced the track, Lydia must be out. The breaker is the word **always**; the stem says Lydia retained operational responsibility and received the reports, so a contractor does not automatically erase the res ipsa responsibility link.

**B.** This choice sells the precision trap: no named clamp, cable, or motor defect, no res ipsa. **Gold Key:** res ipsa exists for cases where direct proof of the specific negligent act or defect is missing but the event itself supports the negligence inference.

**C.** This choice sells the plaintiff-friendly overreach: if the prop fell, Martha wins outright. The breaker is the remedy jump; res ipsa permits an inference of negligence, not automatic judgment.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The credited outcome is unchanged from the original: res ipsa loquitur is likely available. Restatement (Second) of Torts § 328D permits an inference of negligence when the event is the kind that ordinarily does not occur absent negligence, other responsible causes including the plaintiff are sufficiently eliminated, and the indicated negligence is within the defendant’s duty. ([Open Casebook][1]) Courts also treat res ipsa as a jury-permissive inference, not an automatic directed verdict for the plaintiff. ([Justia][2]) The control element is not applied as a rigid magic phrase in modern formulations; the point is whether the evidence sufficiently points to the defendant’s responsibility for the probable causes. ([Justia][3])

Under that rule, Lydia’s retained show-use authority and receipt of inspection reports preserve the responsibility link despite Timothy’s contractor role. Martha’s normal position and lack of contact with the controls eliminate her as a responsible cause. The variant preserves the original issue, rule, distractor mechanics, and credited answer.
