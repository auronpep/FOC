## 1. Final Question

Ruth leased a street-level shop in an old warehouse from Mary for a nine-year term. The space included a sales room and a small workroom. At the start of the lease, Ruth took possession and, with Mary's oral consent, installed a checkout counter, print-drying racks, wall-mounted display rails, and adjustable gallery lighting for her Scripture-print and handmade-journal business. Both parties complied with all lease terms.

The lease will expire in six weeks. Last week, when Mary asked Ruth about a possible renewal, Mary learned that Ruth had decided not to renew and planned to remove all of the above-listed items on or before the lease termination date. Mary claimed that all of the items had become part of the building and had to remain in the shop. Ruth asserted her right and intention to remove all the items. Both the lease and the statutes of the jurisdiction are silent on the matter in dispute. When Mary consented and Ruth installed the items, nothing was said about Ruth's right to retain or remove the items.

Mary has sued Ruth to enjoin the removal of the items.

**How is the court likely to rule?**

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A commercial tenant installed business equipment with the landlord's consent, the lease is silent, and the tenant plans to remove the items before the lease ends. May the tenant remove the items if she restores the premises or pays restoration costs?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** A commercial tenant may generally remove tenant-installed trade fixtures before the lease ends when the lease and statutes are silent, so long as the removal does not materially injure the real property and the tenant restores the premises or pays the reasonable cost of restoration. Ruth installed the checkout counter, drying racks, display rails, and gallery lighting to operate her Scripture-print and handmade-journal business. Mary consented to the installation, the lease is still running, and Ruth plans removal before the termination date.

The right answer does not give Ruth a free pass to leave holes, wiring damage, or other repairable injury behind. It gives her the removable trade-fixture right plus the matching restoration obligation.

**Gold Key:** A tenant's trade-fixture right is not just "tenant may remove." It is "tenant may remove before the lease ends if the premises can be restored, and the tenant must restore or pay restoration costs."

**Silver Key:** The answer array is built around two axes: who wins and whether restoration is required. The balanced answer that gives the tenant removal rights while preserving the landlord's restoration protection is the clean residual.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A** sells a precise-looking split between attached and unattached items. That is the dominant trap because fixture law often starts with physical attachment, so the "bolted versus resting by weight" line feels lawyerly. The breaker is that attachment is only a factor; tenant-installed items used in the tenant's business may be removable trade fixtures even when attached, as long as removal and restoration are handled properly. **Silver Key:** When the array offers an attachment-only split and a tenant-with-restoration answer, check whether the split ignores the trade-fixture restoration rule.

**C** correctly sees Ruth's trade-fixture removal right, then removes the landlord's protection. That makes it attractive to a student who remembers "trade fixtures belong to the tenant" but forgets the restoration condition. The rule does not let Ruth strip the shop and leave repairable damage behind. **Gold Key:** The tenant's right to remove trade fixtures carries a restoration duty when removal affects the premises.

**D** treats installation as the end of the story. That is tempting because fixtures often become part of the real estate, and Mary's consent does not expressly reserve Ruth's removal right. But trade fixtures are the tenant-friendly exception: items installed by a commercial tenant to carry on the tenant's business may remain removable by the tenant before the lease ends, subject to restoration.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule:** Under the modern default rule, a tenant may remove permissible annexations, including trade fixtures, when the lease and governing statute do not provide otherwise, if the landlord's property can be restored to its former condition and the tenant bears the restoration obligation. The authority floor is Restatement (Second) of Property: Landlord and Tenant section 12.2.

The rule is reflected in multiple authorities. In *Interior Energy Corp. v. Alaska Statebank*, 771 P.2d 1352 (Alaska 1989), the Alaska Supreme Court adopted the Restatement approach and treated tenant-installed business property as removable if the parties had not agreed otherwise and the premises could be restored. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/alaska/supreme-court/1989/s-2309-1.html. In *State v. Teller Native Corp.*, 909 P.2d 847 (Alaska 1995), the court stated that, where a lease is silent, removal at lease termination depends on whether the property can be restored within a reasonable time. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/alaska/supreme-court/1995/s-6160-1.html. In *Consiglio v. Carey*, 421 N.E.2d 1257 (Mass. App. Ct. 1981), the court required restoration after tenant-fixture removal. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/court-of-appeals/1981/12-mass-app-ct-135-1.html. *Chapman v. Katz*, 862 N.E.2d 735 (Mass. App. Ct. 2007), is also consistent with the trade-fixture distinction for tenant-installed business property. Source: https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ma-court-of-appeals/1419276.html.

Georgia's codified rule also shows the same bar-exam default: during the tenancy, a tenant may remove trade fixtures erected by the tenant; after the term and possession end, remaining trade fixtures are treated as abandoned to the landlord. Ga. Code section 44-7-12. Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/2020/title-44/chapter-7/article-1/section-44-7-12/.

But Ruth also cannot remove them with no repair obligation. The clean answer is B: Ruth may remove the items if she reasonably restores the premises or pays restoration costs.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-REALPROP-TRADE-FIXTURE-01:** A commercial tenant may remove trade fixtures installed for the tenant's business before the lease ends, unless the lease or statute says otherwise, if the premises can be restored.

**GK-REALPROP-RESTORE-01:** The trade-fixture removal right is paired with a restoration duty; the tenant must restore the premises or pay reasonable restoration costs.
