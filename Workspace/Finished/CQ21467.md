### 7. Full right-answer explanation
C is correct. FRE 410 protects a defendant's statement made during plea discussions with an attorney for the prosecuting authority when those discussions do not result in a guilty plea. Gold Key: failed plea negotiations with the prosecutor's lawyer are protected because they failed; the rule does not require an entered plea, a signed agreement, or an oath in court. Silver Key: read the answer array as a fight between the actual trigger and extra formality traps. The stem gives the trigger directly: defense lawyer, prosecuting attorney, possible guilty plea, defendant's statement, and no resulting guilty plea.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because "no guilty plea" sounds like nothing formal ever happened, so students treat the conversation as ordinary admissible talk. The breaker is that a failed plea discussion is the protected category; no guilty plea is the reason the exclusion applies, not a reason to admit the statement.

B is attractive because courtroom plea proceedings and sworn statements sound like the safest version of plea protection. The breaker is that Rule 410 separately protects statements made during plea discussions with a prosecuting attorney, even when the statement was not made under oath in court.

D is attractive because a signed plea agreement feels like the moment legal protection should begin. The breaker is that Rule 410 does not require a written or signed agreement; it protects qualifying plea discussions that fail to produce a guilty plea.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Evidence, Privileges and Other Policy Exclusions, Plea negotiations and withdrawn plea offers, and the verified outline code is 35030303. The current Federal Rules of Evidence, as published by the Office of the Law Revision Counsel in the U.S. Code as amended to January 23, 2026, state in Rule 410(a) that qualifying plea-related evidence is not admissible against the defendant who made the plea or participated in the plea discussions. Rule 410(a)(4) covers a statement made during plea discussions with an attorney for the prosecuting authority if the discussions did not result in a guilty plea or resulted in a later-withdrawn guilty plea. The official U.S. Courts rules page confirms the Evidence Rules were last amended in 2024, and the current U.S. Code text of Rule 410 carries the same controlling language. Sources checked: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title28%2Ftitle28a%2Fnode232%2Farticle4 and https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/current-rules-practice-procedure/federal-rules-evidence.

Daniel's statement was made during a plea-negotiation meeting with an assistant United States attorney, while Daniel's lawyer was present, and the discussion did not result in a guilty plea. The prosecutor is offering the statement against Daniel in the government's case-in-chief. That is the exact prohibited-use lane. No exception in Rule 410(b) is triggered by these facts because Daniel did not introduce another statement from the same discussion and this is not a perjury or false-statement prosecution based on an under-oath, on-the-record statement with counsel present. The transformed item preserves the source rule, legal issue, credited outcome, and trap structure. No key disagreement was found.
