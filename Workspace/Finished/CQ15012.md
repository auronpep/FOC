### 6. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. Ruth owns the building whose upstairs rooms depend on the loft passage in Cedar Hall. When Mary split the property by first conveying Cedar Hall while retaining Lampstand Hall, the facts made continued access through that passage necessary to the normal use of Lampstand Hall's upstairs rooms. The deed's silence does not end the access right.

Gold Key: When a common owner severs land in a way that leaves one parcel without reasonably necessary access for its normal use, the law may imply an easement by necessity even though the deed is silent; that easement carries a limited right to make reasonable repairs needed for its enjoyment.

Silver Key: Separate the winning party from the reason. Ruth wins because she has a limited easement-and-repair right, not because she can do anything she thinks might reduce tort exposure.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**C.** This choice keeps the winning side and appeals to fear of liability for an unsafe passage. Silver Key: keep the correct result separate from an unlimited reason.

**D.** This choice treats the silent deed as if it erased all access rights. The breaker is the Gold Key: a necessary access easement can be implied from severance even when the deed does not mention the passage.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged from QID 15012: an implied easement by necessity arose when the common owner severed title while the retained building needed the access passage, and the owner of the dominant estate has a limited implied right to enter the servient estate to repair the easement.

Authority floor: Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes section 2.15 recognizes servitudes created by necessity when a conveyance would otherwise deprive the conveyed or retained land of rights necessary to reasonable enjoyment. A public copy of section 2.15 is available here: https://www.decoulos.com/kitras2/Restatement_Necessity.pdf. Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes section 4.10 states the easement holder's use rights in the servient estate and limits them to uses reasonably necessary for enjoyment without unreasonable damage or interference; its comments include maintenance and repair as reasonably necessary uses. A public copy of selected sections including section 4.10 is available here: https://rowalaska.net/archives/projects/2022_klutina_lake_rs2477/archives/relocation/Relocation_of_Easements_Restatement_Provisions.pdf. The American Law Institute identifies the Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes as covering creation, interpretation, enforcement, and related servitude issues: https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-third/property-servitudes.

On these facts, Lampstand Hall's upstairs rooms need the passage located in Cedar Hall. The access pattern existed while Mary owned both buildings, Mary severed the buildings by conveying Cedar Hall first, and the deed did not clearly show an intent to deprive Lampstand Hall of necessary access. The easement then benefited Lampstand Hall and passed to Ruth when Mary later conveyed it. Ruth's repair work is within the limited right to keep the access easement usable. Peter therefore should not receive an injunction barring the repair work. The tort-liability answer is still wrong because the right is limited to reasonable easement repair, not unlimited self-help.
