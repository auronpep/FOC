B sells the personal-action instinct: Timothy did not carry the soundboard, so the theft feels too distant. The breaker is that Pinkerton liability does not require personal commission; the live question is whether Timothy had effectively withdrawn before the later theft.

D sells the dominant trap by making Pinkerton liability sound unstoppable. The Gold Key kills that overclaim: withdrawal does not erase the existing conspiracy, but effective withdrawal can cut off vicarious liability for later substantive crimes.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged: Pinkerton can impose liability for reasonably foreseeable substantive crimes committed by co-conspirators in furtherance of the conspiracy while the defendant remains a member. Effective withdrawal before the later substantive crime can cut off that later Pinkerton liability, although it does not erase liability for the already-formed conspiracy.

Pinkerton v. United States, 328 U.S. 640 (1946), is the anchor for co-conspirator substantive-offense liability. Smith v. United States, 568 U.S. 106 (2013), confirms that withdrawal does not negate the completed conspiracy offense but can be a defense that ends continuing participation. The District of Massachusetts pattern Pinkerton instruction states the same cutoff in exam-ready terms: Pinkerton liability requires the substantive crime to be committed while the defendant is a member of the conspiracy, and there is no vicarious liability for acts committed after a true withdrawal. Sources checked: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/328/640/, https://www.oyez.org/cases/2012/11-8976, and https://www.mad.uscourts.gov/resources/pattern2003/html/patt0izp.htm.

Timothy's facts support effective withdrawal before the later theft. He told the conspirators he was out, took back the access badge he had supplied, and reported the plan to police. Those facts cut off later Pinkerton liability if the withdrawal was effective before the soundboard theft.
