### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The source row supplies the official key, the pick rates, and the explanation that the seller’s two-week delay was only a partial breach and that land-sale specific performance remained available.  

The verified outline placement is **51060304 — Remedies > Equitable Remedies > Specific performance**. 

The legal rule is unchanged. A late tender on a stated date does not itself discharge the other party’s remaining duties unless the agreement or surrounding circumstances make timely performance important. Restatement (Second) of Contracts §242(c) states that failure to perform on a stated day does not by itself discharge remaining duties unless the agreement and circumstances indicate that performance by that day is important. ([Open Casebook][1])

Specific performance is discretionary but available against a party who has breached or threatens breach, subject to limits including adequacy of damages. Restatement (Second) of Contracts §357 and §359 state the availability rule and the adequacy-of-damages limitation. ([Open Casebook][2]) Restatement (Second) §360 identifies the adequacy factors, including difficulty of proving damages and difficulty procuring a suitable substitute performance with money. ([Matthew Miner][3]) For land-sale contracts, authority recognizes that damages are treated as inadequate for promises to transfer or to buy specific land, including seller-side enforcement against a buyer. ([Caselaw][4])

The credited outcome remains the same: the seller probably succeeds. Mary’s late tender parallels the original partial breach; Timothy’s refusal to close parallels the original buyer repudiation; the action remains one for specific performance.

A closing date in a land-sale contract is ordinarily not a drop-dead condition by itself; a short late tender is usually a partial breach unless the contract or circumstances make time essential.

**GK-CONTRACTS-LYDIA-ROOFTOP-02**
Land-sale contracts are the classic specific-performance setting; money damages are treated as inadequate, and either side may seek specific performance when the other refuses to close.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-CONTRACTS-LYDIA-ROOFTOP-01**
In a 2-yes/2-no because-clause array, do not pick by outcome. Keep the result separate from the reason, then clash the missed-date answer against the time-not-essential answer.
