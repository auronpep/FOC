### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** This is a common-law service contract, not a sale of goods. Under the common-law preexisting-duty rule, a promise to pay more for the same performance already owed is not supported by consideration. Peter had already promised to complete the labor-only installation over the weekend for $900. Lydia's later promise to pay the extra $150 did not buy a new legal performance from Peter; it only induced Peter to finish the same weekend installation he had already promised to perform.

The modern Restatement exception does not save the modification. A modification may be enforceable without new consideration when it is fair and equitable in light of circumstances not anticipated when the contract was made. That exception is not triggered here. Peter could have finished on time if he had started Saturday morning. The time pressure came from his own delay and underestimation, not from an unanticipated outside circumstance. Lydia therefore owes only the original $900.

**Gold Key:** A common-law promise to pay more for performance already owed needs new consideration unless the modification is fair and equitable because of circumstances not anticipated when the contract was made.

**Silver Key:** On a dollar-amount call, separate the amount from the reason. A choice can reach the right dollar figure and still be wrong if its reason attacks a nonissue.

### 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule:** Under the common-law preexisting-duty rule, performance of a legal duty owed to the promisor that is neither doubtful nor honestly disputed is not consideration for a new promise. Restatement (Second) of Contracts sec. 73 states that principle. Restatement (Second) of Contracts sec. 89 provides the modern modification exception: a promise modifying a duty under an executory contract can be binding if the modification is fair and equitable in view of circumstances not anticipated by the parties when the contract was made. The classic preexisting-duty case, *Alaska Packers' Association v. Domenico*, 117 F. 99 (9th Cir. 1902), reflects the same rule against enforcing extra compensation for the same promised performance when the demand rests on no new consideration.

**Application:** Peter promised to install Lydia's fixtures and lettering over the weekend for $900. His later demand for $150 more was a demand for additional compensation to complete the same weekend work. Lydia received no new legal performance in exchange for the extra promise. The sec. 89 exception does not apply because the need for a helper was not an unanticipated outside circumstance; Peter caused the time pressure by choosing not to start on Saturday morning and by underestimating the job.

**Unjust enrichment:** Restatement (Third) of Restitution and Unjust Enrichment sec. 2(2) reflects the principle that a valid contract displaces unjust-enrichment analysis as to matters within the contract's scope. Lydia's receipt of the completed installation was governed by the express $900 contract.

**Impossibility:** The facts do not show impossibility or impracticability. Peter could complete the job with a helper and could have completed it alone with timely performance. The original contract was not discharged.

The extra $150 promise was for the same job, and the problem came from his own late start, not a new unanticipated circumstance. A is the right amount for the wrong reason; B invents impossibility; D leaves the contract lane. Pick C."

## remediation_card

- Card: Same Work, Extra Pay
- Signal: A party asks for more money after already promising the same performance.
- Move: Ask what new legal performance supports the extra promise.
- Tiny rule: Same duty is not new consideration.
- Trap: Choosing the right dollar amount for the wrong reason.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-CONTRACTS-PREEXISTING-DUTY-01:** A common-law promise to pay more for performance already owed needs new consideration unless the modification is fair and equitable because of circumstances not anticipated when the contract was made.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-CONTRACTS-PRICE-REASON-01:** On a dollar-amount call, separate the amount from the reason: a choice can land on the right dollar figure and still be wrong if its reason attacks a nonissue.
