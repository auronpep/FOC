# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

On September 3, Lydia, a wholesaler of Bible-study journals, highlighters, and retreat-supply kits, sent Hannah, who coordinated private Bible-study retreats, a packet containing Lydia's catalog and a signed note that stated: "For the retreat season running October 1 through June 30, we will sell you as many catalog items as you order. We assure you that this offer and the listed prices will remain firm for that entire season." On October 2, before Hannah had replied, Lydia notified Hannah that, effective October 20, prices would increase on several listed journal kits.

Is the price increase effective for catalog orders Lydia receives from Hannah during November?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A seller of goods signs a catalog offer promising firm prices for a long season, then announces higher prices before three months have passed. Are the higher prices effective for orders inside the first three months?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

A. Yes, because Lydia received no consideration for her assurance that she would keep the catalog prices unchanged.

B. Yes, because Lydia's assurance that the catalog prices would remain firm covered a period longer than three months.

C. No, because Lydia's original offer, including the price term, became irrevocable under the doctrine of promissory estoppel.

D. No, because Lydia is a merchant with respect to the devotional supplies, and her signed offer, including the price term, was irrevocable during November.

## 5. Correct Answer

D (original key B).

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

D is correct. Lydia is a merchant in the goods she sells, and her signed catalog note gave assurance that the offer and prices would stay firm. Gold Key: under the UCC firm-offer rule, a merchant's signed offer to buy or sell goods that assures it will stay open is irrevocable without consideration for the stated time, capped at three months. Silver Key: the longer season does not cancel the firm offer; it trims the protected window. November is inside the first three months after September 3, so Lydia's October 20 price increase is not effective for Hannah's November catalog orders.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A sells the ordinary option-contract instinct: a promise to hold an offer open usually needs consideration. Gold Key: UCC firm offers are the tested exception. Lydia's signed merchant offer for goods stayed irrevocable without consideration during the first three months, so lack of consideration does not make the November price increase effective.

B notices the three-month limit and turns it into a total-invalidity rule. The cap does not make a longer assurance worthless from day one. It protects the offer for up to three months, and November remains inside that protected period.

C reaches for a familiar promise-enforcement doctrine because the stem contains a promise to keep prices steady. The stem gives no reliance facts for promissory estoppel, and reliance is not needed once the signed merchant-firm-offer route is available. The right path is the UCC firm-offer rule, not promissory estoppel.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: a merchant's signed goods offer that assures the offeree it will be held open is irrevocable without consideration for the stated time, but the period of irrevocability cannot exceed three months. UCC Section 2-205 is the governing rule. Cornell LII states the uniform text at https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-205, and the New York Senate's current UCC Section 2-205 page confirms the same three-month cap at https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/UCC/2-205. UCC Section 2-104(1), available at https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-104, defines a merchant as a person who deals in goods of the kind or otherwise has merchant knowledge or skill in the transaction.

Lydia is a wholesaler of the goods in the catalog. The packet included a signed note assuring firm prices. Hannah gave no consideration and had not replied, but Section 2-205 makes consideration unnecessary during the protected period. The stated season was longer than three months, so the assurance is capped at three months rather than enforceable for the full season. November falls within three months of September 3, so the price increase is not effective for those orders. The supplied bank key is confirmed.
