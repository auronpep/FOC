### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Gold Key: the Fifth Amendment protects compelled testimonial communication, and forcing a suspect to say a memorized passcode forces her to reveal knowledge from her mind. Silver Key: separate the warrant from the compelled statement. A warrant may let police seize or search a tablet, but it does not automatically force Esther to speak the passcode without a testimonial-privilege analysis.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A. The all-physical-evidence answer overclaims. The Fifth Amendment does not protect every compelled physical feature or item; the passcode matters because saying it communicates knowledge.

B. The custody answer borrows Miranda's setting and makes it do too much. The Fifth Amendment privilege against compelled testimonial self-incrimination can apply outside custodial interrogation when the government compels testimonial communication.

D. The warrant answer is the dominant trap because the valid warrant sounds like it should solve the access problem. The breaker is that a warrant to seize or search the tablet does not erase the separate Fifth Amendment problem created by forcing Esther to orally disclose a memorized passcode.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule is unchanged: the Fifth Amendment protects a person from compelled testimonial self-incrimination. Doe v. United States, 487 U.S. 201 (1988), explains that a communication is testimonial when it explicitly or implicitly relates a factual assertion or discloses information, and it describes the privilege as implicated by forced disclosure of the contents of one's mind. See https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/487/201/.

United States v. Hubbell, 530 U.S. 27 (2000), confirms that compelled production can have testimonial value when the government uses the witness's mental effort to identify or disclose information not already known with reasonable particularity. See https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-166.ZO.html.

Esther is not being required merely to provide a physical characteristic like a fingerprint or voice sample. She is being ordered to orally state a memorized passcode. Because the government did not already know the passcode and offered no immunity, the order compels disclosure of knowledge from her mind. That is her strongest Fifth Amendment argument.
