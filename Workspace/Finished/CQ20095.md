### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**B is correct.** The independent-source doctrine allows admission of evidence when police later obtain it through a lawful source that is genuinely independent of the constitutional violation. Here, the unlawful entry and the warrant were entirely separate: a different detective, unaware of that entry, prepared an affidavit using only supplier records, a volunteer witness, and parking-lot security footage — none of which was prompted by or derived from the unlawful entry. Because the warrant rested on an independent lawful source, the equipment is admissible.

**Gold Key (GK-CRIM-INDEPENDENT-SOURCE-01):** When you see "unlawful entry" + "later warrant by a different officer," check whether the warrant affidavit relied on information that was independent of — not derived from or prompted by — the unlawful search. A genuinely independent lawful source breaks the causal chain and saves the evidence under the independent-source doctrine.

---

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice D (dominant trap):** This choice sells the overclaim that once police taint evidence through an unlawful search, no later lawful action can save it — the evidence is "permanently tainted." It's attractive because students correctly know that the exclusionary rule suppresses evidence obtained through police illegality, and they often extend this to mean *all* evidence related to the illegally viewed items is forever suppressed. **The breaker:** The independent-source doctrine is a recognized exception. When a warrant is obtained on facts wholly separate from the unlawful entry — as here — the evidence is admissible regardless of what the officers saw during the illegal entry. **Gold Key GK-CRIM-INDEPENDENT-SOURCE-01** is the unlock: the warrant was genuinely independent, so "permanently tainted" is wrong.

**Choice A:** This choice sells a false limitation on the independent-source doctrine — that it only protects witness testimony, not physical evidence. It's attractive because students sometimes associate "independent source" with the *Wong Sun* line of cases that involved witness testimony. **The breaker:** The independent-source doctrine applies to physical evidence as well. *Murray v. United States* established that physical evidence observed during an illegal entry is admissible if a later warrant rests on genuinely independent information. The type of evidence (physical vs. testimonial) doesn't limit the doctrine.

**Choice C:** This choice sells the opposite overclaim — that *any* later warrant automatically purges the taint of an illegal search. It's attractive because the independent-source doctrine *does* allow evidence when a later warrant is independent, and students who understand the doctrine partially may overgeneralize it. **The breaker:** A later warrant cures the taint only if it is genuinely independent. If the warrant affidavit was based on or prompted by the unlawful entry, the warrant is tainted. Here it was independent — but the rule is not automatic. The doctrine requires independence, not mere sequence.

---

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing rule:** The independent-source doctrine is an exception to the fruit-of-the-poisonous-tree rule. Evidence obtained from an unlawful search is admissible if police later obtain it through a source genuinely independent of the constitutional violation. *Murray v. United States*, 487 U.S. 533, 537 (1988). The doctrine applies when (1) police had probable cause to obtain the warrant independent of information gained through the unlawful entry, and (2) the decision to seek the warrant was not prompted by what they observed during the unlawful entry. *Id.* at 542. *See also* *Segura v. United States*, 468 U.S. 796 (1984) (independent source applied when warrant based on information unrelated to illegal entry).

**Application:** The unlawful entry was into a workshop. Officers saw power tools but left without touching anything. A *different* detective — unaware of the entry — independently prepared an affidavit from supplier records, a volunteer witness, and parking-lot security footage. The magistrate issued a warrant on that independent information. Under *Murray*, the independent-source doctrine applies because the warrant was based on information unrelated to and unprompted by the unlawful entry.

**Verdict:** The credited answer (No — independent source) is correct under controlling doctrine. The legal issue, tested rule, and credited outcome are **unchanged** from the original. `bank_validation_verdict: PASS`.

#### Wrong-Answer Recovery Paths

**If a student picked D ("permanently tainted"):**
- **What happened:** They overgeneralized the exclusionary rule — they assumed any illegal police action permanently poisons all later evidence.
- **Skipped move:** They didn't consider whether the warrant was based on information independent of the unlawful entry.

**If a student picked A (testimony-only limitation):**
- **What happened:** They accepted a false limitation on the independent-source doctrine, possibly confusing it with the attenuation doctrine's initial application in *Wong Sun* (which involved witness testimony).
- **Skipped move:** They didn't check whether the doctrine actually has a testimony-only restriction (it doesn't).
- **Recovery step:** The independent-source doctrine applies to all evidence types, including physical evidence. *Murray* involved physical evidence (contraband), not testimony.

**If a student picked C (warrant automatically cures):**
- **What happened:** They understood that a later warrant can save evidence, but they didn't appreciate the independence requirement. They confused "later in time" with "cures all."
- **Skipped move:** They didn't check whether the warrant was genuinely independent — they assumed any warrant sufficed.
- **Recovery step:** A later warrant cures only if it is genuinely independent. "Automatically" is an absolute — the law requires independence, not mere sequence.
