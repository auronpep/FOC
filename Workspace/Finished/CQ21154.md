## 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule is Congress's investigatory power in aid of legislation, with the special separation-of-powers limits that apply when Congress subpoenas information connected to the President.

Authority floor: *Trump v. Mazars USA, LLP*, 591 U.S. ___, 140 S. Ct. 2019 (2020), held that congressional subpoenas seeking the President's personal financial information implicate significant separation-of-powers concerns and require a balanced inquiry rather than either automatic immunity or ordinary subpoena review. The Court stated that congressional subpoenas must serve a valid legislative purpose and that courts should consider whether the legislative purpose warrants involving the President and his papers, whether other sources could reasonably provide the information, whether the subpoena is no broader than reasonably necessary, the evidence supporting the legislative purpose, and burdens on the President.
