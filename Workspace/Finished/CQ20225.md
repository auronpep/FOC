### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

D is correct. The facts give Martha a fire, smoke, soot, and intent, but they do not give the required burning of the cottage structure. Gold Key: for common-law arson, the line is charring or burning of the structure, not smoke staining alone. Silver Key: when the call asks whether arson is completed, check the completed-result element before letting intent or dramatic smoke carry the answer.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A has the right "No" result, but it sells an over-demanding version of arson. The breaker is that common-law arson does not require total destruction; slight charring of part of the structure can be enough.

B is attractive because the soot is visible on the dwelling, so it feels like fire damage. The Gold Key breaks it: common-law arson needs burning or charring of the structure, and soot discoloration alone does not cross that line.

C leans on Martha's angry purpose and treats bad intent as a substitute for the completed result. The breaker is element order: intent does not complete arson unless the dwelling structure is actually burned or charred.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Common-law arson required the malicious burning of the dwelling of another. The burning element does not require the whole dwelling to be consumed, but it does require actual burning or charring of some part of the dwelling structure. Smoke, soot, or discoloration alone is insufficient for completed common-law arson.

Authority floor: State v. Shaw, 289 S.E.2d 325 (N.C. 1982), states the common-law line: partial burning or the slightest charring is enough, but discoloration, smoke, and stains are not. State v. Oxendine, 286 S.E.2d 546 (N.C. 1982), likewise explains that some portion of the dwelling itself must be burned, though the least burning of any part is enough. The Criminal Law Deskbook also states under MCM part IV, paragraph 52c(2)(c), that actual burning or charring is required and mere scorching or discoloration is insufficient. The source key is therefore sound: Martha has not completed common-law arson on facts limited to soot discoloration without scorching, charring, or burning.
