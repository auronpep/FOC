**Call:** If Stephen does not sue for partition but brings an appropriate action for contribution against his brothers, which of the following statements most accurately describes the rights of the parties?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (C)

A deed to two or more grantees that does not specify the tenancy is presumed to create a **tenancy in common**. Cotenants must share the **carrying charges** that protect the common title — principal, interest, and real estate taxes — so Stephen can compel Andrew and Philip to contribute their shares of those payments. He cannot compel contribution for the **glazing room**: improvements are not contributable absent an agreement (the improver carries that cost and is credited, if ever, only in a partition accounting). And there is **no rental-value setoff**: a cotenant in sole possession owes the others nothing for occupancy unless he has *ousted* them, and Stephen never excluded his brothers — they simply chose not to occupy. The repair to the roof appears in the facts but in none of the choices, which is correct: a bare contribution action does not reach necessary repairs in most courts either.

**Gold Key:** cotenants share carrying charges (principal, interest, taxes) but not improvements absent agreement, and a cotenant in sole possession owes no rental value absent ouster. **Silver Key:** the choices toggle three sub-rules (carrying costs ON, improvements OFF, setoff OFF) — pick the one that turns on only the true sub-rule, and distrust the "everything" choice.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A — carrying charges + a rental-value setoff.** It gets the recoverable core exactly right (principal, interest, taxes), which makes a knowledgeable student trust the rest, and the setoff feels fair — Stephen has had the workshop to himself for years, so surely the brothers can charge him rent. The breaker is ouster: a cotenant in possession is accountable for rental value only when he has affirmatively excluded the others, and a setoff against a contribution claim otherwise arises only in a partition accounting — neither is present here. **Gold Key:** no rental value (and no rental setoff) absent ouster.

**B — improvements + carrying charges + setoff (the everything answer).** It rewards the instinct that Stephen paid for everything alone, so the brothers should reimburse all of it minus rent — the maximal, "fairest-looking" payout. The breaker is that it bundles two items the law withholds: contribution for improvements (not available absent agreement) and a rental-value setoff (not available absent ouster); stacking true carrying-cost recovery on top does not cure either false add-on. **Silver Key:** the choice that grants the most by bundling a known-false element is the trap, not the answer.

**D — improvements only.** It fastens on the most visible, value-adding expense — the new glazing room Stephen built with his own money — and treats the benefit to the property as the trigger for contribution. The breaker is the improvement rule: a cotenant who improves cannot force the others to share that cost, and this choice also omits the items that *are* recoverable (principal, interest, taxes), so it is wrong on both ends.

## 9. Black-Letter Verification + Legal Reasoning for C (lawyer-confirmation layer)

**Tested rule (confirmed unchanged):** A conveyance to two or more persons that does not specify the tenancy is presumed to create a tenancy in common. Among tenants in common: (1) **carrying charges** — payments of mortgage principal, interest, and taxes that preserve the common estate — give the paying cotenant a right of contribution; (2) **improvements** are not contributable absent an agreement (credited only in a partition/accounting); (3) **rental value** — a cotenant in sole possession is not accountable to the others for the value of his occupancy, and his contribution claim is not subject to a rental-value setoff, **absent ouster** (and, where a setoff is allowed defensively, only in a partition or accounting, not a bare contribution action).

**Affirmative reasoning for C:** TIC presumption applies (deed silent on tenancy) → Andrew and Philip are cotenants. Stephen paid carrying charges (principal, interest, taxes) beyond his share → contribution lies for those. Improvements (glazing room) → no contribution. No ouster (brothers chose not to occupy; Stephen never excluded them) and the action is for contribution, not partition → no rental-value setoff. Recoverable: principal, interest, and taxes only. Outcome identical to the source item's credited answer.

**Authority:** TIC presumption — common-law rule (most state recording/conveyancing statutes codify it). No-rent-absent-ouster — *Spiller v. Mackereth*, 334 So. 2d 859 (Ala. 1976) (cotenant in exclusive possession not liable for rent absent ouster; demand-and-refusal needed to establish ouster). Contribution for carrying charges and no contribution for improvements — general cotenancy rule (see "Right of Cotenant to Contribution From Other Cotenants," 23 Notre Dame L. Rev. 503). **Split-doctrine flag (`attorney_review: true`):** some jurisdictions (e.g., California) allow a **defensive** rental-value offset against a possessing cotenant's claim for improvement/preservation expenditures — but only in a **partition or accounting**, which this stem expressly forecloses ("does not sue for partition but brings an action for contribution"), and only where the claim is for improvements/upkeep. The non-partition posture plus the absence of ouster keep the majority outcome clean; research **agrees** with the official key (B → new C). Not a fork — the credited outcome does not move.

**Doctrinal currency:** Not on the hotlist; the only currency note is the partition-only defensive-offset split, flagged above and foreclosed by the stem.

**program_frame** — Real Property, concurrent estates. Tenancy-in-common presumption plus an additive three-variable contribution matrix (carrying charges, improvements, rental setoff). Anchor-dependent; solvable with two taught Gold Keys.

**student_access_test** — Allowed: C3 lessons, subject overlays, the two Gold Keys (contribution scope; no-rent-absent-ouster) and two Silver Keys (contribution matrix; ouster axis). Not assumed: outside cotenancy treatise knowledge or the jurisdiction-specific partition-offset doctrine.

**question_data** — Stem and choices as in Pass-1 §1/§3. Credited answer: **C**.

**distilled_core_question** — Three siblings hold a building as tenants in common; one occupies it alone (no ouster) and alone pays mortgage principal/interest and taxes, plus an improvement and a repair, then sues for contribution (not partition). **call_and_prediction** — Call = "rights of the parties." Predict before reading choices: carrying charges are shared; improvements are not; and with no ouster there is no rental setoff.

**trigger_facts** — (1) deed silent on tenancy → TIC; (2) investment hold → co-ownership, not partnership; (3) Stephen occupies alone, brothers chose not to → no ouster; (4) Stephen paid principal/interest/taxes → recoverable; (5) glazing room → improvement, not recoverable; (6) roof repair → red herring (in facts, in no choice); (7) action is contribution, NOT partition → defensive offset foreclosed.

**governing_c3_lane** — RULE lane (TIC presumption + fixed contribution scope + ouster rule). Deciding phase: CLASH (C vs A).

**choice_by_choice_walkthrough** —

*D — improvements only.*

2. **Student label:** improvements only.
3. **True/responsive version:** would require an agreement to share the improvement's cost.

Tiny rule: principal/interest/taxes shared; improvements not (absent agreement); no rental setoff absent ouster. Trap: charging the occupying cotenant rent with no ouster, or making the others pay for improvements.

## Gold Key(s)
- **GK-REAL_PROPERTY-COTENANT-CONTRIBUTION-SCOPE-01 (distinction):** Tenants in common owe contribution for carrying charges (principal, interest, taxes) but NOT for improvements absent an agreement. *Unlocks:* the improvements traps (D, B). *Trigger:* a paying cotenant seeks contribution and one expense is a value-adding improvement.
- **GK-REAL_PROPERTY-COTENANT-OUSTER-RENTAL-02 (exception):** A cotenant in sole possession owes no rental value, and his contribution claim is not offset by rental value, ABSENT ouster; any defensive offset otherwise arises only in partition/accounting. *Unlocks:* the rental-setoff traps (A, B). *Trigger:* one cotenant occupies alone and a choice charges him rent.

## Silver Key(s)
- **SK-REAL_PROPERTY-CONTRIBUTION-MATRIX-01 (answer_array):** Read A–D as three toggles (carrying = true, improvements = false, setoff = false); pick the choice that turns on only the true toggle, and treat the "everything" choice as the trap.
- **SK-REAL_PROPERTY-OUSTER-AXIS-02 (clash):** When two choices both award carrying charges and differ only by a rental setoff, the axis is ouster — and "does not sue for partition" tells you the defensive offset is off the table too.
