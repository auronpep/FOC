## 6. Right-answer explanation

**B** is correct because a decision in favor of Lydia would need all three points. The writing is short and does not recite the full oral condition, so the parol-evidence issue matters. The six-week language matters because Daniel attempted to cancel before the agreed fundraising period expired. Lydia’s later bank commitment matters because specific performance depends on her ability to perform.

**Gold Key:** In a land-sale specific-performance item, do not stop at “there is a signed paper.” Ask whether the court must decide both enforceability/interpretation and whether the buyer can perform.

**Silver Key:** This is an all-issues array. If each listed issue connects to a required step in the buyer’s win path, the complete bundle beats every partial bundle.

## 7. Wrong-answer explanations

**A** is attractive because it captures the practical fight: Lydia got financing and Daniel tried to cancel before the six weeks ran. The breaker is that it omits the parol-evidence issue, which is part of explaining how the short signed slip interacts with the oral time condition.

**C** is attractive because the short signed slip makes the parol-evidence issue visible. The breaker is that it leaves out two live points: the time period Daniel gave Lydia and Lydia’s ability to close.

**D** is the dominant trap. It captures the writing issue and the six-week timing issue, so it feels complete. The breaker is that a decree compelling conveyance also requires Lydia’s ability to perform; leaving that out makes the answer incomplete.

## 8. Black-letter verification

A land-sale contract falls within the Statute of Frauds. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 131 states that a writing signed by or on behalf of the party to be charged must reasonably identify the subject matter, indicate a contract was made, and state the essential terms with reasonable certainty. ([Open Casebook][1]) A land-transfer contract may sometimes be specifically enforced despite a Statute of Frauds problem where reliance makes specific enforcement necessary. ([Open Casebook][2]) A buyer seeking specific performance of real estate generally must be ready, willing, and able to perform. ([Gordon Rees Scully Mansukhani][3]) The parol-evidence rule remains relevant to whether the oral fundraising period can be considered alongside the signed writing; Restatement sections on the parol-evidence rule are commonly grouped in §§ 209–218. ([Open Casebook][4])
