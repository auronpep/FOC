## Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam-register)

**C is correct.** The testimony that Mary had consumed alcohol and was impaired immediately before takeoff is relevant under FRE 401 because it has a tendency to make a fact of consequence (causation of the crash) more or less probable than it would be without the evidence. In John's products liability claim alleging a defect in the flight-control linkage, the defendant is entitled to offer evidence of an alternate cause — pilot impairment — to show that the defect did not cause the crash. This is a specific condition or state of the operator at the precise time of the event, not character evidence. It is admissible under FRE 402.

**Silver Key (navigation):** Read the precise purpose for which the evidence is offered. When the defendant offers the evidence to explain what actually happened on *this* flight (alternate causation), it is not an attempt to prove Mary's general character for sobriety or carefulness. The call is about admissibility for the purpose stated in the stem, not a free-floating character objection.

## Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is attractive** because students often default to "any evidence about drinking or bad conduct must be character evidence." It sells the familiar Evidence trap of over-labeling specific-act facts as propensity. The breaker is that the evidence is offered as the operator's actual state at the moment of the incident, directly bearing on what caused the crash on this specific flight. It is not offered to show "Mary is the kind of person who drinks and therefore probably caused it through bad character."

**B (dominant trap) is attractive** because the facts involve alcohol and a fatal crash, triggering a strong "this feels unfair to the deceased pilot / widow" moral reaction. Students apply FRE 403 too quickly without first confirming relevance and the exact purpose. The breaker is that the probative value on the central causation dispute in a products liability case is high; the stem provides no facts showing the evidence is *unfairly* prejudicial beyond the ordinary sting of relevant defense evidence. Courts routinely admit intoxication evidence on causation in vehicle/aircraft accident cases.

**D is attractive** because students memorize the prohibition on proving character by specific acts (FRE 404/405) and see any mention of alcohol as "specific conduct used to prove character." The breaker is the same as A: this is not character at all. It is direct evidence of the pilot's condition at the time the plane's controls allegedly failed. The method-of-proof rules for character never come into play.

## Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer (lawyer-confirmation layer only)

The credited outcome and tested rule are unchanged from the source row.

**Governing authority:** Fed. R. Evid. 401 and 402.

FRE 401: Evidence is relevant if (a) it has any tendency to make a fact more or less probable than it would be without the evidence; and (b) the fact is of consequence in determining the action.

FRE 402: Relevant evidence is admissible unless the United States Constitution, a federal statute, these rules, or other rules prescribed by the Supreme Court provide otherwise. Irrelevant evidence is not admissible.

**Application:** In a products liability wrongful death action claiming a manufacturing or design defect in the aircraft's flight-control system caused the crash, the cause of the crash is a fact of consequence. Evidence that the pilot had consumed alcohol and was impaired immediately before takeoff has a clear tendency to make it more probable that pilot impairment (rather than, or in addition to, any defect) was a cause. This is classic alternate-causation / contributory conduct evidence. It is not offered for a propensity inference under FRE 404(a) or (b). Therefore it is relevant and admissible. FRE 403 does not require exclusion on these facts; the probative value on the central disputed issue (causation) is substantial, and the stem supplies no facts establishing unfair prejudice that substantially outweighs that value.

No research contradicted the official key. The rule is stable; no 2023–2024 FRE or Supreme Court changes affect basic relevance or the character/relevance distinction for specific-time condition evidence.
