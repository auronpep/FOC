## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

The correct answer is **A**.

The bank advanced funds that Lydia used to buy the parcel, and Lydia gave the bank a mortgage at the same closing. That makes the bank's loan a purchase-money mortgage. Naomi did finance part of the price, but Lydia gave Naomi only a promissory note. The note created a debt; it did not give Naomi a mortgage.

**Gold Key:** A purchase-money mortgage given as part of the acquisition is property security from the moment the buyer takes title. An unsecured seller note is not a mortgage, and lender knowledge of the unpaid seller does not make the seller's note senior.

**Silver Key:** In a priority call, identify who has real property security in the stem before following notice language. The answer that points to the actual mortgage given at closing beats the answer that only points to knowledge of an unpaid seller.

Choice A is the only answer that matches the bank's secured purchase-money status and the seller's unsecured note.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**C:** This choice is attractive because it reaches the bank-priority result and uses a familiar land-finance device. The breaker is the word "only." A seller can finance part of a land sale through more than an installment land contract; the defect is the reason, not the winner.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The credited answer is correct under the governing purchase-money mortgage rule. A mortgage is purchase-money security when it secures a loan whose proceeds are used to acquire the real estate and the mortgage is given as part of the acquisition transaction. Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages section 7.2 treats both vendor and third-party acquisition lenders as possible purchase-money mortgagees and gives purchase-money mortgage status special priority over claims arising through the purchaser.

Here, the bank's funds were used to buy the parcel, and the buyer gave the bank a mortgage at the same closing. The bank therefore holds a purchase-money mortgage. Naomi, by contrast, received only Lydia's promissory note. A note is a personal obligation. Without a mortgage or retained security interest, Naomi did not become a purchase-money mortgagee. The possible equitable vendor's lien theory does not defeat the bank in this item: the deed recited full consideration paid, the seller did not reserve a lien, and the bank's loan was secured by the actual purchase-money mortgage. The bank's knowledge that Naomi financed part of the price does not turn Naomi's unsecured note into a superior property lien.
