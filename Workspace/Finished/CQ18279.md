### 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. Self-defense is judged from the circumstances as they reasonably appeared at the moment force was used. Gold Key: a reasonable apparent deadly threat can justify deadly self-defense even if the apparent weapon is later discovered to be fake. ### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

B. The actual-gun answer is attractive because the later discovery of the stage-prop pistol feels like it should erase the threat. Gold Key: self-defense turns on a reasonable belief at the time of force, so the later prop discovery does not defeat a reasonable apparent-deadly-threat claim.

C. The deposit-satchel answer is attractive because the demand for property explains why Stephen was confronting Ruth. The breaker is the basis for deadly force: the decisive fact is the apparent handgun and imminent deadly threat, not the property demand by itself.

D. The wait-until-fired answer is attractive because it sounds cautious before authorizing deadly force. The breaker is that the law does not require a defender to absorb the first shot; reasonable belief in imminent deadly force is enough.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Deadly self-defense can be justified when the defendant reasonably believes deadly force is necessary to prevent imminent death or serious bodily harm. Model Penal Code section 3.04(1) frames self-protection around the actor's belief that force is immediately necessary, and section 3.04(2)(b) limits deadly force to threats such as death or serious bodily harm. People v. Goetz, 68 N.Y.2d 96 (1986), confirms that deadly self-defense requires a belief that is both actually held and objectively reasonable. Here, Stephen pointed what appeared to be a real handgun at Ruth while issuing an immediate demand. A reasonable person in Ruth's position could believe she faced imminent deadly force. The later discovery that the gun was a realistic prop does not change the reasonableness of that belief at the time of the shot.
