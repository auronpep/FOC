### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**(C) is correct.** The crime of solicitation is complete the moment the defendant makes the request with the specific intent that the solicited crime be committed. Solicitation consists of inciting, inducing, or urging another to commit a felony with the specific intent that the person solicited commit the crime. The offense is complete at the time the solicitation is made — whether the solicitee agrees or not, and whether any payment is made or act performed. Daniel's later change of heart does not undo the completed crime.

**Gold Key:** Solicitation = complete on request + intent. Withdrawal after the ask is too late.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**(A) — attractive because:** Students naturally think calling off the plan before anything happens should erase liability — it feels like a "take-back" should work. **Breaker:** The crime was already complete the moment Daniel made his request to Marcus. Nothing after that moment — including withdrawal — can undo solicitation. The timing of payment is irrelevant to the completion of the offense. **Gold Key:** Solicitation is complete on request + intent; withdrawal after the ask is too late.

**(B) — attractive because:** Students confuse solicitation with attempt, which requires a "substantial step" toward the crime. **Breaker:** Solicitation does not require any act toward the underlying crime by either party. The request itself, with the required intent, is the entire crime. The "substantial act" standard belongs to attempt, not solicitation. **Silver Key:** When the call asks about solicitation, cut any choice that requires an act beyond the words of the request — solicitation is a words-plus-intent crime, not a step-crime.

**(D) — attractive because:** Students see the officer's agreement and think that agreement is what makes it a crime — it looks like a deal. **Breaker:** The officer's agreement is irrelevant. Solicitation is complete whether the solicitee agrees or refuses. The crime is in the asking with intent, not in the acceptance. Daniel would be convicted even if Marcus had said no.

### 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing rule:** Solicitation is complete when the defendant requests, commands, or encourages another to commit a felony with the specific intent that the crime be committed. The offense does not require performance of any act toward the underlying crime, nor agreement by the solicitee.

**Authority:**
- Common law: Solicitation is complete the moment the incitement is made. *See* 18 U.S.C. § 373 (federal solicitation statute — "solicits, commands, induces, or otherwise endeavors to persuade" another to commit a crime of violence); Model Penal Code § 5.02(1) — a person is guilty of solicitation if "with the purpose of promoting or facilitating its commission, he commands, encourages or requests another person to engage in specific conduct" that would constitute a crime.
- Withdrawal is not a defense at common law because the crime is already complete. *See* PastPaperHero, *Inchoate Crimes; Parties — Solicitation* ("At common law, withdrawal is not a defense to solicitation. The crime is complete the moment the request is made.").
- The MPC recognizes a limited renunciation defense under § 5.02(2) only if the defendant "completely and voluntarily renounces" and prevents the crime — but the MBE tests the common-law rule unless a statute is supplied. No statute is present here.

**Verification:** The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the original. The official key (C) is correct. `bank_validation_verdict: PASS`.

**(B) — Attempt-standard trap:**
- **Why a student picks this:** "No one did anything — how can there be a crime?"

- **Recovery step:** Re-read the call: "charged with solicitation." Solicitation does not require an act. Cut any choice that demands one.

**(D) — Agreement trap:**
- **Why a student picks this:** "Marcus agreed — that makes it a deal."
- **Skipped move:** Did not identify what legally completes solicitation. Focused on the wrong fact (agreement vs. request).
- **Recovery step:** Return to the governing rule: solicitation is complete on request + intent. The solicitee's response is irrelevant.
