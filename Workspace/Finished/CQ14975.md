Full right-answer explanation:
The credited answer is C. Under Klaxon Co. v. Stentor Electric Mfg. Co., 313 U.S. 487 (1941), a federal court sitting in diversity must apply the choice-of-law rules of the state in which it sits. However, when the action concerns real property, the court applies the choice-of-law rules of the state where the property is located (the situs) because real property is governed by the law of the situs. Here, the house is located in State A, so the federal court in State A will apply State A's choice-of-law rules, not State B's. John's domicile (State B) and the location of the fraud (State B) are irrelevant to the choice-of-law determination for real property.
