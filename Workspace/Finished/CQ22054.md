### 7. Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** In jurisdictions that recognize tenancy by the entirety, a conveyance to a validly married couple is presumed to create TBE even in the absence of the words "as tenants by the entirety." **Gold Key (TBE Marriage Prerequisite):** TBE requires that the grantees be validly married to each other — that is the threshold, non-negotiable element. When the grantees are validly married and the grantor conveys "to Timothy and Lydia," the marital status triggers the TBE presumption; no magic words are required. B is the only scenario where Timothy and Lydia are validly married, so B is the only scenario where TBE is available. **Silver Key (Prerequisite First):** In a "most likely" scenario question about TBE, scan each choice for valid marriage before reading any deed language — the deed language follows from the legal status; it cannot manufacture the status.

### 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A — Timothy and Lydia had raised their children together for 22 years but were not married. Hannah, knowing they were not married, executed a deed "to Timothy and Lydia as tenants by the entirety."**
A is attractive because 22 years of raising children together reads like a marriage — these two are practically spouses in every sense that matters to most people. The cognitive move is fairness: they've done everything married people do, so the law should treat them as such. But TBE is a legal status that turns exclusively on valid marriage, not on the lived reality of the relationship. **Gold Key:** Long cohabitation — even decades of shared life — is not a substitute for valid marriage in TBE analysis. Hannah's knowledge of their actual status makes this the scenario most clearly opposed to TBE, and the explicit TBE deed language cannot cure the absence of marriage.

**C — Timothy and Lydia were not married, but Hannah, believing them to be husband and wife, executed a deed "to Timothy and Lydia as tenants by the entirety."**
C is the dominant trap. The deed says "as tenants by the entirety" in clear terms. Students who follow the deed language conclude that TBE is created — after all, the grantor explicitly intended it and used the magic words. But TBE is not conjured by deed language; it is a status that arises only when valid marriage exists. **Gold Key (TBE Marriage Prerequisite):** Explicit TBE language in the deed cannot create TBE when the grantees are not validly married. Hannah's mistaken belief does not change the grantees' legal status. The deed reflects an error, not a valid TBE conveyance. **Silver Key (Prerequisite First):** The "married" fact in B beats the "TBE" language in C — valid marriage is the prerequisite; language is downstream from status.

**D — Timothy and Lydia were not married but believed themselves to be married. After Hannah executed a deed "to Timothy and Lydia as joint tenants," they re-conveyed the land to themselves "as tenants by the entirety."**
D has the most legal activity of any choice: a re-conveyance with explicit TBE language. Students who know that parties sometimes create TBE through self-conveyance and see TBE language might stop here. But the same analysis applies: TBE requires valid marriage. The good-faith belief of marriage — however sincere — does not create a valid marriage, and without valid marriage the TBE re-conveyance is legally ineffective regardless of the language used.

### 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** Tenancy by the entirety — prerequisite and presumption.

**Marriage prerequisite:** TBE is a form of co-ownership available only to validly married spouses. This is the universal requirement in all jurisdictions that recognize TBE. See Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers § 26 cmt. d; see also 4 Thompson on Real Property § 33.06 (2d ed.). Neither deed language, grantor intent, good-faith belief in marital status, nor long cohabitation can substitute for valid marriage.

**TBE presumption from marital conveyance:** In TBE jurisdictions, a conveyance to a husband and wife is presumed to create TBE absent a contrary indication in the deed. Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers § 26; see also traditional common law rule (4 Kent's Commentaries 362). No magic words ("as tenants by the entirety") are required when the grantees are validly married.

**Effect of deed language without marriage:** Explicit TBE deed language to non-spouses does not and cannot create TBE. The deed may be recharacterized as a joint tenancy or tenancy in common depending on whether the four unities are present and whether there is a clear intent to create survivorship. The TBE label alone is inoperative.

**Key agreement:** Official key (D → now B) is correct. No disagreement; no NEEDS_HUMAN flag.

#### 8. choice_by_choice_walkthrough

**Choice A — 22 years raising children, not married, Hannah knew, TBE deed language**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* 22 years of raising children together on the same farm — this reads like a marriage in every practical sense. The deed even says TBE. But TBE turns on valid marriage, not the lived reality of the relationship. The "not married" fact in this choice closes off TBE; the deed language and shared life history are legally irrelevant without valid marriage.
2. *Student label:* Fairness instead of legal test — colloquialism.
3. *True/responsive version would look like:* "Timothy and Lydia had been in a valid common law marriage recognized by the state for 22 years, and Hannah executed a deed to them as tenants by the entirety."
4. *Lawyer confirmation:* TBE is available only to validly married spouses. Long-term cohabitation — even with children, shared property, and years of shared life — does not create the marital status required for TBE. Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers § 26 cmt. d. **Gold Key (TBE Marriage Prerequisite):** No cohabitation period, however long, can substitute for valid marriage.

**Choice B — Timothy and Lydia were married; deed "to Timothy and Lydia" ← CORRECT**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* Valid marriage + joint conveyance. In a TBE-recognizing jurisdiction, a conveyance to a married couple creates a TBE presumption. No magic words ("as tenants by the entirety") are needed. B has the one thing no other choice has: valid marriage.
2. *Student label:* Correct — valid marriage triggers TBE presumption.
3. *True/responsive version:* This IS the true and responsive answer. Valid marriage is the threshold; joint conveyance invokes the TBE presumption even without TBE language.
4. *Lawyer confirmation:* In TBE jurisdictions, a conveyance to husband and wife is presumed to create TBE absent contrary intent. Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers § 26. **Gold Key (TBE Marriage Prerequisite)** confirms B as the only viable TBE scenario.

**Choice C — Not married, Hannah believed married, deed "as tenants by the entirety"**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* The deed explicitly says "as tenants by the entirety." A student reading the deed language stops here and thinks TBE is done. But TBE is a status, not a deed label. **Silver Key (Prerequisite First):** The "not married" fact in C kills TBE before the deed language matters. Hannah's mistaken belief changes nothing about Timothy and Lydia's legal status. CUT C — dominant trap.
2. *Student label:* Deed language bait — dominant trap.
3. *True/responsive version would look like:* "Timothy and Lydia were married, and Hannah, believing them to be married, executed a deed 'to Timothy and Lydia as tenants by the entirety.'"
4. *Lawyer confirmation:* Explicit TBE deed language to non-spouses does not create TBE. The marital status is the prerequisite; deed language is downstream from status and cannot override its absence. Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers § 26 cmt. d. **Gold Key (TBE Marriage Prerequisite)** kills C.

**Choice D — Believed married, original deed joint tenants, re-conveyed as TBE**

1. *Student-accessible C3 signal:* D involves the most legal activity — a re-conveyance with TBE language. Students who know parties can create TBE through self-conveyance see the form being followed and stop there. But the same principle applies: TBE requires valid marriage. A sincere, good-faith belief of marriage is not a valid marriage.
2. *Student label:* Form present, valid marriage absent.
3. *True/responsive version would look like:* "Timothy and Lydia were validly married. After Hannah executed a deed 'to Timothy and Lydia as joint tenants,' they re-conveyed to themselves 'as tenants by the entirety.'"
4. *Lawyer confirmation:* A good-faith belief in marital status — even a genuine, sincere conviction — does not create a valid marriage, and without valid marriage the TBE re-conveyance is legally inoperative. Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers § 26. **Gold Key (TBE Marriage Prerequisite)** kills D.
