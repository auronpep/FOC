### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam-Register)

**B is the credited answer under the source row's merger premise.** The agreement and the shooting are different legal units. **Gold Key:** conspiracy does not merge into the attempted or completed target offense because the agreement is punished as a separate danger. Martha and Stephen can be convicted of conspiracy even though the planned attack moved forward.

Attempted murder also stands. Paul survived, but the attempt was complete when Stephen fired the crossbow with the intent to kill. The survival fact blocks a murder conviction; it does not block attempted murder.

The source row treats assault with a deadly weapon as the lesser same-act offense. On that premise, the assault conviction drops out because the same shot is the act that supports attempted murder. **Silver Key:** split the charges into the agreement charge and the act charges. Keep the agreement, keep the greater act offense, and drop the lesser act offense when both rest on the same weapon strike.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A** sells the "only completed crime counts" shortcut. It is attractive because assault with a deadly weapon was completed while murder was not. The breaker is that attempt is itself a completed inchoate offense once the defendant, with intent to kill, takes the required step toward killing. Paul did not need to die for attempted murder to stand, and the conspiracy was complete at the agreement.

**C** sells a strong lawyerly test: if each offense has distinct elements, all convictions can stand. It is attractive because conspiracy plainly has an agreement element and attempted murder plainly has an intent-plus-act element. The breaker is the source's same-act lesser-offense premise: the assault is being treated as the lesser offense swallowed by the attempted murder conviction when both arise from the same weapon strike. **Gold Key:** conspiracy remains separate, but the lesser same-act offense does not survive beside the greater act offense under the source's merger rule.

**D** sells overgeneralized merger. It is attractive because students remember that some inchoate crimes merge and then apply that memory to every preparatory offense. The breaker is the non-merger rule for conspiracy. The agreement is its own punishable wrong, so conspiracy does not merge into attempted murder.

### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule.** Conspiracy is generally treated as separate from the target offense and does not merge with the substantive or attempted offense. In *Pinkerton v. United States*, 328 U.S. 640 (1946), the Supreme Court recognized conspiracy and substantive offenses as separate and distinct, even when acts overlap. The separate-offense analysis is also framed by *Blockburger v. United States*, 284 U.S. 299 (1932), which asks whether each statutory provision requires proof of a fact the other does not.

**Lesser-offense merger.** General merger doctrine drops the lesser offense when the same act simultaneously fulfills a greater and lesser offense. Cornell's Wex summary states that in criminal law, when a single act fulfills two offenses, the lesser offense drops out. Justia's lesser-included-offense discussion notes that whether assault with a deadly weapon is included in a homicide-type offense can depend on whether the jurisdiction uses an elements test, pleadings test, or related-offense approach.

**Source-key confirmation with caveat.** The transform preserves the source row's legal issue and credited outcome: conspiracy and attempted murder stand, while assault with a deadly weapon merges into attempted murder. Research confirms the conspiracy non-merger rule and the general lesser-offense merger principle. Research also shows that whether assault with a deadly weapon is necessarily a lesser-included offense of attempted murder is jurisdiction-sensitive, especially under a strict elements test. Therefore this item is not a clean national-rule PASS; it is marked `FORK_OR_SPLIT` and should receive attorney review before live use.
