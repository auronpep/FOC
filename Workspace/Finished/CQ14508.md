### 6. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Lydia clearly repudiated before closing by saying she would not go through with the sale. A repudiating party can sometimes retract before performance is due, but not after the injured party materially changes position in reliance on the repudiation. Stephen bought a different parcel at a higher price before Lydia tried to retract. That substitute purchase was a material reliance move. Lydia's March 11 retraction therefore did not undo the repudiation, and Stephen did not have to tender the price at the original April 18 closing to preserve his breach claim.

Gold Key: A repudiating party may retract only before the injured party materially changes position in reliance, treats the repudiation as final, or sues; after material reliance, the retraction is ineffective.

Silver Key: Put the three dates in order: repudiation, reliance move, retraction. If the reliance move comes before the retraction, do not let the later performance date rescue the repudiator.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because land-sale contracts normally involve concurrent closing duties: deed and payment are exchanged at the same closing. The breaker is the repudiation sequence. Once Lydia repudiated and failed to retract effectively, Stephen's failure to tender at the later closing did not defeat his claim. Gold Key: the reliance move before retraction keeps the repudiation alive and excuses Stephen from treating April 18 as an ordinary closing.

C sells a clean timing shortcut: Lydia retracted before the closing date, so the deal should be back on. The breaker is that the performance date is not the only timer. Stephen had already bought replacement land before Lydia's retraction, so the retraction came too late. Silver Key: the decisive clock is repudiation -> reliance -> retraction, not repudiation -> retraction -> closing.

D is the dominant trap because it reaches the right result for a sweeping reason. Communication alone does not make every repudiation permanently irrevocable. A repudiation can be retracted before material reliance, final treatment, or suit. The Gold Key kills the overclaim: Stephen wins because reliance happened before retraction, not because retraction is always impossible after receipt.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The verified outline code is `56050403`, listed in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md` as Contracts, Performance, Breach, and Excuse > Breach > Anticipatory repudiation.

The governing common-law authority is Restatement (Second) of Contracts Section 256(1): a statement's effect as a repudiation is nullified by retraction only if the retraction reaches the injured party before the injured party materially changes position in reliance on the repudiation or indicates that the repudiation is final.
