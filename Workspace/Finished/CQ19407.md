### 1. Final question
At Lydia's privately owned Scripture-art studio, Timothy's hand was burned when a wax warmer tipped during a paid candle-making class. While EMTs wrapped Timothy's hand, Paul, the studio supervisor, said, "Lydia's studio will cover your ambulance ride and burn-clinic bills." Paul said nothing about who caused the warmer to tip. At Timothy's negligence trial against the studio, Timothy offers Paul's statement to prove the studio was liable for the burn. How should the court rule?

May an injured plaintiff use a supervisor's promise to pay ambulance and medical bills as proof that the business was liable for the injury?

### 6. Full right-answer explanation
D is correct. The statement is a promise to pay medical or similar expenses resulting from an injury, and Timothy is offering it to prove the studio was liable for that injury. FRE 409 blocks that use. Gold Key: Rule 409 covers furnishing, promising to pay, and offering to pay medical, hospital, or similar expenses; it does not require a disputed claim. Silver Key: sort the statement by container first, then by purpose. The container is medical-payment policy, and the purpose is liability proof, so the court excludes it.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because "disputed claim" sounds like the formal trigger for excluding settlement-related proof. The breaker is that this is not the compromise-offer container. FRE 409 is the medical-payment container, and it does not wait for a disputed claim. Gold Key: the disputed-claim trigger belongs to Rule 408, not to Rule 409.

B is attractive because it tries to draw a clean line between money already paid and money merely promised. The breaker is the word "promise." Rule 409 reaches furnishing, promising to pay, and offering to pay medical or similar expenses, so a promise to cover ambulance and burn-clinic bills is inside the rule.

C is attractive because the promise may feel like a practical signal that Paul believed the studio bore responsibility. The breaker is the offered purpose. Timothy is using the statement to prove liability, and that is the exact use Rule 409 excludes.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue is Evidence, Privileges and Public Policy Exclusions, Payment of medical expenses. The verified outline code is `35030305`.

FRE 409 makes evidence of furnishing, promising to pay, or offering to pay medical, hospital, or similar expenses resulting from an injury inadmissible when offered to prove liability for the injury. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_409

FRE 408 is the neighboring compromise-offer rule and uses the disputed-claim frame for compromise negotiations. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_408

Those authorities confirm the credited outcome. Paul's statement was a promise to cover ambulance and burn-clinic bills. Timothy offers that promise to prove the studio's liability for the burn. Because the evidence sits inside Rule 409's medical-payment exclusion and is offered for the barred liability purpose, the court should exclude it. The legal issue, tested rule, trap structure, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item.
