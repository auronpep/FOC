# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question — stem + call

Naomi, an ailing widow, executed a deed to her hillside vineyard naming her two sons, Daniel and Timothy, as joint tenants with full right of survivorship. Because Daniel was away on a long journey across the sea, Naomi handed the deed to Timothy, telling him to let Daniel know about it as soon as he returned. She explained that she was conveying the vineyard now, while she was alive, so that her sons would not be burdened with the inheritance levy, but that she wished to keep tending the vines and living in the farmhouse until her death. Timothy had the deed duly recorded at the land registry and then returned it to Naomi, asking her to keep it for him in her strongbox. Naomi went on tending the vineyard and living in the farmhouse for about six weeks, until she died. Timothy died ten days later, never having worked the vineyard and never having told Daniel about the conveyance. Naomi's will left all her property to her sister, Lydia. Timothy's will left all his property to his friend, Philemon.

**Who is entitled to the vineyard?**

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A grantor deeds land to two people as joint tenants with right of survivorship and hands the single deed to only one of them, who records it and then returns it to the grantor for safekeeping. The grantor dies; then the joint tenant who held and returned the deed dies, leaving a will to a friend. Does the surviving joint tenant take the whole, or does the deceased joint tenant's friend take a share?

- **C:** Daniel, because Timothy received the deed as Daniel's agent, and Daniel succeeded to Timothy's interest upon Timothy's death.
- **D:** Lydia, because Naomi delivered the deed to Timothy in an attempt to avoid the inheritance levy while making a testamentary disposition.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

C is correct. A deed takes effect on delivery to and acceptance by the grantee or the grantee's agent, and delivery to one co-tenant is delivery to all co-tenants — so handing the deed to Timothy delivered it to Daniel as well. Acceptance is presumed when a conveyance benefits the grantee, and recording confirms the present intent to pass title; Timothy's recording while Naomi was alive completed the conveyance of a joint tenancy to both sons. Returning the deed to Naomi for safekeeping did not unwind a conveyance that had already vested.

**Gold Key:** A joint tenant's interest ends at death and passes automatically to the surviving joint tenant by right of survivorship — it is not devisable. When Timothy died after the joint tenancy had vested, his interest passed by survivorship to Daniel, and his will to Philemon could not touch it. **Silver Key:** Two choices name Lydia on two different theories — that pairing is bait; the real contest is Philemon (the deceased JT's friend) versus Daniel (the surviving JT), and survivorship decides it for Daniel.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

- **A (Philemon) — DOMINANT TRAP.** The deed went into Timothy's hands and Timothy's will gives everything to Philemon, so it feels like a clean chain: Timothy's share flows to his chosen beneficiary. **Gold Key:** a joint tenant's interest is extinguished at death and passes by survivorship, not by will — Timothy had nothing devisable to leave Philemon, so the survivorship right defeats the devise.
- **B (Lydia — deed returned / never occupied).** The deed sat back in Naomi's strongbox at her death and Timothy never worked the vineyard, which reads like a conveyance that never fully took hold. Delivery and acceptance had already completed the transfer when Timothy recorded it; occupancy is not required, and returning the deed to the grantor for safekeeping does not undo a conveyance that has already vested.
- **D (Lydia — testamentary).** Naomi's wish to keep living there until death and her tax-avoidance motive make the arrangement sound like a disguised will, defeasible for want of will formalities. A transfer made by a deed delivered while the grantor is alive is an inter vivos conveyance regardless of motive or a reserved right to occupy; a testamentary disposition is one made by will, which this was not.

## 9. Black-letter verification + right-answer legal reasoning

Tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are **UNCHANGED** from the original: a deed delivered to one of several co-tenants (with acceptance presumed because beneficial, and recording evidencing delivery) effects a present conveyance to all; once vested, return of the deed to the grantor does not revoke it; and on a joint tenant's death his interest passes by right of survivorship to the surviving joint tenant rather than by devise.
