### 2. Distilled Core Question

A borrower promises to repay a loan, later spends the money, leaves for months, and has not repaid. Did that alone make the loan larceny by trick or false pretenses?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is the best answer. Peter made a promise to repay a loan. The stem does not say he lied about any present or past fact, and it does not say that, when Barnabas handed over the money, Peter already intended never to repay it. Gold Key: a later broken promise is not enough by itself to prove the fraud or deceptive intent needed for larceny by trick or false pretenses. Silver Key: in a both/one/neither theft matrix, test the shared fraud trigger first; if that trigger is missing, all offense choices fall together.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A sells the title-passes version of the theft story: Barnabas handed over money, so false pretenses feels closer than larceny by trick. The breaker is the missing false present-or-past fact and missing proof that Peter already meant not to repay when he received the loan. Gold Key: nonpayment later cannot be treated as the required deception by itself.

C sells the broadest punishment answer: if Peter borrowed money, lost it, left town, and did not repay, both theft labels can feel morally right. The breaker is that both labels require fraud at the front end; the stem gives only a promise plus later nonpayment.

D sells the possession-only version of the trap: Barnabas intended a loan, so larceny by trick sounds available because Peter first received possession. The breaker is that larceny by trick still needs fraudulent inducement and intent to permanently deprive at the time of obtaining possession.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Larceny by trick requires obtaining possession by fraud or misrepresentation with the intent to permanently deprive. False pretenses requires obtaining title through a false representation of present or past fact with fraudulent intent. A bare promise to repay, followed only by nonpayment, does not prove either offense.

Authority floor: Model Penal Code section 223.2 requires theft by unlawful taking or control to be purposeful as to depriving another of property. Model Penal Code section 223.3 governs theft by deception and states the parallel promise rule: deception about an intention to perform a promise is not inferred from later nonperformance alone. Cornell Wex's larceny-by-trick summary confirms the common-law distinction between possession obtained by trick and title obtained by false pretenses: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/larceny_by_trick. The MPC theft-by-deception text checked for section 223.3 is available through H2O/Open Casebook: https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/6034-crim-law-textbook-spring-2023-chan-based-on-balloberman/resources/6.1-model-penal-code-theft-by-deception/.
