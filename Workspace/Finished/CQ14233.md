### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule is the Fourteenth Amendment state-action requirement. The Equal Protection Clause limits state action, not purely private conduct. A private entity's decision becomes state action only when the challenged decision is fairly attributable to the state, such as through coercion, significant encouragement, joint participation, management, or performance of a function traditionally and exclusively reserved to the state.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: U.S. Const. amend. XIV, section 1 is framed against state conduct. The Constitution Annotated states that the Fourteenth Amendment limits governmental entities, not private parties. Source: https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt14-2/ALDE_00000810/. In *Blum v. Yaretsky*, 457 U.S. 991 (1982), the Court held that state regulation, licensing, subsidies, and payment support did not convert private nursing-home transfer decisions into state action absent coercion, significant encouragement, or an exclusive public function. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/457/991/. In *Rendell-Baker v. Kohn*, 457 U.S. 830 (1982), even substantial public funding and regulation did not make a private school's personnel decisions state action when those decisions were not compelled or influenced by the state. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/457/830/.
