### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The supplied row tests QID 19537, a Civil Procedure item on the Klaxon choice-of-law rule. The official key is B: the federal court uses the choice-of-law rules of the state in which it sits.

In Klaxon Co. v. Stentor Electric Manufacturing Co., 313 U.S. 487 (1941), the Supreme Court held that a federal court sitting in diversity must apply the choice-of-law rules of the state in which it sits. The Court reasoned that the constitutional requirement of uniform federal law does not extend to choice-of-law rules, and that Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64 (1938), requires the federal court to follow the substantive law of the forum state, including its choice-of-law rules. The accident location and the defendant's citizenship may be relevant under the forum state's choice rules, but they are not the Klaxon anchor. The transformed outcome is identical: the federal court in Illinois applies Illinois choice-of-law rules.
