### 6. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)
B is correct. Federal-question jurisdiction is not created by putting a federal defense into a declaratory-judgment complaint. The court asks what the natural coercive lawsuit would be if the declaratory posture were stripped away. Here, Ruth's natural suit would be a state-law contract claim for payment. Daniel's federal fire-safety argument would come in only as his defense to paying. That anticipated defense does not make the case arise under federal law. Gold Key: Declaratory relief changes the timing and remedy, not the federal-question test. Silver Key: In a declaratory-judgment posture, flip the lawsuit back to the natural coercive claim before deciding whether federal law creates the claim.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because "declaratory judgment" sounds like a federal procedural device, and Daniel did file in federal court. The breaker is that the Declaratory Judgment Act supplies a remedy only when the court already has jurisdiction. It does not turn every declaratory case into a federal-question case.

C is attractive because it reaches the correct no-jurisdiction outcome and points to Daniel's status. The breaker is that Daniel's private Christian business status is not the jurisdictional test. The problem is not who Daniel is; the problem is that the underlying coercive claim is a state contract claim and the federal issue is only a defense.

D is the dominant trap. It is attractive because the federal fire-safety rules appear on the face of Daniel's complaint, so the filing looks federal at first glance. The breaker is the Gold Key: a declaratory plaintiff cannot manufacture federal-question jurisdiction by pleading the federal defense it expects to raise.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Civil Procedure, Jurisdiction and Venue, Federal Subject-Matter Jurisdiction, Federal-Question Jurisdiction, and the verified outline code is `94010200`.

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1331, federal district courts have original jurisdiction over civil actions arising under federal law. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1331. Under 28 U.S.C. § 2201, the Declaratory Judgment Act allows declaratory relief only in a case already within the court's jurisdiction. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2201. Skelly Oil Co. v. Phillips Petroleum Co., 339 U.S. 667 (1950), confirms that the Declaratory Judgment Act did not expand federal jurisdiction. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/339/667. Franchise Tax Board v. Construction Laborers Vacation Trust, 463 U.S. 1 (1983), applies the same principle where the federal issue would arise only as a defense to a state-created action. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/463/1/. Louisville & Nashville Railroad Co. v. Mottley, 211 U.S. 149 (1908), supplies the well-pleaded complaint baseline: an anticipated federal defense does not create federal-question jurisdiction. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/211/149/.

Those authorities confirm the official key. Daniel's declaratory complaint includes a federal safety-rule defense, but Ruth's natural coercive action would be a state-law contract action for payment. Because federal law would enter only as Daniel's defense, the case does not arise under federal law for § 1331 purposes. The Declaratory Judgment Act does not independently supply subject-matter jurisdiction. The tested rule, legal issue, trap structure, and credited outcome are unchanged.
