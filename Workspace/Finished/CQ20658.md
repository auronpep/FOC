## 1. Final Question
Paul joins a conspiracy to steal valuable supplies from a merchant's wagon. Before any theft occurs, Paul sends a message to every conspirator stating he is withdrawing, returns the horses he had supplied for the wagon, and notifies the city guard of the planned heist. The remaining conspirators later steal the supplies using different horses. Paul is charged with conspiracy and with the later theft under Pinkerton. Which statement is most accurate?

A) Effective withdrawal may cut off liability for the later theft, but it does not automatically erase the earlier conspiracy.
B) Withdrawal erases both the conspiracy and every later crime.
C) Withdrawal has no effect on any charge once a conspiracy exists.
D) Paul avoids all liability only because the stolen supplies are unique property.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
Paul took affirmative steps to withdraw and neutralize his aid before the theft, which may bar Pinkerton liability for later substantive crimes. But joining the conspiracy already completed the conspiracy offense.

A conspirator who effectively withdraws may avoid liability for later substantive crimes, but withdrawal generally does not erase liability for the conspiracy already joined. Paul's actions effectively neutralized his earlier aid but do not erase the completed agreement.
