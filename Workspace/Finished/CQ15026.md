### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** Under the common law, a landowner has no inherent right to the unobstructed flow of sunlight, air, or view across a neighbor's parcel. This is a foundational property rule: the bundle of ownership rights does not include a solar easement by default. Because Peter holds no easement, covenant, servitude, or statutory right to sunlight, Daniel's construction — even though it casts a shadow that increases Peter's costs — does not violate any legal right of Peter's. The court should therefore enter judgment for Daniel.

**Gold Key:** *No common-law right to light, air, or view.* A landowner cannot enjoin a neighbor's otherwise-lawful construction merely because it blocks sunlight, absent an express easement, covenant, or applicable statute. This is the American rule; the English "ancient lights" doctrine was rejected by U.S. courts.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Rule:** Under American common law, a landowner has no legal right to the unobstructed flow of sunlight, air, or view from across a neighbor's property. Absent an express easement, covenant, servitude, or applicable statute, a neighbor may build a lawful structure even if it casts shadows, blocks airflow, or obstructs the view on the adjoining parcel.

**Authority:**
- *Fontainebleau Hotel Corp. v. Forty-Five Twenty-Five, Inc.*, 114 So. 2d 357, 359 (Fla. Dist. Ct. App. 1959) ("There being, then, no legal right to the free flow of light and air from the adjoining land, it is universally held that where a structure serves a useful and beneficial purpose, it does not give rise to a cause of action, either for damages or for an injunction.").
- *Kulbitsky v. Zimnoch*, 196 Md. 504, 77 A.2d 14 (1950) (doctrine of ancient lights and prescriptive easements of light and air not recognized in Maryland).
- 1 Am. Jur. 2d *Adjoining Landowners* § 90 et seq.
- Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes § 4.1 (no implied easement for light, air, or view).
- *Bury v. Pope*, 1 Cro. Eliz. 118, 78 Eng. Rep. 375 (Ex. 1586) (early English case establishing the rule favoring development over light rights; American courts followed the same policy but rejected the "ancient lights" prescriptive easement framework).

**English "ancient lights" doctrine:** Under English common law, a landowner who had received light through windows for 20+ years could acquire a prescriptive easement preventing obstruction. American courts uniformly rejected this doctrine as inconsistent with the policy favoring land development. See Britannica, "Ancient Lights."

**Application:** Daniel's new storage facility is permitted by zoning and building ordinances and serves a useful purpose. Peter has no easement, covenant, or statutory right to sunlight. The shadow, while economically inconvenient, does not violate any legal right. Judgment for Daniel.

**Invariant layer confirmed:** The tested rule (no right to sunlight), the legal issue (whether shadow from a lawful structure is actionable), the credited outcome (judgment for the builder), each distractor's mechanic, the dominant trap (injunction), and the call function are all unchanged from the original.
