### 7. Full right-answer explanation
A is correct. The order forces Stephen to display a physical identifying trait: the shape and style of his handwriting. It does not force him to reveal a thought, belief, memory, or factual admission from his mind. Gold Key: the Fifth Amendment protects compelled testimonial communication, not compelled physical identifiers such as handwriting, voice, fingerprints, or blood. Silver Key: separate what the prosecution wants to compare from what the defendant is being forced to say. Here, the jury is comparing handwriting form, so the refusal should be rejected.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
B is attractive because it turns the word "compelled" into the whole test. The breaker is that compulsion alone is not enough; Gold Key: the privilege blocks compelled testimony, not a compelled display of handwriting used as an identifying physical characteristic.

C is attractive because it sounds like testimony might be the switch that opens the door to using the defendant's own evidence. The breaker is that a handwriting exemplar does not depend on waiver by taking the stand; the sample is physical identifying evidence either way.

D is attractive because "privilege" language can make any courtroom evidence dispute sound like an attorney-client issue. The breaker is the call's privilege: the refusal is based on the Fifth Amendment, while attorney-client privilege protects confidential lawyer-client communications and does not shield an ordinary handwriting comparison.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue is the Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination in a criminal prosecution, routed under Criminal Procedure rather than Evidence because the defendant is asserting a constitutional right against the criminal process. The verified outline code is `75080200`, the deepest listed node for non-testimonial identification evidence in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md`. The source row's supplied Evidence code `35030201` is a real code, but it maps to spousal privileges and does not fit this rule.

The Fifth Amendment provides that no person shall be compelled in a criminal case to be a witness against himself. The governing distinction is testimonial communication versus physical identifying evidence. In *Gilbert v. California*, 388 U.S. 263 (1967), the Supreme Court held that a handwriting exemplar, unlike the content of what is written, is an identifying physical characteristic outside the Fifth Amendment privilege. *Schmerber v. California*, 384 U.S. 757 (1966), supports the same physical-evidence distinction. Research sources checked: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/388/263/; https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/384/757/; https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt6-6-3-4/ALDE_00013440/; https://www.justice.gov/archives/jm/criminal-resource-manual-253-handwriting-exemplars-self-incrimination. The supplied official key is confirmed: the court should reject the refusal because the exemplar is not compelled testimonial communication.
