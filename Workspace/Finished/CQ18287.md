## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Mary, the drama-ministry director at a small church, rented a hand-carved olive-wood manger cradle from Daniel, a Christian craftsman who runs a small home-based Nativity-prop rental business, to use as the centerpiece of the church's outdoor Living Nativity display. The rental agreement was a standard weekly rental at a normal fee. Mary took the cradle home a week before Christmas Eve. To thread a hidden low-voltage LED light cord through the base so the display would not show wires, Mary drilled four small pilot holes in the cradle's bottom plate. On Christmas Eve, while Mary was setting the carved baby-Jesus figurine on the cradle, the cradle's right side rail snapped away from the base and the figurine fell. Mary cannot show whether the failure came from a hidden joint defect in Daniel's carving or from Mary's drilled pilot holes.

What is the best res ipsa loquitur analysis of Mary's res ipsa claim against Daniel?

A renter of a hand-crafted item (a wooden display piece) alters it after rental and the item later collapses; the renter cannot tell whether the collapse came from a defect in the item or the renter's own alteration. Does the res ipsa inference survive?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

The best res ipsa analysis is that res ipsa is **likely unavailable** on these facts. Res ipsa loquitur permits a permissive inference of negligence when (1) the event ordinarily does not occur in the absence of negligence, (2) the instrumentality was within the exclusive control of the defendant, and (3) the injury was not due to any voluntary action or contribution by the plaintiff. The third prong is the killer here: Mary's own post-rental drilling of pilot holes in the base plate is a plausible competing cause of the failure that is attributable to the plaintiff's own handling, not to Daniel's carving. Because the causation is shared between Daniel's hand-crafted cradle and Mary's alterations, the inference that "this would not have happened absent Daniel's negligence" cannot be drawn with the required probability — both Daniel's joint and Mary's drilled holes are independently sufficient to break the rail. **Gold Key:** res ipsa is an inference from circumstances, not a strict-liability backstop; it collapses when the plaintiff's own conduct is a plausible alternative cause.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B. Res ipsa makes Daniel strictly liable for all damage to rented hand-carved items.**

This choice confuses res ipsa (an evidentiary inference that lets the plaintiff get to the jury on negligence) with strict liability (a theory of liability without fault, e.g., ultrahazardous activities, products liability). The doctrine of res ipsa is a way to prove negligence circumstantially; it does not make the renter an insurer of the item. The breaker is the **call focus**: the call asks for the best res ipsa analysis, not for the strongest liability theory; res ipsa is not a substitute for the elements of strict liability, and a strict-liability answer is not responsive to a res ipsa call.

**C. Res ipsa clearly applies because hand-carved manger cradles never collapse unless the maker was negligent.**

This is the **dominant trap**. The "never unless" framing is an absolute-overclaim, and it is exactly backwards on these facts. The collapse can readily be explained by Mary's own post-rental drilling of pilot holes in the base plate — a competing cause wholly attributable to the plaintiff. The "never" half of the choice is false as a matter of common experience (carved wooden cradles can and do fail when a renter drills into the structural base), and the inference collapses when the plaintiff's own conduct is a plausible cause. **Gold Key:** res ipsa fails when the plaintiff's own post-receipt conduct is a plausible alternative cause; "never unless" wording is the tell.

**D. Res ipsa applies only if a state statute required Daniel to inspect every cradle before each rental.**

This choice is a statutory false-premise. The doctrine of res ipsa loquitur is a common-law evidentiary inference that does not require a statutory inspection regime. The inference is drawn from the circumstances of the event itself (type of accident, instrumentality, control), not from any statutory duty. The breaker is the **call focus**: a res ipsa call is a circumstantial-evidence call, not a statutory-violation call. The presence or absence of a state inspection statute is irrelevant to whether res ipsa can be inferred on these facts.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The supplied row tests QID 18287, a Torts item involving a rented folding ladder that collapsed after the homeowner renter had altered the lock pins to make the ladder fit in a closet; the homeowner could not show whether the collapse came from a store defect or the altered pins. The official key is B: "Res ipsa is likely unavailable because the accident may have resulted from the plaintiff's own handling after rental."

Res ipsa loquitur permits a circumstantial inference of negligence where (1) the event is of a kind that ordinarily does not occur in the absence of negligence, (2) the instrumentality was within the exclusive control of the defendant, and (3) the injury was not due to any voluntary action or contribution by the plaintiff. The third element is the failure point on facts of this kind: where the plaintiff has materially altered the instrumentality after leaving the defendant's control, the plaintiff's own conduct is a plausible alternative cause that defeats the inference. Restatement (Second) of Torts § 328D codifies the doctrine, and the third element expressly requires that "the [instrumentality] has not been altered by the plaintiff." Restatement (Second) of Torts § 328D cmt. d, e (control and absence of contribution). The doctrine does not make the renter a strict insurer of the item; it is an evidentiary inference, not a liability theory. The doctrine is also distinct from products-strict-liability and from res ipsa's product-liability analog (the "malfunction theory" in Restatement (Third) of Torts: Products Liability § 3), which is not at issue because the dispute here is renter negligence, not product defect.

The transformed outcome is identical to the original: Mary's best res ipsa argument fails because her own drilling of pilot holes in the base plate is a plausible competing cause attributable to the plaintiff, and the res ipsa inference that "this would not have happened absent Daniel's negligence" cannot be drawn with the required probability.

## choice_by_choice_walkthrough

### A. Res ipsa is likely unavailable because the accident may have resulted from Mary's own alterations after rental.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This is the residual answer. The third prong of the res ipsa test (absence of voluntary action or contribution by the plaintiff) is not satisfied because Mary's own post-rental drilling is a plausible alternative cause.
2. **Student label:** Residual answer (third-prong plaintiff-contribution defeat).
3. **True/responsive version:** This choice already states it.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Res ipsa is unavailable when the plaintiff's own post-receipt conduct is a plausible alternative cause. Restatement (Second) of Torts § 328D (third element and cmt. e: the injury must not be due to any voluntary action or contribution by the plaintiff).

### B. Res ipsa makes Daniel strictly liable for all damage to rented hand-carved items.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The choice mischaracterizes res ipsa as a strict-liability backstop. Visible from the call: "best res ipsa loquitur analysis" — not a strict-liability question.
2. **Student label:** Res ipsa → strict-liability mischaracterization.
3. **True/responsive version:** "Daniel might be strictly liable for some defect in the cradle under a products-liability theory, but that is not what the call asks. The call asks for a res ipsa analysis."
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Res ipsa is an evidentiary inference of negligence, not a strict-liability theory. The doctrine does not make the renter an insurer of the item. Restatement (Second) of Torts § 328D.

### C. Res ipsa clearly applies because hand-carved manger cradles never collapse unless the maker was negligent.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This is the dominant trap. The "never unless" wording is an absolute-overclaim, and the collapse can readily be explained by Mary's own post-rental drilling of pilot holes — a competing cause wholly attributable to the plaintiff. **Gold Key:** res ipsa is an evidentiary inference that fails when the plaintiff's own conduct is a plausible alternative cause.
2. **Student label:** Absolute-when-uncertain overclaim.
3. **True/responsive version:** "Res ipsa would clearly apply if Mary had not altered the cradle after rental. The drilling of pilot holes in the structural base is a plausible alternative cause, so the 'never unless the maker was negligent' wording is not supported by the facts."
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Res ipsa fails when the plaintiff's own post-receipt conduct is a plausible alternative cause. Restatement (Second) of Torts § 328D cmt. e. The doctrine is a circumstantial inference, not a strict-liability backstop; the inference collapses when the plaintiff has materially altered the instrumentality.

### D. Res ipsa applies only if a state statute required Daniel to inspect every cradle before each rental.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The "only if" wording is a false premise. Res ipsa is a common-law evidentiary inference, not a statutory-violation theory.
2. **Student label:** Statutory inspection false premise.
3. **True/responsive version:** "A state inspection statute might give rise to a negligence-per-se claim, but that is not what the call asks. The call asks for a res ipsa analysis."
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Res ipsa is drawn from the circumstances of the event itself (type of accident, instrumentality, control), not from any statutory duty. The presence or absence of a state inspection statute is irrelevant to whether res ipsa can be inferred on these facts.
