### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. The federal government and a state are separate sovereigns. A prosecution by one does not, by itself, bar the other under the Double Jeopardy Clause. The shared discovery event does not change the sovereign identity of the two prosecutions.

Gold Key: double jeopardy asks whether the same sovereign is trying the defendant again. Federal and state prosecutions are separate-sovereign prosecutions.

Silver Key: in a double-jeopardy answer array, check the prosecutor labels before getting pulled into the facts of the search.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The credited answer is unchanged: the state prosecution is not barred because it is brought by a different sovereign from the federal prosecution. The Supreme Court reaffirmed the separate-sovereigns doctrine in Gamble v. United States, 587 U.S. ___ (2019), holding that a crime under one sovereign's law is not the same "offence" as a crime under another sovereign's law for Double Jeopardy Clause purposes. The same-elements rule from Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S. 299 (1932), also confirms that importation and possession have different elements, but the decisive answer here is separate sovereigns. The search distractor remains wrong because ordinary border searches are reasonable without probable cause or a warrant under United States v. Ramsey, 431 U.S. 606 (1977).
