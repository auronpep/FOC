### 7. Full right-answer explanation
D is correct. The complaint is not merely thin; it is so vague that the defendants cannot tell who did what, what happened, or when it happened. Gold Key: Rule 12(e) is available before a responsive pleading when the pleading is so vague or ambiguous that the party cannot reasonably prepare a response; it is not a discovery device for evidence. Silver Key: keep the call on the pleading-stage response problem, not on the defendant's broader wish to learn the plaintiff's proof.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because a student may hear "more definite statement" and treat it like a request for the plaintiff's evidence before answering. The breaker is Gold Key: Rule 12(e) asks for missing pleading details needed to respond, not the plaintiff's evidence file.

B is attractive because "more definite" can sound like later clarification after the case has developed. The breaker is Silver Key plus Rule 12(e)'s timing: the motion is made before filing a responsive pleading, not after trial.

C is attractive because notice pleading is forgiving, so a student may overcorrect and think vague complaints are untouchable. The breaker is that Rule 12(e) exists for the narrow situation where the pleading is so vague or ambiguous that a reasonable response cannot be prepared.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Civil Procedure, Motions, Motion for a More Definite Statement, and the verified outline code is `92080102`. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(e) provides the governing rule: a party may move for a more definite statement of a pleading to which a responsive pleading is allowed but which is so vague or ambiguous that the party cannot reasonably prepare a response; the motion must be made before filing a responsive pleading and must point out the defects and details desired. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(e), Cornell LII text, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_12. The transformed complaint's failure to identify actor, conduct, and timing matches the rule's "cannot reasonably prepare a response" trigger. Rule 12(e) does not let a defendant demand all evidence, is not a post-trial motion, and does not leave vague complaints unchallengeable. The official key is unchanged.
