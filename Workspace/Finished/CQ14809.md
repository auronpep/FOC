### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** Mary is not just repeating Paul's threat. She is repeating Stephen's report that Paul made the threat. That creates two statement layers. The inner layer is Paul's threat. That layer may have a route because Paul is the opposing party and the threat may show Paul's intent. But the outer layer is Stephen's statement to Mary that the threat happened. That outer layer is being offered for its truth, and no exception in the stem covers it.

**Gold Key:** For hearsay within hearsay, every layer needs its own exclusion or exception. A clean inner statement does not rescue an uncovered outer statement.

**Silver Key:** Count the speakers from the witness stand outward. Mary heard Stephen; Stephen heard Paul. Clear Mary's account of Stephen first before letting Paul's statement do any work.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A.** This is the dominant trap. It spots a real feature of the inner statement: Paul is the opposing party, and his own threat can have an admissibility route when offered against him. The breaker is the outer layer. Stephen's report to Mary still needs its own exception, and the stem gives none. **Gold Key:** for hearsay within hearsay, every layer needs its own path.

**B.** This choice sells the same single-layer shortcut from a different angle. Paul's threat may show Paul's intent or state of mind, but Mary is offering Stephen's out-of-court report to prove that Paul made the threat. The state-of-mind route does not cover Stephen's retelling to Mary.

**D.** This choice reaches the inadmissible result but gives the wrong reason. The testimony is not being excluded because the threat is a prior bad act offered for propensity or impeachment. The actual defect is hearsay within hearsay: one layer may be usable, but Stephen's layer has no exception.

### 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The governing rule is unchanged from the source item. Federal Rule of Evidence 801(c) defines hearsay as an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of what it asserts. Rule 802 excludes hearsay unless a federal statute, the Evidence Rules, or another Supreme Court-prescribed rule provides otherwise. Rule 801(d)(2)(A) treats a party's own statement, offered against that party, as nonhearsay. Rule 803(3) can cover a declarant's then-existing state of mind, such as intent or plan. Rule 805 controls the tested issue: hearsay within hearsay is not excluded only if each part of the combined statement conforms to an exception or exclusion. Authority reviewed: 28 U.S.C. Appendix, Federal Rules of Evidence, Article VIII, including Rules 801, 802, 803, and 805, https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title28%2Ftitle28a%2Fnode232%2Farticle8.

Applied here, Paul's quoted threat may have an inner-layer route. But Stephen's report to Mary is a separate out-of-court statement offered to prove that Paul threatened him. The stem gives no present-sense, excited-utterance, dying-declaration, former-testimony, statement-against-interest, or other exception for Stephen's report. The credited answer remains inadmissible hearsay not within any exception.

No doctrinal-currency hotlist item is triggered. The official key is consistent with FRE 805.
