### Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam-register)
The facts clearly establish larceny. Timothy took donated ancient artifacts from their proper display area (taking), moved them past the prayer garden toward dormitories (carrying away - even small movement satisfies this element), and stored them in a locked storage room (concealment). The artifacts are tangible personal property owned by the missionary organization. Timothy exercised dominion over property that did not belong to him, knowing it was donated for mission purposes. When he seized and moved the artifacts while intending to permanently deprive the organization of their use or value, all six elements of larceny were satisfied. 

**Silver Key:** Focus first on the movement element - students often fixate on the need to exit the premises, but larceny requires only any movement, however slight, away from its original location. The prayer garden to dormitory path proves the carrying-away element.

**Choice B (non-movement-as-defense):** What made this choice attractive: Many equate larceny with shoplifting scenarios requiring movement beyond the store, but the law requires only any movement. ### Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer
Larceny requires: (1) taking and (2) carrying away of (3) tangible property (4) of another, (5) by trespass or without consent, (6) with intent to permanently deprive. 

**Authority:** California Penal Code § 484; Common Law tradition dating back to Sir Edward Coke's "In the King's Bench..." (1599); Restatement (Third) of Torts § 17.

**Analysis:** (1) Taking: Timothy physically seized the artifacts from their display area - this satisfies the taking element. (2) Carrying away: Moving artifacts from the prayer garden area to dormitories constitutes carrying away, even small movements suffice. (3) Tangible property: Ancient artifacts are physical, valuable items. (4) Of another: The artifacts belonged to the missionary organization, not Timothy. (5) By trespass: Timothy lacked authorization to take artifacts intended for mission display. (6) Intent to permanently deprive: Timothy's seizure with concealment shows intent to permanently deprive the organization of use or value.

The credited outcome (larceny) matches the original. The legal issue (whether Timothy's actions constitute larceny despite hiding the artifacts in a storage room) is identical to the original case.
