### 1. Final question
At a weekend Christian family reunion held at a rented lakeside lodge for fellowship, Bible study, and outdoor activities, someone broke into the main lodge kitchen late at night and took several coolers of food and supplies that had been prepared for the group. Investigators found a muddy footprint from a size-nine hiking boot on the wooden deck right outside the forced window. Police later discovered a pair of size-nine hiking boots in the duffel bag of Daniel, a young adult cousin who was staying in one of the reunion cabins with other relatives. Daniel objects that size nine is one of the most common shoe sizes for adult men and that these ordinary boots cannot be linked to the exact print at the lodge. How should the court rule on the prosecution's offer of the boots into evidence?

### 6. Student black-letter-law answer explanation
D is correct. Under FRE 401, evidence is relevant if it has any tendency to make a fact of consequence more or less probable than it would be without the evidence. The size-nine boots found in Daniel's duffel at the reunion cabins have some tendency to connect him to the size-nine print at the lodge kitchen, even though size nine is common and the boots are not uniquely matched to the print. That weakness affects only the weight the jury gives the evidence, not whether it clears the low relevance threshold for admission. FRE 402 makes relevant evidence admissible unless another rule excludes it.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation
D is correct. The boots satisfy FRE 401's "any tendency" test. A size match between the crime-scene print and boots found in the defendant's possession at the scene makes it at least slightly more probable that Daniel was present and involved in the break-in than it would be without that evidence. The fact that many men wear size nine does not erase the probative link; it simply means the evidence is not strong on its own. The commonness of the characteristic goes to weight for the jury, not to the gatekeeping question of relevance. Gold Key: FRE 401 requires only "any tendency"; weak or non-distinctive physical evidence still counts if it moves the probability needle at all. Silver Key: when a choice demands an "exact print" match or "unique identification" before the evidence can come in, cut it immediately — that is a sufficiency or authentication demand, not the relevance test.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because it feels fair to require the defendant to own up to the boots before they can be used against him. The breaker is that FRE 401 does not condition relevance on the defendant's admission or on any authentication step being completed first. The boots' tendency to connect Daniel to the scene exists independently of whether he admits wearing them.

B is attractive because it sounds precise and forensic: if the boots cannot be tied to the exact print, they should not be used. The breaker is that FRE 401 does not require the evidence to prove the exact source or to be conclusive. "Any tendency" is deliberately low; exact linkage is a matter of weight or, at most, authentication under FRE 901, not a prerequisite for relevance.

C is attractive because common traits feel non-probative at first glance. The breaker is the explicit "any tendency" language in FRE 401. Even common characteristics can have probative value when combined with other facts (here, the boots were found in Daniel's possession at the reunion site where the print was left).

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested rule, legal issue (threshold relevance of weak/non-unique physical evidence), and credited outcome are identical to the source question. The governing authority is the text of the rules themselves.

FRE 401: "Relevant evidence" means evidence having any tendency to make the existence of any fact that is of consequence to the determination of the action more probable or less probable than it would be without the evidence. (Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_401 — stripped of tracking parameters.)

FRE 402: Relevant evidence is admissible unless any of the following provides otherwise: the United States Constitution; a federal statute; these rules; or other rules prescribed by the Supreme Court. (Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_402)

The Advisory Committee Note to FRE 401 confirms the low, logical-relevance standard drawn from Thayer: the law furnishes no mechanical test; relevancy is a matter of logic and general experience. The Note explicitly rejects Wigmore's higher "legal relevance" standard that would have required more probative force. Common or non-unique circumstantial evidence routinely clears this bar; its weakness is for the jury under the "weight" function.

No doctrinal-currency issue applies (FRE 401 was not amended in the 2023–2024 cycle that affected Rules 106, 702, etc.). The credited answer remains correct under current black-letter law. The variation changes none of the legal substance.
