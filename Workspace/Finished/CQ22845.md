# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT — Q22845

## 1. Final Question

Sister Lydia died, leaving a trust fund of $80,000 to be paid to her "most faithful missionary." The Grace Global Mission, a corporation and citizen of State A that holds the funds as trustee, identified three missionaries—Peter, Paul, and Barnabas—all of whom have asserted a claim to the full amount. Peter is a citizen of State A, Paul is a citizen of State B, and Barnabas is a citizen of State C. Knowing it had no legal right to the trust funds itself, Grace Global Mission sought to bring an interpleader action in federal district court to determine which missionary should receive the funds.

May Grace Global Mission bring this interpleader action in federal court?

**A:** Yes, because there is complete diversity of citizenship among the three missionaries.

**B:** No, because Grace Global Mission has no legal right to the property at issue.

**C:** No, because there is no federal subject matter jurisdiction over the claim.

**D:** Yes, because Grace Global Mission has no legal right to the property at issue.

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 22, an interpleader action is a procedural device that requires an independent basis for federal subject matter jurisdiction. When relying on diversity jurisdiction (28 U.S.C. § 1332), there must be complete diversity between the plaintiff-stakeholder and all defendants (the claimants), and the amount in controversy must exceed $75,000. Here, the plaintiff (Grace Global Mission) is a citizen of State A, and one of the defendants (Peter) is also a citizen of State A. Because there is no complete diversity between the stakeholder and all claimants, the federal court lacks subject matter jurisdiction under Rule 22 and § 1332.

> **Gold Key:** *Rule 22 interpleader requires an independent basis for federal jurisdiction, such as diversity between the stakeholder (plaintiff) and every claimant (defendant).*

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is attractive**—and is the **dominant trap**—because students often remember that interpleader allows for "minimal diversity" between claimants and may assume that any federal interpleader action is valid as long as the claimants are from different states. **The breaker:** While diversity among the three missionaries would satisfy the "minimal diversity" requirement of Statutory Interpleader (§ 1335), this answer choice incorrectly identifies "complete diversity between missionaries" as the standard. Furthermore, under Rule 22 (the path indicated by the source answer), the focus is on diversity between the stakeholder and the claimants, not among the claimants themselves.

**B is attractive** because the stakeholder's lack of interest in the property is a standard feature of a "strict" interpleader action, and students may assume that the form of the action dictates its jurisdiction. **The breaker:** A stakeholder's lack of interest (being "disinterested") relates to the form of the interpleader, but it does not satisfy the threshold requirement for federal subject matter jurisdiction. SMJ must still be established through a federal question or diversity of citizenship.

**D is attractive** because it reaches a "Yes" conclusion based on the fact that the stakeholder is disinterested. **The breaker:** Similar to Choice B, a stakeholder's lack of interest does not somehow create or confer federal jurisdiction where it is otherwise lacking. The Grace Global Mission must still satisfy the complete diversity requirement under § 1332 to use Rule 22 interpleader.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Rule statement:** Rule 22 interpleader requires that the action satisfy the standard requirements for federal subject matter jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 (federal question) or § 1332 (diversity). In a diversity case, there must be complete diversity between the stakeholder (plaintiff) and all claimants (defendants), and the amount in controversy must exceed $75,000. 

**Authority:** Fed. R. Civ. P. 22; 28 U.S.C. § 1332; State Farm Fire & Cas. Co. v. Tashire, 386 U.S. 523 (1967).

**Application to this item:** The Grace Global Mission (State A) and Peter (State A) are co-citizens. Thus, there is no complete diversity between the stakeholder-plaintiff and all defendant-claimants. Jurisdiction under Rule 22 is absent.

**Conflict Note:** The source question and explanation (BarMatrix 22845) explicitly state that jurisdiction is absent because diversity between the insurance company (stakeholder) and the nephews (claimants) is missing. This outcome correctly describes a failure of Rule 22 jurisdiction but appears to ignore the availability of Statutory Interpleader under § 1335 (which only requires minimal diversity between claimants). Because the pipeline mandates preserving the source outcome, the verdict is set to NEEDS_HUMAN.

## 9. Rigor/Difficulty Note

This variation maintains a "Core" level difficulty. It requires the student to distinguish between the two types of interpleader and recognize that Rule 22 (the procedural rule) follows standard § 1332 diversity rules, whereas § 1335 (the statute) has its own unique "minimal diversity" rules.
