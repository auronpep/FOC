## 1. Final question

Hannah was steering her fishing boat across the lake when it collided with a pontoon boat. Just after the collision, Hannah told a waterway officer at the dock that the pontoon boat had been going far over the posted speed limit and had cut across her path without ever checking whether another boat was beside it. The officer copied Hannah's account into the official incident report that every boater is required to file after a collision. Hannah later sued the owner of the pontoon boat for her injuries. At trial, Hannah wanted to testify about the account she had given the waterway officer. Is the testimony admissible?

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

Hannah's account to the waterway officer is an out-of-court statement, and she is offering it to prove the very thing it asserts — that the pontoon boat sped and cut across her path. That is hearsay, and hearsay is inadmissible unless an exemption or exception applies. None does. **A** is correct.

**Gold Key:** A prior consistent statement is non-hearsay only when the declarant's credibility has been attacked and the statement is offered to rebut a charge of recent fabrication/improper influence or to rehabilitate after another attack — no attack, no exemption. **Gold Key:** Logging a statement inside an official or public record does not cure the statement itself; each hearsay layer needs its own exception. **Silver Key:** When two "Yes" choices each invoke a *different* exception to admit the *same* statement, they usually split the wrong-answer vote — lock the plain "inadmissible hearsay" unless one exception's predicate is actually satisfied on the facts.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

- **B (Yes — prior consistent statement) ★ dominant trap.** The account genuinely is prior and consistent with what Hannah would now say, and she is the one testifying — so the exemption feels tailor-made. The breaker: that exemption activates only when her credibility has been attacked (recent fabrication, improper influence, or another impeachment ground); nobody has impeached her, so the predicate never fires. **Gold Key:** prior consistent statements are non-hearsay only to rebut/rehabilitate after a credibility attack — without the attack the statement is ordinary hearsay.
- **C (Yes — public record).** The stem dangles a required official incident report, so the public-records exception looks like the unlock. The breaker: the exception reaches the report's own contents, not a party's account embedded inside it — that embedded layer is separate hearsay and needs its own exception, which it lacks. Writing the statement down changes its location, not its character.
- **D (No — only the officer can testify).** It lands on the right outcome and sounds like a personal-knowledge or best-evidence limit, which makes "let the officer say it" feel procedurally correct. The breaker: there is no rule confining the account to the officer's mouth, and the officer repeating it would be hearsay too — the defect is hearsay, not the identity of the witness.

## 9. Black-letter verification & legal reasoning (lawyer-confirmation layer)

- **Tested rule (unchanged):** An out-of-court statement offered for the truth of the matter asserted is hearsay (**FRE 801(c)**) and inadmissible absent an exemption or exception (**FRE 802**). Hannah's scene account, offered to prove the pontoon boat's negligence, is hearsay.
- **Choice B:** **FRE 801(d)(1)(B)** treats a prior consistent statement as non-hearsay only where the declarant testifies and is cross-examinable and the statement is offered (i) to rebut a charge of recent fabrication/improper influence or motive, or (ii) to rehabilitate credibility attacked on another ground; the *Tome v. United States*, 513 U.S. 150 (1995) timing limit (statement pre-dating the alleged motive) survives the Dec. 1, 2014 amendment. No impeachment has occurred, so the predicate is absent.
- **Choice C:** **FRE 803(8)** (public records) covers the report's qualifying contents; a third party's/party's statement embedded in the report is hearsay within hearsay under **FRE 805** and needs an independent exception. Recording does not transform it.
- **Choice D:** No evidentiary rule restricts an out-of-court statement to a single permissible relayer; the bar is the hearsay character, identical whether Hannah or the officer testifies.
- **Credited outcome:** unchanged from the original — inadmissible hearsay. `bank_validation_verdict: PASS`. Authorities are rule numbers plus one leading case; authority-floor satisfied; no attorney-review flag required.
