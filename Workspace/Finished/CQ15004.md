## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Ownership, landlord-tenant termination, and the verified outline code is `82010405`. The legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row. When a tenant continuously uses leased premises for an illegal purpose and the landlord is not a participant in the illegality, the landlord may terminate the lease if acting while the illegal use continues or within a reasonable time after public authorities stop it; alternatively, the landlord may hold the tenant to the lease and seek appropriate legal and equitable relief, including damages and an injunction. The facts satisfy that rule because Ruth and Lydia used the leased loft three nights a week for six weeks to print and sell counterfeit festival wristbands, Hannah knew and remained during the activity, and police stopped the operation and notified Naomi.

The authority floor is satisfied by Restatement (Second) of Property: Landlord and Tenant section 12.5. Research located a practitioner discussion quoting that Restatement rule and explaining the two remedy paths: termination plus damages, or keeping the lease and pursuing legal and equitable relief. Current landlord-tenant statutes point the same way in their own statutory settings. Nebraska Revised Statute section 76-1431 allows landlord termination for qualifying tenant noncompliance and separately allows damages and injunctive relief for tenant noncompliance. Virginia Code section 55.1-1245 similarly recognizes termination for criminal or willful nonremediable acts posing health or safety threats and separately preserves damages and injunctive relief. The security-deposit trap is also unsupported: Nebraska Revised Statute section 76-1416 and Virginia Code section 55.1-1226 limit security-deposit application to rent, damages, noncompliance losses, or authorized charges rather than automatic forfeiture.
