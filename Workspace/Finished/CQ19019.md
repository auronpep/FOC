### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. The fig-drying yard is far from Lydia's farmhouse, used for farm storage, and outside the home-life area. Gold Key: The Fourth Amendment protects the home and curtilage, but open fields outside curtilage do not become protected just because they are private, fenced, locked, or posted. Silver Key: On a search-threshold call, decide whether the place is protected before asking how officers discovered the contraband.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A. This choice is attractive because the yard is behind a farmhouse, so it borrows the feel of home territory. The breaker is that curtilage is not every backyard directionally behind a house; proximity, enclosure with the home, domestic use, and shielding steps matter.

B. This is the dominant trap because locked gates, fences, and "Private" signs sound like privacy signals. Gold Key: Oliver keeps posted and fenced open fields outside Fourth Amendment protection when they are outside the home's curtilage.

D. This choice is attractive because it focuses on the officers' observation of the illegal plants. Silver Key: The threshold issue is whether entering that place was a search at all, not whether officers touched the plants after seeing them.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The governing rule is unchanged. The Fourth Amendment protects persons, houses, papers, and effects, and it protects the home's curtilage as part of the home. It does not protect open fields outside curtilage. Hester v. United States, 265 U.S. 57 (1924), established that Fourth Amendment protection does not extend to open fields. Oliver v. United States, 466 U.S. 170 (1984), reaffirmed that rule and held that fences and no-trespassing signs do not create a reasonable Fourth Amendment expectation of privacy in open fields. United States v. Dunn, 480 U.S. 294 (1987), states the main curtilage factors: proximity to the home, whether the area is within an enclosure surrounding the home, the nature of its use, and steps taken to shield it from observation. Here, the area is remote from the farmhouse and used for farm storage, so entry into it is not a Fourth Amendment search.
