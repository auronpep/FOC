## 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

Federal Rule of Evidence 801(c) defines hearsay as an out-of-court statement offered for the truth of what it asserts, and Rule 802 excludes hearsay unless a federal statute, the Evidence Rules, or another Supreme Court-prescribed rule provides otherwise. ([U.S. Code][1])

Federal Rule of Evidence 803(1) excludes from the hearsay bar a present sense impression: a statement describing or explaining an event or condition made while or immediately after the declarant perceived it. Federal Rule of Evidence 803(5) excludes a recorded recollection when the record concerns a matter the witness once knew, the witness cannot recall well enough to testify fully and accurately, the record was made or adopted while fresh, and it accurately reflects the witness’s knowledge. ([U.S. Code][1])

The transformed item preserves the tested legal substance. Stephen’s audio statement is offered for the truth of the rental tag number, so it is hearsay; it is nevertheless covered by both FRE 803(1) and FRE 803(5).
