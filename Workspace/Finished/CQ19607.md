## 6. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.** **Gold Key:** School-funding disparities tied to local property-tax bases do not trigger strict scrutiny under federal Equal Protection merely because education is important or because the lower-funded district is poorer. **Gold Key:** Racially unequal effects from a facially neutral formula do not by themselves trigger heightened scrutiny without a discriminatory-purpose or classification trigger. **Silver Key:** In a scrutiny-tier answer array, do not start with the strongest-sounding tier; first ask what valid trigger the choice names. If no valid heightened-review trigger survives, the default tier is rational basis.

The plan likely survives because local control of public schools and the connection between local tax revenue and local spending are legitimate interests under rational basis review.

## 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells the intuitive move that a sharp funding gap harming low-income students must make poverty a suspect class. **Gold Key:** Under the school-funding rule, poverty or local-district wealth does not create a suspect-class trigger, so strict scrutiny is not the tier.

**B.** This choice is attractive because education feels central, and “equal access to public education” sounds like a fundamental-right phrase. **Gold Key:** Federal Equal Protection does not treat education as a fundamental right for school-funding disparities, so strict scrutiny is not triggered.

**C.** This choice is attractive because racial overlap makes the inequality feel like a race-based constitutional problem. The breaker is the trigger: a facially neutral funding formula with disparate racial effects does not receive heightened scrutiny from impact alone.

## 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The transform preserves the original legal issue: a federal Equal Protection challenge to a public-school finance system that uses state aid plus local property-tax revenue, producing interdistrict spending disparities. The credited outcome remains rational basis review and likely survival.

In **San Antonio Independent School District v. Rodriguez**, the Court treated local-property-tax school finance disparities as unsuitable for strict scrutiny because the challenged system did not disadvantage a suspect class and education was not a fundamental right under the federal Constitution; the Court held the system rationally related to legitimate state purposes, including local control. ([Justia Law][1]) For the racial-impact distractor, **Washington v. Davis** confirms that disproportionate racial impact alone does not establish an Equal Protection violation absent discriminatory purpose; facially neutral laws do not become invalid merely because they affect one race more than another. ([Justia Law][2])
