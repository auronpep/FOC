## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam-Register)

**Gold Key:** "So long as" is durational language that creates a fee simple determinable. When a grantor conveys a fee simple determinable, the grantor retains a possibility of reverter — a future interest that exists immediately upon conveyance and becomes possessory automatically if the stated use ceases. The interest does not depend on breach or enforcement; it is inherent in the estate created.

**Silver Key:** When you see "so long as" or "while" in a conveyance, immediately tag it as a fee simple determinable. The grantor's retained interest is a possibility of reverter — not a reversion, not a right of entry. Cut any choice that labels it as one of those other interests.

Timothy conveyed "so long as the property remains a residence." That is classic durational language → fee simple determinable in Naomi. Timothy retains a possibility of reverter. The fact that Naomi still lives there means the interest is not yet possessory, but it exists now and will become possessory automatically if the property ceases to be a residence.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (was D): No interest, because Naomi has not breached the condition.**
This choice sells the tempting idea that because Naomi is still complying, Timothy has nothing. The breaker: a possibility of reverter exists immediately upon conveyance — it is not contingent on breach. Current compliance means the interest is not yet possessory, but it is not eliminated. This is the "no breach yet" misconception. A student who picks this is confusing the timing of possession with the existence of the interest.

**C (was B): A reversion, because all future interests retained by a grantor are reversions.**
This is the overgeneralization trap. A reversion arises when the grantor conveys a finite estate (like a life estate) and retains the leftover. But here, Timothy conveyed a fee simple — just a defeasible one. The future interest retained after a fee simple determinable is a possibility of reverter, not a reversion. Not all grantor future interests are reversions; the label depends on the estate conveyed. **Gold Key:** The type of future interest retained by the grantor depends on the estate conveyed — fee simple determinable → possibility of reverter; life estate → reversion; fee simple subject to condition subsequent → right of entry.

**D (was A): A right of entry, because Timothy must choose whether to terminate Naomi's estate.**
This choice confuses two different defeasible fees. A right of entry (power of termination) accompanies a fee simple subject to *condition subsequent* — where the grantor must actively choose to enforce the condition. But "so long as" creates a fee simple *determinable*, where the estate ends automatically. Timothy has no choice to make; the interest reverts on its own if the condition ceases. **Silver Key:** "So long as" = automatic reversion (possibility of reverter). "On condition that" or "provided that" = grantor must act (right of entry). The language tells you who has the power.
