Choice C is correct because the deal is an oral contract for the sale of land. A land-sale contract must be evidenced by a signed writing unless a recognized exception removes it from the Statute of Frauds. Barnabas's partial payment does not, by itself, take the contract out of the statute; the usual part-performance signal needs more than payment alone, such as possession plus improvements.

## 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

Authority floor: Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 125 provides that a promise to transfer or buy an interest in land is within the Statute of Frauds. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 129 recognizes a part-performance exception for land contracts when reliance, typically including possession and improvements, makes injustice likely unless the contract is enforced. The American Law Institute's Restatement (Second) of Contracts publication identifies Chapter 5 as the Statute of Frauds chapter, confirming the authority source.

Applied here, Martha seeks specific performance of a land-sale contract. The contract was oral. Barnabas paid $4,200, but the facts do not show buyer possession or buyer improvements. Martha's tender of a deed and her continued control of the lot do not create the buyer-side part-performance facts needed to avoid the statute. Therefore, if Martha loses, the most likely reason is the absence of a signed writing.
