### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation
Burglary is a specific-intent crime. To be guilty, the defendant must not only break and enter a building but must also possess the specific intent to commit a felony or theft inside at the moment of entry. Voluntary intoxication is a recognized defense to specific-intent crimes because it can be used to show that the defendant’s mental state was so impaired that they were unable to form the required specific intent. If Timothy was so intoxicated that he genuinely believed he was entering his own storage room simply to sleep, he lacked the burglarious intent required for a conviction, even if his entry was trespassory.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
- **A:** This is the dominant trap because it describes the rule for general-intent and malice crimes (like battery or arson). However, the rule is different for specific-intent crimes. Voluntary intoxication *is* relevant to whether a defendant formed a specific purpose or objective, such as the intent to steal required for burglary.
- **B:** This choice identifies the standard for "involuntary" intoxication. While involuntary intoxication provides a broader defense (often treated similarly to insanity), voluntary intoxication is still a valid defense for the limited purpose of negating specific intent. 
- **D:** This is an overclaim. While intoxication might negate the specific intent for burglary, it does not provide a blanket excuse for all crimes. Timothy could still be liable for general-intent offenses that his intoxication does not excuse, such as criminal trespass.

### 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification
**Authority:** *People v. Hood*, 1 Cal. 3d 444 (1969); MPC § 2.08(1).
Voluntary intoxication is generally not a defense to criminal liability, but it may be admitted to negate an element of the offense. In common-law jurisdictions and under the Model Penal Code, this means it can negate the "specific intent" required for certain crimes. Burglary is the prototypical specific-intent crime. The transform preserves the rule (voluntary intoxication negates specific intent), the issue (burglary mental state), and the credited outcome.
