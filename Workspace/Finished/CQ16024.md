### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** Murder requires proof that the defendant's act caused the victim's death. Lydia's intentional act toward a live-born child gives the prosecution a strong malice and act basis, and her later rescue effort does not erase that act. But the child lived for seven years before dying. That long delay gives Lydia her best available defense: the prosecution may have difficulty proving that Lydia's act was the proximate legal cause of the death, especially in jurisdictions that retain or codify a time limit on homicide causation.

**Gold Key:** In a homicide result crime, intent and a dangerous act are not enough. The prosecution still must prove legal causation; an unusually remote death can be the only plausible defense even when the original attack was intentional.

**Silver Key:** When the call asks for the "most effective argument," do not demand a guaranteed acquittal. Pick the only answer that attacks an element the prosecution still must prove.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. Rescue breaths.** This choice is attractive because Lydia changed course and tried to keep the child alive. The breaker is timing. A later rescue attempt does not erase a completed intentional life-endangering act if that earlier act legally caused the death. It may matter to remorse, mitigation, or factual causation disputes, but it is not itself a murder defense.

**C. State of mind.** This is the dominant trap because it keeps the student's eyes on the medical procedure and Lydia's reluctance. The breaker is that the charge is murder of the live-born child, and Lydia then placed the child's face under water trying to end his life. **Gold Key:** once the victim is born alive, a later intentional act to kill that victim supplies the homicide mens rea; the usual result of the earlier pregnancy-ending procedure does not control the mental state for the later attack.

**D. Constitutional right.** This choice borrows the abortion-right frame from the pregnancy facts. The breaker is twofold. First, after *Dobbs*, there is no federal constitutional right to abortion. Second, even under older viability framing, the murder charge is based on an intentional act against a child who was born alive, not on regulating Hannah's pregnancy decision.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are preserved. The item tests murder liability where the defendant intentionally attacks a live-born child, later tries to save the child, and the child dies years later. The best defense is not abortion rights, rescue, or lack of mens rea. It is causation.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Model Penal Code section 210.1 defines criminal homicide as purposely, knowingly, recklessly, or negligently causing the death of another human being, and section 210.2 treats purposeful or knowing criminal homicide as murder. Model Penal Code section 2.03 supplies the causal-relationship requirement for result offenses. The common-law born-alive approach is reflected in Model Penal Code section 210.0(1), which defines "human being" as a person who has been born and is alive. *Rogers v.
