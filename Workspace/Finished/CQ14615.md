### 1. Final question

Lydia, a county prosecutor, obtained a grand-jury indictment charging Daniel with robbing the cash courier for a private Christian bookstore called The Upper Room. Daniel could not post bail and was held in the county jail. At booking, officers gave Daniel Miranda warnings; Daniel said nothing.

Wanting Daniel to talk about the charged robbery, Lydia arranged for Barnabas, a jailhouse informant, to be assigned to Daniel’s two-person jail book-cart duty, where old hymnals and Bible-study workbooks were sorted for the unit. Lydia told Barnabas to find out what he could about the bookstore-courier robbery but not to ask Daniel any direct questions about it. During several shifts, Barnabas loudly recounted exaggerated stories about robberies he claimed to have committed. Trying to top Barnabas, Daniel finally said that he had committed the bookstore-courier robbery charged in the indictment.

At Daniel’s trial, his lawyer moved to exclude Barnabas’s testimony about Daniel’s statement. The motion should be:

### 2. Distilled Core Question

After indictment on a charged robbery, the prosecutor plants an informant to draw out a statement about that charge without direct questions; should the statement be excluded?

C. Granted, because Daniel’s right to counsel was violated.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Daniel was formally charged before the jailhouse contact. Lydia then used Barnabas as a government agent to draw out a statement about that charged offense while counsel was absent. **Gold Key:** after formal charges, the government may not deliberately elicit charged-offense statements from the accused through an agent without counsel, even if the agent avoids direct questions. **Silver Key:** read the answer set as a lane fight; indictment plus prosecutor-created contact points to the right-to-counsel lane, not the Miranda-warning or no-direct-question lane.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells the remembered Miranda fact. Miranda warnings matter in the Fifth Amendment custodial-interrogation lane, but this stem’s load-bearing facts are formal charge, government agent, and the charged offense. Those facts route the item to the right-to-counsel Gold Key.

**B.** This is the dominant trap. It rewards the visible fact that Barnabas did not ask direct questions and turns that into a no-interrogation shortcut. **Gold Key:** direct questions are not required when the prosecutor deliberately creates a setup likely to draw out a charged-offense statement after formal charges.

**D.** This choice reaches the same grant result as the credited answer, which makes it attractive. The breaker is the because-clause. The reason must match the lane. The operative lane is counsel after formal charge, not the general privilege against self-incrimination.
