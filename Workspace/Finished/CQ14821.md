# CQ14821

## 1. Final Question

Daniel was charged with possessing stolen tablets with intent to sell them. On direct examination, Daniel testified that he mentored teens in a Christian robotics league, that he hated theft, that he would "never possess or sell stolen electronics," and that he had never acquired stolen electronics and would not touch them. The government offered as a rebuttal witness a detective who would testify that, two years earlier, she saw Daniel trade a box of phone chargers to a flea-market fence for a stolen tablet. Daniel objected.

Is the detective's testimony about the prior stolen-tablet trade admissible to impeach Daniel?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

When a criminal defendant testifies that he would never handle the same kind of illegal property charged in the case, may the government use a rebuttal witness to contradict that testimony with a prior specific act?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

A. No, because the testimony contradicts Daniel only on a collateral matter.

B. Yes, because it is proper contradiction.

C. Yes, because taking stolen property in trade shows a disregard for the law and makes it less likely that Daniel would respect the oath of truthfulness.

D. No, because taking stolen property in trade is not sufficiently probative of a witness's character for truthfulness.

## 5. Correct Answer

B (original key C).

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. Daniel did more than deny guilt. He volunteered a broad, specific claim that he would never possess or sell stolen electronics and had never acquired them. The detective's testimony directly contradicts that claim on a point tied to the charged possession-and-sale case. Gold Key: impeachment by contradiction is different from attacking general truthfulness character; extrinsic contradiction evidence can be used when it contradicts material testimony. Silver Key: sort the answers by purpose before sorting by admissibility. The testimony is being used to show that Daniel's specific courtroom story is false, not to show that he is generally dishonest.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A sells the collateral-matter shortcut. Extrinsic contradiction evidence cannot be used to chase side issues, but Daniel's statement was not a side issue. The breaker is materiality: the prior stolen-tablet trade directly undercuts his sweeping denial about handling the same kind of property involved in the charge.

C sells the correct result with the wrong reason. Lawbreaking may sound like it says something about oath-taking, but that is a character-for-truthfulness theory. The breaker is Rule 608(b): extrinsic proof of a prior act is not admissible merely to prove a witness's truthfulness character. The admissible route is contradiction, not "he broke the law, so he lies."

D is the dominant trap because it recognizes that taking stolen property is weak proof of general truthfulness. That point would matter if the government were offering the trade only to attack Daniel's veracity character. The breaker is purpose. The detective is offered to contradict Daniel's specific claim that he never acquired or handled stolen electronics. Gold Key: do not use the Rule 608(b) truthfulness frame when the evidence directly contradicts material testimony.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: extrinsic evidence of a prior act may impeach a witness by contradiction when it directly contradicts material testimony, but the same extrinsic act cannot be admitted merely to prove the witness's character for truthfulness. Daniel's own testimony made the denial material. The prior tablet trade is not offered to prove propensity or general dishonesty. It is offered to show that Daniel's specific sworn claim was false.

Federal Rule of Evidence 404(b)(1) bars other-act evidence when used to prove character conformity, while Rule 404(b)(2) allows other-act evidence for another noncharacter purpose. Federal Rule of Evidence 608(b) bars extrinsic evidence of specific acts when the act is offered to attack or support character for truthfulness, but the 2003 Advisory Committee note explains that extrinsic evidence offered for other impeachment grounds, including contradiction, is left to Rules 402 and 403. Rule 403 remains the usual balancing screen. Here, the MBE stem gives no special prejudice fact that would override the contradiction use.
