## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**D is correct.**

Voluntary manslaughter is an intentional killing committed in the heat of passion, upon legally adequate provocation, without a cooling-off period. The critical elements here: (1) Peter *intended* to kill Stephen — he said so out loud, he armed himself, and he fired deliberately; (2) Peter acted in the *heat of passion* — discovering the murdered bodies of his wife and daughters and hearing his wounded son name the killer is more than adequate provocation to trigger the heat-of-passion standard; (3) Stephen's doorstep confession — "Yes, I killed them. Now what?" — renewed and extended that passion rather than allowing it to cool.

**Gold Key (elements):** Voluntary manslaughter requires an intentional killing + heat of passion + adequate provocation + no cooling-off period. Finding murdered family members is classic adequate provocation. The passion reduces murder to manslaughter; it does not excuse the killing or eliminate criminal liability.

**Silver Key:** Lock the elements of voluntary manslaughter before reading any choice. Any choice that substitutes a murder element (deliberation, premeditation, malice aforethought) as the *reason* for guilt or innocence under a voluntary manslaughter charge is automatically wrong — those are first-degree or second-degree murder elements, not voluntary manslaughter elements. D names the right elements: intent + belief-based passion.

## 7. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A — dominant trap.** The pull: Stephen confessed on his own doorstep to killing Peter's wife and daughters. The neighbor's guilt is established beyond any doubt in the stem, and the moral logic — "he killed my family, so I had the right to kill him" — is the strongest emotional pull in this question. A student operating on the instinct that the law recognizes a right of familial retribution will land on A without hesitation. The breaker: criminal law does not recognize a law of vendetta. The state prosecutes crimes; individuals do not have a legal right to execute even admitted killers. Stephen's guilt provides moral context and establishes the heat of passion — it does not give Peter a legal license to kill. **Gold Key:** adequate provocation reduces murder to manslaughter; it does not excuse the homicide or create an affirmative defense.

**B.** The pull: Peter loaded his rifle, walked next door, and shot Stephen after a brief confrontation — this looks deliberate and premeditated, and "deliberate and premeditated" = murder, not manslaughter. A student who reasons "premeditated = murder → NOT manslaughter" picks B. The breaker: voluntary manslaughter is a lesser included offense of first-degree murder. A defendant can be convicted of voluntary manslaughter even if the facts also support a first-degree murder charge. Guilt of a greater offense does not eliminate guilt of a lesser included offense. Additionally, deliberation and premeditation require a cool, rational mind — under these facts, Peter probably lacked that capacity.

**C.** The pull: Peter clearly intended to kill Stephen and did so in a focused, armed confrontation — that sounds like it was deliberate. C picks up the "deliberate and premeditated" phrase from the first-degree murder statute and uses it to explain the guilty verdict. The breaker: deliberation and premeditation are elements of first-degree murder under this statute, not elements of voluntary manslaughter. C proves the right outcome (guilty) by applying the wrong legal test. The reason Peter is guilty of voluntary manslaughter is that he killed intentionally in the heat of passion — not because the killing was deliberate and premeditated.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Tested rule confirmed.** Voluntary manslaughter at common law requires: (1) an intentional killing; (2) committed in the heat of passion; (3) upon legally adequate provocation; (4) before the passion has had a reasonable time to cool. Discovering that a person has murdered your family is the paradigm case of legally adequate provocation — it is recognized in virtually every common-law jurisdiction. The neighbor's doorstep confession renews and extends the passion, eliminating any argument of cooling off. These elements are fully satisfied here.

**Lesser included offense rule confirmed.** Voluntary manslaughter is a lesser included offense of first-degree murder. See, e.g., MPC § 210.3 (manslaughter as a lesser grade of criminal homicide); LaFave, Substantive Criminal Law § 15.2. A defendant who is technically guilty of first-degree murder can still be convicted of voluntary manslaughter; the greater charge does not destroy the lesser included.

**No vendetta defense.** Criminal law is a matter between the state and the defendant; the victim's guilt does not justify or excuse the killing. See LaFave § 10.1. Adequate provocation reduces the degree of the offense but is not an affirmative defense.

**Credited outcome unchanged:** original key A maps to new key D; legal substance — guilty of voluntary manslaughter based on intentional killing in the heat of passion upon adequate provocation — is identical.
