## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

A is correct. The problem is not that Naomi never inspected the canvas. The problem is that Esther's hypothetical leaves out the basement flood, an admitted prior event that could explain the same rippling Barnabas disputes. Gold Key: an expert may answer a hypothetical without firsthand examination, but the hypothetical must include the significant case facts needed for a reliable and helpful opinion. ## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer

The tested issue remains Evidence, Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence, Expert Testimony, Basis of expert testimony, and the verified outline code is `32020803`. The credited outcome is unchanged: the best objection is that the hypothetical omitted a clearly significant fact.

FRE 702 requires expert testimony to help the trier of fact and to be based on sufficient facts or data, reliable principles and methods, and reliable application of those methods. The December 1, 2023 amendment to Rule 702 confirms that the proponent must show these requirements by a preponderance standard; that current framing supports the same result here. FRE 703 permits an expert to base an opinion on facts or data the expert has personally observed or has been made aware of. FRE 705 allows an expert to state an opinion without first disclosing all underlying facts or data unless the court orders otherwise, subject to cross-examination. Together, those rules preserve expert hypotheticals while requiring a reliable factual basis.

Here, Esther admitted the earlier basement flood. The flood is a clearly significant causal fact because it could explain the same rippling for which Esther seeks damages. A hypothetical that asks Naomi to opine on cause while omitting that admitted alternative cause does not give the expert a sufficient, reliable factual basis for a helpful opinion. By contrast, Naomi's lack of firsthand inspection is not itself a bar to a hypothetical opinion; hypothetical questions remain permitted; and the Evidence Rules do not make the best objection turn on failure to give opposing counsel the exact hypothetical before trial.
