## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

**(B) is correct.** Robbery is larceny from the person by force. The force element is satisfied by any physical force used to overcome the attachment of property to the victim's body — even a forceful yank that tears clothing. *See* Common law robbery elements; FBI UCR definition ("taking … from the care, custody, or control of a person or persons by force or threat of force or violence"); *see also* Model Penal Code § 222.1 (robbery requires "in the course of committing a theft" the actor "inflicts or threatens serious bodily injury" or "commits or threatens to commit" a felony of the second degree — force used to take property from the person satisfies this). Here, Barnabas yanked the satchel hard enough to tear Naomi's sleeve. That is physical force used to take property from the person. It is robbery.

**Silver Key:** When you see force applied to remove something from the victim's body, the force element of robbery is met — stop looking for a threat. Threat is an alternative path, not a requirement.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**(A)** This choice sells the overgeneralization that snatching from the body is "always" larceny. The word "always" is the tell. The satchel was on Naomi's shoulder — on her person — and Barnabas used real force to remove it (the torn sleeve proves it). That force is what elevates a taking-from-the-person to robbery. The absolute "always" collapses the moment force enters the picture.

**(C)** This choice invents an element that doesn't exist: successful escape. The crime of robbery is complete the moment force is used to take property from the person. Whether the thief gets away, gets caught two steps later, or drops the goods is irrelevant to the robbery charge. Escape goes to sentencing or apprehension, not to the elements.

**(D)** This choice reverses the force/threat relationship. Robbery can be committed by force *or* by threat — they are alternative means, not joint requirements. The fact that Barnabas didn't threaten Naomi before grabbing the satchel doesn't help him; the yank itself is the force. Students who pick this one are reading "force" as "threat" and missing that the force was right there in the yank.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification

**Governing rule:** At common law, robbery is larceny plus (1) taking from the person (or presence) and (2) by force or intimidation. *See* 3 W. Blackstone *Commentaries* 242 (force or fear as the distinguishing element); Model Penal Code § 222.1. The force element is satisfied by any physical force used to take property from the victim's person, including force used to overcome the physical attachment of the property to the victim's body. *See, e.g.*, *People v. Williams*, 2014 IL App (1st) 130428 (forceful snatching from victim's hand constitutes robbery); *State v. Smith*, 2017-Ohio-7424 (force used to remove property from victim's person satisfies robbery's force element). No threat is required when actual force is used. Escape is not an element of robbery.

**Verification:** The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the original. The original key (C) and the transformed key (B) both stand for the same legal proposition: force used to take property from the person = robbery. The transform preserves the invariant layer.
