## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. Lydia obtained John's money by threatening future reputational and economic harm: a false safety accusation that would damage his ropes-course business. Gold Key: extortion covers coerced payment induced by a wrongful threat, including a threat that pressures the victim's business reputation. Silver Key: lock the pressure mechanism before choosing the offense label; this was future coercion, not immediate taking and not deception used to get possession.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A. Robbery is attractive because Lydia used a threat to make John part with money. The breaker is the kind of pressure: robbery turns on taking property from the person or presence by immediate force or intimidation at the taking, while this text threatened future reputational harm if John did not pay.

B. Larceny by trick is attractive because Lydia used words and the accusation was false. The breaker is the mechanism: she did not trick John into handing over possession by deception; she coerced payment through a wrongful threat, so the Silver Key points away from a deception label.

D. No theft offense is attractive because John pressed send himself and the money moved by wire. The breaker is consent quality: a coerced payment caused by a wrongful threat can still be the property transfer that completes extortion.

## 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

The credited outcome remains extortion. The tested rule is that extortion obtains property through coercive consent induced by a wrongful threat, including future reputational or economic harm. California Penal Code section 518 defines extortion as obtaining property or other consideration from another, with consent, induced by wrongful force or fear; section 519 includes threats to impute disgrace or expose damaging information; and section 520 punishes obtaining property by those threats under circumstances not amounting to robbery. Pennsylvania's theft-by-extortion statute, 18 Pa. Cons. Stat. section 3923, likewise treats obtaining property by threats to expose damaging information or inflict other harm as theft by extortion. The Hobbs Act definition in 18 U.S.C. section 1951(b)(2) also confirms the core structure: obtaining property from another, with consent, induced by wrongful use of fear.

Those authorities support the supplied key. Lydia demanded money and threatened a future false reputational injury to John's business if he did not pay. John wired the money because of that threat. Robbery is not the best label because the facts do not describe a taking from John's person or immediate presence by force at the taking. Larceny by trick is not the best label because the false statement was not used to obtain possession by deception; it was the content of a coercive threat. The transformed facts preserve the same rule, issue, and credited outcome as the original.
