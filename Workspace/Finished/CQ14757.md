### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** The estate is offering Stephen’s old testimony for its truth: that Lydia gave him the “whatever it takes” instruction. **Silver Key:** scan the transcript outside-in. The transcript layer must be admissible before the quoted instruction layer matters. **Gold Key:** hearsay within hearsay requires every layer to have its own exclusion or exception. Here, Stephen’s former testimony does not qualify as an opposing-party agent statement because Stephen gave it after he no longer worked for Bethany Bible Quest. It also does not qualify as former testimony against Bethany Bible Quest because the company was not a party to Stephen’s criminal case and had no prior chance to develop Stephen’s testimony.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells a real-looking layer move: Lydia’s instruction may look like the important statement. The breaker is the outer transcript layer. **Silver Key:** scan outside-in. Even if Lydia’s instruction had its own path in, Stephen’s criminal-trial testimony still needs a separate path. **Gold Key:** each hearsay layer must independently clear the hearsay rule.

**C.** This choice sells the familiar party-opponent agent label. The breaker is timing. **Gold Key:** an employee or agent statement works under the party-opponent rule only when made during the agency or employment relationship. Stephen’s testimony was given after he left Bethany Bible Quest.

**D.** This is the dominant trap. It sells the strongest surface cue: a sworn transcript from a prior trial sounds like former testimony. The breaker is the missing party-opportunity fact. **Gold Key:** former testimony can be used only against a party, or civil predecessor in interest, who had an opportunity and similar motive to develop the testimony. Bethany Bible Quest was not a party to Stephen’s criminal case.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The supplied source row identifies QID 14757 as an Evidence hearsay item with official key D, and it explains that the transcript is inadmissible because the former employee’s prior testimony is hearsay, not an agent admission, and not former testimony against the store. 

The current federal rule matches that outcome. Rule 801(c) defines hearsay as an out-of-court statement offered for the truth of what it asserts. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) Rule 801(d)(2)(D) excludes an agent or employee statement from hearsay only when the statement concerns a matter within the relationship’s scope and is made while the relationship exists. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) Rule 804(b)(1) admits former testimony only when offered against a party, or in a civil case a predecessor in interest, who had an opportunity and similar motive to develop it by examination. ([Legal Information Institute][2]) Rule 805 requires each layer of hearsay within hearsay to conform to an exception or exclusion. ([Legal Information Institute][3])

For hearsay within hearsay, every layer needs its own exclusion or exception. A clean inner statement does not save an inadmissible transcript layer.

**GK-EVIDENCE-BETHANY-TRANSCRIPT-02**
An agent or employee statement qualifies under the party-opponent rule only if it was made during the agency or employment relationship.

**GK-EVIDENCE-BETHANY-TRANSCRIPT-03**
Former testimony works only against a party, or in a civil case a predecessor in interest, who had an opportunity and similar motive to develop that testimony.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-EVIDENCE-BETHANY-TRANSCRIPT-01**
When a transcript quotes a witness who quotes someone else, scan outside-in: current trial offer, old transcript, then quoted instruction.
