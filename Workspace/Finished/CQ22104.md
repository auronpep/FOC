## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

After visiting nearly every rural property near the county seat, Stephen decided that Esther's private acreage called Mercy Grove would be perfect for the retreat cabins and walking gardens he wanted to build. After negotiations, Stephen and Esther signed a contract for the sale of the land. The contract described the property this way:

"All realty known as Mercy Grove, identified as Section 22, Garden Map 8, Parcel 417 in the Parcel Index maintained by the office of the Recorder of Maranatha County; said realty consisting of 180 acres more or less, one stone residence, one 45-foot by 120-foot equipment barn, and six small storage sheds measuring approximately 70 square feet each."

Mercy Grove was the only parcel in Maranatha County known by that name and the only parcel identified by those Section, Map, and Parcel numbers. Local real estate lawyers usually described rural acreage by a surveyed metes-and-bounds description instead. The contract set the purchase price at $390,000, with title to close 10 weeks after signing.

Three weeks after signing, Stephen's financing collapsed, and he decided not to buy Mercy Grove. At the time and place of closing, Esther tendered a deed containing the same property description used in the contract. Stephen refused to accept it.

In Esther's later action against Stephen for damages for breach of contract, should the court find for Esther?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Is a land-sale contract and deed description sufficient when it uniquely identifies the property, even though it does not use the area's customary metes-and-bounds form?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. No, because the contract and deed did not contain a metes-and-bounds description.

B. Yes, because Stephen waived any objection to the validity of the description by signing a contract that used it.

C. No, because local practice used a different form of land description than the one in the contract and deed.

D. Yes, because Mercy Grove was the only realty in the county known by that name and the only parcel identified by the Section, Map, and Parcel numbers used in the description.

### 5. Correct answer

D (original key A).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

D is correct. A land-sale contract and a deed need a description that identifies the property with reasonable certainty. Mercy Grove was the only county parcel with that name and the only parcel matching the listed Section, Map, and Parcel numbers. Gold Key: the test is identification, not use of a particular survey style. Silver Key: when the stem gives unique identifiers and then mentions local custom, ask whether the description points to one parcel, not whether it uses the usual local format.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because metes-and-bounds descriptions sound like the safest and most formal way to describe land. The breaker is that they are not the only valid method; a description that clearly identifies the parcel is enough.

B is attractive because Stephen signed the same description he later challenged. The breaker is that waiver is not what makes an otherwise defective land description enforceable; the description works because it identifies the property.

C is attractive because the stem says local lawyers usually used a different form. The breaker is that local custom is not the legal test. A noncustomary description still works if it identifies the land with reasonable certainty.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is unchanged: Esther should recover because Stephen breached an enforceable land-sale contract after Esther tendered a deed with an adequate description. The Statute of Frauds requires a land-sale memorandum to identify the subject matter with reasonable certainty. A deed likewise needs a description that supplies enough data to identify the land conveyed. The description here named the property and gave county index identifiers, and those identifiers matched only one parcel. That makes the contract enforceable and the tendered deed adequate even though the description was not in metes and bounds and was not the locally customary form.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 131 states that a Statute of Frauds memorandum is enforceable when a signed writing reasonably identifies the subject matter, indicates a contract, and states essential terms with reasonable certainty. Its land-sale illustration treats a description such as "the land on the corner of X and Y Streets" as sufficient where the seller owns only one matching parcel. For deed-description confirmation, AIC Management v. Crews, 246 S.W.3d 640, 645 (Tex. 2008), states the common formulation that a property description is sufficient when the writing, or another writing it references, furnishes the means or data to identify the particular land with reasonable certainty.
