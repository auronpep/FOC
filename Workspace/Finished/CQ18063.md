## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Mary once worked evenings at Daniel's privately owned Scripture-calligraphy studio. A month before Peter and Ruth planned to break into the studio after closing, Mary gave them a marked alarm-zone map and back-door keypad layout that she had copied during her work. Ten days before the planned entry, Mary called Peter and Ruth and said she was leaving the plan and wanted no part in it. She did not delete the shared map, change the keypad information, retrieve the copy, or contact the police. Peter and Ruth used Mary's map and keypad layout to enter the studio at night and steal several framed works.

Mary is charged as an accomplice to burglary. Is Mary's withdrawal effective to defeat accomplice liability?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Does an accomplice escape burglary liability by announcing withdrawal when the previously supplied entry aid remains available and is used?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. No, because Mary did not neutralize her prior assistance before the burglary was committed.

B. Yes, because Mary communicated her withdrawal to Peter and Ruth before the burglary occurred.

C. No, but only because Mary failed to notify the police.

D. Yes, because Mary's role was complete before the burglary occurred and she had no further duty to act.

### 5. Correct answer

A (original key B).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. Gold Key: withdrawal from accomplice liability is not just a speech act when earlier help is still doing work. Mary supplied the alarm-zone map and keypad layout. Her later phone call showed repudiation, but it did not erase, retrieve, disable, or otherwise neutralize the aid. Because Peter and Ruth used the information to commit the burglary, Mary's prior assistance remained effective.

Silver Key: the answer array splits between "yes, the withdrawal worked" and "no, it did not," then splits again between the reason. Once the map still works and gets used, the winning answer is the "no" answer tied to neutralizing prior aid.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

B is attractive because it gives weight to Mary's clean timing: she called before the burglary and said she was out. Gold Key: timing and communication do not finish the withdrawal when the earlier aid remains useful. The shared map and keypad layout still helped Peter and Ruth enter the studio.

C is attractive because police notice can be a valid way to prevent the crime. The breaker is the word "only." Mary failed because she did not neutralize the map, did not warn police, and did not otherwise prevent the burglary; police notice was not the exclusive route.

D is attractive because Mary's physical role ended before the night of entry. The breaker is that completed aid can keep operating after the accomplice walks away. A completed map that still guides the burglary is exactly the kind of assistance withdrawal must neutralize.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The source item tests effective withdrawal from accomplice liability after the accomplice has already furnished useful aid. Under Model Penal Code section 2.06(6)(c), a person is not an accomplice if the person terminates complicity before the offense and either wholly deprives it of effectiveness, gives timely warning to law enforcement, or otherwise makes a proper and substantial effort to prevent the offense. The same rule is reflected in the source row's black-letter reference: repudiation alone is insufficient when prior assistance remains effective.

Mary aided planning by supplying the alarm-zone map and keypad layout. She later repudiated the plan, but she did not deprive that assistance of effectiveness and did not prevent the offense. The principals used the aid in the burglary. The credited outcome remains no effective withdrawal.

Authority checked: Model Penal Code section 2.06(6)(c), reflected in the UNODC-hosted text of a code provision using the same language.
