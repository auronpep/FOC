## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The transform preserves the tested rule: a defendant who commits limited trespass/property damage to avoid an imminent natural danger with no reasonable legal alternative is best classified under necessity, not duress or self-defense.

The necessity defense is commonly described as a lesser-evil justification: the actor's otherwise unlawful conduct is justified when necessary to prevent a more serious harm, with no reasonable alternative and without substantially contributing to the emergency. LII's necessity-defense summary lists the core requirements, including preventing injury, no reasonable alternative, proportionality/no greater danger, reasonable belief, and no substantial contribution to the emergency. citeturn413096search0 Model Penal Code § 3.02 frames the same idea as conduct believed necessary to avoid a harm or evil, provided the avoided harm is greater than the harm prevented by the offense. citeturn185201search2
