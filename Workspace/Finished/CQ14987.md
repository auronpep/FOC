# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

Several years before his death, Paul owned Bethany Kiln Yard in fee simple. Wanting Lydia to keep using the parcel during her life, Paul executed and delivered a proper warranty deed conveying Bethany Kiln Yard to Lydia "for and during the term of Lydia's natural life." The deed did not name anyone else to take an estate or interest. Lydia moved her kiln and Scripture-calligraphy supplies onto the parcel and has remained in possession.

Fifteen months ago, Paul died. His will, duly admitted to probate, provided: "I devise Bethany Kiln Yard to Ruth for her natural life and from and after her death to Barnabas, his heirs and assigns, forever." Administration of Paul's estate has been completed. Ruth claims the immediate right to possess Bethany Kiln Yard. Barnabas also asserts an immediate right to possess it.

In an appropriate lawsuit to which Lydia, Ruth, and Barnabas are parties, who should be adjudged to have the right to immediate possession?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

An owner deeds land to Lydia for Lydia's life, names no future taker, then later dies with a will leaving the same land to Ruth for life and then Barnabas. While Lydia is alive and in possession, who has the immediate right to possess the land?

## 3. Final answer choices

A. Ruth, because Paul's will was the last formal expression of where he wanted Bethany Kiln Yard to go.

B. Barnabas, because Lydia's estate ended at Paul's death and Paul had retained only a reversion in fee simple to pass by will.

C. Lydia, because Paul's later will could not disturb the life estate he had already conveyed to her.

D. Ruth, because Lydia's life estate was measured by Paul's life and ended when Paul died.

## 5. Correct answer

C (original key: A)

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Lydia received a present life estate measured by Lydia's own life when Paul delivered the deed. Gold Key: a later will cannot take back a present life estate already conveyed by deed; the will can pass only the future interest Paul still owned. Silver Key: the call asks for immediate possession, so start with the present possessory estate before looking at future takers. Because Lydia is alive and still in possession, she keeps the immediate right to possess Bethany Kiln Yard.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because a probated will sounds like the final, official statement of Paul's property plan. The breaker is that Paul's will spoke only to what Paul still owned after the earlier deed, and he no longer owned Lydia's present life estate. Gold Key: a later will cannot disturb the already-delivered life estate.

B is attractive because it uses the right future-interest word and points to the person who ultimately receives the fee. The breaker is that the reversion does not become possessory until Lydia's life estate ends, and Lydia's life estate did not end at Paul's death. Silver Key: an immediate-possession call does not let a future-interest holder jump ahead of the current life tenant.

D is attractive because it chooses the will's first taker and treats the owner's death as the obvious transition point. The breaker is visible in the deed language: the estate was for Lydia's natural life, not Paul's. The measuring life is Lydia, so Paul's death does not move possession to Ruth.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. A deed to a grantee "for life" creates a life estate measured by the named measuring life; when the deed says "for and during the term of Lydia's natural life," Lydia's life is the measuring life. If no remainder is named, the grantor retains a future interest. The grantor may transfer or devise that retained future interest, but the future interest does not give immediate possession until the prior life estate naturally ends.

The authority check supports the source key. Restatement (First) of Property secs. 18 and 154 frame the life estate and the retained reversionary interest. The Cornell Legal Information Institute's Wex entry on life estates likewise describes a life estate as lasting for the life of a specific person and recognizes the life tenant's possessory right during that period: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/life_estate. Pigg v. Haley, 294 S.E.2d 851 (Va. 1982), recognizes that language giving property for a person's natural life creates an express life estate with later interests taking after that life estate: https://law.justia.com/cases/virginia/supreme-court/1982/800529-1.html. Nothing in the research contradicts the supplied key.
