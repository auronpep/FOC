## 1. Final Question

Daniel served as the volunteer treasurer of Cornerstone Community Church for eight years, managing the church's building-fund account. After an unexplained $42,000 shortfall was discovered, police opened an investigation into Daniel for possible embezzlement. No charges had been filed. Before any arrest, Daniel hired a criminal-defense attorney, Lydia, who mailed a letter to the police department stating that Daniel would not submit to any questioning unless Lydia was present. Four days later, detectives arrested Daniel without a warrant at a coffee shop based on probable cause. They administered Miranda warnings at the station, and Daniel — who had forgotten about Lydia's letter — waived his rights orally and answered questions. He made several incriminating admissions about diverting building-fund money to his personal accounts.

Daniel moves to suppress the statements under the **Sixth Amendment**.

**Should the court suppress Daniel's statements on Sixth Amendment grounds?**

**A.** No, because Daniel validly waived his Miranda rights, and the Fifth Amendment privilege — which governs pre-charge custodial interrogation — was fully satisfied.

**B.** Yes, because Daniel had retained counsel and affirmatively invoked his right to have counsel present before any interrogation occurred.

**C.** Yes, because any statement taken from a defendant who has retained counsel is automatically inadmissible unless counsel was notified and given the opportunity to be present.

**D.** No, because the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had not yet attached at the time of the interrogation, since no formal adversarial judicial proceedings had been initiated.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam-Register)

**Answer D is correct.** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel is not triggered merely because a suspect has hired a lawyer or because the lawyer has contacted police. The right attaches only when the government has initiated formal adversarial judicial proceedings — by indictment, information, arraignment, or preliminary hearing. *Rothgery v. Gillespie County*, 554 U.S. 191 (2008). At the time Daniel was questioned, no charges had been filed and no judicial proceedings had begun. The Sixth Amendment therefore had not attached, and the interrogation was governed exclusively by the Fifth Amendment and *Miranda v. Arizona*, 384 U.S. 436 (1966). Because Miranda warnings were administered and Daniel voluntarily waived his rights, the Fifth Amendment was satisfied. The Sixth Amendment provides no basis for suppression.

**Gold Key (GK-CRIM-RTC-01):** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches at the initiation of *adversarial judicial proceedings* — not at arrest, not at retention of counsel, not when a lawyer sends a letter to police. The trigger is a formal step: indictment, information, arraignment, or preliminary hearing. Anything before that is *Miranda* territory only.

## 9. Black-Letter Law Verification

### Governing Rule

The Sixth Amendment provides: "In all criminal prosecutions, the accused shall enjoy the right … to have the Assistance of Counsel for his defence." U.S. Const. amend. VI.

The right to counsel does **not** attach at the moment of arrest, upon retention of an attorney, or when counsel contacts law enforcement. It attaches only "at or after the time that judicial proceedings have been initiated against [the defendant] — 'whether by way of formal charge, preliminary hearing, indictment, information, or arraignment.'" *Rothgery v. Gillespie County*, 554 U.S. 191, 198 (2008) (quoting *McNeil v. Wisconsin*, 501 U.S. 171, 175 (1991), and *Brewer v. Williams*, 430 U.S. 387, 398 (1977)).

### Application to This Question

At the time Daniel was interrogated: (1) no indictment had been returned; (2) no information had been filed; (3) no arraignment had occurred; (4) no preliminary hearing had been held. The Sixth Amendment right to counsel had therefore **not attached**. The interrogation was governed solely by the Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination as implemented through *Miranda v. Arizona*, 384 U.S. 436 (1966). Miranda warnings were administered. Daniel voluntarily waived his rights and spoke. No Fifth Amendment violation occurred.

### Distractor Audit

- **Choice B (dominant trap):** Misstates the law by treating retention of counsel + lawyer's letter as a Sixth Amendment invocation. The Sixth Amendment had not attached. *Rothgery*; *McNeil*.
- **Choice C (overbroad rule):** Invents a blanket inadmissibility rule for statements taken from represented defendants pre-indictment. No such rule exists. Pre-charge interrogation is governed by *Miranda*, not the Sixth Amendment. *McNeil v. Wisconsin*, 501 U.S. 171 (1991).
- **Choice A (Miranda-waiver framing):** While factually correct that Miranda was satisfied, this choice frames the outcome as a waiver of the *Sixth* Amendment, which misstates the doctrine. The Sixth Amendment right did not exist to be waived. The correct basis is non-attachment.

### Verdict

The credited answer (D) is legally sound. The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are **unchanged** from the original. `bank_validation_verdict: PASS`.

### 2. The 17-Section Student Case Study

#### Program Frame

This item tests your ability to distinguish between the Fifth Amendment right to counsel (Miranda) and the Sixth Amendment right to counsel — specifically, **when the Sixth Amendment right attaches**. This is one of the most frequently tested distinctions in Criminal Procedure, and the MBE loves to bait students into conflating the two.

#### Student Access Test

Every claim in this case study can be evaluated using only the facts in the stem, the C3 method, and the Gold/Silver Keys provided. No outside legal doctrine is required to eliminate any choice. The controlling test is satisfied.

#### Question Data

**Stem:** Daniel, a church treasurer, is suspected of embezzling $42K. He retained attorney Lydia, who mailed police a letter saying he wouldn't speak without her. Detectives arrested Daniel at a coffee shop pre-indictment. Miranda warnings were given. Daniel forgot about the letter, waived his rights, and confessed.

**Call:** Should the court suppress Daniel's statements on Sixth Amendment grounds?

**Choices:**
- **A:** No — Miranda rights were validly waived; 5th Amendment satisfied.
- **B:** Yes — Daniel retained counsel and invoked his right to counsel.
- **C:** Yes — any statement from a represented defendant without counsel is automatically inadmissible.
- **D:** No — the 6th Amendment right to counsel had not yet attached.

#### Distilled Core Question

A suspect retained a lawyer who notified police that the client would not speak without counsel. Police later arrested the suspect pre-indictment, gave Miranda warnings, and the suspect waived and talked. Does the Sixth Amendment require suppression?

#### Call and Prediction

**Call type:** "Should the court suppress… on Sixth Amendment grounds?" — This is a specific-amendment call. The question is not "should the court suppress?" generally — it asks about the **Sixth Amendment** specifically. Any valid answer must address the Sixth Amendment.

**Prediction:** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel requires formal adversarial proceedings to attach. No charges had been filed. The right had not attached. The answer should be **No** (do not suppress), and the reason should be **non-attachment** of the Sixth Amendment.

#### Trigger Facts

| Fact | Significance |
|------|-------------|
| Daniel retained Lydia before any charges | Retention alone does not trigger 6th Amendment |
| Lydia mailed a letter to police | Lawyer's letter does not invoke 6th Amendment; relevant to Miranda only if suspect personally invokes |
| No indictment, information, arraignment, or preliminary hearing | **Critical**: no formal adversarial proceedings → 6th Amendment has not attached |
| Arrested at a coffee shop without a warrant | Arrest does not trigger 6th Amendment attachment |
| Miranda warnings administered | 5th Amendment/Miranda framework applies |
| Daniel waived his rights orally | Valid Miranda waiver → 5th Amendment satisfied |
| Daniel forgot about Lydia's letter | Confirms no personal invocation of Miranda right to counsel |

#### Choice-by-Choice Walkthrough

##### Choice A

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This choice says "No" (don't suppress) and talks about Miranda and the Fifth Amendment. It reaches the right conclusion but answers the *wrong question*. The call asks about the **Sixth** Amendment. This choice never mentions the Sixth Amendment. It's about the Fifth Amendment and Miranda. 3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** A correct "No" answer must address the Sixth Amendment directly, e.g., "No, because the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had not yet attached."

4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Pre-charge interrogation is governed by Miranda/5th Amendment. While Miranda was satisfied, framing the outcome as a waiver of the 6th Amendment misstates the doctrine. The 6th Amendment right did not exist to be waived. Not responsive to the 6th Amendment call.

##### Choice B

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** Daniel did hire a lawyer. The lawyer did send a letter. But the Sixth Amendment doesn't kick in just because you hired a lawyer. It only attaches when formal court proceedings begin — indictment, arraignment, information, or preliminary hearing. No charges had been filed. The 6th Amendment hadn't attached yet.

2. **Student label:** Conflates retaining a lawyer with invoking the 6th Amendment.

3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** "Yes, because after formal charges were filed, Daniel had a 6th Amendment right to counsel at all critical stages, and police interrogated him without counsel about the charged offense."

4. **Lawyer confirmation:** The 6th Amendment right to counsel attaches only at the initiation of formal adversarial judicial proceedings. *Rothgery v. Gillespie County*, 554 U.S. 191 (2008). Retaining counsel and having counsel contact police does not trigger the right.

##### Choice C

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This choice says any statement from a represented defendant without counsel is "automatically inadmissible." That's an absolute, categorical claim. The word "automatically" is a structural red flag. There is no such blanket rule — the Sixth Amendment doesn't even apply before formal proceedings begin. This overclaim is detectable from the text itself.

2. **Student label:** Overbroad absolute rule — too strong, doesn't exist.

3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** "Yes, because after indictment, the 6th Amendment right to counsel attached, and police deliberately elicited statements from Daniel about the charged offense without counsel present." (That would be a *Massiah* violation — but only post-indictment.)

4. **Lawyer confirmation:** No blanket rule bars statements from represented defendants pre-indictment. The 6th Amendment does not attach until formal adversarial proceedings begin. *McNeil v. Wisconsin*, 501 U.S. 171 (1991).

##### Choice D

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** No formal charges had been filed — no indictment, no arraignment, no preliminary hearing. The Sixth Amendment right to counsel only attaches when formal adversarial judicial proceedings begin. Since none had started, the 6th Amendment didn't apply. The interrogation was governed by Miranda, which was satisfied. This is the correct answer.

2. **Student label:** Correct — 6th Amendment had not attached.

3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** This IS the true/responsive version. It addresses the Sixth Amendment directly and gives the legally precise reason the right didn't apply.

4. **Lawyer confirmation:** The 6th Amendment right to counsel attaches only at the initiation of formal adversarial judicial proceedings. *Rothgery v. Gillespie County*, 554 U.S. 191 (2008). No proceedings had begun. Miranda warnings were given and Daniel voluntarily waived. No constitutional violation.
