## 6. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct. Gold Key:** A guilty plea is constitutionally valid only if the record shows the defendant knowingly and voluntarily waived core trial rights, including the right to a jury trial. Here, the judge reviewed the charges, possible defenses, and maximum sentences, and asked whether Daniel knew he did not have to plead guilty. But the judge did not determine whether Daniel understood that he had a right to a jury trial.

**Silver Key:** When the call asks which ground sets aside a guilty plea, lock onto the plea colloquy. The answer must attack the knowing-and-voluntary waiver itself, not an earlier motion, factual guilt, or the prosecutor’s file.

## 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A is incorrect.** The pending motion to dismiss is the dominant trap. It sounds procedural and important, especially because the delay is stated in the facts. But the call asks for a constitutional basis to set aside the guilty plea. A prior unresolved motion does not by itself show that Daniel’s plea was not knowing and voluntary. **Silver Key:** stay inside the plea colloquy.

**B is incorrect.** The judge was not constitutionally required to make an independent finding that Daniel committed the robbery and murder before accepting an ordinary guilty plea. This answer points to factual guilt, not to whether Daniel understood the trial right he was giving up. **Gold Key:** the constitutional plea defect is missing waiver understanding, not a separate judicial fact-finding of guilt.

**D is incorrect.** The prosecutor-file answer points to discovery and trial fairness. That is the wrong door for this call. The question is not asking whether there was an actual Brady violation or whether a later suppression claim could ever matter. It asks whether the judge’s acceptance of the plea was constitutionally defective. **Silver Key:** cut file/discovery answers unless they connect to the plea’s knowing-and-voluntary character.

## 8. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule is unchanged from the original. A guilty plea in state court must be knowing and voluntary. In *Boykin v. Alabama*, the Supreme Court identified the guilty plea as a waiver of several constitutional rights, including the right to trial by jury, and held that those waivers cannot be presumed from a silent record. ([Justia Law][1]) The Court also held that accepting a plea is reversible error where the record does not show that the defendant voluntarily and understandingly entered it. ([Justia Law][1])

That makes C the constitutional defect. The judge did not determine whether Daniel understood that he had a right to jury trial. A does not work because a valid guilty plea generally breaks the chain of earlier proceedings and does not allow later independent attacks on antecedent constitutional claims. ([Justia Law][2]) B does not work because an ordinary guilty plea itself normally supplies the admission supporting judgment; a separate express factual admission is not the same as the Boykin waiver inquiry. ([Justia Law][3]) D does not work as a judge-colloquy defect. The Supreme Court has emphasized that the Constitution does not require complete pre-plea knowledge of all useful information and, in *Ruiz*, did not require pre-plea disclosure of material impeachment evidence. ([Justia Law][4])

#### C. The judge did not determine whether Daniel understood that he had a right to a jury trial.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This is the only answer aimed at what Daniel understood he was giving up by pleading guilty.
2. **Student label:** Waiver-right defect.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** This is already true and responsive.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Boykin requires the record to show that a guilty plea waives core constitutional trial rights knowingly and voluntarily, including jury trial.

#### D. The judge did not determine whether the prosecutor’s file contained any undisclosed exculpatory material.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This answer points to prosecutor-file discovery. That is not the plea-colloquy waiver door.
2. **Student label:** Discovery-file trap.

4. **Lawyer confirmation:** The judge need not inspect the prosecutor’s file before accepting a plea. This answer asks a different question from the Boykin plea-waiver inquiry.

**Residual answer: C.**
