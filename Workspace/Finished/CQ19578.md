### 6. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. Rule 16 does not limit pretrial conferences to a single meeting, and it does not make attendance purely lawyer-only when settlement management makes party presence appropriate. The court may require a party to be present, or reasonably available by other means, to consider possible settlement at a pretrial conference. Gold Key: if appropriate, a federal court may require a party to be present or reasonably available by other means at a pretrial conference to consider possible settlement. Silver Key: when two answers say yes, choose the one that states the legal authority for the result, not the one that merely points to the party's status as plaintiff.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because it sounds protective of a reluctant party and treats pretrial conferences as lawyer-only events. The breaker is that Rule 16(c)(1) expressly allows the court, if appropriate, to require a party or representative to be present or reasonably available by other means to consider possible settlement.

C is attractive because it turns "second conference" into a neat cap. The breaker is that Rule 16 permits one or more pretrial conferences; there is no one-conference limit.

D is attractive because it reaches the same yes result and uses a visible fact from the stem. The breaker is that plaintiff status is not the legal reason the court may require attendance. A correct result with the wrong legal reason is not responsive.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Civil Procedure, Pretrial Procedures, Pretrial Conferences and Orders, and the verified outline code is `95070600`. Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(a) authorizes a federal court to order attorneys and any unrepresented parties to appear for one or more pretrial conferences for purposes including expediting the action, discouraging wasteful pretrial activity, improving trial preparation, and facilitating settlement. Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(c)(1) provides that a represented party must authorize at least one attorney to make stipulations and admissions about matters reasonably anticipated for discussion, and if appropriate, the court may require that a party or its representative be present or reasonably available by other means to consider possible settlement. Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(f) supplies sanctions for failure to appear at a scheduling or other pretrial conference, lack of good-faith participation, or failure to obey a scheduling or other pretrial order. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(a), 16(c)(1), 16(f); Cornell LII, Rule 16, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_16. That confirms the credited answer: Peter must attend or be reasonably available because the court may require party presence for settlement when appropriate. No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is triggered.
