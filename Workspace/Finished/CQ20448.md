### 7. Correct answer explanation

C is correct. The tested rule is the timing prerequisite for a renewed judgment as a matter of law after a jury verdict. Under Rule 50, a party must move for judgment as a matter of law before the case is submitted to the jury. Only after making that pre-submission motion may the party renew the motion after judgment. The bank waited until after entry of judgment, so the court should deny the renewed motion without reaching the merits.

Gold Key: A renewed Rule 50(b) motion after judgment is unavailable unless the movant made a Rule 50(a) motion before the case was submitted to the jury.

Silver Key: Before weighing the evidence, check whether the movant preserved the Rule 50 path before the jury received the case.

### 8. Wrong-answer explanations

A. The trap sells the strongest merits story: Martha had only one witness and a few emails, while the bank produced records and experts. That makes the answer feel powerful. But the bank's post-verdict motion is procedurally unavailable because the bank did not preserve the Rule 50 path before submission. The merits may be attractive, but the timing prerequisite controls.

B. The trap uses a familiar-sounding review phrase, but "clearly erroneous" is not the governing standard for this motion. More importantly, the bank did not make the required pre-submission Rule 50(a) motion, so the renewed motion cannot be granted after judgment.

D. The trap states a true point: a judgment-as-a-matter-of-law motion does not require the movant to present new evidence. But that point does not answer the precise call. The bank's problem is not lack of new evidence; it is failure to move before the case went to the jury.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

Authority floor: Fed. R. Civ. P. 50(a)(2) provides that a motion for judgment as a matter of law may be made at any time before the case is submitted to the jury. Fed. R. Civ. P. 50(b) allows renewal after judgment only if a Rule 50(a) motion was made. Cornell LII's Rule 50 text states the same timing rule: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_50. The Supreme Court's Rule 50 preservation principle is reflected in Unitherm Food Systems, Inc. v. Swift-Eckrich, Inc., 546 U.S. 394 (2006).

Applied to the transformed facts, the bank's experts and records may make the merits look strong, but the bank did not make a Rule 50(a) motion before the case was submitted to the jury. Because the renewed post-judgment motion was not preserved, the court should deny it. The result is unchanged from the original item: the correct answer is the denial grounded in the missing pre-submission motion, not a merits ruling or a clearly-erroneous review standard.
