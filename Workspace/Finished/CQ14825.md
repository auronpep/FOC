### 1. Final Question

Lydia has volunteered for years at Grace Community Food Pantry, a Christian ministry that gives groceries to families in need. She was working the distribution line on a busy Saturday when a man she later identified as the defendant took several boxes of donated canned goods from the loading area without authorization and loaded them into his truck. ### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam-register)

**C is correct.** Under FRE 612(a), an adverse party has options when a witness uses a writing to refresh memory: (1) while testifying, or (2) before testifying, if the court decides that justice requires the party to have those options. FRE 612(b) then gives the adverse party the right to have the writing produced at the hearing, to inspect it, to cross-examine the witness about it, and to introduce in evidence any portion that relates to the witness's testimony.

Because Lydia used the report to refresh before testifying, the court has discretion and may allow the defendant to examine it if justice so requires. If she had used the report while actually on the witness stand, the court would have no discretion—the defendant would be entitled to examine it. The timing of the refresh controls the strength of the adverse party's right.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is attractive** because students often assume that only the witness's own prior statements or adopted writings can be demanded by the opponent (a hearsay or Jencks-like intuition). The breaker is that FRE 612 gives the adverse party inspection and cross-examination rights regardless of whether the writing contains the witness's own statement or was prepared by a third party. The rule focuses on the refresh act and its potential effect on testimony, not on authorship or adoption.

**B is attractive** because students remember the "fresh in memory" requirement from the recorded-recollection hearsay exception (FRE 803(5)) and import it here. The breaker is that FRE 612 has no such prerequisite. An adverse party may obtain the writing used to refresh regardless of whether the witness read and approved it while the matter was fresh. The rule's purpose is to let the opponent test the credibility of the refreshed testimony, not to police how the writing was created.

**D is attractive** (and was the dominant trap at 18%) because it states a clean, memorable half-rule: "only on the stand." Students picture the writing being handed to the witness in open court and assume pre-testimony refresh is off-limits or irrelevant. The breaker is the explicit "before testifying, if the court decides that justice requires" clause in FRE 612(a)(2). The court may order production for pre-testimony refresh; it must do so for refresh that occurs while the witness is testifying.
