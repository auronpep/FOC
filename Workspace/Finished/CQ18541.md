## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Naomi owned, in fee simple absolute, a lakeside cabin lot. In a valid will, she devised the lot to "those of my nieces who survive me, in equal shares, as joint tenants and not as tenants in common, with full right of survivorship." When Naomi signed the will, Ruth and Esther, both adults, were her only nieces. Two months later, Ruth borrowed $18,000 from Cedar Gate Credit Union and signed a note secured by a mortgage on the cabin lot. A few weeks later, Ruth died without repaying the loan. The next spring, Naomi died, survived by Esther as her only surviving niece. A state statute provides that a mortgagee of realty holds equitable title to the realty.

Which statement best describes Esther's interest in the cabin lot after Naomi's death?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Naomi's will did not give Ruth anything while Naomi was alive. It gave the cabin lot only to nieces who survived Naomi. Ruth died before Naomi, so Ruth never entered the taking class and never acquired a mortgageable interest in the lot. Gold Key: a will speaks at the testator's death, and a survival-conditioned class gift includes only the class members alive at that death. A person who dies before the testator cannot burden the later devise by mortgaging an expectancy. Silver Key: read the answer array in two gates: first identify who took under the will, then ask whether the lender could attach to that person's interest. Once Ruth is outside the surviving class, the mortgage-title statute has nothing to operate on.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because the statute says a mortgagee holds equitable title, and that can make a lender sound like a co-owner once a mortgage is signed. The breaker is the first gate: Ruth had only an expectancy before Naomi died, and she died before the will took effect. The co-tenancy frame never starts.

C is the dominant trap because it gets the first half right: Esther is the sole owner. The trap is keeping Cedar Gate's lien alive anyway. Gold Key: Ruth never acquired the devised interest because she did not survive Naomi, so her attempted mortgage could not leave a lien on property she never owned.

D is attractive because the will itself uses joint-tenancy language, and the statute makes the mortgagee sound like a title holder. The breaker is that joint tenancy was created only among nieces who survived Naomi. Cedar Gate is not a surviving niece, and Ruth never became a joint tenant whose interest could be transferred.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The original and transformed items both test a testamentary class gift conditioned on survival, plus a mortgage from a person who had only an expectancy and died before the testator.

The black-letter anchor is that a will operates at the testator's death, and the members of a survival-conditioned testamentary class are determined at that point. Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers section 13.1 treats a class gift as a disposition to beneficiaries described by a group label whose identities and shares may fluctuate. Modern probate statutes also treat class-gift survival as a real gate: Massachusetts General Laws ch. 190B, section 2-603, for example, addresses a person who would have been a devisee under a class gift "if he had survived the testator." Source: https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/ArticleII/Section2-603. Section 2-601 makes construction rules yield to contrary intent shown in the will, and survivorship language is a classic express condition. Source: https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleII/Chapter190B/ArticleII/Section2-601. Courts also state the basic timing rule directly: a deed is effective on delivery, but a will speaks at the testator's death. Source: Murphy v. Traylor, 289 So. 2d 584 (Ala. 1974), https://law.justia.com/cases/alabama/supreme-court/1974/289-so-2d-584-1.html.

The mortgage layer does not change the result. Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages section 1.1 defines a mortgage as a conveyance or retention of an interest in real property as security for an obligation; courts quote that rule in mortgage-validity analysis. Source: JPMorgan Chase & Co. v. Casarano, https://masslawyersweekly.com/fulltext-opinions/2012/02/28/jpmorgan-chase-co-inc-et-al-v-casarano-lawyers-weekly-no-11-032-12/. A lender can receive only the real-property interest its borrower can mortgage. Ruth had no present interest while Naomi lived, and Ruth never acquired the devise because she died before Naomi. Therefore Cedar Gate received no title, no co-tenancy interest, and no lien in the cabin lot. The official key is confirmed.
