## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
D is correct. Lydia's qualification as an expert is a preliminary admissibility question. Gold Key: the judge decides preliminary questions about whether a witness is qualified, and the judge is not bound by the evidence rules except privilege rules when making that decision. Silver Key: lock the decisionmaker first. The email may help the judge decide whether Lydia is qualified, but that does not make the email jury evidence.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer
The tested issue remains Evidence, Presentation of Evidence, preliminary questions, and the verified outline code is `31010107`. The legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row: a writing offered to support an expert's qualifications may be considered by the judge, without regard to the hearsay rule, when the judge is deciding the preliminary admissibility question.

The authority floor is satisfied by Federal Rule of Evidence 104(a). Rule 104(a) provides that the court decides preliminary questions about whether a witness is qualified, whether a privilege exists, or whether evidence is admissible; in making that decision, the court is not bound by evidence rules except those on privilege. Federal Rule of Evidence 702 supplies the expert-witness backdrop by requiring the proponent to demonstrate to the court that a qualified expert's testimony satisfies the rule. The Dec. 1, 2023 amendment to Rule 702 does not alter the answer because this item tests the Rule 104(a) decisionmaker and evidence-rules exemption, not the reliability elements of expert testimony.
