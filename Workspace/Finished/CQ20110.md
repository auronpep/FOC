### 1. Final Question

Peter owns a small Christian bookshop. He signed a written contract with Lydia, a print-shop owner, for Lydia to supply 200 copies of a devotion workbook in standard navy-blue covers. The contract included a clause requiring all changes to be in a signed writing. ### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam-register)

**Gold Key:** Under UCC § 2-209(4), an attempted oral modification that fails because of a no-oral-modification clause (or the Statute of Frauds) may nonetheless **operate as a waiver** of the particular contract term. The waiver can become irretractable when the other party has **materially relied** on the changed performance — UCC § 2-209(5) permits retraction only on reasonable notice, and courts limit that power when reliance makes retraction inequitable.

Here, Lydia's performance of three shipments in teal-and-gold covers was not a signed writing, so it was not an enforceable *modification*. But it **was** a waiver of the navy-blue-cover term. Peter then relied by spending on revised signage and advertising. That reliance grounds an estoppel-like bar to Lydia's sudden retraction without notice. The strongest argument for Peter tracks that waiver-and-reliance path: C in the original, **B** post-shuffle.

---

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (new letter D — dominant trap).** *What makes it attractive:* The parol evidence rule is the first doctrine many students reach for whenever a contract dispute involves evidence outside a signed writing. The stem mentions "signed writing" twice, and that language triggers a parol-evidence reflex. The rule is correctly described — it does bar evidence outside the original writing — so the answer *sounds right* at first pass. *The breaker:* The parol evidence rule applies to **prior or contemporaneous** agreements and negotiations, not to **post-formation conduct** like Lydia's three shipments and Peter's advertising spend. The evidence Peter would offer is later waiver evidence, not prior parol. Knowing that timing distinction — this is a post-formation change, not pre-contract parol — is the entireSilver Key: lock the call to "strongest contract-content argument," then separate *modification* (blocked by NOM clause) from *waiver* (separate UCC 2-209(4) path).

### 9. Black-Letter Legal Verification

**Governing rule — UCC § 2-209:**
- § 2-209(1): Modification needs no consideration (good faith suffices).
- § 2-209(2): A signed agreement excluding modification except by signed writing cannot be otherwise modified — this is the no-oral-modification (NOM) clause.
- § 2-209(4): An attempted modification that fails under subsection (2) or (3) **may operate as a waiver**. "A party who has made a waiver affecting an executory portion of the contract may retract the waiver if the retraction would be equitable and if the other party has not materially changed position in reliance on the waiver." — UCC § 2-209(5).

**Authority:** UCC Article 2, §§ 2-209(4) & 2-209(5). See also *Margolin v. Franklin*, 270 N.E.2d 140 (Ill. Ct. App. 1971) (accepting late payments over several months constituted a waiver despite Statute of Frauds bar); *Trad Industries v. Brogan*, 805 P.2d 54, 59 (Mont. 1991) (waiver requires reliance to limit retraction). The leading UCC commentary (White & Summers) confirms that § 2-209(4) creates a separate waiver channel even when a NOM clause blocks modification.

**Parol evidence rule:** Governed by UCC § 2-202 for sale-of-goods contracts; applies to prior/contemporaneous agreements, not post-formation conduct.

**Outcome confirmed:** The official key **C** — "Even if the oral change did not satisfy the signed-writing clause, the supplier's repeated performance may have operated as a waiver, and retraction may be ineffective after material reliance" — is correct under prevailing UCC § 2-209(4)-(5) doctrine. The transformation preserves this outcome exactly: post-shuffle answer **B** matches substance `kept`.

Outline code 52040100 (Gap-Fillers) verified in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md`. #### Choice-by-Choice Walkthrough

**Choice A: "Peter must first prove lost profits..."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The call asks about the contract *term* — what the cover-color obligation requires. 2. **Student label:** Damages-as-prerequisite.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** A correct version would state a rule about what the contract term requires, not a precondition for judicial action.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** No rule requires proof of lost profits to establish an operative contract term. Damages are a remedy question; term interpretation is a liability/obligation question. NOT_RESPONSIVE — wrong_element.

**Choice B: "Even if the phone request did not satisfy the signed-writing clause, Lydia's repeated shipments... may have operated as a waiver..."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The phone request failed the NOM clause — that's clear. But Lydia didn't just agree; she *performed* three times. 2. **Student label:** Waiver + reliance.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** This IS the true/responsive version. It correctly identifies the waiver path and the reliance limitation on retraction.

**Choice C: "The parol evidence rule bars Peter's evidence..."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The parol evidence rule is about what evidence a court can consider when interpreting a contract. But it applies to statements made *before or at the same time as* the contract — not to what the parties *did after* signing. Three shipments and ad spend are post-formation conduct, not prior negotiations.
2. **Student label:** Parol evidence misfit.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** A correct version would identify the right doctrine for post-formation conduct (waiver), not the wrong-timing doctrine (parol evidence).

**Choice D: "The no-oral-modification clause conclusively prevents any later conduct..."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** "Conclusively prevents" is an absolute word. Absolute words in MBE choices are usually wrong because the law almost always has exceptions. 2. **Student label:** NOM overclaim.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** A correct version would acknowledge that the NOM clause blocks modification but does not absolutely bar waiver through conduct.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** UCC 2-209(4) expressly creates a waiver channel that bypasses the NOM clause. "Conclusively prevents" is an overclaim. NOT_TRUE — tiered_absolute.
