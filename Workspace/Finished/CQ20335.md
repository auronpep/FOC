### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam-Register)

**C is correct.** **Gold Key: solicitation can be complete and still merge.** Solicitation is complete when the defendant intentionally asks, encourages, or requests another person to commit a crime. But if the solicited crime is actually committed, the usual merger rule absorbs the solicitation into the completed target offense. Mary can be liable for the completed larceny as an accomplice because Peter stole the tuners after her request. She may not also be convicted of solicitation to commit that same larceny when both charges arise from the same request and theft.

**Silver Key: read the call as "both convictions," not "was solicitation committed."** The answer does not turn on whether Mary committed solicitation at the moment she asked Peter. She did. The call asks whether the separate solicitation conviction survives after the completed larceny occurs. Under the usual merger rule, it does not.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. "Yes, because Peter, not Mary, carried the tuners out of the studio."**

This choice sells a physical-taker myth: if Peter carried the property away, Mary must be limited to the asking offense. The attractive move is to treat larceny as requiring Mary's own hands on the property. The breaker is accomplice liability. A person who intentionally solicits or aids another's theft can be liable for the completed theft as an accomplice. The merger question remains whether Mary can also keep a separate solicitation conviction for the same completed larceny. She cannot.

**B. "No, but only because handbell tuners are not property that can be stolen."**

This choice reaches the correct "No" result by changing the issue. It distracts the student into debating whether the item counts as stolen property. The stem assumes a completed larceny charge and asks only about merger. Brass handbell tuners are ordinary personal property, and their value is not what defeats the double conviction. The breaker is responsiveness: the reason must answer merger, not redefine the property.

**D. "Yes, because solicitation was complete as soon as Mary asked Peter to steal the tuners."**

This is the dominant trap because it starts with a true rule. Solicitation is complete when the request is made with the required intent; Peter did not need to agree for solicitation to exist. **Gold Key:** completion of solicitation is not the same thing as survival after merger. Once Peter completed the same larceny and Mary is charged as an accomplice to that completed offense, the solicitation is absorbed into the completed target crime. **Silver Key:** when the call asks whether both convictions may stand, do not stop at "the inchoate crime was complete." Ask whether the later completed offense swallows it.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule:** Solicitation is an inchoate offense. At common law and under the usual MBE merger rule, solicitation and attempt merge into the completed target offense when that target offense is committed; conspiracy is the standard exception. A defendant may be liable for the completed offense as an accomplice, but may not also be convicted of solicitation to commit that same completed offense when both charges rest on the same criminal episode.

**Authority and research confirmation:** Model Penal Code section 1.07(1)(b) treats preparation to commit another offense as a bar to multiple convictions for both the preparatory offense and its object, and the explanatory note to MPC section 5.05 states that section 1.07(1)(b) prohibits conviction of both the inchoate offense and the substantive offense that is its object. MPC section 5.05(3) also bars more than one Article 5 inchoate conviction for conduct designed to culminate in the same crime. Cornell LII's Wex summary states the same MBE-level rule: inchoate offenses merge into the target offense except conspiracy. Sources checked: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/inchoate_offense and https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2018/WorkGroups/House%20Judiciary/Bills/S.267/S.267~Matthew%20Valerio~Model%20Penal%20Code%205.05%20Brief~4-18-2018.pdf.

**Application:** Mary intentionally asked Peter to steal Lydia's handbell tuners. Peter agreed and completed the theft. Mary may be liable for the completed larceny under accomplice principles because the theft was the crime she asked Peter to commit. But the separate solicitation count is absorbed into the completed target offense. The correct result is no: Mary may not be convicted of both solicitation to commit larceny and the completed larceny when both charges arise from the same request and theft.

**Credited outcome confirmed:** The tested legal issue, merger rule, accomplice-liability posture, and credited outcome remain unchanged.
