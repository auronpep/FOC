## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** The state's witness is being used to prove a character-propensity chain: Timothy attacked without justification before and lied about self-defense, so he probably attacked without justification and lied about self-defense this time. **Gold Key:** the prosecution may not start with a defendant's prior bad act to show that the defendant acted in character on the charged occasion. That remains true even when the earlier act resembles the current defense. The testimony is not reputation or opinion evidence in rebuttal to a character witness; it is extrinsic proof of a specific prior violent act, offered for propensity. It is inadmissible.

**Silver Key:** when an answer pair splits between "inadmissible because character" and "inadmissible because irrelevant," do not stop at relevance. Relevant evidence can still be excluded by a rule. Here, the blocker is the character-evidence rule, not a lack of logical relevance.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A** is attractive because the prior event looks almost tailor-made to answer the defendant's self-defense story. It invites the student to say, "He falsely claimed self-defense before, so he is probably falsely claiming it now." The breaker is the Gold Key: that is exactly the propensity inference Rule 404 forbids. A prior bad act may be admissible for a noncharacter purpose, but this answer uses it to prove action in conformity with violent, false-claim conduct.

**C** reaches the same inadmissible result but gives the wrong reason. The prior event has a logical relationship to the self-defense story; a jury could think it makes Timothy's current self-defense claim less believable. The Silver Key controls the split: relevance is only the first door. The character-evidence rules can exclude evidence that is relevant when the relevance depends on a forbidden propensity chain.

**D** has a real hook because the prior story includes a false claim, and Timothy testified. The problem is the route. Even if the earlier false self-defense claim were treated as bearing on truthfulness, a specific instance used to attack truthfulness may not be proved through extrinsic testimony. This witness is not cross-examining Timothy with a limited truthfulness inquiry; she is adding outside proof of a prior violent episode.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

**Authority floor and current rule.** Federal Rule of Evidence 404(a)(1) bars character evidence offered to prove that a person acted in accordance with a character trait on a particular occasion. Federal Rule of Evidence 404(b)(1) similarly bars other crimes, wrongs, or acts offered to prove character in order to show action in accordance with that character. Rule 404(b)(2) allows other acts for genuine noncharacter purposes such as motive, opportunity, intent, preparation, plan, knowledge, identity, absence of mistake, or lack of accident, but the answer here uses the prior act to prove Timothy was again the aggressor and again falsely claiming self-defense. That is propensity.

Rule 405 confirms that when character evidence is admissible, ordinary proof is reputation or opinion, and specific instances are reserved for when character is an essential element or for limited cross-examination of a character witness. Rule 608(b) also blocks extrinsic evidence of specific conduct when the purpose is to attack or support a witness's character for truthfulness.
