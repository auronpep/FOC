### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** Under FRE 403 (and its state equivalents), even relevant evidence may be excluded if its probative value is substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice, confusion of the issues, misleading the jury, or waste of time. Polygraph results are inadmissible in this jurisdiction. When the witness volunteered that she passed a polygraph test, she injected inadmissible evidence into the trial. The testimony was also unresponsive to the yes/no question about lighting. Because polygraph evidence carries a high risk of unfair prejudice — jurors tend to treat polygraph results as highly reliable even though they are not — the probative value of this unresponsive, inadmissible testimony is substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice. The court should grant the motion to strike.

> **Gold Key (GK-EVID-POLY-01):** Polygraph/lie detector results are treated as inadmissible in many jurisdictions, and even where a jurisdiction has no per se rule, referencing them in testimony is grounds for exclusion under FRE 403 because jurors tend to overvalue them, creating unfair prejudice that substantially outweighs any probative value.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is incorrect.** This choice sells the misconception that evidence barred by a jurisdiction's rules becomes admissible simply because a witness volunteers it. That is not how evidence rules work. A jurisdiction that excludes polygraph results excludes them — the manner of injection (volunteered vs. elicited) does not override the exclusionary rule. The choice is a flat misstatement of law: polygraph results are not "admissible when volunteered." This distractor tests whether the student knows that a jurisdiction's bar on polygraph evidence is categorical.

**B is incorrect.** This is the dominant trap. The "opened the door" doctrine allows otherwise inadmissible evidence to be admitted when the opposing party has introduced the subject matter, but it does not override a jurisdiction's categorical bar on certain types of evidence. Polygraph results are inadmissible in this jurisdiction, and the open-door principle does not create an exception to that rule. The cross-examiner asked about lighting conditions — a proper impeachment topic — and the witness's unresponsive polygraph reference did not become admissible simply because the question was asked. The student who picks this choice conflates the open-door principle with a blanket license to introduce any evidence once a related topic is raised.

> **Gold Key (GK-EVID-POLY-01) applies here:** The open-door doctrine does not override a jurisdiction's categorical bar on polygraph evidence. The polygraph reference remains inadmissible regardless of who "opened the door."

**D is incorrect.** This choice confuses two separate issues. The motion to strike targets the witness's answer, not the form of the question. Leading questions are permitted on cross-examination, so even if the question were leading, that would not be a basis to strike the witness's answer. More importantly, the objection and motion to strike are directed at the content of the answer (inadmissible polygraph testimony), not at whether the question was properly formulated. The student who picks this choice is distracted by a surface-level procedural observation that has nothing to do with the real problem: inadmissible, unfairly prejudicial testimony.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Rule:** Federal Rule of Evidence 403 provides: "The court may exclude relevant evidence if its probative value is substantially outweighed by a danger of one or more of the following: unfair prejudice, confusing the issues, misleading the jury, undue delay, wasting time, or needlessly presenting cumulative evidence." *See* Fed. R. Evid. 403; *see also* Fla. Stat. § 90.403; Md. Rule 5-403.

**Authority:** The rule is a codification of the common-law balancing test. *See* Notes of Advisory Committee on Proposed Rules (citing Slough, *Relevancy Unraveled*, 5 Kan. L. Rev. 1, 12–15 (1956)). "Unfair prejudice" means "an undue tendency to suggest decision on an improper basis, commonly, though not necessarily, an emotional one." *Id.*

**Application:** Polygraph evidence is per se inadmissible in many jurisdictions, and even where not per se barred, courts routinely exclude it under Rule 403 because jurors tend to treat polygraph results as highly reliable — creating unfair prejudice that substantially outweighs the evidence's probative value. *See* BridgeLegal, *Polygraph Evidence in Legal Proceedings* (2026) (noting Rule 403 as the basis for excluding polygraph evidence). Here, the jurisdiction does not allow polygraph results, the witness volunteered the testimony unresponsive to the question, and the defense promptly moved to strike. The court should grant the motion.

**Verification:** The tested rule (FRE 403 discretionary exclusion), the legal issue (striking inadmissible, unfairly prejudicial testimony), and the credited outcome (grant the motion) are all unchanged from the original. The official key is confirmed correct. **Bank validation verdict: PASS.**

#### Wrong Answer Recovery Paths

**If student picked A:** The student believes that evidence barred by a jurisdiction's rules becomes admissible when volunteered. Remediation: A jurisdiction's evidence bar is categorical. The manner of injection (volunteered vs. elicited) does not override the bar. Review FRE 401-402 and the concept of categorical exclusion rules.

**If student picked B (dominant trap):** The student conflates the open-door doctrine with a blanket license to introduce inadmissible evidence. Remediation: The open-door doctrine permits otherwise inadmissible evidence when the opposing party introduces the subject, but it does not create exceptions to a jurisdiction's categorical bar. Review FRE 403 and the limits of the open-door doctrine. **Gold Key GK-EVID-POLY-01.**

**If student picked D:** The student confuses the leading-question issue with the motion to strike. Remediation: Leading questions are permitted on cross-examination (FRE 611(c)). The motion to strike targets the answer's content, not the question's form. Review the distinction between objections to questions and motions to strike answers.

#### Review Truth

Even relevant evidence may be excluded if its probative value is substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice.

#### Gold Key(s)

**GK-EVID-POLY-01**
- **Statement:** Polygraph/lie detector results are treated as inadmissible in many jurisdictions, and even where not per se barred, referencing them in testimony is grounds for exclusion under FRE 403 because jurors tend to overvalue them, creating unfair prejudice that substantially outweighs any probative value.
- **Type:** rule
- **Unlocks:** The open-door trap (B) and the volunteered-evidence trap (A)
- **Trigger:** Any fact pattern where polygraph or lie detector testimony is referenced in a jurisdiction that bars it
- **Tested choice:** B
- **Authority:** Fed. R. Evid. 403; Fla. Stat. § 90.403; Md. Rule 5-403
- **Last minute review:** true

#### Silver Key(s)

**SK-EVID-403-01**
- **Statement:** When a witness volunteers inadmissible testimony, first ask: "Is there a jurisdiction-specific bar on this evidence?" If yes, the bar controls — open-door, rehabilitation, and "volunteered" arguments don't override it. Then ask: "What's the correct basis for striking it?" The answer is almost always FRE 403 unfair prejudice.
- **Type:** cut
- **Navigates:** The initial decision to grant vs. deny the motion; eliminates the "deny" choices quickly once the student recognizes the jurisdiction's bar is categorical
- **Trigger:** Witness references polygraph, lie detector, or other categorically barred evidence
- **Tested choice:** B
- **Outline code:** 32020302
- **Last minute review:** true
