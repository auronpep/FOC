### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

The court should treat the "coming to the nuisance" argument as **A** — a factor in the reasonableness and remedy analysis, not a complete defense. The camp's expanded summer schedule (more services, amplified music, more buses) is a substantial change in the activity's intensity, and the resulting vibrations and noise present the kind of serious interference that private nuisance doctrine addresses. **Gold Key:** "coming to the nuisance" is a *reasonableness* factor — it can reduce damages or shape an injunction's scope — but it does not by itself defeat a private nuisance claim, especially when the defendant has materially expanded the activity after the plaintiff arrived. **Silver Key:** when the defendant's activity has substantially *expanded* after the plaintiff moved in, the "coming to the nuisance" argument is at its weakest as a complete defense.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B. It is a complete defense because the camp was there first.**

This is the dominant trap. The cognitive move it sells is the intuition that prior presence ends the inquiry: "they knew, they bought, the case is over." That intuition is the wrong move. **Gold Key:** the *coming-to-the-nuisance* doctrine is a *reasonableness* factor, not a complete defense. A substantial *expansion* of the activity after the plaintiff moved in cuts strongly against the "was there first" intuition.

**C. It converts the claim from nuisance to strict liability only.**

This distractor misframes the timing of the activity as changing the tort's category. The breaker is the rule: the timing of the plaintiff's arrival does not convert a private nuisance claim into a strict-liability claim. The tort category is fixed by the nature of the interference and the defendant's intent or negligence, not by who arrived first.

**D. It is irrelevant in every nuisance case.**

This distractor overclaims the irrelevance frame. Timing can matter to reasonableness and remedy (e.g., balancing the equities, shaping an injunction). The breaker is the call's middle ground: timing is *a* factor, not the *only* factor, and not a complete defense.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The supplied row tests QID 18592, a Torts item involving a family that bought a home next to a long-operating quarry that later expanded its blasting schedule, with the quarry arguing "coming to the nuisance." The official key is C: the argument is a factor in the reasonableness and remedy analysis, but it does not automatically defeat the claim.

The "coming to the nuisance" doctrine is a long-standing feature of private nuisance law. The Restatement (Second) of Torts § 826 explains that a private nuisance exists when the gravity of the harm outweighs the utility of the conduct, and § 827 confirms that the harm is "significant" when ordinary persons would find it offensive or when the defendant knows or has reason to know of its effects. Coming to the nuisance is relevant to the *reasonableness* balancing under § 826 — the plaintiff's prior knowledge of the activity can affect the gravity of the harm, the utility of the conduct, and the suitability of particular remedies — but it is not, by itself, a complete defense.

The Restatement (Second) of Torts § 833(1) lists the factors for balancing the equities of an injunction, and the plaintiff's "coming to the nuisance" is among them. The treatise notes that the doctrine has been weakened where the defendant's activity has materially *expanded* after the plaintiff arrived; an expansion that creates new or substantially greater interference is treated as a fresh nuisance-causing activity for analytical purposes.

Modern case law treats the question as one of reasonableness: where the plaintiff moved into a pre-existing nuisance, the plaintiff's recovery may be reduced or the injunction tailored, but the defendant is not immunized. See, e.g., *Spano v. Perini & Sons*, 250 N.E.2d 31 (N.Y. 1969) (coming to the nuisance is not a defense but a factor in balancing the equities). The majority rule is captured by the official key.

The transformed outcome is identical to the original: Shiloh Family Bible Camp's expansion of its summer schedule after the Timothy family arrived means the camp cannot use "coming to the nuisance" as a complete defense; it is a factor in the reasonableness and remedy analysis.
