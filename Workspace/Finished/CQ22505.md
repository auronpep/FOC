# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

Lydia owned a private Christian music studio that rented rehearsal rooms and hosted paid workshops. She entered into a written contract with Daniel, a carpenter, to convert one storage room into a small recording booth. The written contract required the booth to be finished by August 31.

During negotiations, Lydia told Daniel that the recording booth project depended on the studio receiving a city small-business grant because she wanted to use the booth for grant-funded community workshops as well as for ordinary studio lessons. Daniel thought Lydia was very likely to receive the grant, so he orally agreed that the booth project was dependent on the studio receiving the grant.

After signing the contract, Lydia left on a three-week teaching tour and did not respond to Daniel's calls. Because the grant committee's public scoring sheet ranked Lydia's application first, and because Daniel was worried about finishing by the contract deadline, Daniel went ahead and completed the booth on August 12.

On August 13, the city denied Lydia's grant application after the grant budget was cut. Daniel submitted his bill the next day. Lydia contacted her attorney and said she did not want to pay Daniel under the contract.

What is Lydia's attorney's best argument against paying under the contract?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

If parties orally agree that a written service contract depends on a grant award, and the worker performs before the grant is denied, what is the best contract argument against payment?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

A. The rules of construction allow evidence of the oral agreement to clear up an ambiguity in the contract.

B. At the time of contracting, both parties believed Lydia would receive the grant.

C. Receiving the grant was a condition precedent to the contract.

D. The purpose of the contract was frustrated when Lydia did not receive the grant.

## 4. Correct Answer

C (original key B).

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. The strongest argument is that the written contract never became effective because both parties orally agreed that Lydia's receipt of the grant was a condition precedent. Daniel completed the booth before that condition occurred. When the grant was denied, the condition failed, so Lydia's duty to pay under the contract did not arise.

Gold Key: A condition precedent is an event that must occur before a contractual duty becomes due; an oral agreement that a written contract is subject to that condition can keep the writing from being effective as to that condition.

Silver Key: Separate a "contract never turned on" argument from a "contract turned on but later got excused" argument. The condition-precedent answer wins because it attacks the duty before it arises.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

D. This is the dominant trap. The grant denial feels like it destroyed Lydia's reason for ordering the booth, especially because the grant-funded workshops were mentioned during negotiations. The breaker is the Gold Key: Lydia's better argument is not that an existing duty was later excused by frustration, but that the oral grant condition had to occur before the contract duty arose.

A. This choice is attractive because it invites the student to treat the oral agreement as interpretation evidence. The breaker is the call's best-argument posture. The attorney does not need an ambiguity theory; the sharper rule is that the oral condition precedent may prevent the written contract from taking effect as to that condition.

B. This choice sells the fairness instinct that both sides made a bad prediction. Shared optimism about a future event is not itself a contract defense. The missing piece is the actual oral agreement making grant receipt a condition precedent.

## 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer

The tested legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged from source question 22505. The item tests the effect of an oral condition precedent attached to a written contract. The credited answer remains that the nonoccurrence of the agreed condition prevents the contractual duty from arising.

Authority floor: Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 217 states the rule for an integrated agreement subject to an oral requirement of a condition: when parties to a written agreement orally agree that performance depends on a stated condition, the writing is not integrated with respect to that oral condition. Restatement section 224 defines a condition as an uncertain event that must occur before performance becomes due, unless excused. Restatement section 225 addresses the effect of nonoccurrence of a condition. Cornell Wex describes the parol evidence rule baseline for prior or contemporaneous extrinsic agreements, while the oral-condition rule supplies the relevant exception for this item.

Authority links: Restatement section 217, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/11720-bruckner-howard-law-contracts-2024/resources/7.6-restatement-second-of-contracts-217/; Restatement section 224, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/11720-bruckner-howard-law-contracts-2024/resources/11.1.1-restatement-second-of-contracts-224/; Restatement section 225, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/11720-bruckner-howard-law-contracts-2024/resources/11.1.2-restatement-second-of-contracts-225/; Cornell Wex parol evidence overview, https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/parol_evidence_rule; Morgan Stanley High Yield Securities, Inc. v. Seven Circle Gaming Corp., https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/269/206/2528327/.

The authority confirms the credited answer. The grant award was not merely Lydia's hoped-for purpose; Daniel orally agreed that the project depended on the grant. The grant denial therefore supports the condition-precedent argument. Frustration is weaker because the booth can still be used for ordinary studio lessons and because the cleaner argument is that the contract duty never became due. Ambiguity is weaker because the oral condition is not being used to clarify an ambiguous written term. Mutual expectation is weaker because prediction alone is not a condition.
