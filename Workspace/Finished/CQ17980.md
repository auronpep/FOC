Their contract contained a valid mandatory forum-selection clause requiring any federal lawsuit to be filed in the federal district court in State Bethany. After a payment dispute, Martha filed a diversity action against Barnabas in federal court in State Alpha. On Barnabas's motion, the case was transferred to federal court in State Bethany to enforce the clause. ### 7. Full right-answer explanation
A is correct. This is not an ordinary convenience transfer where the transferor state's choice-of-law rules tag along to prevent forum shopping. Gold Key: when a plaintiff files outside a valid mandatory forum-selection clause and the case is transferred to the agreed federal forum to enforce that clause, the plaintiff does not keep the transferor-law benefit. Silver Key: name the CLASH as ordinary convenience transfer versus clause-enforcement transfer before choosing which state's choice-of-law rules travel. Because the transfer enforced the State Bethany federal forum clause that Martha violated, the State Bethany federal court generally applies State Bethany choice-of-law rules.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
B is attractive because dismissal sounds like a stronger remedy for violating a mandatory forum clause. The breaker is that a federal forum-selection clause can be enforced through transfer to the agreed federal court, so a dismissal-only answer uses the wrong remedy frame.

C is attractive because it remembers the ordinary Van Dusen/Ferens transfer rule: after a normal Section 1404(a) transfer, the transferee court generally applies the transferor state's choice-of-law rules. The breaker is the Gold Key: Atlantic Marine creates the forum-selection-clause exception, so a plaintiff who filed in the wrong contractual forum does not carry State Alpha's choice-of-law rules into State Bethany.

D is attractive because "federal common law" sounds like a neutral way for a federal court to manage a multi-state diversity problem. The breaker is that courts do not choose the most plaintiff-friendly law; the question asks which state's choice-of-law rules apply after a forum-selection transfer.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Civil Procedure, Jurisdiction and Venue, Transfer, and the verified outline code is `94050200`. The governing statute is 28 U.S.C. Section 1404(a), which permits a district court to transfer a civil action to another district where it might have been brought or to a district to which all parties have consented. See https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1404.

The controlling Supreme Court authority is Atlantic Marine Construction Co. v. U.S. District Court, 571 U.S. 49 (2013). Atlantic Marine holds that a valid forum-selection clause pointing to another federal forum is enforced through Section 1404(a), that the plaintiff's forum choice gets no weight when the plaintiff filed in violation of the clause, and that a transfer enforcing the clause does not carry the original venue's choice-of-law rules. The transferee court in the agreed federal forum therefore ordinarily applies its own state's choice-of-law rules. See https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/12-929.

This confirms the supplied key. The variation preserves the same rule, same legal issue, same credited outcome, and same trap: the plaintiff wants the ordinary transferor-law benefit, but Atlantic Marine withholds that benefit when the plaintiff violated a valid forum-selection clause.
