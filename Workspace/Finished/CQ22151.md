## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Stephen was tried for receiving stolen property after police found him using a sound mixer that had been taken from a Christian music school. The prosecution called Paul to the stand. Paul testified that, during a conversation with Stephen in the county jail shortly after Stephen's arrest, Stephen admitted that he knew the sound mixer had been stolen before he brought it to a youth worship night.

Which fact or inference would best support Stephen's motion to exclude Paul's testimony?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

What fact best supports excluding a jailhouse confession as protected by attorney-client privilege?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

D is the strongest support for exclusion. Attorney-client privilege can extend beyond the lawyer to the lawyer's agents when the communication is made in confidence for legal advice. Gold Key: the privilege turns on a confidential legal-advice channel, so a reasonable belief that Paul was the lawyer's investigator gives Stephen the only listed path to privilege. Silver Key: separate "this witness is unreliable" facts from "this communication is privileged" facts; only the latter supports exclusion on privilege grounds.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

B is attractive because Paul used the lawyer label. The breaker is purpose: merely saying "I am a lawyer" does not show that Stephen was speaking to obtain legal advice or within a confidential attorney-client channel.

A is attractive because Paul steered Stephen toward counsel and asked for case facts. The breaker is agency: recommending a lawyer does not make Paul the lawyer, the lawyer's agent, or a necessary part of Stephen's legal-advice relationship.

C is attractive because it attacks Paul's motives hard. The breaker is remedy: a plea bargain may support impeachment for bias, but it does not make Stephen's prior admission privileged or inadmissible on this objection.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule is unchanged: the defendant needs a privilege basis to exclude a jailhouse admission. In federal criminal cases, privileges are governed by common law under Federal Rule of Evidence 501. Attorney-client privilege generally protects confidential communications made for the purpose of obtaining or providing legal advice. The privilege may cover communications through agents who assist the lawyer in providing legal advice; United States v. Kovel, 296 F.2d 918 (2d Cir. 1961), is a leading agent-assistance case, and Restatement (Third) of the Law Governing Lawyers section 68 states the basic communication-in-confidence-for-legal-assistance frame. On the listed facts, only Stephen's reasonable belief that Paul was employed as an investigator for Stephen's lawyer gives a possible privilege argument. Authority: FRE 501, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_501; Restatement (Third) of the Law Governing Lawyers section 68; United States v. Kovel, 296 F.2d 918 (2d Cir. 1961).
