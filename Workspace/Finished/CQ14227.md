## 6. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

The statute imposes a severe and complete bar on Priscilla's ability to associate with voters in the general election as an independent or petition candidate after she participated in (and lost) her party's primary for the same office. This directly and severely burdens the First Amendment right of political association. When a state election regulation severely burdens a fundamental right such as political association, strict scrutiny applies. Under strict scrutiny the state—not the challenger—bears the burden of proving that the statute is narrowly tailored, i.e., the least restrictive means of achieving a compelling state interest.

## 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested black-letter rule is unchanged from the original: when a state statute severely burdens First Amendment political association rights in the candidate-ballot-access context (here, a categorical bar on a primary participant running in the general for the same office by petition or write-in), the regulation is subject to strict scrutiny. The state bears the burden of demonstrating that the statute is narrowly tailored (least restrictive means) to serve a compelling state interest.

Governing authorities (lawyer-confirmation layer only):
- First Amendment freedom of association protects the right of individuals to associate for the advancement of political beliefs and to cast votes effectively. See, e.g., *Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party*, 520 U.S. 351 (1997); *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724 (1974).
- Ballot-access restrictions that severely burden association or voting rights trigger strict scrutiny or the functional equivalent under the *Anderson-Burdick* balancing framework: the court weighs the character and magnitude of the burden against the state's asserted interests and requires narrow tailoring when the burden is severe. *Burdick v. Takushi*, 504 U.S. 428 (1992); *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780 (1983).
- In the specific "sore-loser" / post-primary disaffiliation context, the Supreme Court has upheld reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions that leave open alternative means of ballot access, but a complete bar with no reasonable alternative triggers the highest level of review and places the justification burden on the state. The credited outcome (state must prove least-restrictive-means / compelling interest) is identical to the source question.

No key disagreement with the official answer. Research confirms the MBE formulation of strict scrutiny + state burden for this severe-association burden is the tested rule.
