### 1. Final question

Daniel worked as a counselor at a private Christian horse-and-Scripture camp operated by Joyful Trails Camp, Inc. During several Saturday sessions, a young camper, Thomas, arrived with dark strap marks on his shoulders and legs. A statute in the jurisdiction requires youth-camp counselors to report to the police when there is probable cause to suspect child abuse and provides for immediate protective removal of suspected victims. Daniel did not know about the statute. He did tell Mary, the camp's program director, about the marks. Mary told him not to say anything outside the camp because the camp could be accused of falsely blaming a parent.

At Daniel's murder trial, the evidence showed that Peter had been beating Thomas for some time and that the earlier beatings caused the marks Daniel had seen. Thomas's mother, Ruth, knew about the beatings but had not stopped them because she feared Peter.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Daniel may have failed to do what the reporting statute required, but the murder charge asks whether he can be held responsible for Peter's killing. Gold Key: accomplice-style responsibility for another person's crime requires purposeful help or a culpable mental state tied to that crime; a duty breach alone does not make Daniel Peter's partner in murder. Silver Key: keep the call locked on murder, not on the reporting offense. Daniel warned Mary, did not want Peter to kill Thomas, and did not act with the purpose of helping Peter commit murder.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A. The causation answer is attractive because a report might have led to protective removal before the fatal beating. The breaker is that the best murder defense is not a broad claim that causation is impossible; the fatal beating could be treated as a foreseeable result of nonreporting, while Daniel still lacks the mental state needed to be responsible for Peter's murder.

B. The camp-blame answer is attractive because Mary was a supervisory agent and told Daniel to stay quiet. The breaker is that the statute places the reporting burden on the youth-camp counselor, and shifting blame to the camp does not answer whether Daniel is guilty of Peter's murder.

D. The ignorance answer is attractive because Daniel did not know about the reporting statute. The breaker is that this focuses on the wrong legal fight; whether ignorance helps on a reporting charge is incidental to whether Daniel purposely helped Peter commit murder.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule and outcome are unchanged. A person is not criminally responsible for another person's offense merely because he omitted a legally required act. For accomplice liability, the prosecution must connect the defendant's assistance or qualifying omission to the required culpability for the principal offense. Model Penal Code section 2.06(3)(a) frames accomplice liability around acting with the purpose of promoting or facilitating the offense; section 2.06(3)(a)(iii) includes failures to act when the actor has a legal duty to prevent the offense, and section 2.06(4) requires the culpability sufficient for a result offense. Here, Daniel's failure to report may matter to a reporting offense and may be argued as causal, but the record does not show that Daniel intended Peter's murder or purposely facilitated it. The supplied key therefore remains correct.

Research layer checked: Model Penal Code section 2.06 as reproduced in H2O Open Casebooks, and the District of Columbia Criminal Code Reform Commission discussion noting that omission-based accomplice liability requires both a legal duty and an intent to aid the offense.
