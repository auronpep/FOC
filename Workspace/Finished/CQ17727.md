## 1. Final Question

On May 2, Peter deeded his privately owned Selah Ridge cabin parcel to Mary and Martha as joint tenants with right of survivorship. On June 9, Peter purported to sell that same parcel to Daniel by general warranty deed. Daniel paid cash and did not know about the prior deed. Mary and Martha recorded their deed on June 12. Daniel recorded his deed on June 17. Mary died on June 20. The jurisdiction has a statute that provides, "In determining the priority of conflicting interests in land, the first such interest to have been recorded shall have priority." Who has priority on June 21?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A prior joint-tenancy deed is recorded before a later buyer's deed, and one joint tenant dies after both recordings; under a first-recorded-wins statute, who has priority?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

A. Martha, because Peter conveyed the cabin parcel to Mary and Martha before Daniel recorded his deed.

B. Daniel, because Peter conveyed the cabin parcel to him by general warranty deed.

C. Martha, because the deed to Mary and Martha was recorded before Daniel's deed was recorded.

D. Daniel, because Martha's survivorship interest did not ripen until after Daniel's deed had been recorded.

## 4. Correct Answer

C (original key: A).

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is the best answer. The statute tells you how to sort the fight: the first conflicting land interest recorded has priority. The deed to Mary and Martha was recorded on June 12. Daniel's deed was recorded on June 17. Martha claims through the earlier recorded deed, and Mary later dying does not move the priority date to June 20.

Gold Key: In a race-recording problem, a surviving joint tenant's priority is tied to the recorded deed that created the joint tenancy, not to the later death that leaves one survivor.

Silver Key: Rank the recorded instruments first, then attach the current holder second.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A sells the common "first conveyance" move. Martha is the right claimant, but the reason points to the conveyance date instead of the recording date. Under this statute, priority is not won because Peter deeded the parcel to Mary and Martha first; it is won because that deed was recorded first.

B sells the warranty-deed detour. A general warranty deed may matter in a claim between Daniel and Peter, because warranties address promises about title. It does not beat the recording statute in the priority fight between Daniel and Martha.

D sells the survivorship-ripening trap, the strongest wrong answer in this array. It treats Mary's death as if that later event created Martha's priority. Gold Key: Martha's claim traces back to the recorded deed that created the joint tenancy, so the June 12 recording controls even though Mary's death happened after Daniel recorded.

## 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. This is a Real Property recording-act item testing the difference between a race statute and other recording statutes. Under the stated statute, the first conflicting interest to be recorded has priority. Notice, deed type, and the date of the later survivorship event do not outrank the earlier recorded deed.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: the question's own statutory text is a pure race rule. A real statutory example is N.C. Gen. Stat. Section 47-18(a), which makes certain land instruments effective against purchasers from the time of registration and provides that registered instruments have priority based on the order of registration. Official source: https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByArticle/Chapter_47/Article_2.html. Cornell Wex also describes a race statute as one that gives priority to the party who records first, even with notice of an earlier unrecorded claim: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/race_statute.

Here, Mary and Martha's deed was recorded on June 12. Daniel's deed was recorded on June 17. Martha therefore has priority on June 21. The later death of Mary affects which joint tenant remains to assert the earlier recorded interest, but it does not create a new priority date. Daniel may have warranty claims against Peter, but the warranty deed does not defeat Martha's earlier recorded interest.
