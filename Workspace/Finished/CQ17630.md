### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

The court should award **only the damages caused by Daniel's removal of the Bible from Paul's land**. Daniel has a qualified privilege to enter Paul's land to retrieve a chattel that got there through no fault of his own, but the privilege protects only the entry — it does not protect damage caused by the entry and retrieval. The Bible's being blown onto the land is not itself a trespass (the gust of wind is not a volitional act by Daniel, and the hauling activity is not an abnormally dangerous activity, so Daniel has no liability for damage caused by the spill under any theory). The damage Paul can recover is the damage caused by Daniel's act of walking into the orchard and removing the Bible — the trampled saplings and broken branches. **Gold Key:** a qualified privilege to enter protects the entry but not the damage the entry itself causes. **Silver Key:** the test is whether each item of damage was caused by the *spill* (no recovery absent abnormally dangerous activity) or by the *removal* (recovery permitted because the entry is a volitional act).

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B. All damages resulting from the Bible's being blown onto Paul's land.**

This is the dominant trap. The student treats the spill as the entry and stacks all downstream damage onto the trucker. The breaker is the **Gold Key**: Daniel is not liable for damage caused by the spill because (i) the gust of wind was not his volitional act, and (ii) hauling Bibles is not an abnormally dangerous activity. Liability for damage to realty caused by participation in an abnormally dangerous activity is the only path to spill-caused damages; absent that, the spill is not actionable. The qualified privilege covers the *entry*, not the *spill*.

**C. Nominal damages only.**

This is the under-compensation half-truth. The student treats the qualified privilege as a complete shield and stops at nominal. The breaker is the **Silver Key**: the privilege protects the entry, not the damage the entry itself causes. Daniel is liable for the actual damage caused by his trampling the saplings and breaking the branches — that is the truer measure of trespass-to-land damages when the entry is privileged but the damage from the entry is not.

**D. No damages.**

This is the full-privilege misread. The student treats the qualified privilege as absolute. The breaker is the same as for C: a qualified privilege protects the entry but does not shield the actor from liability for damage caused by the entry itself. Daniel walked into the orchard, trampled saplings, and broke branches — that is actionable damage.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The supplied row tests QID 17630, a Torts item involving a trucker who enters a landowner's land to retrieve a chattel that got there through no fault of his own. The official key is C: only the damages caused by the trucker's removal of the gravel from the landowner's land.

For lawyer confirmation: the qualified privilege to enter another's land to retrieve a chattel that came onto the land through no fault of the actor is a well-established common-law rule. The Restatement (Second) of Torts § 77 addresses the privilege to enter land in possession of another, and § 79 addresses the privilege to enter for the purpose of removing a chattel. The privilege is qualified, not absolute: it protects the entry but does not shield the actor from liability for damage caused by the entry itself. The actor must compensate the landowner for actual damage that results from the entry and removal.

The damage caused by the spill itself is not recoverable against the trucker absent an abnormally dangerous activity, because the spill was not the trucker's volitional act. Restatement (Second) of Torts § 427 et seq. and § 519 address abnormally dangerous activities and the strict-liability rules that apply; absent such an activity, the actor is not strictly liable for damage caused by the unintended escape of the chattel. Negligence would require fault, and the trucker had no fault. So spill-caused damage is not recoverable.

The transformed outcome is identical to the original: the landowner recovers only the damages caused by the trucker's removal of the chattel from the landowner's land — here, the trampled saplings and broken branches. Spill-caused damage is not recoverable; nominal or no damages would understate the trespass-to-land recovery for damage caused by the privileged entry itself.

## program_frame

This is a Torts trespass-to-land and damages-measure question. The call asks which measure of damages the court should award when a chattel lands on a landowner's land through no fault of the actor, the actor enters to retrieve, and the entry causes damage. The right answer tracks the qualified-privilege rule: the privilege protects the entry only; the actor is liable for actual damage caused by the entry and removal.

## student_access_test

## distilled_core_question

A box falls off a vehicle onto a landowner's property through no fault of the driver; the driver enters the land to retrieve it and damages crops in doing so. The hauling activity is not abnormally dangerous. What damages can the landowner recover for trespass to land?

## call_and_prediction

Prediction: spill-caused damage is not recoverable absent an abnormally dangerous activity; removal-caused damage is recoverable because the entry is a volitional act; nominal or no damages would understate the recovery for actual damage caused by the entry.
