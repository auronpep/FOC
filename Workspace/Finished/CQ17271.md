## 0. Source Question (Original Q17271)

**Stem:** An attorney decided to invest $4,000 in art for her office. On November 1, she decided to purchase a painting from the defendant, with delivery to be made on December 28 after framing. On November 15, the attorney learned the defendant had accidentally sold the painting to another buyer. The attorney then gave another dealer $4,000 for a different painting. On November 30, the defendant got the painting back. On December 28, the defendant arrived with the framed painting; the attorney refused it. The attorney sued for specific performance on December 29.

**Call:** With respect to the attorney's demand for specific performance, a court is most likely to find for

- **A:** the defendant, since the attorney refused to accept delivery of the painting on December 28.
- **B:** the defendant, since the attorney has already succeeded in investing $4,000 in art for her office.
- **C:** the attorney, since the painting is unique.
- **D:** the attorney, since the defendant's sale to the other person was an anticipatory repudiation.

**Correct Answer:** A

## 1. Black-Letter Rule Tested

When a seller tenders conforming performance and the buyer refuses acceptance, the buyer waives the right to specific performance — even if the seller committed an anticipatory repudiation that the buyer initially relied on. The buyer's refusal of tender discharges the seller from further obligation.

**Invariant layer:**
- Rule: Refusal of tender waives specific performance rights
- Issue: Effect of buyer's refusal to accept conforming goods on specific performance remedy
- Credited outcome: Defendant/seller wins (buyer loses specific performance)
- Dominant trap: D — the sale to another buyer was an anticipatory repudiation (attractive because it IS a correct statement of law, but it doesn't overcome the waiver-by-refusal rule)
- Array geometry: 2-vs-2 split (two for defendant, two for plaintiff)
- Call function: Who wins on the specific performance claim

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**Gold Key:** A buyer who refuses a seller's tender of conforming performance waives the right to specific performance, even when the seller previously committed an anticipatory repudiation. The buyer's refusal of tender discharges the seller from any further obligation under the contract.

**B is correct.** Barnabas tendered conforming performance on December 15 — the exact harp, strung and tuned, on the contract date. Timothy's refusal to accept it waived whatever specific performance rights he otherwise had. This is true even though Barnabas's sale of the harp to the concert venue on November 5 constituted an anticipatory repudiation by conduct (a voluntary act rendering Barnabas apparently unable to perform). Timothy was initially justified in relying on that repudiation by commissioning a substitute harp. But when Barnabas recovered the harp and tendered it on the contract date, Timothy could no longer refuse and simultaneously demand specific performance. His refusal discharged Barnabas.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is incorrect** because while the custom-built harp's uniqueness would normally support specific performance, Timothy's refusal to accept Barnabas's tender waived that right. Uniqueness is a necessary precondition for specific performance but not sufficient when the buyer has refused conforming tender. **Gold Key:** The waiver-by-refusal rule defeats specific performance even for unique goods.

**C is incorrect** because although Barnabas's sale of the harp to the concert venue was indeed an anticipatory repudiation by conduct (a voluntary act making him apparently unable to perform), Timothy's subsequent refusal to accept Barnabas's tender of the same harp waived any specific performance rights arising from that repudiation. The anticipatory repudiation gave Timothy the right to rely (which he did by commissioning a substitute), but it did not entitle him to refuse conforming tender and still demand specific performance. **Gold Key:** Anticipatory repudiation does not override the waiver-by-refusal rule.

**D is incorrect** because Timothy's success in obtaining a substitute harp is legally irrelevant to whether Barnabas breached the contract. The seller's obligation to deliver conforming goods is not excused by the buyer's independent decision to obtain a substitute. This answer mistakes a practical outcome for a legal defense. Timothy's substitute purchase was a reasonable response to the anticipatory repudiation, but it doesn't provide Barnabas a defense — Timothy's refusal of tender does.

## 9. Black-Letter Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing Rule:** Under the Restatement (Second) of Contracts:
- **§ 250(b):** A repudiation includes "a voluntary affirmative act which renders the obligor unable or apparently unable to perform without such a breach." Barnabas's sale of the specific harp to the concert venue constitutes anticipatory repudiation by conduct.
- **§ 253(2):** One party's repudiation discharges the other party's remaining duties to render performance. Timothy was therefore excused from further performance (e.g., payment) and was entitled to rely by commissioning a substitute.
- **§ 256(2):** The effect of events constituting a repudiation is nullified if, to the knowledge of the injured party, those events have ceased to exist before the injured party materially changes position. When Barnabas recovered the harp (November 20) and tendered it (December 15), the basis for the repudiation ceased to exist. Timothy knew the harp was recovered.
- **Specific performance waiver:** A party seeking specific performance must be ready, willing, and able to perform. Refusal of the seller's tender of conforming goods demonstrates the buyer is not ready and willing to perform, waiving the right to specific performance. *See* Di Millo v. 2099232 Ontario Inc., 2018 ONCA 1051 (innocent party need not tender when other party repudiated, but once the repudiating party retracts and tenders, the buyer cannot refuse and still demand specific performance).

**Authority:** Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§ 250, 253, 256 (American Law Institute, 1981).

### 2. The 17-Section Student Case Study

#### Program Frame

This item tests the intersection of two Contracts doctrines: (1) anticipatory repudiation by conduct and (2) waiver of specific performance by refusal of conforming tender. The student must recognize that while the seller's sale to a third party IS an anticipatory repudiation, the buyer's subsequent refusal of the seller's tender of the exact goods waives the buyer's right to specific performance. This is a CLASH item — two choices survive the CUT phase and fight on the axis of whether the anticipatory repudiation or the refusal of tender controls the remedy.

#### Student Access Test

**Controlling test:** Could a smart 10-year-old who completed only the C3 lessons identify each choice's problem WITHOUT outside legal doctrine?

- **A (uniqueness):** Partially accessible. Uniqueness is a visible concept, but the waiver-by-refliction rule requires a Gold Key. → `anchor_assisted`
- **B (refusal of tender):** Accessible via Gold Key. The refusal is a visible act; the legal consequence is taught. → `anchor_assisted`
- **C (anticipatory repudiation):** Partially accessible. The sale to a third party is visibly problematic, but the student needs the Gold Key to know the waiver overrides. → `anchor_assisted`
- **D (substitute purchase):** Fully accessible. The substitute purchase is visibly irrelevant to the specific performance question. → `hard_structural`

#### Question Data

**Stem:** Timothy is the music director for a regional Christian music festival. In October, he contracted to buy a custom-built folk harp from Barnabas, a luthier, for $5,000, with delivery to be made on December 15 after the harp was strung and tuned. On November 5, Timothy learned that Barnabas had accidentally sold the completed harp to a concert venue. Based on this knowledge, Timothy paid another luthier $5,000 to build him a different harp. On November 20, Barnabas discovered the mistake and recovered the harp from the concert venue. On December 15, when Barnabas arrived at Timothy's home with the strung and tuned harp, Timothy refused to accept it. Timothy filed an action against Barnabas on December 16 seeking specific performance of the October agreement.

**Call:** With respect to Timothy's demand for specific performance, a court is most likely to find for

**Choices:**
- A: Timothy, since the harp is a custom-built instrument and therefore unique.
- B: Barnabas, since Timothy refused to accept delivery of the harp on December 15.
- C: Timothy, since Barnabas's sale to the concert venue was an anticipatory repudiation of the contract.
- D: Barnabas, since Timothy already succeeded in obtaining a harp for the festival.

#### Distilled Core Question

Buyer contracts for custom goods. Seller sells them to someone else (anticipatory repudiation). Buyer buys substitute. Seller recovers the goods and tenders them on the contract date. Buyer refuses. Can buyer get specific performance?

#### Call and Prediction

**Call type:** "Most likely to find for" — asks who wins on the specific performance claim.

**Prediction before looking at choices:** The buyer refused conforming tender. That should waive specific performance. The seller wins.

#### Choice-by-Choice Walkthrough

**Choice A: "Timothy, since the harp is a custom-built instrument and therefore unique."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The choice states a true fact — a custom-built harp IS unique, and uniqueness IS a basis for specific performance. But the choice ignores the most important fact in the stem: Timothy refused the harp when Barnabas brought it. A true precondition (uniqueness) doesn't help if the buyer refused the goods.
2. **Student label:** "Uniqueness is real but incomplete — ignores the waiver."
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** "Timothy, since the harp is unique AND Timothy has not waived his right to specific performance by refusing conforming tender."
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Specific performance requires (a) a valid contract, (b) uniqueness or inadequacy of damages, and (c) that the plaintiff is ready, willing, and able to perform. Timothy's refusal of tender demonstrates he is not ready and willing. The uniqueness of the harp is necessary but not sufficient. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 253.

**Choice B: "Barnabas, since Timothy refused to accept delivery of the harp on December 15."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** Timothy refused to accept the exact harp, on the exact contract date, strung and tuned. This is the dispositive fact. **Gold Key:** A buyer who refuses the seller's tender of conforming performance waives the right to specific performance, even when the seller previously committed an anticipatory repudiation.
2. **Student label:** "True and responsive — refusal of tender waives specific performance."
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** This choice IS true and responsive as stated.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** A buyer who refuses conforming tender waives specific performance. This is true even though Barnabas's earlier sale constituted an anticipatory repudiation. The repudiation gave Timothy the right to rely (which he did), but it did not entitle him to refuse conforming tender and still demand specific performance. Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§ 253, 256.

**Choice C: "Timothy, since Barnabas's sale to the concert venue was an anticipatory repudiation of the contract."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** Barnabas DID sell the harp to someone else — that IS an anticipatory repudiation by conduct. But the choice ignores what happened next: Barnabas got the harp back and tendered it. **Gold Key:** Anticipatory repudiation does not override the waiver-by-refusal rule. The repudiation was real, but Timothy's refusal of conforming tender waived his specific performance right.
2. **Student label:** "Anticipatory repudiation is real but doesn't overcome waiver."
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** "Timothy, since Barnabas's sale was an anticipatory repudiation AND Timothy did not subsequently waive his right to specific performance by refusing conforming tender."
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Barnabas's sale to the venue was anticipatory repudiation by conduct under R2C § 250(b). However, under R2C § 256(2), the effect of the repudiation was nullified when the basis for it ceased to exist (Barnabas recovered the harp) before Timothy materially changed his position. Timothy's refusal of tender then waived specific performance. The repudiation does not control the remedy.

**Choice D: "Barnabas, since Timothy already succeeded in obtaining a harp for the festival."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** Timothy DID get a substitute harp. But so what? The question is about specific performance of the Barnabas contract, not about whether Timothy found a workaround. The substitute purchase is legally irrelevant to whether Barnabas breached or whether Timothy can get specific performance.
2. **Student label:** "Legally irrelevant — substitute purchase doesn't excuse seller."
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** This choice cannot be made true and responsive to the call. The substitute purchase is a misfit — it answers "Is Timothy made whole?" not "Who wins on specific performance?"
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** The buyer's independent substitute transaction is not a defense to the seller's breach. It may be relevant to damages (mitigation), but it has no bearing on the availability of specific performance.

#### Residual Answer

**B** is the only choice that is both true and responsive. It states the correct party (Barnabas) and the correct legal reason (Timothy refused conforming tender, waiving specific performance).
