## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam-Register)

**Gold Key:** A life tenant must pay all ordinary carrying costs—property taxes, mortgage interest, insurance, and routine repairs. The remainderman must pay mortgage principal and capital improvements because these build equity in the property the remainderman will eventually own outright.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice B** sells the idea that the ultimate owner should bear all financial burdens. It is attractive to students who reason that Paul, as the remainderman, has the greater stake in the property's long-term value. The breaker: taxes are a cost of current possession, not future ownership. Paul has no right to possess or use the property during Esther's lifetime, so he does not bear the carrying costs associated with that use.

**Choice C** sells the trap of a memorized split applied backwards. It is attractive to students who remember that obligations are divided between the life tenant and remainderman but confuse which party pays which expense. The breaker: current costs follow the present possessor, and capital costs follow the future owner. Esther must pay taxes and interest; Paul must pay principal.

**Choice D** sells the intuitive but overbroad notion that the person in possession must pay all bills. It is attractive to students who reason that Esther lives on the property and runs the stable, so she should cover every expense. The breaker: principal payments build equity for the remainderman. Esther's interest ends at her death; she gains no benefit from reducing the mortgage balance.
