The legal substance is unchanged from the original: a malpractice plaintiff must prove breach **and** causation; proof that accepted practice required a specialist consult does not establish liability without evidence that the missed consult caused the injury. **TOR-REM-CAUSATION-LINK-01 — Breach is not causation**

**Signal:** The stem proves what the doctor should have done but not what would have happened if the doctor had done it.
**Student move:** Ask what evidence links this specific missed step to this specific injury.
**Tiny rule:** No link, no negligence win.
**Trap:** Choosing breach-only or procedure-caused-harm answers.
**Confidence:** ANCHOR_ASSISTED.

### Gold Key(s)

**GK-TORTS-MEDMAL-CAUSATION-01**
In malpractice, proof that the doctor broke a professional rule is not enough; the plaintiff must prove that the broken rule caused the injury.

**GK-TORTS-LIFE-SUPPORT-01**
When the alleged tort creates the condition requiring life support, later withdrawal of support normally does not cut off causation.

### Silver Key(s)

**SK-TORTS-CAUSATION-LINK-01**
Track the alleged breach, not the whole medical timeline. The question is not procedure-to-death; it is missed-consult-to-injury.
