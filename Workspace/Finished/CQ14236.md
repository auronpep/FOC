## 6. Right-Answer Explanation

**Gold Key:** Under Employment Division v. ## 8. Black-Letter Legal Verification

**Governing Rule — Free Exercise Clause, First Amendment:**

The Free Exercise Clause prohibits the government from targeting or suppressing religious practice. However, under Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872 (1990), a neutral law of general applicability does not violate the Free Exercise Clause merely because it incidentally burdens religious conduct. The government is not required to justify every burden on religion by showing a compelling interest.

To establish a Free Exercise violation, the challenger must demonstrate that the government action was motivated by a desire to interfere with or suppress religion — i.e., that the law or action is not neutral and generally applicable but instead targets religious conduct because it is religious. See Smith, 494 U.S. at 877-79; Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993) (ordinance targeting Santeria animal sacrifice was not neutral or generally applicable).

Once the challenger shows that the government action targets religion, strict scrutiny applies: the government must show the action is narrowly tailored to serve a compelling interest. In practice, government actions that intentionally target religion almost never survive strict scrutiny.

**Application to this question:** The Forest Service concedes sincerity and adverse impact. Under Smith, that is not enough. The community must show the permit was *aimed at* suppressing their worship — that the government acted with anti-religious intent. Choice C correctly states this requirement.

**Doctrinal currency check:** Kennedy v. Bremerton School District, 597 U.S. ___ (2022), and Fulton v. City of Philadelphia, 593 U.S. 522 (2021), have refined Free Exercise doctrine in specific contexts (government employee prayer, individualized exemptions), but neither overruled Smith's core holding that neutral, generally applicable laws do not trigger strict scrutiny. The question's fact pattern — a general land-use permit on federal public land — falls squarely within Smith's framework. The credited answer (intent to target) remains correct.

**Authority:** U.S. Const. amend. I; Employment Div. v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872 (1990); Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993).
