## Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The credited answer (C / original B) is correct under the governing rule. Fed. R. Civ. P. 45(c)(1) states:

"(1) For a Trial, Hearing, or Deposition. A subpoena may command a person to attend a trial, hearing, or deposition only as follows:
(A) within 100 miles of where the person resides, is employed, or regularly transacts business in person; or
(B) within the state where the person resides, is employed, or regularly transacts business in person, if the person
(i) is a party or a party's officer; or
(ii) is commanded to attend a trial and would not incur substantial expense."

The rule was consolidated and clarified in the 2013 amendments (effective Dec. 1, 2013), but the core 100-mile limit for non-party deposition attendance has been stable in substance. The official key is confirmed: because Paul (non-party) lives 500 miles away, a subpoena cannot compel him to attend a deposition noticed for State A. The attorney remains free to take the deposition in State B (by traveling there or using other compliant means), but cannot force Paul to travel to State A.
