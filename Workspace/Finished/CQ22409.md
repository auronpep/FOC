## 2. The 15-section student case study

### 1. program_frame

This is a **Criminal Law homicide grading** item. The answer choices are not four unrelated propositions. They are a charge ladder:

**Murder → voluntary manslaughter → involuntary manslaughter → no crime.**

The call asks for the **most serious** crime. That word matters. The task is not “what crime is possible?” It is “what is the highest supported grade?”

### 2. student_access_test

This item is **anchor-assisted**.

A student can see the ladder structure from the choices. A student can also see the extreme danger facts in the stem. But the final move requires the homicide grading anchor: murder can be based on depraved-heart recklessness, not only intent to kill. The C3 Master Deck’s homicide anchor states that murder includes depraved-heart recklessness, voluntary manslaughter is heat of passion or imperfect self-defense, and involuntary manslaughter is criminal negligence or misdemeanor manslaughter.

### 7. choice_by_choice_walkthrough

#### A. Murder.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This is the top of the homicide ladder and matches the extreme-risk facts.
2. **Student label:** Depraved-heart survivor.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** This choice already is the true-and-responsive version. It answers the “most serious” call.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Murder can be supported by malice aforethought; implied malice includes deaths from conduct displaying depraved indifference to human life. The federal statutory analog defines murder as unlawful killing with malice aforethought, and Wex explains that implied malice includes depraved-heart murder. ([Justia Law][1])

#### B. Voluntary manslaughter.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This is a real homicide category, but the stem gives no provocation, sudden quarrel, heat-of-passion, or imperfect-self-defense fact.
2. **Student label:** Provocation-frame neighbor.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** It would need facts showing adequate provocation, heat of passion, or imperfect self-defense.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Manslaughter is killing without malice; federal manslaughter text divides voluntary manslaughter as killing upon a sudden quarrel or heat of passion. Cornell’s Wex similarly describes voluntary manslaughter as heat-of-passion or adequate-provocation killing. ([Legal Information Institute][2])

#### C. Involuntary manslaughter.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This is the dominant trap. It is attractive because there is no intent to kill. But the call asks for the **most serious** crime, and the risk level is extreme.
2. **Student label:** Lesser-grade trap.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** It would fit if the facts showed criminal negligence or a lower reckless risk, but not depraved indifference to human life.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Federal manslaughter text treats involuntary manslaughter as an unlawful act not amounting to a felony, or a lawful act done without due caution and circumspection, causing death. That is a lower-grade homicide than murder when malice-level recklessness is present. ([Legal Information Institute][2])

#### D. No crime.

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** This denies criminal liability after a death caused by the woman’s dangerous conduct.
2. **Student label:** No-crime denial.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** It would need facts breaking causation, culpability, or criminal liability entirely.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** A homicide offense is supported when culpable conduct causes death. Here, the official explanation and the homicide grading authorities support criminal liability at the murder level. ([Legal Information Institute][3])
