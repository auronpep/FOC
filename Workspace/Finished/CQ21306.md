# Pass-1 Transform Report - Q21306 (Criminal Law and Procedure - Eighth Amendment Capital Punishment)

## 1. Final Question

A defendant, Paul, is convicted under a state statute of the capital sexual assault of a nine-year-old child who was attending a private Christian summer program. The offense caused grave physical and psychological injury to the child. The jury, applying the state's capital sentencing statute and weighing all aggravating and mitigating circumstances, sentences Paul to death. The aggravating circumstances found by the jury include the child's very young age, the seriousness of the injuries, and Paul's prior conviction for a sex offense. The child did not die.

Paul challenges the death sentence as cruel and unusual punishment under the Eighth Amendment. Is Paul's challenge correct?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

May a state impose the death penalty for a non-homicide sexual assault of a child when the victim survives and the jury found serious aggravating circumstances?

## 3. Final Answer Choices (Post-Shuffle)

**B.** Yes, because the Eighth Amendment categorically bars the death penalty for non-homicide crimes against individual persons, even when the crime is sexual assault of a child and causes severe harm.

**C.** Yes, but only because using the prior sex-offense conviction as an aggravating factor violates Apprendi and requires the death sentence to be vacated.

**D.** No, because the jury made an individualized sentencing determination after weighing all aggravating and mitigating circumstances.

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**B is correct.** The Eighth Amendment forbids the death penalty for a non-homicide crime against an individual person when the victim did not die and death was not intended. That categorical rule controls even when the offense is sexual assault of a child, the harm is severe, the victim is very young, the defendant has a prior sex-offense conviction, and the jury carefully weighed aggravating and mitigating circumstances.

**Gold Key:** Under *Kennedy v. Louisiana*, the death penalty is categorically unavailable for non-homicide crimes against individual persons, including child rape or sexual assault, unless the victim's death resulted or was intended. Individualized sentencing and aggravating factors do not reopen a category the Eighth Amendment closes.

**Silver Key:** Lock the call to death eligibility before weighing aggravators. If the victim survived and the crime is against an individual person, the categorical bar decides the item before the severity facts can compete.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### Choice A - DOMINANT TRAP

**Why it is attractive:** The facts are morally and emotionally severe: a very young child, grave injuries, a prior sex-offense conviction, and a legislature that classified the crime as capital. A student may think proportionality means the worse the facts are, the more likely the death sentence survives.

**The breaker:** The Eighth Amendment rule in this lane is categorical. For non-homicide crimes against individual persons, *Kennedy* bars death even when aggravating facts are powerful. **Gold Key:** severity, youth, and legislative authorization cannot convert a non-homicide offense against an individual into a death-eligible category.

### Choice C

**Why it is attractive:** The answer uses a familiar sentencing phrase, Apprendi, and points at the prior conviction, which appears in the aggravating-circumstances list. It feels like a modern jury-finding trap.

**The breaker:** The sentence falls because the category of crime is not death-eligible under the Eighth Amendment, not because of an Apprendi defect. Apprendi and later cases, including *Erlinger*, protect jury findings for facts that increase punishment, while preserving a narrow prior-conviction exception and requiring jury findings for additional recidivism facts beyond the fact of conviction. Here, the jury found the aggravators anyway. **Silver Key:** do not let a neighboring sentencing doctrine distract from the call; the challenge asks whether death is cruel and unusual for this non-homicide offense.

### Choice D

**Why it is attractive:** Capital sentencing normally requires individualized consideration of aggravating and mitigating circumstances, and the stem says the jury did exactly that. A student may treat correct procedure as enough.

**The breaker:** Individualized sentencing is necessary in death cases, but it is not sufficient. A sentencing procedure cannot authorize death for a category of crime that the Eighth Amendment excludes. **Gold Key:** first ask whether the offense is death-eligible at all; only then do aggravation and mitigation matter.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule:** The Eighth Amendment, applied to the states through the Fourteenth Amendment, prohibits cruel and unusual punishments. In *Kennedy v. Louisiana*, 554 U.S. 407 (2008), the Supreme Court held that the death penalty is disproportionate for the rape of a child where the crime did not result in, and was not intended to result in, the victim's death. The Court limited its concern to crimes against individual persons and did not decide crimes against the state such as treason, espionage, terrorism, or drug-kingpin activity.

**Authority floor:** U.S. Const. amend. VIII; *Kennedy v. Louisiana*, 554 U.S. 407 (2008), available at https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep554/usrep554407/usrep554407.pdf. The Supreme Court's official opinion states that its concern was limited to crimes against individual persons and that non-homicide crimes against individual persons, even child rape, cannot be compared to murder in severity and irrevocability. For the Apprendi-adjacent distractor, *Apprendi v. New Jersey*, 530 U.S. 466 (2000), and *Erlinger v. United States*, 602 U.S. ___ (2024), available at https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-370_i4dj.pdf, confirm that facts increasing punishment generally require jury finding, while the prior-conviction exception remains narrow and does not decide this Eighth Amendment categorical-bar item.
