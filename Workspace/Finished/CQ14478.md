## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question
Paul contracted in a signed writing to sell Lydia's private Christian retreat-supply business 1,200 cedar fish ornaments each week for eight weeks, the ornaments to be delivered to Lydia's warehouse on Thursdays and payment to be made on Mondays of each week. Lydia's business packed all of its weekly Scripture craft kits on Fridays. On Thursday morning of the first week, Paul tendered delivery of 960 ornaments to Lydia, and Lydia accepted them on Paul's assurance that the remaining 240 ornaments would be delivered later that evening, which they were. Lydia paid for both deliveries on Monday. On Thursday of the second week, Paul tendered delivery of 600 ornaments to Lydia and said that the remaining 600 ornaments could not be delivered on Thursday but would be delivered by Saturday. Lydia rejected the tender. Was Lydia legally justified in rejecting the tender of the 600 ornaments?

## 2. Distilled Core Question
In an installment sales contract, may a buyer reject a half shipment when the seller cannot deliver the rest until after the buyer's known production day for that installment?

C. No, because the tender did not substantially impair the value of the entire contract, and Paul had assured Lydia that he would cure the shortage.

D. Yes, because Lydia was legally entitled to reject any tender that did not conform perfectly to the contract.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
A is correct. This is not a one-shot sale where ordinary perfect tender alone decides the buyer's rejection right. The writing requires separate weekly deliveries, so it is an installment contract. Gold Key: in an Article 2 installment contract, the buyer may reject a nonconforming installment only when the defect substantially impairs that installment and cannot be cured in time. Silver Key: once the stem gives repeated separate deliveries, sort the choices by the standard they use before debating the cure. Here, Paul delivered only half of the Thursday installment, and the missing half would not arrive until Saturday, after Lydia's Friday kit-packing day. That late cure would not repair the value of that week's installment, so Lydia was legally justified in rejecting the tender.

The breaker is that accepting a timely cure of one nonconforming installment does not fabricate a standing waiver of future conforming installments. ## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer
The tested issue remains Contracts, Performance, Breach, and Discharge, Perfect Tender Rule, with verified outline code `56050402`. This is an Article 2 sale of goods. UCC 2-601 states the ordinary perfect-tender rule, but it is expressly subject to the Article 2 rule for installment contracts. UCC 2-612(1) defines an installment contract as one requiring or authorizing delivery in separate lots to be separately accepted. UCC 2-612(2) provides that the buyer may reject a nonconforming installment if the nonconformity substantially impairs the value of that installment and cannot be cured; if the seller gives adequate assurance of cure and the standard is not met, the buyer must accept. UCC 2-508 confirms that cure turns on timely conforming delivery within the contract time or, in proper surprise-rejection cases, within a further reasonable time.

The authorities checked were UCC 2-612, UCC 2-601, and UCC 2-508 at Cornell LII: https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-612, https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-601, and https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-508. Those authorities confirm the supplied key. The contract requires separate weekly deliveries; Paul tendered only half of the second weekly installment; and the promised remaining half would arrive after Lydia's known Friday production need. The late promised cure would not cure the value impairment for that week's installment, so the credited outcome is unchanged.

## review_truth
In an installment sale of goods, a buyer may reject a nonconforming installment when the defect substantially impairs that installment and the promised cure will arrive too late to cure that impairment.
