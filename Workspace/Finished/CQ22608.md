## 1. Final Question

A public high school in State X hired Daniel, a committed Christian, to teach history under a one-year contract. Before hiring, Daniel had been an active dues-paying member of a Christian teachers' league that, eight years earlier, had organized a campaign of civil disobedience in which members deliberately destroyed several abandoned government-owned buildings scheduled for demolition. ## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**Gold Key: A bill of attainder is a legislative act that singles out a named or ascertainable group and imposes punishment on them without the benefit of a judicial trial.** Article I, Section 9, Clause 3 prohibits Congress from passing bills of attainder; Article I, Section 10, Clause 1 extends the prohibition to the states. The Supreme Court has held that a law need not impose a death sentence to qualify — any disability imposed on a specific group for past conduct, without trial, counts as punishment. *See* Nixon v. Administrator of General Services, 433 U.S. 425 (1977); Cummings v. Missouri, 71 U.S. 277 (1866); Ex parte Garland, 71 U.S. 333 (1866).

Here, the statute does not set a neutral qualification that anyone might fail to meet (like a residency requirement or a bar exam). It names the Christian teachers' league specifically and bars all its members from publicly funded employment because of their past association. That is a textbook bill of attainder: a named group, a punitive disability (exclusion from public employment), no trial. The court will strike it down.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### Choice A (original B) — "Rational qualifications for publicly funded employees"

**What makes it attractive:** Students know that states have broad power to set qualifications for their employees and officials. The idea that a legislature can decide who is fit for public employment feels intuitively correct, and many students will stop here without asking whether this particular law is a neutral qualification or something more targeted.

**The breaker:** The rational-qualifications frame is the wrong doctrinal lens. This statute doesn't set a generally applicable standard — it names a specific organization and punishes its members. When a law singles out an identifiable group for a disability based on past conduct, the Bill of Attainder Clause, not rational-basis review, governs the analysis. *Silver Key: Before choosing a "rational basis" or "state authority" answer, check whether the law targets a specific named group — if it does, the attainder analysis likely applies.*

### Choice B (original A) — "Formal designation as a subversive organization"

**What makes it attractive:** The legislature formally called the group "subversive." Students may reason that if the legislature went through the process of officially designating the group, that designation must carry legal weight — perhaps even enough to justify the ban. This is the dominant trap because it mirrors the surface logic of the statute itself.

**The breaker:** The legislature's label doesn't change the constitutional analysis. No matter what the group is called, a law that punishes identifiable members without a trial is a bill of attainder. The formal designation is precisely what makes the law an attainder — it names the group — not what saves it. **Gold Key: A bill of attainder is a legislative act that singles out a named or ascertainable group and imposes punishment on them without the benefit of a judicial trial.** The official label is the problem, not the solution.

### Choice C (original D) — "No rational basis for excluding former members"

**What makes it attractive:** Students who sense something is wrong with the statute may reach for rational-basis language because it's a familiar constitutional tool. The conclusion ("the statute is unconstitutional") is correct, but the reasoning is wrong — and on the MBE, wrong reasoning with a right conclusion is still a wrong answer.

**The breaker:** The statute isn't analyzed under rational-basis review at all. The correct framework is the Bill of Attainder Clause. Rational-basis analysis applies to equal-protection challenges to general economic/social legislation. When a legislature names a specific group and bars them from employment, the attainder prohibition controls. Choosing this answer confuses two entirely different constitutional doctrines. *Silver Key: If the law targets a specific named group, think "bill of attainder," not "rational basis."*

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule:** The Bill of Attainder Clause (U.S. Const. art. I, § 9, cl. 3; art. I, § 10, cl. 1) prohibits both Congress and state legislatures from passing laws that single out a named or ascertainable group and impose punishment on them without a judicial trial.

**Authority:**
- U.S. Const. art. I, § 9, cl. 3 ("No Bill of Attainder or ex post facto Law shall be passed.")
- U.S. Const. art. I, § 10, cl. 1 ("No State shall ... pass any Bill of Attainder...")
- Nixon v. Administrator of General Services, 433 U.S. 425 (1977) (three-part test for punishment: historical, functional, motivational)
- Cummings v. Missouri, 71 U.S. 277 (1866) (state constitutional provision barring clergy who couldn't swear loyalty oath from ministry held unconstitutional bill of attainder)
- Ex parte Garland, 71 U.S. 333 (1866) (federal statute requiring loyalty oath for attorneys held unconstitutional bill of attainder)
- American Communications Ass'n v. Douds, 339 U.S. 382 (1950) (attainder requires punishment, not merely burden)
- Constitution Annotated, ArtI.S9.C3.2 (Congress.gov)

**Application:** The statute names the Christian teachers' league — an ascertainable group. It imposes a disability (bar from all publicly funded employment) based on past membership and conduct. No trial is afforded. Under Nixon's functional test, exclusion from employment constitutes punishment. The statute is a bill of attainder.

**Confirmed:** The tested rule (Bill of Attainder Clause), legal issue (whether a law targeting a specific group for a punitive disability is an attainder), and credited outcome (unconstitutional) are unchanged from the original.

#### Question Data

**Stem:** A public high school teacher, Daniel, was a dues-paying member of a Christian teachers' league that destroyed abandoned government buildings eight years ago. The state legislature designated the league as subversive and passed a statute barring all members from publicly funded employment. The league disbanded, but the statute was never repealed. Daniel's school board election opponent discovered his past membership and petitioned the state to remove him from the ballot. The state agreed. Daniel sued, arguing the statute is unconstitutional.

**Call:** Is the court likely to find the statute constitutional?

**Choices:**
- A: Yes, rational qualifications for public employees.
- B: Yes, formal subversive designation.
- C: No, no rational basis.
- D: No, bill of attainder.

#### Distilled Core Question

A state law names a specific group and bars all its members from publicly funded employment based on past membership. Is this a bill of attainder?

#### Call and Prediction

The call asks whether the statute is constitutional. Before looking at the choices, predict: the law targets a specific named group, imposes a disability (bar from employment) based on past conduct, without a trial. That's a bill of attainer. The statute is unconstitutional. Look for the answer that says "bill of attainder."

#### Trigger Facts

1. **State X legislature formally designated the league as subversive** → This is the naming that makes it an attainder.
2. **Statute bars any present or former member from any publicly funded position** → This is the punitive disability.
3. **Daniel was a dues-paying member** → He's within the named group.
4. **League disbanded; statute never repealed** → The law still applies to past members.
5. **No judicial proceeding before Daniel was barred** → No trial — a required element of attainder.

#### Choice-by-Choice Walkthrough

**Choice A: "Yes, because the state legislature has broad authority to set rational qualifications for publicly funded employees."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The law doesn't look like a neutral qualification (like a degree requirement or a residency rule). It names a specific organization. Neutral qualifications apply to everyone equally.
2. **Student label:** Wrong — this isn't a neutral qualification.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** If the statute said "all public employees must pass a background check" or "must hold a teaching certificate," that would be a rational qualification. This statute doesn't do that.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Rational-basis review applies to generally applicable economic/social legislation. A law targeting a named group for punitive disability is governed by the Bill of Attainder Clause, not the Equal Protection Clause.

**Choice B: "Yes, because the state legislature formally designated the league as a subversive organization."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The legislature named the group. That's exactly what a bill of attainder does — it singles out a specific group. The naming is the problem, not the solution.
2. **Student label:** Wrong — the official designation is what makes this an attainder.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** If the statute didn't name any group and instead set a generally applicable rule, the designation wouldn't matter.
