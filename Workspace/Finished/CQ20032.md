## 1. Final Question

After repeated nighttime rockslides on federally owned cliffs, the National Heritage Service adopts a rule closing Cana Canyon Reserve from 9 p.m. to 5 a.m. The reserve lies entirely within the state. Although the United States owns the tract, holders of state stargazing permits may use the reserve's overlooks; Lydia, Peter, and Mary had often bought those permits for the state's evening watch program. After the federal closing, fewer people purchase the state permits, and state revenue drops. The state Department of Outdoor Licensing brings a proceeding in federal court challenging the regulation's constitutionality.

Which of the following would provide the National Heritage Service with its strongest argument in support of the regulation's constitutionality?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A federal agency limits public access to federally owned land, and a state objects because state permit revenue falls. What constitutional power best supports the federal rule?

D. The Property Clause of Article IV of the United States Constitution.

## 5. Correct Answer

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

D is the strongest argument because the regulation controls access to land that belongs to the United States. **Gold Key:** federal land points to the Property Clause. That clause gives Congress power to make needful rules and regulations respecting federal property, and a federal agency acting under that power may regulate public use of federally owned land. **Silver Key:** on a strongest-argument call, match the power to the object being regulated. The object is not interstate trade, a private taking, or local safety in the abstract; it is federal land.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. The federal police power.** This choice sells the safety story: rockslides make the rule look like ordinary health-and-safety regulation. The breaker is the federal-power anchor. **Gold Key:** the federal government has no general police power; it needs an enumerated source, and this stem gives a better one because the land belongs to the United States.

**B. The Commerce Clause of Article I of the United States Constitution.** This choice sells the state-revenue bait. Fewer state permits and less state money can make the situation feel commercial. The breaker is the call focus. **Silver Key:** identify what the federal rule regulates. It regulates access to federal land, so the Property Clause is the cleaner and stronger support.

**C. The federal government's right of eminent domain.** This choice sells a property-law word near federal land. The breaker is the missing taking. Eminent domain is the power to take private property for public use with compensation; the stem has a rule governing public use of land the federal government already owns.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item: a federal regulation governing federally owned land is best supported by the Property Clause, not by the Commerce Clause, federal police power, or eminent domain.

Authority layer: Article IV, Section 3, Clause 2 gives Congress power to make rules and regulations respecting property belonging to the United States. The Library of Congress publishes the constitutional text at [U.S. Constitution, Article IV](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-4/). The Supreme Court in **Kleppe v. New Mexico, 426 U.S. 529 (1976)** treated the Property Clause as a broad source of congressional power over public lands; Justia's opinion page is [Kleppe v. New Mexico](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/426/529/).

Applied here, Cana Canyon Reserve is federally owned. The challenged rule closes the reserve overnight because of safety conditions on that federal property. The state revenue consequence does not change the source of federal authority. D is therefore the strongest constitutional support. The source key is confirmed.
