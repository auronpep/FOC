During the grape harvest, Stephen and Paul — two hired vineyard workers — quarreled over unpaid wages at the winepress. Stephen seized a heavy wooden winepress lever and struck Paul across the head, gravely injuring him. Stephen was indicted for aggravated battery, and counsel was appointed. While Stephen sat in custody awaiting trial, a detective read him his Miranda warnings and questioned him about the very same harvest-day altercation — but framed the questions around whether Paul might die, i.e., around a possible homicide charge, rather than around the battery already charged. Believing the detective was only exploring a hypothetical future charge, Stephen waived his Miranda rights and made statements. Paul later died of his injuries, and Stephen was charged with murder. Stephen moved to suppress his statements.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (A)

The Sixth Amendment right to counsel is offense-specific. When the detective questioned Stephen, the only offense charged was aggravated battery — murder had not been charged. **Gold Key:** the right attaches charge-by-charge and, under the *Blockburger* same-elements test, does not reach a legally distinct uncharged offense, even one growing out of the identical incident. So no Sixth Amendment right had attached to the homicide. Because Stephen received Miranda warnings and validly waived, his statements are admissible in the murder prosecution. **Silver Key:** A and C both say "admissible"; lock A, because non-attachment is the precise ground — there was no Sixth Amendment right to the murder to waive, which is exactly why C's "waiver" rationale is the wrong reason for the right result.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B (dominant trap).** It feels right that once counsel is appointed, that lawyer shields the client against questioning about anything from the same fight — the "same criminal episode" language sells continuity of protection. **Gold Key** breaks it: the Sixth Amendment is offense-specific and turns on the legal identity of the offense, not the factual transaction; battery and murder are distinct offenses, so counsel on the battery did not attach to the uncharged homicide.

**C.** This one lands on the correct outcome — admissible — so it is easy to grab, and "waived after Miranda warnings" sounds like textbook law. The breaker: there was no Sixth Amendment right to the murder charge to waive, because that right never attached; Miranda governs the Fifth Amendment waiver, while the Sixth Amendment is simply inapplicable. **Silver Key:** when two choices share the right outcome, pick the precise reason — non-attachment, not waiver.

**D.** The detective's deliberate framing around a "possible homicide" looks like gamesmanship designed to dodge the lawyer, and importing a circumvention / deliberate-elicitation rule feels protective. The breaker: the offense-specific rule is objective — because murder had not been charged and is legally distinct from battery, the Sixth Amendment right had not attached, and police investigative strategy cannot trigger a right that does not yet exist.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification + right-answer legal reasoning

**Tested rule (unchanged from original):** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel is *offense-specific*. It attaches only to charges that have been formally initiated, and "offense" is defined by the *Blockburger* same-elements test, not by the factual transaction. A different statutory offense is a different offense even when it arises from the same events; counsel on the charged offense does not bar Miranda-waived interrogation about a legally distinct uncharged offense.

**Authority (lawyer-confirmation layer only):** *McNeil v. Wisconsin*, 501 U.S. 171 (1991) (Sixth Amendment right to counsel is offense-specific); *Texas v. Cobb*, 532 U.S. 162 (2001) (the offense-specific definition is governed by the *Blockburger* same-elements test; the right does not extend to factually related but legally distinct uncharged offenses). Fifth Amendment waiver of Miranda rights governs the custodial-interrogation question independently.

**Application:** At questioning, Stephen was charged only with aggravated battery. Murder requires the victim's death — an element battery does not — so the two are distinct offenses under *Blockburger*. The Sixth Amendment right therefore had not attached to the murder. A valid Miranda waiver made the statements admissible in the later murder prosecution. Credited outcome (admissible) is identical to the source item. **Doctrinal currency:** *Cobb* (2001) remains the controlling test; nothing on the doctrinal-currency hotlist disturbs it. **Verdict:** research agrees with the official key → PASS.
