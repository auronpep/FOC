### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Stephen has already been indicted, and counsel has already been appointed. At that point, the Sixth Amendment right to counsel has attached. A live lineup is a critical stage because the witness confronts the accused in a procedure that may lock in the prosecution's identification evidence. The police therefore cannot conduct the post-indictment live lineup without giving counsel a chance to attend. Because the detectives arranged the live lineup without notifying Stephen's lawyer, Martha's lineup identification should be suppressed.

**Gold Key:** Once formal adversary proceedings have begun, a live lineup or showup is a critical stage requiring counsel; a photo array and a pre-charge identification do not trigger that same Sixth Amendment lineup rule.

**Silver Key:** Sort identification answers by two visible facts: timing and format. "Post-indictment" plus "live lineup" is the narrow counsel-right lane; answers that say only due process, later in-court ID, or every post-arrest/photo ID are too wide or off target.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A is attractive** because a witness may sometimes make a later in-court identification if the prosecution proves an independent source for that identification. The breaker is that the motion here targets Martha's lineup identification itself. A possible later courtroom identification does not cure the uncounseled post-indictment lineup or make the lineup identification admissible. **Silver Key:** keep the call locked on the evidence being challenged - the lineup identification, not a later in-court identification.

**B is attractive** because it grabs the right direction - "yes, counsel matters" - and then stretches it too far. The breaker is that the Sixth Amendment lineup rule is not "every photo and live identification after arrest." The Gold Key kills the overclaim: counsel is required for a post-charge live lineup or showup, but not for every post-arrest procedure and not for a photographic display simply because charges have been filed.

**D is attractive** because due process reliability is a real identification doctrine, especially when procedures are suggestive. The breaker is the word "only." Due process is not the only doctrine after indictment. The Sixth Amendment also applies to a post-indictment live lineup, and that counsel rule requires suppression of the lineup identification when counsel was not notified.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule is unchanged: after formal adversary criminal proceedings begin, a corporeal identification procedure such as a live lineup is a critical stage of the prosecution at which the accused has a Sixth Amendment right to counsel. The source row says Stephen's counterpart had been indicted, counsel had been appointed, police arranged an in-person lineup, counsel was not notified, and the witness identified the accused at that lineup. The transformed item preserves each legally operative fact.

The authority floor is satisfied by the Sixth Amendment and Supreme Court lineup cases. In **United States v. Wade**, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), the Court treated a post-indictment lineup as a critical stage requiring counsel. In **Gilbert v. California**, 388 U.S. 263 (1967), testimony about an uncounseled post-indictment lineup identification was excluded. The identification right is not merely a due process reliability inquiry after formal charges. At the same time, the rule is narrower than "all photo and live identifications after arrest": **United States v. Ash**, 413 U.S. 300 (1973), holds that the Sixth Amendment does not require counsel at a post-indictment photographic display. Congress's Constitution Annotated summarizes the same doctrine: post-indictment live lineups fall inside the counsel-right rule; photo displays and pre-charge procedures do not receive the same Sixth Amendment treatment.
