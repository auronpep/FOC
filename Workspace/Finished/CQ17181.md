# PASS 1 — TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

In a federal civil trial over a disputed commercial fishing contract, Galilee Fishing, LLC designates John as its Rule 615 representative. Galilee also wants Matthew, another employee who will testify, to remain at counsel table because he maintains the company's vessel logs and catch ledgers. The opposing party requests sequestration. Galilee makes no showing that Matthew is essential to presenting its defense. How should the court rule?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A non-natural party designates one employee as its FRE 615 representative, then asks to keep a second employee-witness at counsel table because he knows company records — with no showing of essentiality. Opponent requests sequestration. What result?

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.**

FRE 615 requires the court to exclude witnesses at a party's request. However, three categories of persons are exempt from exclusion. Under FRE 615(a)(2), when a party is not a natural person, that party may designate one officer or employee to remain as its representative — even if that person will testify. That exemption covers exactly one person: Galilee's designated representative, John.

**Gold Key:** The one-representative exemption under FRE 615(a)(2) is categorical and limited to a single designee. A second employee-witness does not inherit that exemption. Matthew could qualify only under FRE 615(a)(3) — which requires the party to make a showing that the person's presence is essential to presenting its claim or defense. Because Galilee made no such showing, Matthew must be excluded.

**Silver Key:** When you see a non-natural party with two employees who want to stay: count them. One gets the 615(a)(2) slot automatically. The second must earn a separate hook. "Knows the records" is a reason to call Matthew as a witness — not a reason to exempt him from sequestration. Absent a showing, he goes out.

## 7. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A — dominant trap.** The pull: Matthew maintains the vessel logs and catch ledgers — those records will matter at trial, and it seems practical for him to follow the testimony and assist counsel. A student who conflates "likely to be useful" with "essential" will land here without realizing the distinction. The breaker: FRE 615(a)(3) requires an affirmative showing of essentiality, not merely a plausible reason. Matthew knowing records makes him a valuable witness; it does not automatically qualify him for the essential-person exception. **Gold Key:** the "essential" standard in 615(a)(3) demands a showing — it is not satisfied by relevance, familiarity with documents, or potential usefulness at counsel table.

**B.** The pull: FRE 615 exempts "a party who is a natural person" under 615(a)(1), and Galilee is an LLC, not a natural person. A student who stops at 615(a)(1) without continuing to 615(a)(2) will cut both employees. The breaker: FRE 615(a)(2) specifically provides that when the party is not a natural person, it may designate one officer or employee representative. B ignores this subsection entirely.

**D.** The pull: if a corporation can have any representative at all, why not keep all of its knowledgeable employees present? Corporations can't participate in person the way humans can, so the exemption should be broad. The breaker: the entity-representative exception under FRE 615(a)(2) is limited to one designated person. Expanding it to "all employees" would swallow the sequestration order for any corporate or entity litigant — exactly what the rule's "one designee" limit prevents.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Tested rule confirmed.** FRE 615 (as amended) provides four categories exempt from exclusion:
- (a)(1): a party who is a natural person
- (a)(2): an officer or employee of a party that is not a natural person, designated as its representative by its attorney
- (a)(3): a person whose presence a party shows to be essential to presenting its claim or defense
- (a)(4): a person authorized by statute to be present

The key elements here: (1) Galilee, LLC is not a natural person → 615(a)(2) governs the representative slot; (2) one designee only — FRE 615(a)(2) says "an" officer or employee (singular); (3) Matthew must independently qualify, and 615(a)(3) requires a showing of essentiality that Galilee has not made.

**Authority:** FRE 615 (as amended through 2020). Advisory Committee Notes to FRE 615 confirm the one-designee limit for non-natural parties and the separate-showing requirement for the essential-person exception.

**Credited outcome unchanged:** original key B maps to new key C; legal substance — permit the single designated representative, exclude the second employee absent a showing of essentiality — is identical.
