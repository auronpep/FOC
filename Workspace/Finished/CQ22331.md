### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. Lydia is trying to suppress evidence found in Peter's van and Peter's metal box. Gold Key: Fourth Amendment suppression is personal; a defendant may suppress evidence only when the challenged search violated that defendant's own reasonable expectation of privacy or property interest. Silver Key: when every police fact sounds bad, ask whose privacy was invaded before asking how bad the police conduct was. Lydia had no privacy or possessory interest in Peter's van or box, so she cannot use Peter's Fourth Amendment claim to keep the bags out of her trial.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested legal issue is Fourth Amendment standing within the exclusionary-rule lane, and the verified outline code is `75100000`. The source row's `72060200` code points to criminal-law justification defenses, which does not fit this search-and-seizure item; the corrected code is logged as a source metadata defect.

The Fourth Amendment exclusionary rule may be invoked only by a defendant whose own Fourth Amendment rights were violated. In *Rakas v. Illinois*, 439 U.S. 128 (1978), the Court confirmed that Fourth Amendment rights are personal and may not be asserted vicariously, and that a defendant injured only because evidence from another person's premises or property is introduced has not shown a violation of her own Fourth Amendment rights. See https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/439/128/. The Library of Congress Constitution Annotated likewise frames suppression standing around whether the claimant's own Fourth Amendment rights were violated. See https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt4-7-3/ALDE_00000807/. Applying that rule here, Lydia had no reasonable expectation of privacy or possessory interest in Peter's van or metal box. Even if Peter could object to the stop, search, or coerced opening of the box, Lydia cannot vicariously assert his Fourth Amendment rights. The credited outcome remains unchanged: the court should deny Lydia's motion.
