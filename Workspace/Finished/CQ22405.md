### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. Daniel intentionally set a building on fire. That is arson, and arson is the dangerous felony that carries the accidental death into felony murder. The death happened during the fire Daniel set, so his belief that Naomi would be elsewhere does not rescue him from murder liability.

Gold Key: In felony murder, the dangerous predicate felony does the work. Arson is a classic predicate; a related insurance-fraud motive is not the reason the homicide becomes murder.

Silver Key: When two choices say "guilty," read the because-clause. The correct guilty answer names the dangerous felony, not the broader fraud plan.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because Daniel did not know Naomi was in the loft and did not mean to kill her. The breaker is that felony murder does not require intent to kill when the death occurs during a dangerous felony. The belief fact attacks murder intent, but the arson predicate supplies the murder route.

B is attractive because Naomi helped design the fraud, so her own participation feels like it should reduce Daniel's liability. The breaker is that a victim's agreement to an insurance scheme does not make the arson safe or erase the homicide that occurred during it.

D is attractive because it reaches the same guilty result as the credited answer and points to the fraudulent reason for the fire. The breaker is the because-clause. Insurance fraud is not the dangerous felony doing the homicide work here; the arson is. Gold Key: Do not swap the motive crime for the predicate felony.

### 8. Full Black-Letter Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule is felony murder. At common law, an unintended killing committed during an inherently dangerous felony such as arson is murder. The official federal murder statute similarly treats murder committed in the perpetration of arson as first-degree murder. See 18 U.S.C. section 1111, https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title18%2Fpart1%2Fchapter51. The Model Penal Code also identifies arson in its murder provision as one of the predicate felonies creating a presumption of extreme indifference. See Model Penal Code section 210.2(1)(b), https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/6141-tanaka-criminal-law-casebook/resources/9.1.2-model-penal-code-mpc-article-210-criminal-homicide/.

The legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged: the fatal accident happened while the actor was committing arson, not merely insurance fraud. The credited result remains guilty of murder.
