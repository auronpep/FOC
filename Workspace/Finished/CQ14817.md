## 1. Final Question

In a federal civil trial over a private Bible-study curriculum contract, Ruth wishes to establish that Daniel, the defendant, had previously been convicted of fraud in a state court. Daniel denies the conviction. Which mode of proof of the conviction is LEAST likely to be permitted?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

Which proof method is least likely to prove a disputed prior conviction: public record, firsthand witness, party admission, or judicial notice based on a judge's private phone call?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

A. Testimony of Ruth, who was present in state court when Daniel was sentenced.

B. Judicial notice of the conviction, based on the federal judge's telephone call to the state-court clerk, whom the judge knows personally.

C. Testimony by Timothy, to whom Daniel orally admitted that he had been convicted.

D. A certified copy of the judgment of conviction, offered as a self-authenticating document.

## 5. Correct Answer

B (original key D).

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is the least likely to be permitted. Judicial notice is not a shortcut for a judge to collect disputed facts through a private call. Gold Key: under FRE 201, a judicially noticed adjudicative fact must be not subject to reasonable dispute because it is generally known or can be accurately and readily determined from sources whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned. A personal telephone call to a clerk the judge knows is not that kind of indisputably accurate source. Silver Key: the call asks for the LEAST likely proof method, so cut the ordinary evidence channels first and leave the judge's private shortcut.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because Ruth is the opposing party and may look biased. The breaker is foundation, not bias: a witness who was present at sentencing can testify from personal knowledge.

C is attractive because Daniel's oral statement sounds like hearsay. The breaker is the opposing-party statement rule: Daniel's own statement, offered against Daniel, is not excluded as hearsay under FRE 801(d)(2). This is the dominant trap because it invites students to stop at "oral statement" instead of routing the speaker.

D is attractive only if a student distrusts documents. The breaker is FRE 902: a certified copy of a public record can be self-authenticating and is a conventional way to prove a judgment of conviction.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested issue and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. FRE 201(b) allows judicial notice only for adjudicative facts not subject to reasonable dispute because they are generally known in the trial court's territorial jurisdiction or can be accurately and readily determined from sources whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned. A judge's private phone call to a personally known state-court clerk does not satisfy that standard for a disputed conviction. The other proof modes are ordinary evidence channels: FRE 902 permits certain certified public records to be self-authenticating; FRE 602 allows testimony from a witness with personal knowledge; and FRE 801(d)(2) treats an opposing party's own statement, offered against that party, as not hearsay. Therefore the judicial-notice-by-phone-call method remains the least likely to be permitted.
