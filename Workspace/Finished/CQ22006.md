## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Naomi orally told Timothy that he could build a gravel path across the edge of Naomi's meadow so Timothy could reach the public road from his own parcel and bring supplies to the small prayer garden he kept there. Relying on Naomi's permission, Timothy hired workers, bought gravel and drainage pipe, and completed the path. Six months later, Naomi told Timothy that she was revoking permission and sued him for trespass when he continued using the path.

What right, if any, did Timothy have to use the path?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

What right does a land user have after spending substantial money to build an access path in reliance on an oral permission?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. Timothy had no right to use the path because the permission violated the Statute of Frauds.

B. Timothy had an irrevocable license.

C. Timothy had an irrevocable easement.

D. Timothy had a revocable license.

### 5. Correct answer

B (original key C).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Naomi gave permission orally, so Timothy did not receive an express easement. But Timothy spent substantial money and labor building the path in reliance on the permission. Gold Key: substantial reliance can make an oral license irrevocable. Silver Key: do not jump from "not a written easement" to "no right"; check whether reliance turns the license irrevocable.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because easements usually need a writing. The breaker is that a license does not require the Statute of Frauds. The oral permission can create a license, and reliance can make it irrevocable.

C is attractive because Timothy built something permanent and now has a protected access right. The label is too strong for the MBE answer array. The oral permission failed as an express easement, so the better label is irrevocable license.

D is the dominant trap. It states the ordinary license rule: licenses are usually revocable. The breaker is Timothy's substantial reliance expenditures after Naomi's permission.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is unchanged: Timothy has an irrevocable license because he substantially relied on Naomi's oral permission. The issue is license by estoppel, not an express easement.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes section 2.10 recognizes servitudes created by estoppel when reliance on permission makes it unjust to permit revocation. Courts and teaching materials also describe the result as an irrevocable license or easement by estoppel. See CALI's lesson on licenses and easements by estoppel at https://www.cali.org/lesson/637 and the Oregon briefing excerpt discussing Restatement section 2.10 at https://ojd.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p17027coll8/id/94498/download.
