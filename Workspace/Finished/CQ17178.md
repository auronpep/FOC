At a late-night Christian music festival, Ruth and Daniel agree to take a private food-truck vendor's locked cashbox while the vendor wheels it toward his car. ### 7. Full right-answer explanation

D is correct. Ruth joined a plan to take the vendor's cashbox by confronting the vendor at night. Daniel's use of a metal microphone stand to overcome the vendor's resistance helped complete that plan, and that kind of force can be a reasonably foreseeable consequence of the agreed robbery. Gold Key: Pinkerton does not require Ruth to personally use the weapon; it asks whether Daniel's crime was committed during the conspiracy, in furtherance of it, and was reasonably foreseeable. Silver Key: In this answer array, look for the bounded yes, not a no-personal-use answer and not an every-future-crime answer.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because it tries to narrow Pinkerton to the kind of white-collar setting where many students first learn the doctrine. The breaker is that Pinkerton is a co-conspirator liability rule, not a fraud-only rule; the limiting terms are furtherance, foreseeability, and timing.

B is attractive because it catches the word "agreement" and turns the conspiracy into an unlimited liability hook. The breaker is the word "any": Pinkerton does not cover every later crime by a co-conspirator; it is limited to crimes committed during and in furtherance of the conspiracy and reasonably foreseeable from the plan.

C is the dominant trap because it feels fair to refuse deadly-weapon liability when Ruth never carried or swung the microphone stand. Gold Key: Pinkerton can impose liability without personal use of the weapon when the co-conspirator's substantive crime was in furtherance of the conspiracy and reasonably foreseeable.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. Pinkerton liability allows a conspirator to be held liable for a co-conspirator's substantive offense when the offense is committed while the conspiracy continues, in furtherance of the conspiracy, and as a reasonably foreseeable consequence of the unlawful agreement. The primary authority is Pinkerton v. United States, 328 U.S. 640 (1946), available at https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/328/640. The Court sustained liability where substantive offenses were committed in furtherance of the unlawful agreement and treated the conspirators' ongoing partnership in crime as the basis for attribution, while recognizing limits for acts outside the scope, not in furtherance, or not reasonably foreseeable.

Here, the agreed plan was a late-night taking from a vendor. Force used to overcome the victim's resistance advanced that taking. A metal microphone stand used as a striking object supports the charged assault-with-a-deadly-weapon theory. Ruth's lack of personal weapon use does not defeat Pinkerton because the assault was in furtherance of the plan and a foreseeable escalation of the agreed robbery.
