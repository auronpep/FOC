## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

Deacon Stephen was brought to the police station for questioning regarding a discrepancy in his church’s benevolence fund. The detectives read Stephen his Miranda rights and presented him with a written waiver form. Before Stephen signed the form, the lead detective leaned in and said, "Stephen, we know your daughter, Phoebe, helps with the church bookkeeping. If you don’t sign this waiver and tell us where the money went right now, we are going to arrest her as an accomplice this afternoon." In reality, the detectives had no evidence whatsoever linking Phoebe to any financial misconduct. Stephen, fearing for his daughter’s safety and reputation, signed the form and provided a detailed confession.

Is the signed waiver form sufficient to make Stephen’s confession admissible in court?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Police threaten to arrest a suspect's innocent relative if the suspect does not sign a Miranda waiver and confess. The suspect signs and confesses. Is the waiver valid?

### 3. Final Answer Choices

(A) Yes, because a signed written waiver is conclusive proof that a suspect has waived their Miranda rights voluntarily.

(B) Yes, because police may use psychological pressure, including threats against relatives, to encourage a suspect to speak the truth.

(C) No, because the baseless threat against Stephen's daughter constituted coercion that invalidated the voluntariness of the waiver and confession.

(D) No, but only if Stephen had explicitly stated that he was only signing the form to protect his daughter.

### 5. Correct Answer

C (original key C).

### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

(C) is correct. To be valid, a Miranda waiver must be voluntary, knowing, and intelligent. Voluntariness is determined by the "totality of the circumstances," and a waiver is involuntary if it is the product of police coercion that overbears the suspect's free will. A threat to arrest or charge a close relative, when made without a legitimate factual basis (probable cause), is considered a form of psychological coercion that invalidates both the waiver and the subsequent confession. Because the detectives had no evidence against Phoebe, the threat was a "sham" intended to force Stephen into a waiver. Gold Key: A police threat against a relative made without a factual basis is coercive and invalidates a Miranda waiver.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

(A) This choice is attractive because a signed waiver form is strong evidence of a valid waiver. However, it is incorrect because it is not conclusive proof. The court must still evaluate the circumstances surrounding the signing to ensure it was not induced by coercion or fraud.

(B) This choice is attractive because police are allowed to use some level of psychological tactics (like lying about evidence against the suspect). However, it is incorrect because threats against third parties/relatives are viewed much more strictly and, when baseless, cross the line into unconstitutional coercion.

(D) This choice is attractive because it suggests a requirement for the suspect to preserve the issue. However, it is incorrect because the burden is on the prosecution to prove voluntariness; the suspect does not need to explicitly state their motive for the coercion to be legally effective.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: Miranda Waiver Voluntariness (Coercion via threats to relatives). A confession or waiver is involuntary if it was extracted by threats or violence, or obtained by any direct or implied promises, however slight, or by the exertion of any improper influence. Threats to arrest a relative, especially when the police lack probable cause to do so, are inherently coercive. Authority: People v. Trout, 54 Cal. 2d 576 (1960); United States v. Tingle, 658 F.2d 1332 (9th Cir. 1981). The lack of a factual basis mentioned in the stem is the dispositive fact that makes the threat a "sham" and therefore coercive. The outcome (No) is consistent with the source item.

### 10. Rigor and Difficulty Preservation

The item is "Core" rigor. It tests the limits of "permissible interrogation tactics" and the evidentiary weight of a signed waiver form. The student must recognize that a formal document (the waiver) cannot cure a substantive constitutional defect (coercion).

### 11. C3 Elimination Walkthrough

CUT (B): Flatly misstates the limit on police tactics; baseless threats against relatives are coercive.

CUT (D): Invents a procedural requirement for the suspect to "narrate" the coercion.

CLASH (A) vs. (C): The axis is Formalism vs. Substantive Voluntariness. (A) sells the idea that the "paperwork" (the signed form) is the final word. (C) correctly looks past the form to the actual mental state induced by the state's coercive act.

CALL (C): The sham threat overbore the suspect's will, rendering the waiver and confession involuntary under the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments.
