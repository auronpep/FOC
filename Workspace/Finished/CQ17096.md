## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. In a federal civil action, a jury may begin with seven members because the allowed range is at least six and no more than 12. But jury size is only half of the rule. The verdict itself must be unanimous unless the parties stipulate otherwise. A 5-2 split is not unanimous, so the court may not enter a verdict for Mary on that verdict form.

Gold Key: Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 48 has two separate default requirements: the jury size must be within the 6-to-12 range, and the verdict must be unanimous unless the parties stipulate otherwise.

Silver Key: When the stem gives both the number of jurors and the vote split, label both facts separately. Seven jurors clears the size rule; a 5-2 vote fails the unanimity rule.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because students often remember "twelve jurors" as the classic jury image. The breaker is that federal civil juries do not need 12 members; seven is within the Rule 48 range, so the problem is not jury size.

B is the dominant trap because "more than two-thirds" sounds more legally serious than a bare majority. The breaker is the Gold Key: absent a stipulation, Rule 48 asks for unanimity, not a supermajority.

D is attractive because 5 out of 7 is a majority and the majority favored the plaintiff. The breaker is the Silver Key: the call is not asking who got more votes; it is asking whether the federal civil verdict may be entered under the default verdict rule.
