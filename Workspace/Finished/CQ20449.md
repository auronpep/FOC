### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B** is correct. The lineup occurred before any formal adversarial judicial proceeding began. Martha had been arrested and placed in a lineup, but she had not been charged by complaint or information, indicted, arraigned, or otherwise brought into the formal prosecution. **Gold Key:** the Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches only when formal adversarial proceedings begin; a pre-charge lineup does not trigger the Sixth Amendment lineup-counsel right. **Silver Key:** in a lineup question, check the attachment fact before reacting to the suspect's request for a lawyer.

The detectives therefore did not violate Martha's Sixth Amendment right to counsel by proceeding without her attorney. A separate due process challenge could exist if the lineup were impermissibly suggestive and unreliable, but these facts do not raise that issue.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A.** This choice sells the clean-sounding rule that lineups are always critical stages. The breaker is the attachment point. A post-charge lineup can be a critical stage, but the Sixth Amendment right has not attached before formal adversarial proceedings begin. **Gold Key:** lineup-counsel doctrine turns first on whether the prosecution has formally begun.

**C.** This choice reaches the correct yes/no result but gives the wrong constitutional reason. The issue is not waiver by participation. The reason there was no Sixth Amendment violation is that the right had not attached yet. If the right had attached, waiver would require a valid waiver analysis; merely standing in the lineup after the detective refuses counsel is not the clean rule.

**D.** This is the dominant trap. The request for counsel feels decisive because Martha asked and the detective said no. The breaker is that a request cannot attach the Sixth Amendment before the criminal prosecution begins. The right is triggered by formal adversarial proceedings, not by the suspect's demand during a pre-charge investigation. **Silver Key:** do not let "asked for a lawyer" outrun the timing fact.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The credited outcome is correct. The Sixth Amendment provides the right to assistance of counsel in "criminal prosecutions," and the Supreme Court has tied attachment to the initiation of formal adversarial judicial proceedings. In *Kirby v. Illinois*, 406 U.S. 682 (1972), the Court held that the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had not attached at a pre-indictment identification confrontation because adversarial judicial proceedings had not yet begun. In *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), the Court recognized a right to counsel at a post-indictment lineup, which confirms the timing distinction. *Rothgery v. Gillespie County*, 554 U.S. 191 (2008), restates the attachment rule: formal charge, preliminary hearing, indictment, information, arraignment, or a similar first appearance can initiate the adversarial process.
