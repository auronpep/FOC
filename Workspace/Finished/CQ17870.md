### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

A is correct. Under the agency theory, felony murder reaches only killings committed by the felon or a co-felon acting in furtherance of the felony. A death caused by a victim or police officer resisting the felony is outside that theory. Here the deputy, a third party, fired the fatal shot.

Gold Key: the deciding fact is who actually did the killing, not just that the death happened during the robbery. Silver Key: cut any answer that relies only on "during the felony" or proximate cause and keep the answer that tracks the shooter.

*People v. Washington* and *Commonwealth v. Redline* reflect that common-law agency limit.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice B - "Yes, because Peter's death occurred during the commission of the robbery Lydia was participating in."**

What made it attractive: This is the classic felony-murder shortcut. It looks complete because the death and the felony happen at the same time, which is exactly the kind of fact pattern students memorize.

The breaker: Agency theory needs more than a death during the felony. It requires the killing to be committed by the felon or co-felon, not by a deputy resisting the crime. Gold Key: "during the felony" is not enough if the fatal shot came from a third party.

**Choice C - "No, because Peter's own conduct (pointing a gun at the deputy) was a superseding intervening cause."**

What made it attractive: It gives the student's brain a causal story. Peter escalated, so the death feels like it should be blamed on Peter's own conduct.

The breaker: That is a different causation question. Agency theory does not ask whether Peter's conduct was a superseding cause; it asks who fired the fatal shot. Silver Key: do not swap into a general causation analysis when the stem names agency theory.

**Choice D - "Yes, because Lydia proximately caused Peter's death by initiating the robbery."**

What made it attractive: Proximate cause is a real felony-murder theory, so this answer sounds polished and doctrinally serious.

The breaker: This jurisdiction follows agency theory, not proximate cause. The deputy was not acting as Lydia's agent or as a co-felon, so proximate-cause language does not save the answer. Gold Key: third-party killing cuts off agency-theory felony murder.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning

The tested issue remains Criminal Law and Procedure, Homicide, Murder, and the verified outline code is `74020101`. The common-law agency theory of felony murder limits liability to killings committed by the felon or an accomplice/co-felon acting in furtherance of the felony. A killing by a victim, police officer, or other non-felon resisting the felony does not count. That is the rule stated in *People v. Washington*, 62 Cal.2d 777 (1965), and it is consistent with *Commonwealth v. Redline*, 391 Pa. 486 (1958). Those cases reject pure proximate-cause reasoning in this setting and treat the identity of the killer as the load-bearing fact.

Here, the deputy shot Peter while resisting the robbery. Because the deputy was a third party, not a co-felon, Lydia is not guilty of felony murder for Peter's death under the agency theory. The official key is unchanged.
