## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

At a private Bible-calligraphy studio, Martha shot Lydia to death during a late-night confrontation beside the paper-cutting table. Martha was later charged with voluntary manslaughter. Which additional fact, if true, would lead to an acquittal on that charge?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Which fact turns a voluntary-manslaughter shooting into a full self-defense acquittal?

### 3. Final Answer Choices

A. At the time of the shooting, a reasonable person in Martha's place would have believed that Lydia was about to stab Martha with a craft knife, but Martha did not actually hold that belief.

B. At the time of the shooting, Martha mistakenly, but reasonably, believed that Lydia was about to stab her with a craft knife.

C. At the time of the shooting, Martha did not know whether Lydia was about to stab her with a craft knife.

### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is the only fact that supplies both pieces needed for a complete self-defense acquittal: Martha actually believed Lydia was about to stab her, and that mistaken belief was reasonable. Gold Key: perfect self-defense needs both an actual defensive belief and a reasonable basis for that belief. Silver Key: read the choices as a two-part grid; only the square with actual belief plus reasonable belief answers the acquittal call.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A has the objective half, so it sounds close. The breaker is the missing subjective half: if Martha did not actually believe Lydia was about to stab her, the reasonable-person fact does not give Martha a personal self-defense acquittal.

C sells uncertainty as caution. The breaker is that "did not know whether" is not an actual defensive belief; uncertainty is not the same thing as reasonably believing imminent harm is coming.

D is the strongest trap because it gives Martha an actual fear. Gold Key: actual fear alone is not perfect self-defense; if a reasonable person would not have held the belief, the choice preserves the voluntary-manslaughter problem instead of producing acquittal.

### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The credited answer remains unchanged in substance. A person who intentionally uses deadly force can be fully justified if the person actually and reasonably believes that deadly defensive force is necessary to prevent imminent death or serious bodily harm. A reasonable mistake can support perfect self-defense and therefore acquittal; an unreasonable actual belief is the imperfect-self-defense trap and does not fully acquit on this charge.

Authority floor: Model Penal Code sections 3.04(2)(b) and 3.09(2) frame deadly-force self-protection and culpable mistaken belief, and People v. Goetz, 68 N.Y.2d 96 (1986), confirms that self-defense uses an objective reasonableness component rather than a purely subjective fear test. Sources checked: https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/archives/p_goetz.htm and https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/3_04-3_07.htm.
