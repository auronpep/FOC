### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested issue remains Criminal Law and Procedure, Other Crimes, receipt of stolen property, and the verified outline code is `73030801`. The completed offense of receiving stolen property requires property that is actually stolen, receipt or control of that property, knowledge that it is stolen, and intent to deprive the owner. Model Penal Code section 223.6 uses the same core formulation by treating a person as guilty when he knowingly receives movable property of another knowing that it has been stolen, or believing that it probably has been stolen; it defines receiving to include acquiring possession, control, or title.

The recovery distinction is confirmed by receiving-stolen-property doctrine: property that has been recovered by police no longer has stolen status for the completed offense. In *People v. Rojas*, 55 Cal. 2d 252 (1961), the court recognized that goods recovered by police were not in stolen status for the consummated receiving offense, even though the defendants could still be guilty of attempt under that jurisdiction's approach. The Oklahoma Uniform Jury Instructions commentary similarly states that the property must be stolen and that property which has lost its stolen taint does not qualify; it also states that receipt may be established by dominion or control even without manual possession. Federal receiving-stolen-property statutes and DOJ guidance also frame the offense around receiving or possessing goods that have been stolen while knowing that status. See 18 U.S.C. section 2315; DOJ Criminal Resource Manual 1327; Oklahoma Uniform Jury Instructions - Criminal 5-111; *People v. Rojas*, https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/people-v-rojas-24303; DOJ, https://www.justice.gov/archives/jm/criminal-resource-manual-1327-elements-first-paragraph-18-usc-2315; OCCA, https://okcca.net/ouji-cr/5-111/.

The credited outcome is unchanged. In C, the mixer was actually stolen and Stephen knowingly controlled its delivery by directing Barnabas to give it to John. In B, the mixer was no longer stolen when Stephen paid because police had already recovered it. In A and D, the mixer was never stolen. No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is implicated.
