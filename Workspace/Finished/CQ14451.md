## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.** Lydia promised to pay for Barnabas's quitclaim deed. Barnabas delivered exactly that deed. **Gold Key:** a bargained-for quitclaim, release, or written surrender of a doubtful claim can supply consideration even if the claim later proves worthless. **Silver Key:** keep the reason attached to the result; the winning answer must say why Barnabas wins through the bargain itself, not through a substitute theory.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This answer sells formal consideration vocabulary: “legal detriment” sounds like the issue. The breaker is the Gold Key: executing the quitclaim deed was the act Lydia bargained to receive, so the answer denies legal effect to the very performance in the exchange.

**B.** This is the dominant trap. It turns the later loss and Barnabas's known doubt into a reason the bargain fails. **Gold Key:** a doubtful claim is not poison to consideration; it is often the reason the quitclaim or release has bargain value.

**C.** This answer sells the right result through the wrong path. The first payment makes “estoppel” feel attractive, but **Silver Key:** a correct result with the wrong because-clause is not the residual. The facts support a bargain-for-consideration answer, not a promissory-estoppel substitute.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

**Black-letter rule tested.** Consideration is satisfied by a bargained-for performance or return promise. The performance can be an act, a forbearance, or a change in legal relation. A bargained-for surrender or written release of a doubtful claim can be consideration even if the claim later proves invalid. If consideration exists, courts do not require equivalence in the values exchanged.

**Authority floor.** The ALI identifies the Restatement (Second) of Contracts as an authoritative common-law contracts source covering formation and consideration. Restatement § 71 provides the bargained-for-exchange frame and includes acts, forbearance, and changes in legal relation as possible consideration. Restatement § 74 addresses surrender of doubtful claims, and § 79 rejects any extra requirement of equivalent exchanged value once consideration exists. ([American Law Institute][1])

**Application.** Barnabas executed and delivered the exact quitclaim deed Lydia requested. Lydia received the no-warranty instrument she bargained for. The later judgment for Naomi does not retroactively erase the consideration. The official credited outcome is unchanged from the supplied row, whose key and explanation identify the quitclaim deed as sufficient consideration.
