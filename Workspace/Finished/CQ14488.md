## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam-register)

**B is correct.** Under UCC § 2-509(1), when a contract requires or authorizes the seller to ship goods by carrier and does not require delivery at a particular destination, the contract is a shipment contract. Under a shipment contract, risk of loss passes to the buyer when the goods are duly delivered to the carrier. Comment 5 to UCC § 2-503 provides that where the contract is otherwise silent, a shipment contract is presumed when the contract requires shipment by a third-party carrier. Because the contract here requires Paul to ship the mugs by a common carrier and says nothing about a particular destination, this is a shipment contract. Therefore, risk of loss passed when Paul duly delivered the mugs to the carrier and made a proper contract for their carriage.

**Gold Key:** UCC § 2-503 Comment 5 — if the contract says "ship by carrier" but doesn't specify a destination, presume a shipment contract. Risk passes on delivery to the carrier, not on arrival at the buyer.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (originally A) — attractive because:** Students may think that once a binding contract exists, all risk shifts immediately — a "deal is a deal" intuition. **Breaker:** UCC § 2-509(1) explicitly rejects this. Risk of loss follows delivery obligations, not contract formation. The UCC has specific rules for when risk passes, and contract formation is not one of them.

**C (originally D) — attractive because:** Students hear "ship by carrier" and picture the goods traveling to the buyer's location, so they assume risk stays with the seller until the goods physically arrive and are unloaded. This feels like the seller "owns" the goods until they reach the buyer. **Breaker:** This describes a destination contract under UCC § 2-509(1)(b), not a shipment contract. Comment 5 to UCC § 2-503 presumes a shipment contract when the contract requires shipment by a third-party carrier and is silent on destination. Under a shipment contract, risk passes on delivery to the carrier — not on arrival or unloading. **Gold Key:** UCC § 2-503 Comment 5 — shipment contract presumed; risk passes at carrier delivery, not at buyer's doorstep.

**D (originally B) — attractive because:** Students know that "identification of goods" is an important UCC concept (UCC § 2-501) and may assume it governs risk of loss too. **Breaker:** Identification of goods determines when the buyer acquires special property rights and when the seller can identify which goods are the subject of the contract, but it does not govern when risk of loss passes. The governing provisions are UCC §§ 2-509(1) and 2-503, Comment 5.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing rule:** UCC § 2-509(1) — Risk of Loss in the Absence of Breach.

- **§ 2-509(1)(a):** If the contract does not require the seller to deliver goods at a particular destination, risk of loss passes to the buyer when the goods are duly delivered to the carrier.
- **§ 2-509(1)(b):** If the contract does require delivery at a particular destination and the goods are there duly tendered while in the possession of the carrier, risk passes when the goods are duly so tendered as to enable the buyer to take delivery.
- **UCC § 2-503, Comment 5:** Where the contract is otherwise silent, a shipment contract is presumed when the contract requires shipment by a third-party carrier.

**Application:** The contract requires Paul to ship by a common carrier and is silent on freight costs and place of tender. Under Comment 5 to § 2-503, this is presumed to be a shipment contract. Under § 2-509(1)(a), risk of loss passes when the seller duly delivers the goods to the carrier.

#### Wrong-Answer Recovery Paths

**If student picked A (contract formation):**
- Recovery: Ask — "Does the UCC tie risk of loss to contract formation or to delivery obligations?" The UCC has specific delivery-based rules. Contract formation is never the trigger.

**If student picked C (identification):**
- Recovery: Ask — "What does identification of goods govern?" It governs when the buyer acquires special property rights (§ 2-501), not risk of loss. The risk-of-loss rules are in § 2-509.

**If student picked D (unloading at buyer's premises) — DOMINANT TRAP:**
- Recovery: Gold Key — UCC § 2-503 Comment 5. Ask — "Does the contract specify a destination?" It doesn't. So it's presumed to be a shipment contract. In a shipment contract, risk passes on delivery to the carrier, not on arrival. The unloading-at-buyer's-place answer describes a destination contract, which this is not.
