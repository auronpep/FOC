## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. The court would take judicial notice of federal treaties and immigration laws because those materials could show whether federal law has occupied the field, supplied a federal rule, or created a conflict that prevents the state election rule from operating. That is a Supremacy Clause inquiry.

Gold Key: When a state law's validity depends on federal statutes, treaties, or a federal regulatory scheme, route the question to Supremacy Clause preemption. The point is not whether the state rule is wise or fair; the point is whether federal law controls over contrary state law.

Silver Key: Follow the source of law in the clue. "Treaties and immigration laws of the United States" points to federal law overriding state law, not to a personal-rights clause or a branch-structure doctrine.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. The rule is that federal law, including valid federal statutes and treaties, is supreme over inconsistent state law. When a state statute regulating noncitizens is challenged, federal immigration laws and treaties may be relevant because they can show whether Congress has preempted state regulation or whether the state law conflicts with federal policy.

Authority floor: U.S. Const. art. VI, cl. 2 makes the Constitution, federal laws made under it, and treaties the supreme law of the land, binding state judges despite contrary state law. Constitution Annotated explains that modern Supremacy Clause doctrine is channeled through federal preemption, including express preemption, field preemption, and conflict preemption. In Arizona v. In Hines v. Davidowitz, 312 U.S. 52 (1941), the Court held that the federal alien-registration scheme precluded enforcement of a state alien-registration law.
