# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

A bright 12-year-old was enrolled in **Martha's Harvest Helpers**, a private after-school farm-skills program that operated for a fee on grounds adjoining a grain-storage site owned by **Carmel Grain Company, a corporation**. During the autumn harvest, the company's tall steel grain bin held a load of shelled corn that was being drawn down from a chute below, so the surface crust sat over a hollow, flowing column of grain. While the bin was obviously only partially full and the grain was plainly settling and shifting, the child slipped away from the program's grounds and climbed through a hatch into the bin. The grain crust collapsed, and the child sank into the corn; he suffered shock and would have suffocated had he not been pulled out by Andrew, a passing farmhand. The bin and the surrounding site were clearly marked with numerous signs that stated, **"DANGER—FLOWING GRAIN—KEEP OUT."** When the child slipped away, Martha's Harvest Helpers was staffed with a reasonable number of qualified counselors, and the counselors were exercising reasonable care to ensure that the children in their charge did not leave the grounds. There had not been a previous instance of a child leaving the program and entering the grain company's site. The jurisdiction follows a rule of pure comparative negligence.

**In a suit brought on the child's behalf against Martha's Harvest Helpers and based only on the facts above, who is likely to prevail?**

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A supervised child sneaks off a care provider's grounds and is hurt by a dangerous artificial condition on a *third party's* land. The provider had adequate, careful staff and no prior elopements. In the child's suit **against the provider** (not the landowner), who prevails?

## 3. Final Answer Choices (post-shuffle)

- **B.** Martha's Harvest Helpers, because the child was a trespasser.
- **C.** The child, because he was in the program's charge when he slipped away.
- **D.** Martha's Harvest Helpers, because it was not negligent.

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam register)

**D is correct.** The suit is against the *program*, not the landowner. A care provider is not an insurer of the children in its charge; it is liable only if its own negligence is shown. The facts affirmatively foreclose breach — a reasonable number of qualified counselors, reasonable care to keep the children on the grounds, and no prior instance of a child wandering onto the company's site. With no breach, the program prevails.

**Silver Key:** When two choices reach the *same* outcome — here B and D both say the program wins — don't stop at the verdict; cut the one whose *reason* fits a different party. The reason that actually explains why **this** defendant wins ("it was not negligent") is the one to lock.

**Gold Key:** The limited duty owed to a trespasser, and attractive-nuisance liability for a dangerous artificial condition, bind only the **possessor of the land** where the hazard sits — that is Carmel Grain Company. The supervising program owes ordinary care and is judged on its own conduct, so the child's trespasser status decides nothing against it.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

- **A — "operated on grounds adjoining the grain company's storage site."** The pull is the felt sense that putting children next to a known danger is itself careless. But location is only a fact about the setting, not a failure of care; it might *call for* extra-vigilant supervision, and the stem says the staff in fact exercised reasonable care. Proximity without a lapse in supervision is a true fact missing the breach element it pretends to supply.
- **B — "because the child was a trespasser." (dominant trap)** This one is seductive because it reaches the *correct outcome* (the program wins) and dresses it in real premises-liability vocabulary. **Gold Key:** trespasser status limits the duty owed by the *possessor of the land* — Carmel Grain Company — and is irrelevant to whether the *program* used reasonable care; the reason answers a different defendant's question. **Silver Key:** with B and D both favoring the program, the move is to keep the choice whose rationale fits this defendant, not the one borrowed from the landowner's defense.
- **C — "because he was in the program's charge when he slipped away."** The attraction is the intuition that a child's caretaker is automatically on the hook when the child is hurt. That treats custody as strict liability; a provider's duty is reasonable supervision, and liability requires a breach the facts here negate. "In its charge" establishes a duty, not its violation.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Right-Answer Legal Reasoning (lawyer-confirmation layer)

**Tested rule (unchanged from the original):** A custodian/supervisor of a child owes a duty of *reasonable care*, not strict liability; recovery on a negligence theory requires proof of breach. Premises-based theories — the limited duty to trespassers and the attractive-nuisance doctrine for artificial conditions dangerous to trespassing children — run against the **possessor of the land**, a different party from the supervisor.

- One who takes charge of a child owes reasonable care in supervision: **Restatement (Second) of Torts § 320** (duty of a person having custody of another). Not an insurer — breach must be shown. The facts negate breach (adequate qualified staff, reasonable care, no prior elopement).
- Duties to trespassers and to trespassing children are imposed on the *possessor of the land*: **Restatement (Second) of Torts § 333** (general rule — possessor's duty to trespassers) and **§ 339** (artificial conditions highly dangerous to trespassing children — the attractive-nuisance rule). These bind Carmel Grain Company, the possessor where the bin sits, not Martha's Harvest Helpers.
- **Pure comparative negligence** reduces a plaintiff's recovery by his fault share but never bars it; it is immaterial here because the defendant program committed no breach to begin with.

**Credited outcome unchanged:** provider prevails because it was not negligent. Research **agrees** with the supplied official key (original C). No doctrinal-currency item on the hotlist is implicated (this is core negligence/premises duty). Authority floor satisfied by Restatement sections; no case citation is asserted, to avoid fabrication. `bank_validation_verdict: PASS`.
