### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Ownership, Future Interests, and the verified outline code is `82010201` (Nature and Ownership of Land > Future Interests > Contingent and vested remainders). The source row's credited answer is confirmed.

Common-law estate doctrine treats a life estate as a present possessory estate that lasts for a measuring life. Cornell Wex states that a life-estate holder has the right to possess and use the property and may transfer the interest during life; if the life tenant sells the interest to a third party, the third party must surrender the property when the original life tenant dies. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/life_estate

Cornell Wex separately defines a life estate pur autre vie as a life estate whose duration is determined by a measuring life other than the holder's life. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/life_estate_pur_autre_vie

Georgia Code § 44-6-83 reflects the life tenant's present-use rule and preservation duty: a tenant for life is entitled to full use and enjoyment of the property while exercising ordinary care and not permanently injuring the remainder or reversion. Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/2020/title-44/chapter-6/article-5/section-44-6-83/

The Virginia Attorney General's 2013 tax opinion describes the common-law tax-duty frame: a life tenant has possession of the freehold and responsibilities of property ownership, including the duty to pay taxes, while a remainderman lacks present control. Source: https://www.tax.virginia.gov/laws-rules-decisions/attorney-generals-opinion/13-070

Those authorities support the official key. Ruth held a life estate and Timothy held a vested remainder. Ruth's deed did not divest Timothy's remainder and did not forfeit Ruth's life estate. It transferred Ruth's life-estate interest to Lydia, creating a life estate pur autre vie measured by Ruth's life. As current life-estate holder, Lydia is entitled to income and must pay ordinary taxes from available income. Timothy holds the remainder and has no current tax duty on these facts. The tested rule, legal issue, trap structure, and credited outcome are unchanged.
