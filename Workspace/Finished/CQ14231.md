## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** Martha is suing Covenant Mutual, a private insurer, over its own premium decision. Equal protection limits governmental action. The state insurance commissioner audits the insurer for solvency, but the state does not set the company's premiums, write its policies, compel the challenged price difference, or otherwise make the pricing choice the state's own act. Because the constitutional guarantee of equal protection does not apply to this private insurer's pricing decision, Martha's equal protection suit against Covenant Mutual will not succeed.

**Gold Key:** Equal protection starts with the actor. The Fourteenth Amendment restricts state action, not ordinary private pricing; state inspection or general regulation of a private business does not make every private business decision constitutional state action.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The tested rule is the state-action requirement for constitutional equal protection. The Fourteenth Amendment's text binds state action: "No State shall" deny equal protection. National Archives, 14th Amendment transcript: https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/14th-amendment. The source row's result also matches the state-action doctrine summarized by the Library of Congress Constitution Annotated: ordinary state regulation of a business does not by itself convert a private business decision into state action, and a state is normally responsible for a private decision only when it coerces, significantly encourages, delegates an exclusive public function, or is closely connected with the challenged action. Constitution Annotated, State Action Doctrine: https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt14-2/ALDE_00000810/.

The leading case analogy is Jackson v.
