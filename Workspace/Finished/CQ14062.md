# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question (stem + call)

Stephen, an orchard keeper, sued Crestline Irrigation Company in federal court, asserting that a defective irrigation valve manufactured by Crestline negligently flooded and destroyed his apple orchard. Stephen furnished Crestline with a written request for the jury instruction on the standard of care that Stephen wanted the court to give. The court informed the parties that it would not give Stephen's requested instruction and told the parties the essence of the instruction on the standard of care that the court proposed to give. The court then gave the parties an opportunity to object on the record and out of the jury's presence. Stephen objected to the court's refusal to give his proposed instruction and stated the grounds for his objection, but did not separately object to the court's proposed instruction. After the parties made their final arguments to the jury, the court instructed the jury on the standard of care. The court's instruction on the standard of care was modified from the instruction that the court had previously proposed to the parties. Stephen promptly objected to the judge's standard-of-care instruction. The jury then returned a verdict for Crestline, and the trial court entered judgment for Crestline. Stephen filed a timely notice of appeal, arguing that the judge's standard-of-care instruction was erroneous and prejudicial.

**Is the appellate court likely to rule on the merits of Stephen's appeal?**

In a federal civil jury trial, the court gives a jury instruction that it modified from the version it had earlier proposed, and the losing party objects right after the instruction is read to the jury. Did that party preserve the instruction issue for appeal?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam register)

**C is correct.** Stephen could not have objected to the standard-of-care instruction that was actually given until he heard it — because the court *changed* it from the version it had previewed at the objection conference. He objected the moment the modified instruction was read. That objection is timely, so the instruction issue is preserved and the appellate court will reach the merits.

**Gold Key:** A party who was not informed of an instruction (or a change to one) before the formal objection opportunity preserves the issue by objecting *promptly after learning of it* — a late change by the court reopens the objection window. **Silver Key:** Two choices here say "Yes" (C and D); lock the one whose stated *reason* is also true, and reject the one that concedes a waiver the record never shows.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (dominant trap).** The stem dangles the fact that Stephen "did not separately object to the court's proposed instruction," which sells the idea that he slept on his objection and forfeited the appeal. But that proposed instruction is not the one he is challenging — the court gave a *different, modified* instruction, and Stephen objected to it at once. **Gold Key:** the late change reopened the objection window, so the objection that matters was timely and nothing was waived.

**B.** It pairs a confident-sounding outcome with a real appellate vocabulary word — "clearly erroneous" — which feels lawyerly and lets a test-taker stop reading. The standard is simply mismatched: "clearly erroneous" reviews a judge's findings of *fact*, never an error in a jury instruction. **Gold Key:** an unpreserved instruction error is reviewed for *plain error* affecting substantial rights, and a preserved one for legal correctness — so the labeled standard is wrong even before reaching timeliness.

**D.** It reaches the right destination ("Yes, the appeal is heard") and even uses the magic word "timely," so a student who has correctly concluded the objection was timely may grab it. But it buys that result by conceding that Stephen "waived his objection to the instruction prior to trial" — something that never happened; his pre-trial conduct was objecting to the *refusal* of his own instruction, which waives nothing. **Silver Key:** when two choices share the correct outcome, the one resting on an invented waiver loses to the one whose reasoning matches the record (C).

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning (lawyer-confirmation layer)

**Tested rule (unchanged from source):** Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 51 governs requests for, and objections to, jury instructions, and when an instruction error is preserved for appeal.

- **FRCP 51(b)(2)** requires the court to inform the parties of its proposed instructions and to give them an opportunity to object on the record and out of the jury's hearing before instructions and arguments.
- **FRCP 51(c)(1)** requires a party to object on the record, stating distinctly the matter objected to and the grounds.
- **FRCP 51(c)(2)** fixes timeliness: an objection is timely if **(A)** the party objects at the opportunity provided under 51(b)(2); **or (B)** the party "was not informed of an instruction or action on a request before that opportunity to object, and the party objects promptly after learning that the instruction or request will be, or has been, given or refused." Because the court *modified* its previewed instruction, Stephen falls squarely within 51(c)(2)(B): he had no earlier opportunity to object to the version actually delivered, and he objected promptly upon learning of it.
- **FRCP 51(d)(1)(A)** allows a party to assign error to an instruction "actually given" if the party properly objected. Stephen did.
- **FRCP 51(d)(2)** supplies *plain error* review for an instruction error not properly preserved that affects substantial rights — the correct fallback standard, and the reason choice B's "clearly erroneous" label is wrong even on its own terms.
- **FRCP 52(a)(6)** is where "clearly erroneous" actually lives — review of a court's findings of *fact* — confirming that choice B has imported the wrong standard.

**Credited outcome confirmed:** the objection was timely under Rule 51(c)(2)(B); the issue is preserved; the appellate court will rule on the merits. Research does not contradict the supplied official key. **Verdict: PASS.**
