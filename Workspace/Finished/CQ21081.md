### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Felony murder does not stop at the instant the robber crosses the store threshold. The felony-murder time window includes the immediate flight phase until the robber reaches a place of temporary safety. Martha was still in the alley, being chased, about forty seconds after the robbery, with no car, building, hiding place, or unchallenged control of the stolen property. The killing was committed to avoid capture during the escape from the robbery.

**Gold Key:** For felony murder, the endpoint is not "left the building." The endpoint is temporary safety: successful escape, no chase, and a place where the felon has a real break from the crime scene.

**Silver Key:** When the answer pair fights over whether the felony was "over," do not look at the doorway. Look at the safety checkpoint: still being chased, seconds away, no hiding place, no car, no pause. That points to felony murder.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A is attractive** because Stephen was not the bookstore cashier, clerk, owner, or customer who was directly robbed. The breaker is that felony murder does not require the person killed to be the direct victim of the predicate felony. The tested connection is between the killing and the robbery enterprise, including escape. Stephen was killed while pursuing Martha in the immediate aftermath of the armed robbery, so his status as a neighboring delivery driver does not remove the killing from felony murder.

**C is attractive** because the conclusion is right - Martha is guilty - and the shooting sounds like a separate serious felony. The breaker is the reason. **Gold Key:** felony murder is already supplied by the armed robbery and its immediate flight. Using the assaultive shooting itself as the felony-murder predicate points to the wrong engine and raises merger concerns because the assaultive act is the homicide act itself.

**D is attractive** because Martha had physically left the bookstore, so the robbery feels complete in ordinary speech. The breaker is the legal endpoint. **Silver Key:** the doorway is not the checkpoint; temporary safety is. Martha was still being chased in the alley seconds after the robbery and had not reached safety, so the escape phase was still inside the felony-murder window.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged. Felony murder can apply to a killing committed during the commission of a qualifying felony, including the immediate flight from that felony before the perpetrator reaches a place of temporary safety. Robbery is a classic felony-murder predicate. Model Penal Code section 210.2(1)(b) recognizes murder liability where the actor is engaged in the commission of, attempt to commit, or "flight after committing or attempting to commit" robbery. See https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/210_2.htm. The escape-rule formulation is also reflected in felony-murder cases such as *People v. Wilkins*, 56 Cal.4th 333 (2013), which discusses the rule that a felony continues for felony-murder purposes until the perpetrator reaches a place of temporary safety. See https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/people-v-wilkins-34208. Current CALCRIM felony-murder instructions separately require the underlying felony and the fatal act to be connected in time and purpose. See CALCRIM No. 540A, https://www.justia.com/criminal/docs/calcrim/500/540a/.

On these facts, Martha killed Stephen forty seconds after the robbery, while actively fleeing and being chased, before reaching any temporary safety. That is inside the felony-murder window. The direct-victim argument fails because felony murder does not require the deceased person to be the robbery victim. The independent-shooting-predicate answer fails because the original robbery, not the assaultive homicide act itself, supplies the predicate.
