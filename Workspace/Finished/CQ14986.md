## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Ownership, Rights and duties of co-tenants, and the verified outline code is `82010303`. The credited result remains unchanged: the court should grant partition and order an accounting.

The common-law cotenancy rule is that each tenant in common has an equal right to possess the whole, and one cotenant's ordinary possession does not exclude the other. Zaslow v. Kroenert, 29 Cal.2d 541 (1946), explains that each tenant in common may share possession of the entire property and that ouster requires adverse acts such as claiming the whole, denying the other cotenant's title, or refusing entry. See https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/zaslow-v-kroenert-26018. A modern statutory codification states the same adverse-possession point: possession by a cotenant is not adverse until actual or constructive ouster, and a tenant in common claiming adverse possession must prove ouster or constructive ouster. See N.C. Gen. Stat. Section 41-89, https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByArticle/Chapter_41/Article_7.html.

The accounting piece also checks. Dabney-Johnston Oil Corp. v. Walden, 4 Cal.2d 637 (1935), recognizes the ordinary surface-estate rule that a cotenant who receives rents from third persons for use of the land must account to the other cotenants for their shares. See https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/dabney-johnston-oil-corp-v-walden-24648. McWhorter v. McWhorter, 99 Cal.App. 293 (1929), distinguishes rent for the occupying cotenant's own occupancy from rents collected from third-party tenants and confirms the third-party-rent accounting rule. See https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/591477d3add7b049343da651. Modern cotenant statutes likewise provide reimbursement or credits for common charges such as taxes and necessary repairs in the accounting setting. See N.C. Gen. Stat. Section 41-86, https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByArticle/Chapter_41/Article_7.html. These authorities support the official key: Mary gets partition, and the court must account for Paul's rent receipts and carrying-cost payments.
