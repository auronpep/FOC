### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam-Register)

**C is correct.** The exclusionary rule exists to deter police officers from violating the Fourth Amendment. Under the fruit of the poisonous tree doctrine, information obtained as a result of an unlawful search may not be used to justify the issuance of a warrant authorizing a further search. Here, the deputy's initial entry into Daniel's workshop was unlawful — the landlord's consent to enter does not validate a warrantless search by police of a tenant's separate living/working space without exigent circumstances or a warrant. The deputy observed the table saw during that unlawful entry. He then used that observation as the basis for his warrant affidavit. The warrant was therefore tainted — it was the fruit of the poisonous tree. All evidence seized pursuant to that warrant, including the stolen power tools, must be suppressed.

### 9. Black-Letter Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing doctrine:** Exclusionary rule; fruit of the poisonous tree doctrine; Fourth Amendment.

**Rule:** The exclusionary rule prohibits the use of evidence obtained in violation of the Fourth Amendment. Under the fruit of the poisonous tree doctrine, not only must illegally obtained evidence be excluded, but also any evidence derived from the initial illegality. *Wong Sun v. United States*, 371 U.S. 471 (1963). A search warrant based on information obtained during an unlawful search is itself tainted, and all evidence seized pursuant to that warrant is inadmissible. *Silverthorne Lumber Co. v. United States*, 251 U.S. 385 (1920). The purpose of the exclusionary rule is to deter police from violating the Fourth Amendment. *Mapp v. Ohio*, 367 U.S. 643 (1962).

**Application:** The deputy entered Daniel's workshop without a warrant, without exigent circumstances, and without valid consent from anyone with authority to consent to a police search of Daniel's separate living/working space. The landlord's consent does not extend to a police search of a tenant's premises. The deputy's observation of the table saw during that unlawful entry was the basis for the warrant affidavit. The warrant was therefore fruit of the poisonous tree. The stolen power tools found during the warrant search must be suppressed.
