7) Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

Under Rule 701, a non-expert witness may give opinion testimony that is rationally based on the witness’s perception and helpful to understanding the testimony (e.g., "the line appeared frayed"). However, a conclusion that the line failed due to internal metal fatigue implicates specialized engineering knowledge and falls within Rule 702’s expert domain. The court should therefore admit Ezra’s perception-based observation but exclude any expert-style causation opinion unless Ezra is qualified as an expert.

8) Full wrong-answer explanations

B (Admit both). Attractive move (dominant trap): treats any observed fact as permitting causal conclusions by the observer. Breaker: FRE 701 permits perception-based opinions but channels scientifically or technically grounded causation opinions to Rule 702; without engineering qualification, the metal-fatigue opinion is inadmissible.

C (Exclude both). Attractive move: over-strings the expert requirement. Breaker: ordinary visual observations (appearance of a frayed line) are within lay perception and admissible under FRE 701; excluding them merely because the witness lacks engineering credentials is incorrect.

D (Exclude broken-line, admit fatigue). Attractive move: defers causation to jury. Breaker: the jury decides causation only after admissible evidence; expertized causation testimony that requires specialized methods is gatekept under Rule 702, and a lay witness cannot be allowed to offer technical causation without qualification.

Black-letter rule (student-facing): Lay opinion based on perception is admissible (FRE 701); specialized causation opinions require expert qualification and reliability under FRE 702.
