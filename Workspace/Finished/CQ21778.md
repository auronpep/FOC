## Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

The correct answer is **C**.

The original document rule (best evidence rule) requires that to prove the contents of a writing, the proponent must produce the original unless a qualifying excuse for non-production is shown. Here, Barnabas offers a photocopy of the pledge and asserts facts showing the original is in his control (via the mission team) but located in rural Honduras. Retrieving it would require an unplanned international trip that is not practical before trial. These facts support an excuse under the rule: the original cannot be obtained by any available judicial process (or is treated as practically unavailable). A photocopy is therefore admissible.

## Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested black-letter rule is the original document rule (best evidence rule), codified in Federal Rules of Evidence 1002–1004.

FRE 1002: "An original writing, recording, or photograph is required in order to prove its content unless these rules or a federal statute provides otherwise."

FRE 1003: Duplicates are admissible to the same extent as the original unless a genuine question is raised about the original’s authenticity or the circumstances make it unfair to admit the duplicate.

FRE 1004: An original is not required and other evidence of the content is admissible if:
- (a) all the originals are lost or destroyed, and not by the proponent acting in bad faith;
- (b) an original cannot be obtained by any available judicial process;
- (c) the party against whom the original would be offered had control of the original, was at that time put on notice (by pleadings or otherwise) that the original would be a subject of proof at the trial or hearing, and fails to produce it at the trial or hearing; or
- (d) the writing, recording, or photograph is not closely related to a controlling issue.
