### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. The President's pardon can affect Lydia's federal criminal exposure for bribery, but it does not cancel the House impeachment or shut down the Senate trial. Gold Key: the Pardon Clause gives the President power over offenses against the United States "except in Cases of Impeachment," so a criminal pardon does not reach impeachment consequences. Silver Key: split the fact pattern into two tracks before reading the answers: federal criminal punishment is one track, and impeachment removal or disqualification is the other. The pardon may matter on the criminal track, but the Senate may still proceed on the impeachment track.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because it treats bribery as a criminal matter and tries to make a criminal indictment the gateway to Senate action. The breaker is that impeachment is a constitutional process, not a criminal prosecution, so the Senate trial does not depend on prosecutors first indicting Lydia.

C is attractive because "full pardon" sounds complete and the President's announcement says every consequence is over. The breaker is the constitutional exception itself: Gold Key: presidential pardons do not apply in cases of impeachment, so the President cannot use a pardon to terminate impeachment consequences. Silver Key: do not let a broad pardon phrase merge the two tracks; criminal forgiveness is not impeachment immunity.

D is attractive because it tries to preserve House control over impeachment by requiring a fresh House vote after the pardon. The breaker is that the pardon does not erase the House's existing impeachment, so no new articles are needed before the Senate can try the impeachment already voted by the House.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue is Constitutional Law, Separation of Powers, Executive Branch, Pardon, and the verified outline code is `42010206`. The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row: a presidential pardon may forgive federal criminal offenses, but the pardon power does not apply to impeachment consequences.

The governing authority is the U.S. Constitution. Article II, Section 2, Clause 1 gives the President power to grant reprieves and pardons for offenses against the United States, except in cases of impeachment. Article I, Section 3, Clause 7 separately provides that impeachment judgment is limited to removal and possible disqualification, while the convicted party remains subject to ordinary criminal indictment, trial, judgment, and punishment. That structure confirms the two-track answer: a pardon can reach federal criminal liability, but it cannot stop or undo impeachment proceedings. Research checks: Constitution Annotated's pardon-power discussion was checked at `https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution-conan/article-2/section-2/clause-1/scope-of-the-pardon-power`; Constitution Annotated's impeachment-judgment discussion was checked at `https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution-conan/article-1/section-3/clause-7/overview-of-impeachment-judgments`.
