## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. Daniel's $40 repayment portion is not larceny because he took money he honestly claimed as repayment of the cash debt. Gold Key: claim of right can defeat the felonious intent needed for larceny when the defendant claims the money itself, but it does not let him take substitute cash for a different nonfungible item. The $40 Daniel took as payment for the leather Bible cover is larceny because he did not take back the cover; he took Martha's cash instead. Silver Key: split the cash by legal claim before applying the value threshold. Only $40 counts as larceny, so the offense is one misdemeanor.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The credited outcome is unchanged: Daniel is guilty of one misdemeanor only. Common-law larceny requires a trespassory taking and carrying away of the personal property of another with felonious intent. Carter v. United States, 530 U.S. 255 (2000), quoting Blackstone, describes common-law larceny as the felonious taking and carrying away of another's personal goods. The Department of Justice's Criminal Resource Manual section 1006 likewise lists wrongful taking and carrying away plus intent to deprive as larceny elements. Claim-of-right doctrine negates felonious intent when the accused honestly claims a legal right to the property or money taken; current claim-of-right summaries such as the NSW Judicial Commission benchbook also distinguish claims to property or money from overbroad claims that exceed the entitlement.

Daniel's first $40 is treated as a claim-of-right recovery of the money debt. That portion is not larceny. Daniel's second $40 is different. The leather Bible cover is a specific nonfungible item; taking cash of equal value from Martha's envelope is not reclaiming the cover itself. That $40 is the property of another and is taken with intent to permanently deprive. Because the larcenous portion is $50 or less, the statute grades it as a misdemeanor, not a felony.

Sources checked: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-5716.ZD.html, https://www.justice.gov/archives/jm/criminal-resource-manual-1006-larceny, and https://www.judcom.nsw.gov.au/publications/benchbks/criminal/larceny.html.
