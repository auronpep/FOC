## 1. Final question — stem + call

Paul owned in fee simple two adjoining waterfront parcels at a busy seaport, known as the North Wharf and the South Wharf. He ran his shipping trade and kept his living quarters in the warehouse on the North Wharf. Later he built a warehouse on the South Wharf and sold that parcel — warehouse and land — to Mary. Consistent with the contract of sale and purchase, the deed conveying the South Wharf from Paul to Mary contained the following clause:

> In the event Mary, her heirs or assigns, decide to sell the property hereby conveyed and obtain a purchaser ready, willing, and able to purchase the South Wharf and the improvements thereon on terms and conditions acceptable to Mary, said South Wharf and improvements shall be offered to Paul, his heirs or assigns, on the same terms and conditions. Paul, his heirs or assigns, as the case may be, shall have fourteen days from said offer to accept said offer and thereby to exercise said option.

Four years after delivery and recording of the deed and payment of the purchase price, Mary's trade declined and she relocated to a distant port. Mary's son orally offered to purchase the premises from Mary at its then fair market value. Mary declined her son's offer but instead deeded the South Wharf to her son, Stephen, as a gift. Immediately thereafter, Stephen sold the South Wharf to Barnabas at the then fair market value of the South Wharf. The sale was completed by the delivery of deed and payment of the purchase price. At no time did Mary or Stephen offer to sell the South Wharf to Paul. Paul learned of the conveyance to Stephen and the sale by Stephen to Barnabas one week after the conveyance of the South Wharf from Stephen to Barnabas. Paul promptly brought an appropriate action against Barnabas to enforce rights created in him by the deed of Paul to Mary. Paul tendered the amount paid by Barnabas into the court for whatever disposition the court deemed proper. The common law Rule Against Perpetuities is unmodified by statute.

**Call:** Which of the following will determine whether Paul will prevail?

Which body of law determines whether the seller can enforce the right of first refusal?

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

The deed clause is a right of first refusal — a preemptive option — running to Paul "and his heirs or assigns" with no time limit. The question is which body of law decides whether such an interest is enforceable. **Gold Key:** a right of first refusal or option to purchase held as a personal contract/covenant right (not a presently vested estate or the grantor's reversion) IS subject to the common-law Rule Against Perpetuities; one running to "heirs or assigns" with no time limit could be exercised far beyond any life in being plus twenty-one years, so its validity is governed — and here defeated — by the Rule Against Perpetuities. That is the single doctrine that determines the outcome, so **C** is correct. **Silver Key:** when the call asks which doctrine "determines" the result and the choices bundle doctrines, pick the choice naming all and only the dispositive doctrine — RAP standing alone — and reject any choice that staples a true-but-inactive doctrine onto it.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

- **A (Statute of Frauds + recording statute) — dominant trap.** The gift-to-Stephen-then-sale-to-Barnabas chain reads exactly like a recording-act / bona-fide-purchaser race, and the writing requirements make the Statute of Frauds feel implicated, so a recording analysis looks decisive. The breaker: Stephen took by **gift**, so he was never a bona-fide purchaser and could pass no shelter to Barnabas, and Paul's right of first refusal sat in a **recorded** deed — recording priority decides nothing here. **Silver Key:** a donee is never a BFP, so once you spot a gift in the chain, cut every choice that turns on the recording act.
- **B (parol evidence rule).** A deed with a negotiated clause invites the instinct that some side-agreement dispute is lurking. The breaker: no one is trying to add or vary a term outside the writing — the clause is right there in the deed — so the parol evidence rule has nothing to operate on.
- **D (Statute of Frauds + Rule Against Perpetuities).** This one correctly spots RAP, then adds the Statute of Frauds because Stephen's offer to Mary was made "orally." The breaker: that oral offer was declined and nothing oral is being enforced — Mary conveyed by a written gift deed and Barnabas bought by written deed — so the Statute of Frauds never enters; the choice is over-inclusive. **Gold Key:** the only doctrine that determines the outcome is RAP, which is why bundling SoF with it is wrong.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification + legal reasoning for the right answer

**Tested rule (unchanged from original):** At common law the Rule Against Perpetuities applies to options to purchase land and to rights of first refusal that are held as mere contract/covenant rights in a transferee, because such an interest may vest (be exercised) beyond lives in being plus twenty-one years. An option/ROFR with no durational limit that runs to "heirs and assigns" violates the Rule and is void. (Distinguish the grantor's classic *retained* reversionary interests — reversions, possibilities of reverter, rights of entry — which are RAP-exempt; the exam point is that a preemptive option is *not* such a retained estate.)

**Affirmative reasoning:** Paul's interest is a preemptive option created by deed, exercisable by "Paul, his heirs or assigns" whenever any future owner decides to sell — potentially centuries hence. It is contingent (it vests only if and when a future owner elects to sell) and is not limited to any measuring life or to 21 years. It therefore offends the common-law Rule and is void, and whether Paul prevails is governed by the Rule Against Perpetuities — making **C** the body of law that determines the result. The Statute of Frauds (no oral land contract is being enforced), the parol evidence rule (no extrinsic-term dispute), and the recording act (the donee was not a BFP and the interest was recorded) are all inert.

**Authority (lawyer-confirmation layer only):** common-law Rule Against Perpetuities; Restatement (First) of Property §§ 393, 413 (options to purchase subject to the Rule); *Symphony Space, Inc. v. Pergola Properties, Inc.*, 88 N.Y.2d 466 (1996) (option to purchase real property held void under the Rule Against Perpetuities). Doctrinal-currency note: many jurisdictions have since reformed RAP (wait-and-see / USRAP) and some exempt commercial options or apply a reasonableness rule to rights of first refusal — but the stem expressly fixes the **common-law Rule unmodified by statute**, so the classic rule controls and the credited outcome is unchanged. `attorney_review: false` for the rule statement (rule + Restatement section + leading case all real and on point).
