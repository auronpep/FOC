### 1. Final question
Police had probable cause to arrest Daniel for a string of armed robberies at small shops, including a Christian bookstore. They obtained an arrest warrant. At 5:45 a.m., officers quietly entered Daniel's house, moved to his bedroom with weapons drawn, and woke him. Daniel asked what was happening. One officer said, "This is over." Another immediately asked whether Daniel had robbed the bookstore, and Daniel said yes. Only after that did the officers tell Daniel he was under arrest and order him to dress.

Charged with robbery, Daniel moves to suppress the statement. What is Daniel's best argument?

### 2. Distilled Core Question
Police with an arrest warrant wake a suspect at home, question him before warnings, and get an admission; what is the best suppression argument?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation
Choice C is the strongest argument. Daniel was awakened by armed officers and immediately questioned about the robbery before warnings, so the admission came from custodial interrogation. Silver Key: Separate the entry issue from the statement issue before choosing the best suppression argument. Gold Key: Custody for Miranda can exist inside a suspect home when officers restrain freedom and ask incriminating questions before warnings.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
- A: Voluntariness is attractive because the scene is intimidating. The breaker is the best-argument call: the cleaner defect is unwarned custodial interrogation, not a harder due-process coercion claim.
- B: The search-warrant answer feels powerful because officers entered a home. The breaker is the arrest warrant: for the suspect's own home, the entry problem is not the best attack here. Silver Key: separate entry from statement suppression.
- D: The timing of the formal arrest announcement looks important because it came after the admission. The breaker is that Miranda turns on custody in substance, not magic words announcing arrest.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The prosecution may not use a statement from custodial interrogation unless Miranda warnings precede questioning. Custody can exist in a bedroom at home when officers restrain freedom and ask incriminating questions. An arrest warrant can authorize entry into the suspect's home when there is reason to believe the suspect is within, so the best attack is the unwarned interrogation. Authority: Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966); Orozco v. Texas, 394 U.S. 324 (1969); Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573 (1980). The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged.
