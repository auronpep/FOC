### Full Right-Answer Explanation
The correct answer is C. Voluntary manslaughter requires four elements: (1) adequate provocation, (2) actual provocation, (3) no cooling-off period, and (4) a causal connection. The critical element absent here is adequate provocation. At common law, verbal insults, mockery, and verbal abuse — even when sustained over a long period — are not legally sufficient to constitute adequate provocation. To reduce murder to voluntary manslaughter, the provocation must be of a type that would cause an *objective* reasonable person to lose self-control. Mere words do not meet this standard.

### Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
A: This choice incorrectly focuses on a purely subjective standard. Voluntary manslaughter provocation is not determined by whether this *specific* defendant was provoked to a heat of passion; it requires an *objective* test of whether a reasonable person would have been so provoked.
B: This choice (the dominant trap) is attractive because it highlights the sustained nature of Stephen's harassment. However, the rule of law remains that even sustained verbal harassment does not reach the level of adequate provocation needed to satisfy the objective reasonable person standard.
D: This choice attempts to use premeditation as the justification for murder. While retrieving a weapon from a truck may indeed demonstrate premeditation, it is not the *only* reason for the classification. The primary legal failure for manslaughter here is the lack of adequate provocation, making D less precise than C.

### Full Black-Letter Law Verification
Voluntary manslaughter requires adequate provocation that would cause a reasonable person to lose self-control; the standard is objective (reasonable person), not purely subjective; minor insults or petty slights are legally insufficient (e.g., *State v. Gounagias*, 88 Wash. 304 (1915)).
