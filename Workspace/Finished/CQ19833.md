# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

After a jury convicted Timothy of felony arson for burning a storage shed at a private Christian summer camp, his lawyer withdrew before sentencing. When the sentencing hearing began, Timothy appeared alone. The judge did not ask for, and Timothy did not give, any waiver of counsel. After the prosecutor argued aggravating facts about the fire, the judge imposed the statutory maximum sentence. Timothy challenges the sentence. What is the best Sixth Amendment answer?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A convicted defendant appears at sentencing without counsel and without a waiver. Does the Sixth Amendment give him a right to counsel at sentencing?

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.** Timothy was at sentencing, his lawyer had withdrawn, and he did not waive counsel. **Gold Key:** sentencing is a critical stage of a criminal prosecution, so the defendant has the Sixth Amendment right to counsel there unless the right is validly waived. **Silver Key:** lock the stage before judging the sentence. This is a counsel-at-sentencing question, not a verdict-finality question or a statutory-range question.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells a clean endpoint: the jury returned a guilty verdict, so counsel must be finished. The breaker is the Gold Key: sentencing is still a critical stage.

**B.** This choice uses a true comfort fact: Timothy had counsel during trial. The breaker is stage-specific: representation at one critical stage does not answer the later sentencing-stage counsel problem.

**C.** This is the dominant trap. It notices the statutory maximum and aggravating-fact language and shifts into a sentence-authority frame. **Gold Key:** sentencing itself is a critical stage requiring counsel unless validly waived. **Silver Key:** sentence length does not answer whether Timothy had counsel at that stage.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The source row is Q19833, a Criminal Procedure / Sixth Amendment item keyed to the defendant having a right to counsel at sentencing because sentencing is a critical stage and no waiver occurred. The verified outline code is **75090400**, “Sixth Amendment—Other Rights of the Accused > Right to Counsel.”

The Sixth Amendment supplies the criminal accused's right to assistance of counsel. In *Mempa v. Rhay*, the Supreme Court held that counsel must be afforded in a post-trial proceeding involving probation revocation and deferred sentencing, and the syllabus states that “the time of sentencing is a critical stage in a criminal case.” [Mempa v. Rhay, 389 U.S. 128 (1967)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/389/128/). Constitution Annotated likewise describes the Sixth Amendment right as attaching after adversary judicial proceedings begin and applying at critical stages of criminal prosecutions. [U.S. Constitution Annotated, Amendment VI](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution-conan/amendment-6/overview-of-when-the-right-to-counsel-applies).

The source row includes aggravating facts and a statutory maximum, so the doctrinal-currency hotlist was checked. *Erlinger v. United States* confirms the Apprendi line for facts that increase a defendant's prescribed sentencing range, but this item does not turn on an increased range; the sentence stayed within the statutory maximum. [Erlinger v. United States, 602 U.S. ___ (2024)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/602/23-370/). The tested issue and credited outcome remain unchanged: Timothy had a Sixth Amendment right to counsel at sentencing, and no valid waiver appears.
