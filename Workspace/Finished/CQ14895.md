# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

Daniel was charged with defrauding the federal disaster-relief office where he worked as a grants clerk. At trial, the court allowed Daniel to call Naomi, the manager of a Christian publishing warehouse where Daniel had previously worked. Naomi testified that Daniel had a good reputation in the community for honesty.

Over objection, Daniel then sought to have Naomi testify that, during several large Bible-conference weekends, the warehouse had entrusted Daniel with locked cash boxes containing substantial conference receipts and that no money was ever missing.

Should Naomi’s testimony about the locked cash boxes be admitted?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A criminal fraud defendant first offers reputation evidence for honesty, then tries to prove honesty through specific past incidents of being trusted with money. Is the specific-acts testimony admissible?

## 3. Final answer choices

A. Yes, because honesty is a pertinent character trait offered by an accused.

B. Yes, because the testimony tends to show that Daniel was unlikely to have taken money as charged.

C. No, because the testimony is extrinsic evidence on a collateral matter.

D. No, because good character cannot be proved by specific instances of conduct unless character is an essential element of the charge or defense.

## 5. Correct answer

**D**. Original key: **B**.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.** Daniel may offer evidence of a pertinent good character trait, and honesty is pertinent in a fraud prosecution. But the form matters. On direct examination, when character evidence is admissible, it is ordinarily proved through reputation or opinion testimony. Specific instances of conduct are permitted only when the character trait is an essential element of the charge, claim, or defense. Here, Daniel’s honesty is circumstantial character evidence, not an element of fraud or a defense. Naomi could testify to reputation or opinion, but not to particular times Daniel was trusted with cash boxes.

**Gold Key:** Character doorway is not character method. A criminal defendant may open the character door, but direct proof still travels through reputation or opinion unless character is itself an essential element.

**Silver Key:** When the choices say “Yes, because pertinent” and “No, because specific acts,” do not stop at admissibility. Move to **method of proof**.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells the strongest surface fact for the defense: honesty is a pertinent character trait in a fraud case, and a criminal defendant may offer a pertinent trait. That gets Daniel through the first doorway. It does not answer the second doorway: how the trait may be proved. **Gold Key:** character doorway is not character method. The testimony is barred because it uses specific incidents to prove the trait.

**B.** This choice sounds practical because the cash-box history would make Daniel look unlikely to steal. That is ordinary relevance thinking. The Evidence issue is narrower: character evidence has special form limits. Relevant character evidence can still be inadmissible when the offered form is wrong.

**C.** This reaches the same “No” result, but it points to the wrong exclusion reason. The problem is not a collateral-matter/extrinsic-evidence impeachment rule. Naomi is being asked on direct examination to prove Daniel’s good character through specific incidents. The correct reason is the Rule 405 method limit.

## 9. Black-letter verification and legal reasoning

Federal Rule of Evidence 404(a)(2)(A) allows a criminal defendant to offer evidence of the defendant’s pertinent trait; Cornell’s rule text reflects that exception. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) Federal Rule of Evidence 405(a) provides that when character evidence is admissible, it may be proved by reputation or opinion testimony; Rule 405(b) permits specific instances only when the character trait is an essential element of a charge, claim, or defense. ([Legal Information Institute][2])

The credited outcome is unchanged. Daniel’s evidence is not excluded because honesty is irrelevant. It is excluded because he tries to prove honesty by specific past conduct, and character is not an essential element in the fraud prosecution.

## 10. Rigor/difficulty note

The variation preserves difficulty because the trap remains two-layered: the defendant has a valid character-evidence doorway, but the answer turns on the form of proof. The dominant trap remains the attractive “pertinent trait offered by accused” answer.
