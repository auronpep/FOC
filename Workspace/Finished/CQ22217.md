## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam-Register)

**C is correct.** Under FRE 803(18), once an expert witness (or judicial notice) establishes that a published treatise, periodical, or pamphlet is a reliable authority in the field, statements from the work may be read into evidence as substantive proof — but only by being read aloud. The document itself is never admitted as an exhibit. Here, the building inspector identified the structural-engineering manual as a reliable authority on direct examination. The inspector may therefore read relevant passages from the manual to the jury, and those passages will be admissible to prove what they assert about proper building techniques.

**Gold Key (GK-EVI-LEARNT-1):** The learned-treatise exception is a *read-only* rule — the jury hears the content, but the document never goes to the jury room and is never marked as an exhibit.

## 9. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing rule:** Federal Rule of Evidence 803(18) — "Statements in Learned Treatises, Periodicals, or Pamphlets."

**Rule text (LII / Cornell Law Institute):** To be admissible under this exception, the statements must be in a published treatise, periodical, or pamphlet on a subject of history, medicine, or other science or art, *established as a reliable authority* by the testimony or admission of the witness or by expert testimony or by judicial notice. If admitted, the statements *may be read into evidence but may not be received as an exhibit*.

**Affirmative reasoning for credited answer (C / original B):** The expert identified the manual as a reliable authority during direct testimony — satisfying the foundational requirement of FRE 803(18). The rule then expressly permits reading relevant passages aloud, which is exactly what answer C describes.

**Distractor audit:**
- A (jury takes to room): Violates the "may not be received as an exhibit" bar — a flat misstatement of the rule.
- B (not in any manner): An overclaim — the rule permits reading passages, so an absolute ban is fabricated.
- D (given to jury in judge's presence): Not authorized by the rule in any setting — flat misstatement.

**Legal authority:** FRE 803(18) (Federal Rules of Evidence); LII, Cornell Law Institute, [Rule 803. Exceptions to the Rule Against Hearsay](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803).
