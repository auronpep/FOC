### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are preserved. The source asks what happens when a murder defendant honestly but unreasonably believes deadly force is necessary. The transformed version asks the same question using different surface facts.

Black-letter verification: Complete self-defense requires an actual and reasonable belief in imminent death or serious bodily harm and reasonable use of force. Imperfect self-defense is not a complete defense; it negates malice or mitigates murder, producing voluntary manslaughter rather than acquittal when the belief is genuine but unreasonable.

Authority floor: State v. Faulkner, 301 Md. 482, 483 A.2d 759 (Md. 1984), explains that perfect self-defense results in acquittal, while imperfect self-defense mitigates murder to voluntary manslaughter because it is not a complete defense. Source: https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/state-v-faulkner-no-893582852. CALCRIM No. 571 states the same operating distinction for voluntary manslaughter based on imperfect self-defense and identifies People v. Flannel, 25 Cal.3d 668 (1979), as defining authority. Source: https://www.justia.com/criminal/docs/calcrim/500/571/. People v. Flannel is available at https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/25/668.html.

Application: Peter intentionally used deadly force, so "no crime" is unavailable unless self-defense is complete. The stem tells us the belief was genuine but unreasonable. Under the majority imperfect-self-defense framing supplied by the call, that unreasonable belief prevents complete justification but mitigates murder to voluntary manslaughter.
