At a Christian youth retreat, Daniel browsed Lydia's private vendor table of engraved brass bookmark sets. Daniel decided to take one set without paying for it. He slipped the boxed set into his canvas tote and took three steps toward the side doors of the vendor hall. He then noticed a small inventory sensor on the box and worried it would trigger an alarm. ## 2. Distilled Core Question

A person hides another's merchandise, moves it a few steps with intent to steal it, then returns it before leaving. Is the crime completed or only attempted?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

D. only attempted larceny, because he meant to carry the bookmark set away but put it back before leaving.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is the best answer. Daniel did the legally complete act before he changed his mind: he took Lydia's boxed bookmark set, concealed it in his tote, and moved it several steps while intending to steal it. Gold Key: larceny does not require getting outside the building; a slight carrying away with intent to steal is enough. Silver Key: lock the timeline before reading the remorse facts. If the completed taking happened first, the later return does not downgrade the offense.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A sells the mercy instinct: Daniel repented quickly and returned the property before anyone confronted him. The breaker is timing. His later return came after the taking and carrying away had already occurred with intent to steal, so it does not erase the completed larceny.

C sells the exit myth: because Daniel never crossed the vendor-hall doors, it feels like the theft never matured. The breaker is the Gold Key. Larceny requires taking and carrying away, not a successful escape from the premises.

D is attractive because Daniel did not finish the ordinary story arc of a shoplifting theft. The breaker is the Silver Key: attempt is the backup label only when the completed act never happens. Here the completed act happened when Daniel moved and concealed the set with intent to steal it.

## 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Common-law larceny requires a trespassory taking and carrying away of the personal property of another with intent to permanently deprive. Model Penal Code section 223.2(1) similarly treats theft of movable property as unlawfully taking or exercising unlawful control over another's movable property with purpose to deprive. The asportation requirement is slight: Craighead v. State, 126 Ga. App. 300, 190 S.E.2d 606 (1972), states that any unlawful asportation, however slight, can satisfy the taking element and that removal from the owner's premises is not required. Daniel's movement and concealment of Lydia's boxed set with intent to steal it complete the larceny. His later return does not make the offense no crime or only an attempt.
