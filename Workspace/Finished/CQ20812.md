### 1. Final question

At a Christian music gear swap held in a hotel ballroom, Agent Ruth, working undercover, approached Stephen and suggested that they jointly sell stolen sound mixers and guitar pedals through a "Psalm Night" online listing. Stephen agreed and sent Ruth a spreadsheet of the gear he could supply. Ruth never intended to complete any sale. The jurisdiction has enacted the Model Penal Code. Stephen is charged with conspiracy to sell stolen property. Under the MPC unilateral conspiracy rule, is Stephen guilty of conspiracy?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A defendant agrees with an undercover agent to sell stolen property and sends an inventory list, but the agent is only pretending; in an MPC jurisdiction, is the defendant guilty of conspiracy?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.**

**Gold Key:** MPC conspiracy is unilateral. The exam asks whether Stephen agreed and acted with the purpose of promoting the stolen-property sale, not whether Ruth secretly meant to go through with it.

**Silver Key:** Lock the jurisdiction first. The words "Model Penal Code" and "unilateral conspiracy rule" tell you not to compare two real conspirators; they tell you to test Stephen's own agreement and purpose.

Stephen agreed to the stolen-property plan and sent a spreadsheet of the gear he could supply. Ruth's hidden undercover intent does not save him under the MPC unilateral approach. The conspiracy analysis centers on Stephen's culpable agreement and purpose.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. No, because the MPC requires an overt act and Stephen did not take one.**

This choice attracts the student who remembers an overt-act requirement and tries to stop the analysis there. The breaker is visible in the stem: Stephen sent Ruth a spreadsheet of the gear he could supply, which is a planning act in furtherance of the agreement.

**B. Yes, but only because Stephen took the lead by sending the inventory sheet.**

This choice sells a status story: Stephen looks more culpable once he sends the inventory sheet, so the answer tries to make leadership the reason guilt attaches. The breaker is the word "only"; MPC guilt does not depend on who led, initiated, or looked more active once Stephen purposely agreed to the crime.

**C. No, because there was no genuine bilateral agreement.**

This is the dominant trap. It is attractive because ordinary conspiracy language sounds like it should require two real criminal minds.

**Gold Key:** Under the MPC unilateral rule, Ruth's feigned agreement does not defeat Stephen's conspiracy liability. The bilateral-agreement objection belongs to a different conspiracy rule, not this MPC call.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The credited outcome is legally sound. Model Penal Code section 5.03(1)(a) defines conspiracy through the actor who, with the purpose of promoting or facilitating a crime, agrees with another person that they will engage in conduct constituting the crime. The MPC formulation is unilateral: the defendant's culpable agreement and purpose can suffice even when the supposed partner is an undercover officer or decoy who is only pretending to agree. Model Penal Code section 5.03(5) also recognizes an overt-act requirement for many conspiracies, but preparatory conduct can satisfy it; here, sending the inventory spreadsheet is an act in furtherance.

Research check: the Lumen Criminal Law discussion of conspiracy explains the unilateral approach and states that a conspiracy may exist with a law-enforcement decoy who is pretending to agree; it also discusses MPC section 5.03(1)(a) and section 5.03(5). Source URL: https://courses.lumenlearning.com/suny-criminallaw/chapter/8-2-conspiracy/. The H2O Open Casebook page identifies the relevant Model Penal Code provision as MPC 5.03 Criminal Conspiracy. Source URL: https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/3185-tanaka-criminal-law-casebook/resources/10.3.2-mpc-503-criminal-conspiracy/.

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row: MPC unilateral conspiracy makes Stephen guilty despite Ruth's feigned agreement.
