## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Esther owns three small lakeside cottages that she rents to families attending private Bible retreats. In one written contract, she hired Daniel, a decorative mural artist, to paint three different Scripture-themed wall murals, one in the reading room of each cottage. The parties specified a separate price for each mural, and each mural was distinct from the other two. Daniel finished the first mural exactly as the contract required and demanded the specified price for that mural. At the same time, Daniel told Esther that he was "done with mural work" and would not even begin the other two murals. Esther refused to pay Daniel anything.

Is Daniel likely to prevail in a suit for the agreed-upon price of the first mural?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A contractor completes one separately priced part of a single multi-part contract, then refuses to perform the remaining parts. Can the contractor recover the agreed price for the completed part?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. Yes, because the contract is divisible, and Esther will be required to bring a separate claim for Daniel's failure to complete the other two murals.

B. Yes, because the contract is divisible, but Esther will be able to deduct any recoverable damages caused by Daniel's failure to complete the contract.

C. No, because Daniel's refusal to continue performance without legal excuse was a willful breach of the contract.

D. No, because substantial performance of the entire contract is a constructive condition to Esther's duty to pay at the contract rate.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. The contract separated Daniel's work into three distinct murals and assigned a specific price to each one. Daniel completed the first corresponding part, so he may recover the agreed price for that part. Gold Key: when a contract is divisible, full performance of one agreed part triggers the other side's duty to pay the agreed equivalent for that part. Silver Key: do not stop at "divisible." The best answer also accounts for the rest of the contract, so Esther may deduct or recover damages caused by Daniel's refusal to perform the other two murals.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because it spots the key word: divisible. It then adds a procedural requirement the rule does not impose. Silver Key: after the divisible-contract move, keep the remedy inside the same dispute if the answer gives you that option. Esther does not have to lose her damages or bring a separate lawsuit just because Daniel can recover for the completed mural.

C is attractive because Daniel's refusal sounds like the fact that should end the case against him. The breaker is that the question asks for the agreed price of the completed divisible part. Gold Key: a later breach on the unperformed parts does not erase the payor's duty to pay for a completed agreed equivalent, though it does leave damages for the breach.

D is attractive because substantial performance is a familiar construction-contract phrase. The breaker is the unit of performance. Daniel fully completed the first separately priced mural; the issue is divisibility of the contract, not whether he substantially performed the entire three-mural package before any payment could be owed.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested issue remains Contracts, Performance, Breach, and Discharge, with outline code `56050300`. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 240 states the controlling divisible-contract rule: when the parties' exchanged performances can be apportioned into corresponding agreed equivalents, performance of one part has the same effect on the other party's duty to render the agreed equivalent as if that pair had been separately promised. The American Law Institute identifies the Restatement (Second) of Contracts as an authoritative modern common-law contract source, and publicly available Restatement materials identify § 240 as the "Part Performances as Agreed Equivalents" rule. Research source URLs: https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-second/contracts and https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/2nd%2BSemester/LAW%2B506-002%2B%E2%80%93%2BContracts%2BII/R2C%2B%C2%A7%2B240.

The supplied official key is consistent with that rule. The original and transformed facts both create one contract divided into three distinct, separately priced units. The performing party completed one full unit and repudiated the rest. Under § 240, completion of that unit gives the performer the right to the agreed equivalent for that unit, while the nonbreaching party keeps ordinary contract remedies for damages caused by the failure to perform the remaining units. No doctrinal-currency hotlist item applies. Authority floor satisfied by Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 240; attorney_review: false.
