### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

**A**. The church committee is directly liable for negligent selection of an independent contractor. A principal who hires an independent contractor with knowledge of the contractor's incompetence, prior violations, or unsafe record may be directly liable for injuries caused by the contractor's negligent performance, even though respondeat superior does not apply because the contractor controlled the work. The committee's own selection decision, made with knowledge of the safety record, is the alleged breach. **Gold Key:** direct negligence in selection, supervision, or instruction remains available even when vicarious liability is not; the independent-contractor rule is not absolute.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**C. No possible liability because independent contractors always insulate principals.**

This is the dominant trap. The tiered-absolute form is seductive because respondeat superior does not apply to independent contractors. The breaker is the **Gold Key**: direct negligence in selection is a separate route that the independent-contractor rule does NOT bar. Visible from the stem: the committee knew of the contractor's safety record and hired him anyway — facts that support negligent selection, not respondeat superior.

**B. Strict liability for every public-event injury.**

The strict-liability overclaim. The student imports a strict-liability framework into a negligence call. The breaker is that strict liability for abnormally dangerous activities and for products has narrow doctrinal triggers; firing fireworks at a church festival is neither. The call is based on negligent selection, not on a strict-liability theory.

**D. Automatic respondeat superior because all contractors are employees.**

The flat-misstatement trap. The student treats respondeat superior as automatic. The breaker is that the stem says the contractor controlled the launch details, so respondeat superior does not apply; the principal is liable, if at all, on a direct-negligence theory.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The supplied row tests QID 19045, a Torts item on direct negligence in the selection of an independent contractor. The official key is A: direct negligence for hiring an obviously unsafe contractor.

A principal generally is not vicariously liable for the torts of an independent contractor, because the contractor controls the work and is not the principal's employee. Restatement (Second) of Agency § 220 enumerates the factors that distinguish a servant from an independent contractor; control over the manner of work is the central factor. The general rule, however, is not absolute. A principal may be directly liable for its own negligence in selecting, instructing, or supervising an independent contractor, particularly where the contractor's incompetence or unfitness was known or discoverable. Restatement (Second) of Torts § 411 ("An employer is subject to liability for bodily harm caused to a third person by the negligent physical employment of an existing contractor, if . . . the employer should have known that the contractor was, because of his lack of training or experience, or because of his known propensity to act dangerously, incompetent to do the work with reasonable safety"). The committee's knowledge of Stephen's safety record and suspended license supports a direct-negligence claim. The transformed outcome is identical: the committee is directly liable for negligent selection.
