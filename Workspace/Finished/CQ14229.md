## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** The city is not merely regulating how Daniel may use his bakery property. It is conditioning a building permit on Daniel's surrender of valuable floor space for a city-preferred public service. That makes the condition a land-use exaction. The city must show that the required homework room has an essential nexus to a legitimate land-use interest and is roughly proportional to the impact of Daniel's proposed expansion. A general assertion that the city lacks supervised homework space does not show that Daniel's bread-class addition creates an impact roughly proportional to the demanded give-back.

**Gold Key:** A permit exaction is not ordinary rational-basis zoning. When the government says "no permit unless you give land, money, floor space, or another public-serving concession," the government must satisfy the Nollan/Dolan exaction test.

**Silver Key:** In an answer set that offers rational basis, strict scrutiny, and rough proportionality, classify the government act first. A demanded give-back attached to a land-use permit belongs in the exaction lane.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A.** This choice is attractive because it reaches the right bottom-line result: the city's unsupported condition should fall. The breaker is the standard. Strict scrutiny is not the test for a land-use permit exaction; the city did not need to prove that the homework-room condition was necessary to serve a compelling interest. **Gold Key:** the exaction lane asks for essential nexus and rough proportionality, not compelling-interest necessity.

**C.** This is the dominant trap. Ordinary zoning and social-welfare land-use decisions often receive deferential rational-basis review, and the citywide shortage of supervised homework space sounds like a legitimate public problem. The breaker is the price of the permit. The city demanded an equal-floor-space give-back from this owner as a condition of expansion, so the city carries the exaction burden. **Gold Key:** do not let the word "zoning" pull a permit exaction back into ordinary rational basis.

**D.** This choice sounds demanding because it uses compelling-interest language, but it turns the burden in the wrong direction and keeps the condition constitutional. The breaker is twofold: strict scrutiny is not the governing test, and the owner does not have to disprove the city's exaction. **Silver Key:** once the city demands the concession, the decisive question is whether the city justified the fit between that concession and the proposed development's impact.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The governing constitutional provision is the Takings Clause of the Fifth Amendment, incorporated against the states through the Fourteenth Amendment. A government may condition a land-use permit on a concession only if the condition has an essential nexus to a legitimate land-use interest and is roughly proportional to the proposed development's impact.

In *Nollan v. California Coastal Commission*, 483 U.S. 825 (1987), the Court required an essential nexus between the permit condition and the governmental interest that would justify denial of the permit. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/483/825/

In *Dolan v. City of Tigard*, 512 U.S. 374 (1994), the Court held that the government must make an individualized determination showing rough proportionality between the demanded dedication and the development's projected impact, and that the burden rests on the city in that permit-exaction setting. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/512/374/

In *Koontz v. St. Johns River Water Management District*, 570 U.S. 595 (2013), the Court confirmed that Nollan/Dolan scrutiny applies to governmental demands made in the land-use permitting context, including money demands tied to a specific parcel. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/570/595/

Current-law check: *Sheetz v. County of El Dorado*, 601 U.S. ___ (2024), confirms that the Takings Clause does not exempt permit conditions merely because they were imposed by legislation rather than by an ad hoc administrative decision. The source item's zoning-ordinance authorization therefore does not defeat the Nollan/Dolan analysis. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/601/22-1074/

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The city conditioned a building permit on a substantial give-back unrelated to any proven project-specific impact. Because the city presented no evidence or findings showing rough proportionality between Daniel's proposed expansion and the demanded homework room, the condition is unconstitutional.
