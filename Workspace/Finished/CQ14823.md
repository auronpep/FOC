### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** Ruth was a witness, not the defendant. The prosecution's offer of Ruth's former co-worker is **extrinsic** evidence offered to prove specific instances of Ruth's conduct (fabricated inventory counts eighteen months ago) for the purpose of attacking Ruth's **character for truthfulness**. Under **Federal Rule of Evidence 608(b)**, extrinsic evidence is not admissible to prove specific instances of a witness's conduct in order to attack or support the witness's character for truthfulness. The cross-examiner is stuck with the witness's answer on cross. Because the witness has been excused from further attendance, the prosecution has no vehicle to offer this evidence at all.

**Gold Key:** When a character-for-truthfulness attack depends on extrinsic evidence of a specific past act, the attack dies under FRE 608(b), even if the act is something a jury would obviously want to hear about.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This is the dominant trap. It sells the reflex that any extrinsic evidence attacking a witness is barred by FRE 608(b). The breaker is that the $7,500 offer is not a truthfulness attack at all — it is bias, motive, or interest to lie. Bias is never collateral: extrinsic evidence of a witness's bias is admissible to discredit the witness. **Gold Key:** bias, motive, or interest to testify in a particular way is not a character-for-truthfulness attack; FRE 608(b) does not reach it.

**C.** This choice looks like a problem because "police officer / deputy testifying to reputation" can sound like a character attack on the defendant. It is the opposite. The defendant opened the door by calling Ruth to offer opinion testimony that the defendant is honest and trustworthy. Under **FRE 405(a)**, the prosecution may then call its own character witness to testify to the defendant's reputation for the opposite trait. The sheriff's deputy is the prosecution's rebuttal character witness, not a forbidden propensity attack. **Silver Key:** when the defendant opens the character door by opinion testimony, the prosecution may walk through it with reputation evidence of the opposing trait.

**D.** This choice is the cleanest of the four. The witness has been excused, but reputation evidence is not "the witness's own testimony." The neighbor's testimony is **opinion or reputation** evidence about a witness's character for truthfulness, which is admissible under **FRE 608(a)** without ever placing the witness back on the stand. The choir-member framing is window dressing, not a defect. **Gold Key:** reputation or opinion evidence about a witness's character for truthfulness is admissible under FRE 608(a).

### 2. The 17-section student case study

## program_frame

This is an Evidence classification question. The surface story is a coastal bakery embezzlement trial, but the call is narrower: **identify the one inadmissible rebuttal item among four**.

The work is to classify each of the four rebuttal items by the type of attack it makes, then apply the right rule. Three of the four are admissible; only one is barred.

## student_access_test

This item is not a hard structural solve. The decisive moves require Gold Keys.

A student who can read the four items can sort them into the four attack categories (specific-acts truthfulness attack, bias/motive, reputation for truthfulness, opened-door character rebuttal). The two items that are most often confused are the specific-acts item (B) and the bias item (A). The bias item looks like a specific-acts truthfulness attack because it mentions money for testimony, so a student may wrongly pick A as the barred choice. The specific-acts item is barred under FRE 608(b); the bias item is not within FRE 608(b) at all.

The final answer requires the FRE 608(b) Gold Key for B and the bias/motive Gold Key for the A trap.

After a character witness testifies by opinion that the defendant is honest, which of four rebuttal items is LEAST likely to be admissible — extrinsic evidence of the witness's bias, the witness's prior false acts, the defendant's reputation for dishonesty, or the witness's reputation for untruthfulness?

## choice_by_choice_walkthrough

### A

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The dollar amount and "for favorable testimony" framing is the tell. This is a witness's bias / motive / interest to testify in a particular way — not a character-for-truthfulness attack.
2. **Student label:** bias-pretending-as-truthfulness.
3. **True/responsive version:** "Testimony by Barnabas's former business partner that he overheard Ruth say she had been promised a free supply of bakery goods if her testimony helped Barnabas avoid conviction" — that is the bias frame.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Bias, prejudice, motive, or interest to testify in a particular way is treated as a separate, never-collateral form of impeachment. Extrinsic evidence of bias is admissible to discredit the witness, and FRE 608(b) does not reach it. (See United States v. Abel, 469 U.S. 45 (1984) on bias as never-collateral.)

### B

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The classic specific-acts-by-extrinsic-evidence structure. A third party testifies about a specific past lie. FRE 608(b) bars this.
2. **Student label:** true and responsive.
3. **True/responsive version:** This is the true and responsive version.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Federal Rule of Evidence 608(b): "extrinsic evidence is not admissible to prove specific instances of a witness's conduct in order to attack or support the witness's character for truthfulness." The court may allow cross-examination about those specific instances, but the prosecution did not cross-examine Ruth and Ruth is excused.

### C

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The defendant opened the character door by opinion testimony that the defendant is honest. FRE 405(a) lets the prosecution call its own character witness to testify to reputation for the opposing trait. The sheriff's deputy is the prosecution's rebuttal character witness, not a propensity attack.
2. **Student label:** opened-door rebuttal.
3. **True/responsive version:** "Testimony by a sheriff's deputy that, in his opinion, Barnabas is dishonest and untrustworthy" — the opinion form is also admissible under FRE 405(a).
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Federal Rule of Evidence 405(a) controls methods of proving character when character is admissible; reputation or opinion is permitted in the form provided by the rule. When a character witness testifies by opinion that the defendant has a pertinent good trait, the prosecution may call its own character witness to testify to reputation or opinion for the opposing trait.

### D

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The witness is excused, but reputation evidence is not the witness's own testimony. FRE 608(a) allows reputation or opinion testimony about a witness's character for truthfulness without placing the witness back on the stand. The choir-member source is window dressing.
2. **Student label:** reputation for truthfulness.
3. **True/responsive version:** "Testimony by a longtime neighbor of Ruth that Ruth has a reputation in the community as a truthful person" — the supporting form is admissible under FRE 608(a) only after the witness's character for truthfulness has been attacked.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Federal Rule of Evidence 608(a): "A witness's credibility may be attacked or supported by testimony about the witness's reputation for having a character for truthfulness or untruthfulness, or by testimony in the form of an opinion about that character. But evidence of truthful character is admissible only after the witness's character for truthfulness has been attacked."

B answers the exact call. The former co-worker is offering extrinsic evidence of a specific act (fabricated inventory counts eighteen months ago) to attack Ruth's character for truthfulness. Under FRE 608(b), that is barred. The witness is excused, so cross-examination is not a vehicle. The other three items are admissible under three different Evidence rules.
