## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**Gold Key:** A conspiracy agreement can be inferred from coordinated conduct, parallel acts, and communications showing a shared criminal objective — no express statement of agreement, written contract, or completion of the target crime is required.

The correct answer is **C**. The stem shows coordinated conduct (hammer-tap signals, loading boards in tandem, shared stash location, joint exit) plus a text message explicitly referencing a recurring plan ("Same routine as last Saturday"). This constellation of facts is classic circumstantial evidence of an agreement. The law does not require co-conspirators to utter magic words, sign documents, or finish the crime. Once the agreement exists, the conspiracy is complete in most jurisdictions (some also require an overt act, which the loading and stashing easily satisfy).

**Silver Key:** When three answer choices all say "the charge fails" and one evaluates the evidence differently, pause to check whether each "fails" reason rests on a real legal requirement or an invented hurdle — the majority framing is often bait.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. The conspiracy charge fails because both carpenters would have needed to sign a written plan.**
This choice sells the myth that serious criminal agreements require documentation. It appeals to students who confuse conspiracy with the Statute of Frauds or who assume the law demands formal proof. The breaker: criminal conspiracy is proved like any other fact — by direct or circumstantial evidence. Co-conspirators rarely sign contracts. The hammer taps, shared stash, and text are sufficient.

**B. The conspiracy charge fails because the oak boards never left the lumber yard.**
This choice sells the misconception that conspiracy merges with or requires completion of the target offense. It traps students who conflate conspiracy with the substantive crime. The breaker: conspiracy is a separate inchoate offense complete upon agreement (plus an overt act in most jurisdictions). Whether the larceny was ever finished is irrelevant to the conspiracy charge.

**D. The conspiracy charge fails because neither carpenter said aloud, "I agree to steal the lumber."**
*Gold Key:* A conspiracy agreement can be inferred from coordinated conduct, parallel acts, and communications showing a shared criminal objective — no express statement of agreement is required.

This is the **dominant trap**. It sells the intuitive but false requirement that agreements must be explicit and verbal. The stem deliberately includes the fact that no express words were spoken — precisely to test whether the student knows this fact is legally irrelevant. The text message ("Same routine as last Saturday") coupled with coordinated signals and parallel conduct is exactly how prosecutors prove conspiracy every day.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule:** To establish conspiracy, the prosecution must prove that two or more persons entered into an agreement to commit a crime. The agreement is the gist of the offense. The agreement need not be express; it may be inferred from the defendants' conduct, communications, and surrounding circumstances. Completion of the target offense is not required. No writing, formal contract, or magic words are necessary. See *United States v. Falcone*, 311 U.S. 205 (1940); *Directors Guild of America, Inc. v. Superior Court of Los Angeles*, 714 P.2d 1085 (Cal. 1986); Model Penal Code § 5.03.

**Authority floor:** The rule is well-established under the common law and modern statutes. The Model Penal Code § 5.03(1) defines conspiracy as an agreement with another person to commit a crime. Federal and state courts uniformly hold that "the agreement need not be explicit, but may be inferred from the facts and circumstances of the case." *United States v. Beck*, 615 F.3d 441 (5th Cir. 2010). The overt-act requirement (where applicable) is satisfied by any slight act in furtherance of the agreement — here, loading boards and stashing them near the gate.

**Rule/answer/issue unchanged from original:** Confirmed. The original tested that conspiracy agreement can be inferred from coordinated conduct and communications without express agreement, completion, or writing. The variation tests the identical rule, issue, and outcome.
