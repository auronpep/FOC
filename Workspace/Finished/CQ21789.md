Defense counsel must be notified of the exam's prosecutorial purpose so they can advise the defendant on whether to submit and how to limit the scope.

**C is correct.** Daniel's Sixth Amendment right to counsel attached at indictment. Under *Estelle v. ### 7. Black-Letter Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule:** After the Sixth Amendment right to counsel has attached (by way of formal charge or indictment), the government violates the right to counsel when it arranges for a defendant to be questioned about matters relevant to sentencing without the knowledge of defense counsel, and then uses the defendant's statements against him at the penalty phase.

**Authority:** *Estelle v. Smith*, 451 U.S. 454 (1981). The Court held that a psychiatric examination ordered to determine competency to stand trial became a "critical stage" of the proceedings when the prosecution used it to elicit evidence of future dangerousness for the capital sentencing hearing. Because defense counsel were not notified that the examination would include an inquiry into future dangerousness, the defendant was denied the assistance of counsel in making the decision whether to submit to the examination and how to respond. The Court vacated the death sentence, finding violations of both the Fifth and Sixth Amendments.

**Application to this item:** Daniel was indicted (right to counsel attached). The court ordered a competency exam. The prosecutor, without notifying defense counsel, asked the psychiatrist to question Daniel about future dangerousness — a sentencing-phase issue. The prosecution then offered those statements at sentencing. This is factually indistinguishable from *Estelle v. Smith*. The credited answer (C) correctly identifies the constitutional problem: counsel was not notified that the exam would be used to elicit sentencing evidence.

**Invariant layer confirmed:** The tested rule (post-attachment, notice to counsel required when a government-arranged exam is used for prosecutorial purposes), the legal issue (Sixth Amendment right to counsel at a critical stage), and the credited outcome are all unchanged from the original.

#### Wrong-Answer Recovery Paths

**If a student picked A (dominant trap):**
- Recovery: Re-read the stem. The prosecutor directed the psychiatrist to ask about future dangerousness. When the government uses an exam to gather incriminating evidence, it IS criminal questioning regardless of the clinical setting. The word "never" is an absolute — one counterexample kills it.

**If a student picked B:**
- Recovery: Sentencing determines punishment. It is a critical stage of the criminal case. The Sixth Amendment applies at sentencing. The claim that sentencing is "not part of the criminal case" is flatly wrong.

**If a student picked D:**
- Recovery: The Sixth Amendment applies to all government agents — police, prosecutors, informants, and court-appointed psychiatrists. The test is whether the government arranged the examination to elicit incriminating evidence, not what the agent was wearing.

#### Crossover Intersection Map

- **Fifth Amendment:** *Estelle v. Smith* also found a Fifth Amendment violation (no Miranda warnings before the psych exam). This item focuses on the Sixth Amendment angle — notice to counsel — but students should know both protections apply.
- **Massiah line:** The "deliberate elicitation" doctrine from *Massiah v. United States*, 377 U.S. 201 (1964), and *United States v. Henry*, 447 U.S. 264 (1980), reinforces that the government cannot circumvent counsel by using agents to gather incriminating statements post-indictment.
- **Sentencing procedure:** This item connects to the broader rule that sentencing is a critical stage triggering full Sixth Amendment protections.

#### Review Truth

After the Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches, the government may not use a court-ordered psychiatric examination to elicit incriminating sentencing evidence without notifying defense counsel of that prosecutorial purpose.
