## 1. Final Question

Solomon, a renowned art collector in his seventies, executed a duly probated will several years before his death. In the will, he "gave and bequeathed his entire art collection, absolutely," to "my grandchildren," and left the residue of his estate to his brother, Aaron.

Solomon had two children: a son, Asher, and a daughter, Lydia. Asher predeceased Solomon, leaving a young widow, Phoebe, and a small son, Benjamin. At the time Solomon executed the will, Benjamin was alive. By the time of Solomon's death, however, Benjamin had also died, in childhood and without issue. Lydia survived Solomon and was 30 years old at his death. She had two minor children, Stephen and Tabitha, both of whom survived Solomon.

Under the applicable intestate succession laws, Benjamin's sole heir was his mother, Phoebe.

A statute of the jurisdiction provides as follows:

> "If a devisee, including a devisee of a class gift, who is a grandparent or a lineal descendant of a grandparent of the testator is dead at the time of execution of the will or fails to survive the testator, the issue of such deceased devisee shall take the deceased's share under the will, unless the will expressly provides that this statute shall not apply. For this purpose, words of survivorship, such as 'if he survives me,' are a sufficient expression that the statute shall not apply."

Who now owns Solomon's art collection?

A class gift to "my grandchildren" closes at the testator's death. One grandchild survived only until after the will's execution; he then died with no issue (only his mother as heir-at-law). ## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam register)

**A is correct.** The class of "Solomon's grandchildren" closed at Solomon's death. At that moment, the class had two surviving members: Stephen and Tabitha. They are the sole members entitled to take under the will. The gift is to the class as a unit; the surviving class members take the whole.

The deceased grandchild, Benjamin, was alive at execution but predeceased the testator, so the anti-lapse statute is potentially relevant. The statute, however, substitutes the deceased devisee's **"issue"** (lineal descendants) — not the deceased's heir-at-law. Benjamin died without issue. His sole heir was his mother, Phoebe, but Phoebe is not Benjamin's "issue" within the meaning of the statute. Because there is no "issue" for the statute to substitute, the statute does not apply, and Benjamin's share **lapses**. For a class gift, a lapsed share does not fall into the residue — the class is the unit, and the surviving class members take the full gift.

**Gold Key:** the anti-lapse statute substitutes the predeceased devisee's "issue" (lineal descendants) — **never the heir-at-law**. If the predeceased devisee left no issue, the share lapses.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### B — "Stephen, Tabitha, and all future grandchildren born to Lydia" *(open-class fallacy, 18% inherited)*

The trap invites the student to think the class stays open so that any grandchildren Lydia might have in the future can also take. The trap is attractive because the class-gift rule is real and "class gifts can be open" is a true doctrine in some contexts.

The breaker: the class of "Solomon's grandchildren" closed at Solomon's death. Solomon is dead, so no more of his grandchildren can come into existence. A child later born to Lydia is Lydia's child, not Solomon's grandchild. The class is the group of grandchildren alive (or in gestation) at the testator's death, no more. Stephen and Tabitha are the only class members, and the gift is theirs alone.

### C — "Solomon's brother, Aaron" *(residue-fall-through fallacy, 1% inherited)*

The trap invites the student to think that, because one grandchild predeceased the testator, the gift "fails" or "partially fails" and falls into the residue. The trap is built on a real rule: a specific bequest that fails or partially fails falls into the residue.

The breaker: the art collection is a specific bequest to the class of Solomon's grandchildren, not part of the residue. The class is satisfied by Stephen and Tabitha; the gift is not failing. Aaron is named only as the residuary beneficiary, which means he takes "everything else" — not the specifically bequeathed art collection. The art collection would only fall into residue if no class member could take, which is not the case here.

### D — "Stephen, Tabitha, and Phoebe" *(anti-lapse-to-heir-at-law fallacy, 44% inherited, dominant trap)*

The trap invites the student to apply the anti-lapse statute broadly: "Benjamin predeceased the testator, and the statute is on point, so someone in Benjamin's family must take his share." The student substitutes Benjamin's heir-at-law (his mother, Phoebe) for Benjamin and concludes that Phoebe shares in the gift alongside the surviving grandchildren.

The breaker: the anti-lapse statute does not substitute the deceased devisee's **heir-at-law**; it substitutes the deceased devisee's **"issue"** (lineal descendants). Benjamin had no issue. Phoebe is Benjamin's mother, not his issue. "Heir-at-law" and "issue" are not the same — an heir is whoever would take by intestacy, while issue is a narrower category of lineal descendants. Because the statute has nothing to substitute, it does not apply, and Benjamin's share **lapses**. For a class gift, the lapsed share goes to the surviving class members — not to the deceased's heir-at-law, not to the residue.

> **Gold Key (kill shot):** the anti-lapse statute says "issue" — never the heir-at-law. If the predeceased devisee left no issue, the share lapses.
>
> **Silver Key (answer-array tell):** the right answer (A) and this trap (D) both list "Stephen and Tabitha." The single added word — Phoebe, the deceased's heir-at-law — is the trap tell. The statute says "issue" (lineal descendants), not "heir-at-law" (intestate taker).

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

### Tested rule (unchanged from the original)

For a class gift, the class closes at the testator's death under the **rule of convenience** — the class closes whenever any member is entitled to demand possession, and for an outright class gift under a will, that moment is the testator's death. A predeceased class member has a share that **lapses**, and the lapsed share goes to the surviving class members, not to the residue (the gift is to the class as a unit).

### Anti-lapse statute (unchanged from the original)

Modern American anti-lapse statutes prevent the lapse of a testamentary gift when the predeceased devisee is in a protected class — typically a grandparent or a lineal descendant of a grandparent of the testator — by substituting the deceased devisee's **issue** (lineal descendants) for the deceased devisee. The Uniform Probate Code § 2-604 and Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers § 5.1 codify this rule. Representative state enactments include Fla. Stat. § 732.603 ("antilapse; deceased devisee; class gifts") and Cal. Prob. Code § 21110.

The question's statute mirrors this structure:
1. Applies to a devisee, including a devisee of a class gift.
2. Triggers when the devisee is a grandparent or descendant of a grandparent of the testator.
3. Triggers when the devisee is dead at execution or fails to survive the testator.
4. Substitutes the deceased devisee's **"issue"** — never the heir-at-law.
5. Can be excluded by express language (e.g., "if he survives me").

### Application to the present facts

- The class of "Solomon's grandchildren" closes at Solomon's death. The class is satisfied by the two surviving members, Stephen and Tabitha.
- Benjamin, a member of the class, was alive at execution but predeceased the testator. He is a lineal descendant of a grandparent of the testator (he is the testator's grandchild). The statute's trigger conditions are satisfied.
- The statute's substitution target is "issue" — lineal descendants of the predeceased devisee. Benjamin had no issue. His sole heir was his mother, Phoebe. Phoebe is not Benjamin's issue.
- The statute has nothing to substitute. The share **lapses**.
- For a class gift, the lapsed share goes to the surviving class members, not the residue.
