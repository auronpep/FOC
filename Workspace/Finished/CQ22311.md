### 1. Final question
Lydia was the weekend register lead at a privately owned Christian bookstore called Fishers & Scrolls. As part of her duties, she counted the day's cash and locked it in Naomi's store cashbox each night. On Friday night, after placing the receipts in the cashbox, Lydia took $640 from it without permission. She knew Naomi would not check the cashbox until Monday and planned to use the money for travel and entry costs at a weekend Bible-trivia tournament that awarded cash prizes. Lydia intended to replace the money Monday morning if her team won enough prize money. If her team did not win, she planned to change the store's register reports so the missing money would not be noticed.

On Monday morning, Lydia returned to the bookstore. Her team had won prize money, so she put the $640 back in the cashbox. Barnabas, another employee, saw her returning the money and questioned her until she admitted what she had done. Before Lydia closed the cashbox, Barnabas said that unless she handed him $30 from the store's cash, he would tell Naomi. Lydia knew the register totals were often off by about $30 and Naomi usually ignored small differences, so Lydia took $30 from the cashbox and gave it to Barnabas.

If Lydia is charged with larceny for taking the $640 from the cashbox on Friday night, should she be found guilty?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. Larceny is judged at the time of the trespassory taking and carrying away. Lydia did not merely borrow the cash with an unconditional plan to return it. Gold Key: a conditional plan to return property only if a hoped-for event occurs still supplies larceny intent when the fallback plan is to keep or conceal the loss. Silver Key: lock the charge date before reading the later cleanup facts; the Monday return and the separate $30 episode do not change the Friday intent. Because Lydia planned to alter the register reports if the tournament went badly, she had at least a contingent intent to permanently deprive Naomi's store when she took the $640.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because it grabs the friendly half of Lydia's plan: she did hope to put the money back if the prize money came through. The breaker is the other half of the same plan; Gold Key: conditional return is not enough when the backup plan is to conceal the missing property instead of restoring it.

C is attractive because the cash was physically returned before anyone made a loss calculation. The breaker is that larceny turns on Lydia's intent at the Friday taking, not on the lucky Monday result.

D is attractive because it points to fresh wrongdoing with the same cashbox and a cleaner permanent loss. The breaker is the charge is for the $640 Friday taking, so the Monday $30 handoff is a separate event and does not answer the charged larceny question.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Criminal Law and Procedure, Other Crimes, Crimes against Property > Larceny, with verified outline code `73030100`. Common-law larceny requires a trespassory taking and carrying away of another's personal property with fraudulent intent to deprive the owner of the property. *United States v. Corona-Sanchez*, 291 F.3d 1201 (9th Cir. 2002), summarizes the common-law larceny elements and traces theft to common-law larceny: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/291/1201/593187/. Modern theft formulations also preserve the same core idea; Model Penal Code section 223.2(1) treats unlawful control over movable property with purpose to deprive as theft by unlawful taking: https://ristrophcriminallaw.lawbooks.cali.org/chapter/chapter-5-property-crimes/.

The key point is the conditional-intent rule. *People v. Davis*, 19 Cal. 4th 301 (1998), discusses larceny cases where return was conditioned on a future payment or event and treats that conditional return plan as supporting intent to permanently deprive: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/19/301.html. Lydia's plan mirrors that rule. If the tournament went well, she would return the cash; if it went badly, she would conceal the loss. That fallback plan supplies the required intent at the Friday taking. The later return does not erase the completed offense, and the later $30 handoff is outside the charged taking. The official key's result is therefore confirmed.
