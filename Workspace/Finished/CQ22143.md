## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. The prosecution used Naomi's recorded police interview as testimony against Timothy, but Naomi did not appear and Timothy had no prior chance to cross-examine her. **Gold Key:** testimonial police statements from an absent witness cannot be used for their truth unless the witness is unavailable and the defendant had a prior opportunity to cross-examine. **Silver Key:** focus on the missing cross-examination, not on the emotional reason Naomi stayed away or the contents of the recording.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because it reaches the same yes/no result and points to Timothy's own statements inside the interview. The breaker is that the Confrontation Clause problem is not the interview's internal content; it is the prosecution's use of Naomi's testimonial account without giving Timothy a chance to cross-examine Naomi.

B is the dominant trap because Naomi's fear makes her absence sound excused. The breaker is that witness unavailability alone does not satisfy the confrontation rule. **Gold Key:** an absent testimonial witness still must have been subject to a prior opportunity for cross-examination.

D sounds official because police made the recording. The breaker runs the other direction: a police interview describing past events for possible prosecution is the kind of testimonial statement that triggers confrontation protection when used at trial.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer

The verified rule is the Sixth Amendment Confrontation Clause rule for testimonial hearsay in a criminal trial. The Sixth Amendment gives the accused the right to be confronted with the witnesses against him. In Crawford v. Washington, 541 U.S. 36 (2004), the Supreme Court held that the prosecution's use of a spouse's tape-recorded police statement against the defendant, with no cross-examination opportunity, violated that right. Davis v. Washington, 547 U.S. 813 (2006), confirms that statements to police are testimonial when their primary purpose is to establish past events for later prosecution rather than meet an ongoing emergency. Smith v. Arizona, 602 U.S. ___ (2024), confirms the current rule that testimonial hearsay from an absent witness is barred unless the witness is unavailable and the defendant had a prior chance to cross-examine.
