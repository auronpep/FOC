### 7. Full right-answer explanation
A is correct. The call asks why a court could label the remainder contingent. Gold Key: a remainder is contingent when the takers are unascertained or when taking depends on a condition precedent. If the court reads Naomi's will to require the remaindermen to survive Mary, that survival requirement is the condition precedent. Silver Key: when a call asks why an interest is contingent, hunt for the answer that adds a prerequisite to taking; timing answers do not do that unless they create a condition.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
B is attractive because the signing date appears early and can feel like the moment Naomi's plan became fixed. The breaker is that a will does not create present property interests merely because it was signed, and signing-time vesting does not explain why the remainder would be contingent.

C is attractive only if the student tries to balance the family tree by giving Paul something parallel to Mary's life estate. The breaker is that the will gives Mary the life estate in the whole parcel and says nothing about a life estate in Paul; inventing that estate also does not explain contingent remainder status.

D is the dominant trap because a will does speak at the testator's death, and that timing rule sounds real. The breaker is that vesting at Naomi's death would not by itself make the remainder contingent. Gold Key: the needed construction is a condition precedent, and the only answer that supplies one is survival of Mary.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: the item tests the difference between vested and contingent remainders, specifically whether a court's chosen construction implies a survival condition before the remaindermen can take. The verified outline code is `82010201`, Nature and Ownership of Land > Future Interests > Contingent and vested remainders.

For the lawyer-confirmation layer, Restatement (First) of Property Section 157 uses the condition-precedent framing for what older terminology called a contingent remainder. First Natl. Bank of Cincinnati v. Tenney, 165 Ohio St. 513, 138 N.E.2d 15 (1956), discusses that Restatement section and contrasts an interest subject to a condition precedent with a vested defeasible interest subject to a condition subsequent. Cornell Wex's entry on "remainder subject to a condition precedent" also confirms the bar-review formulation: a contingent remainder is one that passes only if the specified circumstance exists, including a survival circumstance. Sources checked: https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/5914c9f4add7b049347f68f7 and https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/remainder_subject_to_a_condition_precedent.

On these facts, if the court says Mary has a life estate in all of Cedaracre and the remainder is contingent, it cannot be because the will-signing date controls, because the death date controls, or because Paul has an added life estate. It must be because the court reads the remainder as limited to the descendants who survive Mary. That survival requirement is the condition precedent that makes the remainder contingent. The research does not contradict the supplied official key.
