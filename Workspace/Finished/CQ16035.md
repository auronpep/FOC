## 6. Right-Answer Explanation (exam register)

**Gold Key: private-search government-action gate** - The Fourth Amendment is triggered by government action, not by a private person acting alone. A private person's later handoff of evidence to police does not, by itself, retroactively make the private search governmental.

**Silver Key: search-actor first** - Before asking whether there was a warrant exception, identify who made the search. If the searcher was private and police did not direct or participate in the search, suppression has no Fourth Amendment hook.

Ruth searched Lydia's cottage because Ruth suspected drug activity. The facts do not show that the police asked Ruth to search, encouraged her, paid her, directed her, or used her as a tool before the search happened. Ruth then gave the packets to police, and the police used them to arrest Lydia. That later police use does not convert Ruth's independent private search into a government search. The suppression motion should be denied.

### A - Granted because Ruth deliberately looked for drug evidence

**What makes it attractive:** This choice sees the privacy invasion and the purposeful hunt for evidence. It feels like intent should matter because Ruth was not merely stumbling into the packets.

**The breaker:** **Silver Key: search-actor first** - the first split is not accidental versus deliberate; it is private actor versus government actor. A deliberate private search still falls outside the Fourth Amendment when police did not direct or participate in it. Ruth's purpose may make her conduct intrusive, but it does not make the search governmental. (Filter: NOT_RESPONSIVE - wrong_element.)

### C - Granted because police relied on the packets for the arrest

**What makes it attractive:** The police did use the evidence in a criminal case, so the choice grabs a fact that matters downstream. It sounds responsive because suppression is about keeping evidence away from the prosecution.

**The breaker:** The suppression question asks whether the search or seizure violated the Fourth Amendment. Police reliance after Ruth's independent private search does not supply the missing government action at the search stage. The later arrest may depend on the packets, but the packets were not obtained through a government search. (Filter: NOT_RESPONSIVE - wrong_element.)

### D - Granted because Ruth became a government agent by delivering the packets

**What makes it attractive:** This is the dominant trap because it gives a clean conversion story: private person finds evidence, hands it to police, and supposedly becomes part of the prosecution team. That story feels especially tempting when the police immediately use the evidence.

**The breaker:** **Gold Key: private-search government-action gate** - agency requires government involvement before or during the search, not merely government receipt after the search. Ruth was not acting under police authority when she searched. Her later handoff did not retroactively make her a de facto government agent. (Filter: NOT_TRUE - fabricated_rule.)

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Tested rule:** The Fourth Amendment restricts governmental searches and seizures. It does not apply to a search or seizure conducted by a private person acting independently of the government, even if the private person later gives the evidence to police. The private-search doctrine may also limit what later government actors can do, but this item stops at the private person's independent search and handoff.

**Authority:** U.S. Const. amend. IV limits unreasonable searches and seizures by the government. In *Burdeau v. McDowell*, 256 U.S. 465 (1921), the Supreme Court held that Fourth Amendment limits address governmental action, not evidence obtained by private persons acting on their own. In *United States v. Jacobsen*, 466 U.S. 109 (1984), the Court reaffirmed that an initial private search does not itself violate the Fourth Amendment because of its private character. Source references used for lawyer confirmation: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/256/465/ and https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/466/109/.

**Invariant confirmation:** The tested rule, legal issue, credited outcome, and answer-array trap structure are unchanged from the source. Lydia's motion is denied because Ruth acted as a private person, not a government agent.
