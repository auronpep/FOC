Lydia owns a small Christian bookstore that sells hymnals and study materials. She sues Daniel in federal court for breach of a written purchase agreement. ### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. Rule 12(e) is not a demand for the plaintiff's evidence. The question is whether the pleading is so vague or ambiguous that the defendant cannot reasonably prepare a response. This complaint identifies the written agreement, attaches it, names the promised goods, gives the delivery deadline, and states the breach. Daniel can admit, deny, or otherwise respond to those allegations.

Gold Key: Rule 12(e) tests intelligibility, not evidentiary completeness. A complaint can be answerable even if it does not include witnesses, delivery records, emails, or proof.

Silver Key: When the motion asks for "more facts," sort the requested material into answer-needed facts versus discovery evidence. If the defendant can answer the claim, Rule 12(e) is the wrong tool.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because the missing text messages sound close to the delivery dispute. The breaker is that Rule 12(e) does not require a contract plaintiff to plead every message exchanged about performance; those details belong in discovery unless the complaint is too vague to answer.

C is attractive because trial evidence and witness names would help Daniel investigate the case. The breaker is that Rule 12(e) is not a pre-answer trial-evidence checklist; a complaint need not include all evidence or identify every witness to be clear enough for a response.

D is attractive because sworn proof sounds stronger than an unverified pleading. The breaker is that a Rule 12(e) motion does not require the plaintiff to prove breach by affidavit before the defendant answers; it asks whether the pleading is vague or ambiguous.

### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The credited answer is correct under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(e). The rule permits a party to move for a more definite statement only when a pleading to which a responsive pleading is allowed is so vague or ambiguous that the party cannot reasonably prepare a response. The motion must identify the defects and desired details. The current rule text is available through Cornell's Legal Information Institute at https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_12.

The transformed item preserves the original legal issue and credited outcome. Both questions test the same black-letter rule: a complaint that identifies the contract, the performance date, and the alleged breach is not made Rule 12(e)-defective merely because it omits evidentiary details such as witness identities, emails, or delivery communications. No researched authority contradicts the supplied official key.
