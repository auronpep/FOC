# BarMatrix Criminal Law and Procedure Question 16163

**Outline Code:** 74020101

**MBE Subject:** Criminal Law and Procedure

**Subtopic:** Homicide

**Outline:** Murder

---

**Question:**
A defendant is charged with murder. The evidence shows that she pointed a gun at the victim and pulled the trigger. The gun discharged, killing the victim. The gun belonged to the victim. The defendant testifies that the victim told her, and she believed, that the “gun” was a stage prop that could fire only blanks, and that she fired the gun as part of rehearsing a play with the victim at his house. If the jury believes the defendant's testimony and finds that her mistaken belief that the gun was a prop was reasonable, they should find her

**Answer Choices:**
- **A:** guilty of murder.
- **B:** guilty of manslaughter.
- **C:** guilty of either murder or manslaughter.
- **D:** not guilty of murder or manslaughter.

**Correct Answer:** D

**Explanation:**
Murder requires a mental state of malice aforethought, which includes (1) intent to kill, (2) intent to inflict great bodily harm, (3) reckless indifference to an unjustifiably high risk to human life, or (4) intent to commit a felony. In this scenario, the jury believes the defendant thought the gun was a prop containing only blanks. Therefore, none of the required mental states are present:
1. No intent to kill.
2. No intent to cause serious bodily injury.
3. No reckless indifference, as the belief that the gun was a harmless prop is deemed reasonable.
4. No felony intent.

Involuntary manslaughter requires a killing caused by gross negligence or reckless conduct. Because the jury finds the defendant’s belief reasonable, she did not act with gross negligence or recklessness. Voluntary manslaughter also requires intent to kill or serious injury, which is absent.

Thus, the defendant lacks the requisite mens rea for both murder and manslaughter. The appropriate verdict is **not guilty of murder or manslaughter** (Answer D).
