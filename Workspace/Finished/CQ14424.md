## 1. Final question — revised stem + call

Esther, a general event contractor, was preparing a sealed bid for Lydia’s private Christmas Pageant Pavilion. Paul, a lighting installer, sent Esther this signed email:

> Will install all overhead lantern trusses and speaker wiring for the pavilion, per Lydia’s specifications, for $32,000 if you accept within a reasonable time after the prime contract is awarded.

**Which of the following, if proved, would most strengthen Esther’s prospect of recovery?**

**D.** Esther dealt with all of her subcontractors in good faith and without seeking to renegotiate lower prices they had bid.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C** most strengthens Esther’s recovery.

**Gold Key:** A subcontractor’s bid can become temporarily irrevocable when the subcontractor should expect the general contractor to rely on it in a prime bid and the contractor is locked into that reliance before acceptance.

**Silver Key:** In a “most strengthens recovery” array, pick the fact that supplies the missing legal link. C supplies that link. The bid bond means Esther could not withdraw or amend her own prime bid without a real forfeiture. That makes her use of Paul’s bid a locked-in reliance fact, not just a business inconvenience.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** The attraction is blame. Paul’s careless math feels like the reason the loss should fall on him. The breaker is the missing legal link: negligence in calculating the bid does not show that the bid became temporarily irrevocable when Esther used it.

**B.** The attraction is practical damages cleanup. Esther’s search for the cheapest substitute looks responsible and directly tied to the $8,000 difference. **Silver Key:** this answers the damages-cleanup question, not the recovery-right question; it does not explain why Paul’s revoked bid stayed open.

**D.** The attraction is clean conduct. Esther looks fair because she did not bid-shop or squeeze her subcontractors after receiving their prices. The breaker is that general good faith does not show that she was locked into her prime bid before Paul revoked.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The source row tests whether a subcontractor’s bid may be held temporarily irrevocable when a general contractor relies on it in submitting a prime bid; the credited fact is the bid-bond / no-withdrawal fact. The supplied QID row identifies answer B as correct and gives the bid-bond rationale. 

The legal grounding is Restatement-style reliance. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 87(2) treats an offer as binding as an option contract when the offeror should reasonably expect substantial pre-acceptance reliance, the reliance occurs, and enforcement is needed to avoid injustice. ([Open Casebook][1]) Drennan v. Star Paving Co., 51 Cal. 2d 409, is the classic subcontractor-bid case: the general contractor used the subcontractor’s bid, became committed on the prime contract, hired a higher substitute, and recovered the difference; the opinion also emphasizes that the subcontractor should expect such reliance and that the general contractor’s own bid commitment matters. ([Justia][2])
