### 1. Final Question

Harbor Credit Union foreclosed on a small riverfront bakery property called Bethlehem Bread, a private business Ruth owned. The foreclosure sale did not raise enough money to satisfy the secured loan.

### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** Timothy did more than buy property burdened by a mortgage. His contract and deed say that he agreed "to assume the existing mortgage debt." A buyer who assumes the mortgage debt becomes personally liable on that debt. If the buyer later defaults and foreclosure leaves a deficiency, the lender may pursue the buyer for the deficiency to the extent of the assumption.

**Gold Key:** "Assumes the mortgage" creates personal liability for the buyer; "subject to the mortgage" only leaves the land burdened by the lien. The original borrower remains liable unless the lender releases that borrower or agrees to a novation.

**Silver Key:** In a yes/no answer set with reasons attached, do not stop when two choices say "yes." Keep the answer that gives both the right result and the right liability allocation.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A transfer with assumption makes Timothy personally liable, but it does not by itself prove that Harbor released Ruth or accepted Timothy as the sole debtor. **Silver Key:** a correct yes/no result with the wrong liability reason is still not the best answer.

**C.** This is the dominant trap. It protects the person who signed the original note and treats Ruth as the only real debtor. The breaker is the assumption language. **Gold Key:** once Timothy assumed the mortgage debt, Timothy became personally liable for that debt and for any deficiency, even though Ruth remains liable unless Harbor released her.

The deed and sale contract language are enough to show Timothy undertook the secured obligation.

### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The source row identifies Q14955 as a Real Property / Mortgages / Transfers by mortgagor item. The tested rule is the effect of a grantee's assumption of an existing mortgage debt after the mortgagor transfers the mortgaged real estate.

The black-letter rule is unchanged in the transformed question. A transferee who assumes liability on a mortgage debt promises to perform the obligation secured by the mortgage and becomes personally liable for that obligation. If the transferee defaults and foreclosure leaves a deficiency, the mortgagee may proceed against the transferee personally to the extent of the assumption. The transferor remains personally liable unless the mortgagee releases the transferor or agrees to a novation. Assumption is different from taking property merely "subject to" the mortgage.

Authority check: Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages section 5.1 supplies the core rule for transfers with assumption of liability. A GovInfo-hosted federal court order quotes and applies section 5.1, including the rule that on default the mortgagee may proceed personally against the assuming transferee and may proceed against the transferor or transferee for a deficiency according to their liabilities. It also quotes comment a explaining that assumption language need not follow a special formula and may appear in the deed or a separate agreement. The American Law Institute publication page confirms that Chapter 5 of the Restatement covers transfers of mortgaged real estate and mortgages. HUD's FHA assumption notice separately confirms the operational distinction: a purchaser who assumes and agrees to pay the mortgage debt undertakes personal liability, while the seller remains liable unless released.
