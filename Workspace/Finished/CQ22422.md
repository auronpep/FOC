## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

Timothy recovers the salary he would have earned during the six-month gap before he found comparable employment, plus the reasonable costs he incurred while searching for replacement work (the Christian job board fees and resume printing).  

**Gold Key:** When an employee under a fixed-term contract is wrongfully discharged, damages equal the contract wages for the period until the employee obtains comparable employment at the same or higher pay, plus any reasonably foreseeable consequential damages (such as job-search expenses) incurred before mitigation, reduced by actual earnings from the new position. Full remaining-term wages are cut off once mitigation occurs. Severance or transition stipends are not awarded by default; they require an express contractual term.

The stem shows Timothy mitigated by taking "the same general type of ministry leadership work and earning the same salary." That fact limits recovery to the gap plus search costs. The church's reorganization eliminated the position without cause, breaching the fixed-term contract. No facts suggest an agreement for automatic transition pay.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

**Governing rule (lawyer-confirmation layer):**  
Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 347 (Measure of Damages in General): the injured party has a right to damages based on the expectation interest measured by (a) loss in value of the other party's performance caused by the breach, plus (b) any other loss, including incidental or consequential loss, caused by the breach, less (c) any cost or other loss avoided by not having to perform.  

Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 350 (Avoidability as a Limitation on Damages): an injured party cannot recover damages for loss that could have been avoided by reasonable efforts to mitigate.  

In the employment-contract context, courts routinely apply these sections to limit recovery to the period of actual unemployment (the gap) once the employee secures comparable employment at equivalent pay. Reasonable job-search expenses incurred during the gap are recoverable as consequential damages when they were a foreseeable result of the breach. Severance pay is a creature of contract; it is not a default remedy for breach of an employment agreement.
