## 1. Final Question

A state statute provides: "The maintenance of any ongoing enterprise in the nature of a betting parlor or bookmaking organization is a felony."

Mary owns a small livestream studio attached to a Christian music lesson space. She licenses the back sound booth to Daniel by the month. A prosecutor has evidence that Daniel has been using the booth as a sports-betting call room within the meaning of the statute, and that Mary knows about that use.

Which of the following additional pieces of evidence would be most useful to the prosecutor's effort to convict Mary as an accomplice to Daniel's violation of the statute?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A property owner knowingly lets another person use rented space for an illegal betting enterprise. Which added fact best shows purposeful aid rather than knowledge alone?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

A. Mary has personally placed wagers through Daniel's call room.

B. Daniel has paid Mary the monthly booth fee in cash traceable to illegal wagers.

C. Mary charges Daniel far more for the booth than she charged the prior monthly user, who used it for ordinary music lessons.

D. Mary was previously convicted of operating a betting room in the same building.

## 4. Correct Answer

C (original key B).

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is the most useful additional evidence. The question is not asking what proves Mary knew Daniel was running the betting room. The prosecutor already has that. The missing step is intent to aid the ongoing venture. Charging Daniel far more than an ordinary lawful user paid gives Mary a personal financial stake in Daniel's illegal operation continuing.

Gold Key: Accomplice liability needs purposeful help or encouragement, not bare awareness; a premium price tied to the illegal use can show that the helper wants the illegal venture to succeed.

Silver Key: Once knowledge is already supplied in the stem, cut answers that merely re-prove knowledge and look for the added fact that makes Mary an interested participant.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because a person who places wagers plainly knows what Daniel is doing. The breaker is that the stem already gives knowledge, and being a customer does not by itself show that Mary aided Daniel's maintenance of the operation or had a stake in its continued success. Gold Key: knowledge of the crime is not the same thing as purposefully helping it.

B is attractive because the money came from the illegal operation. The source of Daniel's cash does not add the missing intent if the fee itself is ordinary market rent; it shows tainted payment, not Mary's purposeful aid to the betting enterprise.

D is attractive because prior operation of a similar betting room sounds like powerful criminal-history evidence. The breaker is current accomplice liability: a prior conviction does not necessarily show that Mary helped Daniel maintain this betting room now.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested issue is unchanged: whether the added evidence best supports accomplice liability for a landlord-like actor who knowingly provides space used for an illegal betting enterprise. The credited outcome is unchanged: the premium rent answer is best because it supports an inference that the lessor had a personal stake in the illegal venture and intended to aid its continuation.

Authority floor: Model Penal Code section 2.06(3)(a) states the core accomplice rule: a person is an accomplice when, with the purpose of promoting or facilitating the offense, the person solicits, aids, agrees to aid, or attempts to aid another in planning or committing it. Rosemond v. United States, 572 U.S. 65 (2014), quotes the traditional formulation that an aider and abettor must participate in the venture as something the person wishes to bring about and seek by action to make succeed. Direct Sales Co. v. United States, 319 U.S. 703 (1943), confirms that a "stake in the venture" is relevant evidence of criminal association and intent, especially when profit depends on the illegal operation's success. Sources checked: https://criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/2_06%283%29.htm, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/12-895, https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/319/703/.
