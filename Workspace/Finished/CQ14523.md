## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

**Lawyer-confirmation layer.** The tested rule is specific performance for a contract obligation when the legal remedy is inadequate. Specific performance is an equitable contract remedy used when monetary damages do not adequately repair the breach; Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 359 frames adequacy of damages as the limiting principle, and § 360 identifies difficulty of proving damages with reasonable certainty as a factor in inadequacy. ([Legal Information Institute][1])

The credited outcome is unchanged from the source row: the answer is the yes answer based on inadequacy of legal remedy. The source explanation identifies the same reason: damages are impracticable because the number of future monthly payments depends on an uncertain lifetime. **Right-answer legal reasoning.** Peter's promise is a continuing payment promise to a third person, not a personal-service promise requiring forced labor or ongoing personal supervision. The uncertainty is the duration of Hannah's life. A lump-sum legal damages award would require the court to value an unknown number of future payments. That uncertainty makes damages an inadequate substitute, so the court may order Peter to continue performing the payment obligation.
