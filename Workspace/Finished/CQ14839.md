### 5. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**Gold Key:** Under FRE 201(f), the jury instruction for judicially noticed facts depends on case type: in a civil case, the jury *must* accept the fact as conclusive; in a criminal case, the jury is told it *may, but is not required to*, accept the noticed fact as conclusive. This is the civil/criminal split — and it is the single most tested distinction in judicial notice questions.

C is correct. Under FRE 201(c)(2), the court **must** take judicial notice when a party requests it and supplies the necessary information showing the fact is not subject to reasonable dispute. Here, the prosecutor supplied official federal grant records from HUD — a source whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned — establishing that the community center received federal funding. This satisfies FRE 201(b)(2). The court has no discretion to refuse. However, because this is a criminal case, FRE 201(f) requires the court to instruct the jury that it *may, but is not required to*, accept the noticed fact as conclusive. Timothy's objection is overruled.

### 6. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is attractive but wrong** because it invents a blanket rule that judicial notice cannot be taken of elements of the offense in a criminal case. No such prohibition exists. FRE 201 contains no exception for essential facts or elements of the offense. When the fact meets the criteria of 201(b) and the party supplies the necessary information under 201(c)(2), the court must take judicial notice regardless of whether the fact relates to an element. The mandatory nature of the notice is the trap — students who conflate judicial notice with the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden may assume the prosecution cannot shortcut proof of an element this way. **Gold Key:** FRE 201 applies to any adjudicative fact meeting the criteria, including facts that happen to be elements of the offense. The rule does not carve out an exception for essential facts.

**B is attractive but wrong** because it fixates on only one of the two independent avenues for judicial notice under FRE 201(b). While the community center's federal funding status may not be "generally known within the court's territorial jurisdiction" under 201(b)(1), the prosecutor satisfied the second avenue: the fact "can be accurately and readily determined from sources whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned" under 201(b)(2). Official HUD grant records are exactly that kind of source. When either avenue is satisfied, judicial notice is proper. The choice is designed to trap students who know about the "generally known" prong but forget about the "reliable sources" prong.

**D is attractive but wrong** because it correctly identifies that judicial notice is mandatory but then applies the *civil* jury instruction to this *criminal* case. This is the dominant trap. In a civil case, FRE 201(f) requires the court to instruct the jury to accept the noticed fact as conclusive. But in a criminal case, the defendant's Sixth Amendment jury-trial right means the jury retains its fact-finding role — the court must instruct that the jury *may, but is not required to*, accept the noticed fact as conclusive. Students who memorize the civil rule without noting the criminal exception will pick this every time. **Gold Key:** The civil/criminal split in FRE 201(f) is the deciding distinction — civil = conclusive; criminal = may or may not accept.

### 7. Black-Letter Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Authority:** Federal Rule of Evidence 201.

**FRE 201(b):** A court may judicially notice a fact that is "not subject to reasonable dispute" because it:
(1) is generally known within the trial court's territorial jurisdiction; **or**
(2) can be accurately and readily determined from sources whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned.

**FRE 201(c)(2):** The court **must** take judicial notice if a party requests it and the court is supplied with the necessary information.

**FRE 201(f):** "In a civil case, the court must instruct the jury to accept the noticed fact as conclusive. In a criminal case, the court must instruct the jury that it may or may not accept the noticed fact as conclusive."

**Verification against original:** The tested rule (FRE 201 mandatory notice + criminal jury instruction), the legal issue (judicial notice procedure in a criminal case), and the credited outcome (must take notice; jury may but need not accept as conclusive) are all unchanged from the original question. The official key is confirmed correct.
