### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Robbery is larceny aggravated by taking property from a person or presence through force, violence, intimidation, or fear. Cornell Wex summarizes common-law robbery as taking another's property by violent force or threat of force with intent to permanently deprive, and lists taking from the person or presence by force or threat as core elements: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/robbery. Model Penal Code section 222.1 likewise treats robbery as theft accompanied by inflicting injury, threatening immediate serious injury, or committing or threatening an immediate serious felony in the course of the theft: https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/222_1.htm.

Peter satisfies the robbery rule because he used force to overcome Lydia's resistance and complete the taking. Ruth does not because she took unattended property without force or fear. The official key is therefore preserved.
