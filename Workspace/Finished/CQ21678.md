## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Peter had severe anxiety. He was scheduled for an interview to become the maintenance coordinator at Galilee House, a private Christian retreat center. Because he was nervous, Peter took three times the prescribed dose of his anxiety medicine and drank a bottle of beer with lunch. The medicine and beer left him severely intoxicated. While driving a small delivery van to the interview, Peter entered an intersection on a green light at the posted speed. Daniel, driving a pickup from a side street, ran a red light and struck Peter's van. Daniel died from the collision. The police investigation showed that Peter had been driving at the correct speed and had obeyed all traffic rules before impact.

Peter is charged with involuntary manslaughter. How should the court rule?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Can a defendant be guilty of involuntary manslaughter when he was grossly negligent before driving, but that negligence did not cause the fatal crash?

### 3. Final answer choices

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Peter's intoxication could support a finding of gross negligence, but involuntary manslaughter still requires the negligent conduct to cause the death. Silver Key: on a homicide charge, do not stop after spotting dangerous conduct; ask whether that conduct caused the death. The investigation shows that Peter drove at the speed limit, obeyed the traffic rules, and was hit when Daniel ran a red light. The death would have occurred even without Peter's intoxication, so the causal link is missing. Gold Key: culpable negligence plus a death is not manslaughter unless the culpable conduct caused the death.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because "totality of the circumstances" sounds like the broad way courts evaluate negligence. The breaker is that the call is not asking whether Peter looked negligent in the abstract; it asks whether his culpable conduct caused the homicide.

C is attractive because intoxicated driving sounds inherently dangerous and morally serious. The breaker is that a serious traffic-related wrong still must be causally tied to the death before it can support involuntary manslaughter.

D is the dominant trap. It correctly spots the strongest bad act: Peter mixed excess medicine with alcohol before driving. Gold Key: the manslaughter rule does not stop at gross negligence; the negligence must be an actual and proximate cause of death. Silver Key: once the answer proves only breach, return to the crash facts and ask who made the fatal event happen.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Involuntary manslaughter based on criminal negligence requires more than ordinary negligence, but it also requires causation. Model Penal Code section 210.1 frames criminal homicide around purposely, knowingly, recklessly, or negligently causing the death of another human being, and section 210.4 treats negligent homicide as a negligent criminal homicide. Model Penal Code section 2.03 supplies the causal-relationship requirement for result crimes. State-law summaries likewise treat proximate cause as mandatory for involuntary manslaughter; State v. Bruton, 344 N.C. 381, 393 (1996), is cited for that proposition in the North Carolina criminal-law review source.

Here, Peter's intoxication is culpable, but the collision was produced by Daniel's red-light entry while Peter was driving lawfully. Because Peter's intoxication was neither a but-for cause nor the proximate cause of Daniel's death, the involuntary manslaughter charge should fail.
