# CQ19382 - Martha's Riverwalk Booth Mats

## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question - revised stem and call

Martha operated a booth at a city riverwalk festival selling hand-lettered Scripture cards and hymn prints. A city ordinance required each festival-booth operator to keep the public pedestrian ramp immediately beside the booth reasonably safe while the festival was open. Martha hired Daniel Event Services, an independent contractor, to place and monitor nonslip rain mats along that ramp. The company negligently left one corner of a rain mat folded upward at the ramp's turn, and Peter tripped and was injured. Martha had no reason to know Daniel Event Services would do poor work.

Is Martha likely liable to Peter in a negligence claim based on the unsafe ramp?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A safety ordinance places the duty to keep a public pedestrian area reasonably safe on a booth operator, and the operator hires an independent contractor that negligently leaves the area unsafe. Is the operator still likely liable to the injured pedestrian?

- **D.** No, because Daniel Event Services was independent and Martha was not negligent in hiring it.

### 6. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**A is correct.** The ordinance put the sidewalk-style safety duty on Martha as the booth operator. Martha could hire Daniel Event Services to perform the work, but she could not use that contract to shed the duty owed to Peter. When the contractor left the ramp unsafe, Martha remained liable to the pedestrian for breach of the ordinance-imposed, nondelegable duty.

**Gold Key:** A safety duty imposed by statute, ordinance, or regulation can be nondelegable. Hiring an independent contractor may give the duty-holder a claim against the contractor, but it does not defeat liability to the injured person protected by the safety law. **Silver Key:** in the two-by-two answer array, split the answers by reason: ordinance-duty liability beats the independent-contractor defense because the call asks whether Martha remains liable to Peter.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

- **B.** This choice is attractive because Daniel Event Services created the dangerous mat condition, so it feels like the clean defendant. The breaker is the word **only**. Peter may have a claim against Daniel Event Services, but the ordinance also keeps Martha on the hook for the nondelegable duty assigned to her.

- **C.** This choice grabs the right bottom-line direction, but sells the wrong liability theory. Ordinary rain-mat placement at a festival is not an abnormally dangerous activity. The correct route is the ordinance-based nondelegable duty, not strict liability for dangerous activity.

- **D (dominant trap).** This choice sounds like the default independent-contractor rule: Martha hired a separate company and had no reason to expect poor work. **Gold Key:** that general rule gives way when a safety law places a nondelegable duty on the defendant. **Silver Key:** the clash is not whether Martha was careful in hiring; it is whether the ordinance let her delegate the duty owed to Peter.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

**Tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item.** The item tests whether a party on whom an ordinance places a safety duty remains liable to an injured third person when an independent contractor negligently performs the work. The credited outcome remains **Yes**: the duty-holder is liable because the ordinance-imposed duty is nondelegable.

**Authority floor.** Restatement (Second) of Torts section 409 states the baseline independent-contractor nonliability rule, but sections 410-429 collect exceptions. Restatement (Second) of Torts section 424 recognizes liability when a statute or regulation imposes safety precautions and the hired contractor fails to provide them. Courts discussing section 424 treat that kind of law-imposed safety obligation as nondelegable. Sources checked: *Schlenk v. Northwestern Bell Telephone Co.*, https://law.justia.com/cases/north-dakota/supreme-court/1983/10259-2.html; *Parker v. Neighborhood Theatres, Inc.*, https://law.justia.com/cases/maryland/court-of-special-appeals/1988/1727-september-term-1987-0.html; *Maloney v. Rath*, https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2d/69/442.html.

**Application.** The ordinance assigned the ramp-safety duty to Martha. Peter is the kind of pedestrian protected by that safety duty. Daniel Event Services negligently failed to keep the ramp reasonably safe. Martha's lack of negligent hiring does not answer the nondelegable-duty rule; it may matter between Martha and the contractor, but it does not defeat Peter's negligence claim against Martha.

**Doctrinal-currency note.** This issue is not on the QA hotlist. The answer does not depend on a superseded constitutional or evidence test. The current MBE-level rule remains that nondelegable duties are exceptions to the usual independent-contractor nonliability baseline.
