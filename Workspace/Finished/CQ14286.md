## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** The First and Fourteenth Amendments do not immunize a broadcaster from liability when it films and airs a performer's entire act without consent. In *Zacchini v. Scripps-Howard Broadcasting Co.*, 433 U.S. 562 (1977), the U.S. Supreme Court held that the Constitution does not deprive a state from requiring a broadcaster to compensate a performer for broadcasting his act. The broadcast of a performer's entire act poses a substantial threat to the economic value of that performance — if the public can see the act free on television, it will be less willing to pay to see it live. The Court analogized this to the broadcast of a copyrighted dramatic work, a prize fight, or a baseball game, where the promoters or participants had other plans for publicizing the event. The First Amendment protects the press's right to report news, but it does not grant the press a constitutional immunity from tort liability when it appropriates the commercial value of a performer's entire act.

**Gold Key (GK-CONLAW-PRESS-01):** The press's First Amendment right to cover newsworthy events is NOT a blanket immunity from tort liability. When a broadcaster films and airs a performer's *entire act* without consent, the performer may recover damages for the appropriation of the commercial value of the performance. *Zacchini v. Scripps-Howard Broadcasting Co.*, 433 U.S. 562 (1977).

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing Rule:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments do not immunize the news media from tort liability when they broadcast a performer's entire act without the performer's consent. A state may require a broadcaster to compensate a performer for the unauthorized appropriation of the commercial value of his or her performance.

**Authority:** *Zacchini v. Scripps-Howard Broadcasting Co.*, 433 U.S. 562 (1977). The Supreme Court, in a 5-4 decision, held that (1) the broadcast of a performer's entire act poses a substantial threat to the economic value of that performance; (2) the Constitution no more prevents a state from requiring compensation for broadcasting a performer's act than it would privilege the broadcast of a copyrighted dramatic work, a prize fight, or a baseball game without liability; and (3) the First and Fourteenth Amendments do not immunize the news media in these circumstances.

**Verification against original:** The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the original. The original tested whether the First Amendment immunizes a broadcaster from liability for filming and airing a performer's entire act without consent. The credited answer was that it does not. This is precisely what *Zacchini* holds. The transform preserves the rule, the issue, and the outcome. **PASS.**
