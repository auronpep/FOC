## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Timothy borrowed $40,000 from Boaz, a private lender, to buy a fishing vessel and nets for his trade. When Timothy fell behind on the loan, Boaz told him that unless he arranged adequate security, Boaz would sue him for the balance. Timothy's mother, Hannah, then signed a mortgage to Boaz on her own seaside guesthouse to secure repayment of Timothy's loan. Hannah signed no promissory note and made no promise that made her personally liable for Timothy's debt to Boaz.

Is the mortgage valid?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Is a mortgage valid when an owner pledges her own property to secure another person's debt without becoming personally liable or receiving any benefit?

### 3. Final answer choices

D. No, because Hannah received no direct benefit for granting the mortgage.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. A mortgage pledges specific property as security for repayment of a debt, and an owner may pledge her own property to secure someone else's debt. The mortgage runs against the land itself, so it does not depend on the owner also being personally liable on the debt, and it does not depend on the owner receiving anything in return. Hannah's guesthouse therefore stands as valid security for Timothy's loan even though Hannah signed no note, gave no personal guaranty, and got no benefit. Gold Key: an owner may mortgage her own property to secure another person's debt, and the mortgage is valid even though the owner signs no note, gives no personal guaranty, and receives no direct benefit. Silver Key: when three choices each invent a different reason the deal "fails" and the fourth is a bare "Yes," test the bare answer against the rule before hunting for a disqualifier.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

B is attractive because the stem stresses that Hannah never became personally liable, so it feels as though there is no enforceable obligation behind the mortgage. The breaker is that a mortgage enforces against the property, not the person; personal liability and the security interest are separate, and the mortgage is valid without any guaranty.

C is attractive because it states a clean, absolute-sounding rule, and a student who is unsure may default to "you can only mortgage for your own debt." The breaker is that this is simply not the law: an owner may mortgage her own property to secure the debt of another.

D is attractive because Hannah gives up security and gets nothing back, which feels one-sided and unsupported by consideration. The breaker is that granting a mortgage does not require the mortgagor to receive any direct benefit; the validity turns on the pledge of the property, not on what the owner receives.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is unchanged: the mortgage is valid. A mortgage is a conveyance of an interest in real property as security for an obligation. The obligation it secures need not be the mortgagor's own obligation; an owner may grant a mortgage on her own property to secure the debt of a third person, in which case the mortgagor stands in the position of a surety whose exposure is limited to the mortgaged property. Two corollaries follow and dispose of the distractors: first, because the mortgage operates in rem against the land, the mortgagor need not be personally liable on the underlying note (no privity, no personal covenant, no guaranty required); second, the grant does not require the mortgagor to receive any direct benefit. Applying that here, Hannah's mortgage on her guesthouse validly secures Timothy's $40,000 debt; if Timothy defaults, Boaz may foreclose on the guesthouse even though Hannah owes nothing personally and received nothing in return.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: the Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages section 1.2 and chapter 7 recognize that a mortgage may secure the obligation of a person other than the mortgagor and treat the third-party mortgagor as a surety; the no-personal-liability point follows from ordinary privity doctrine (a person is not personally bound on a note to which she is not a party). This is not on the doctrinal-currency hotlist, and the research agrees with the supplied official key, so PASS is available.
