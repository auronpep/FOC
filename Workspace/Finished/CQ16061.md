### 1. Final question

A statute provides that "If the death of any person proximately results from the commission of or attempt to commit any misdemeanor or non-forcible felony, the person committing said misdemeanor or non-forcible felony shall be guilty of third degree manslaughter." Because he had been convicted three times of operating a boat while under the influence of alcohol, Timothy's boating license was revoked. ### 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. The statute supplies the rule: if a death proximately results from the commission of any misdemeanor or non-forcible felony, the person committing that offense is guilty of third degree manslaughter. Gold Key: when the MBE gives you a homicide statute, apply its words before importing common-law misdemeanor-manslaughter limits. Silver Key: the deciding axis is statutory proximate cause, not whether the misdemeanor is morally wrong, dangerous in the abstract, or foreseeable. Timothy was committing the misdemeanor of operating a boat without a license, and the deaths proximately resulted from that unlicensed operation.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged: where a homicide statute expressly makes a death manslaughter if it proximately results from the commission of any misdemeanor or non-forcible felony, the court applies the statutory proximate-cause requirement and does not add a common-law malum-in-se, dangerousness, or foreseeability element absent from the statute.

The authority floor supports the statutory/causation framing. Oklahoma's misdemeanor-manslaughter instruction under 21 O.S. 2021, section 711(1), requires death as a direct result of an act or event in the commission of a misdemeanor. The Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals' committee comments summarize State v. Ceasar, 2010 OK CR 15, 237 P.3d 792, as holding that a driving-after-revocation misdemeanor can support misdemeanor manslaughter and that proximate cause was satisfied where the unlawful driving was a substantial factor in the victim's death. The same comments also quote Logan v. State for the point that a misdemeanor not connected with the death is not enough. Model Penal Code section 2.03 also reflects the general criminal-law requirement of a causal relationship between conduct and result. Sources checked: https://okcca.net/ouji-cr/4-94/ and https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/6141-tanaka-criminal-law-casebook/resources/8.1.1-model-penal-code-mpc-203-causal-relationship-between-conduct-and-result-divergence-between-result-designed-or-contemplated-and-actual-result-or-between-probable-and-actual-result/.

Timothy's case fits the supplied statute. He was committing the misdemeanor of operating without a license, and the deaths proximately resulted from his operation of the boat. The statute does not ask whether the misdemeanor is malum in se, whether Timothy was intoxicated that evening, or whether he specifically foresaw death.
