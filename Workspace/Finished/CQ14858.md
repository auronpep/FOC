## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** In a federal criminal case, the adverse spousal testimonial privilege belongs to the witness spouse. Ruth is Daniel's spouse and she objects to testifying against him. Because she is the witness spouse and refuses to give adverse testimony, she should not be required to testify.

**Gold Key:** The federal adverse spousal testimonial privilege is held by the witness spouse, not by the accused spouse. A willing spouse may testify, but an unwilling witness spouse may refuse.

**Gold Key:** Do not swap in the marital communications privilege. The testimonial privilege can block adverse spousal testimony even when the testimony concerns observed conduct rather than a confidential marital communication.

**Silver Key:** Split the item into two questions: who is trying to testify, and which spousal privilege is being invoked. Once Ruth refuses as the witness spouse, the non-confidential nature of the observation does not make her compellable.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. Yes, because Ruth's observation of Daniel's jacket and boots was not a confidential marital communication.**

This is the dominant trap because the fact is true: seeing a jacket and boots is not a confidential marital communication. The breaker is that the call is not limited to the marital communications privilege. **Gold Key:** Adverse spousal testimonial privilege is separate from marital communications privilege, and it lets the witness spouse refuse adverse testimony in a criminal case even when the testimony is about observed conduct.

**B. Yes, because the spousal testimonial privilege is unavailable in criminal prosecutions.**

This answer sells a scope reversal. The breaker is that the federal adverse spousal testimonial privilege is a criminal-case privilege; criminal prosecution is the setting where this privilege does its work.

**D. No, because Daniel has a privilege to stop Ruth from testifying against him in a criminal case.**

This answer reaches the right yes/no result but gives the wrong holder. It is tempting because Daniel is the defendant and both spouses objected. The breaker is holder identity. **Gold Key:** Under the federal rule, the witness spouse controls the privilege. Daniel cannot stop a willing Ruth from testifying; Ruth can refuse for herself.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

Federal Rule of Evidence 501 provides that federal privilege questions are governed by common law as interpreted by United States courts in light of reason and experience unless a superior source controls. Source: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title28%2Ftitle28a%2Fnode232%2Farticle5.

The governing Supreme Court authority is *Trammel v. United States*, 445 U.S. 40 (1980). *Trammel* modified the older federal rule and vested the adverse spousal testimonial privilege in the witness spouse alone. The witness spouse may refuse to testify adversely, but may not be prevented from testifying if willing. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/445/40.

Application: Ruth is the witness spouse. The proposed testimony is adverse to Daniel in a federal criminal trial because it connects Daniel's clothing to the arson suspect's appearance. Ruth objects. Under *Trammel* and FRE 501, the court should not compel her adverse testimony. Daniel's separate objection is not the deciding fact because the privilege is Ruth's to assert. The clothing observation also does not have to be a confidential communication, because the testimonial privilege is distinct from the marital communications privilege.

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: the witness spouse's refusal controls in a federal criminal case, so the wife should not be required to testify.
