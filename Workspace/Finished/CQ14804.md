### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**A is correct.** Timothy’s statement was made outside the trial and is offered to prove the truth of what it asserts: that Lydia supplied him with the counterfeit badges. Timothy did not give usable testimony about the statement and was not subject to cross-examination about it. That keeps the prior-statement routes closed.

**Gold Key:** A prior consistent statement or prior identification under Rule 801(d)(1) first requires the declarant to testify and be subject to cross-examination about the statement; a witness who refuses to answer has not opened that gate.

**Silver Key:** When the choices offer prior-statement labels, check the gate before picking the label: did the declarant actually testify and become subject to cross about the statement?

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**C.** This choice is attractive because “prior consistent statement” is a real hearsay-exclusion label. The breaker is that the label does not fit this record. Timothy refused to answer, gave no usable testimony, and there is no qualifying credibility attack that this statement rebuts.

**D.** This is the dominant trap. The statement names Lydia, so “identification” has surface pull. ### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

Federal Rule of Evidence 801(c) defines hearsay as a statement not made while testifying at the current trial or hearing that is offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) Rule 802 makes hearsay inadmissible unless a federal statute, the Evidence Rules, or other Supreme Court-prescribed rules provide otherwise. ([Legal Information Institute][2])

Rule 801(d)(1) excludes certain prior witness statements from hearsay only if the declarant testifies and is subject to cross-examination about the prior statement. The listed routes include prior consistent statements and prior identifications. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) Rule 801(d)(1)(C) covers a statement that identifies a person as someone the declarant perceived earlier, but it still depends on the same declarant-testifies-and-cross-examination gate. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) The 2014 Committee Note also preserves the Tome limitation for prior consistent statements offered to rebut recent fabrication or improper influence or motive. ([U.S. Code][3])

Here, Timothy refused to answer any questions about Lydia and was held in contempt. Officer Ruth’s testimony would repeat Timothy’s out-of-court accusation for its truth. The declarant-witness gate is missing, so neither the prior-consistent route nor the prior-identification route makes the statement nonhearsay. The credited outcome is unchanged from the source item.
