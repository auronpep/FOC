## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Ownership, Future Interests, and the verified outline code is `82010200`. A power of appointment lets the powerholder designate who receives appointive property, but the powerholder does not automatically own the appointive property merely by holding a life income interest and a testamentary power. Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers sections 17.1 and 17.3 define and classify powers of appointment, and section 19.4 addresses whether a donee's residuary or general estate clause exercises a power. Section 19.4 states the key default point for this item: a residuary clause does not manifest intent to exercise a power unless the power is general and there is no effective gift-in-default, or the instrument otherwise manifests the necessary intent. Older UPC 2-610 and modern UPC-style statutes such as Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 190B, section 2-608 likewise frame the issue as whether the will specifically refers to the power or otherwise manifests intent to include the appointive property.

Here, the trust provides an effective gift in default to John. Therefore, if Ruth wins, it cannot be because Hannah owned Hopeacre outright, because the RAP categorically never applies, or because Hannah disliked the default taker. Ruth wins only if the court treats Hannah's phrase "my entire estate" as reaching Hopeacre or Hannah's general power of appointment. The RAP distractor does not defeat the official key: even under the common-law lives-in-being-plus-21-years formulation, the relevant power/default structure resolves no later than Hannah's death, and the daughter takes only if the court finds an effective appointment.
