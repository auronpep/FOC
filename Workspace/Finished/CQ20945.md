### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A sells the familiar attempt instinct: a student sees "preparation" and wants a substantial step. The breaker is the call. This is a conspiracy overt-act question, and the Gold Key kills the trap because conspiracy requires less than attempt when the jurisdiction asks only for an overt act.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged: when a jurisdiction requires an overt act for conspiracy, a slight act in furtherance of the agreement is enough; the act need not itself be criminal; and one conspirator's overt act can satisfy the requirement for the conspiracy.

Model Penal Code section 5.03(5) requires an overt act in pursuance of the conspiracy for many conspiracies and allows the act to be done by the defendant or a person with whom the defendant conspired. The federal conspiracy statute, 18 U.S.C. section 371, similarly requires that one or more conspirators do an act to effect the object of the conspiracy. The Third Circuit model conspiracy instructions state that at least one member may perform the overt act, that the act need not itself be illegal, and that acts as innocent as writing a letter or talking on the telephone may be sufficient when done to further the conspiracy. Sources checked: https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/overtact.htm and https://www.ca3.uscourts.gov/sites/ca3/files/2021%20Chapter%206%20Conspiracy%20for%20posting%20final.pdf.

Renting a cargo van to haul away the agreed target property is a practical step toward the theft. It is enough for the overt-act requirement even though the police arrest the conspirators before they drive to the theater.
