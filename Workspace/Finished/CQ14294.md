### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. The state supreme court did not merely mention the state constitution while relying on federal law. It expressly said that it need not decide how the United States Supreme Court's free-speech cases would resolve the dispute, then rested the judgment on the broader free-expression guarantee of the state constitution. That state ground is independent because it does not depend on federal law. It is adequate because it fully supports the same judgment: reversal of Peter's conviction. Once that separate state ground supports the judgment, a United States Supreme Court ruling on the federal free-speech issue would not change the result. The proper disposition is to dismiss the writ as improvidently granted.

Gold Key: A clear state constitutional ground that is both independent of federal law and adequate to support the judgment leaves the United States Supreme Court with no merits work to do, even when the state opinion discusses federal cases.

Silver Key: Lock the court-action verb before reading the merits bait. If the state court gives a plain state-law ground that already supports the judgment, choose the jurisdictional exit: dismiss.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule is unchanged. The source item and this variation both test the adequate-and-independent-state-ground doctrine: the United States Supreme Court may review qualifying highest-state-court judgments under 28 U.S.C. section 1257(a), but it will not review a state court judgment that rests on a state law ground that is independent of federal law and adequate to support the result.

Authority floor:

- 28 U.S.C. section 1257(a) authorizes Supreme Court certiorari review of final judgments from the highest state court when a federal right, privilege, immunity, treaty, statute, or constitutional issue is properly involved. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1257
- Michigan v. Long, 463 U.S. 1032 (1983), states the plain-statement approach: if a state court clearly and expressly bases its judgment on separate, adequate, and independent state grounds, the United States Supreme Court will not review the judgment. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/463/1032/
- The jurisdictional reason is advisory-opinion avoidance. If correcting the state court's federal-law view would not change the judgment because state law independently supports it, the Supreme Court has no effective federal judgment to alter.

Application: the state supreme court here gave the necessary plain statement. It said it need not decide the federal First Amendment question and then held that the state constitution, interpreted more broadly, did not permit the conviction because no riot or violence occurred. That state constitutional holding is independent of federal law and adequate to support reversal. The official key remains correct, and the credited outcome is unchanged.

Current-law check: no doctrinal-currency hotlist issue controls the answer because the federal incitement/free-speech merits are not reached. The item turns on Supreme Court review jurisdiction, not the current merits test for threats or incitement.
