### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

A is the best answer. The judge is asking a federal court to supervise the Senate's internal method for trying an impeachment. The Constitution gives the Senate the power to try impeachments, and the Supreme Court has treated challenges to Senate impeachment-trial procedures as political questions.

Gold Key: When the complaint attacks how the Senate structured an impeachment trial, the court usually stops at nonjusticiability because the trial function is textually committed to the Senate.

Silver Key: Read the call as a "who may review this procedure?" question before grading whether the procedure feels trial-like.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Article I, Section 3, Clause 6 gives the Senate the "sole Power to try all Impeachments." The controlling authority is Nixon v. United States, 506 U.S. 224 (1993), where the Supreme Court held that a federal judge's challenge to Senate Rule XI, which allowed a Senate committee to receive evidence and report to the full Senate, presented a nonjusticiable political question.
