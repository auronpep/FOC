## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Daniel broke into a locked storage building behind Lydia's private Christian music school at night to steal expensive speaker cabinets used for hymn concerts. While dragging one heavy speaker cabinet toward the exit, Daniel accidentally dropped it onto a night-security guard who had stepped inside without Daniel's knowledge. The guard died from the impact. Daniel was charged with felony murder predicated on burglary.

Is Daniel guilty of felony murder?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

If a burglar accidentally kills a security guard while carrying stolen property during a nighttime burglary, is the burglar guilty of felony murder?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. Yes, because burglary is an inherently dangerous felony and the death occurred in furtherance of it.

B. No, because Daniel did not know the guard was present and therefore lacked malice.

C. No, because burglary is a property crime and cannot serve as a predicate for felony murder.

D. No, because the death was accidental and Daniel did not cause it intentionally.

### 5. Correct answer

A (original key C).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is the best answer. Daniel was committing burglary when the guard died from the dropped speaker cabinet. Felony murder does not require Daniel to intend the killing or know the guard was present. Gold Key: felony murder supplies murder liability from a qualifying dangerous felony; burglary is a traditional predicate. Silver Key: once the call says felony murder, test the predicate felony and the death-in-furtherance link before using ordinary intent-to-kill instincts.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

B attracts a student who treats the case like ordinary murder: Daniel did not even know the guard was inside. The breaker is that felony murder does not require malice toward a specific victim or awareness that a victim is present when the qualifying felony supplies the murder rule.

C has a clean-sounding category objection because burglary is a property offense. The breaker is that burglary is one of the traditional felony-murder predicates precisely because nighttime intrusion creates serious confrontation risks.

D is the dominant trap because the death was accidental and Daniel did not mean to kill anyone. The breaker is that accidental deaths during a qualifying burglary are within the felony-murder rule. Gold Key: felony murder is built for deaths that happen during the dangerous felony even without intent to kill.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged: felony murder can apply when a death occurs in furtherance of burglary, even if the death is accidental and the burglar did not know the victim was present.

Model Penal Code section 210.2(1)(b) provides a modern rule-number authority floor: recklessness and extreme indifference are presumed when the actor is engaged in, attempting, or fleeing from listed felonies, including burglary. The MPC text is available here: https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/210_2.htm. Cornell Wex summarizes the felony murder rule as allowing murder liability when a felony results in death even without intent to kill, and lists burglary among typical violent felonies: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/felony_murder_rule. Justia's criminal-law summary likewise states that felony murder covers killings during inherently dangerous felonies and lists burglary among typical inherently dangerous crimes: https://www.justia.com/criminal/offenses/homicide/felony-murder/.

Under those rules, Daniel is guilty. He committed a nighttime burglary, carried stolen property during the felony, and the guard died from a direct accidental impact during that felony. The lack of intent to kill and lack of awareness of the guard do not defeat felony murder.
