### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. Authentication of a telephone conversation requires evidence sufficient to support a finding that the claimed speaker made the call. Gold Key: when the recipient does not know the caller, self-identification by the caller plus case-related content is not enough; the proponent needs evidence the call was made to the number assigned to the claimed person or other circumstances connecting the voice to that person (FRE 901(b)(6)). Silver Key: cut every choice that treats the call's relevance to the case as proof of who made it; the call could have come from anyone who heard about the relief boxes. The foundation here is insufficient, so the objection is sustained.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because the words "relief boxes" and "outreach" sound directly tied to the investigation, so students treat case-related content as automatic authentication. The breaker is that relevance and authentication are separate requirements; the content could have been repeated by anyone who learned of the planned drop.

C is attractive because students often believe juries are allowed to guess or fill in gaps on identity when the story feels connected to the defendant. The breaker is that authentication is a threshold the proponent must meet with evidence before the jury ever weighs the call; speculation does not satisfy the "sufficient to support a finding" standard.

D is attractive because a recording would have made voice comparison easy, so students treat the absence of a recording as the decisive flaw. The breaker is that a recording is not required; live testimony or other foundation can authenticate a call, but none was supplied here beyond self-identification.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Evidence, Relevancy and Excluding Relevant Evidence, Authentication and Identification, and the verified outline code is 32020700. FRE 901(a) requires evidence sufficient to support a finding that the matter is what its proponent claims. FRE 901(b)(6) specifically addresses telephone conversations: when the witness does not know the person, the call may be authenticated by evidence that a call was made to the number assigned at the time to that person or business, if circumstances (including self-identification) show the person answering to be the one called. Mere self-identification by the caller, without more (no assigned-number evidence, no prior voice familiarity under 901(b)(5), no distinctive knowledge or other connecting circumstances), is insufficient to support a finding of identity. This is confirmed by standard treatments of the rule (e.g., the Advisory Committee notes to 901 and practical foundation guides). The variation preserves the exact legal issue, the credited outcome (sustain for insufficient foundation), and the dominant trap (relevance-overbreadth). No key disagreement with the source row. Authority floor met with FRE 901(a) and (b)(6).
