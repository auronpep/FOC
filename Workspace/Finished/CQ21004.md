## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

At a Christian baking contest, Ruth quietly pulled Peter, a volunteer sound technician, behind the display tables and asked him to poison her husband Daniel's bottled smoothie before the evening worship concert. Ruth said she would give Peter $42,000 from an inheritance account after Daniel died. Peter refused, reported the conversation to police at the fairgrounds, and Ruth never contacted Peter or anyone else again. Ruth is charged with solicitation to commit murder. Is Ruth guilty of solicitation?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Did a person commit solicitation by asking someone to commit murder with intent that the murder occur, even though the person asked refused and the requester did nothing more?

### 3. Final Answer Choices

C. No, because Peter refused and therefore no agreement was formed.

D. Yes, because solicitation is complete when the request is communicated with intent that the crime be committed.

### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

D is correct. Ruth made a direct request that Peter kill Daniel, and she made it with the intent that the killing happen. The offense is complete when the request or encouragement is communicated with that criminal purpose. Peter's refusal, his police report, and Ruth's later silence do not undo the completed solicitation.

Gold Key: Solicitation is complete at the request. Agreement, success, payment completion, and the solicited person's willingness are not elements.

Silver Key: Lock the call on "guilty of solicitation." The answer that treats the request itself as the completed act beats answers that ask for an agreement, later follow-through, or a separate payment proof.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because it sells a clean-sounding exit: Peter refused, Ruth stopped, so the plan looks over. The breaker is timing. The request had already been communicated with the required intent, and later inaction after refusal does not erase that completed offense. Gold Key: Solicitation is complete at the request, so a post-refusal stop is too late unless a separate recognized renunciation rule is actually satisfied.

B is attractive because the promised $42,000 makes the payment evidence feel central. The breaker is that payment is proof of intent, not an element. A bare request to commit the crime, made with intent that the crime happen, can be enough.

C is the dominant trap. It borrows the agreement idea from conspiracy and makes Peter's refusal feel decisive. The breaker is that solicitation is the asking, not the deal. Peter did not have to agree, and the murder did not have to begin.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The credited answer preserves the original rule, legal issue, and outcome. Criminal solicitation is committed when a person, with the purpose that a crime be committed, commands, encourages, or requests another person to engage in conduct constituting that crime. Model Penal Code section 5.02(1) states that a person is guilty if, with the purpose of promoting or facilitating a crime, the person commands, encourages, or requests another person to engage in conduct that would constitute the crime, an attempt, or complicity. Section 5.02(2) further shows that success of the communication is not the core completion point; the actor's solicitation conduct is enough when designed to communicate. Section 5.02(3) recognizes a limited renunciation defense only where the actor persuades the solicited person not to do it or otherwise prevents the crime under circumstances showing complete and voluntary renunciation. Ruth did not persuade Peter away from the crime or prevent anything; Peter refused on his own and went to police.
