# CQ21694 - Lydia's Mountain Tunnel

## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Lydia Infrastructure, Inc., a private road-building company, received a contract from the Federal Transportation Commission to build a freight tunnel through a mountain pass located entirely within the state so delivery trucks could reach a neighboring state for business. The contract required Lydia Infrastructure to buy all tunnel panels within the state. A state statute imposes a 6 percent sales tax, payable by the buyer, on purchases made within the state. Lydia Infrastructure bought reinforced concrete panels from Barnabas Supply in the state for use in the tunnel and refused to pay the tax. The state prosecuted Lydia Infrastructure for violating the sales tax statute.

If Lydia Infrastructure's only defense is that it was performing a federal government contract and therefore was immune from the state sales tax statute, the court should find Lydia Infrastructure

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Can a private federal contractor avoid a nondiscriminatory buyer-paid state sales tax on materials it buys for a federal project by claiming federal tax immunity?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. not guilty, because the tunnel was intended to help traffic moving in interstate commerce.

B. guilty, because private independent contractors working for the federal government are subject to nondiscriminatory state taxes.

C. not guilty, because the state lacks power to tax activities performed for the federal government.

D. guilty, because Lydia Infrastructure's contract with the Federal Transportation Commission was not on a cost-plus-fixed-fee basis.

### 5. Correct answer

B (original key C).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Lydia Infrastructure is a private independent contractor, not the United States itself. The state sales tax is imposed on the buyer's in-state purchase of materials, and nothing in the facts shows discrimination against the federal government or against those who deal with it.

Gold Key: Federal tax immunity turns on the legal taxpayer, not the federal purpose or economic burden. A private independent contractor is taxable unless the levy falls on the United States itself or an inseparable federal instrumentality.

Silver Key: Before weighing federal purpose or interstate movement, name the taxpayer. Here, the taxpayer is the private buyer of the panels, so the federal-contract label does not carry the company to immunity.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because the tunnel is meant to help trucks reach another state, so the Commerce Clause feels nearby. The breaker is the call: Lydia Infrastructure's only defense is federal-contract tax immunity. A general sales tax on the contractor's local purchase is not defeated merely because the completed tunnel will serve interstate traffic.

C is the dominant trap. It sells a true-sounding federalism idea: states may not tax the United States directly. The breaker is the Gold Key: federal immunity turns on the legal taxpayer. The tax lands on Lydia Infrastructure, a private contractor, not on the United States itself or an inseparable federal instrumentality.

D sounds lawyerly because cost-plus contracts are a familiar federal-procurement detail. The breaker is that the pricing formula is not the immunity switch. Even a cost-plus-fixed-fee arrangement does not make a private contractor immune from a nondiscriminatory state tax imposed on the contractor.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. This is a Constitutional Law question under the federal-state relationship and intergovernmental tax immunity doctrine. A state cannot tax the United States directly or discriminate against the United States or those with whom it deals, but it may impose a nondiscriminatory tax on a private party doing business with the federal government when the legal incidence falls on that private party.

Authority checked:

- U.S. Const. art. VI, cl. 2 supplies the federal supremacy basis for intergovernmental tax immunity.
- United States v. New Mexico, 455 U.S. 720 (1982), confirms that private contractors are independent taxable entities unless the state levy falls on the United States itself or on an agency or instrumentality so closely connected that the two cannot realistically be viewed separately. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/455/720/
- Alabama v. King & Boozer, 314 U.S. 1 (1941), upheld state sales-tax liability involving federal cost-plus-fixed-fee contractors buying materials for a federal project; reimbursement by the federal government did not create constitutional tax immunity. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/314/1
- The Constitution Annotated summary of intergovernmental tax immunity states the modern rule consistently: states may tax private parties dealing with the United States if the tax is nondiscriminatory and does not directly tax the United States. Source: https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artI-S8-C1-1-5/ALDE_00013391/

Those authorities support the supplied official key. The source answer is correct because the legal incidence of the sales tax is on Lydia Infrastructure as buyer, not on the United States. The facts do not show the company is a federal agency or inseparable instrumentality, and they do not show discrimination against federal operations. There is no key conflict and no doctrinal-currency hotlist issue.
