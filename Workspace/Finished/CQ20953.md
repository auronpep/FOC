### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

D is correct. The best classification is involuntary manslaughter. Daniel committed a misdemeanor designed to prevent dangerous launching devices in crowded indoor spaces. That dangerous unlawful act directly caused the visitor's death. Gold Key: unlawful-act manslaughter covers an unintended killing caused by a sufficiently dangerous misdemeanor or non-felony unlawful act. Silver Key: classify by the predicate level first; misdemeanor plus death points toward involuntary manslaughter, not felony murder.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because Daniel did not mean for the launcher to hit anyone. The breaker is the Gold Key: unlawful-act involuntary manslaughter does not require intent to kill when the dangerous unlawful act proximately causes death.

B is the dominant trap because it sees a death during an unlawful act and reaches for the most familiar homicide shortcut. The breaker is the predicate level: the law violated is a misdemeanor, not a felony-murder predicate. Silver Key: unlawful act is not enough; felony murder needs a felony predicate.

C is attractive because the launcher proved deadly. The breaker is that dangerousness alone does not supply intent to kill, premeditation, or an enumerated first-degree murder trigger.

### 9. Full Black-Letter Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Involuntary manslaughter includes an unlawful killing without malice committed during an unlawful act not amounting to a felony, or during a lawful act done unlawfully or without due caution. 18 U.S.C. section 1112 states this federal formulation, and it tracks the common-law unlawful-act manslaughter lane. People v. Cox, 23 Cal. 4th 665 (2000), discusses involuntary manslaughter based on an unlawful act not amounting to a felony and the requirement that the act be dangerous under the circumstances of its commission.

Daniel's predicate offense was a misdemeanor, not a felony. The ordinance was aimed at dangerous launchers in crowded halls, and Daniel's violation directly caused a fatal head injury. Because the killing was unintended and without malice but resulted from a dangerous misdemeanor, involuntary manslaughter is the best classification. Felony murder is unavailable on these facts because the predicate is not a felony. First-degree murder is unavailable because the facts do not show intent, premeditation, deliberation, or an enumerated first-degree predicate.

Authority links: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1112 ; https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/23/665.html
