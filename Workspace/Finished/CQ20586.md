### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This answer sells the familiar move “bad medicine equals constitutional problem.” The breaker is the word **automatically**: Equal Protection scrutiny does not jump to strict scrutiny merely because a medical policy is disputed.

**B.** This answer is attractive because the statute does treat minors differently from adults. The breaker is **every medical setting**: that is an adult-rights overclaim and not the threshold classification question.

**D.** This answer is attractive because the same medication is allowed for other diagnoses, so the line feels suspect. **Gold Key:** medical-use difference alone is not automatically a sex classification; the threshold question remains the classification actually drawn.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The legal rule and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row. The governing threshold is whether the law classifies by sex or instead by medical use and age. In *United States v. Skrmetti*, the Supreme Court held that a Tennessee law regulating puberty blockers and hormones for minors was not subject to heightened scrutiny because it classified by age and medical use, not sex or transgender status; classifications based on age or medical use received rational-basis review. This confirms the source row’s stated rule: identify the classification actually drawn; medical-use and age classifications generally receive rational-basis review unless the law classifies by suspect status or sex as such. The transformation keeps the same issue and answer.
