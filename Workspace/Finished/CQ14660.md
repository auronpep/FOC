## 7. Right-Answer Explanation

Hannah is not guilty of any crime because she owed no legal duty to warn the congregation or the revival staff about what she observed.

Criminal liability for homicide by omission — failing to act — requires a threshold showing that the defendant had a legal duty to act. At common law, no general duty exists to rescue or warn strangers. Such a duty arises only from a defined set of relationships: parent–child, employer–employee, voluntary assumption of care, contract, or a defendant who created the danger. Hannah had none of these with the congregation. She was a bystander attending a public event. Her failure to warn, however morally troubling, was not accompanied by any legal obligation to act.

**Gold Key (GK-CRIM-OMISSION-01):** Criminal liability for homicide by omission requires a legal duty to act. Common law recognizes no general duty to rescue or warn strangers. Without a special relationship, contract, or voluntary assumption of care, no legal duty exists, and even a reckless failure to warn is not criminal.

## 9. Black-Letter Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** Criminal liability for homicide based on omission requires (a) a legal duty to act and (b) a breach of that duty that causes death with the applicable mental state. At common law, no general duty exists to rescue or warn strangers. A legal duty arises from: (1) a special relationship recognized by law (parent–child, spouse–spouse in some jurisdictions, employer–employee, innkeeper–guest, common carrier–passenger); (2) a contractual or statutory obligation; (3) voluntary assumption of care for the victim; or (4) conduct by the defendant that created the peril.

**Applied:** Hannah had none of these with the revival congregation. She was an adult attendee who happened to observe a third party preparing to commit arson. Her failure to warn does not satisfy a legal duty under any of the recognized categories. Absent a legal duty, her omission — however morally condemnable — is not a predicate act for criminal homicide.

**Why not C (Manslaughter):** The gross-negligence/reckless-indifference prong of manslaughter requires that the defendant's unreasonably dangerous conduct cause death. Where the charged "conduct" is an omission, the omission must be a breach of a legal duty to constitute the predicate act. MPC § 2.01(3) expressly states: "Liability for the commission of an offense may not be based on an omission unaccompanied by action unless (a) the omission is expressly made sufficient by the law defining the offense; or (b) a duty to perform the omitted act is otherwise imposed by law." No duty → no breach → no crime.

**Why not A (Murder 2nd):** Murder at common law requires malice aforethought. Where recklessness forms the malice theory (depraved heart), it still requires that the defendant's reckless conduct create an unjustified risk of death. An omission cannot supply this act unless there is a prior legal duty. No duty → the omission is not unlawful → malice cannot be established.

**Why not D (Murder 1st):** In addition to the duty problem, the jurisdictional definition requires intentional homicide with premeditation and deliberation. Hannah harbored no intent to kill; she intended only to leave. This choice fails on intent grounds independently of the duty issue.
