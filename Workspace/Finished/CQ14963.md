### 1. Final question

Mary owned a row of small commercial studios. Lydia orally agreed to rent one studio for six months beginning on October 1 so she could run private Christian calligraphy workshops. ### 6. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. The court's ruling for Mary means the jurisdiction is applying the legal-possession rule. Under that rule, Mary had to give Lydia the legal right to possess the studio at the start of the lease, but Mary did not also have to remove Stephen and deliver actual physical possession. Stephen was a holdover, not Mary and not someone still rightfully holding under Mary at the start of Lydia's term. Lydia's remedy was to proceed against Stephen for possession, which she did.

Gold Key: distinguish legal possession from actual possession. A landlord who is required to deliver only legal possession can win even though a holdover tenant keeps the incoming tenant out of the space. Silver Key: because the call asks why the landlord wins, test each answer against the landlord-win rule; facts that explain Lydia's lawsuit sequence are not enough unless they explain why Mary's delivery duty was satisfied.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice is attractive because constructive eviction is a familiar landlord-tenant escape hatch. The breaker is fit. Lydia was not claiming that she vacated after Mary's wrongful interference made the studio unusable; she was seeking damages for the delay caused by Stephen's holdover. Constructive eviction is the wrong lane.

**B.** This choice is the dominant trap because the stem deliberately mentions that Lydia did not tell Mary before suing Stephen. That makes notice feel like the missing procedural step. The breaker is that the legal-possession rule does not make Mary's win turn on advance notice from Lydia. Gold Key: if the jurisdiction requires only legal possession, the landlord's delivery duty is satisfied even without prior notice of the tenant's action against the holdover.

**D.** This choice is attractive because Lydia first chose a possession action against Stephen, and that can sound like a binding election. The breaker is the overclaim. Suing the holdover for possession does not, by itself, waive or replace any separate claim Lydia might have against Mary. Mary's win comes from the scope of Mary's delivery duty, not from an election-of-remedies bar.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested issue remains Real Property, Ownership, landlord-tenant delivery of possession at the start of a lease. The verified outline code is `82010401`, which appears verbatim in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md` as `Nature and Ownership of Land > Landlord and Tenant > Types of tenancies`.

The governing legal split is the English rule versus the American rule. Under the English rule, a landlord must deliver actual possession at the start of the term. Under the American rule, the landlord must deliver the legal right to possess, but has no implied duty to oust a wrongfully holding-over prior tenant unless the lease says so. The source item states that the court finds for the landlord, so the explanation must be the American-rule/legal-possession rationale.

The authority floor is satisfied by the leading case `Hannan v. Dusch`, 154 Va. 356, 153 S.E. 824 (1930). The Virginia court framed the precise issue as whether, without an express covenant, a landlord must oust a former tenant who wrongfully holds over so the new tenant can enter at the beginning of the term. The court described the American rule as recognizing the tenant's legal right to possession but implying no landlord duty to remove wrongdoers, and it affirmed judgment for the landlord. Research source checked: https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/hannan-v-dusch-895080792. No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is triggered, and the research does not contradict the supplied official key.

Applied here, Mary did not personally keep Lydia out, and Stephen no longer had a right to possess under his expired lease. In a jurisdiction following the legal-possession rule, Mary satisfied the duty by giving Lydia the legal right to possess the studio. That is why the credited answer remains the legal-possession answer.
