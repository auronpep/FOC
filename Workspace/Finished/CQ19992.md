## 1. Final question

Stephen robbed a private Bible-manuscript gallery after closing time and killed the night guard while escaping with the gallery's cash box. A few hours later, Stephen arrived at the apartment of his aunt Naomi. Stephen told Naomi that he had just killed the guard during the robbery and needed to disappear. Naomi believed him. Naomi is charged as an accomplice to the murder and robbery. What is the most accurate characterization of Naomi's criminal liability?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

When a person knowingly hides and transports a felon only after a murder and robbery are complete, is that person an accomplice to those felonies or an accessory after the fact?

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

C is correct. Naomi knew Stephen had committed the murder and robbery, and then she hid him, fed him, clothed him, and drove him away so he could avoid arrest. That is classic accessory-after-the-fact conduct. Gold Key: post-crime help to a known felon is not accomplice liability for the completed felony; it is the separate offense of accessory after the fact. Silver Key: time-stamp the help first. Before-or-during help points toward accomplice liability; after-completion help points toward accessory-after-the-fact liability.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because Naomi and Stephen did coordinate after he arrived, and concealment sounds criminal. The breaker is the call: the best characterization is accessory after the fact, not a conspiracy label for the already-completed murder and robbery.

B is attractive because Naomi is helping a family member in panic. The breaker is that family loyalty is not a common-law immunity from accessory-after-the-fact liability, and the relationship here does not erase knowing assistance intended to hinder apprehension.

D is the dominant trap because the assistance is concrete and substantial. The breaker is timing: Naomi's help came only after the murder and robbery were over, so it cannot make her a principal or accomplice to those completed felonies. Gold Key: substantial help after the felony is accessory-after-the-fact evidence, not retroactive accomplice evidence.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. An accessory after the fact is a person who knows a felony has been committed and then receives, relieves, comforts, or assists the felon for the purpose of hindering apprehension, trial, or punishment. That is a separate, lesser offense from liability as a principal or accomplice to the completed felony.

Authority layer: 18 U.S.C. section 3 codifies the accessory-after-the-fact formulation: knowledge of a completed offense plus receiving, relieving, comforting, or assisting the offender to hinder apprehension, trial, or punishment. 18 U.S.C. section 2 separately covers principal liability for one who commits an offense or aids, abets, counsels, commands, induces, or procures its commission. The federal statutes are not the source of every MBE common-law question, but they provide low-risk rule-number confirmation of the same distinction. Sources checked: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/3 and https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2.

Here, the murder and robbery were complete before Naomi acted. She knew what Stephen had done and helped him avoid arrest. That satisfies accessory-after-the-fact liability but does not make her guilty of the murder or robbery themselves. No doctrinal-currency hotlist item is triggered. Bank validation verdict: PASS.
