## 6. Full right-answer explanation

**B.** The attractive move is to treat Lydia’s own confession as making Barnabas’s confession redundant or harmless. **Silver Key:** track whose confession the appeal challenges. Lydia challenges Barnabas’s confession, so a reason about Lydia’s own confession answers a different object.

## 8. Full black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row: use of a non-testifying codefendant’s confession against the accused at a joint criminal trial violates the accused’s confrontation right when the accused has no opportunity to cross-examine the codefendant.  The outline routing is Criminal Procedure, not general Constitutional Law: the outline reference routes a criminal defendant’s Confrontation Clause trial right to the 75xx Criminal Procedure codes, and lists **75080300** as “Right to Confrontation.” 

Authority floor: **Bruton v. United States** held that admitting a non-testifying codefendant’s confession implicating the defendant at a joint trial violated the defendant’s Sixth Amendment right of cross-examination. ([Justia Law][1]) **Nelson v. O’Neil** confirms the opposite side of the hinge: when the declarant is present at trial and subject to full and effective cross-examination, the Confrontation Clause problem does not arise in the same way. ([Justia Law][2]) **Cruz v. New York** confirms that a defendant’s own confession does not cure the Bruton problem created by a non-testifying codefendant’s confession. ([Justia Law][3]) **Crawford v. Washington** reinforces that testimonial statements, including police interrogations, require confrontation through unavailability plus a prior opportunity for cross-examination when the declarant does not appear at trial. ([Legal Information Institute][4])
