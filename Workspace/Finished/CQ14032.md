## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. Lydia should ask the State A federal court to transfer venue for convenience. The case is already in federal court, and the original forum has jurisdiction and venue because the accident happened in State A and the parties are diverse with more than $75,000 at stake. **Gold Key:** when a federal civil action was filed in a proper district, 28 U.S.C. Section 1404(a) is the convenience-transfer tool. **Silver Key:** lock the procedural lane before reading the reason. This is federal-to-federal movement for convenience, not dismissal, jurisdiction repair, or removal.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is attractive** because Lydia lives in State B and removal sounds like a defendant-controlled way to move a case. The breaker is posture. Removal moves a case from state court to federal court; this case is already pending in federal court. **Silver Key:** once the stem says "federal court," removal is the wrong lane unless the case began in state court.

**C is the dominant trap.** It sees the transfer destination and the State B forum, so it feels close to the right move. The breaker is the reason: there is no personal-jurisdiction defect to cure. Lydia created case-linked forum contact by allegedly causing the injury in State A during the expo, so the motion should be framed as a convenience transfer, not a repair of jurisdiction.

**D is attractive** because the stem says Lydia has been in State A only once, which tempts a minimum-contacts objection. The breaker is twofold. Minimum contacts concerns personal jurisdiction, not subject-matter jurisdiction, and Lydia's alleged negligent act in State A supplies case-linked contact with the forum. The amount and diverse citizenship support federal subject-matter jurisdiction.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: a federal defendant who wants out of a proper but burdensome federal venue should move for transfer based on convenience, rather than seek dismissal for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction, transfer to cure personal jurisdiction, or removal.

Lawyer-confirmation layer: 28 U.S.C. Section 1404(a) authorizes a district court, for the convenience of parties and witnesses and in the interest of justice, to transfer a civil action to another district where it might have been brought or to a district to which all parties consent. 28 U.S.C. Section 1391(b)(2) makes venue proper where a substantial part of the events giving rise to the claim occurred; the expo accident occurred in State A. 28 U.S.C. Section 1332(a) supplies diversity jurisdiction because the parties are citizens of different states and the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000. 28 U.S.C. Section 1441(a) confirms why removal is unavailable: removal applies to a civil action brought in state court, not to a case already filed in federal court.
