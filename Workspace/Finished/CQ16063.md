## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**C. "Paul discovered his partner embezzled the church fund; next day Paul killed him with a crossbow."**

A court would find that a reasonable person would have cooled off in that interval, so the heat-of-passion/voluntary-manslaughter reduction doesn't apply. Paul intended to kill, he waited and planned (ambush with a crossbow), and the cooling time defeats any EED defense. This is murder.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing rule:** Murder is the unlawful killing of another human being with malice aforethought. Malice aforethought exists when the defendant has: (1) intent to kill, (2) intent to inflict great bodily harm, (3) depraved-heart recklessness (conscious disregard of a substantial and unjustifiable risk to human life), or (4) felony murder (death during commission of an inherently dangerous felony). *See* 2 W. LaFave, *Criminal Law* § 14.1 (3d ed.); *People v. Knoller*, 41 Cal. 4th 139 (2007); *Commonwealth v. Malone*, 354 A.2d 445 (Pa. 1976) (depraved-heart murder).

**Voluntary manslaughter / heat of passion:** An intentional killing that would otherwise be murder is reduced to voluntary manslaughter if the defendant acted in a reasonably provoked heat of passion with no reasonable opportunity to cool off. Cooling time defeats the defense. *See* *Girouard v. State*, 583 A.2d 718 (Md. 1991); Model Penal Code § 210.3(1)(b).

**Mercy killing:** No jurisdiction recognizes "mercy" or "compassion" as a defense to murder. An intentional killing at the victim's request is still murder. *see* *People v. Roberts*, 211 Mich. 187 (1920).

**Felony murder:** Requires death during the commission of an inherently dangerous felony, with a causal connection between the felony and the death. A regulatory violation that is not a felony cannot support felony murder, and the violation must be causally connected to the death. *See* *People v. Howard*, 34 Cal. 4th 1129 (2005) (inherent-dangerousness test).

**Application to D (correct answer):** Stephen's only violation was operating a boat without a safety beacon — a regulatory infraction, not a felony. The collision was accidental. No intent to kill, no intent to cause great bodily harm, no depraved-heart recklessness, no felony murder. Without malice aforethought, there is no murder.
