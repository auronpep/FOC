# Pass-1 Transform Report - Q21002 (Evidence - Materiality and Probative Value)

## 1. Final Question

Stephen is charged in federal court with possessing ammunition after having been convicted of a qualifying felony. Stephen offers to stipulate that, before the ammunition possession, he had been convicted of a qualifying felony. Stephen objects to the name and nature of the prior crime.

How should the court rule?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**B is correct.** The prior felony is relevant because the government must prove the status element. But relevance is not the end of the question. When Stephen offers a complete stipulation that he had a qualifying felony before the ammunition possession, the stipulation gives the government the point it needs on status. That sharply reduces the added probative value of telling the jury the name and violent nature of the old offense.

The court should then apply Rule 403. If the violent label creates a danger that jurors will punish Stephen for the old assault or treat him as a bad person likely to possess ammunition now, and that danger substantially outweighs the details' reduced added probative value, the court may exclude the name and nature while allowing proof of the qualifying-felony status.

**Gold Key:** When a prior conviction is offered only to prove a status element, a full stipulation can satisfy that element and make the name and nature of the old crime unfairly prejudicial under Rule 403.

**Silver Key:** Separate the fact the government must prove from the form of proof it wants to use. The fact of a qualifying conviction may come in; the inflammatory story behind it may stay out.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### Choice D - Dominant Trap

**Why it is attractive:** This choice sells the familiar prosecution-story instinct: the government normally gets to prove its case with evidence it chooses, not with a defendant's sanitized substitute. Students who remember that general principle may overapply it and think the prosecution can always pick the most forceful proof.

**The breaker:** "Always" is the tell. Rule 403 still limits the form of proof when a less prejudicial stipulation fully proves the status element and the violent details add little real value. **Gold Key:** In this narrow status-element setting, the stipulation changes the Rule 403 balance by reducing the need for the full judgment's inflammatory details.

### Choice A

**Why it is attractive:** Possession is the main conduct issue in the charge, so students may think the prior-felony details matter only if Stephen contests possession. That feels like a clean trial-management compromise.

**The breaker:** The possession issue is a different axis. The objection targets the name and nature of the prior felony, not whether ammunition possession occurred. **Silver Key:** Keep the axes separate: status is one element, possession is another. A fight over possession does not make the violent label more probative of status.

### Choice C

**Why it is attractive:** The answer sounds protective of the defendant and attacks the unfairness of letting jurors hear about a prior felony. It also converts a prejudice concern into a constitutional-sounding objection.

**The breaker:** The government may prove every element of the charged offense, including qualifying-felony status. The defect is not that all status proof is unconstitutional; the defect is the unnecessarily prejudicial form of proof. Rule 403 may trim the name and nature, but it does not erase the element.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

**Governing authority:** Federal Rule of Evidence 403 permits a court to exclude relevant evidence when its probative value is substantially outweighed by a danger of unfair prejudice, confusing the issues, misleading the jury, undue delay, wasting time, or needlessly presenting cumulative evidence. See the current rule text at the U.S. Code site: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=2023&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-2023-title28a-node230-article4-rule403.

**Status-offense authority:** In *Old Chief v. United States*, 519 U.S. 172 (1997), the Supreme Court held that, in a felon-in-possession prosecution, a district court abuses its discretion under Rule 403 when it rejects the defendant's offer to concede the prior-conviction element and admits the full judgment record over objection, where the name or nature of the prior offense risks unfair prejudice and the evidence is offered solely to prove prior-conviction status. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/519/172/.

**Application:** Stephen's qualifying felony is relevant to the status element, but his stipulation supplies the evidentiary value needed to prove that element. The violent-assault label adds little to status proof and creates a serious risk that the jury will misuse the prior crime as propensity evidence. Under Rule 403 and *Old Chief*, the court may exclude the name and nature of the prior crime while permitting proof of the qualifying-felony status.

**Invariant check:** The tested rule is unchanged from the original: Rule 403 can limit the form of prior-conviction proof when a stipulation fully proves the status element and the old crime's details create unfair prejudice.
