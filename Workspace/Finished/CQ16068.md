### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested issue, rule, and credited outcome are unchanged: murder may be based on malice aforethought, including depraved-heart or implied malice, when the defendant acts with a wanton disregard for human life. MPC section 210.2(1)(b) states the parallel modern rule for murder when homicide is committed recklessly under circumstances manifesting extreme indifference to the value of human life: https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/210_2.htm. Commonwealth v. Malone, 354 Pa. 180, 47 A.2d 445 (1946), recognizes malice from grossly reckless conduct showing callous disregard of likely harmful effects: https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/supreme-court/1946/354-pa-180-47-a-2d-445.html. People v. Watson, 30 Cal. 3d 290 (1981), confirms the vehicular-homicide distinction between gross negligence manslaughter and implied-malice murder where driving shows conscious disregard for life: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/30/290.html.

The correct answer is C because 96 miles per hour into an intersection can be evidence of wanton disregard for human life. A misdemeanor-manslaughter theory, a license violation, or culpable negligence may support lesser liability, but those reasons do not explain a murder conviction.
