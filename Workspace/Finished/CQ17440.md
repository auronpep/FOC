## 7. Right-Answer Explanation

When a witness's answer contains both a responsive portion and a volunteered, potentially inadmissible portion, a motion to strike that targets the entire answer is overbroad. The court should strike only the portion that is nonresponsive or inadmissible.

**Gold Key:** A motion to strike must be targeted. When an answer has a responsive part — one that directly answers the question asked — the court should not strike that part along with the inadmissible portion. The responsive observation stays; the volunteered hearsay goes (if otherwise inadmissible).

Miriam was asked: "Did you see Timothy leave the warehouse?" Her answer "Yes" — along with the accompanying direct-observation detail that she saw him leave — is responsive and based on personal knowledge. That portion should stand. The added bystander statement ("I overheard a man near the entrance say Timothy had been pouring something along the walls") is volunteered, beyond the scope of the question, and potentially hearsay. If it is inadmissible, that portion should be struck.

**Silver Key:** When the stem has a motion to strike the ENTIRE answer, first check whether any part of the answer was actually responsive. If the witness correctly answered the yes/no question and only then volunteered more, the motion is overbroad as to the responsive part. The correct ruling targets only the nonresponsive or inadmissible tail.

## 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice C — Grant the motion; hearsay contaminates the whole answer (DOMINANT TRAP):**
The pull here is strong and understandable: hearsay is inadmissible, the answer contains hearsay, so the whole answer must go. The trap works by collapsing "part of the answer is inadmissible" into "the entire answer must be struck." But that is not how motions to strike operate. The responsive core — Miriam's direct observation that she saw Timothy leave — is not hearsay and answered the question asked. Striking it along with the volunteered portion punishes the witness for answering correctly and removes admissible evidence. **Gold Key:** The motion must be targeted; "any answer containing hearsay must be completely removed" is a flat misstatement of the rule.

**Choice D — Deny; hearsay can't be struck once heard:**
The pull is a confused half-memory that once the jury has heard something, there's no remedy. That is not the law. A timely motion to strike is the correct procedural vehicle for dealing with inadmissible testimony that has already come in — including hearsay. The court can instruct the jury to disregard the stricken portion. Choice D flatly misstates the rule.

**Choice A — Deny; Miriam is the only witness with direct knowledge:**
The pull is a practical intuition: if Miriam is the only one who saw Timothy, striking her answer would be too harmful to the prosecution. But witness uniqueness — or the importance of testimony — has no bearing on whether a motion to strike should be granted. The motion-to-strike analysis asks whether the testimony was responsive and admissible, not whether the witness is indispensable. This rationale is a complete misfit: it applies a practical-value judgment where a procedural-admissibility rule governs.

## 9. Black-Letter Verification

**Tested rule:** A motion to strike must be directed at the specific testimony that is improper. When an answer contains both a responsive portion and a nonresponsive or inadmissible portion, the moving party must target the improper portion; the court should grant the motion only as to that portion. FRE 611(a) gives the court authority to control the mode and order of examination, including striking specific testimony. FRE 103(a)(1) requires that a timely objection or motion be "specific" — identifying the portion of evidence to which the party objects and the grounds for the objection.

**Hearsay and motions to strike:** Inadmissible hearsay that comes in through a witness's answer may be struck by timely motion. The mere fact that the jury has already heard the testimony does not preclude a motion to strike — the court can instruct the jury to disregard it. See FRE 103(a)(1).

**The overbroad-motion doctrine:** A motion to strike that sweeps up admissible, responsive testimony alongside the inadmissible portion should be denied as to the admissible portion. Courts regularly allow the responsive portion to stand while granting the motion as to the hearsay tail. This is the "targeted motion" principle that underpins the correct answer.

**Key agreement:** The credited answer (B) is consistent with FRE 611(a) and FRE 103(a)(1), and with standard federal evidence practice on overbroad motions to strike. No key disagreement. Bank validation verdict: PASS.

#### review_truth

A motion to strike must be targeted — when only part of an answer is nonresponsive or inadmissible, the court should strike only that portion while allowing the responsive, properly grounded part to stand.

---

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-EV-OVERBROAD-MOTION-STRIKE-01**

A motion to strike must be targeted: when an answer contains both a responsive portion (directly answers the question, based on personal knowledge) and a nonresponsive or inadmissible portion (volunteered hearsay), the court strikes only the inadmissible tail — the overbroad motion that seeks the entire answer is denied as to the responsive part.

- **Type:** rule
- **Unlocks:** dominant trap (Choice C: partial hearsay does not require striking the entire answer)
- **Trigger:** Motion targets "entire answer" when the witness answered the question correctly and then volunteered hearsay
- **Tested choice:** C

## Silver Key(s)

When the stem has a motion to strike the ENTIRE answer, first check whether any part of the answer was responsive.
