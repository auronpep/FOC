### 7. Right-Answer Explanation (Choice B)

A formal information initiates adversary proceedings — so the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had already attached before the preliminary hearing began. A hearing is a "critical stage" when counsel's absence might derogate the defendant's fair trial right. Here, a government forensic accountant testified, and the magistrate's probable-cause ruling determined whether Hannah would be bound over for trial. Cross-examination opportunities were permanently lost.

**Gold Key (GK-CRIMINAL-SIXTHCOUNS-01):** A preliminary hearing at which witnesses testify and the magistrate evaluates probable cause is a critical stage even though it does not determine guilt — the critical-stage test asks only whether significant rights can be forfeited in the absence of counsel.

Choice B correctly identifies both required elements: (1) formal adversary proceedings had begun via the information, and (2) the preliminary hearing — with live testimony and binding-over stakes — was a critical stage.

### 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice A** — The appeal is that a preliminary hearing is "really" a probable-cause screen, not a guilt determination, which makes it feel categorically different from the trial where the Sixth Amendment clearly applies. But the critical-stage doctrine does not ask whether the proceeding determines guilt; it asks whether the absence of counsel might compromise the fairness of the eventual trial. **Gold Key (GK-CRIMINAL-SIXTHCOUNS-01):** The test is whether significant rights can be lost — here, cross-examination of the government's key witness was forfeited. The probable-cause framing of the hearing does not remove it from the critical-stage category. Choice A's guilt-determination requirement is not the law.

**Choice C** — This reads as a reasonable middle ground: maybe the right did attach, but only because Hannah asked, making her request the triggering event. The appeal is procedural: if you don't invoke a right, you waive it. But the Sixth Amendment right to counsel at a critical stage is not waived by silence or failure to demand — and it is not created by a request. Once adversary proceedings begin and a critical stage occurs, the right exists regardless of whether the defendant asked. **Gold Key (GK-CRIMINAL-SIXTHCOUNS-02):** After the Sixth Amendment right attaches, it does not depend on the defendant's request. Hannah's request is evidence the court was on notice, not the source of the entitlement.

**Choice D** — This is a clean overclaim: the right applies only during trial. A student who knows anything about the critical-stage doctrine can cut this immediately. **Silver Key (SK-CRIMINAL-SIXTHCOUNS-01):** "Only during trial" is an absolute-restriction overclaim — the MBE never rewards limiting a constitutional right to a single proceeding when doctrine extends it further. Cut D first and move to the real clash between A and B.

### 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches at the initiation of formal adversary judicial proceedings (indictment, information, arraignment, or formal charge). Once attached, the right applies at all "critical stages" — proceedings where the absence of counsel might derogate the defendant's right to a fair trial, measured by whether significant rights may be affected and whether counsel's presence would help preserve them.

**Governing authority (lawyer-confirmation layer only):**
- *Coleman v. Alabama*, 399 U.S. 1 (1970): The Supreme Court held that a preliminary hearing is a "critical stage" of the criminal process requiring counsel. Key factors: witness examination that could preserve evidence, the ability to cross-examine witnesses, and the binding-over consequences.
- *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218 (1967): Established the "critical stage" doctrine — any confrontation critical to the prosecution where significant rights may be affected requires the presence of counsel.
- *Kirby v. Illinois*, 406 U.S. 682 (1972): Sixth Amendment right attaches at the initiation of formal adversary judicial proceedings.
- *Gideon v. Wainwright*, 372 U.S. 335 (1963): Sixth Amendment right to counsel is incorporated against the states through the Fourteenth Amendment.

**Rule unchanged:** The tested rule (Sixth Amendment attaches at formal proceedings; preliminary hearing with witnesses is critical stage; right does not depend on request; right extends beyond trial) is identical to the original. The credited outcome (YES, the right applied) is unchanged.

**Doctrinal currency:** No cases on the hotlist affect this item. The critical-stage doctrine is stable.
