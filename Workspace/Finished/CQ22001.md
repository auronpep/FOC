## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Twenty-three years ago, Daniel built a cedar workshop behind his home where he carved Scripture memory boxes for local homeschool families. Because an old survey stake had shifted, roughly one-third of the workshop stood across the boundary on the neighboring parcel. Lydia, the current owner of the neighboring parcel, has demanded that Daniel remove the part of the workshop that crosses onto her land. Daniel has refused, claiming that he has become the owner of that strip by adverse possession. A statute in the jurisdiction fixes the period for acquiring title by adverse possession at 20 years.

Lydia has brought legal proceedings seeking an order requiring Daniel to remove the encroachment. Daniel has counterclaimed for a judgment declaring him the owner of the portion of Lydia's land occupied by the workshop. Which of the following additional facts, if it were the only one true, would be LEAST likely to result in a judgment for Lydia?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

When a structure has openly occupied a strip for more than 20 years, which added fact least helps the record owner defeat adverse possession?

D. After part of the workshop had been built on the neighboring parcel, the owner of that parcel told Daniel that the workshop could remain there so long as Daniel used it for carving Scripture boxes.

### 6. Full right-answer explanation

B is the best answer. The call asks which added fact is least likely to produce a judgment for Lydia. Daniel's use had already run for 14 years before Lydia bought the neighboring parcel, and Lydia waited seven more years before suing. That gives Daniel 21 years of open occupation before the action, exceeding the 20-year statutory period in the question. A transfer of record title from Naomi to Lydia does not restart the clock or erase the prior years of adverse possession.

Gold Key: The adverse-possession clock runs against the landowner's title; a later buyer takes the parcel subject to the time already run unless the possession is interrupted or the use is permissive.

Silver Key: In a LEAST-likely call, sort the choices into facts that give the owner a permission defense and facts that merely change the identity of the record owner.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

A sells the tempting idea that Daniel's awareness of the boundary strengthens his hostile claim. The breaker is the second half: promising to move the workshop whenever asked makes the continued occupation look permissive, which gives Lydia a strong answer to hostility. Gold Key: Permission or a license defeats the hostile element while it lasts.

C looks neighborly rather than legal, so it tempts a student to ignore the honey. The phrase "in return for permission" makes the jars function like rent or consideration for a license, and occupation by permission is not hostile.

D sounds limited because Daniel could keep the workshop only while using it for one purpose. That limitation still comes from the owner's permission, so it undermines hostility rather than helping Daniel.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged. Adverse possession generally requires actual, open and notorious, exclusive, continuous, and hostile possession for the statutory period. The question supplies the 20-year period. The contested element in the wrong answers is hostility: permission, rent, or an agreement to leave on request makes possession subordinate to the owner's consent rather than hostile. The credited answer preserves the opposite point: the record owner's later conveyance to Lydia does not by itself interrupt Daniel's continuous adverse possession or reset the limitations clock.

Authority layer: Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code section 16.021(1) defines adverse possession as an actual and visible appropriation of real property, commenced and continued under a claim of right inconsistent with and hostile to another person's claim; section 16.021(3) defines peaceable possession as continuous possession not interrupted by an adverse suit; section 16.026 supplies a limitations example for land held in peaceable and adverse possession. Cornell LII's Wex summary likewise states that consent or license means possession is not hostile. Sources checked: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/GetStatute.aspx?Code=CP&Value=16.003 and https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/adverse_possession.
