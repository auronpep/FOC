### 1. Final Question

Timothy was arraighed on a charge of stealing rare books from a small Christian bookstore. The day before Timothy's trial, a police detective told Timothy that he would be placed in a lineup so that a witness could attempt to identify the thief. ### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**The correct answer is C.** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches once adversarial judicial proceedings have been initiated against a defendant — here, Timothy had already been arraigned. A post-charge lineup is a "critical stage" of the prosecution because of the substantial risk of misidentification and the difficulty of reconstructing lineup conditions at trial. The Supreme Court held in *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), that a defendant is entitled to have counsel present at a post-indictment lineup. The officers' statement that Timothy had "no right to a lawyer at a lineup" was incorrect, and the objection should be sustained.

**Gold Key (GK-CRIM-LINEUP-01):** Post-charge = counsel required. Once formal charges are filed, the Sixth Amendment right to counsel applies to any lineup. This is a bright-line rule: the attachment of the right is event-triggered (initiation of adversarial proceedings), not discretion-triggered.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice A (original B) — the dominant trap.** This choice sells the misconception that a defendant waives his constitutional rights by failing to physically resist authority. The detective's veiled threat ("non-cooperation will be noted") pressured Timothy into participating. But the law does not require a prisoner to resist commands from those in apparent authority as a condition of preserving constitutional rights. The choice frames the defendant's compliance as waiver — a backwards reading of the doctrine. **Gold Key GK-CRIM-LINEUP-01** defeats this trap: the right exists by operation of law once charges are filed; no amount of pressure from officers creates a waiver.

**Choice B (original D).** This choice states a true legal principle — an in-court identification *can* purge the taint of an improper lineup if it was arrived at by means independent of the lineup (*Gilbert v. California*, 388 U.S. 263 (1967)). But here, the victim's courtroom testimony was nothing more than a repetition of the lineup identification. There was no independent source. The choice applies a correct rule to facts where it does not fit — a classic half-truth trap.

**Choice D (original C).** This choice focuses on the physical composition of the lineup — five other men of similar height, build, and hair color. While lineup fairness is relevant to a due process challenge under *Stovall v. Denno*, 388 U.S. 293 (1967), it is not the basis for a Sixth Amendment right-to-counsel violation. The call asks whether the objection should be sustained, and the correct doctrinal ground is the denial of counsel, not lineup composition. This distractor tests whether the student can distinguish between two separate identification doctrines.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing rule:** The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches once adversarial judicial criminal proceedings have been initiated (arraignment, indictment, preliminary hearing, or formal charge). A post-charge lineup is a "critical stage" of the prosecution. *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218 (1967); *Gilbert v. California*, 388 U.S. 263 (1967).

**Application:** Timothy had been arraigned — formal charges were pending. The lineup was therefore a post-charge identification procedure. Under *Wade*, Timothy had a Sixth Amendment right to have his attorney present. The officers denied that right. The resulting identification is subject to exclusion unless the prosecution can show by clear and convincing evidence that the in-court identification had an independent source. Here, the victim's in-court identification was a direct product of the lineup — no independent source exists.

**Authority:** *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218 (1967) (right to counsel at post-indictment lineup); *Gilbert v. California*, 388 U.S. 263 (1967) (in-court ID must be shown independent of illegal lineup); *Stovall v. Denno*, 388 U.S. 293 (1967) (due process test for identification procedures).

**Doctrinal currency check:** No hotlist items triggered. The right to counsel at lineups remains good law post-*Wade*. *Kirby v. Illinois*, 406 U.S. 682 (1972), later held that the right does NOT attach pre-charge, confirming that the triggering event is the initiation of formal proceedings — consistent with this item's facts (post-arraignment).
