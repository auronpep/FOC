### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Lydia sought legal advice about lawful preservation, production, and objections. The subpoena raised the stakes, but it did not turn a private request for legal advice into nonprivileged testimony. **Gold Key:** a subpoena does not erase attorney-client privilege for lawful compliance advice; crime-fraud requires a request for help committing or covering up wrongdoing. **Silver Key:** separate the pressure fact from the purpose fact: legal process is pressure, while the client's purpose here is lawful compliance.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. Yes. After a subpoena has been served, communications with counsel about the subpoena are outside the privilege.**
This answer sells the panic move: once legal process arrives, the privilege must disappear. The breaker is the absolute timing rule. A subpoena does not automatically waive privilege or create a privilege exception.

**B. No, but only because the meeting is protected as attorney work product rather than as an attorney-client communication.**
This answer sounds lawyerly because discovery settings often involve work product. The breaker is the protected object. Lydia had a private lawyer-client communication for legal advice, so the answer does not need to swap in a neighboring doctrine and deny attorney-client privilege.

**D. Yes. The crime-fraud exception applies whenever a lawyer gives advice about documents that could be evidence.**
This is the dominant trap because it grabs a real exception and attaches it to subpoena pressure. The breaker is the missing wrongful-purpose fact. **Gold Key:** crime-fraud needs advice sought to further wrongdoing; documents being evidence does not make lawful compliance advice fraudulent.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule is Evidence, attorney-client privilege, and the verified deepest outline code is `35030205`. The source row listed `33040300`, which is a hearsay-exceptions code, but the actual legal issue is privilege, not hearsay. The corrected code appears verbatim in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md`.

For lawyer confirmation, Federal Rule of Evidence 501 supplies the federal privilege framework by directing courts to common-law privilege principles unless a constitution, statute, or Supreme Court rule provides otherwise: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_501. The Supreme Court in *Upjohn Co. v. United States*, 449 U.S. 383 (1981), confirms that attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications made to obtain legal advice, including communications tied to legal compliance problems and government demands: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/449/383/. *United States v. Zolin*, 491 U.S. 554 (1989), confirms the crime-fraud limit: privilege does not protect communications made to obtain advice for future or ongoing crime or fraud, but that exception turns on wrongful purpose, not on the mere existence of a subpoena: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/491/554.

Those authorities support the supplied official outcome. A subpoena does not itself defeat attorney-client privilege. The decisive distinction is lawful compliance advice versus advice sought to obstruct, conceal, falsify, or defraud.
