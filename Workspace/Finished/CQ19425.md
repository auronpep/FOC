## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** The statute gives Paul an abatement only if something that was part of the realty was removed or destroyed before closing. The cedar-press rig started as Timothy's personal property. Its size and usefulness on the land matter, but they do not automatically turn it into realty. **Gold Key:** fixture status asks whether the chattel was intended to become a permanent part of the land, with intent inferred from the surrounding facts. Timothy used the rig for his own work and did not intend it to become part of Ruth's land; Ruth agreed that she never treated it as part of the property. **Silver Key:** classify the removed item first; the phrase "all the realty" only reaches the rig if the rig became realty.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This is the dominant trap. It sees a two-ton, shed-sized rig used on the parcel and turns size plus land-use into fixture status. The breaker is the missing permanence intent. **Gold Key:** size, usefulness, and difficulty of moving are evidence, not the whole test; the decisive fixture question is whether the item was intended as a permanent accession to the land.

**C.** This answer reaches the right bottom-line result but sells the wrong legal shortcut. The trap is the idea that only physically fastened objects can be fixtures. Fixture analysis is not a bolt-counting rule; an item can be treated as a fixture or as personal property depending on the full relationship to the land, especially intent.

**D.** This answer locks onto the contract label. The words "all the realty" matter only after the item is classified as realty. **Silver Key:** classify first, then apply the sale language. Because the cedar-press rig did not become part of the realty, the contract phrase does not pull it into the land sale.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The source item tests the definition of a fixture: when a chattel has become part of real property for purposes of a land-sale contract and a price-abatement statute. The credited outcome is preserved. The removed equipment is not treated as part of the realty where the facts show that the person who installed and used it did not intend it to become a permanent part of the land, and the landowner shared that understanding.

Black-letter verification: A fixture is personal property that has become so related to real property that real-property law treats an interest in it as arising in the land. UCC section 9-102(a)(41) states the standard definition of fixtures as goods that have become so related to particular real property that an interest in them arises under real-property law. Source: [D.C. Code section 28:9-102(a)(41)](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/28%3A9-102). The leading American fixture case, *Teaff v. Hewitt*, 1 Ohio St. 511 (Ohio 1853), frames the fixture test around annexation, adaptation to the use of the realty, and intent to make a permanent accession to the freehold. Source: [*Teaff v. Hewitt*](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/teaff-v-hewitt-1071414734). A modern Ohio filing summarizing fixture law likewise states that courts examine annexation, appropriation to the use or purpose of the realty, and the owner's intention to make the chattel part of the realty. Source: [Ohio Supreme Court PDF filing discussing *Teaff* and *Masheter*](https://www.supremecourt.ohio.gov/pdf_viewer/pdf_viewer.aspx?pdf=932872.pdf&source=DL_Clerk&subdirectory=2022-1247%5CDocketItems).

Applied here, the cedar-press rig was Timothy's work equipment. It was large and useful on the parcel, but the facts point away from permanent accession: Timothy used it for his own craft, removed it before closing, and expressly had not intended it to become part of the land; Ruth also had not considered it part of the property. Because the rig was not part of the realty, the sale of "all the realty" did not include it, and the statutory abatement provision was not triggered. The supplied official key is confirmed. No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is triggered.

- Personal property crossover: a chattel can remain personal property even when used on land.

## review_truth

A chattel is not realty merely because it is large or useful on land; fixture status turns on whether it was intended to become a permanent part of the land.
