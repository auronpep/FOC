## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**B is correct.** The offered evidence is Lydia's prior sexual conduct with men other than Timothy. Federal Rule of Evidence 412 bars that kind of evidence in a criminal sexual-misconduct case unless a narrow exception applies. The recognized criminal-case exceptions are evidence offered to show the source of semen, injury, or other physical evidence; evidence of prior sexual behavior between the victim and the defendant offered on consent; and evidence whose exclusion would violate the defendant's constitutional rights. None of those facts is present here. Timothy admits the intercourse, so there is no source-of-physical-evidence dispute. And the defense offer concerns Lydia's sexual conduct with other men, not with Timothy. The evidence is therefore inadmissible.

**Gold Key:** When the evidence is the victim's sexual history with third parties, FRE 412 asks only whether an exception fits. If no exception fits, the evidence stays out for every proposed purpose.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. Yes, as substantive evidence and for the purpose of impeachment.**  
This choice is attractive because it treats the evidence as maximally useful: if it helps on consent and credibility, maybe the court should let it in. That is the trap. FRE 412 is not a balancing invitation on these facts. It is a categorical bar unless an exception fits. **Gold Key:** a student who remembers that third-party sexual-conduct evidence stays out unless the rule itself opens a door will cut this answer immediately.

**C. Yes, for the purpose of impeachment only.**  
This choice feels cautious, which is why students pick it. It sounds narrower than admitting the evidence to prove consent directly. But FRE 412 does not create a special impeachment carveout for the victim's sexual history with third parties. The statute still bars the evidence.

**D. Yes, as substantive evidence only.**  
This choice sells the classic bad instinct that a victim's past conduct with others can prove consent on this occasion. That is exactly the use FRE 412 is designed to block. The evidence is not about Lydia's prior conduct with Timothy, so the consent exception does not apply.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule is unchanged from the source item: in a criminal sexual-misconduct case, Federal Rule of Evidence 412(a)(1) generally makes inadmissible evidence offered to prove that the victim engaged in other sexual behavior. Rule 412(b)(1) creates narrow criminal-case exceptions, including evidence of specific instances to show the source of semen, injury, or other physical evidence; evidence of specific instances of sexual behavior between the victim and the defendant, if offered by the defendant to prove consent; and evidence whose exclusion would violate the defendant's constitutional rights. That rule confirms the credited outcome here. Timothy admits the intercourse, so the defense does not need third-party evidence to identify a physical source. The offered conduct is also with other men, so it does not fit the victim-defendant consent exception. Nothing in the facts suggests a constitutional-rights exception. The correct result is that the evidence is inadmissible. Authority floor: Fed. R. Evid. 412(a)(1), 412(b)(1)(A)-(C).
