## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** Timothy's January 2021 letter is a clear, unequivocal repudiation of his obligation under the requirements contract. Under UCC §2-610(a), when a party repudiates a contract before the time for performance, the aggrieved party may "await performance for a commercially reasonable time" or resort to any remedy for breach. Barnabas need not wait until the end of 2021 to sue; the anticipatory repudiation itself gives rise to an immediate cause of action. The contract is valid under UCC §2-306(1), which expressly recognizes requirements contracts, and the stated minimum of 1,500 boards fixes the quantity with sufficient certainty for damages purposes. Timothy's statement that he "will not be ordering any cedar boards" is an unambiguous refusal to perform — the textbook definition of anticipatory repudiation.

**Gold Key (GK-CONTRACTS-REPUDIATION-01):** Under UCC §2-610, an anticipatory repudiation occurs when a party makes a clear, unequivocal statement that they will not perform. The non-repudiating party may sue immediately — they do not have to wait for the performance date. This is critical on the MBE: if the stem contains an unambiguous repudiation, the seller's action is timely even though the performance period hasn't expired.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

Second, this contract contains a stated minimum (1,500 boards), which means that even if Timothy ordered nothing, damages for failure to order the minimum can be calculated with certainty under UCC §2-708. The "impossible to determine damages" framing is a Gold Key trap — **Gold Key GK-CONTRACTS-REPUDIATION-01** unlocks this: the existence of a stated minimum quantity means damages are ascertainable, and UCC §2-306 validates the contract form itself.

**B is the dominant trap and is incorrect.** This is the most attractive wrong answer because it exploits a natural intuition: the performance year hasn't ended yet, so maybe Timothy will change his mind. Timothy's letter — "I will not be ordering any cedar boards in 2021" — is not a maybe or a perhaps; it is a flat refusal. ## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing authority:**

2. **UCC §2-610(a)** — When either party repudiates the contract with respect to a performance not yet due, the aggrieved party may (a) for a commercially reasonable time await performance; (b) resort to any remedy for breach; or (c) in either case suspend his own performance. *See* LII, Cornell Law School, [https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-610](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-610).
