# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question
A mission congregation led by Timothy rented a spacious hall from a local landowner for a term of 10 years to host their ministry and annual revivals. A vital part of the ministry’s tradition was to refresh the sanctuary with a new, bright color each year to signify a new season of grace. The congregation explained to the landowner that this aesthetic renewal was central to their mission, and the landowner expressly agreed in the written lease to repaint the hall with a color chosen by the congregation during the week following each annual revival. The congregation agreed to pay $1,000 per month in rent. 

At the conclusion of the second year’s revival, the landowner refused to repaint the hall as agreed. Which of the following statements is true?

### 2. Distilled Core Question
A tenant in a 10-year lease has an express covenant from the landlord to repaint the premises annually. The landlord refuses to repaint in the second year. What is the tenant's remedy?

### 3. Final Answer Choices
A. The congregation may treat the lease as terminated and stop paying rent, provided they vacate the hall.
B. The congregation may not paint the hall themselves because altering the landowner's property would constitute ameliorative waste.
C. The congregation may paint the hall themselves and deduct the reasonable cost of labor and paint from their monthly rent.
D. The congregation may refuse to pay any further rent while remaining in possession of the hall until the repainting is completed.

### 4. Correct Answer
C (Original Key: B)

### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation
The landowner's refusal to repaint the hall constitutes a breach of an express covenant in the lease. In many jurisdictions, when a landlord breaches a specific covenant to maintain or repair the premises (and the breach does not rise to the level of a constructive eviction), the tenant's remedy is to perform the work themselves (self-help) and deduct the reasonable cost from the rent. This is often referred to as the "repair and deduct" remedy. Since the hall remains usable for its intended purpose, the breach does not constitute an actual or constructive eviction, making self-help the most appropriate and legally recognized remedy for the tenant to maintain the benefit of their bargain.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
- **A:** This choice is attractive to students who confuse any breach of a lease with a constructive eviction. Constructive eviction requires that the landlord’s breach be so substantial that the premises are rendered unsuitable for their intended purpose, forcing the tenant to vacate. A refusal to paint, while a breach, does not meet this high threshold of interference.
- **B:** This is the dominant trap. Students often recall the doctrine of waste, which prohibits a tenant from making substantial alterations that change the nature or value of the property. However, ameliorative waste (which increases value) is generally permitted if the landlord consents or if it's necessary for the tenant's use. More importantly, a simple repainting is a minor decorative change, not a structural or permanent alteration that would constitute waste, especially when the landlord already agreed to the practice in the lease.
- **D:** Rent withholding while remaining in possession is a remedy typically reserved for breaches of the implied warranty of habitability in residential leases (which doesn't apply to this commercial-style hall lease) or in cases of partial actual eviction. A minor breach of a decorative covenant does not permit the tenant to live rent-free.

### 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification
**Authority:** Restatement (Second) of Property, Landlord & Tenant § 7.1; *Marini v. Ireland*, 56 N.J. 130 (1970).
The "repair and deduct" remedy (self-help) is widely recognized for breaches of express covenants to maintain or repair. The covenant to paint is a "minor" covenant in that its breach does not deprive the tenant of the essence of the lease (possession and basic use), so it does not trigger the right to terminate (constructive eviction) or the right to withhold rent (warranty of habitability/partial eviction). The transform preserves the rule (self-help/deduct), the issue (landlord's breach of express covenant), and the credited outcome.

### 9. Rigor/Difficulty Note
The item maintains a moderate level of difficulty by requiring students to distinguish between different categories of landlord breaches and their respective remedies. The "waste" distractor (B) is particularly effective for testing the boundaries of tenant rights.

### 10. C3 Elimination Walkthrough
- **CUT:** D is eliminated because rent withholding without vacating is an extreme remedy not supported by a decorative breach. A is eliminated because termination requires a substantial interference (constructive eviction) which is not present.
- **CLASH:** Between B (Waste) and C (Self-help).
- **CALL:** Repainting is a minor alteration and was part of the original lease agreement, thus it cannot be waste. Self-help with rent deduction is the standard remedy for this type of breach.
