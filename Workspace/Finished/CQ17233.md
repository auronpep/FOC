### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. The burden of persuasion on the helmsman claim never leaves the plaintiff, and a prior inconsistent statement used to impeach her does not create a new procedural burden on her credibility. **Gold Key: impeachment affects the weight and credibility of testimony for the jury; it does not shift the burden of persuasion on the underlying claim.** **Silver Key: when an answer invents a procedural rule the stem never authorized (a brand-new burden of proving credibility, a brand-new duty for the judge to decide truthfulness, or a brand-new rule that impeachment nullifies testimony), it is answer-fabrication, not a real evidence rule.** That is why the court should let the impeachment go to the jury's evaluation while leaving the burden of persuasion on Ruth's claim exactly where it started.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
C is attractive because it borrows a real procedural vocabulary — burden, preponderance — and tells the student a clean story: the plaintiff was impeached, so she now bears a new burden of proving credibility. The breaker is that no rule shifts the burden of persuasion on the underlying claim to a new burden of proving the witness's credibility after impeachment. The Gold Key kills this trap.

A is attractive because students hear "judge decides preliminary questions" under FRE 104 and slide from admissibility into truthfulness. The breaker is that witness truthfulness is not a FRE 104 preliminary question; it is exactly the kind of weight-and-credibility call the rule reserves for the jury under FRE 104(e). The Silver Key would have caught the fabricated judge-as-truth-finder role.

D is attractive because it dramatizes impeachment as disqualification — once a witness has been impeached, the story goes, her testimony must be ignored. The breaker is that impeachment goes to weight, not admissibility, and the witness's personal knowledge of what she saw is not destroyed by a minor misstatement about a collateral detail like the wind direction.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Evidence, Presentation of Evidence, Burden of production and burden of persuasion, and the verified outline code is `31010102`. The governing framework sits in the Federal Rules of Evidence. **Fed. R. Evid. 301** supplies the default rule that in a civil case the party who would bear the burden of persuasion on a claim continues to bear it; a presumption does not shift the burden of persuasion in a civil case unless a federal statute or the Constitution so provides. **Fed. R. Evid. 104(a)** assigns the court, not the jury, certain preliminary questions — qualifications of a witness, existence of a privilege, and admissibility of evidence — and **Fed. R. Evid. 104(e)** explicitly preserves the parties' right to introduce before the jury evidence that is "relevant to the weight or credibility of other evidence." **Fed. R. Evid. 613** governs impeachment by prior inconsistent statement and treats the prior statement as going to credibility rather than as a procedural device that strips the witness of personal knowledge. The result is that the burden of persuasion on Ruth's helmsman-negligence claim remains with her throughout, and the deposition inconsistency on wind direction goes to the jury's evaluation of her testimony, not to a new burden, not to the judge, and not to automatic disregard. The official key stands.
