## 6. Full right-answer explanation

**B** is correct. **Gold Key:** a requirements quantity is not an empty quantity; it is measured by the buyer’s actual good-faith requirements. **Silver Key:** in a 3-vs-1 no-contract array, do not let “no fixed number” erase a quantity phrase that the stem already gives you. ## 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells the familiar “courts check fairness” instinct: $18 per slab sounds like something a court might examine before enforcing the deal. The breaker is that “adequate price” is not the formation gate; the trap imports a fairness review instead of answering whether a requirements quantity can work.

**C.** This choice sells the price-quote frame because the email contains a price and a supply arrangement. The breaker is the stem’s actual language: Lydia sent a buyer’s offer, Barnabas accepted it, and the fight is over the flexible quantity term, not whether a seller’s price quotation invited an offer.

**D.** This choice sells the dominant trap: no fixed number feels like no quantity at all. **Gold Key:** requirements language can supply the quantity through good-faith requirements, so “always too indefinite and illusory” is the overclaim.

## 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The verified rule is unchanged. UCC § 2-306(1) treats a buyer’s requirements term as measuring quantity by actual good-faith requirements, subject to disproportionate-quantity limits. UCC § 2-204(3) also allows a sales contract to survive open terms when the parties intended to contract and there is a reasonably certain remedial basis. Those rules confirm the credited outcome: no fixed slab number is needed where the agreement measures quantity by Lydia’s requirements. ([Legal Information Institute][1])

The adequacy trap is also unchanged. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 79 states that, once consideration exists, there is no additional requirement of equivalence in the values exchanged; courts ordinarily do not police price fairness as a formation condition. ([Open Casebook][2])
