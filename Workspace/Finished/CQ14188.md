### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. A party served with a specific Rule 34 request for production of electronically stored information must produce the responsive documents; it may not simply hand over its entire undifferentiated set of files and force the requesting party to do the work of identifying which ones match the request. Gold Key: Rule 33(d)'s business-records option (allowing a party to point the requester to records when the burden of deriving the answer is substantially the same for both sides) applies only to interrogatories, not to Rule 34 document and ESI production requests. Silver Key: when the stem shows a targeted Rule 34 request for a specific product-line slice and the responder offers the whole chronological pile, cut every answer that treats the difficulty of hunting through the pile as the deciding factor; those answers import the wrong rule.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because it sounds like a fair "who has it easier" test that should control discovery fights. The breaker is that once a specific Rule 34 request has been served, the responding party bears the identification burden; there is no need to compare relative difficulty unless the responder first shows the ESI is not reasonably accessible because of undue burden or cost under Rule 26(b)(2)(B).

C is attractive because it feels equitable to let the requesting party do the hunting when the files are already organized chronologically and the requester "knows what she wants." The breaker is the same: the Rule 33(d) business-records option that would allow this kind of burden shift applies only to interrogatories. A specific Rule 34 request for ESI keeps the production and identification obligation on the responding party.

D is attractive because "advertising strategy" sounds like a potentially vague term that a publisher might legitimately need defined before it could know what to pull. The breaker is that there are no facts suggesting the publishing house could not understand what "advertising strategy ... concerning the Heritage Family Discipleship Kits" meant; the request described the subject matter and the specific product line with reasonable particularity.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Civil Procedure, Pretrial Procedures, E-Discovery, and the verified outline code is 95070404. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 34(a) authorizes a party to serve a request for production of documents and electronically stored information. Under Rule 34(b)(2)(E), unless otherwise stipulated or ordered, a party must produce documents or ESI as they are kept in the usual course of business or must organize and label them to correspond to the categories in the request. The responding party may not simply dump undifferentiated files and shift the burden of identifying the responsive subset to the requesting party. Rule 26(b)(2)(B) provides a limited safe harbor only when the responding party shows the ESI is "not reasonably accessible because of undue burden or cost"; the court then decides whether to order production anyway after a proportionality analysis. Rule 33(d)'s option to produce business records in lieu of answering an interrogatory (when the burden of ascertaining the answer is substantially the same for both parties) does not apply to Rule 34 requests for production. The source explanations correctly identify that there is no need to resolve relative difficulty once a specific Rule 34 request has been served. The credited answer and the trap structure are unchanged from the original.
