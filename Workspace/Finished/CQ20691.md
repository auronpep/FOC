## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**B is correct.** Barnabas is the proximate cause of Timothy's death. Barnabas's shove was the but-for cause of the fatal internal bleeding: without the shove, the bleeding would not have occurred. Timothy's blood-clotting disorder did not intervene after the shove as a new independent cause. **Gold Key:** In criminal causation, a defendant takes the victim as found. A hidden physical vulnerability may make the result unforeseeably severe, but it does not break causation when the defendant's act set the fatal injury in motion.

**Silver Key:** In a 2x2 causation answer array, do not stop at "Yes" versus "No." Compare the reasons. The correct "Yes" answer names the legal bridge between the minor act and the severe result; the wrong "Yes" answer gives a non-dispositive time fact.

### Choice D

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing rule:** Criminal homicide requires actual causation and proximate causation. A defendant's conduct is an actual cause when the result would not have occurred but for that conduct. Proximate cause asks whether the result is sufficiently connected to the defendant's conduct and not cut off by an independent superseding cause. Under the thin-skull or eggshell-skull principle, the defendant takes the victim as found. A preexisting physical vulnerability that makes the injury worse, or makes death occur when an ordinary person would have survived, does not itself break the causal chain.

**Authority floor:** Model Penal Code Section 2.03 addresses causal relationship between conduct and a result and frames proximate-cause limits through whether the actual result is too remote or accidental to bear on liability. *People v. Stamp*, 2 Cal. App. 3d 203 (1969), is a leading criminal-causation thin-skull authority: the court held that a victim's predisposing physical condition and the defendant's ignorance of it do not destroy criminal responsibility when the defendant's felonious act is a substantial cause of death. The opinion also quotes a proximate-cause instruction stating that an unlawful act may be a proximate cause of death even if the victim was already weakened by disease or physical condition and a person in sound condition probably would not have died. See https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/2/203.html. Cornell's Wex summary of the eggshell-skull rule likewise states the common-law principle that a defendant is responsible for harm proximately caused by the wrongful act even if the victim's reaction is uncommon or unexpectedly severe. See https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/eggshell_skull_rule.

**Key verification:** The official key is correct. The tested legal issue remains criminal proximate causation in a homicide setting. The transformed fact pattern preserves the same causal chain: minor unlawful physical contact, unknown preexisting blood condition, fatal internal bleeding, normal person would have suffered only a bruise, defendant unaware. The credited outcome remains that the defendant is the proximate cause of death.
