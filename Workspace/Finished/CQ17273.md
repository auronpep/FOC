# CQ17273 — Rule 49(b)(3) Christian Divergent Transform

## 1. Final question

**Stem.** At a paid author event in Ruth's small Christian bookstore, Daniel installed a freestanding display rack. The rack tipped during the event, scattering signed books across the aisle. Ruth sued Daniel in federal court for negligence and sought damages for the lost sales she said the collapse caused. The federal civil jury returned a general verdict for Ruth. In written answers, the same jury found that Daniel used ordinary care and that Ruth's lost sales were not legally caused by the display rack's collapse. The answers are consistent with each other but inconsistent with Ruth's general verdict. Which statement best describes the court's options?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

In a federal civil case, a jury returns a general verdict for one side, but its written answers are consistent with each other and inconsistent with that general verdict. What may the court do?

## 3. Final answer choices (A–D, post-shuffle)

A. The court must dismiss the case for lack of subject matter jurisdiction.

B. The court must always enter judgment on the general verdict and ignore the written answers.

C. The court may enter judgment according to the written answers, direct the jury to further consider its answers and verdict, or order a new trial.

D. The court must question each juror individually and then choose the answer most jurors prefer.

## 5. Correct answer

**Correct answer: C** (original key B).

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

**C** is correct. Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 49(b)(3), when the written answers are consistent with each other but one or more is inconsistent with the general verdict, the court has three defined options: enter judgment according to the answers, direct the jury to further consider its answers and verdict, or order a new trial.

**Gold Key:** Rule 49(b)(3) does not make the general verdict automatically win. If the written answers are internally consistent but conflict with the general verdict, the court may enter judgment on the answers, send the jury back, or order a new trial.

**Silver Key:** Treat the correct option as a three-option menu. Reject answers that say the general verdict “always” controls or that invent a polling procedure.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A** sells a wrong-frame trap: it treats a verdict-answer mismatch as if the court lost subject-matter jurisdiction. The stem gives a trial-procedure problem after a federal jury verdict, not a jurisdiction defect. Rule 49(b)(3) supplies the court's options; it does not require dismissal.

**B** is the dominant trap. It is attractive because the general verdict sounds final, so a student may treat the written answers as decorative. The breaker is that Rule 49(b)(3) says the court **may** choose among three options when the answers are internally consistent but conflict with the general verdict; it does not say the court must always enter judgment on the general verdict.

**D** sells a practical-looking repair: poll each juror and choose the answer most jurors prefer. The rule does not create that mandatory polling procedure. The court's options are the Rule 49(b)(3) menu, not a juror-by-juror preference count.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

**Authority floor:** Fed. R. Civ. P. 49(b)(3) provides the governing rule. Cornell LII's current Rule 49 text states that when “the answers are consistent with each other but one or more is inconsistent with the general verdict,” the court may: “approve … an appropriate judgment according to the answers,” “direct the jury to further consider its answers and verdict,” or “order a new trial.” Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_49.

The source question and the Christian variation test the same black-letter rule: a federal civil jury returns a general verdict, gives written answers that are consistent with each other, and those answers conflict with the general verdict. The correct legal outcome is unchanged: the court may enter judgment according to the answers, send the jury back for further consideration, or order a new trial. The bank validation verdict is **PASS** because the transformed item agrees with the supplied official key after the post-shuffle Letter Map.

No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is triggered. Rule 49 was restyled in 2007, and the Cornell notes describe that restyling as stylistic only.
