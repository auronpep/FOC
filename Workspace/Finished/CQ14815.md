### 6. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Ruth's statement was made as soon as the stage-riser panel crashed down and Daniel stepped back. The statement identifies Daniel and relates directly to the startling event while Ruth is still under its stress. That is the excited-utterance path. Gold Key: under the excited-utterance exception, availability is not the gate; the gate is whether the statement relates to a startling event and was made while the stress of that event was still operating. ### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

A. The prior-testimony point feels procedural, and it uses a visible fact: Ruth testified but the prosecutor did not ask her about the statement. The breaker is that the FRE 803 exceptions do not require the declarant to be unavailable or first questioned about the statement; the immediate stress facts supply the admissibility route.

C. The hearsay objection feels natural because Naomi is repeating Ruth's out-of-court identification of Daniel. The breaker is that the statement fits a hearsay exception: it was made immediately after the stage-riser strike and related to the startling event.

D. This is the dominant trap. Ruth's words sound like death-language, and the answer correctly hints that a declarant need not actually die for a dying declaration. ### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited answer is legally sound under the Federal Rules of Evidence. A statement is hearsay if the declarant does not make it while testifying at the current trial or hearing and the proponent offers it for the truth of what it asserts. Fed. R. Evid. 801(c), https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_801. Ruth's out-of-court statement is offered to prove that Daniel was the attacker, so the hearsay rule is triggered.

FRE 803 supplies exceptions that do not depend on the declarant being unavailable. FRE 803(2) admits an excited utterance: a statement relating to a startling event or condition, made while the declarant is under the stress caused by that event. Source: Fed. R. Evid. 803(2), https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803. Ruth's cry came as soon as the panel crashed down and Daniel stepped back; it related to the attack and identified Daniel while the stress of the attack was still active. Naomi's testimony is therefore admissible as an excited utterance.

The dying-declaration trap does not change the result. FRE 804(b)(2) applies only if the declarant is unavailable, and then only in a homicide prosecution or civil case when the statement concerns the cause or circumstances of the believed impending death. Source: Fed. R. Evid. 804(a), 804(b)(2), https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_804. Ruth testified, so she is not unavailable, and Daniel is charged with aggravated assault, not homicide. The tested rule, legal issue, credited outcome, and distractor mechanics remain unchanged from the source item. No researched authority contradicts the supplied key.

## Silver Key(s)

SK-EVIDENCE-YES-BECAUSE-01: When two choices say yes, do not stop at the result; test whether the because-clause names the exception the facts actually satisfy.
