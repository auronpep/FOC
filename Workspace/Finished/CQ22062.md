## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
C is correct. A foreclosure can cut off subordinate interests only if the holders of those interests are made parties or otherwise bound by the foreclosure. Grace Finance may have lost priority to Cedar Bank by recording late, but Cedar Bank did not name Grace Finance in the foreclosure. That omission means Grace Finance's mortgage was not wiped out by Cedar Bank's sale. Stephen took the building subject to the omitted mortgage, so Grace Finance may foreclose its surviving interest. Gold Key: omission from the foreclosure is the survival fact. Silver Key: when the choices argue purchase-money, late recording, and no notice, lock onto the post-default event: who was joined in the foreclosure?

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
A is attractive because the first loan financed Mary's purchase, and purchase-money mortgages often have special priority rules. The breaker is that purchase-money status is not the reason this mortgage survived Cedar Bank's foreclosure. Grace Finance survives because it was omitted from that foreclosure, not because the loan funded the purchase.

B is attractive because Grace Finance recorded after Cedar Bank, so Cedar Bank's mortgage had priority. The breaker is that priority and survival after foreclosure are different steps. Late recording may make Grace Finance subordinate to Cedar Bank, but a subordinate mortgage is not extinguished unless the subordinate mortgagee is joined or otherwise bound in the foreclosure.

D is attractive because Stephen had no knowledge of Grace Finance's mortgage when he bought at the foreclosure sale. The breaker is that a foreclosure-sale purchaser receives only the title the foreclosure can deliver. Because Grace Finance was omitted, Cedar Bank's foreclosure could not deliver the building free of Grace Finance's surviving mortgage.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer
The tested issue is Real Property, Mortgages and Security Devices, Foreclosure > Parties and priorities. The verified outline code is `83050603`, which appears verbatim in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md`.

The governing exam rule is that foreclosure by a senior mortgagee generally cuts off junior interests only when those junior interest holders are joined or otherwise bound in the foreclosure. An omitted junior or subordinate mortgagee is not bound by the foreclosure, and the purchaser at the foreclosure sale takes subject to that surviving interest. Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages § 7.1 cmt. a identifies the general foreclosure effect on junior interests; courts also state the omitted-party rule directly. In Citizens State Bank of New Castle v. Countrywide Home Loans, Inc., 949 N.E.2d 1195 (Ind. 2011), the Indiana Supreme Court explained that an omitted junior lienholder is not bound by the foreclosure and the purchaser takes subject to existing liens. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/indiana/supreme-court/2011/06291102rdr.html

Additional confirmation: Washington courts cite Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages § 7.1 cmt. a for the rule that foreclosure terminates junior interests when the holders are named, and that senior interests remain on the property. Source: https://www.courts.wa.gov/opinions/pdf/304019.pub.pdf

Those authorities confirm the supplied key. Grace Finance recorded after Cedar Bank, so the variation treats Grace Finance as subordinate to Cedar Bank. But Cedar Bank did not name Grace Finance in the foreclosure. The foreclosure sale therefore did not eliminate Grace Finance's mortgage, and Grace Finance may foreclose the surviving mortgage. The tested rule, legal issue, trap structure, and credited outcome are unchanged.
