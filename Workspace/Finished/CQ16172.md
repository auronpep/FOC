## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Timothy was indicted and arrested for robbing a chapel bookstore. At his first appearance before a magistrate judge, Timothy was told of the robbery charge and told that counsel would be appointed for him the next day. After the appearance, the police required Timothy to stand with other prisoners in a lineup. Each person in the lineup had to wear a gray sweatshirt and say, "Place the offering bag on the counter." Witnesses from the bookstore identified Timothy as the robber. The next day, Timothy was arraigned on the charge.

Timothy argues that his Sixth Amendment right to counsel was violated when he was denied counsel at two critical stages: the first appearance before the magistrate judge and the lineup identification. Was Timothy's Sixth Amendment right to counsel violated?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Was the right to counsel violated at an uncounseled lineup held after the defendant had appeared before a magistrate, even though the initial appearance itself was not the violating stage?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. Yes, based only on the denial of counsel at the first appearance.

B. Yes, based only on the denial of counsel at the lineup.

C. Yes, based on the denial of counsel at both stages of the proceeding.

D. No.

### 5. Correct answer

B (original key letter C).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Timothy's Sixth Amendment right to counsel had attached by the time of the lineup because formal adversary proceedings had begun. Gold Key: after attachment, the government may not conduct a critical stage without counsel. A post-attachment lineup is a critical stage. The first appearance mattered because it started the right-to-counsel clock, but the absence of counsel at that appearance did not itself create the violation on these facts. Silver Key: separate the attachment event from the critical-stage event; the first can trigger the right without itself being the stage that violates it.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because the first appearance is where the right attaches. The breaker is that attachment is not the same as a critical-stage violation; the lineup is the uncounseled critical stage.

C is the dominant trap. It captures the true sequence: first appearance, then lineup. Gold Key: the Sixth Amendment violation occurs when counsel is absent at a critical stage after attachment, not merely because counsel was not present at every attachment event. Silver Key: mark the first appearance as the trigger and the lineup as the violation.

D is attractive because counsel was promised for the next day, which can make the delay sound reasonable. The breaker is that the police conducted a lineup before counsel was appointed, and a post-attachment lineup is a critical stage.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Rothgery v. Gillespie County, 554 U.S. 191 (2008), holds that an initial appearance before a judicial officer where the accused is told of the formal accusation and liberty is restricted marks the start of adversary judicial proceedings for Sixth Amendment attachment. United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), holds that a post-indictment lineup is a critical stage at which the accused is entitled to counsel. Kirby v. Illinois, 406 U.S. 682 (1972), confirms the attachment line: the right applies after adversary judicial proceedings have begun.

Timothy had already been indicted and had appeared before a magistrate before the lineup. The lineup required him to stand with others and repeat words for eyewitness identification. That post-attachment lineup was a critical stage, so counsel was required there. The first appearance itself was the attachment event, but on these facts it was not the violating critical stage. The correct answer remains violation based only on the lineup.
