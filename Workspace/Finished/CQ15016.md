## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The source row is Q15016, a Real Property / Rights in Land item keyed to no liability because the plaintiff has no legal right to sunlight, fresh air, or view. The verified outline code is **81020101**, "Non-Possessory Rights and Interests in Land > Easements > Nature and Type."

The tested rule is the traditional American rule on light, air, and view. In *Fontainebleau Hotel Corp. v. Forty-Five Twenty-Five, Inc.*, the court described a lawful fourteen-story addition casting shadows over a neighboring property and held that, absent contractual or statutory obligation, a landowner has no legal right to the free flow of light and air across a neighbor's land.[1] The court also stated that where there is no such legal right, a useful structure does not give rise to damages or an injunction merely because it cuts off light, air, or view.[1]

A modern solar-access wrinkle exists. *Prah v. Maretti* recognized that a solar-heated residence stated a private nuisance claim when neighboring construction obstructed sunlight used as an energy source.[2] This item does not use that wrinkle. Ruth has rental-value loss from shade, not a solar-energy system; the stem also removes statutes, ordinances, easements, subsidence, and construction mishaps. The supplied key is therefore consistent with the traditional MBE rule.
