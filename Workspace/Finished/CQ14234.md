## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** The denial of a permit for a residence for adults moving from prison sentences into parole supervision does not classify by race, national origin, alienage, sex, legitimacy, or another suspect or quasi-suspect category. It also does not burden a fundamental constitutional right. The action is therefore reviewed under rational basis. Under that standard, Daniel, the challenger, must show that the denial is not rationally related to a legitimate governmental interest.

**Gold Key:** Equal protection does not move to strict or intermediate scrutiny just because the fact pattern involves housing, reentry, or a sympathetic need. Without a recognized suspect/quasi-suspect class or fundamental right, the lane is rational basis and the challenger bears the burden.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The governing constitutional provision is the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment. The Supreme Court's general rule is that legislation or official classification is presumed valid and is sustained if rationally related to a legitimate state interest, unless it uses a suspect classification or burdens a fundamental right. In *City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, Inc.*, 473 U.S. 432 (1985), the Court applied rational-basis review to a special-use-permit denial involving a group home and rejected heightened scrutiny where no suspect/quasi-suspect classification or fundamental right was present. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/473/432/

Housing is important, but it is not a fundamental constitutional right under the Supreme Court's equal protection doctrine. *Lindsey v. Normet*, 405 U.S. 56 (1972), rejected a claimed constitutional guarantee of access to housing of a particular quality and treated the housing-related regulation as social/economic legislation subject to rational basis. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/405/56/

Under rational-basis review, the burden is on the challenger. *Heller v. Doe*, 509 U.S. 312 (1993), states that classifications not involving fundamental rights or suspect lines carry a strong presumption of validity and must be upheld if any reasonably conceivable state of facts supplies a rational basis; the challenger must negate conceivable bases. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/312/

The source item's tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: because the permit denial does not implicate a recognized suspect or quasi-suspect class and does not burden a fundamental right, rational basis applies, and the person challenging the denial bears the burden of showing no rational relation to a legitimate governmental interest.
