### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.** The notes are out-of-court written assertions offered to prove the thing they report: that real people confused the two trade names. That is hearsay.

**Gold Key:** A record does not become a business record merely because employees wrote it at work. FRE 803(6) requires a regular practice of making that kind of record as part of a regularly conducted activity, and FRE 803(5) requires a testifying witness who cannot recall well enough.

**Silver Key:** Do not stop at "excluded." In Evidence, a choice can land on the right result with the wrong route. The clean route here is hearsay with no exception.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This is the trap that treats any employee-made work note as a business record. The missing piece is the regular-practice foundation: these are special confusion notes created because the trade-name dispute matters, not ordinary records of the coffee business's regular activity. **Gold Key:** FRE 803(6) asks whether making this kind of record was a regular practice of the activity, not whether a business employee wrote it.

**B.** This choice reaches the exclusion result, so it feels safe. The reason does not match the defect. Confusion evidence is probative in a trade-name case, and the stem does not give a 403 overload problem. **Silver Key:** In a same-result trap, test the route, not just the destination.

**C.** This choice sees "notes" and reaches for recorded recollection. That exception requires a witness with insufficient present memory, plus a record made or adopted when the matter was fresh and accurate. The stem offers the packet itself, not a witness using a past recollection record to fill a memory gap.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The transformed item preserves the original rule, legal issue, trap structure, and credited outcome. The offered notes are out-of-court written assertions offered to prove that marketplace confusion occurred, so they fit FRE 801(c)'s hearsay definition. No exception in the answer array admits them.

FRE 803(6) admits records of regularly conducted activity only when the record was made at or near the time by someone with knowledge, kept in the course of a regularly conducted activity, and making that record was a regular practice of that activity. These special confusion notes do not satisfy the regular-practice foundation merely because employees prepared them. FRE 803(5) admits recorded recollection only when a witness once knew the matter but now cannot recall well enough to testify fully and accurately, the record was made or adopted when fresh, and it accurately reflects the witness's knowledge. The stem gives no such testifying-witness memory foundation. FRE 403 does not supply the reason for exclusion because the confusion evidence is probative and the facts do not show unfair prejudice or confusing waste.
