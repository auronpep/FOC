## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. The lawyer is conducting direct examination of Lydia's own friendly witness. The question does not neutrally ask what Stephen saw; it tells him the answer Lydia wants and asks for confirmation. Gold Key: FRE 611(c) generally bars leading questions on direct examination except as necessary to develop testimony, and ordinarily permits leading questions on cross-examination or for hostile/adverse witnesses. Silver Key: match the objection to the form of the question before reaching neighboring evidence labels. Because the question suggests the desired answer on direct, the best objection is leading.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is wrong because nonresponsive is an objection to a witness's answer. Here the witness has not answered yet; the objection is to the lawyer's question.

B is attractive because the question contains the words "you saw," and students may look for a perception foundation problem. The better objection is leading because the form of the question itself suggests the desired answer to a friendly witness on direct.

D is wrong because the question asks Stephen about what he personally saw at the intersection. It is not asking him to repeat an out-of-court statement.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The credited outcome is unchanged: the best objection is leading. Federal Rule of Evidence 611(c) provides that leading questions should not be used on direct examination except as necessary to develop the witness's testimony, while leading questions ordinarily are allowed on cross-examination and when a party calls a hostile witness, an adverse party, or a witness identified with an adverse party. The source item states that the witness is friendly and is being examined on direct, so the ordinary direct-examination restriction applies.

The question, "You saw Barnabas enter the intersection against the red light before the crash, didn't you?" suggests the factual answer in the lawyer's own words and asks the witness to confirm it. That is the defect. Hearsay does not fit because the question asks what Stephen saw, not what another person said. Lack of personal knowledge is not the best immediate objection because the question itself assumes Stephen saw the event; the question-form defect is more direct. Nonresponsive does not fit because no answer has been given.

Sources checked: https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-evidence-dec-1-2024_0.pdf and https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_611.
