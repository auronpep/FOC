## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

On March 2, Martha and Silas signed a written contract for the sale of Martha's riverside fig orchard parcel. When the contract was signed, Silas handed Martha a bank draft drawn on an out-of-region bank for the entire purchase price. As the contract directed, Martha executed a deed to the parcel and deposited it with a licensed escrow firm, instructing the firm to deliver the deed to Silas as soon as the draft cleared the bank.

The draft cleared the bank on March 14. Because a flood had closed the bank's offices, the escrow firm did not learn that the draft had cleared until March 20 and did not deliver the deed to Silas until March 21. On March 16, Silas executed a deed purporting to convey the parcel to Philip.

A statute in the state makes it a misdemeanor for any person "to execute any document purporting to convey an interest in real estate that the person executing the document did not actually hold at the time the document was executed, whether or not that person knew he or she did not hold the interest the document purported to convey." If Silas is charged with violating this statute by executing the deed to Philip on March 16, should he be convicted?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

When a deed is held by an escrow agent to be delivered once a condition outside the grantor's control is met, when does title pass to the buyer?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. Yes, because Silas did not become the owner until March 20, when the escrow firm learned that the draft had cleared.

B. No, because when his draft cleared, his title related back to March 2, when the contract of sale was executed.

C. Yes, because Silas did not become the owner until the deed was delivered to him on March 21.

D. No, because his title passed on March 14, when the draft cleared.

### 5. Correct answer

D (original key C).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

D is correct. When a grantor deposits a deed with a commercial escrow agent to be delivered on a condition outside the grantor's control, title passes to the grantee automatically the moment the condition is satisfied. Here the condition was the draft clearing, which happened on March 14. Silas therefore owned the parcel from March 14, so when he executed the deed to Philip on March 16 he actually held the interest, and the statute is not violated. Gold Key: a deed left with an escrow agent to be delivered on a condition outside the grantor's control passes title automatically when the condition is met, not when the agent learns of it or physically hands over the deed, and not back to the date of the sales contract. Silver Key: the problem is built on "delivery," but find the single date the escrow condition was satisfied and place title there before testing each choice.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

C is attractive because the entire fact pattern is about delivery of the deed, so the instinct is that title passes when the deed is physically handed over, which the stem dates to March 21. The breaker is the escrow rule: when delivery turns on a condition outside the grantor's control, title passes when the condition is met, not when the paper changes hands.

A is attractive because the escrow firm could not act on something it did not know, so it feels as though ownership must wait until the firm learned the draft had cleared on March 20. The breaker is that the agent's knowledge is irrelevant; the condition was satisfied on March 14 whether or not anyone yet knew it.

B is attractive because in many jurisdictions the buyer's interest and the risk of loss arise when the sale contract is signed, which points to March 2 and still yields an acquittal. The breaker is that this is risk-of-loss and equitable-conversion thinking; legal title does not relate back to the contract date, so the reason is wrong even though the bottom line is "no."

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is unchanged: Silas held title when he executed the second deed, so he should not be convicted. A deed deposited with an escrow agent to be delivered to the grantee upon a condition the grantor cannot control is a valid conditional delivery; legal "delivery" is a question of the grantor's present intent to be bound, not of physical hand-over. When the stated condition is performed, the grantee's title vests automatically. Applying that here, the condition was the draft clearing, satisfied on March 14; title passed to Silas that day. Two corollaries dispose of the "guilty" choices: the escrow firm's later knowledge (March 20) and its later physical delivery (March 21) do not control the moment of passage. And while risk of loss may shift to a buyer at contract under equitable conversion in some states, legal title does not relate back to the contract date, which disposes of the contract-date choice even though it reaches the right result.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: the conditional-delivery-in-escrow rule and the relation-back doctrine are settled common-law conveyancing principles (see Stoebuck & Whitman, The Law of Property section 11.3, deeds delivered in escrow; and the widely applied relation-back doctrine, under which a deed delivered out of escrow is deemed delivered as of the escrow deposit when needed to do equity, e.g., on the grantor's death). attorney_review: true — the support here is treatise and general doctrine rather than a single confirmed controlling cite; a jurisdiction-specific primary authority should be confirmed before relying on the authority line.
