## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** Esther knew before the conveyance that the city had already ordered the delivery bay shut down after 60 days. That fact was material because losing the delivery bay substantially reduced the parcel's value. It was also hidden from Timothy in the legally important sense: the pending city notice was not posted, was not discussed, and was known to Esther but not to Timothy. **Gold Key:** in a land sale, a seller's silence can be actionable when the seller knows a hidden material fact or pending restriction that seriously affects the property's value and the buyer does not know it. **Silver Key:** sort the claim labels by lane; this is not a title-warranty problem, not a consideration problem, and not a two-sided mistake. The fit is failure to disclose.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### A. breach of the covenant of warranty. Dominant trap - 32% inherited.

This choice sounds strong because Timothy received a general warranty deed and then lost an important use of the building right after closing. The breaker is the title/condition lane. **Gold Key:** a warranty deed's covenants protect title, not every undisclosed land-use problem or lost business feature. ### B. mutual mistake.

### D. failure of consideration.

This choice tries to convert a bad bargain into a no-consideration bargain. The breaker is visible in the stem: Timothy paid substantial consideration, and Esther conveyed the parcel by general warranty deed. The later loss in value does not erase the fact that consideration was exchanged.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

**Rule verified.** A seller's nondisclosure can support rescission or fraud-type relief when the seller knows a material fact affecting the property's value or use, the buyer does not know the fact, and the fact is not readily available to the buyer. Restatement (Second) of Contracts sec. 161(b) treats nondisclosure as equivalent to an assertion when the speaker knows disclosure would correct the other party's mistake as to a basic assumption and silence fails good faith and fair dealing. Source: https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/1st%2BSemester/LAW%2B505-002%2B%E2%80%93%2BContracts%2BI/R2C%2B%C2%A7%2B161.

**Land-sale confirmation.** Johnson v. Davis, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985), adopted the Lingsch v. Savage disclosure rule for real property and held that a seller who knows facts materially affecting property value, not readily observable and not known to the buyer, has a duty to disclose. The same opinion explains that the duty applies to all forms of real property, new and used. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/supreme-court/1985/65330-0.html.
