# CQ14816

## 1. Final Question

Martha suffered a fractured wrist in a scuffle with Stephen while they were both spectators at a gospel softball tournament. If Martha sues Stephen for personal injury damages, which of the following actions must the trial court take if Martha requests it?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

When a party requests exclusion of witnesses, which evidence-control action is mandatory rather than discretionary?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

A. Refuse to let Stephen cross-examine Martha's orthopedic expert on matters not covered on direct examination of the expert.

B. Exclude nonparty eyewitnesses from the courtroom during the testimony of other witnesses.

C. Require the production of a writing used before trial to refresh a witness's memory.

D. Prevent Stephen's principal eyewitness from testifying, upon a showing that six years ago the witness was convicted of perjury and the conviction has not been the subject of a pardon or annulment.

## 4. Credit

Credited answer: B.

## 5. Correct Answer

B is correct. Federal Rule of Evidence 615 makes witness exclusion mandatory when a party requests it, subject to the rule's listed exceptions. Nonparty eyewitnesses are ordinary witnesses, so the court must exclude them from hearing other witnesses' testimony.

## 7. Source Mechanics

The source item tested the difference between a mandatory trial-control order and nearby discretionary or impeachment-based evidence rules. The wrong answers invoked true-adjacent rules but overstated what the court must do: FRE 611(b) allows discretionary inquiry beyond direct, FRE 612 makes pretrial refresh-writing production conditional on justice requiring it, and FRE 609 supplies impeachment by conviction rather than a witness-disqualification rule.

## 9. Rule Proof

Federal Rule of Evidence 615 provides that, at a party's request, the court must exclude witnesses from the courtroom so that they cannot hear other witnesses' testimony, except for the rule's protected categories.

Federal Rule of Evidence 611(b) says cross-examination should stay within direct examination and credibility, but the court may allow additional matters as if on direct examination.

Federal Rule of Evidence 612 gives an adverse party options when a writing refreshes memory while testifying, and for pretrial use only if the court decides justice requires those options.

Federal Rule of Evidence 609 concerns use of criminal convictions to attack truthfulness. Federal Rule of Evidence 601 starts from general witness competency, so a perjury conviction is impeachment material rather than a basis to bar the witness from testifying.

## 10. Choice Forensics

A is attractive because students remember the limited-scope language of cross-examination, but it fails the call. FRE 611(b) gives the trial judge discretion to allow extra matters, so the court is not required to refuse the cross-examination.

B is correct because FRE 615 uses mandatory language for requested exclusion of ordinary nonparty witnesses.

C is attractive because writings used to refresh memory often trigger production rights. It fails because before-trial use depends on the court deciding that justice requires production.

D is attractive because perjury is a dishonesty conviction and strong impeachment material. It fails because impeachment does not equal incompetency or exclusion from testifying.

## 11. Student Diagnostic

A student who misses this item is likely treating every evidence-control rule as mandatory once a party asks. The repair move is to sort each answer by verb: must exclude under FRE 615, may allow under FRE 611(b), may require under FRE 612 before trial, and may admit/impeach under FRE 609. The winning answer is the only one with a mandatory action tied to the precise request.

## 12. Authority Floor

- Federal Rule of Evidence 615: mandatory requested exclusion of witnesses, with exceptions.
- Federal Rule of Evidence 611(b): discretionary allowance of cross-examination beyond direct and credibility matters.
- Federal Rule of Evidence 612(a): pretrial refresh-writing production only if justice requires.
- Federal Rule of Evidence 609(a): conviction evidence is an impeachment rule.
- Federal Rule of Evidence 601: every person is competent unless the rules provide otherwise.

## 13. Review Truth

On request, FRE 615 requires excluding ordinary nonparty witnesses so they cannot hear other witnesses' testimony; the neighboring rules about cross-examination scope, pretrial memory-refresh writings, and conviction impeachment do not create mandatory exclusion.
