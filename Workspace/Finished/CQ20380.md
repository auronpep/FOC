### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**B is correct.** A federal court sitting in diversity does not take a state's self-applied "procedural" or "substantive" label at face value. Under *Erie* and its progeny, the court runs its own analysis: first, it asks whether a valid federal directive (a Federal Rule of Civil Procedure or federal statute) is directly on point — if so, and the directive is valid under the Rules Enabling Act and the Constitution, the federal directive controls regardless of the state label. If no federal directive is on point, the court applies the modified outcome-determination test measured against the twin aims of *Erie*: discouraging forum-shopping and avoiding the inequitable administration of the laws. The state calling its pre-suit-demand rule "procedural" is relevant context but does not bind the federal court or trigger automatic disregard.

> **Gold Key (Labels Don't Control Erie):** A state's own characterization of its rule as "procedural" or "substantive" never binds a federal court in diversity. The court classifies the rule by its function — first checking for a valid federal directive on point, then applying the twin-aims analysis. This is the unlock that kills the conclusive-label trap in answer D.

> **Silver Key (Measured-Beats-Absolute Read):** Three choices state self-executing shortcuts ("ignore all," "every…always," "labels are conclusive"); only B states a fact-sensitive analysis. On an Erie label question, the measured "you must analyze" choice is the answer — the shortcut never is.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing framework:** The *Erie* doctrine and the Rules of Decision Act, 28 U.S.C. § 1652, require a federal court sitting in diversity to apply state substantive law. Whether a given rule is "substantive" for *Erie* purposes turns on the rule's function and the policies underlying *Erie*, **not** on the label the state attaches to it.

**Authority floor:**
- *Erie R.R. Co. v. Tompkins*, 304 U.S. 64 (1938) — federal courts in diversity apply state substantive law; there is no general federal common law.
- *Guaranty Trust Co. v. York*, 326 U.S. 99 (1945) — outcome-determination test for the substance/procedure line.
- *Byrd v. Blue Ridge Rural Elec. Coop.*, 356 U.S. 525 (1958) — countervailing federal interests are weighed; the label is not mechanical.
- *Hanna v. Plumer*, 380 U.S. 460 (1965) — the outcome-determination test is read in light of the **twin aims** (discouraging forum-shopping; avoiding inequitable administration of the laws); where a valid Federal Rule of Civil Procedure is on point, it controls so long as it falls within the Rules Enabling Act, 28 U.S.C. § 2072, and the Constitution.

*Hanna* squarely rejects a mechanical, label-driven application of *Erie*: the substantive/procedural classification is not controlling, and the court's task is functional. A state legislature's declaration that its rule is "procedural" therefore does not require a federal court to disregard the rule automatically; the federal court conducts the independent two-track inquiry (valid federal directive on point → it controls; otherwise twin-aims analysis).

**Doctrinal currency:** The *Erie/Hanna* framework is stable; the most recent major refinement (*Shady Grove Orthopedic Assocs. v. Allstate Ins. Co.*, 559 U.S. 393 (2010)) confirms, not disturbs, the principle that the state label is not controlling and a valid FRCP on point governs. No hotlist item applies. **PASS** on currency.
