## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**B is correct.** Under UCC § 2-210(1), a party may perform his duty through a delegate unless the other party has a substantial interest in having the original promisor perform. Here, Timothy is a well-known and experienced bar-prep tutor — comparable to Peter — and there is no indication Hannah had a substantial interest in Peter personally performing. Under UCC § 2-210(4), Peter's assignment of the contract to Timothy operated as both an assignment of rights and a delegation of duties, and by accepting, Timothy became obligated to perform. Under UCC § 9-406(1), once Hannah received notification of the assignment, her obligation could only be discharged by paying Timothy (the assignee), not Peter (the assignor). Since Hannah already paid Timothy, Timothy has no further claim against Hannah — but the question asks about Timothy's action against Peter for the fee Peter collected. Peter had no right to collect from Hannah after notice of assignment; Timothy, as assignee, is entitled to the payment. **Gold Key:** Under UCC § 9-406(1), once an account debtor receives authenticated notification that the amount due has been assigned, payment to the assignor no longer discharges the obligation — the account debtor must pay the assignee.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing Law:** UCC Article 2 — Sale of Goods (applied by analogy to service contracts on the MBE; the common-law principles mirror UCC § 2-210).

**Authority:** UCC § 2-210 (Delegation of Performance; Assignment of Rights); UCC § 9-406(1) (Discharge of Account Debtor; Notification of Assignment).

**UCC § 2-210(1):** "A party may perform his duty through a delegate unless otherwise agreed or unless the other party has a substantial interest in having his original promisor perform or control the acts required by the contract."

**UCC § 2-210(4):** "An assignment of 'the contract' or of 'all my rights under the contract' ... is an assignment of rights and ... is a delegation of performance of the duties of the assignor and its acceptance by the assignee constitutes a promise by him to perform those duties."

**UCC § 9-406(1):** "An account debtor ... may discharge its obligation by paying the assignor until, but not after, the account debtor receives a notification, authenticated by the assignor or the assignee, that the amount due or to become due has been assigned and that payment is to be made to the assignee. After receipt of the notification, the account debtor may discharge its obligation by paying the assignee and may not discharge the obligation by paying the assignor."

**Analysis:** The original question tests whether a valid assignment + delegation occurred and whether the obligor's payment to the assignor discharged the obligation after notice. The credited answer (D) is correct: the assignment and delegation were valid because the delegatee was comparable and there was no substantial interest in the original promisor performing. Payment to the assignor after notice does NOT discharge the obligation under UCC § 9-406(1). The third-party beneficiary distractor is wrong because the assignee's rights derive from the assignment, not from third-party beneficiary status.

**Choice A: "Peter, because Hannah paid the session fee to Peter, which discharged her obligation for that session."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** Hannah received notice of the assignment from Peter. The stem says Peter "informed Hannah of the sale." 1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** Timothy does prevail, but this choice gives the wrong reason. Timothy's rights don't come from the Peter-Hannah contract — that contract was between Peter and Hannah, not made to benefit Timothy. Timothy's rights come from Peter assigning the contract to him.

#### Wrong Answer Recovery Paths

**If you picked A:** You thought paying the original party always discharges the obligation. But once you receive notice of an assignment, the rule flips — you must pay the assignee. Review: UCC § 9-406(1).

**If you picked C:** You thought the obligor must expressly consent to a delegation. The actual rule: delegation is valid unless the obligor has a substantial interest in the original party performing. Express consent is not required.

**If you picked D:** You confused an assignee with a third-party beneficiary. An assignee receives rights through assignment of the contract. A third-party beneficiary receives rights because the original contract was made to benefit them. These are different doctrines.
