## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** Congress needs a constitutional power hook. The Necessary and Proper Clause helps Congress choose means for carrying out a federal power; it does not create a free-floating police power over any problem Congress calls national.

**Gold Key:** Necessary and Proper is not freestanding. First find the enumerated or otherwise vested federal power, then ask whether the chosen means helps execute it.

**Silver Key:** In congressional-power questions, do the power-hook check before policy usefulness. If the stem says the law is justified only by Necessary and Proper and supplies no power hook, cut answers that substitute usefulness, national scope, or state-law timing.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

## 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

Article I, Section 8, Clause 18 authorizes Congress to make laws necessary and proper for carrying into execution the federal powers vested by the Constitution. [1] The Clause supports implied means for executing other powers; it is not itself a standalone grant of legislative police power. Constitution Annotated explains that the Clause is an extension of other federal powers and is not an independent grant of congressional power, citing *Kinsella v. United States ex rel. Singleton*, 361 U.S. 234, 247 (1960). [2] *McCulloch v. Maryland* confirms that Congress may choose appropriate means to carry legitimate constitutional ends into execution, but the end must still be within federal power. [3]

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row. In both items, Congress acts only under Necessary and Proper, with no commerce, tax, spending, federal-property, or other enumerated-power hook. The strongest objection is therefore that Necessary and Proper is not freestanding.
