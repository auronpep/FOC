### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Paul is guilty of conspiracy because he agreed with Barnabas and Stephen to commit robbery, then took a planning step by stealing and staging the getaway van. The agreement did not disappear just because Paul was arrested for an unrelated parole violation before the shop entry.

Under **Gold Key: Pinkerton liability for the planned attempt**, a conspirator can be liable for a co-conspirator's crime committed in furtherance of the conspiracy while the conspiracy continues. Paul's custody was not an affirmative withdrawal or defeat of the plan. **Silver Key: test each crime separately.** In an "attempt, conspiracy, both, or none" array, do not stop after finding one true crime. ### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row. The source tests two linked inchoate-crime rules: conspiracy liability for the agreement to rob, and co-conspirator liability for the attempted robbery later committed in furtherance of that agreement.

Conspiracy requires an agreement to commit a crime and the purpose of promoting or facilitating that crime. Under Model Penal Code section 5.03, a person is guilty of conspiracy when, with the purpose of promoting or facilitating a crime, he agrees that one or more conspirators will engage in conduct constituting that crime, attempt, or solicitation. The source row also includes an overt act: stealing and staging the getaway vehicle.

Attempt requires intent to commit the target offense plus conduct that crosses the line from preparation into attempt. Model Penal Code section 5.01 treats a substantial step strongly corroborative of criminal purpose as attempt. Barnabas and Stephen entered the store, threatened the cashiers, and demanded the cash; that is attempted robbery even though police stopped them before they escaped with money.

Pinkerton v. United States, 328 U.S. 640 (1946), confirms that a conspirator may be held liable for substantive offenses committed by co-conspirators in furtherance of the unlawful agreement while the conspiracy continues. The Supreme Court emphasized that no direct participation is required when the substantive offense is within the scope and in furtherance of the conspiracy. Here, the attempted robbery was exactly the planned object, and Paul had not withdrawn. His arrest for a parole violation was not a voluntary act disavowing or defeating the plan.

Authority sources used: Pinkerton v. Fills: agreement, overt act, co-conspirator liability, attempted target offense, answer-array discipline.

Adjacent to master: attempt, accomplice liability, robbery, withdrawal from conspiracy.

## crossover_intersection_map

Attempt: Barnabas and Stephen crossed from preparation to attempted robbery when they entered, threatened the cashiers, and demanded money.

Robbery: the target offense involves taking property by force or threat.

Accomplice liability: the item resembles aiding another, but the decisive route is conspiracy/Pinkerton liability.

## review_truth

A conspirator is guilty of conspiracy when he joins the criminal agreement and can also be liable for a co-conspirator's attempted target offense committed in furtherance of that agreement.
