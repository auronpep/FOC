## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning
The tested rule is unchanged: the implied warranty of habitability is a residential landlord-tenant doctrine and does not make a landlord liable to a commercial tenant merely because leased business premises contain an unsafe condition. Restatement (Second) of Property: Landlord and Tenant section 5.1 frames the habitability warranty in the residential lease setting. Javins v. First National Realty Corp., 428 F.2d 1071 (D.C. Cir. 1970), recognized the warranty for covered housing leases, not for ordinary commercial premises. Cornell Wex likewise summarizes the doctrine as applying to residential leases: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/implied_warranty_of_habitability. Javins is available at https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/428/1071/172969/.

The source item's credited answer was "No, because this is a lease for a commercial warehouse." The variation keeps that same legal issue and outcome: Ruth's company leased a commercial workshop, so the court should not rule for Ruth's company on an implied-warranty-of-habitability claim. The research does not contradict the supplied key. Some jurisdictions recognize separate commercial doctrines such as suitability or contract-based remedies, but the claim here is specifically the implied warranty of habitability, so the MBE rule remains clean.
