### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)
A is correct. The prosecution is offering two earlier, unrelated violent acts during its case-in-chief. Stripped of any distinctive connection to the charged attack, that proof can do only one thing in the jury's mind: suggest that because Stephen acted violently before, he acted violently here. That is the forbidden propensity inference, and the evidence is excluded as improper character evidence. **Gold Key:** prior bad acts come in for a non-propensity purpose (identity, plan, motive, and the rest of the MIMIC list) ONLY when a distinctive feature or genuine common scheme ties them to the charged crime; "he is a violent person" is propensity, not a permitted purpose. **Silver Key:** read the four choices as a two-by-two grid — two "excluded," two "admitted" — and when the stem gives no distinctive link, cut BOTH "admitted" choices as disguised propensity, then within the two "excluded" choices take the one resting on improper character rather than a procedural-only theory.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
B is attractive because "identity, plan, or motive" are genuine FRE 404(b)(2) purposes, and a student who has memorized that list reaches for it the moment any prior act appears, treating the label as a license. The breaker is that those purposes require a real, distinctive link between the prior acts and the charged crime; throwing stones through a window and swinging a sickle at a stranger share nothing with the iron-weight attack except that all are violent, so invoking identity/plan/motive is non-responsive to what these facts actually prove. **Gold Key:** the 404(b)(2) purposes need a distinctive connection — general violence is not a "plan" or an "identity." (Dominant trap — preserved with care.)

C is attractive because it points at a real rule — specific instances of conduct can be raised on cross-examination — and pairs it with the correct bottom line (excluded), so it feels doubly safe. The breaker is that the rule it borrows is the wrong one: specific-act cross-examination under FRE 608(b) reaches only conduct probative of truthfulness and only after the defendant testifies, and here Stephen has not testified and these acts say nothing about honesty, so "only during cross-examination" is simply false. **Silver Key:** a choice that reaches the right outcome through a borrowed, ill-fitting rule is still wrong on a "because" question.

D is attractive because it states out loud the very inference the prosecution wants — that the prior acts show Stephen is prone to violence — and that inference feels intuitively relevant. The breaker is that propensity is the one use FRE 404(b)(1) flatly forbids; naming the prohibited purpose does not convert it into an admissible one.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested rule, the legal issue, and the credited outcome are unchanged from source row 14898. Under FRE 404(b)(1), evidence of a crime, wrong, or other act is not admissible to prove a person's character in order to show that on a particular occasion the person acted in conformity with that character — the propensity bar. FRE 404(b)(2) permits such evidence for another, non-propensity purpose (motive, opportunity, intent, preparation, plan, knowledge, identity, absence of mistake), but only where the act is genuinely probative of that purpose; for identity in particular the prior conduct must share a distinctive "signature" or modus operandi with the charged crime, and for a common plan there must be a true overarching scheme. Here the two prior incidents have no distinctive feature or scheme connecting them to the iron-weight attack, so 404(b)(2) is not triggered and the evidence reduces to character/propensity proof that 404(b)(1) excludes. The accused may "open the door" with good-character evidence, but nothing in the stem shows he has done so. As to choice C, FRE 608(b) allows inquiry into specific instances of conduct only on cross-examination and only when probative of truthfulness; the defendant has not testified and these violent acts are not truthfulness evidence, so that route is unavailable. Authority (lawyer-confirmation layer only): FRE 404(b)(1)-(2); FRE 608(b); Huddleston v. United States, 485 U.S. 681 (1988) (other-acts evidence under 404(b) judged by the conditional-relevance standard). No research contradicted the supplied official key (original B / new A). This area is not on the doctrinal-currency hotlist; FRE 404(b) was amended in 2020 only as to pretrial-notice procedure, which does not affect the substantive propensity bar applied here.

### 2. The 17-section student case study
**program_frame**  
C3 case study on the FRE 404(b) character-evidence propensity bar. The item teaches that unrelated prior bad acts offered in the prosecution's case-in-chief are excluded as improper character evidence, and that the 404(b)(2) purposes (identity, plan, motive) apply only when a distinctive link ties the prior acts to the charged crime.

**student_access_test**  
Every elimination, label, and explanation is written so a smart 10-year-old with only the C3 CUT → CLASH → CALL framework, the subject overlays, and the two taught tiny anchors (Gold Key for the MIMIC-link requirement; Silver Key for the excluded/admitted grid read) can see why B, C, and D fail and why A succeeds, without outside legal doctrine.

**question_data**  
See the Question YAML barmatrix_row block for the exact stem, call, and post-shuffle choices.

**distilled_core_question**  
When the prosecution, in its case-in-chief, offers a defendant's unrelated prior violent acts that share no distinctive link with the charged crime, is the evidence admissible as proof of identity, plan, or motive, admissible to show propensity, excludable only via cross-examination, or excluded as improper character evidence?

**call_and_prediction**  
The call is "This evidence should be ..." Prediction: students gravitate to the MIMIC bait (B — the dominant trap) because the 404(b)(2) list is memorized and feels like a license, or to the open-propensity choice (D). The resolving signal is the absence of any distinctive feature or scheme connecting the prior acts to the charged attack.

**trigger_facts**  
- Criminal prosecution; the State is offering in its case-in-chief.  
- The defendant has not testified.  
- Two prior violent acts are offered (stones through a widow's window; a sickle swung at a traveler).  
- The prior acts share no distinctive feature or common scheme with the charged iron-weight attack.

**governing_c3_lane**
