## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The source item is an Evidence question under Contents of Writings, Recordings, and Photographs, and the verified outline node is `34050101`, Contents of Writings, Recordings, and Photographs > Requirement of the Original > Best evidence rule.

Federal Rule of Evidence 1002 requires an original writing, recording, or photograph when a party proves that item's contents. The Advisory Committee note confirms the trigger: an event may be proved by nondocumentary evidence even though a record of the event exists. FRE 602 supports a witness's testimony based on personal knowledge. FRE 801(d)(2)(A) covers an opposing party's own statement, and FRE 801(d)(2)(E) covers a coconspirator statement made during and in furtherance of the conspiracy. *United States v. Inadi*, 475 U.S. 387 (1986), confirms the federal treatment of coconspirator statements under Rule 801(d)(2)(E). The researched authorities do not contradict the supplied official key.

Here, Agent Hannah personally heard the conversation. The government is not asking her to recite the contents of the recording as a recording; it is asking her to recount the conversation she perceived. The recording's partial inaudibility is not the operative reason for admissibility. The official answer remains correct: the testimony is admissible because the original-document rule does not require proof through the recording.

## outline_mastery_map

Placement: `34050101` - Contents of Writings, Recordings, and Photographs > Requirement of the Original > Best evidence rule.

Adjacent mastery: FRE 602 personal knowledge; FRE 801(d)(2) opposing-party and coconspirator statements; FRE 1004 exceptions only after the original-document rule is triggered.

## crossover_intersection_map

- **FRE 602:** personal knowledge supplies the testimony foundation.
- **FRE 801(d)(2):** hearsay detour is defeated by opposing-party and coconspirator-statement rules.
- **FRE 1002:** original-document rule applies to contents proof, not every event that was recorded.
- **Recording quality:** may matter to the recording's usefulness, but it is not the reason the firsthand testimony is admissible.

## review_truth

The original-document rule asks whether a party is proving a recording's contents, not whether a recording exists somewhere in the background.
