# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

Timothy conveyed his lakeside cottage to his sister Martha for life, remainder to his daughter Esther. Timothy later died, devising his entire estate to Esther. Martha removed a plain iron wall sconce in the cottage's entryway and installed a stained-glass Advent lantern that had long been in her family. She stored the plain sconce in the cellar. Martha then told Barnabas, a close friend, and Esther that after Martha's death the Advent lantern should be removed and the plain sconce put back. Martha died, devising her entire estate to Barnabas and naming him personal representative of her estate. Acting as personal representative shortly after Martha's death, Barnabas removed the Advent lantern and reinstalled the plain sconce. Esther sued to have the Advent lantern reinstalled. Who will likely prevail?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A life tenant installs an heirloom fixture, makes clear it should be removed after death, and the personal representative removes it promptly. Does the estate or the remainderman get the fixture?

B. Barnabas, because he had the right to remove the Advent lantern within a reasonable time after Martha's death.

C. Esther, because the Advent lantern could not be legally removed after Martha died.

D. Barnabas, because of the doctrine of accession.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** Martha was a life tenant. She installed the Advent lantern during her life estate, stored the original sconce, and stated that the Advent lantern should be removed after her death. Barnabas removed it shortly after Martha died. **Gold Key:** a fixture annexed by a life tenant may be removed by the life tenant's estate within a reasonable time after the life estate ends when the annexation was temporary. **Silver Key:** in a fixture-removal array, lock three facts before choosing: who installed it, whether the installation was meant to be temporary, and whether removal happened within a reasonable time.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice invents a restriction. Trade fixtures are a familiar fixture category, but the rule here is not limited to trade fixtures. **Gold Key:** life-tenant fixture removal can apply to a temporarily annexed fixture, not only to business or trade fixtures.

**C.** This is the dominant trap. It treats Martha's death as an automatic freeze that makes any later removal illegal. **Gold Key:** the removal window can extend for a reasonable time after the life estate ends. Martha's stated intent and Barnabas's prompt removal keep the fixture on the removable side.

**D.** This choice uses a property-law label that does not answer the call. The problem is not who owns transformed materials under accession. It is whether a life tenant's estate may remove a fixture the life tenant annexed temporarily.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The source row is Q15015, a Real Property / Rights in Land item keyed to the personal representative. The tested rule is the life-tenant fixture-removal rule: a life tenant who annexes a fixture temporarily may remove it during the life estate, and the life tenant's estate or representative may remove it within a reasonable time after the life estate ends.

Cornell's Wex defines a fixture as a movable chattel that becomes part of real property because of attachment, and identifies attachment, relation to the property's use, and intention as fixture-classification factors. citeturn176859search0 The life-tenant removal rule is reflected in current state property statutes. Rhode Island General Laws § 34-17-4 states that fixtures annexed by a life tenant may be removed during the life estate or within a reasonable time after it ends, and the official Rhode Island legislature page carries that text. citeturn729371search4 Massachusetts General Laws chapter 184, § 12 states the same rule and adds that the creating instrument may provide otherwise. citeturn729371search7

The variation preserves the legal issue and credited outcome. The Advent lantern replaces the original sconce as Martha's heirloom fixture. Her statement that it should be removed and the original restored shows temporary intent. Barnabas removed it shortly after death, so the removal was within the post-life-estate reasonable-time window.
