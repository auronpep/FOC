## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Daniel runs a private Jonah-themed escape-room booth for Christian conventions. He called Lydia, a wholesaler of ordinary catalog-stock olivewood fish keychains, and said that he would buy 800 keychains for his booth if Lydia could get them to him within seven days. Daniel did not mention a price. At the time of the call, the market price for 800 of those keychains was about $9,600.

Lydia did not say yes or no on the call. Six days later, her driver arrived at Daniel’s storage unit with the 800 keychains and an invoice for $9,600. Before the driver arrived, the convention organizer had canceled Daniel’s booth space. Daniel refused to receive the keychains or pay the invoice.

Is there an enforceable contract between Daniel and Lydia?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Buyer orally orders ordinary goods worth more than $500, leaves the price open, seller delivers, buyer refuses to accept, and no writing exists. Is the deal enforceable?

### 3. Final answer choices

C. Yes, because leaving the price open does not prevent a valid sale-of-goods contract when the parties otherwise intended to contract.

D. No, because the parties did not put their agreement in writing.

### 5. Correct answer

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.** This is a sale of ordinary goods for about $9,600, and no writing exists. **Gold Key:** a sale-of-goods deal priced at $500 or more must satisfy the UCC writing requirement unless an exception applies. Lydia’s delivery did not solve that problem because Daniel refused to receive and accept the keychains. **Silver Key:** lock the call word **enforceable** before rewarding a Yes answer; formation and enforceability are separate gates.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** The delivery-plus-reliance story feels fair because Lydia showed up with the goods after Daniel’s phone call. The breaker is the missing buyer-side acceptance: Daniel refused to receive the keychains, so seller effort alone does not answer the UCC writing gate. **Gold Key:** shipment by itself is not the partial-performance exception when the buyer refuses to receive and accept the goods.

**B.** The price-term answer targets the most visible missing term and makes the oral deal look incomplete. The breaker is the open-price rule: for a sale of goods, a missing price can be filled if the parties otherwise intended a contract.

**C.** This is the dominant trap because it states a real UCC open-price idea and was the most-selected distractor in the source row. **Silver Key:** the choice answers whether a missing price blocks formation, but the call asks whether the deal is enforceable despite no writing.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The governing rule is unchanged. UCC § 2-201(1) requires a sufficient writing signed by the party against whom enforcement is sought for a sale-of-goods contract priced at $500 or more, subject to listed exceptions. UCC § 2-201(3)(c) enforces an unwritten deal only for goods for which payment was made and accepted or goods that were received and accepted. Here, Daniel refused receipt and payment, so that exception does not apply. UCC § 2-305 separately permits a sale contract even though the price is not settled when the parties otherwise intended to contract; that rule defeats the no-price distractor but does not satisfy the writing requirement. ([Legal Information Institute][1])

The credited legal outcome remains: **no enforceable contract**.

**Student move:** Check the writing gate before rewarding the open-price rule.
**Tiny rule:** Open price can be filled, but the UCC writing gate still blocks enforcement unless an exception applies.
**Trap:** Yes because open price does not defeat contract formation.
**Confidence:** PURE_ANCHOR.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-CONTRACTS-FISH-KEYCHAINS-01**
For goods priced at $500 or more, the UCC writing gate must be satisfied before the oral deal can be enforced. Mere shipment does not unlock the partial-performance exception when the buyer refuses to receive and accept the goods.

**GK-CONTRACTS-FISH-KEYCHAINS-02**
Open price and writing are separate gates. UCC § 2-305 can fill a missing price term, but it does not satisfy the UCC § 2-201 writing requirement.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-CONTRACTS-FISH-KEYCHAINS-01**
Lock the call word enforceable before grading the Yes choices. A choice can prove formation with an open price and still miss the separate writing gate.
