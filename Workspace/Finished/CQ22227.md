## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** Under FRE 612, when a witness refreshes her recollection before testifying by reviewing a writing, the trial court has discretion to require the writing to be produced for inspection by the opposing party. The witness here refreshed her memory by reviewing her interview notes before coming to court. On proper motion, the court should direct that the notes be brought into court for the defendant's attorney to inspect. This is the core procedural safeguard of FRE 612: the opposing party gets to see what the witness used to refresh, so the opposing party can effectively cross-examine. The notes themselves are not automatically admitted into evidence — the witness testifies from her refreshed memory, not from the writing. But the court can and should order production for inspection.

**Gold Key (GK-EVIDENCE-REFRESH-01):** FRE 612 gives the opposing party the right to inspect writings used to refresh recollection *before* trial — the writing is a tool for cross-examination, not an exhibit. The witness testifies from memory; the notes are for the lawyer's use.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (original D) — ISSUE_SENSE / misfit:** This choice is attractive because students see "notes from an interview with the plaintiff's attorney" and think "admission." But an admission under FRE 801(d)(2) is a statement by a party offered against that party. Daniel is a witness, not a party. His notes are not the plaintiff's statement — they are the witness's own notes of a conversation. The admission doctrine simply does not apply here. This misfit is a classic bait: it uses a real evidence concept (admissions) in a context where it doesn't belong.

**B (original A) — EAR_FALSITY / flat_misstatement:** This is the dominant trap. Students hear "notes" and "before trial" and immediately think of the best evidence rule (FRE 1002). Under the best evidence rule, if a witness were testifying to the *contents* of a writing, the original would be required unless unavailable. But Daniel is not testifying to the contents of the notes — he is testifying from his own memory after refreshing it by looking at the notes. The best evidence rule applies to proof of contents, not to refreshment of recollection. Because the best evidence rule is inapplicable, there is no basis to strike his testimony. **Gold Key GK-EVIDENCE-REFRESH-01** unlocks this: the notes are for cross-examination, not for proving contents, so the best evidence rule never enters the analysis.

**D (original C) — EAR_FALSITY / flat_misstatement:** This choice misstates FRE 612. Students who haven't internalized the rule might think that because the notes haven't been offered into evidence, they can't be produced. But FRE 612 exists precisely for this situation — writings used to refresh recollection before trial are subject to production *regardless* whether they've been offered into evidence. The rule overrides the normal sequence: refreshment triggers the opposing party's right to production, full stop.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing rule:** Federal Rule of Evidence 612 — Writing Used to Refresh a Witness's Recollection.

FRE 612(a) provides that if a witness uses a writing to refresh memory for the purpose of testifying, the party against whom the witness is called may inspect the writing, cross-examine the witness about it, and introduce in evidence those portions that relate to the witness's testimony. The court may require production of writings used to refresh recollection before trial.

**Key authorities:**
- **FRE 612** (the governing rule; 2023 amendment to FRE 612(a) clarified that the rule covers writings used before trial and that the court "may" require production — discretionary, not mandatory).
- **FRE 1002** (Best Evidence Rule) — applies only when a party seeks to prove the *contents* of a writing. A witness who refreshes recollection and then testifies from memory is not proving contents; the best evidence rule is inapplicable. See *United States v. Shyne*, 617 F.3d 103 (2d Cir. 2010) (distinguishing refreshment from best-evidence-rule analysis).
- **FRE 801(d)(2)** (Admissions) — limited to statements by a party-opponent. A non-party witness's own notes do not qualify as admissions.

**Verification:** The tested rule (FRE 612 production on proper motion), the legal issue (what the court should do when a witness refreshed from notes), and the credited outcome (direct production for inspection) are all unchanged from the original. The official key is confirmed correct.
