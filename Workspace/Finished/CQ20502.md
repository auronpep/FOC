# Pass-1 Transform Report — Q20502 (Evidence · Multiple hearsay)

## 1. Final Question

A church bus carrying a youth group home from a weekend mission trip in a neighboring state was traveling south on a two-lane highway near dusk. The bus drifted off the road, struck a guardrail, and rolled into a ravine. A wrongful-death action was brought by Martha, the surviving spouse of Daniel — a deacon at the host church who was among those killed.

At trial, the plaintiff called Peter, a state trooper assigned to the State Highway Patrol's accident-investigation unit. Peter read aloud from an investigation report that quoted an unidentified 911 caller as saying, "I saw a red pickup truck swerve across the center line just before the bus went off the road." Peter testified that the report from which he was reading was kept in the regular course of business by the Highway Patrol, that the entry from which he was reading had been made by another trooper who also worked for the Highway Patrol, that the trooper who made the entry had been sworn to investigate serious motor-vehicle accidents and to keep honest and accurate records of the results, and that the trooper who made the entry was now dead.

Upon appropriate objection, the evidence should be ruled

**A.** admissible as an official written statement.

**B.** admissible as a business record.

**C.** inadmissible as hearsay not within any exception.

**D.** admissible as past recollection recorded.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** The witness's testimony contains two layers of out-of-court statement. The first layer is the 911 caller's quoted statement ("I saw a red pickup truck swerve across the center line…") — an out-of-court assertion offered for the truth of the matter asserted, and therefore hearsay under FRE 801(c). The second layer is the trooper's written investigation report containing that quoted statement. Under FRE 805 (Hearsay Within Hearsay), a statement that includes further hearsay is not excluded by the hearsay rule "if each part of the combined statements conforms with an exception to the rule." Both layers must independently qualify. Even if the outer report itself satisfies a business-records or public-records exception, the inner statement (the 911 caller's quoted assertion) does not. The 911 caller is unidentified, no exception is identified that covers an unidentified bystander's report of a motor-vehicle accident, and nothing in the record establishes the bystander's personal knowledge, contemporaneity, or professional reliability. Because the inner layer fails, the whole statement is inadmissible. This is the heart of the multiple-hearsay rule.

**Gold Key:** Under the multiple-hearsay rule (FRE 805), each layer of an out-of-court statement must independently qualify for a hearsay exception. A quoted witness's statement inside a qualifying business record or public record is not made admissible merely because the surrounding record qualifies. The proponent must point to an exception that covers the inner statement — a bystander's quoted assertion about who caused a crash rarely carries its own guarantee of trustworthiness.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### Choice B (Original A) — ⚠️ DOMINANT TRAP

**Why it's attractive:** The stem recites every classic business-record foundation fact: the report was "kept in the regular course of business" by the Highway Patrol; the trooper who made the entry was "sworn to … keep honest and accurate records." Students who learned FRE 803(6) as "the most-tested hearsay exception" will recognize the language and stop analyzing. They assume that if the *report* is a business record, then everything *in* the report is admissible. The cognitive move: spot the regular-course language, label it "business record," and stop.

**The breaker:** Even if the outer report qualifies as a business record under FRE 803(6), the quoted 911 caller's statement inside the report is itself an out-of-court assertion that must independently qualify for an exception. The 911 caller is unidentified, no exception is named, and nothing in the record shows that the bystander's statement was made under circumstances giving it a separate guarantee of trustworthiness (no present sense impression, no excited utterance, no business duty, no personal knowledge anchor). Per FRE 805, when one layer fails, the whole statement is excluded. **Gold Key:** The business-records exception (FRE 803(6)) only covers the part of the record based on personal knowledge or transmission from a person with knowledge. A quoted bystander's statement inside an otherwise-qualifying report is not covered by 803(6) alone.

### Choice A (Original B)

**Why it's attractive:** The State Highway Patrol is a public agency, and the report concerns a matter within the agency's official duties. Students who learn FRE 803(8) as "public records" reflexively think "agency report — admissible!" The choice produces the right outcome (admissible) and invokes a familiar doctrine. The cognitive move: identify the public-office source, label it "official written statement," and stop.

**The breaker:** Two problems. First, FRE 803(8)(B)(i) expressly *excludes* from the public-records exception "investigative reports by police and other law enforcement personnel, except when offered by an accused in a criminal case." A State Highway Patrol accident-investigation report is exactly that — an investigative report by law enforcement personnel — and this is a civil case, not one where an accused is offering the report. So the public-records exception is unavailable on its face. Second, even if the exception applied to the outer report, the quoted 911 caller's statement is not the agency's own observation or factual finding — it's a bystander's out-of-court assertion, and it must independently qualify for an exception under FRE 805. The official-statement exception fails for the same multiple-hearsay reason the business-records exception fails. **Gold Key:** FRE 803(8)(B)(i) categorically excludes police and law-enforcement investigative reports from the public-records exception in civil cases (and even in criminal cases, only the accused may offer them).

### Choice D (Original C)

**Why it's attractive:** Past recollection recorded (FRE 803(5)) is a familiar exception, and the witness's apparent "lack of memory" of the underlying crash is implied by his reading from a report. Students who recognize the "witness reading from a record" pattern may default to PRR. The cognitive move: see "witness reading from a record" and label it PRR.

**The breaker:** PRR requires *the witness* (the person on the stand) to once have had personal knowledge of the matter recorded. Peter is reading a report that Paul wrote. Peter did not witness the crash; he did not write the report; he cannot now testify that "I once knew this, but I can no longer remember it fully." The record does not reflect Peter's own knowledge — it reflects Paul's. A witness who is reading someone else's record cannot satisfy the personal-knowledge and adopt-the-record prongs of FRE 803(5). **Gold Key:** Past recollection recorded requires the testifying witness to have had personal knowledge of the matter and to have made or adopted the record when the matter was fresh. A third party's report, read by a witness who never had personal knowledge, is not PRR.

## 9. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing Rule (multiple hearsay):** Federal Rule of Evidence 805 — "Hearsay within hearsay is not excluded by the rule against hearsay if each part of the combined statements conforms with an exception to the rule." The Advisory Committee Note confirms the underlying principle: the hearsay rule "should not call for exclusion of a hearsay statement which includes a further hearsay statement when both conform to the requirements of a hearsay exception." A common-law-style "trustworthiness pass-through" does not exist — each link in the chain must independently qualify.

**Governing Rule (business records):** Federal Rule of Evidence 803(6) — a record of a regularly conducted activity qualifies only if, among other things, "the record was made at or near the time by — or from information transmitted by — someone with knowledge" and "neither the source of information nor the method or circumstances of preparation indicate a lack of trustworthiness." The "transmitted by" language requires the source of the transmitted information to have personal knowledge. A bystander's quoted assertion inside an otherwise-qualifying business record is not made admissible merely because the record itself is regular-course.

**Governing Rule (public records):** Federal Rule of Evidence 803(8) — a record of a public office is admissible if it sets out the office's regularly conducted activities, a matter observed under legal duty, or factual findings from a legally authorized investigation. Critically, FRE 803(8)(B)(i) excludes from this exception "investigative reports by police and other law enforcement personnel, except when offered by an accused in a criminal case." A State Highway Patrol accident-investigation report is a paradigmatic law-enforcement investigative report, and a wrongful-death civil case is not a criminal case in which the accused is offering the report.

**Governing Rule (recorded recollection):** Federal Rule of Evidence 803(5) — the record must concern a matter about which the *witness* once had personal knowledge, the record must have been made or adopted by the witness when the matter was fresh, and the record must accurately reflect the witness's knowledge. The witness must now have "insufficient recollection to enable the witness to testify fully and accurately." A witness who never had personal knowledge cannot satisfy the rule simply by reading someone else's record.

**Authority floor:**

### 2. The 17-Section Student Case Study

### Program Frame

This item tests **Hearsay → Multiple Hearsay** under the **Federal Rules of Evidence** (outline code 33040103). The core conflict: a witness testifies by reading aloud from an official accident-investigation report that quotes an unidentified bystander's out-of-court statement. The question is whether the inner bystander statement is admissible. This is a Core-difficulty item requiring the student to (1) identify the multiple layers of hearsay, (2) apply a hearsay exception to each layer independently per FRE 805, and (3) resist traps that assert a single exception covers the entire record.

### Student Access Test

A student who has learned the multiple-hearsay rule and the Gold Key for the business-records exception can solve this item without outside legal doctrine. The CUT phase applies the multiple-hearsay rule to all three "admissible under X exception" choices and eliminates them in one move. The CLASH between the three exception answers and the "no exception" answer is resolved by recognizing that the inner hearsay layer has no exception. The Gold Key confirms that a quoted witness's statement inside a qualifying record is not made admissible merely because the surrounding record qualifies.

### Question Data

**Stem:** A church bus carrying a youth group home from a weekend mission trip crashes on a two-lane highway near dusk. A wrongful-death action is brought by Martha, the surviving spouse of Daniel (a deacon at the host church who was among those killed). At trial, the plaintiff calls Peter, a state trooper assigned to the State Highway Patrol's accident-investigation unit. Peter reads aloud from an investigation report that quotes an unidentified 911 caller as saying, "I saw a red pickup truck swerve across the center line just before the bus went off the road." Peter testifies that the report was kept in the regular course of business by the Highway Patrol, that the entry was made by another trooper (Paul) who was sworn to investigate serious motor-vehicle accidents and to keep honest and accurate records, and that Paul is now dead.

**Call:** Upon appropriate objection, the evidence should be ruled

**Choices:**
- A: admissible as an official written statement.
- B: admissible as a business record.
- C: inadmissible as hearsay not within any exception.
- D: admissible as past recollection recorded.

### Distilled Core Question

A witness testifies by reading aloud from an official accident-investigation report that quotes a bystander's out-of-court statement about what caused the accident. The reporting investigator is deceased. Is the quoted bystander statement admissible?

### Call and Prediction

The call asks for the admissibility ruling. The witness's testimony contains two layers of out-of-court statement: the 911 caller's quoted assertion and the trooper's report containing that assertion. Per FRE 805, each layer must independently qualify for an exception. The 911 caller's statement is offered for the truth of the matter asserted (that a red pickup truck swerved) and is therefore hearsay. No exception is named that covers an unidentified bystander's report of a motor-vehicle accident. Prediction: inadmissible. This points to C. The student must then check whether any of the three "admissible under X" answers (A, B, D) actually covers the inner layer.

### Trigger Facts

1. **Peter reads aloud from a Highway Patrol accident-investigation report** — the report is a written out-of-court statement.
2. **The report quotes an unidentified 911 caller** — the 911 caller's quoted assertion is an out-of-court statement offered for the truth (that a pickup truck swerved). This is the inner hearsay layer.
3. **The report was kept in the regular course of business** — the outer report may qualify as a business record (FRE 803(6)) if its other foundation requirements are met.
4. **The reporting trooper (Paul) was sworn to keep honest and accurate records** — supports the regular-course and trustworthiness prongs of 803(6).
5. **The reporting trooper (Paul) is now dead** — supports the personal-knowledge / unavailability backdrop, but does not save the inner statement.
6. **The 911 caller is unidentified** — no foundation for any exception covering the inner statement. No personal knowledge anchor, no present sense impression, no excited utterance, no business duty, no professional reliability.

### Governing C3 Lane

**CUT → CLASH → CALL**

- **CUT:** Apply the multiple-hearsay rule (FRE 805) to all three "admissible under X" answers. The inner 911-caller statement has no exception. Eliminate A, B, and D.
- **CLASH:** Three exception answers vs. one "no exception" answer. The axis: does the outer record's qualification transmit to the inner statement? Per FRE 805, no.
- **CALL:** C is the only true and responsive choice.

### Choice-by-Choice Walkthrough

**Choice A: "Admissible as an official written statement."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The Highway Patrol is a public agency, but the report is an *investigative report by law enforcement personnel*. FRE 803(8)(B)(i) categorically excludes such reports from the public-records exception in civil cases. Even setting that aside, the inner 911-caller statement is not the agency's own observation — it must independently qualify for an exception under FRE 805. The choice sells a familiar rule (public records) and stops before checking the carve-out or the multiple-hearsay layer.
2. **Student label:** The Public-Records Reflex.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** A correct public-records answer would identify a non-law-enforcement public record of the agency's own regularly conducted activities or factual findings — and would still need to address the inner hearsay layer.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** FRE 803(8)(B)(i) excludes law-enforcement investigative reports from the public-records exception in civil cases. The Highway Patrol accident-investigation report is a paradigmatic law-enforcement investigative report. `Gold Key:` FRE 803(8)(B)(i) categorically excludes investigative reports by police and other law enforcement personnel from the public-records exception, except when offered by an accused in a criminal case.

**Choice B: "Admissible as a business record." (DOMINANT TRAP)**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The stem recites every classic business-record trigger phrase: "kept in the regular course of business," "sworn to … keep honest and accurate records." A student who reflexively applies FRE 803(6) will stop at the outer record and conclude that the entire report is admissible. But the inner 911-caller statement is itself hearsay and must independently qualify for an exception. The 911 caller is unidentified, no exception is named, and nothing establishes the bystander's personal knowledge or any other exception. Per FRE 805, when one layer fails, the whole statement is excluded.
2. **Student label:** The Business-Records Layer-Skip.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** A correct business-record answer would (1) confirm that the outer report qualifies under FRE 803(6), (2) confirm that the inner 911-caller statement independently qualifies for an exception (e.g., present sense impression, excited utterance, or business duty), and (3) cite that exception.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** FRE 803(6) covers records made at or near the time by, or from information transmitted by, a person with knowledge. The business-records exception does not transmit trustworthiness to a quoted bystander's out-of-court assertion. `Gold Key:` Under the multiple-hearsay rule (FRE 805), each layer of an out-of-court statement must independently qualify for a hearsay exception. A quoted witness's statement inside a qualifying business record is not made admissible merely because the surrounding record qualifies.

**Choice C: "Inadmissible as hearsay not within any exception." (CORRECT)**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The witness's testimony contains two layers of hearsay. The inner 911-caller statement is hearsay with no applicable exception. Per FRE 805, the whole statement is excluded. The choice correctly identifies that no exception covers the inner hearsay layer and that the multiple-hearsay rule requires exclusion.
2. **Student label:** Correct — No Exception Covers the Inner Hearsay.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** This IS the true and responsive answer.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** FRE 805, multiple hearsay rule, requires exclusion when any layer of the combined statement fails to qualify for an exception. The 911 caller's statement is hearsay with no applicable exception. The whole statement is excluded.

**Choice D: "Admissible as past recollection recorded."**

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** The "witness reading from a record" pattern looks like past recollection recorded (FRE 803(5)). But PRR requires the *testifying witness* to once have had personal knowledge of the matter recorded and to have made or adopted the record when the matter was fresh. Peter is reading Paul's report. Peter never had personal knowledge of the crash and never made the record. PRR's personal-knowledge prong fails on its face. The choice sells a familiar exception and stops before checking who the witness actually is and what they actually knew.
2. **Student label:** The PRR Misapplication.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** A correct PRR answer would require Peter (the testifying witness) to have once had personal knowledge of the matter and to have made or adopted the record when the matter was fresh. The current record was made by Paul, not Peter.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** FRE 803(5) requires the witness's own personal knowledge and adoption of the record. A third-party report read by a witness who lacks personal knowledge is not PRR. `Silver Key:` The "PRR pattern" is witness + record. The witness must have known the matter AND made or adopted the record. If the witness is reading someone else's record, PRR fails the personal-knowledge prong.

After CUTTING A, B, and D (all three fail the multiple-hearsay rule or their own exception's requirements), the residual is **C** — the only choice that is both true and responsive.
