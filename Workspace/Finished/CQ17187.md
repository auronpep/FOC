Under cover of darkness in Antioch, Silas pried open the heavy wooden shutter of Barnabas’s home. While standing on a stone ledge outside the house, Silas inserted a long, curved shepherd’s hook through the opening. He used the hook to snag a small chest of silver coins from a table near the window. At no point did any part of Silas’s body cross the plane of the window.

Is the "entry" element of common-law burglary satisfied?

### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation
At common law, the "entry" element of burglary is satisfied when any part of the defendant’s body crosses the threshold of the dwelling. However, entry can also be achieved by an instrument if that instrument is inserted for the purpose of committing the intended felony (e.g., a hook to steal property or a gun to shoot someone inside). Here, Silas used the shepherd’s hook specifically to commit the intended larceny (snagging the silver). Therefore, the insertion of the hook satisfies the entry element, regardless of whether Silas's body ever entered the house.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
- **A:** This is attractive to students who over-theorize the "substantiality" of the entry. However, the law does not require the instrument to remain inside; the mere moment of crossing the threshold to commit the felony is sufficient.
- **C:** This is the dominant trap. It plays on the common misconception that burglary requires a "bodily" entry. While many burglaries involve the person's body, the "instrument entry" rule is a well-established common-law exception when the tool is used to execute the felony. 
- **D:** This choice targets confusion between "breaking" and "entry." While Silas did pry open the shutter (which satisfies "breaking" even without damage), the question specifically calls for the "entry" element. Lack of structural damage is irrelevant to the entry analysis.

### 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification
**Authority:** *State v. Ibarra*, 132 Ariz. 447 (1982); *Hebron v. State*, 331 Md. 219 (1993); 4 Blackstone, Commentaries *227.
The common-law rule for entry by instrument distinguishes between tools used only to gain access (which do not satisfy entry) and tools used to commit the felony (which do satisfy entry). Prying a window and inserting a hook to grab jewelry/money is the classic example of an instrument entry that satisfies the element. The transform preserves the rule, issue, and credited outcome perfectly.
