## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Lydia owns a private Christian bookstore that parks a small inventory trailer behind the shop. Her assistant, Timothy, told her that on Friday night he planned to use the trailer to receive stolen tablet projectors and then resell them to youth-group leaders. Lydia replied, "I do not want to hear about it. What you do after closing is your problem." On Friday night, Timothy received the stolen projectors in the trailer. Lydia was not there and received no share of the proceeds.

The prosecution charges Lydia with conspiracy to receive stolen property. Is Lydia guilty?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

If a person knows another plans to receive stolen property on her premises but responds with indifference rather than agreement or purpose to help, is she guilty of conspiracy?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. No, because Lydia was not present when Timothy received the stolen projectors.

B. No, because Lydia did not specifically intend to achieve the objective of receiving stolen property.

C. Yes, because Lydia provided the means, the trailer, for the crime to occur.

D. Yes, because Lydia knew about Timothy's plan and did nothing to prevent it.

### 5. Correct answer

B (original key C).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Conspiracy requires an agreement plus the specific intent to achieve the unlawful objective. Lydia knew what Timothy planned, but her response was indifference, not a shared plan. She did not say she wanted the stolen projectors received, did not share proceeds, and did not take a step showing she meant to make the receiving succeed. Gold Key: for conspiracy, knowledge is not enough; the actor must intend to agree and intend the crime's objective. Silver Key: when a conspiracy answer is built from knowledge, access, or absence, look for the missing meeting-of-minds fact before treating it as liability.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because it points to a fact that feels clean and easy: Lydia was not present when Timothy received the stolen projectors. Presence is not the conspiracy requirement. A person can be a conspirator without showing up for the completed offense, so absence does not explain why Lydia is not guilty.

C is the dominant trap because the trailer looks like the tool that made the crime possible. That fact may sound like help, but the conspiracy charge still needs an agreement and specific intent to further the receiving of stolen property. Gold Key: a place, tool, or ordinary means does not become conspiracy unless it is tied to purpose and agreement.

D is attractive because Lydia had advance knowledge and did not stop Timothy. The breaker is that knowledge plus inaction is still not a conspiratorial agreement. Silver Key: do not let a "bad bystander" fact replace the actual call, which asks whether she joined the criminal plan.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Conspiracy requires at least an agreement between two or more persons and the intent to further the unlawful objective. The original and variant both test whether knowledge, indifference, and passive access to premises can substitute for a specific-intent agreement. They cannot.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Model Penal Code section 5.03 frames conspiracy around acting "with the purpose of promoting or facilitating" the offense and agreeing that the criminal conduct occur. As a federal common-law analogue, Direct Sales Co. v. United States, 319 U.S. 703 (1943), distinguishes mere knowledge, acquiescence, carelessness, indifference, or passive concern from informed and interested cooperation that shows intent and agreement. See Cornell LII, Direct Sales Co. v. United States, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/319/703, and H2O/Open Casebook, Model Penal Code section 5.03, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/6141-tanaka-criminal-law-casebook/resources/10.3.1.2-model-penal-code-mpc-503-criminal-conspiracy/.

Those authorities confirm the supplied key. Lydia's statement does not show a meeting of minds, purpose to promote the receipt of stolen property, or a stake in Timothy's venture. The credited answer is therefore B.
