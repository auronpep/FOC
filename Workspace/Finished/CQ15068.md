## 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is the best answer.

Gold Key: A deed delivered to a grantee who had already died before delivery passes no title to that grantee. Daniel was already dead when Peter delivered the deed. Title therefore did not enter Daniel’s estate and could not pass by will to Barnabas House.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. Peter, free and clear.**
This choice is attractive because it catches the first half of the rule: the deed failed, so Peter still holds title. The breaker is the phrase “free and clear.” Silver Key: same holder, different qualifier. Daniel’s money paid the full price, so the answer must account for repayment security.

**C. Barnabas House.**
This is the dominant trap. It follows the will fact, which feels natural after the stem says Daniel’s will was probated and leaves his estate to Barnabas House. Gold Key: the will matters only if Daniel owned the land. Because the deed to Daniel failed before title ever entered his estate, Barnabas House receives no title to Jubilee Yard.

**D. Lydia.**
This choice follows the visible helper: Lydia paid, received the deed, recorded it, and is Daniel’s heir-at-law. The breaker is role confusion. The deed named Daniel, not Lydia. If Daniel had taken title, the will would point to Barnabas House, not Lydia. If Daniel did not take title, Lydia’s helper role still does not make her the title holder.
