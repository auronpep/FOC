## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)
**B** is correct. Congress has neither authorized nor prohibited the temporary priority-routing directive for the private charitable relief network. This places the President's action in Youngstown Category 2 — the "zone of twilight" — where the Court evaluates the action by weighing the President's independent constitutional powers, congressional silence or acquiescence, and historical practice in comparable domestic emergencies. 

**Silver Key (call focus):** When the stem shows Congress is silent on the precise power, map the case to Category 2 and balance the three practical factors rather than defaulting to "valid because not forbidden" or "invalid because no statute." The call asks for the best description of the analysis, not the ultimate outcome on these facts.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
**A (dominant trap):** Attractive because "Congress didn't say no" feels like permission, especially in an emergency where quick action seems necessary. This is the classic Category 2 error — treating silence as equivalent to authorization or as removing all limits. Congressional silence does not automatically validate the action; the President must still ground the power in independent Article II authority plus the practical factors of acquiescence and history.

**C:** Attractive because students often over-read Youngstown as requiring express statutory authority for any significant executive domestic action (the "President is not a lawmaker" sound-bite without the full tripartite framework). Youngstown does not create a per se invalidity rule for every action lacking express approval; Category 1 requires authorization, Category 2 applies precisely when there is neither authorization nor prohibition.

**D:** Attractive because it injects a concrete, seemingly relevant status fact (church vs. independent nonprofit) that sounds like a real-world limit on federal power. The separation-of-powers question turns on the source and scope of presidential authority under the Constitution and Congress's response (or lack of response), not on the internal organizational form or religious affiliation of the regulated private party.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row. When the President acts without either congressional authorization or congressional prohibition, the validity of the executive action "depends on the balance of congressional silence, historical practice, and the President's independent constitutional powers." Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer, 343 U.S. 579 (1952) (Jackson, J., concurring). This is Jackson's Category 2 ("zone of twilight"). The Court has repeatedly treated the Jackson concurrence as the accepted framework for evaluating executive action in the absence of specific statutory authority or prohibition. See, e.g., Medellín v. Texas, 552 U.S. 491 (2008); Trump v. Hawaii, 585 U.S. 667 (2018) (recognizing the tripartite framework). 

The original stem (port cyberattack, vessel-routing priorities, shipping company) and the transformed stem (food-bank ransomware, supply-delivery priorities, commercial trucking association) present identical legal geometry: domestic executive emergency action + total congressional silence on the specific power + challenge based solely on lack of express statutory authorization.
