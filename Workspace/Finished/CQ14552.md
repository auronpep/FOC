## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question
Under the terms of a written contract, Daniel agreed to build Esther a custom cedar recording booth for her private Bible-memory podcast for $12,000. Nothing was said in the parties' negotiations or in the contract about progress payments during the work. After completing 25% of the booth strictly according to Esther's specifications, Daniel assigned his rights under the contract to a credit union as security for a $9,500 loan. The credit union immediately notified Esther of the assignment. Daniel thereafter, without legal excuse, abandoned the project before it was half-complete. Daniel later defaulted on the credit-union loan. Daniel has no assets. It will cost Esther at least $9,500 to have the recording booth finished by another craftsperson. If the credit union sues Esther for $9,500, which of the following will the court decide?

### 2. Distilled Core Question
After a contractor assigns his contract-payment rights to a lender and then materially breaches the same contract, may the customer use recoupment from that breach to defeat the lender-assignee's claim for payment?

### 3. Final answer choices
A. Esther wins, because her claim against Daniel arose before Daniel defaulted on his loan from the credit union.

B. Esther wins, because her right to recoup damages for Daniel's breach is available against the credit union as Daniel's assignee.

C. The credit union wins, because as Daniel's secured creditor it has priority over Esther's unsecured claim against Daniel.

D. The credit union wins, because the Daniel-Esther contract was in existence and Daniel was not in breach when the credit union gave Esther notice of the assignment.

### 5. Correct answer
B (original key: C)

### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. The credit union received only Daniel's assigned contract-payment rights, and those rights came with the same-contract recoupment problem attached. Gold Key: an assignee stands in the assignor's shoes; the obligor may use a claim arising from the assigned contract to reduce or defeat the assignee's demand. Silver Key: do not let the loan-default date control the answer; first ask whether Esther's claim comes from the same contract the credit union is trying to enforce. It does. Daniel's abandonment creates a recoupment claim tied directly to the assigned contract, and the completion cost is at least the amount the credit union seeks.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is the dominant trap. It is attractive because it reaches the right winner and gives a timing fact that sounds important: Esther's construction claim existed before Daniel defaulted on his credit-union loan. The breaker is that the loan-default date is not the legal hinge. Silver Key: the controlling axis is same-contract recoupment, not whether the customer beat the lender to a particular loan-default milestone.

C is attractive because the phrase "secured creditor" sounds like priority language and can pull students into debtor-creditor ranking. The breaker is that priority between creditors does not answer Esther's defense to the credit union's assigned contract claim. The credit union can enforce only the rights Daniel assigned, and those rights are subject to same-contract defenses and recoupment.

D is attractive because notice often matters in assignment questions, and Daniel had not yet breached when the credit union notified Esther. The breaker is that notice does not freeze Esther's same-contract recoupment defense. Gold Key: for claims arising from the assigned contract itself, the assignee takes subject to recoupment even when the breach occurs after notice.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Contracts, Third-Party Rights, Assignment of Rights, and the outline code `53070200` appears verbatim in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md` as "Nonparties to Contract > Assignment of Rights." The credited outcome is unchanged: the obligor wins because a claim for recoupment arising out of the assigned contract may be asserted against the assignee.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Restatement (Second) of Contracts §336 governs defenses against an assignee. Section 336(1) subjects an assignee's right to defenses and claims of the obligor, and §336(2) preserves recoupment for claims arising from the contract assigned. A public H2O/Open Casebook page identifies the same Restatement section at https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/246-contracts-cases-and-materials/resources/13.5.3-restatement-of-contracts-second-336/. This is also consistent with the standard "assignee stands in the shoes of the assignor" rule. Here, Daniel's assigned right was the right to receive payment under the Daniel-Esther construction contract. Daniel's later material breach of that same contract caused Esther at least $9,500 in completion-cost damages. Because the credit union seeks $9,500 as Daniel's assignee, Esther may recoup that same-contract loss against the credit union's demand. No researched authority contradicts the source key.
