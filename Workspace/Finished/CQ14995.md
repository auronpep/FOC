## 1. Final question (revised stem + call)

Timothy and Lydia, friends, owned a small parcel of land in a coastal Mediterranean
village in fee simple as joint tenants with right of survivorship. The parcel included
a modest stone workshop building where they ran a small olive press, together with
the surrounding olive grove. While Lydia was on an extended Orthodox mission trip in

to buy the property for a planned luxury resort, but he made no attempt to inform

is unmodified by statute. Shortly thereafter, Timothy was killed in an automobile
accident. His will, which was duly probated, specifically devised his one-half
interest in the property to Hannah. Lydia then returned from Greece and learned what
had happened. Lydia brought an appropriate action against Hannah, who claimed a
one-half interest in the property, seeking a declaratory judgment that she, Lydia,
was the sole owner of the property.

In this action, who should prevail?

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**B is correct.** Timothy and Lydia held the property as joint tenants with right of
survivorship, so they were bound by the four unities (time, title, interest,
possession) and shared a single right of survivorship. A joint tenancy can be
terminated during the joint tenants' lifetimes in only one of two ways: by **partition**
or by **severance**. An inter vivos conveyance of a joint tenant's interest is the
classic severance act, because the grantee takes title at a different time than the
other joint tenant and so the unity of time is destroyed. The moment Timothy conveyed
his interest to Hannah, the joint tenancy was severed; Hannah and Lydia then held the
property as **tenants in common**. Hannah's immediate reconveyance back to Timothy did
not restore the joint tenancy because the four unities could no longer be satisfied
(Hannah received her title from Timothy at one moment, Lydia received her title years
earlier; unity of time was already broken and cannot be undone by passing the interest
back). Timothy and Lydia therefore held as **tenants in common at the time of
Timothy's death**, with each owning an undivided one-half interest. Timothy's will
then passed that one-half interest to Hannah, and Hannah — together with Lydia as the
other tenant in common — owns the whole property. **Gold Key:** *the severance act
is the inter vivos conveyance, not the will; once the unities are gone, a return
conveyance cannot bring them back.* Lydia therefore does not take the whole parcel by
survivorship, and the court should enter judgment for Hannah.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A (will severed the joint tenancy).** It feels right because the will is the
dramatic end-of-life event and the will does name Hannah. The breaker: a will cannot
sever a joint tenancy. Severance requires an **inter vivos** act — something that
happens during the joint tenant's lifetime, while the joint tenancy is still
governing. By the time Timothy died, the joint tenancy was already gone; the will
simply passed his tenancy-in-common half-interest to Hannah. **Gold Key:** *the
severance act is the inter vivos conveyance, not the will; a will deals only with
what is left at death, and what was left at death was a tenancy-in-common half-share.*

**C (fiduciary duty breach — DOMINANT TRAP).** It is attractive because Timothy did
hide a material fact (the developer's offer) from Lydia, and "he hid a material fact
from his co-owner" feels like it should impose a duty. The breaker: at common law,
**joint tenants do not owe each other a fiduciary duty** merely by virtue of being
joint tenants. The correct remedy for a cotenant's misconduct — such as an ouster, a
failure to account for rents from third parties, or waste — is an accounting, a
surcharge, or a partition; the remedy is **not** a forfeiture of the wrongdoer's
interest or a transfer of the whole estate to the other cotenant. Lydia's
appropriate claim against Timothy (had he lived) would have been an accounting or a
share of any premium he collected from a sale, not a declaration that she owns the
whole parcel. The trustee-like fiduciary duty Timothy owed Hannah is **not** a duty
he owed Lydia as a co-joint-tenant. **Gold Key:** *the remedy for a cotenant's
misconduct is an accounting or partition, not forfeiture of the wrongdoer's
interest.*

**D (reconveyance reestablished the joint tenancy).** It is the structural mirror
image of the correct rule and the most tempting wrong answer on the law itself: if
the unities are what make a joint tenancy, why can't a quick return-conveyance put
them back? The breaker: the unity of time is permanently broken by the
**inter vivos** conveyance. Hannah received her title at one moment in time; Lydia
received her title years earlier. The four unities are a *simultaneous acquisition*
requirement; that moment is gone, and a return deed cannot move the hands of the
clock. The new Timothy–Lydia relationship after the round trip is therefore a
**tenancy in common**, not a joint tenancy. **Gold Key:** *unities of time and
title are not re-satisfied by handing the interest back; a severed joint tenancy
is gone for good.*

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification + legal reasoning (lawyer-confirmation layer)

**Tested rule (unchanged).** A joint tenancy is an estate held by two or more
persons with the **four unities** of time, title, interest, and possession, plus the
distinguishing incident of a **right of survivorship**. At common law, a joint
tenancy may be terminated during the joint tenants' lifetimes by **partition** or by
**severance**. The classic severance act is an **inter vivos conveyance** of a joint
tenant's interest to a third person; the grantee takes free of the survivorship
right and holds as a **tenant in common** with the other original joint tenant. A
**will cannot sever** a joint tenancy, because severance is an inter vivos act and
the joint tenancy is, by definition, gone the moment the joint tenant dies. Once a
joint tenancy has been severed, the four unities cannot be re-satisfied by a
reconveyance from the grantee back to the original grantor (in particular, the
unity of time is permanently lost), and the parties hold thereafter as tenants in
common. Joint tenants at common law **do not owe each other a fiduciary duty**
merely by virtue of the joint-tenancy relationship; the available remedies for a
cotenant's misconduct are accounting, contribution, surcharge, or partition, not
forfeiture of the wrongdoer's interest.

**Application.** Timothy conveyed his interest to Hannah **inter vivos**; that
conveyance was the severance act. Hannah and Lydia thereupon held as **tenants in
common**. Hannah's immediate reconveyance to Timothy did not restore the joint
tenancy (unities of time and title irreparably broken). At Timothy's death, the
estate was a tenancy in common; Timothy's will passed his undivided one-half
interest to Hannah. The court should enter judgment for Hannah (the wife) because
Lydia does not take the whole parcel by survivorship. Credited outcome (wife wins,
takes one-half by will; Lydia retains her own one-half as surviving tenant in
common) is **identical** to the original.

**Authority floor.** Common-law concurrent-estates doctrine; Restatement (Third) of
Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers §§ 6.1–6.4 (right of survivorship as
incident of joint tenancy; severance by inter vivos transfer); illustrative case
*Harlan v. Tryon* (joint tenancy cannot be re-established by reconveyance). The
leading Restatement rule on severance is stated plainly above as the floor; cases
are cited as illustration only. `attorney_review: true` for any
jurisdiction-specific statute (the original Q specifies the common law is
unmodified by statute, so no statute turns the analysis; in some states RPL
§ 6-104.1 and analogous provisions alter the result, but those are not in play on
this stem).

**Bank validation:** research **confirms** the official key. The original answer
(A: wife wins because tenancy in common at time of death) is correct under the
common-law rule; the variation preserves the rule, the issue, the credited

#### program_frame
Real Property — concurrent estates, joint tenancy. The item tests the four-unities
mechanics of severance by inter vivos conveyance, the rule that a reconveyance
back to the grantor does not re-create a severed joint tenancy, the rule that a
will cannot sever what the inter vivos act already destroyed, and the absence of
a fiduciary duty between joint tenants at common law.

#### student_access_test

the remedy for misconduct is accounting/partition, not forfeiture. No outside
doctrine in the first-pass logic.

#### choice_by_choice_walkthrough

**A — will severed the joint tenancy (wrong mechanism).**
1. *C3 signal:* names the dramatic end-of-life act (the will) as the severance.
2. *Student label:* picks the will as the act that severed.
3. *True/responsive version would look like:* a story in which the will itself

   requires an inter vivos act, and a will is testamentary.

#### remediation_card
**Joint-tenancy severance — the inter vivos flip.** Signal: one joint tenant
conveys his interest to a third person, the third person reconveys it back, then
the original joint tenant dies with a will naming the third person. Move: identify
the inter vivos conveyance as the severance act; ignore the reconveyance (unities
cannot be re-satisfied); treat the will as a devise of a tenancy-in-common
half-share, not as a severance. Tiny rule: severance = inter vivos act;
reconveyance ≠ restoration; will ≠ severance; remedies for a cotenant's misconduct
are accounting/partition, not forfeiture. Trap: picking the will, the
reconveyance, or a fiduciary-duty-forfeiture theory.

## Gold Key(s)
- **GK-REAL_PROPERTY-JOINTTENANCY-INTERVIVOS-01** — Severance of a joint tenancy
  is an inter vivos act, not a testamentary one; the inter vivos conveyance to a
  third person is the severance, and a will can only pass what is left at death —
  which is a tenancy-in-common half-share. *Unlocks A.*
- **GK-REAL_PROPERTY-JOINTTENANCY-UNRESTORABLE-02** — Once a joint tenancy has
  been severed, the four unities cannot be re-satisfied by a reconveyance from
  the grantee back to the original grantor; in particular, the unity of time is
  permanently lost, and the parties thereafter hold as tenants in common.
  *Unlocks D.*
- **GK-REAL_PROPERTY-COTENANT-NOFIDUCIARY-03** — Joint tenants (and tenants in
  common) at common law do not owe each other a fiduciary duty merely by virtue
  of the cotenancy; the remedies for a cotenant's misconduct are accounting,
  contribution, surcharge, and partition — not forfeiture of the wrongdoer's
  interest. *Unlocks C (dominant trap).*
