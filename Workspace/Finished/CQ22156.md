# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question (revised stem + call)

A statute provides that the owner of a motor vehicle is vicariously liable for the negligence of any person driving with the owner's permission. Ruth was injured when a delivery van struck her near a community Bible book fair. Ruth sued Daniel, alleging that she was injured by the negligent driving of Mary, who was operating Daniel's delivery van with Daniel's permission at the time of the accident.

Daniel denies owning the van. Over Daniel's objection, Ruth offers into evidence a liability insurance policy issued by an insurance company. The policy is authenticated by testimony from an officer of the company, who states that the policy was purchased by and issued to Daniel, and that on the day of the accident the policy was in force on the van in question.

Should the policy and authenticating testimony be admitted?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

In a negligence case where vehicle ownership is disputed, may a plaintiff offer authenticated liability-insurance evidence to show that the defendant owned the vehicle?

## 3. Final answer choices (A-D, post-shuffle)

- **A:** No, because policy prohibits the introduction of evidence that a party did or did not have liability insurance on the day of an accident.
- **B:** Yes, because it tends to establish that Daniel owned the van at the time of the accident.
- **C:** No, because it has no probative value relative to the issues in the case.
- **D:** Yes, because it is relevant to Daniel's ability to pay a judgment rendered against him.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**B is correct.** The policy is not being offered to show Daniel was careless or that he can pay a verdict. It is offered to prove a disputed fact of consequence: whether Daniel owned the van. A person is more likely to buy and carry liability insurance on a vehicle he owns than on a vehicle he does not own. **Gold Key:** FRE 411 bars liability-insurance evidence when it is offered to prove negligence or wrongful conduct, but it permits the evidence for another relevant purpose, including ownership or control. **Silver Key:** when an insurance answer appears, ask "insurance for what purpose?" before cutting it.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A - No, because policy prohibits the introduction of evidence that a party did or did not have liability insurance on the day of an accident.** This is the dominant trap because it sounds like the familiar anti-insurance rule. The breaker is its absolute wording. **Gold Key:** the rule blocks insurance evidence for fault, not for every purpose; ownership is an expressly permitted purpose.

**C - No, because it has no probative value relative to the issues in the case.** This answer misses the live ownership dispute. The policy was bought by and issued to Daniel and was in force on the van on the accident date. That makes Daniel's ownership more probable than it would be without the evidence.

**D - Yes, because it is relevant to Daniel's ability to pay a judgment rendered against him.** This answer reaches the right result but gives the wrong reason. A defendant's ability to pay is not a fact of consequence in deciding negligence liability or damages. The admissible purpose is ownership, not collectability.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer (lawyer-confirmation layer)

**Tested rule:** FRE 401 makes evidence relevant if it has any tendency to make a fact of consequence more or less probable. FRE 411 excludes evidence that a person was or was not insured against liability when offered to prove negligence or other wrongful conduct, but the rule allows admission for another purpose, including proving agency, ownership, or control.

**Authority:** FRE 411 and FRE 401. Current rule text is available at https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_411 and https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_401.

**Confirmation:** The credited outcome is unchanged from the source: the policy and authenticating testimony are admissible. The legal issue is unchanged: liability-insurance evidence offered to prove disputed vehicle ownership rather than negligence. The dominant trap is unchanged: overreading the insurance rule as an all-purpose exclusion. Not on the doctrinal-currency hotlist. Authority floor satisfied by FRE 411 and FRE 401.
