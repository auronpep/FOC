## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

**B is the best answer.** Lydia is bound. **Gold Key:** Under FRCP 23(b)(2), when the class seeks injunctive or declaratory relief that is appropriate respecting the class as a whole, individual notice to class members is not required, and the judgment binds all members of the class even if some never received actual notice. **Silver Key:** Lock the call on whether the prior judgment binds the absent member; the (b)(2) label in the stem tells you notice is not the gatekeeper.

The stem explicitly states the class was not predominated by common questions and that the relief sought was injunctive relief appropriate for the group as a whole. That is the definition of a (b)(2) class. The rule and its history make clear that (b)(2) classes are designed for indivisible relief that applies uniformly; the drafters did not require the same individual notice that (b)(3) common-question damages classes demand. Therefore the prior judgment binds Lydia regardless of whether she saw the mailed notice.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. Yes, because Esther's attorney mailed notice to Lydia.**  
This is the attractive "notice was sent, therefore due process is satisfied" trap. It sells the intuition that mailing equals binding. **Gold Key:** For a (b)(2) injunctive class, the rule does not make notice the condition of binding effect. Mailing may have been courteous, but it was not legally required, and its presence or absence does not change whether Lydia is bound.

**C. No, because Lydia did not have actual notice of the class action lawsuit.**  
This is the dominant trap. It sells the powerful "I never heard about it, so it can't bind me" intuition that feels like basic fairness. **Gold Key:** FRCP 23(b)(2) deliberately dispenses with the individual-notice requirement that applies to (b)(3) classes precisely because the relief is class-wide and indivisible. Actual notice is not the test for whether an absent (b)(2) member is bound.

**D. No, because Esther's attorney failed to provide sufficient notice to all members of the class.**  
This is the half-truth that concedes the class existed but then imports the (b)(3) notice standard into a (b)(2) case. **Gold Key:** The (b)(2) category exists for cases in which the court can craft relief that applies uniformly without needing to notify every individual. The rule does not impose a "notice to all" prerequisite for binding effect in this category.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

**Tested black-letter rule (unchanged from source):** Members of a FRCP 23(b)(2) class action seeking injunctive or declaratory relief appropriate for the class as a whole are bound by the judgment even though individual notice to class members is not required.

**Governing authority (lawyer-confirmation layer only):** Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(b)(2) and 23(c)(2)–(3). Rule 23(b)(2) authorizes a class when "the party opposing the class has acted or refused to act on grounds that apply generally to the class, so that final injunctive relief or corresponding declaratory relief is appropriate respecting the class as a whole." Rule 23(c)(2)(A) requires individual notice only for (b)(3) classes. For (b)(1) and (b)(2) classes the rule provides that the court "may direct appropriate notice to the class." The 1966 Advisory Committee Notes explain that (b)(2) was intended for civil-rights-type cases in which the relief would affect the class as a unit and individual notice was not contemplated as a prerequisite to binding effect. See also Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Dukes, 564 U.S. 338 (2011) (discussing the structure and notice differences among the (b) categories). The source row's official explanation and additional_info match this rule exactly: "Members of a (b)(2) class ... are not entitled to notice of the claim" and "all class members will be bound by the judgment rendered in class action lawsuits, even when a class member did not receive notice of the suit."

No key disagreement. The official key (A, now post-shuffle B) is correct under current FRCP 23. No doctrinal-currency hotlist item is implicated.
