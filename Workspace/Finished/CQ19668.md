## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

Daniel is charged with breaking the padlock on a delivery trailer used by a private Bible-printing business. The prosecutor offers a hardware-store receipt showing that Daniel bought bolt cutters two hours before the padlock was cut. Daniel objects that the bolt cutters could have been purchased to trim a rusted chain on his backyard gate.

How should the court rule?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A defendant charged with cutting a padlock bought bolt cutters two hours earlier, but he offers an innocent use for the tool. Is the receipt relevant?

### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. The receipt should be admitted. A bolt-cutter purchase two hours before a padlock cutting has some tendency to make Daniel's involvement more probable. Gold Key: threshold relevance is a low bar; an innocent alternative explanation usually weakens the inference but does not erase it. Silver Key: when an answer says "could have been innocent," ask whether that destroys all probative tendency or merely gives the opponent an argument about weight.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because it tries to make the prosecutor close the innocent-use door before the receipt comes in. The breaker is the invented threshold: relevance does not require the proponent to prove Daniel had no backyard gate.

C is attractive because recovered tools feel cleaner and more concrete than a receipt. The breaker is the fabricated same-object rule; a purchase close in time can be probative even if the exact bolt cutters are not recovered.

D is the dominant trap because it turns a possible innocent explanation into an exclusion rule. The breaker is the Gold Key: possible innocence goes to weight when the evidence still has some tendency to prove a consequential fact.

### 9. Full Black-Letter Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Federal Rule of Evidence 401 defines relevant evidence as evidence having any tendency to make a fact of consequence more or less probable than it would be without the evidence. Federal Rule of Evidence 402 provides that relevant evidence is admissible unless another source of law excludes it, and irrelevant evidence is inadmissible.

The receipt is relevant because a bolt-cutter purchase shortly before the padlock was cut makes Daniel's involvement at least somewhat more probable. The backyard-gate explanation is a competing innocent inference, not a threshold relevance destroyer. It gives Daniel cross-examination and argument about weight, but it does not require exclusion under FRE 401 or 402.

Authority links: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_401 ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_402
