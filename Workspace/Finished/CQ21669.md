## 1. Final question — stem + call

Lydia dyes and sells purple cloth. She agreed to supply a large consignment of her dyed cloth to Stephen, a garment maker who lived in another state, with the full price of $40,000 due on the day she delivered. Stephen took delivery of every bolt but never paid. **Should the federal court apply the state prejudgment-interest rule?**

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**C is correct.** The suit is a state-law breach-of-contract claim heard only because the parties are diverse. In diversity, a federal court applies state substantive law, and the right to prejudgment interest goes to the *measure of recovery* — it is part of the substantive remedy for the contract, not a courthouse-management rule. So the state statute's prejudgment-interest entitlement travels with the claim into federal court.

**Gold Key:** Prejudgment interest on a state-law claim is *substantive* and follows state law in diversity; only **post-judgment** interest is federal (28 U.S.C. § 1961). That single split defeats the "interest is procedure, so federal law controls" pull.

**Silver Key:** The array is two "Yes" and two "No." Fix the outcome first (state rule applies → Yes), cut both "No" choices, then keep the "Yes" whose *reason* names the real ground — the substantive remedy — over the one that invents a mechanism (jury discretion).

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A — No, because all interest questions are federal procedure.** This sells the cleanest version of the defendant's argument: under *Erie* "procedure" is federal, and a student remembers that post-judgment interest really is set by federal statute, so "interest = procedure = federal" feels airtight. The word *all* is the tell. **Gold Key:** prejudgment interest is substantive (part of the remedy) and follows state law; only post-judgment interest is federal under § 1961 — so "all interest questions are federal" overclaims and collapses.

**B — No, because diversity jurisdiction eliminates state remedies.** It trades on the half-memory that "going federal" changes the governing law. But diversity is only a doorway into the federal forum; it changes *where* the claim is heard, never *what* the claim is or what remedies it carries. A jurisdictional basis cannot erase a substantive state-law remedy — that is a category error, not a rule.

**D — Yes, because the amount of prejudgment interest is committed to the jury's discretion.** It reaches the right outcome and sounds plausible because juries do fix damages, which tempts the test-taker to grab the first "Yes." But the call asks *which sovereign's law supplies the interest rule*, and this answer instead invents a jury-discretion rule that does not decide the choice-of-law question. **Silver Key:** when two choices share your yes/no, pick the one whose reason answers the actual call (substantive remedy), not the one that swaps in an unrelated mechanism.

## 9. Black-letter verification & legal reasoning (lawyer-confirmation layer)

- **Tested rule (unchanged):** A federal court sitting in diversity applies state law to substantive matters; entitlement to prejudgment interest on a state-law claim is substantive because it affects the measure of recovery, so state law governs. Post-judgment interest, by contrast, is governed by federal law.
- **Authority:** *Erie R.R. Co. v. Tompkins*, 304 U.S. 64 (1938) (federal courts apply state substantive law in diversity); *Klaxon Co. v. Stentor Elec. Mfg. Post-judgment interest contrast: **28 U.S.C. § 1961** (federal money-judgment interest, federal Treasury-yield rate).
