7) Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

A rebuttable presumption under State X creates a prima facie inference that the missing person is dead after seven years of unexplained absence. Contrary evidence that tends to prove the person was alive during the period (here, a dated photograph and witness testimony placing Daniel alive five years after disappearance) rebuts the presumption and presents a factual question for the trier of fact. In federal diversity cases where state law supplies the rule of decision on the substantive issue, the state-law presumption’s effect governs; the court therefore treats the evidence as making a jury question about whether the presumption is overcome.

8) Full wrong-answer explanations

C (Shifts burden of persuasion). Attractive move (dominant trap): confuses the tactical burden-of-production effect with the ultimate burden of persuasion. Breaker: Federal Rule of Evidence 301 and state-law principles distinguish burden of production from burden of persuasion; the presumption can obligate production of rebuttal evidence but does not necessarily shift persuasion absent state law saying so; here State X’s rebuttable presumption yields a jury question when contradicted.

D (Impeach only). Attractive move: treats the photograph/testimony as only attacking credibility. Breaker: Contrary evidence that goes to the fact presumed directly rebuts the presumption and is admissible on the substantive issue, not merely for impeachment.

B (Album required). Attractive move: invokes the rule of completeness to defeat a photograph. Breaker: The rule of completeness may require related portions when a writing is offered out of context, but it does not render a relevant dated photograph inadmissible absent other specific hearsay or authentication problems; completeness of an album is not a prerequisite.

9) Black-letter verification & lawyer-confirmation

Black-letter rule (student-facing): A rebuttable presumption creates an inference that can be overcome by contrary evidence; such contrary evidence generally creates a jury question rather than conclusively establishing the presumed fact.

Authority (lawyer-confirmation): Federal Rules of Evidence 301 explains the burden-of-production effect of presumptions in civil cases and that the rule does not shift the burden of persuasion (https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_301). Rule 302 provides that when state law supplies the rule of decision in a civil action, state law governs the effect of a presumption (https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_302). The Advisory Committee notes to Rule 302 and Erie-related cases (e.g., Cities Service Oil Co. v. Dunlap; Palmer v. Hoffman; Dick v. New York Life Ins. Co.) support applying state substantive rules on presumptions when the presumption affects a substantive element. No research found that would alter the credited outcome for this fact pattern.
