### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The credited answer is legally sound. M'Naghten's Case, 10 Cl. & Fin. 200, 8 Eng. Rep. 718 (H.L. 1843), states the traditional right-wrong insanity test: a defendant is excused if, because of disease of the mind, he did not know the nature and quality of the act or did not know the act was wrong. Modern jurisdictions vary on whether "wrong" means moral wrong, legal wrong, or both. Kahler v. Kansas, 589 U.S. ___ (2020), recognizes that M'Naghten's wrongfulness inquiry has multiple variants, including a legal-wrongfulness variant. State v. Crenshaw, 98 Wash. 2d 789, 659 P.2d 488 (1983), applies a legal-wrongfulness approach and rejects an insanity claim where the defendant understood the act was illegal despite personal or religious moral beliefs.
