## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Naomi conveyed Lantern Loft "to Lydia so long as the premises are used as a Scripture-copying workshop." Lydia later stopped hosting Scripture-copying sessions and converted the premises into a private indoor pickleball club for paying players. Who owns Lantern Loft after the conversion?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Who owns property after a deed says "so long as" a stated use continues, and that stated use stops?

### 3. Final answer choices

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. "So long as" is durational language. It gives Lydia a fee simple determinable and leaves Naomi with a possibility of reverter. When Lydia stopped the Scripture-copying workshop use, Lydia's estate ended automatically and ownership returned to Naomi. Gold Key: "so long as," "while," "during," and "until" point to fee simple determinable and automatic reverter. Silver Key: when two answers fight over "automatic return" versus "grantor must act," let the deed phrase decide the timing.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because the answer notices that Naomi may get the property back after the stated use stops. The breaker is the action requirement. "So long as" creates automatic termination, not a right that waits for Naomi to sue or reenter. Gold Key: durational language gives Naomi a possibility of reverter; the condition-subsequent reentry rule belongs to different wording.

B is attractive because the players are the visible people now using the premises. The breaker is the ownership source. Using or entering land as a customer does not make a person the fee owner under the deed.

D is attractive because a land-use phrase can sound like a promise about how property should be used. The breaker is the word "always." Deed use language can be a covenant in some settings, but durational language like "so long as" creates a limitation on the estate itself.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is correct. A conveyance using durational language such as "so long as," "while," "during," or "until" creates a fee simple determinable. The grantor retains a possibility of reverter. When the stated event occurs or the stated use stops, the grantee's estate terminates automatically and the property returns to the grantor without a reentry action.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Restatement (First) of Property: Freehold Estates section 44 states the fee simple determinable rule as automatic expiration on the stated event, and Restatement (First) of Property: Future Interests section 154 identifies the possibility of reverter as the grantor's retained reversionary interest. Cornell Wex summarizes the same rule: a possibility of reverter is retained by the grantor of a fee simple determinable, durational language creates it, and the land automatically returns when the condition occurs. See https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/possibility_of_a_reverter and https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/fee_simple_determinable. Research did not contradict the supplied key.

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are preserved: the durational grant created a fee simple determinable, the stated use stopped, and the grantor owns by automatic reverter.
