## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Titles, Recording Acts, Types of statutes, and the verified outline code is `84040402`. The legal issue is whether a later judgment lien against the seller makes title unmarketable when the buyer previously recorded the executory land-sale contract in a notice-act jurisdiction that authorizes recording contracts.

The governing rule is that a notice recording act sets priority among competing real-property claims by protecting later purchasers for value who take without notice, and a recorded instrument gives constructive notice of the recorded interest. Judgment creditors do not automatically occupy a superior position over earlier real-property interests, and a judgment lien generally reaches only the judgment debtor's remaining interest. West Federal Savings & Loan Ass'n v. Interstate Investment, Inc., 57 Wis. 2d 690, 205 N.W.2d 361 (1973), treats an unrecorded conveyance as valid against judgment creditors because they are not bona fide creditors for value under that recording framework. Wisconsin Statutes section 706.08 annotations state the same West Federal rule. Cornell's Wex summary confirms the general recording-act frame: recording acts determine priority between competing real-property claims and vary by notice, race, and race-notice structure.

That authority confirms the supplied key. Daniel's contract was recorded before Ruth filed the judgment, so Ruth had record notice and was not in the protected later-purchaser position. Ruth's judgment lien could attach only subject to Daniel's prior recorded contract interest. Lydia therefore can deliver marketable record title to Daniel, and specific performance should be granted for Lydia. Research checked: West Federal Savings & Loan Ass'n v.
