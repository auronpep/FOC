### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam-register)

**Gold Key:** The best evidence rule (FRE 1002) requires the original of a writing, recording, or photograph to prove *its content* — it does not bar a witness from testifying from memory about an event just because a written record of that event also exists. A witness with personal knowledge may always testify to what he perceived.

C is correct. Timothy is testifying from his own memory about cash he personally handed Paul. ### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (dominant trap)** — This choice tempts students who know the best evidence rule exists and reflexively apply it whenever a document is in the fact pattern. The trap is thinking that because the notebook records the amounts, only the notebook can prove them. But FRE 1002 governs proof of the *content of a writing* — it does not prevent a witness with firsthand knowledge from testifying about the underlying event. Timothy is not trying to prove what the notebook says; he is testifying from his own perception about cash he handed over. **Gold Key:** The best evidence rule applies to writings, recordings, and photographs — not to testimonial evidence about an event a witness personally observed.

**B** — This choice sells the idea that silence in the face of another's statement counts as an adoptive admission. Under FRE 801(d)(2)(B), an adoptive admission requires that the party *manifested* adoption or belief in the truth's truth — mere silence or failure to correct is not enough unless the circumstances clearly show the party accepted the statement as true. Here, the facts say only that Timothy "made no changes" in the notebook. That is passive non-action, not an affirmative manifestation of adoption. No adoptive admission exists.

**D** — This choice invokes the "opened the door" / curative admissibility concept. But Timothy's testimony is independently admissible under FRE 602 as firsthand knowledge. The "door-opening" doctrine is a separate theory of relevance that permits otherwise inadmissible evidence to rebut or explain evidence already admitted. Timothy does not need door-opening to get his testimony in — it is already admissible on its own. The choice states a true rule but gives the wrong *reason* for admissibility.

### 8. Black-Letter Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**FRE 602 (Need for Personal Knowledge):** "A witness may testify to a matter only if evidence is introduced sufficient to support a finding that the witness has personal knowledge of the matter. Evidence to prove personal knowledge may, but need not, consist of the witness's own testimony." The rule is a low threshold — the judge admits the testimony if a reasonable jury could find personal knowledge, and the jury decides weight. *See* FRE 602; 2 Wigmore § 650.

**FRE 1002 (Best Evidence Rule / Requirement of Original):** "An original writing, recording, or photograph is required in order to prove its content unless these rules or a federal statute provides otherwise." The rule applies only when a party seeks to prove the *content* of a writing, recording, or photograph. It does not bar oral testimony about an event merely because a written record of that event exists. *See* FRE 1002 Advisory Committee Notes ("payment may be proved without producing the written receipt which was given"); *Hera v. McCormick*, 425 Pa. Super. 432, 625 A.2d 682 (1993).

**FRE 801(d)(2)(B) (Adoptive Admission):** A statement is not hearsay if "the statement is one the party manifested that it adopted or believed to be true." Mere silence or failure to deny is insufficient; there must be evidence the party affirmatively accepted the statement as true. *See* FRE 801(d)(2)(B); 2 McCormick on Evidence § 255.

An adoptive admission requires the party to have *manifested* acceptance of the statement as true — saying "yes, that's right" or acting in a way that clearly shows agreement. Simply not correcting someone else's writing is passive, not adoptive.

**If you picked D:** You identified the right outcome (admissible) but gave the wrong reason. "Opened the door" is a curative-admissibility concept for evidence that is *otherwise inadmissible*. Timothy's testimony is independently admissible under FRE 602 — he was there, he perceived it. He doesn't need door-opening.
