## 1. Final Question

A grand jury indicted Daniel for armed robbery after a cash box was taken from a private Christian music festival's Bible-booth trailer. Three days after the indictment, detectives arranged a six-person in-person lineup and asked Mary, the booth cashier, to view it. Daniel's appointed attorney was not notified, and no attorney was present. Mary identified Daniel from the lineup. At trial, the prosecution sought to introduce the lineup identification through the detective's testimony and to have Mary identify Daniel in court. Was the lineup identification obtained in violation of Daniel's constitutional rights?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

After indictment, police conduct an in-person lineup without notifying counsel or having counsel present. Does that violate the accused's constitutional rights?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

A. No, because an identification lineup happens before trial and outside the courtroom, so it is not a critical stage of the prosecution.

B. No, because Daniel had not yet appeared for arraignment, so the Sixth Amendment right to counsel had not attached.

C. No, because Mary chose Daniel without threats or promises, so the identification was voluntary.

D. Yes, because Daniel had a Sixth Amendment right to counsel at the post-indictment in-person lineup, and conducting it without notice to counsel or a valid waiver violated that right.

## 5. Correct Answer

D (original key C).

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

D is the best answer. The indictment started formal adversary proceedings, and the later in-person lineup was a critical prosecution stage. Gold Key: after formal charges, a live lineup or showup requires counsel or a valid waiver. Daniel's lawyer was not notified, no lawyer was present, and no waiver appears in the facts. Silver Key: lock the timeline before comparing the answers: indictment first, live lineup second, no counsel third. That sequence makes the lineup a Sixth Amendment violation.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A sells the courtroom-only myth. A lineup feels investigative because it happens outside court, but a post-indictment live confrontation between the accused and an eyewitness is still a critical stage. Gold Key: the Sixth Amendment right reaches a post-charge corporeal lineup even though the lineup occurs before trial and outside the courtroom.

B sells the dominant timing trap. Arraignment is one way the right can attach, so the word sounds important. The breaker is that indictment is also a formal start of adversary proceedings; Daniel had already been indicted before the lineup.

C sells a clean-looking voluntariness answer. If the issue were police coercion or a due-process reliability challenge, threats and pressure might matter. The call here is the counsel right at a post-indictment live lineup, and a calm witness choice does not cure the absence of counsel.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. Under United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), a post-indictment lineup is a critical stage at which the accused is entitled to counsel. Gilbert v. California, 388 U.S. 263 (1967), applies the same lineup-counsel doctrine and treats testimony about an uncounseled lineup as subject to exclusion. Kirby v. Illinois, 406 U.S. 682 (1972), confirms the attachment point: the Sixth Amendment counsel right arises only after adversary judicial proceedings have begun, such as by formal charge, preliminary hearing, indictment, information, or arraignment. Moore v. Illinois, 434 U.S. 220 (1977), states the same attachment formulation for postcharge identification procedures.

Daniel was indicted before the lineup. The lineup was in person. Counsel was not notified, no attorney was present, and the facts show no waiver. The lineup identification was therefore obtained in violation of Daniel's Sixth Amendment right to counsel.
