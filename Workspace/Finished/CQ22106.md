## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Lydia leased a small private garden cottage to Barnabas under a written one-year lease. Rent was due month to month. The lease said that it would automatically renew for successive one-year periods unless either Lydia or Barnabas gave notice of termination at least one month before the end of the current lease year. The lease also said that Lydia and Barnabas would keep each other informed about how the cottage was being used.

More than a year passed. Barnabas kept paying monthly rent, Lydia accepted it, and neither of them contacted the other about the cottage's use or about renewing the lease.

At this point, what is the most likely state of the lease?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

After a one-year lease with automatic renewal and monthly rent continues silently past the year, what tenancy most likely exists?

### 3. Final answer choices

### 5. Correct answer

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. The lease did not simply end, because Barnabas kept paying and Lydia accepted monthly rent. But the parties also did nothing to confirm a full new one-year commitment. Gold Key: when a tenancy continues without a firm final end date, it is periodic, and monthly rent is the strongest signal for a month-to-month period. Silver Key: separate the tenancy type from the notice clause; the question asks the state of the lease, not which sentence sounds most formal.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

B is attractive because the original writing was a one-year lease. The breaker is that a term of years has a fixed end date and ends without notice; this arrangement kept rolling unless notice was given.

C is attractive because neither side talked and the relationship feels loose. The breaker is that an at-will tenancy can end at any time, while the facts still point to a rent period and notice before termination.

D is attractive because the lease said it would automatically renew for successive one-year periods. The breaker is the practical status after silence: courts are likely to treat the continuing, monthly-paid relationship as a month-to-month periodic tenancy rather than bind both sides to another full year without any confirming action.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is unchanged: the most likely current estate is a month-to-month periodic tenancy. A periodic tenancy continues from period to period until one party gives proper notice. A term of years has a fixed end date and ordinarily ends without notice. A tenancy at will lacks the period-and-notice structure. Here, the lease had renewal language, but rent was payable monthly, more than a year passed with no communication, and the parties behaved only as monthly rent-payor and rent-acceptor. On these facts, a court is likely to use the monthly rent interval as the practical period and require one month's notice.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Cornell Wex states that a month-to-month tenancy is a periodic tenancy without a fixed expiration date and continues while the tenant pays monthly rent until proper notice terminates it. Virginia Code section 55.1-1204(F) provides a primary-law example of the rent-period rule by making a tenancy week-to-week when rent is weekly and month-to-month otherwise, unless the rental agreement provides differently.
