Daniel was piloting a small motorboat at the posted no-wake speed across a lake during a private Christian retreat. ### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. Daniel was committing a misdemeanor by operating the boat without a current certificate, but that status did not cause Ruth's death. The stem says Daniel was moving lawfully and could not turn or stop before impact. The death came from Ruth surfacing directly in the boat's path, not from Daniel's expired certificate.

Gold Key: misdemeanor manslaughter still needs the misdemeanor to cause the death. A misdemeanor in the background is not enough.

Silver Key: when the answer choices point to illegal status, ask whether that illegality changed the physical outcome.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because licensing rules sound life-protective, and the certificate was expired. The breaker is causation: even if the certificate rule protects swimmers generally, this death did not happen because Daniel lacked a current certificate.

C is attractive because the correct result is "not guilty," so it offers a simple reason for acquittal. The breaker is the Gold Key: manslaughter can be based on criminal negligence or an unlawful act without an intent to kill; the missing element here is causation, not intent.

D is attractive because it names a real homicide doctrine and matches the misdemeanor fact. The breaker is the Silver Key: the doctrine does not turn every unrelated misdemeanor into manslaughter; the misdemeanor must be tied to the death.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The credited answer is unchanged: the defendant is not guilty of manslaughter because the licensing misdemeanor did not proximately cause the death. Misdemeanor manslaughter requires more than temporal overlap between a misdemeanor and a death. The unlawful act must be a direct or proximate cause of the homicide. Oklahoma's misdemeanor-manslaughter instruction states the death must occur as a direct result of an act or event in the commission of a misdemeanor, and its comments explain that an unrelated misdemeanor is not enough. See OUJI-CR 4-94, https://okcca.net/ouji-cr/4-94/. General criminal causation doctrine also requires a causal connection between the actor's conduct and the prohibited result; Model Penal Code section 2.03 is the standard rule-number reference for causation. Manslaughter can exist without an intent to kill, and Cornell Wex summarizes involuntary manslaughter as negligently causing death and MPC section 210.3 as covering reckless homicide. See https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/manslaughter.

The transformed facts preserve the legal issue: Daniel's expired certificate is an unrelated misdemeanor status. His lawful operation and inability to avoid Ruth defeat the causal link. The correct result remains not guilty.
