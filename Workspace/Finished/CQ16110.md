### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Criminal Law and Procedure, General Principles, Actus reus, and the verified outline code is `72010301`. Under Model Penal Code section 2.01(3), liability for an omission cannot be based on inaction alone unless the offense expressly makes the omission sufficient or a duty to act is otherwise imposed by law. Jones v. United States, 308 F.2d 307 (D.C. Cir. 1962), states the common-law omission rule in criminal homicide terms: the neglected duty must be a legal duty, not a mere moral obligation, and recognized duty sources include law, status relationship, contract, voluntary assumption of care with isolation of the victim, or creation of the peril. Sources checked: https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/3185-tanaka-criminal-law-casebook/resources/5.1.1-mpc-201-requirement-of-voluntary-act-omission-as-basis-of-liability-possession-as-an-act/ and https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/308/307/132737/.

Those authorities confirm the credited outcome. Ruth did not create the peril, did not assume care in a way that isolated Timothy from help, had no contractual or statutory duty in the facts, and ordinary cousin status is not enough to impose criminal homicide liability. The source row's credited answer is therefore consistent with the governing rule.
