## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

A state wildlife statute declares the white dove to be an endangered species and prohibits anyone from killing a white dove anywhere in the state. Later, Congress created Cedar Table National Preserve, a federal preserve located entirely inside the state, to protect native plants and animals. The white doves in the preserve are descended from imported birds released decades ago by private landowners, and they have begun crowding out native ground-nesting birds. Acting under federal statutes governing national preserves, the United States Department of the Interior contracted with Stephen, a wildlife-control specialist and resident of another state, to kill the white doves living inside Cedar Table National Preserve. If Stephen is prosecuted by the state for violating the statute that prohibits killing white doves, which of the following is Stephen's strongest defense?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

What is the strongest defense when a state prosecutes a federal contractor for doing federally authorized wildlife work on federal land that conflicts with state wildlife law?

### 6. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. The federal government authorized Stephen to perform wildlife-control work inside a federal preserve under federal statutes governing that preserve. If the state applies its wildlife statute to punish him for doing that federally authorized work on federal land, the state law gives way. ### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

B is the dominant trap because endangered-species language can make federal authority sound exclusive. The breaker is that states may protect wildlife too; the problem is not the state's power to protect doves in general, but the state's attempt to punish federally authorized work on federal land.

D is attractive because Stephen lives in another state and the federal contract crosses state lines. The breaker is that the stronger defense is not a general interstate-commerce burden; it is a direct conflict between state law and valid federal action.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The credited answer is correct under the Property Clause and Supremacy Clause. The Property Clause gives Congress power to make needful rules and regulations respecting federal property, including federal land. The Supremacy Clause makes valid federal law supreme over conflicting state law. In Kleppe v. New Mexico, 426 U.S. 529 (1976), the Court held that Congress has broad authority over public lands, including authority to regulate and protect wildlife there, and that federal legislation enacted under the Property Clause overrides conflicting state laws under the Supremacy Clause.

The transformed item preserves the tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome. The state wildlife law may be valid generally. But as applied to prosecute a federal contractor for federally authorized wildlife-control work inside a federal preserve, it conflicts with federal law and must yield.
