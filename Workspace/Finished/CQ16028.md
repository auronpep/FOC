## 7. Right-Answer Explanation

**A — obtaining property by false pretenses only** is the correct answer.

Lydia made a knowing false representation of a material fact — the cloth's provenance and sacred identity — with the intent to obtain Timothy's $800, and she succeeded. Title to the $800 passed to her and title to the cloth passed to Timothy, both in reliance on her false statement. Every element of false pretenses is met.

Attempted murder, however, requires specific intent to cause death — either the desire that death result or substantial certainty that it will. Lydia possessed neither. The stem tells us she believed Timothy's faith and corresponding caution would protect him from danger. A defendant who expects the victim to be safe cannot harbor the specific intent to kill that attempted murder demands.

**Gold Key:** Attempted murder requires the defendant to have desired the victim's death or been substantially certain it would result. A defendant who genuinely believed the victim would avoid harm — however wrongly — lacks that specific intent and cannot be convicted of attempted murder.

**Silver Key:** When a "both charges" choice appears after a transaction involving serious harm, ask: does the stem show the defendant *wanted* or *expected* death? If the stem instead shows the defendant believed the victim would be safe, cut the murder charge — safety-belief defeats specific intent to kill.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** Common law obtaining property by false pretenses. Elements: (1) a false representation of a material past or present fact, (2) made knowingly (defendant knows the statement is false), (3) with intent to defraud (intent to cause the victim to transfer title to property), (4) that causes the victim to transfer title to property in reliance on the false representation.

**Application:** Lydia made a false representation of a material present fact (the cloth is an authenticated tabernacle fragment), she knew it to be false (she cut it from a hotel curtain), she acted with intent to obtain $800, and Timothy paid $800 and received the cloth (title to both transferred) in direct reliance. All elements satisfied.

**Attempted murder:** Common law attempt requires the specific intent to commit the target offense (here, murder — intent to cause death) plus an overt act in furtherance. The required mental state for attempted murder is purpose (desire) or knowledge to a substantial certainty that death will result. Lydia had neither. Her stated belief that Timothy's faith-driven caution would protect him is incompatible with intent to kill.

**Authority (lawyer-confirmation layer):**
- Common law false pretenses elements: Wayne R. LaFave, *Substantive Criminal Law* § 20.7 (3d ed.).
- MPC §223.3 (Theft by Deception) codifies the same substance: false impression created purposely to obtain property.
- MPC §5.01(1)(b): criminal attempt requires the actor to act with the kind of culpability otherwise required for commission of the crime. Murder under MPC §210.2 requires purposeful or knowing killing. Lydia's belief that Timothy would be safe negates purposeful or knowing causation of death.
- *People v. Whight*, 36 Cal. App. 4th 1143 (1995) (confirming false pretenses transfer-of-title requirement); LaFave § 19.4 (attempted murder's specific-intent requirement).

Timothy, a missionary preparing to travel to a remote region with a history of violent incidents, came to her shop seeking spiritual protection for his journey. Lydia offered to sell him a piece of woven cloth that she described as an authenticated fragment of the tabernacle used in biblical times, telling him it had been carbon-dated and certified by scholars and that possessing it would provide supernatural protection to the faithful. She charged him $800. Lydia knew that the cloth was a piece she had cut from a hotel curtain the previous week and that it had no special provenance whatsoever, but she believed that if Timothy had sufficient faith in the relic's power, his confidence would lead him to act so cautiously and prayerfully on his mission that he would avoid any real danger. Timothy paid $800 for the cloth, carried it throughout his mission trip, and — believing he was under supernatural protection — ventured into a dangerous area he would otherwise have avoided. He was violently attacked there; although he survived, he suffered severe injuries and came close to death.

If Lydia is prosecuted for her sale of the cloth to Timothy, she may properly be found guilty of

A. obtaining property by false pretenses only.
B. neither attempted murder nor obtaining property by false pretenses.
C. attempted murder only.
D. attempted murder and obtaining property by false pretenses.

A seller knowingly misrepresented a worthless item as a sacred relic to obtain payment, believing the buyer's faith would keep him safe. The buyer, relying on the representation, was gravely injured. Can the seller be convicted of false pretenses, attempted murder, both, or neither?

#### choice_by_choice_walkthrough

---

**Choice A — obtaining property by false pretenses only** ← *CORRECT*

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:**
Lydia knew the cloth was worthless, told Timothy it was a sacred artifact, got his $800, and Timothy paid in reliance. That's a complete false pretenses stack visible from the stem. The stem also tells us Lydia expected Timothy to be safe — so no intent to kill, no attempted murder.

**(2) Student label:** CORRECT

**(3) What a true/responsive version looks like:**
The answer correctly identifies that false pretenses is fully established (knowing lie + property obtained) and correctly rejects attempted murder (seller expected victim to survive).

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:**
Common law false pretenses: (1) false representation of material present/past fact, (2) known to be false, (3) intent to defraud (obtain title), (4) victim transfers title in reliance. All four met. Attempted murder requires specific intent (purpose or substantial certainty) to cause death. Lydia's belief that Timothy would be safe is incompatible with that intent. LaFave § 20.7; MPC §§ 223.3, 5.01, 210.2.

---

**Choice B — neither attempted murder nor obtaining property by false pretenses**

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:**
Lydia knew the cloth was a hotel curtain. She told Timothy it was a tabernacle fragment. He paid $800 because he believed her. That's three out of four false pretenses elements sitting right in the stem. "Neither" cannot survive when one crime is visibly complete.

**(2) Student label:** CUT — flat denial of visible crime

**(3) What a true/responsive version would look like:**
To be true, this choice would need the stem to show either that Lydia actually believed the cloth was authentic (no knowing false statement) or that Timothy gave away the $800 as a gift rather than a transaction (no transfer in reliance). Neither is the case.

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:**

**Choice C — attempted murder only**

**(2) Student label:** CUT — reversed on both charges

**Choice D — attempted murder and obtaining property by false pretenses** ← *DOMINANT TRAP*
