## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation
The Double Jeopardy Clause of the Fifth Amendment, which applies to the states through the Fourteenth Amendment, protects a defendant from being tried twice for the same offense. However, this protection only applies once **jeopardy has attached**. In a jury trial (or its council equivalent in this setting), the constitutional threshold for the attachment of jeopardy is the **administration of the oath** to the jurors. While the selection process (voir dire) is a necessary step, jeopardy does not attach merely because the jurors have been selected or seated. Because Isaac's council members were dismissed before being sworn, jeopardy never attached, and the council is not barred from proceeding with the hearing at a later date.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
**(A) [Original D]** This argument is incorrect because it misidentifies the reason jeopardy failed to attach. Whether a judge (or presiding elder) or a prosecutor (or presenter) initiates the action is relevant to the "manifest necessity" analysis *after* jeopardy has attached, but it does not prevent the attachment of jeopardy itself. Ecclesiastical or judicial actions are not categorically exempt from triggering double jeopardy if the proper threshold (the oath) has been met.

**(C) [Original B]** This argument is a mischaracterization of the procedural posture. A mistrial can only occur after jeopardy has attached. Because the members were never sworn, the continuance was granted before the trial/hearing had legally "begun" for double jeopardy purposes. The mere seating of members without the oath is insufficient to bar retrial.

**(D) [Original A] [DOMINANT TRAP]** This is the most common student error. Students often believe that once the selection process is over and the "jury" is ready to go, the trial has started. However, the law is strict: the administration of the oath is the specific "moment of attachment" in a jury trial. Before that moment, the state (or council) may seek a continuance or dismissal without triggering double jeopardy bars.

## 8. Black-Letter Verification
**Tested Rule:** In a jury trial, double jeopardy attaches when the jury is sworn.
**Authority:** *Crist v. Bretz*, 437 U.S. 28 (1978) (holding that the federal rule that jeopardy attaches when the jury is empaneled and sworn is an integral part of the constitutional guarantee).
**Reasoning:** The threshold for attachment is bright-line. In a bench trial, it is when the first witness is sworn. In a jury trial, it is when the jury is sworn. Since the jurors here were dismissed *before* the oath, Isaac was never "in jeopardy."

**Gold Key: Double Jeopardy Attachment**
