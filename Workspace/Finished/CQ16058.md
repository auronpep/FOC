### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** Kidnapping is not strict-liability transportation of a person. The crime requires intentional or knowing confinement or movement of the person, against that person's will and without lawful authority. Daniel intentionally stole and later extorted money over the puppet theater, but the facts say he never noticed Timothy. Because Daniel did not know Timothy was inside, he could not have intentionally or knowingly confined or moved Timothy as the person kidnapped.

**Gold Key:** Kidnapping requires a culpable mental state directed at the person being confined or moved. If the defendant unknowingly transports a hidden passenger, the asportation element happened in the world, but the kidnapping mens rea is missing.

**Silver Key:** When two choices say "not guilty," do not stop at the outcome. Lock the element the stem explicitly negates. Here, the stem repeats "unaware" facts, so the answer must turn on knowledge of Timothy, not on Daniel's broader purpose.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (not guilty - incidental to theft)** sells the right result with the wrong diagnostic. The appeal is strong because Daniel plainly wanted the puppet theater, not the child, and Timothy's movement rode along with the property theft. But the reason that defeats kidnapping is narrower: Daniel did not know Timothy was there. If Daniel had known the child was inside and still carried the puppet theater away, the fact that his main goal was the puppet theater would not automatically erase kidnapping liability. **Silver Key:** In a same-outcome clash, choose the reason that matches the fact the stem keeps emphasizing.

**B (guilty - moved without consent)** catches the student who treats kidnapping as act-only movement. The facts do show movement, confinement in the puppet theater, and no consent from an infant. The missing piece is mental state. **Gold Key:** Kidnapping requires intentional or knowing confinement or movement of the person. Daniel knowingly moved the puppet theater, but he did not knowingly move Timothy.

**D (guilty - serious felony caused the asportation)** imports a felony-murder style shortcut into kidnapping. The theft and extortion are serious crimes, and the child's movement resulted from that criminal episode. But kidnapping has no automatic "felony-kidnapping" rule that supplies the required intent merely because the movement occurred during another felony. **Gold Key:** A felony in the background does not substitute for knowledge that a person is being carried away or confined.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The source row tests kidnapping mens rea. The supplied explanation defines kidnapping as intentional asportation and confinement of a person against that person's will, by force or threat, and without lawful authority. The credited outcome is that the defendant is not guilty because he did not know the child was in the vehicle when he moved it.

The variation preserves that rule exactly. Daniel knowingly takes the puppet theater and knowingly threatens to burn the puppet theater, but he remains unaware that Timothy is inside. That makes the property crimes plausible, but it leaves the person-directed mental state for kidnapping unproved.

Legal authority: Model Penal Code § 212.1 frames kidnapping around unlawfully removing or confining another with specified purposes, such as ransom or facilitation of a felony. That structure confirms that kidnapping is not mere accidental transportation of a person during a property crime; the confinement or removal must be tied to the defendant's culpable purpose toward the person. Cornell LII's Wex summary of kidnapping identifies MPC § 212.1 and its purpose-based formulation: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/kidnapping. The federal kidnapping statute likewise uses knowledge language for transportation of a person who has been unlawfully seized or confined, reinforcing that kidnapping is not strict-liability movement of an unknown passenger. 18 U.S.C. § 1201(a): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1201.
