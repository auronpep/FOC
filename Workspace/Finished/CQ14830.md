## 1. Final Question

Lydia sued Peter, alleging that she was seriously injured when Peter steered a cargo bike through a retreat-center walkway and knocked her down while she was carrying hymnals. During Peter's case, Timothy testified that Lydia had told him that the cargo bike "only brushed my sleeve." On cross-examination, should the court allow Lydia to ask Timothy whether he is a claims adjuster for Peter's liability insurance company?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

May a party ask a defense witness about employment by the defendant's liability insurer to show bias, and if so, for what purpose?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. The question about Timothy's job is allowed because it tends to show a motive to shade his testimony in favor of Peter. It is not admitted to prove Peter was negligent or that Peter had liability insurance coverage. Gold Key: FRE 411 blocks liability-insurance evidence when it is used to prove negligence or wrongful conduct, but permits the same fact when offered for another purpose such as witness bias. Silver Key: solve the purpose split first; the same insurance fact can be barred for one use and allowed for a narrower impeachment use.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because it notices the bias purpose and then lets that purpose carry the evidence too far. The breaker is the use limit: the insurance connection may impeach Timothy's credibility, but it is not substantive proof that Peter acted negligently. Gold Key: FRE 411 gives the bias exception without turning insurance into proof of fault.

B is attractive because many students remember that insurance is usually kept away from the jury. The breaker is the word "barred"; the rule is not an absolute bar when the evidence is offered to show witness bias or prejudice.

D is attractive because it sounds like a trial-management objection that keeps the case focused. The breaker is that a witness's financial or employment tie to a party's insurer is a direct bias issue, not a collateral detour from credibility.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule is unchanged. Federal Rule of Evidence 411 excludes liability-insurance evidence when offered to prove negligence or wrongful conduct, but allows it for another purpose, including proving a witness's bias or prejudice. Federal Rule of Evidence 607 allows a party to attack a witness's credibility, and Rule 611(b) permits cross-examination on matters affecting credibility. United States v. Abel, 469 U.S. 45 (1984), confirms that bias impeachment is contemplated by the Federal Rules and that evidence tending to show a witness's bias is relevant to credibility. Here, Timothy's employment as an adjuster for Peter's liability insurer gives him a possible interest in minimizing Lydia's injury account. The evidence is therefore admissible for impeachment only, not as substantive proof that Peter acted negligently.
