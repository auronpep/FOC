## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question
A state statute provides that assault at a public festival is generally punishable by a maximum of four years in prison. The statute further provides that the maximum punishment increases to nine years in prison if the defendant possessed a dangerous instrument during the assault. The statute designates the dangerous-instrument fact as a sentencing factor. Daniel has pleaded not guilty to assault after an altercation near a Psalm-singing stage and has requested a jury trial. The prosecutor has announced that she will seek a nine-year sentence based on Daniel's alleged possession of a sharpened metal tent stake during the assault. What finding regarding Daniel's possession of the tent stake is necessary for the court to impose a nine-year sentence?

### 2. Distilled Core Question
When an alleged fact raises the maximum sentence for assault above the otherwise available maximum, who must find that fact and by what burden?

### 3. Final answer choices
A. A jury finding beyond a reasonable doubt.

B. A jury finding by a preponderance of the evidence.

C. A judicial finding by a preponderance of the evidence.

D. A judicial finding beyond a reasonable doubt.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation
A is correct. The tent-stake fact does more than guide the judge inside an existing range; it raises the maximum sentence from four years to nine years. Gold Key: except for the narrow prior-conviction exception, a fact that increases the statutory maximum punishment must be submitted to the jury and proved beyond a reasonable doubt. Silver Key: read the answer array as a 2x2 grid. One axis is factfinder, and the other axis is burden. The only choice that has both required pieces is A.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
B is attractive because it keeps the jury and therefore feels respectful of the trial right. The breaker is the burden. Gold Key: a maximum-raising fact is treated like an element for this purpose, so preponderance is too low.

C is the dominant trap because the statute itself calls the tent-stake fact a sentencing factor, and ordinary sentencing facts are often found by judges by a preponderance. The breaker is that this fact raises the statutory ceiling. Silver Key: once the fact changes the maximum punishment, the "sentencing factor" label does not control the answer.

D is attractive because it uses the criminal-law burden students trust. The breaker is the factfinder. A judge cannot supply the finding that exposes Daniel to the higher statutory maximum.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Criminal Law and Procedure, Other Crimes, Assault, and the verified outline code is `73020200`. The governing constitutional rule is the Apprendi rule: other than the fact of a prior conviction, any fact that increases the penalty beyond the prescribed statutory maximum must be submitted to a jury and proved beyond a reasonable doubt. See *Apprendi v. New Jersey*, 530 U.S. 466 (2000), summarized by the Library of Congress Constitution Annotated at https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt6-5-3-4/ALDE_00013129/. The current doctrine remains consistent with that framing; in *Erlinger v. United States*, 602 U.S. 821 (2024), the Court again required jury determination beyond a reasonable doubt for a factual finding that increased punishment exposure. See https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-370_i4dj.pdf. Here, Daniel's possession of the tent stake is the factual predicate that raises the maximum from four to nine years. The legislature's "sentencing factor" label cannot avoid the jury-and-reasonable-doubt requirement. The credited outcome is unchanged from the source item.
