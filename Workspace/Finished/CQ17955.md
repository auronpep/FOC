### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** FRE 608(a) allows a party to attack a witness's credibility through reputation or opinion evidence — but only if that evidence addresses the witness's **character for truthfulness or untruthfulness**. The rule is narrow: it does not open the door to general bad character evidence. Samuel's testimony that Hannah is "careless with her belongings and quick to anger" says nothing about whether she is honest or dishonest. These are negative character traits, but they are not traits bearing on veracity. Because the offered reputation evidence does not address truthfulness, it falls outside the scope of 608(a), and the court should sustain the objection.

**Gold Key (GK-EVID-608-TRUTH-01):** Under FRE 608(a), the *only* character trait admissible for witness impeachment by reputation or opinion is the witness's character for **truthfulness or untruthfulness** — no other negative trait qualifies, no matter how unsavory.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (wrong):** This choice sells the misconception that the admissibility of character impeachment turns on whether the witness "opened the door" by testifying about her own peacefulness. Under 608(a)(2), evidence of a witness's *truthful* character is admissible only *after* the character for truthfulness has been attacked — but that door-opening rule applies to **rebuttal** of truthful character, not to the initial admissibility of reputation evidence about untruthfulness. Here, the problem is not that Hannah failed to open a door; the problem is that Samuel's testimony addresses the wrong character trait entirely. The "peacefulness" framing is a red herring that reverses the 608(a)(2) trigger. **Silver Key (SK-EVID-608-DOOR-01):** When a choice makes admissibility turn on whether the witness "opened the door," check whether the door-opening rule actually applies — 608(a)(2) governs *rebuttal* of truthful character, not the threshold admissibility of untruthfulness evidence.

**B (wrong) — DOMINANT TRAP:** This is the most attractive wrong answer because it states a principle that *feels* right: if a witness is a bad person, shouldn't the jury know? Many students intuitively believe that any negative character evidence is fair game for impeachment. But FRE 608(a) explicitly rejects this — the rule is limited to truthfulness, not general bad character. The word "any" is the tell: 608(a) does not permit "any negative character trait"; it permits only reputation or opinion about truthfulness or untruthfulness. **Gold Key GK-EVID-608-TRUTH-01** kills this trap: the trait must bear on veracity, not on general bad character.

**D (wrong):** This choice reframes the impeachment as an attack on Hannah's perceptual capacity — the idea that a careless person is less likely to observe accurately. But that is a different impeachment mode entirely (perception/memory under common law, not character impeachment under 608(a)). The seller offered this testimony *solely* to attack Hannah's credibility as a witness through her reputation, not to show a specific defect in her ability to perceive the transaction. Even if carelessness could theoretically bear on perception, that is not the theory on which the evidence was offered. The choice is a wrong-frame bait: it shifts the analysis from character-for-truthfulness to perceptual capacity. **Silver Key (SK-EVID-608-FRAME-01):** Always check the theory on which the evidence was offered — if the proponent says the evidence attacks character, evaluate it under 608(a)'s truthfulness limit, not under a different impeachment mode.
