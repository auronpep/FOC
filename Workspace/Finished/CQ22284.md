## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (Second-degree murder) — Why attractive:** The student sees an intentional killing without an obvious "planning" narrative and defaults to second-degree murder as the "default" intentional murder. The student may think premeditation requires extensive advance planning, not just overnight reflection. **Breaker:** Second-degree murder covers intentional killings *without* premeditation and deliberation, or depraved-heart murder, or felony murder. The father arranged the meeting, brought a weapon, and executed the killing at a time and place of his choosing. That is premeditation. **Gold Key:** Bringing a weapon to a scheduled meeting the next day is premeditation — this is first-degree murder, not second-degree.

**B (Involuntary manslaughter) — Why attractive:** A student who is overwhelmed by the emotional facts may seize on the word "distraught" and conclude the killing was unintentional or merely negligent. **Breaker:** Involuntary manslaughter is an unintentional killing resulting from criminal negligence or an unlawful act. Here, the father deliberately aimed and shot the patriarch. This was an intentional killing — the opposite of involuntary.

**D (Voluntary manslaughter) — Why attractive (DOMINANT TRAP):** The facts are loaded with provocation: a decades-long feud, a secret romance, a pregnancy, a cruel betrayal, and a daughter's suicide. A student who focuses on the emotional gravity may conclude that no one could have cooled off, making this a heat-of-passion killing. **Breaker:** Voluntary manslaughter requires that the defendant acted in the heat of passion with *no reasonable cooling-off period*. Here, the father received the news, then waited overnight and scheduled a meeting for the next morning. That gap — during which he arranged the meeting and brought a weapon — is a cooling-off period as a matter of law. A reasonable person would have regained self-control during that time. The killing was revenge, not passion. **Silver Key:** When the defendant schedules the confrontation and brings a weapon the next day, the cooling-off period has already run — heat-of-passion mitigation fails regardless of how severe the provocation was.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing rule:** At common law, first-degree murder is an intentional killing committed with premeditation and deliberation. Premeditation means the defendant formed the intent to kill before the act; deliberation means the defendant made a considered decision to kill in a "cool state of blood." *See* 2 W. LaFave, *Substantive Criminal Law* § 14.5 (3d ed.); *People v. Anderson*, 70 Cal. 2d 15 (1968) (premeditation and deliberation require more than mere intent to kill; they require reflection and consideration, however brief).

**Voluntary manslaughter mitigation:** A killing that would otherwise be murder is reduced to voluntary manslaughter if the defendant acted in the heat of passion upon adequate provocation, with no reasonable cooling-off period. The four elements are: (1) adequate provocation (objective), (2) actual provocation (subjective), (3) no reasonable time to cool off (objective), and (4) no actual cooling off (subjective). *See* *Girouard v. State*, 321 Md. 532 (1991); *People v. Berry*, 18 Cal. 3d 509 (1976).

**Cooling-off period:** The critical factor here is the time between the provocation and the killing. When the defendant has time to reflect — particularly when the defendant schedules the confrontation and prepares a weapon — the cooling-off period defeats the heat-of-passion defense. *See* *State v. Gounagias*, 88 Wash. 304 (1915) (sufficient time to cool off negates heat of passion).

**Authority:** 2 W. LaFave, *Substantive Criminal Law* §§ 14.5, 15.2 (3d ed.); *People v. Anderson*, 70 Cal. 2d 15 (1968); *Girouard v. State*, 321 Md. 532 (1991); *State v. Gounagias*, 88 Wash. 304 (1915).

**Verification:** The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are UNCHANGED from the original. The father's overnight planning and weapon preparation constitute premeditation and deliberation. The cooling-off period defeats voluntary manslaughter. First-degree murder is correct.
