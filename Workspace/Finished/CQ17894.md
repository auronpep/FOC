## 6. Right-Answer Explanation (exam register)

**Gold Key: pen-register third-party rule** - A pen register that records only the numbers dialed from a telephone is not a Fourth Amendment search because the caller voluntarily conveys those numbers to the phone company to complete the call. No search means no warrant was required, so suppression is denied.

**Silver Key: split number from content before reading the privacy language** - The stem says the device recorded only numbers dialed and not conversation content. That fact routes the item away from wiretap/content protection and into the narrow pen-register rule.

Choice B is correct. Barnabas exposed the dialed numbers to the phone company's equipment in the ordinary course of placing calls. Under the third-party doctrine as applied to pen registers, he has no reasonable expectation of privacy in those numbers. Because the pen register captured routing numbers only, not conversation content or location history, the Fourth Amendment was not triggered and the court should deny the motion to suppress.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Tested rule:** A traditional pen register that records only numbers dialed from a telephone does not conduct a Fourth Amendment search because the caller voluntarily conveys those numbers to the telephone company. No warrant is required on Fourth Amendment grounds, and suppression is denied.

**Authority:** *Smith v. Maryland*, 442 U.S. 735 (1979), held that installation and use of a pen register recording numbers dialed was not a Fourth Amendment search and required no warrant. Source checked: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/442/735/. *Carpenter v. United States*, 585 U.S. 296 (2018), held that historical CSLI is different and requires Fourth Amendment protection, but expressly stated that the decision is narrow and does not disturb Smith and Miller. Source checked: https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/16-402_h315.pdf.

**Credited outcome:** The researched authority agrees with the supplied key. The motion should be denied because the pen-register records are not protected under the Fourth Amendment.
