### 1. Final question - revised stem + call

Five monthly rent payments never reached Mary, who owned a small workshop building in a private arts courtyard. Mary had leased the building to Paul for a 12-year term. Three years after the term began, Paul signed a four-year sublease with Ruth, who used the building for a Scripture-card printing studio.

Under the sublease, Ruth agreed to make monthly rent payments to Paul. Ruth made each payment to Paul on time, but Paul kept five payments instead of paying Mary. Paul has moved out of the jurisdiction and cannot be found. Mary has sued Ruth for the unpaid rent. There is no applicable statute.

If the court rules that Ruth is not liable to Mary for the unpaid rent, what is the most likely reason?

### 6. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. Ruth took a true sublease, not an assignment. Paul still kept a future slice of the 12-year lease because Ruth's four-year sublease did not cover the entire remaining term. That means Ruth did not step into Paul's full leasehold relationship with Mary.

Gold Key: A subtenant under a true sublease is not directly liable to the landlord on the prime lease unless the subtenant is in privity of estate or privity of contract with the landlord. A transfer shorter than the remaining lease term normally leaves the prime tenant as the landlord's direct obligor.

Silver Key: In a rent suit after a lease transfer, ask two questions before chasing fairness: who promised rent to whom, and did the transferee take the entire remaining leasehold? Here Ruth promised Paul, not Mary, and Paul did not transfer the entire remaining term.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B.** This choice sells a compromise: Ruth is not the main debtor, but maybe she is a backup payer because she occupied the building. The breaker is that suretyship is not created merely by being a subtenant. Ruth did not promise Mary that she would answer for Paul's rent debt, and the sublease itself did not make her Mary's surety.

**C.** This is the dominant trap because "novation" sounds like the landlord's practical fix when Paul disappears. The breaker is the Gold Key: a novation requires a new agreement that substitutes or releases obligations; a sublease by itself does not release Paul from the master lease or create direct rent liability between Mary and Ruth.

**D.** This choice focuses on the payment fact and rewards Ruth for doing what her sublease required. The breaker is the Silver Key: the court's reason is not that Ruth discharged a duty to Mary; it is that Ruth never had a direct rent duty to Mary in the first place.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested issue and credited outcome are unchanged from QID 15001: a landlord suing a subtenant for rent under the prime lease loses when there is no privity of estate or privity of contract between them.

Authority floor: Restatement (Second) of Property: Landlord and Tenant section 16.1(2) ties a transferee's obligation on a lease promise running with the land to a transfer that brings the transferee into privity of estate with the person entitled to enforce the promise. A public ABA journal copy quotes section 16.1(2) at page 108: https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/publications/real_property_trust_and_estate_law_journal/v49/02/2014-aba-rpte-journal-v49-no2-fall-issue.pdf. Northside Station Associates v. Maddry, 105 N.C. App. 384, 413 S.E.2d 319 (1992), explains the traditional assignment/sublease line: an assignment transfers the tenant's entire interest without retaining a reversionary interest, while a sublease leaves a reversion in the tenant; it also states that the original lessor has no direct right against a sublessee under that theory. Public copy: https://law.justia.com/cases/north-carolina/court-of-appeals/1992/9110dc86-1.html. Cornell Wex also summarizes that a sublease is a new contract between lessee and sublessee and creates no privity of contract between owner and sublessee: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/sublease.

On these facts, Mary leased to Paul for 12 years. After three years, nine years remained. Paul transferred only four years to Ruth, leaving Paul with a reversionary slice of the term. The transfer is therefore a sublease, not an assignment. Ruth promised to pay Paul, not Mary. There is no fact that Mary released Paul, agreed to substitute Ruth, or received Ruth's promise to pay the prime rent. The court can therefore rule for Ruth because Mary lacks both privity of estate and privity of contract with Ruth.

No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is triggered. Attorney review is not required on the authority floor because a Restatement section and a reported case support the rule.

A subtenant under a true sublease is not directly liable to the landlord on the prime lease unless the subtenant is in privity of estate or privity of contract with the landlord. Trigger it when the tenant transfers less than the remaining lease term and the subtenant promises rent to the tenant, not the landlord.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-REAL_PROPERTY-PRIVITY-RENT-SUIT-01**  
In a rent suit after a lease transfer, map who promised rent to whom and whether the transferee took the entire remaining leasehold before deciding fairness.
