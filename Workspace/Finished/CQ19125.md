### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. Yes, because the carousel and its exposed gears were visible in the lot.**

This is the dominant trap. It sells a surface-level "visible equals appreciated" move: if Timothy could see the machine, Lydia should owe no duty. The breaker is the **Gold Key:** section 339 asks whether a child because of youth discovers or realizes the risk, not merely whether the object can be seen. A six-year-old may see a carousel and still fail to appreciate the danger of exposed moving gears.

**C. No, because land possessors are strictly liable whenever a child is injured by machinery on their property.**

This trap reaches the right yes/no direction but swaps in the wrong liability theory. The attractive pull is sympathy for an injured child around machinery. The breaker is that child-trespasser liability is still negligence-based and element-driven. The possessor is not strictly liable for every child injury on the property.

**D. Yes, because a trespassing child can never recover for injuries caused by conditions on land.**

This trap overreads the trespasser label into an absolute bar. The attraction is the familiar rule that trespassers receive limited protection. The breaker is the child-trespasser exception: when the possessor knows children are likely to trespass and maintains a dangerous artificial condition that young children cannot appreciate, a negligence duty may arise.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested black-letter rule is the child-trespasser/artificial-condition rule commonly stated in Restatement (Second) of Torts section 339. A land possessor may be liable for physical harm to trespassing children caused by an artificial condition if the possessor knows or has reason to know children are likely to trespass, the condition involves an unreasonable risk of death or serious bodily harm, the children because of youth do not discover or appreciate the risk, the utility of maintaining the condition and burden of eliminating the danger are slight compared with the risk, and the possessor fails to exercise reasonable care to protect the children.

Research checked: Restatement (Second) of Torts section 339 is indexed as "Artificial Conditions Highly Dangerous to Trespassing Children" at H2O Open Casebook, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/4643-tobia-torts-2022/resources/8.2.2-restatement-2d-339-artificial-conditions-highly-dangerous-to-trespassing-children/. Helguera v. Cirone, 178 Cal. App. 2d 232 (1960), quotes and applies the section 339 elements for young child trespassers injured by artificial conditions, https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2d/178/232.html.

**A path:** If you picked visibility, ask what exact risk the child must appreciate. Seeing the carousel is not the same as appreciating exposed moving gears.

**C path:** If you picked strict liability, keep the "No" direction but fix the reason. The doctrine is negligence duty with elements.

**D path:** If you picked no recovery for trespassers, circle "never" and look for exception facts. The stem gives child, artificial condition, serious risk, nonappreciation, and cheap guard.

## crossover_intersection_map

Premises liability overlaps with negligence duty and breach. It does not become strict liability merely because the injured person is a child.

## review_truth
