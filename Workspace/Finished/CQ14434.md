### 1. Final question

After a few rounds of bargaining, Daniel, who operated a shop selling premium handbell sets, knew that Hannah wanted a new set for her nephew’s hymn-themed wedding procession. On March 10, Daniel sent Hannah a signed, dated note saying, “If by Friday we land on the same handbell set and price, are we agreed?” Hannah wrote “Yes,” signed below it, and returned the note on March 12.

On March 14, Hannah mailed Daniel a signed letter offering to buy “one new five-octave handbell set, with all carrying cases and accessories, for $24,600 cash on delivery not later than April 15.” By coincidence, Daniel mailed Hannah a signed letter on March 14 offering to sell her “one new five-octave handbell set, with all carrying cases and accessories, for $24,600 cash on delivery not later than April 15.” The letters crossed in the mail and were received and read by both Hannah and Daniel on March 15.

In an action brought by Hannah, if Daniel denies that the parties had a binding contract on March 16, which argument most persuasively supports Hannah’s position?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Buyer and seller of goods exchange signed, matching writings after agreeing that matching terms within a short window would make a deal; seller later denies a contract. Which argument best supports the buyer?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. As to both formation and consideration, identical cross-offers are functionally equivalent to an offer followed by a responsive acceptance.

B. Because Daniel was a merchant seller and Hannah was not a merchant buyer, Daniel is estopped to deny that their writings created a binding contract.

C. A sale-of-goods contract may be made in any manner sufficient to show agreement, even if the exact moment of formation cannot be determined.

D. A sale-of-goods contract does not require the acceptance to mirror the offer exactly.

### 5. Correct answer

C. Original key: A.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is the best support for Hannah. The matching signed letters show the same goods, price, payment method, and delivery date within the short window Daniel’s earlier note created. **Gold Key:** Article 2 can find a sale-of-goods contract from writings that show agreement even when no single offer-and-acceptance moment can be pinned down. **Silver Key:** keep the uncertainty; do not force the facts into a cleaner sequence than the stem gives.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice is attractive because it turns the matching crossed letters into the familiar offer-then-acceptance template. The breaker is the Gold Key: the better support is not that crossed offers equal a responsive acceptance, but that Article 2 can recognize agreement even when the exact formation moment is uncertain.

**B.** This choice is attractive because “merchant” and “nonmerchant” sound like Article 2 labels that should matter. The breaker is that merchant status does not create an estoppel shortcut; it does not answer whether the writings show agreement.

**D.** This choice is attractive because Article 2 often relaxes the common-law mirror-image rule. The breaker is the Silver Key move: the writings here already match, so mirror-image relief answers the wrong problem.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested issue remains sale-of-goods formation by matching manifestations where the exact formation moment is unclear. The source row’s official explanation credits the answer that tracks flexible Article 2 formation and rejects mirror-image, cross-offer, and merchant-status distractors. 

UCC § 2-204 states that a contract for sale of goods may be made “in any manner sufficient to show agreement,” and that an agreement may be found even though the moment of making is undetermined. That directly supports the credited answer. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) UCC § 2-207 explains the separate battle-of-the-forms / non-mirror-image context; that rule concerns a definite expression of acceptance with additional or different terms, which is not the main problem when the writings match. ([Justia][2]) Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 22 likewise recognizes that mutual assent can exist even when neither offer nor acceptance can be identified, while § 23 preserves the separate idea that manifestations must refer to each other. ([Open Casebook][3])
