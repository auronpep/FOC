The tested rule is stable for the common-law MBE frame: duress may excuse many crimes when the defendant acts under an imminent human threat, but common law generally does **not** allow duress as a complete defense to intentional murder. In *People v. Anderson*, the California Supreme Court summarized the common-law rule: duress is no defense to killing an innocent person and is not a defense to any form of murder. The court also rejected reducing murder to manslaughter on duress alone. See [*People v. See [SCOCAL, *People v. Anderson*](https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/people-v-anderson-32259). Justia's current criminal-defense summary likewise states that duress is generally not a defense to murder, while noting that a few states may treat the issue differently. See [Justia, Duress Defense](https://www.justia.com/criminal/defenses/duress/).

## 17. student_version_black_letter_law_answer_explanation

Duress normally concerns a human threat that makes a person commit a crime. But common law does not allow duress as a complete defense to intentional murder. Ruth intentionally killed Lydia, so the complete-defense claim fails even though Stephen threatened Ruth with immediate death. The answer is **B**.
