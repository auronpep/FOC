### 1. Final question
Stephen was charged with first-degree murder. His appointed attorney learned in the first client interview that Stephen claimed he was running the score table at a private Bible-verse quiz tournament across town when the killing occurred, and that four tournament volunteers could confirm his presence. The attorney never contacted any of the volunteers, explaining later that his docket was too crowded. At trial, no alibi evidence was presented. Stephen was convicted. He later filed a motion for a new trial based on ineffective assistance of counsel. His appellate attorney located all four volunteers, each of whom confirmed the alibi. Has Stephen established the deficient performance prong of Strickland?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. The call asks only whether counsel's performance was deficient. Counsel knew about a specific alibi and knew the people who could confirm it, but contacted none of them for workload reasons. Gold Key: strategy deference protects informed strategic choices; it does not protect the failure to make a reasonable investigation before choosing. Silver Key: lock the call to deficient performance, not prejudice. Because the record shows no investigation of a known, readily available alibi, Stephen has established the deficient-performance prong.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because it uses a true general idea: lawyers do not have to present every possible defense. The breaker is that the stem is not about every possible defense; it is about a known alibi supported by identified witnesses whom counsel never contacted.

C is attractive because the missing alibi feels case-changing, so students may want automatic relief. The breaker is the call and the rule. Silver Key: the question asks deficient performance, and Gold Key: Strickland prejudice usually must be shown separately; automatic prejudice is reserved for narrow Cronic-style denial-of-counsel situations, not ordinary failure-to-investigate claims.

D is attractive because bar questions often respect counsel's trial strategy. The breaker is that this was not an informed strategic choice. Gold Key: strategic deference begins after reasonable investigation or a reasonable decision that investigation is unnecessary; a crowded docket is not a strategic investigation decision.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue is ineffective assistance of counsel under the Sixth Amendment right to counsel. The verified BarMatrix outline code is `75090400`, Right to Counsel. The source row's `74020101` homicide/murder code is not the tested doctrine; that metadata defect is logged in the analyzer notes, but it does not change the official key.

Under *Strickland v. Washington*, 466 U.S. 668 (1984), the deficient-performance prong asks whether counsel's representation fell below an objective standard of reasonableness under prevailing professional norms. Strickland also states that counsel has a duty to make reasonable investigations or a reasonable decision that makes further investigation unnecessary. That authority supports the credited answer because counsel knew of named alibi witnesses and failed to contact them for caseload reasons, not because of an informed strategic judgment. *Wiggins v. Smith*, 539 U.S. 510 (2003), reinforces that strategy based on incomplete investigation is reasonable only when professional judgment supports the limited investigation. *United States v. Cronic*, 466 U.S. 648 (1984), explains the narrow class of cases where prejudice may be presumed; ordinary failure-to-investigate claims proceed under Strickland, so automatic prejudice is not the right answer.
