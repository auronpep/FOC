## 8. Black-letter verification and legal reasoning

The original row tests the same rule: voluntary intoxication prevented “cool reflection” required for first-degree murder, but did not preclude second-degree murder’s knowingly-causing-death mental state; the official key is D. 

Lawyer confirmation: voluntary intoxication is generally not a legal excuse, but it may be considered on whether the defendant formed the specific intent required for premeditated and deliberate first-degree murder. The North Carolina pattern instruction states that voluntary intoxication is not generally a legal excuse, but the jury should consider whether it affected the defendant’s ability to formulate the specific intent required for premeditated and deliberate first-degree murder.  Commonwealth v. Henson likewise states that where proof requires specific criminal intent and evidence shows intoxication, the jury may consider intoxication on whether that specific intent was proved, while intoxication remains no excuse if the required intent is proved. ([CaseMine][1])

Lawyer confirmation on manslaughter: common-law heat-of-passion mitigation requires legally adequate provocation. Girouard v. State summarizes the provocation rule and states that words alone, unaccompanied by conduct showing present ability and intent to cause bodily harm, do not constitute adequate provocation. ([CaseBriefs][2])
