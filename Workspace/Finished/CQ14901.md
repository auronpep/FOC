## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

Mary sues Peter for breaching a promise in a handwritten note that Peter allegedly mailed to Mary after a private hymnbook sale. Peter denies writing the note. Which of the following would NOT be a sufficient basis for admitting the note into evidence?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Which handwriting-authentication basis fails when the nonexpert learned the handwriting only for this lawsuit?

### 3. Final Answer Choices

A. Evidence that the note was written as a reply to an earlier note that Mary sent to Peter about the same hymnbook sale.

C. Comparison by the trier of fact of the note with an admitted signature of Peter.

### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. The call asks which basis would NOT be sufficient. Barnabas is a nonexpert, and his familiarity with Peter's handwriting was acquired after the lawsuit solely so he could authenticate the signature. Gold Key: a lay handwriting witness must know the handwriting from a source not acquired for the current litigation. That current-litigation familiarity is the defect, so this is the insufficient authentication basis.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because it feels indirect: Mary is not offering a handwriting expert or a witness who recognizes Peter's signature. The breaker is that authentication can rest on circumstances, including a note that appears to reply to an earlier genuine note between the same parties. Silver Key: in a negative call, do not reject an indirect authentication route just because it is not handwriting testimony.

C is attractive because students often assume handwriting comparison must come from an expert. The breaker is that the trier of fact may compare the disputed writing with an authenticated specimen, so this is a sufficient basis and cannot be the NOT-sufficient answer.

D is attractive if the student over-applies the litigation-familiarity limitation to every lay witness. The breaker is that Mary says she is already familiar with Peter's signature and recognizes it, which is a standard nonexpert handwriting-identification basis.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. Federal Rule of Evidence 901(a) requires evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims. FRE 901(b)(2) specifically permits nonexpert handwriting opinion only when based on familiarity not acquired for the current litigation. FRE 901(b)(3) permits comparison by an expert or the trier of fact with an authenticated specimen. FRE 901(b)(4) permits authentication from distinctive characteristics and surrounding circumstances, which supports the reply-note route. The official key is confirmed: current-litigation-only familiarity is the insufficient basis. Sources checked: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901 and https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-evidence-dec-1-2024_0.pdf.
