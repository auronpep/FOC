## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation
Peter is not criminally liable because the physical movement that caused John’s death was not a voluntary act. Under the `Gold Key: voluntary_act_requirement`, criminal liability requires that the defendant’s conduct include a voluntary act—a bodily movement that is the product of the actor's conscious effort or determination. Physical movements caused by external biological or chemical triggers (such as neurotoxins) that strip the actor of muscular control are classified as involuntary. Because the gas made Peter lose control of his arms, his strike was not a voluntary act, and the `actus reus` element of the crime cannot be satisfied.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
(B) This choice is attractive because it correctly identifies that Peter did not intend the result and that the tremor was unexpected, but it misidentifies the legal reason for the lack of liability. "Act of God" and "foreseeability" are concepts primarily rooted in tort law or the proximate cause analysis of homicide. However, those issues are never reached because the lack of a voluntary act (the `actus reus` gate) precludes liability at the threshold.

(C) This choice is attractive because it correctly identifies Peter’s physical contact as the factual and proximate cause of the drowning. However, causation is only analyzed AFTER a voluntary act has been established. Since Peter’s movement was involuntary due to the neurotoxin, there is no conduct to which causation can attach for criminal purposes.

(D) This is the **Dominant Trap**. It appeals to the student's sense of moral responsibility by pointing to Peter’s prior voluntary decision to enter a dangerous area. While Peter’s decision to sail near the vents was voluntary, the `actus reus` of a crime must be the specific conduct that causes the prohibited result. The voluntary act of sailing to a location does not "transfer" its voluntariness to a subsequent, involuntary physical movement caused by an external force.

## 8. Black-Letter Verification
The `actus reus` requirement (Common Law & MPC § 2.01) mandates that criminal liability be predicated on a voluntary act. Reflexes, convulsions, and movements during unconsciousness or caused by external forces stripping the actor of control are not voluntary acts. Peter's loss of control due to the neurotoxin mirrors the "reflex/convulsion" exception. The tested rule (voluntary act requirement), legal issue (involuntary movement due to toxins), and credited outcome (no liability) are unchanged from original QID 16131.

## 9. Rigor/Difficulty Note
The variation preserves the original rigor by maintaining the "prior voluntary choice" trap (Choice D), which is the primary hurdle for students who confuse general negligence or assumption of risk with the specific `actus reus` requirement.

## 8. choice_by_choice_walkthrough

#### Choice A: No, because the neurotoxin made Peter's physical movement involuntary.
*   **C3 Signal:** This is the residual answer. It correctly identifies the threshold defect in the prosecution's case.

*   **Lawyer Confirmation:** Under Common Law and MPC 2.01, liability requires a voluntary act. Bodily movements that are not the product of the actor's conscious effort (including those caused by external chemical triggers) are involuntary.

#### Choice B: No, because the underwater tremor was an act of God that was unforeseeable.
*   **C3 Signal:** Tries to solve via Tort/Causation before clearing the Act gate.
*   **Student Label:** **FILTER**. This uses "Act of God" and "unforeseeability," which are civil negligence or proximate cause concepts. They don't address the primary criminal law issue of whether an act occurred.
*   **Responsiveness:** A true and responsive version of a "No" answer would focus on the lack of a voluntary act or lack of mens rea, not the "freak occurrence" of the trigger.
*   **Lawyer Confirmation:** Proximate cause is only analyzed if a voluntary act exists.

#### Choice C: Yes, because Peter's strike was the direct and proximate cause of John’s death.
*   **C3 Signal:** Proves causation, but ignores the missing voluntary act.
*   **Student Label:** **FILTER**. Factual and proximate cause are necessary for homicide liability, but they are not sufficient. You cannot have causation for a crime if there was no voluntary act to begin with.
*   **Responsiveness:** Proves a non-dispositive element (causation) while ignoring the dispositive failure (no voluntary act).
*   **Lawyer Confirmation:** Actus Reus is a threshold requirement that must be satisfied before causation is even considered.

#### Choice D: Yes, because Peter voluntarily chose to sail in an area known for dangerous toxic vents.
*   **C3 Signal:** Tries to transfer voluntariness from a prior, non-harmful act.
*   **Student Label:** **FILTER**. This is the **Dominant Trap**. It seeks to satisfy the "voluntary" requirement by looking at a different, earlier act (choosing to sail).
*   **Responsiveness:** The "voluntary act" must be the conduct that causes the harm (the strike), not just the decision to be in the vicinity of the danger.
*   **Lawyer Confirmation:** One cannot "transfer" the voluntary nature of a prior act (sailing) to a subsequent involuntary act (the strike) to establish criminal liability.
