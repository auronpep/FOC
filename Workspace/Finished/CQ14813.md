### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. Lydia is offering Barnabas's own statement against Barnabas in Lydia's lawsuit against him. That fits the opposing-party-statement rule. Gold Key: a party's own statement, when offered against that party, is not hearsay under FRE 801(d)(2)(A). No hearsay exception is needed.

Silver Key: in a two-yes/two-no Evidence array, do not stop at the admissibility result. Match the reason. "Yes, as a subsequent remedial measure" reaches the right admissibility result but uses the wrong doctrine. The cleaner reason is that Barnabas's own words are being used against him.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. Yes, as a subsequent remedial measure.** This choice is attractive because Barnabas talks about rewiring the panel after the damage appears, so the phrase sounds like a later fix. The breaker is that Lydia is offering a statement, not proof that Barnabas actually took a later safety measure. Silver Key: the right admissibility result still needs the right evidentiary path. The statement comes in as Barnabas's own opposing-party statement, not as a subsequent remedial measure.

**C. No, because it is hearsay not within any exception.** This is the dominant trap. The choice correctly notices that Lydia is repeating an out-of-court statement to help prove Barnabas's fault, so hearsay danger feels present. The breaker is the Gold Key: an opposing party's own statement, offered against that party, is classified as not hearsay under FRE 801(d)(2)(A). The statement does not need a hearsay exception.

**D. No, because the statement was an offer in compromise.** This choice is attractive because "I'll rewire it for free" sounds like someone trying to avoid trouble. The breaker is that Rule 408 is about compromising a claim disputed as to validity or amount. At the moment Lydia called, she had just discovered smoke and asked for emergency help. The stem gives no existing dispute, demand, denial, negotiation, or claim amount.

### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The credited answer is legally unchanged from the source item. The tested rule is the hearsay exclusion for an opposing party's own statement.

Authority floor: FRE 801(d)(2)(A) provides that a statement is not hearsay when it is offered against an opposing party and was made by that party in an individual or representative capacity. FRE 801(c) defines hearsay as an out-of-court statement offered for the truth of the matter asserted, but Rule 801(d)(2) excludes qualifying opposing-party statements from hearsay. FRE 408(a) excludes compromise offers and statements made during compromise negotiations only for a disputed claim as to validity or amount. FRE 407 excludes evidence of later remedial measures when offered to prove negligence, culpable conduct, defect, or need for warning, but Barnabas's words are not themselves a completed remedial measure.

Application: Barnabas is the defendant and opposing party. Lydia offers Barnabas's own response against him. The statement therefore clears the hearsay bar under FRE 801(d)(2)(A). Rule 408 does not bar it because the call occurred before the stem supplies a disputed claim or compromise negotiation. Rule 407 does not provide the reason for admission because the evidence is a statement promising a future repair, not proof that a subsequent repair was actually performed. The right answer remains admissible as evidence of Barnabas's fault.
