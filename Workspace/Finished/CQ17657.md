### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item: a criminal defendant in state court challenges prosecution by information on the theory that the Fifth Amendment grand jury indictment requirement applies to state felony prosecutions.

The Fifth Amendment provides a grand jury indictment requirement for federal capital or otherwise infamous crimes. Current doctrine does not incorporate that grand-jury requirement against the states. In `Hurtado v. California`, 110 U.S. 516 (1884), the Supreme Court held that Fourteenth Amendment due process does not require a state felony prosecution to proceed by grand jury indictment. The Library of Congress Constitution Annotated incorporation table likewise lists the Fifth Amendment Grand Jury Clause as not formally incorporated, with Hurtado as the rejection authority.
