### 8. Black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

The tested rule is unchanged from the original: nondeadly force in self-defense does not require retreat, even in a retreat jurisdiction. Restatement (Second) of Torts § 63 states that an actor may use reasonable force not intended or likely to cause death or serious bodily harm to defend against reasonably apprehended harmful or offensive contact, and that the privilege still exists even when the actor could avoid using force by retreating. ([LexisNexis][1]) The same Restatement commentary states the operative point directly: a person threatened with bodily harm or offensive contact may stand ground and repel the attack with reasonable force that does not threaten serious harm or death, even if safe retreat is available. ([LexisNexis][1]) Criminal-law materials frame the retreat doctrine as a limitation on deadly force: in retreat jurisdictions, safe retreat is required before deadly force, and MPC § 3.04 limits deadly force when the actor can retreat with complete safety. ([Social Sci LibreTexts][2])

The source official explanation is therefore preserved: pepper spray is nondeadly force; no retreat was required; A remains the credited answer.
