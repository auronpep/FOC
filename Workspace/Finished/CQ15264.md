### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**A is correct.** The motion should be denied because Lydia has evidence from which a jury could find a design defect and causation. The Roadster performed as designed, so the defect theory is design defect, not manufacturing defect. Lydia offered a specific alternative engine design of equal cost that would eliminate the stalling problem without impairing engine function.

**Gold Key:** In a design-defect products case, alternative-design evidence that removes the dangerous feature at no added functional or cost burden is the kind of fact that gets the plaintiff past a directed verdict.

**Silver Key:** Track the defect chain: **engine stalls → traffic collision → heart attack.** The responsive answer must stay on that chain.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B is incorrect.** It answers a neighboring products-liability question. Crashworthiness asks whether a product failed to protect the user during a crash or enhanced the injury after impact. Lydia’s claim is not that the Roadster performed poorly in the crash. Her claim is that the engine defect subjected her to an unnecessary crash.

**Silver Key:** When the stem gives a defect chain, do not switch defect theories. “Crashworthy” points to a different product-defect story.

**C is incorrect.** A rear-end collision after a car unexpectedly stalls in city traffic is a foreseeable intervening event, not a superseding cause as a matter that would justify taking the case away from the jury.

**Gold Key:** Foreseeable intervening negligence does not cut off the defendant’s liability.

**D is incorrect.** The motion should not be granted merely because a person of normal sensitivity might not have suffered a heart attack. Once the jury could find that the defect caused the crash and resulting physical harm, unusual severity does not defeat the claim.

**Gold Key:** A tort defendant generally takes the plaintiff as found; the “normal sensitivity” shortcut is not the products-liability causation answer here.

### 8. Black-letter verification and lawyer-confirmation layer

The tested rule is unchanged from the original: a design defect claim can survive a directed-verdict motion when the plaintiff presents evidence of a feasible, equal-cost alternative design that would avoid the danger without impairing product function. The American Law Institute describes the Restatement Third products-liability project as covering liability of commercial product sellers for harm caused by product defects and distinguishing manufacturing, design, and warning defects. ([American Law Institute][1]) Restatement (Third) of Torts: Products Liability § 2(b) is commonly stated as a design defect rule requiring that foreseeable risks could have been reduced or avoided by a reasonable alternative design and that omission of that design made the product not reasonably safe. ([FindLaw][2])

B remains wrong because crashworthiness/enhanced-injury doctrine concerns whether the product failed to reasonably protect against injury in a foreseeable collision, not whether the product defect caused the collision in the first place. FindLaw’s discussion of Larsen and crashworthiness frames that doctrine as a duty to make vehicles reasonably crashworthy for foreseeable collisions. ([FindLaw][3]) C remains wrong because an intervening cause cuts off liability only if it is unforeseeable and independent; a second collision after the initial risk leaves a victim exposed can be foreseeable. ([Justia][4]) D remains wrong because the eggshell-plaintiff rule holds defendants liable for the full extent of injury even where the plaintiff’s condition makes the injury greater than expected. ([Justia][4]) Restatement (Second) of Torts § 461 states the same increased-harm-by-physical-condition principle. ([Berman & Simmons][5])
