### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Ruth fired a pistol at Lydia's chest. At that moment, Lydia could reasonably believe she faced imminent deadly force and that shooting back was necessary to protect herself. The fact that Ruth's bullet only grazed Lydia does not change the character of the threat. Gold Key: self-defense asks what the defendant reasonably perceived when she used force, not how bad the first wound turned out to be. Silver Key: lock the call to Lydia's privilege, then test the moment Lydia fired.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because it treats the actual wound as the measure of danger. The breaker is the Gold Key: a gunshot aimed at the chest is deadly force even if the bullet happens to cause only a minor wound.

C is attractive because carrying a pistol sounds like planned violence. The breaker is that self-defense can justify an intentional shooting when the defendant reasonably responds to imminent deadly force. Premeditation evidence does not erase a valid self-defense privilege.

D is attractive because Ruth had warning facts and may have reasonably feared Lydia. The breaker is the Silver Key: that answers Ruth's possible privilege, not Lydia's. Lydia had not used force in this encounter before Ruth fired, and once Ruth fired a pistol at Lydia's chest, Lydia had a self-defense claim of her own.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. The source row's listed outline label points to duress, but the actual issue is self-defense against apparent deadly force. The verified outline code used here is 72060201, "Defenses > Justification Defenses > Self-defense," which appears verbatim in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md`.

The governing rule is self-defense. A person may use force when she reasonably believes that force is immediately necessary to protect herself from unlawful force. Deadly force is justified when the defender reasonably believes deadly force is necessary to prevent death or serious bodily harm. Model Penal Code section 3.04 states the self-protection rule and limits deadly force to threats such as death or serious bodily harm. The modern exam rule is consistent with standard self-defense doctrine: the threat must be imminent, the fear reasonable, and the force proportional to the threat.

Authority layer: Model Penal Code section 3.04, https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/3_04-3_07.htm; Justia, "Self-Defense in Criminal Law Cases," last reviewed October 2025, https://www.justia.com/criminal/defenses/self-defense/.

Lydia was not the initial physical aggressor in this encounter. Her earlier threat and her possession of a pistol may make Ruth's fear understandable, but those facts do not answer whether Lydia could respond after Ruth fired first. Ruth used deadly force by shooting at Lydia's chest. Lydia's return shot was aimed at stopping that deadly threat. The official key's not-guilty result is therefore legally supported.
