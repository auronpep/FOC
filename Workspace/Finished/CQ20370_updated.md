### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The source row identifies BARMATRIX Q# 20370, the Contract Law stem, the four original answer choices, original correct answer A, and an official explanation relying on UCC §§ 2-606, 2-602, and 2-607. 

The legal rule is unchanged. UCC § 2-602 requires rejection within a reasonable time after delivery or tender and makes rejection ineffective unless the buyer seasonably notifies the seller. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) UCC § 2-606 treats a buyer’s failure to make an effective rejection, after a reasonable opportunity to inspect, as acceptance. ([Legal Information Institute][2]) UCC § 2-607 provides that the buyer must pay at the contract rate for accepted goods, but it also requires the buyer to notify the seller of breach within a reasonable time after discovery or be barred from any remedy. ([Legal Information Institute][3]) UCC § 2-709 allows a seller to recover the price of goods accepted. ([Legal Information Institute][4])

Applied here, Lydia had a reasonable opportunity to inspect because Timothy opened the cartons and reported the missing decals on delivery day. Lydia did not reject or notify Paul within a reasonable time. She accepted the mugs and lost the defect remedy. Paul therefore wins the price action. The tested rule, issue, credited outcome, and distractor mechanics remain unchanged.
