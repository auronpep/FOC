## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**Gold Key:** The best evidence rule (FRE 1002) only applies when a party seeks to prove the *content* of a writing, recording, or photograph. A witness who testifies from personal recollection about a fact — even if a writing also records that fact — is not proving the content of the writing; she is testifying from her own knowledge. The rule does not bar her testimony.

**Silver Key:** When a question offers two separate pieces of evidence, evaluate each one independently against the call before combining. Don't let the weakness of one infect the other.

Both items are admissible for independent reasons. Lydia's testimony about the purchase price is based on her own recollection, not on the content of any writing. Under FRE 1002, the best evidence rule applies only when proving the content of a writing — it does not bar a witness's independent memory testimony. The photocopy is admissible under FRE 1003 as a duplicate. Lydia personally made and authenticated it, no genuine question about authenticity has been raised, and there is no showing of unfairness. The fact that the original is in another state does not make the copy inadmissible; duplicates are admissible to the same extent as originals under FRE 1003. Both are admissible.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. The photocopy is admissible, but the testimony is not.**
This choice is attractive because it correctly identifies that the copy is admissible but then wrongly assumes the best evidence rule bars Lydia's personal-recollection testimony. The trap is thinking that because a written record exists, the witness must use it rather than her memory. But FRE 1002 only requires the original when proving the *content of the writing itself*. Lydia is testifying from what she remembers, not reading from or relying on the file. Her testimony stands on its own. **Gold Key:** The best evidence rule does not bar independent recollection testimony just because a writing also exists.

**B. The testimony is admissible, but the photocopy is not.**
This choice correctly admits the testimony but wrongly excludes the copy. The trap is thinking that because the original is in another state, the copy can't come in. But under FRE 1003, a duplicate is admissible to the same extent as the original unless a genuine authenticity issue or unfairness is shown. The witness authenticated the copy herself, no challenge to authenticity was raised, and the original's out-of-state location is not a valid objection. **Silver Key:** Evaluate each piece of evidence independently — the copy's admissibility doesn't depend on the original being in the courtroom.

**D. Neither the testimony nor the photocopy is admissible.**
This is the dominant trap. It overapplies the best evidence rule to bar everything — both the testimony and the copy. The attraction is the student who memorized "best evidence rule = original required" and applies it as an absolute bar to all secondary proof. But the rule has two independent escape hatches here: (1) recollection testimony isn't proving the content of a writing, and (2) a photocopy is a duplicate admissible under FRE 1003. **Gold Key:** The best evidence rule is not an absolute bar — it has specific scope (proving content of writings) and specific exceptions (duplicates under FRE 1003).

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule:** FRE 1002 (Requirement of the Original) and FRE 1003 (Admissibility of Duplicates).

**FRE 1002:** "An original writing, recording, or photograph is required in order to prove its content unless these rules or a federal statute provides otherwise." The rule applies only when the proponent seeks to prove the *content* of a writing. A witness testifying from personal knowledge about a fact is not proving the content of a writing — she is testifying from memory. *See* Fed. R. Evid. 1002; Matthew Miner Evidence Outline, Best Evidence Rule.

**FRE 1003:** "A duplicate is admissible to the same extent as the original unless a genuine question is raised about the original's authenticity or the circumstances make it unfair to admit the duplicate." A photocopy is a duplicate under FRE 1001(4). The witness who made and authenticated the copy eliminates the authenticity concern. No unfairness has been shown. *See* Fed. R. Evid. 1003; *Myrick v. United States*, 332 F.2d 279 (5th Cir. 1964) (photostatic copies admissible where no suggestion of inaccuracy).

**Authority:** FRE 1002, FRE 1003 (Federal Rules of Evidence, Article X); LII, Cornell Law School, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_1003.
