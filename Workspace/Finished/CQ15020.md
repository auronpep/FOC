Martha chairs the board of the Bethel Grove Homeowners' Association, which manages a small residential community and is authorized to adopt rules governing the construction and design of homes. The board is voting on a proposed rule that would cap the height of any newly constructed home in the community at 25 feet. ## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**B is correct.** A private community rule and a public zoning ordinance are two parallel systems that coexist; where they overlap, the **more restrictive** one controls. The ordinance lets homes rise to 35 feet — that is a public ceiling, not a command to build that high. The association's 25-foot cap is stricter, and a home no taller than 25 feet also satisfies the 35-foot ordinance, so the private rule adds restriction without violating the ordinance and is enforceable. **Gold Key:** a private rule may demand *more* than zoning requires but never *less* than it allows; a stricter covenant is enforceable because it does not violate the ordinance — not because it "overrides" it. **Silver Key:** two choices say No and two say Yes, and the two "Yes" choices reach the same result for opposite reasons — pick the "Yes" justified by "doesn't violate the ordinance," not the one claiming a private rule "overrides" zoning.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**C — "Yes, because a private rule overrides a zoning ordinance."** It lands on the right outcome (enforceable), which makes the stated reason feel close enough to wave through. The breaker is direction: a private rule never *overrides* zoning — it may layer additional restriction on top of the ordinance, but it can never permit what the ordinance forbids, so the rule here is enforceable because it is stricter, not supreme. **Gold Key:** the more restrictive of the two controls; private rules add to, they do not trump, the zoning floor.

**D — "No, because the zoning ordinance overrides all private rules."** It taps the instinct that public law always beats private arrangements, and "overrides all" sounds like a tidy supremacy rule. The breaker is the word *all*: zoning sets a floor that private rules may exceed, so a stricter private rule coexists with — rather than yields to — the ordinance.

**A — "No, because zoning laws regulate all property use."** It points to a true-sounding generality about zoning's reach and treats it as the end of the inquiry. The breaker is that the scope of zoning does not answer the question asked: even granting zoning regulates property, that says nothing about whether a *stricter* private rule is enforceable — which is the actual issue.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

**Tested rule (unchanged from the original):** a private restrictive covenant / community-association rule and a public zoning ordinance operate independently and coexist; where they overlap, the **more restrictive** provision controls. A private rule may be **more** restrictive than the ordinance (and is then enforceable because it does not violate the ordinance), but it may **not** be **less** restrictive than the public floor.

- The 35-foot ordinance is a maximum the municipality permits; it does not compel building to that height.
- The association's 25-foot cap is stricter and fully consistent with the ordinance (a ≤25-foot home is necessarily ≤35 feet), so it adds restriction without violating the public rule and is enforceable.
- **Why C fails:** the rule is enforceable because it is more restrictive, not because a private rule "overrides" zoning — private rules never permit what zoning forbids.
- **Why D fails:** zoning is a floor, not an absolute bar on stricter private rules.
- **Why A fails:** zoning's regulatory scope is not the issue; the issue is the enforceability of a stricter private rule.

Credited outcome **B = original C ("Yes, because the rule does not violate the zoning ordinance")** is correct and **unchanged** from the source item. Tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are identical; only the surface (regulated dimension and numbers) moved. `agrees_with_official_key: true`.

**Authority (lawyer-confirmation layer only):** the "more restrictive provision controls; covenant and zoning coexist" rule is uniform state common law; supported by Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes (2000) (validity and enforcement of servitudes). No single controlling federal case or rule number governs (the doctrine is state common law, applied uniformly), so this authority line is flagged `attorney_review: true` for a jurisdiction-pinned primary cite rather than reported as a clean primary-authority PASS.
