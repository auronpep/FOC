### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

D is correct. Esther did not have to aim at death. She used a sharp tool to cut deeply into Ruth's leg and said she meant to hobble her. That is an intent to cause serious bodily injury. Ruth died from that same serious wound, so murder is supported.

Gold Key: Common-law malice includes intent to cause grievous or serious bodily injury, not just intent to kill.

Silver Key: Do not let the call collapse into "did she mean death?" The better axis is whether she meant a serious disabling injury and death came from that injury.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because a lower leg does not sound like the heart, head, or chest. The breaker is that the rule does not require a strike to a traditionally vital organ. A deep disabling wound that severs a major artery can show serious-injury intent.

B is attractive because Esther said she did not mean to kill. The breaker is the Gold Key: murder can rest on intent to cause serious bodily injury when death results. Her "hobble her" statement helps prove the serious-injury intent rather than erase malice.

C is attractive because the exact artery cut was accidental. The breaker is that the precise fatal mechanism need not be intended. If the intended serious wound causes death, the death can support murder rather than only involuntary manslaughter.

### 8. Full Black-Letter Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule is common-law murder through grievous-bodily-harm malice. Malice aforethought is not limited to purpose to kill; it can include intent to inflict serious bodily injury when death results. The federal murder statute states the traditional malice-aforethought frame. See 18 U.S.C. section 1111, https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title18%2Fpart1%2Fchapter51. A modern statutory formulation makes the serious-bodily-injury route explicit: Texas Penal Code section 19.02(b)(2) covers a person who intends serious bodily injury and commits an act clearly dangerous to human life that causes death. See https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?artSec=19.02&chapter=PE.19&code=PE&tab=1.

The legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged: the defendant intentionally inflicted a disabling knife-like wound, the wound caused death, and murder is supported even without a purpose to kill.
