### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct. Gold Key:** in a UCC merchant-confirmation problem, the receiving merchant must give a **written** notice of objection within 10 days after receiving the confirmation. A voicemail objection is not the required objection.

**Silver Key:** do not let the “within 10 days” fact swallow the “written notice” requirement. Timing and form are separate boxes.

Here, Timothy received a signed written confirmation from Martha and objected only by voicemail. Because Timothy sent no written objection, his oral objection does not stop the merchant-confirmation rule from applying.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells the familiar signature instinct: the party resisting enforcement usually must have signed the Statute of Frauds writing. The breaker is the merchant-confirmation unlock: between merchants, the sender’s sufficient signed confirmation can work against the recipient without the recipient’s signature if the recipient fails to send a written objection in time.

**B.** This choice sounds careful because it demands proof that Timothy actually read the confirmation. The breaker is the “every word” over-demand: the merchant-confirmation rule asks whether the recipient had reason to know the contents, not whether the sender proves actual word-by-word reading.

**D.** This is the dominant trap. It correctly notices the 10-day window, then overreads it into “any objection.” **Gold Key:** the objection must be written; **Silver Key:** separate the timing box from the form box.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The source row identifies Q#20805 as a Contracts / Statute of Frauds item, supplies the original merchant-confirmation fact pattern, lists the official key as B, and states that UCC §2-201(2) requires written notice of objection within 10 days; oral objection is insufficient. 

UCC §2-201(2) provides that, between merchants, a writing in confirmation of the contract that is sufficient against the sender and received by a party with reason to know its contents satisfies the Statute of Frauds against the recipient unless **written notice of objection** is given within 10 days after receipt. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) Massachusetts’s codified version states the same rule: between merchants, a confirming writing satisfies the requirement unless written objection is given within ten days. ([Massachusetts Legislature][2])

The legal issue, tested rule, and credited outcome are unchanged. The Christian variation still tests whether an oral objection by a merchant-recipient defeats a signed written merchant confirmation. It does not.

### 10. Why the variation preserves rigor/difficulty

The variation preserves the same hard anchor: UCC merchant confirmation requires **written** objection. The dominant trap remains strong because the recipient objects within the 10-day period, tempting students to treat timing alone as enough.
