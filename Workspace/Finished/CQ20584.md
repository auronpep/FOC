### 6. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

C is correct. Merger does not occur just because Hannah physically possessed the burdened parcel for a lease term. Gold Key: an appurtenant easement ends by merger only when the benefit and burden come into the same ownership in the same quality of title. Hannah owned Selah Orchard in fee simple, but she held only a six-month leasehold in Lantern Lot. Lydia still held fee simple title to Lantern Lot. Silver Key: line up the estates before choosing; fee simple on one side plus leasehold on the other side is not fee-plus-fee unity. Because the dominant and servient interests never united in Hannah in the same title quality, the easement did not terminate.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The black-letter rule is that an appurtenant easement may terminate by merger when the dominant and servient interests come into a single ownership in the same person and in a matching title quality. Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes section 7.5 states the merger principle for servitudes; the American Law Institute identifies Chapter 7 of that Restatement as covering modification and termination of servitudes. Source: https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-third/property-servitudes. The Supreme Court of the Virgin Islands quoted section 7.5 as providing that a servitude terminates when all benefits and burdens come into single ownership, and it treated that Restatement rule as the sound rule of decision. Source: https://cdnsm5-hosted.civiclive.com/UserFiles/Servers/Server_12810860/File/Opinions/Published/2019/File24.pdf. California's official Civil Code similarly provides that a servitude cannot be held by the owner of the servient tenement, and that a servitude is extinguished when the right to the servitude and the right to the servient tenement vest in the same person. Sources: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=805. and https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=811.
