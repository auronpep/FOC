### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Lydia did not fire merely because someone was inside the store. She fired after an unidentified person in the dark threatened to shoot her. Self-defense turns on what reasonably appeared necessary at the moment. If Lydia reasonably believed she faced an imminent threat of deadly force, deadly defensive force could be justified. The later-discovered fact that the person was a police officer does not defeat the defense when Lydia had no reason to know that fact before she fired.

**Gold Key:** Self-defense uses reasonable appearances at the moment of force. A defendant who reasonably but mistakenly believes she faces imminent deadly force can be justified, and a police-officer limitation matters only when the defendant knows the person is an officer.

**Silver Key:** Separate the reason for being armed from the reason for firing. Prior thefts explain why Lydia was in the store with a pistol; the shouted death threat is the fact that routes the answer to personal self-defense.

#### Choice D

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested legal issue is unchanged: whether a defendant charged with attempted murder may rely on self-defense after firing at a person who appeared to be threatening imminent deadly force, even though the person was actually a police officer acting in an official capacity. The credited outcome is unchanged: not guilty, because the defendant reasonably believed her life was in danger.

The lawyer-confirmation authority floor is Model Penal Code section 3.04. Section 3.04(1) frames self-protection around force the actor believes immediately necessary to protect against unlawful force. Section 3.04(2)(b) limits deadly force to situations where the actor believes it is necessary to protect against death, serious bodily harm, kidnapping, or forced sexual intercourse. Section 3.04(2)(a)(i) separately limits force used to resist an arrest that the actor knows is being made by a peace officer. Public text reproduction: Criminal Law Web, Model Penal Code sections 3.04-3.07, https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/mpc/PART1/snippets/3_04-3_07.htm. H2O Open Casebook also identifies the source as Model Penal Code section 3.04, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/6141-tanaka-criminal-law-casebook/resources/12.2.1-model-penal-code-mpc-304-use-of-force-in-self-protection/.

Those principles support the supplied key. Lydia was not using deadly force solely to protect inventory. She heard a person moving in the store, picked up a pistol, and then heard an immediate threat to shoot. A reasonable person in that position could believe deadly force was necessary for self-protection. The police-officer fact does not change the analysis because the known-officer limitation depends on the actor's knowledge; the variant, like the source, gives Lydia no knowledge that the person threatening to shoot was an officer.

## review_truth
