Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966), requires warnings before custodial interrogation. The custody inquiry asks whether there was a formal arrest or restraint on freedom of movement to the degree associated with formal arrest. Oregon v. Mathiason, 429 U.S. 492 (1977), held that a suspect who voluntarily came to the station, was told he was not under arrest, and left after the interview was not in Miranda custody. California v. Beheler, 463 U.S. 1121 (1983), likewise held that Miranda warnings were not required when a suspect voluntarily accompanied police to the station, was told he was not under arrest, and left after a short interview. The Library of Congress Constitution Annotated summary also treats voluntary station questioning without arrest-level restraint as noncustodial. Authorities checked: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/384/436/, https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/429/492/, https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/463/1121/, and https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt5-4-7-4/ALDE_00013689/.

That authority confirms the source key. The legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged: the admission is not suppressed because Mary was not in custody, even though detectives questioned her and received an incriminating answer.
