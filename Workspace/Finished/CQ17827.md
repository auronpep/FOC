### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**B is correct.** Ruth faced an immediate deadly threat, and that fact would matter in an ordinary duress question. But the call is murder under the common law. Common law does not excuse an intentional killing of an innocent person through duress. Ruth deliberately cut the rope so that the lighting bar would fall on Barnabas. That intentional homicide keeps the duress claim from defeating murder.

**Gold Key:** At common law, duress can excuse many crimes, but it does not excuse intentional homicide. A threat of immediate death is not enough when the defendant intentionally kills an innocent person.

**Silver Key:** Lock the call to "common law" and "murder" before reacting to the sympathetic threat facts. The strongest emotional fact in the stem is bait if the homicide exception controls the result.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A** sells a compromise: the threat feels too strong to ignore, so the answer offers manslaughter instead of acquittal. The breaker is that common law does not automatically turn a duress killing into manslaughter. **Gold Key:** the same common-law homicide limit that blocks complete excuse also blocks automatic mitigation absent a statute or separate manslaughter doctrine.

**C** sounds technical because it tries to sort duress by the identity of the coercer. The breaker is that duress normally involves private human threats. The problem is not that the robbers were private persons; the problem is that Ruth intentionally caused an innocent person's death.

**D** is the dominant trap because Ruth faced exactly the kind of immediate death threat that usually powers duress. The breaker is the homicide exception. **Gold Key:** immediate deadly pressure does not make duress a common-law defense to intentionally killing an innocent person.

### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The tested rule, legal issue, trap structure, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. The source asks whether common-law duress excuses an intentional homicide. It does not.

Legal authority supports the bank key. In *United States v. LaFleur*, 971 F.2d 200, 205-06 (9th Cir. 1992), the Ninth Circuit rejected duress as a defense to murder and rejected using duress to reduce murder to manslaughter in a federal murder prosecution. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/971/200/71659/. In *People v. Anderson*, 28 Cal.4th 767 (2002), the California Supreme Court treated the same position as the traditional common-law rule: duress does not excuse murder and does not reduce murder to manslaughter absent legislative change. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/28/767.html. These authorities are consistent with the source explanation and with the MBE-style common-law framing.

Ruth intentionally performed the act that killed Barnabas. The coercion came from private robbers and was immediate, but those facts do not change the common-law homicide limitation. The correct legal result is that duress fails because it is not a common-law defense to intentional homicide. No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is triggered.
