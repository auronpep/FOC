### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are preserved. The source row tests whether a witness in a civil proceeding may invoke the privilege against self-incrimination when a cross-examination answer could expose the witness to criminal liability; the supplied official answer is that the claim should be upheld. 

The Fifth Amendment states that no person shall be compelled in a criminal case to be a witness against herself. ([Congress.gov][1]) The privilege applies in civil proceedings as well; *McCarthy v. Arndstein* states that the constitutional privilege against self-incrimination applies to civil proceedings. ([Justia Law][2]) *Hoffman v. United States* confirms that the privilege covers answers that would themselves support a conviction and answers that would furnish a link in the chain of evidence, so long as the witness has reasonable cause to apprehend danger from a direct answer. ([Legal Information Institute][3])

The impeachment concern in the dominant trap is real but not controlling. FRE 611(b) permits cross-examination into matters affecting credibility. ([Legal Information Institute][4]) FRE 608(b) also recognizes inquiry into some specific instances on cross-examination and states that testifying on another matter does not waive the privilege against self-incrimination for testimony relating only to credibility. ([Legal Information Institute][5]) Those authorities support the same outcome as the source key: the court should uphold Mary’s privilege claim.
