### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** Martha has a life estate, but the parcels produced no rents, profits, or other income for her. A life tenant normally bears ordinary current charges connected to the property, including real estate taxes, but the high-yield exam exception is that the life tenant is not personally chargeable for taxes on land that produces no income when the creating instrument and applicable law do not shift that burden to the life tenant. Ruth paid the taxes to protect the fee, but that does not give her a reimbursement claim against Martha on these facts.

**Gold Key:** A life tenant's tax duty is not an unlimited personal guarantee. For non-income-producing land, the no-income exception controls.

**Silver Key:** When the choices split between "recover" and "nothing," do not stop at the life-tenant label. Match the because-clause to the income fact.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A:** This choice sells a precise-sounding money calculation after Ruth has paid real money to protect the fee. The breaker is that there is no reimbursement duty to measure. The parcels produced no income for Martha, so the answer does not become a smaller present-value recovery.

**B:** This choice is attractive because it lands on the correct zero-recovery outcome and talks about Martha's present possessory estate. The breaker is the reason. **D:** This is the dominant trap. It states the ordinary life-tenant rule in a confident way, which is why it is so attractive. ### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged. The original and transformed questions both ask whether a remainderman who paid delinquent real estate taxes can recover reimbursement from a life tenant when the land subject to the life estate is vacant or otherwise unproductive and produces no income.

Black-letter rule: absent a contrary provision in the creating instrument or an applicable statute, a life tenant generally bears ordinary current charges such as real estate taxes on property enjoyed during the life estate. But where the land is unproductive and yields no income to the life tenant, the life tenant is not personally chargeable for those taxes as against the remainderman. If the remainderman pays to protect the fee simple interest, the remainderman has no reimbursement action against the life tenant on the no-income facts.

Authority checked:

- **Koszela v. Wilcox, 538 A.2d 150, 151 (R.I. 1988)** holds that when land is not productive of income during the life tenancy, the life tenant is not bound to pay ordinary taxes or special assessments; if the remainderman pays taxes to protect the interest, there is no reimbursement action against the life tenant when the property is unproductive. Source: https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148bdeadd7b049345290e6
- **Sheffield v. Cooke, 39 R.I. 217, 244, 98 A. 161, 171 (1916)** states the exception that taxes assessed against unproductive real estate are not chargeable to the life tenant. Source: https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/sheffield-v-cooke-no-894822478
- **Apostolico v. Pagliaro, No. 2024-209-Appeal (R.I. Feb. 20, 2026)** reaffirms Koszela and treats income production, not mere residence or possession, as the controlling fact for the tax burden between life tenant and remainderman. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/rhode-island/supreme-court/2026/24-209.html

No researched authority contradicted the supplied official key. The lawyer-confirmation verdict is PASS.
