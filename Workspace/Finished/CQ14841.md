## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**FRE 615 — Excluding Witnesses:**
"At a party's request, the court **must** order witnesses excluded from the courtroom so that they cannot hear other witnesses' testimony." (Emphasis on "must.") The Advisory Committee Notes confirm: "The rule takes the latter position" — meaning exclusion is a right, not a matter of discretion. *See* Fed. R. Evid. 615(a); LII, Cornell Law School, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_615.

**FRE 611(b) — Scope of Cross-Examination:**
"Cross-examination should not go beyond the subject matter of the direct examination and matters affecting the witness's credibility. The court may, in the exercise of discretion, permit inquiry into additional matters as if on direct examination." Credibility is always a proper subject of cross. The judge also has discretion to expand scope beyond direct. *See* Fed. R. Evid. 611(b).

**FRE 611(c) — Leading Questions:**
Leading questions "should not be used on direct examination" but "ordinarily should be permitted on cross-examination." The word "ordinarily" preserves judicial discretion — the judge may refuse leading questions even on cross. *See* Fed. R. Evid. 611(c).
