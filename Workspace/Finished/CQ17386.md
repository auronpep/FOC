# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

In a jurisdiction that requires retreat before using deadly force, Daniel was standing beside a display table at a private Christian book fair when Peter slapped Daniel on the shoulder and drew back his hand to slap him again. A clear doorway to the lobby was open next to Daniel. Daniel did not leave; he shoved Peter backward with both hands. Peter fell into an empty folding chair but was not seriously injured. Daniel is charged with battery. What is Daniel's best defense?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A person uses a shove to stop an imminent slap even though a safe exit is available. Does a deadly-force retreat rule defeat self-defense?

## 3. Final answer choices

A. No defense, because Daniel had a safe doorway through which he could retreat.  
B. Self-defense only if Peter intended to cause death or serious bodily harm.  
C. Self-defense, because Daniel used reasonable nondeadly force to stop an imminent nondeadly attack.  
D. No defense, because an open-handed slap is never enough to permit self-defense.

## 6. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Daniel used a shove, not deadly force. Peter had already slapped him and was drawing back to slap again, so Daniel faced an imminent unlawful nondeadly attack. **Gold Key:** a duty to retreat is a deadly-force limiter; reasonable nondeadly force may be used to stop an imminent nondeadly attack without first retreating. **Silver Key:** classify the defendant's force before using the safe-exit fact.

## 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This is the dominant trap. The safe doorway matters only if the retreat rule applies. **Gold Key:** the retreat rule in the stem is keyed to deadly force, and Daniel used nondeadly force.

**B.** This imports the serious-bodily-harm threshold from deadly-force self-defense. Daniel did not need to face death or serious bodily harm before using reasonable nondeadly force.

**D.** This is an overclaim. The word **never** is the tell. A slap is not automatically enough for any response, but it can justify proportionate nondeadly defensive force.

## 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The source row is Q17386, a Criminal Law / General Principles / Defenses item keyed to self-defense. The source row supplied no measured pick rates. The verified outline code is **72060201**, “Defenses > Justification Defenses > Self-defense.”

The tested rule is stable. Representative statutory wording separates ordinary defensive force from deadly force. Nebraska Revised Statute § 28-1409(1) justifies force when the actor believes it is immediately necessary to protect against unlawful force; § 28-1409(4) places the complete-safety retreat limitation in the deadly-force subsection; and § 28-1409(5) states that, except as required by the property and deadly-force subsections, a person using protective force may estimate necessity without retreating. Source: https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=s2814009000. Cornell Wex likewise summarizes duty to retreat as usually applying to deadly force. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/duty_to_retreat.
