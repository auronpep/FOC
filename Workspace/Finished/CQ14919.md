### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Contracts for Land, Marketable Title, and the verified outline code is `85030300`. In a land-sale contract, marketable title is generally implied unless the parties clearly agree otherwise. Marketable title is title free from reasonable doubt and from substantial encumbrances that would expose the buyer to litigation or a third party's enforceable interest. A private restrictive covenant is such an encumbrance because a third party may enforce it against the land. By contrast, ordinary zoning or land-use regulations do not, merely by existing or blocking the buyer's intended use, create a cloud on title. The classic authority is `Lohmeyer v. Bower`, 170 Kan. 442, 227 P.2d 102 (1951), which distinguishes municipal land-use restrictions from private restrictive covenants and states that private restrictions constitute encumbrances rendering title unmerchantable; it also explains that the mere existence of municipal restrictions is not the same title burden. Research source: https://law.justia.com/cases/kansas/supreme-court/1951/38-110-0.html. The supplied key is confirmed: the buyer need not purchase because the recorded restrictive covenant renders title unmarketable.
