Stephen runs a small farm-tools workshop and holds a federal patent on an improved threshing sledge. He sued AgriCorp, a large farm-equipment manufacturer, in federal court for patent infringement, seeking money damages for lost profits and the harm to his workshop, and he timely demanded a jury trial. ## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**C is correct.** The appeal turns on the only jury-selection complaint that is both a real legal wrong and one that tainted this verdict. AgriCorp's lawyer used peremptory challenges to strike three jurors solely because they were Black, and Stephen's lawyer objected on the record. **Gold Key:** race-based peremptory challenges are unconstitutional even in a civil case between two private parties — a private litigant exercises the strike through the court's machinery, so striking jurors on account of race violates the Equal Protection Clause. That makes the discriminatory strikes a structural ground for reversal. **Silver Key:** on a "which argument wins the appeal" item, keep the error that actually changed the composition of the deciding jury and cut the complaints that are either false law or harmless.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A — alternate jurors (dominant trap).** The stem all but hands you a conceded error: appointing alternate jurors in a federal civil case is contrary to the modern rule, and a true statement of error feels like a guaranteed reversal ground. The breaker is prejudice — a procedural error reverses only if it affected the outcome, and here the three alternates were never seated and never deliberated, so the error was harmless. **Silver Key:** error ≠ reversal; when the stem neutralizes an error's effect, cut it.

**B — Seventh Amendment.** A federal patent suit feels specialized enough that a student doubts whether the ordinary civil-jury right reaches it, so "the Seventh Amendment doesn't apply" sounds like a sophisticated scope point. The breaker is the nature of the claim: a suit for money damages is a legal action, and the Seventh Amendment jury-trial right squarely attaches — the court did not err by empaneling a jury.

**D — Brazilian jurors' English.** Jurors deliberating in a second language triggers a fairness instinct that a non-fluent juror might have tainted the verdict. The breaker is that non-native English is not itself a disqualification, the stem says their English was adequate, and any competency objection was waived when counsel did not move to strike them for cause during voir dire.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

**Tested rule (unchanged from original):** A private litigant in a civil action may not use peremptory challenges to exclude prospective jurors on account of race; doing so violates the Equal Protection Clause and is reversible error. *Lawyer confirmation:* Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614 (1991) (extending Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 (1986), to civil cases between private parties; the peremptory strike is state action because it invokes the court's authority); see also J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127 (1994) (gender).

- **A (alternates):** Under the modern federal rule, civil juries have no alternates; all impaneled jurors who are not excused participate in the verdict. Fed. R. Civ. P. 48; the 1991 amendment to Rule 47 abolished alternate jurors in civil cases. Seating "alternates" was therefore error, but a verdict is not reversed for error that did not affect substantial rights. Fed. R. Civ. P. 61. The alternates never deliberated, so no prejudice. *Correct outcome: harmless, not a reversal ground.*
- **B (Seventh Amendment):** The Seventh Amendment preserves the jury-trial right in "Suits at common law," i.e., actions seeking legal relief such as money damages. U.S. Const. amend. VII; Curtis v. Loether, 415 U.S. 189 (1974); Dairy Queen, Inc. v. Wood, 369 U.S. 469 (1962). A patent-infringement claim for damages is legal, so the jury right applies and the saying-it-doesn't is a flat misstatement. (Three peremptory challenges per side in civil cases: 28 U.S.C. § 1870.)
- **D (English):** No authority disqualifies a juror merely for non-native English; for-cause competency challenges are made during voir dire and are waived if not raised. The stem states their English was adequate.

**Credited outcome confirmed:** the tested rule, the legal issue (race-based peremptory strikes in a private civil case), and the credited result (reversal on that ground) are identical to the original. No doctrinal-currency conflict; Edmonson remains controlling.
