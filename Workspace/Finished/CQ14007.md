## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

Lydia ran a small Scripture-art booth from a leased stall at a town winter market. She sued Officer Stephen in federal court, alleging that Stephen violated her Fourth Amendment rights when he stepped behind a locked canvas divider into her stall's private stockroom, searched several crates of framed prints, and seized a box of engraved offering plates.

After trial, the verdict was split. Lydia prevailed on the unlawful-search and wrongful-seizure claims. Stephen prevailed on the illegal-entry claim. Judgment was entered, and both sides timely appealed.

At trial, Lydia never moved for judgment as a matter of law on the illegal-entry claim. She did, however, timely object to the admission of a dispatch log and to a jury instruction relevant to that claim.

Is the court of appeals likely to consider Lydia's arguments about the admission of evidence and the jury instruction?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

After final judgment, may an appellant who skipped JMOL on one claim still obtain appellate review of timely objected-to evidence and jury-instruction errors on that claim?

### 3. Final Answer Choices

C. No, because the court of appeals lacks jurisdiction to review those alleged trial errors.

D. No, because any error in the evidence ruling or instruction became harmless when Lydia failed to make a motion for judgment as a matter of law.

### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. Lydia did not preserve a sufficiency-of-the-evidence/JMOL argument on the illegal-entry claim, but that waiver does not erase separate trial-error arguments she did preserve by timely objection. The evidentiary ruling and jury instruction are reviewable after final judgment, and Lydia may try to show that the errors affected substantial rights rather than being harmless.

Gold Key: A missed Rule 50/JMOL motion blocks the sufficiency/JMOL lane; it does not automatically block preserved evidence or instruction objections.

Gold Key: JMOL waiver and preserved trial-error review are different lanes.

### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The transformed item preserves the original tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome. A party who fails to make the required Rule 50 motion may lose the appellate sufficiency/JMOL route, but that failure does not wipe out separate evidentiary or jury-instruction errors that were preserved by timely objection. Those preserved trial errors may be considered on appeal after final judgment, subject to harmless-error analysis.

Authority checked:

- 28 U.S.C. Section 1291 gives federal courts of appeals jurisdiction over final district-court decisions. Source: [U.S. Code, 28 U.S.C. Section 1291](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=/prelim@title28/part4/chapter83).
- Fed. R. Civ. P. 51(d)(1) allows a party to assign error in an instruction when the party properly objected; Rule 51(d)(2) allows plain-error review only for an unpreserved instructional error affecting substantial rights. Source: [U.S. Courts, Federal Rules of Civil Procedure](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-civil-procedure-dec-1-2024_0.pdf).
- Fed. R. Civ. P. 61 requires courts to disregard harmless errors that do not affect substantial rights. Source: [U.S. Courts, Federal Rules of Civil Procedure](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-civil-procedure-dec-1-2024_0.pdf).
- Fed. R. Evid. 103(a) covers preservation of evidentiary error and Rule 103(e) covers plain error. Source: [U.S. Courts, Federal Rules of Evidence](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-evidence-dec-1-2024_0.pdf).
- Fed. R. Civ. P. 50 supplies the JMOL preservation lane. Nothing in that rule turns preserved evidence or instruction objections into harmless error.

No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is triggered. The official key is consistent with the current federal rules and the final-judgment posture.
