### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. Timothy completed larceny when he reached into the lockbox, took the pouch from the hall's control, carried it away, and did so without permission while angry enough to keep it from the business. Gold Key: embezzlement depends on lawful possession at the moment of conversion; once Timothy handed the pouch to Lydia and she placed it in the lockbox, the later taking was not a conversion by someone still in lawful possession. Silver Key: freeze the possession snapshot at the exact taking, not at the earlier job shift.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A sells the idea that Lydia's accusation turns the episode into a workplace dispute rather than a theft. The breaker is the visible act: Timothy took a pouch from the lockbox without permission and left with it, so the larceny frame is live.

C is attractive because Timothy was the ticket-table worker and had handled the cash earlier. Gold Key: earlier custody does not make the later lockbox grab embezzlement; embezzlement requires lawful possession when the property is converted, and Lydia had already reclaimed control.

D is attractive because it sounds like a cautious compromise between two familiar theft labels. The breaker is that the facts do not leave two possible crimes open: after the manager took control, the later unauthorized taking points to larceny and knocks out embezzlement.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested issue is unchanged: larceny versus embezzlement when an employee initially handles business money, turns it over to a manager, and later takes it from the manager's control. The credited outcome is unchanged: larceny, not embezzlement.

For the lawyer-confirmation layer, the Department of Justice Criminal Resource Manual section 1006 states the larceny elements as wrongful taking and carrying away, lack of consent, and intent to deprive, citing United States v. Barlow, 480 F.2d 1245, 1251 (D.C. Cir. 1972): https://www.justice.gov/archives/jm/criminal-resource-manual-1006-larceny. The Department of Justice Criminal Resource Manual section 1005 states that embezzlement is the fraudulent appropriation of property entrusted to the defendant or lawfully in the defendant's hands, quoting Moore v. United States, 160 U.S. 268, 269 (1895): https://www.justice.gov/archives/jm/criminal-resource-manual-1005-embezzlement.

Applied here, Timothy's ticket-table role gave him temporary work access, but he surrendered the pouch to Lydia. When Lydia put it in the lockbox, possession returned to the business through its manager. Timothy's later taking from the open lockbox was without consent and with intent to deprive. That satisfies larceny and defeats embezzlement.
