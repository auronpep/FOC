## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged: voluntary manslaughter is the best classification when an intentional killing that would otherwise be murder is committed in response to adequate provocation, in actual heat of passion, before a reasonable cooling period, and with a causal link between provocation and killing.

Traditional common-law voluntary manslaughter doctrine treats legally adequate provocation and heat of passion as mitigation that negates malice rather than as a complete defense. A serious battery is a classic adequate-provocation category. The Model Penal Code reaches a similar mitigation result through section 210.3, which treats a homicide that would otherwise be murder as manslaughter when committed under extreme mental or emotional disturbance for which there is reasonable explanation or excuse. Cornell Wex's heat-of-passion summary likewise describes the doctrine as a mitigating factor used to negate malice in a murder prosecution. Girouard v. State, 321 Md. 532 (1991), is a common modern reference for the traditional categories of legally adequate provocation, including battery. Sources checked: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/heat_of_passion, https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/crime_of_passion, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/6141-tanaka-criminal-law-casebook/resources/9.1.2-model-penal-code-mpc-article-210-criminal-homicide/, and https://law.justia.com/cases/maryland/court-of-appeals/1991/110-90-3.html.

Stephen's serious punch broke Daniel's teeth. Daniel acted seconds later while bleeding and furious. Those facts support adequate provocation, actual passion, no cooling period, and a causal connection. The intentional blow with a heavy object remains homicide liability, but the provocation mitigates the classification to voluntary manslaughter.
