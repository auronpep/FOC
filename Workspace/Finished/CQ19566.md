## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
B is right because Daniel created the emergency that required rescue transport and surgery. The rescue-van swerve happened during that medical response, and emergency transport complications are foreseeable consequences of a serious stabbing. A foreseeable, dependent intervening cause does not break the causal chain.

Gold Key: Criminal proximate cause survives a normal, foreseeable rescue or treatment response to the defendant's injury; the later event must be extraordinary or too remote to supersede the original attack.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning
The credited answer is correct. Criminal causation requires actual cause and a legal or proximate-cause limit. Under Model Penal Code section 2.03, causation for a result element is not enough when the actual result is too remote or accidental to have a just bearing on liability; conversely, a result that follows through a normal and foreseeable emergency response remains legally connected. The same common-law exam rule treats foreseeable, dependent intervening causes as non-superseding.

Here, Daniel inflicted a serious abdominal wound. Emergency transport and surgery were predictable responses to that wound. A proper swerve during emergency transport, followed by worsened bleeding and surgery, is not the kind of extraordinary, remote, or accidental event that cuts off liability. The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome remain the same as the source item: the defendant remains the proximate cause because the transport complication was a foreseeable response to the original injury.
