## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**A is correct.** Article II gives the President the pardon power for federal offenses, subject to the Constitution's textual exception for impeachment. Congress may pass ordinary statutes over a veto, but that procedural fact does not let Congress narrow a power the Constitution gives to the President. A statute forbidding pardons until a federal offender has served two-fifths of the sentence is therefore unconstitutional.

**Gold Key:** The President's pardon power for federal offenses comes from Article II itself; Congress cannot add a minimum-service condition to that power by statute.

**Silver Key:** Separate enactment procedure from substantive power. A two-thirds override can enact a bill, but it cannot make Congress the owner of a power assigned to the President.
