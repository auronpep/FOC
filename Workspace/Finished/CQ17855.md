### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

D is the best answer. The call tells us the jurisdiction follows the minority retreat rule, so Martha cannot win by ignoring the stipulated rule and applying the majority rule. But the minority retreat rule has a major built-in exception: a person attacked in her own dwelling has no duty to retreat before using necessary deadly force in self-defense. Martha was inside her own apartment when Stephen broke in, advanced toward her, and threatened to kill her.

Gold Key: In a retreat jurisdiction, the castle doctrine is the home exception. A safe exit matters outside the home, but it does not require a person to retreat from her own dwelling before using necessary deadly force.

Silver Key: When the stem says both "minority retreat rule" and "own home," do not choose between the two too early. First lock the stated jurisdiction, then ask whether a home exception sits inside that rule.

The credited answer remains yes, the self-defense claim is valid, because the castle doctrine excepts the defendant's dwelling from the retreat requirement.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Model Penal Code section 3.04(2)(b)(ii) states the retreat limitation for deadly force but also provides that the actor is not obliged to retreat from the actor's dwelling, subject to aggressor and workplace caveats not present here.
