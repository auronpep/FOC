# CQ15062 - Esther's Gravel Lane

## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Esther owned Olive House, a small Scripture-retreat cottage on a wooded tract. Lydia owned Psalm Meadow, an adjoining undeveloped tract suitable for a home. Lydia executed and delivered a deed granting Esther a right-of-way over the eastern 20 feet of Psalm Meadow for convenient ingress and egress to a county road, although Esther's tract also touched a different county road. ### 2. Distilled Core Question

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. The bank can win under the notice recording act only if it took the mortgage for value and without notice of Esther's prior right-of-way. Esther's visible gravel lane was already laid out and being used on Psalm Meadow before the bank took its mortgage. That visible use would prompt a reasonable lender to inspect and ask what right supported the lane.

Gold Key: Open and visible use of land can give inquiry notice of an unrecorded easement. A later purchaser or mortgagee with inquiry notice is not protected as a purchaser without notice.

Silver Key: Freeze the notice question at the moment the bank took the mortgage. Recording before the foreclosure lawsuit is too late if the bank already had inquiry notice when it became the mortgagee.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because appurtenant easements sound permanent: the right is tied to Esther's land, not to Esther personally. The breaker is that the bank is not trying to separate the easement from Esther's land; it is trying to establish mortgage priority and extinguish a subordinate interest through foreclosure. Appurtenance does not answer the notice-statute priority fight.

C is the dominant trap. It sells the familiar recording-act instinct that a recorded instrument is safer than an unrecorded one, and Esther did record before the foreclosure case began. The breaker is timing. The bank's protected status is tested when the bank takes the mortgage, not when the foreclosure suit is filed. Silver Key: ask whether the bank was without notice at mortgage time. Gold Key: visible use of the lane gave inquiry notice before the bank took.

D is attractive because the right-of-way really does provide road access, and road access often appears in easement questions. The breaker is that the item does not turn on easement by necessity. Esther already had an express deeded right-of-way, and her tract also touched another public road, so access-to-road language does not decide whether the bank had notice.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. This is a Real Property recording-act priority question about notice of a prior unrecorded easement. Under a notice recording act, a later mortgagee is protected only if it gives value and takes without notice of the earlier interest. If visible use of the land would cause a reasonable inspection or inquiry to reveal the easement, the later mortgagee has inquiry notice and takes subject to the easement.

Authority checked:

- Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes section 7.14 recognizes that servitudes can be extinguished under recording acts, but preserves servitudes that reasonable inspection or inquiry would reveal. The same section explains that inquiry notice is notice of interests a reasonable inspection or inquiry would disclose. Source: https://rowalaska.net/archives/projects/2022_klutina_lake_rs2477/archives/relocation/Relocation_of_Easements_Restatement_Provisions.pdf
- The Florida Bar's recording-act summary treats a subsequent mortgagee as protected under a notice statute only when it takes for value and without notice, and describes actual, implied, and constructive notice. Source: https://www.floridabar.org/the-florida-bar-journal/implied-and-constructive-notice-title-search-fallibility-and-the-rigidity-of-the-constructive-notice-doctrine/
- California Lawyers Association's BFP/inquiry-notice discussion states that inquiry notice can arise from what a routine inspection would reveal and from possession or use inconsistent with record title. Source: https://calawyers.org/real-property-law/bona-fide-purchasers-and-inquiry-notice/

Those authorities support the supplied official key. There is no key conflict and no doctrinal-currency hotlist issue.
