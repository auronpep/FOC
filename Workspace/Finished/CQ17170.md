### 7. Full right-answer explanation
C is correct. The city is using an express racial classification, so the Equal Protection analysis starts with strict scrutiny even though the city says the rule is remedial. Gold Key: a government racial classification does not get a softer test because it is labeled benign or corrective; the government must show a compelling interest and narrow tailoring. Silver Key: separate the review level from the final result. The answer does not say the rule automatically survives; it says the city must prove the two strict-scrutiny elements.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer
The credited analysis is unchanged: an express race-conscious classification by a city triggers strict scrutiny under the Equal Protection Clause. The city must prove that the classification furthers a compelling governmental interest and is narrowly tailored to that interest. A remedial purpose does not lower the standard to rational basis, economic contracting does not convert race into an intermediate-scrutiny classification, and strict scrutiny does not by itself decide the outcome.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment supplies the constitutional rule. In *City of Richmond v. J.A. Croson Co.*, 488 U.S. 469 (1989), local race-based contracting remedies were evaluated under strict scrutiny. In *Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Pena*, 515 U.S. 200 (1995), the Court stated that governmental racial classifications must serve a compelling governmental interest and be narrowly tailored, and rejected the idea that strict scrutiny is necessarily fatal in every case. In *Students for Fair Admissions, Inc. v. President and Fellows of Harvard College*, 600 U.S. 181 (2023), the Court described strict scrutiny as asking whether the racial classification furthers a compelling interest and is narrowly tailored, and recognized remediation of specific, identified past discrimination that violated law as one of the narrow categories of potentially compelling interests. These authorities support the supplied key because the stem gives a court finding of intentional exclusion from the exact program and a limited, individualized remedy.
