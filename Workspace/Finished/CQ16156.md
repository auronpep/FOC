### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Criminal Law and Procedure, Other Crimes, Crimes against Property > Larceny, with verified outline code `73030100`. Common-law larceny requires a trespassory taking and carrying away of another's personal property with intent to steal it. The U.S. Department of Justice Criminal Resource Manual section on larceny summarizes the specific elements as wrongful taking and carrying away, lack of consent, and intent to deprive, citing *United States v. Barlow*, 480 F.2d 1245, 1251 (D.C. Cir. 1972): https://www.justice.gov/archives/jm/criminal-resource-manual-1006-larceny.

The asportation point is also confirmed. *People v. Davis*, 19 Cal. 4th 301 (1998), states the settled larceny elements and explains that when the taking has begun, the slightest movement of the property is enough carrying away or asportation: https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/people-v-davis-31960. That authority also treats later consent to a different step as irrelevant to whether the defendant consented to the first taking. Daniel took possession of Paul's personal property without consent, with intent to steal, and moved the whole item by lifting it off the table. The later birthday-gift consent did not undo the already completed larceny. The supplied official key is confirmed.
