For sixteen years, Lydia ran a Bible-verse candle studio on a mixed-use block, selling Advent candles and hand-lettered labels from her own building. ### 6. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Private nuisance asks whether the interference with use and enjoyment of land is substantial and unreasonable. Martha's knowledge that Lydia's candle studio already existed matters, but it is not an automatic claim-killer. The harder fact is that Lydia later added an overnight production line, and the new rattling and fumes are the interference Martha is challenging.

Gold Key: Coming to the nuisance is a factor, not a complete bar by itself. It can affect reasonableness, equities, and remedy, but it does not erase a private nuisance claim merely because the plaintiff knowingly bought nearby land.

Silver Key: In a factor-vs-bar clash, choose the answer that preserves the balancing inquiry unless the stem supplies a true complete defense.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

B sells the protective plaintiff answer. It reaches "No," which is the right result, and then overprotects Martha by saying coming to the nuisance is never relevant. ### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row. The source asks whether "coming to the nuisance" completely bars a private nuisance claim. The correct legal answer is no: prior knowledge is relevant to reasonableness and remedy, but it does not automatically defeat the claim.

Lawyer-confirmation authority:

- Restatement (Second) of Torts section 821D defines private nuisance as a nontrespassory invasion of another's interest in private use and enjoyment of land. Source: https://advance.lexis.com/open/document/openwebdocview/-821D-Private-Nuisance/?pdcomponentid=12231&pddocfullpath=%2Fshared%2Fdocument%2Fanalytical-materials%2Furn%3AcontentItem%3A42JH-HPS0-00YF-T13H-00000-00
- Restatement (Second) of Torts sections 822 and 826 frame liability around substantial, unreasonable interference rather than a categorical purchase-knowledge bar.
- Restatement (Second) of Torts section 840D treats acquiring or improving land after the nuisance began as a factor, not a standalone bar. A New Jersey appellate decision quotes and applies that Restatement formulation. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/appellate-division-unpublished/2010/a1204-08-opn.html
- Spur Industries, Inc. v. Del E. Webb Development Co., 108 Ariz. 178, 494 P.2d 700 (1972), is a classic coming-to-the-nuisance case showing that equitable relief and remedy can be adjusted when a plaintiff comes to the nuisance, rather than treating prior arrival as an automatic total bar. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/arizona/supreme-court/1972/10410-0.html

Under those authorities, Martha's prior knowledge of Lydia's studio may reduce or shape relief, and it may affect the reasonableness balance. It does not completely bar the private nuisance claim. The later overnight line strengthens the same outcome because it gives Martha a changed interference beyond the condition she knowingly encountered.
