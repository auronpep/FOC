## 1. Final question

Deacon Barnabas, a longtime member and volunteer coordinator at Grace Fellowship Church, was injured when a ladder slipped during a Saturday service project to build a wheelchair ramp for an elderly widow in the congregation. He sues the volunteer crew supervisor for negligence. ## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

C is correct. Under FRCP 26(e)(1)(A), a party who has responded to an interrogatory must supplement or correct the response in a timely manner if the party learns that in some material respect the response is incomplete or incorrect and the additional or corrective information has not otherwise been made known to the other parties during the discovery process or in writing. Here, the third physician's identity and records are material to the injuries claimed, the defendant has no other notice, and Barnabas learned of them after serving the original response. Supplementation is therefore required.

**Gold Key:** The supplementation duty is triggered by the party's own learning of material incompleteness, not by any action or renewed request from the opponent. Once triggered, the duty is to supplement "in a timely manner" unless the information has already been made known another way.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A: Attractive because it sells the common intuition that discovery is a snapshot — "I answered what I knew when I answered, and they can't complain later." Breaker: The rule expressly creates a continuing duty that activates when the party learns of the material gap; waiting until trial (or until objection) does not satisfy the timely-supplement obligation.

B: Attractive because it feels like fairness or finality — "they got what they asked for at the time; the system shouldn't punish me for later learning." Breaker (dominant trap): This directly contradicts the text of 26(e)(1)(A), which imposes an affirmative, ongoing duty precisely when new material information comes to the party's attention and has not otherwise been disclosed.

D: Attractive because it mirrors how some requests work (you only update if they ask again) and feels like it limits burden. Breaker: The duty is self-executing upon the party's learning of material incompleteness; it does not require a follow-up interrogatory or court order to spring into effect.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited answer is correct under the governing authority. FRCP 26(e)(1) states: "A party who has made a disclosure under Rule 26(a)—or who has responded to an interrogatory, request for production, or request for admission—must supplement or correct its disclosure or response: (A) in a timely manner if the party learns that in some material respect the disclosure or response is incomplete or incorrect, and if the additional or corrective information has not otherwise been made known to the other parties during the discovery process or in writing; or (B) as ordered by the court."
