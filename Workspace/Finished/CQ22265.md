### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Lydia is offering the fact that Barnabas's insurer paid her hospital bill in Barnabas's negligence case. That is exactly the policy-exclusion lane: payment of medical or hospital expenses resulting from an injury is not admissible when offered to prove liability for the injury. It does not matter that the payment came through an insurer. It also does not matter that the payment was conduct rather than a spoken admission.

Gold Key: FRE 409 excludes evidence of paying, furnishing, promising to pay, or offering to pay medical, hospital, or similar expenses when the evidence is offered to prove liability for the injury; the rule is not limited to communications.

Silver Key: When the answer array gives you payment, communication, hearsay, and fault, lock the offered purpose first. If the evidence is medical-bill payment used to prove negligence, the policy exclusion beats the communication and hearsay labels.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because it notices that no one is quoting Barnabas and no statement is being introduced. The breaker is the Gold Key: FRE 409 covers the act of paying or furnishing medical or hospital expenses, so the absence of a communication does not make the evidence admissible.

C is attractive because it reaches the same bottom-line "No" and uses a familiar Evidence objection. The breaker is the label. A bill payment is not hearsay simply because it is useful evidence; hearsay requires a statement offered for its truth. The testimony is excluded because it concerns medical-payment evidence offered to prove liability, not because it is hearsay.

D is attractive because the payment feels like practical proof that Barnabas or his insurer accepted responsibility. The breaker is the policy exclusion.
