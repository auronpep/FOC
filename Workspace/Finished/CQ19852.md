## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

A federal job-site safety agency has statutory authority to require employers to keep ladders, presses, and delivery carts safe and to report job-site hazards. Relying on that authority, the agency orders every large employer nationwide, from Lydia's Purple Cloth Co. to Peter's Fish Market, to fund a paid "Jubilee Leave" bank for employee rest and family care. The leave bank is not tied to any ladder, press, cart, or specific job-site hazard. The agency says rested workers make safer workplaces.

What is the strongest argument against the agency rule?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Can a safety agency use general equipment-and-hazard authority to impose a nationwide paid-leave program not tied to a specific workplace hazard?

### 3. Final Answer Choices

A. The rule is valid because any program that might make workers healthier or calmer is part of job-site safety.

B. The rule is valid because private employers administer the leave bank, not the agency.

C. The rule is vulnerable because the agency is asserting a major economic and political power without clear congressional authorization.

D. The rule is invalid only if Congress expressly barred the agency from requiring paid leave.

### 5. Correct Answer

C (original key: A).

### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is the strongest argument. The agency has a statute about safe equipment and hazard reporting, but it is using that statute to impose a nationwide paid-leave program. That is a major economic and political policy choice. Gold Key: when an agency claims power over a major question, broad safety or welfare words are not enough; the agency needs clear congressional authorization. Silver Key: lock onto the source of the agency's power, not whether the policy sounds useful.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because it treats worker well-being as a bridge into job-site safety. The breaker is that "helpful for workers" is too broad when the agency is claiming authority to impose a major national benefit program. The Gold Key defeats the trap: major policy power needs clear authorization.

B is attractive because the employers do the day-to-day administration. The breaker is that private administration does not answer the call. The question is whether Congress authorized the agency to impose the mandate in the first place.

D is attractive because students often look for an express statutory ban before calling an agency action invalid. The breaker is that the major-questions rule can require clear authorization even when Congress did not expressly forbid the agency's preferred policy.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are preserved. The issue is whether an agency may rely on broad workplace-safety language to impose a nationwide paid-leave program not tied to a specific workplace hazard. The right answer remains that the rule is vulnerable because the agency is asserting major economic and political power without clear congressional authorization.

Research confirms the supplied key. In West Virginia v. EPA, 597 U.S. 697, 721-724 (2022), the Court applied the major-questions doctrine to require clear congressional authorization when an agency asserts highly consequential power. In NFIB v. OSHA, 595 U.S. 109, 117-119 (2022), the Court treated OSHA's broad vaccine-or-test mandate as beyond ordinary occupational-safety regulation. Biden v. Nebraska, 600 U.S. 477, 501-506 (2023), confirms that the doctrine also applies when the agency frames the program as a benefit rather than a burden.
