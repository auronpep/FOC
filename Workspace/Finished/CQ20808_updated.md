# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

Hannah owned a vintage hand-cranked letterpress called the “Jericho Press,” which Timothy liked to use to print private Bible-verse cards. Hannah transferred title to the press to Lydia, but only after Lydia agreed that Timothy could use the press whenever he wished. Lydia agreed but did not tell Timothy about that promise.

Mary lived in a coastal town and had long wanted Timothy to move there for the town’s private Scripture-calligraphy fairs. Mary wrote Timothy a letter saying that, if he moved to the town, she would leave him her estate when she died.

Timothy knew that Lydia now owned the Jericho Press. He told Lydia that, if she would let him use the press until Mary died, he would give Lydia half of whatever he received from Mary. Lydia agreed, and Timothy moved to the town and used the press.

Mary died shortly afterward. Her will left her entire estate to Timothy, but Timothy refused to share any of it with Lydia.

If Lydia asserts a claim against Timothy for a share of the estate that Timothy received from Mary, a court should find for:

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A claimant promises to let another person use property, but she was already obligated to allow that use. The other person promises to share a later inheritance in exchange for that same permission. Can the claimant enforce the sharing promise?

## 3. Final answer choices

A. Lydia, because Timothy promised to share the estate with her if she promised to let him use the press until Mary’s death.

B. Timothy, because his promise to Lydia was not supported by consideration.

C. Lydia, because Timothy is estopped from denying the existence of an enforceable contract between him and Lydia.

D. Timothy, because Lydia’s promise to Hannah created an implied condition precedent to Timothy’s obligation to share the estate with Lydia.

## 5. Correct answer

**B**. Original key: **D**.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** Lydia’s promise to let Timothy use the Jericho Press did not give Timothy anything new. Lydia had already agreed with Hannah that Timothy could use the press whenever he wished. So Lydia’s later promise to Timothy was not a new legal detriment and could not support Timothy’s promise to share Mary’s estate.

**Gold Key:** A promise to do what the promisee is already legally obligated to do is not consideration for a new promise.

**Silver Key:** Do not stop at “promise-for-promise.” Ask what new legal position the plaintiff gave up in this later bargain.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells the visible exchange: Timothy promised to share the estate if Lydia promised to let him use the press. The breaker is the Gold Key: Lydia’s promised permission was not new consideration because she already owed that permission.

**C.** This choice reaches for promissory estoppel as a fairness rescue when consideration fails. The breaker is reliance: Lydia did not change position because of Timothy’s sharing promise; she merely did what she was already bound to do.

**D.** This choice uses the hidden Hannah-Lydia promise to reach Timothy’s winning result. The breaker is frame control: Timothy’s later promise to Lydia was not made subject to an implied condition he did not know about.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged from the original row: the claimant loses because her promised performance was already owed and therefore was not consideration for the later inheritance-sharing promise. The Restatement (Second) of Contracts is a major common-law contracts authority, and ALI describes it as an authoritative work widely relied on and adopted by courts. ([American Law Institute][1])

Restatement §71 states that consideration requires a bargained-for performance or return promise, and §73 states that performance of an existing legal duty owed to the promisor is not consideration unless the performance differs from what the duty already required or the duty is honestly disputed. ([Open Casebook][2])

Promissory estoppel does not repair this item because §90 requires a promise that reasonably induces action or forbearance and actually does induce it. The stem gives no Lydia reliance beyond the permission she already owed. ([Open Casebook][3])

The condition-precedent distractor is also not the route. A condition is an event that must occur before performance under a contract becomes due, but the hidden Hannah-Lydia promise was not a condition Timothy built into his later promise to Lydia. ([Open Casebook][4])

## 10. Why the variation preserves rigor and difficulty

The legal rule is identical. The trap remains difficult because the visible later exchange looks like a bargain, but the hidden earlier obligation defeats consideration. The surface story is different enough to remove recognition memory: farm and family deed become a private Bible-verse letterpress and title transfer.
