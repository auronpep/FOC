## 7. Right-Answer Explanation

The motion to strike should be granted, but not because FRE 408 bars the testimony. The reason is simpler and more fundamental: the answer was not responsive to the question asked.

**Gold Key:** An answer that is otherwise admissible for a limited purpose may still be struck as nonresponsive if it does not answer the specific question asked. Responsiveness and admissibility are separate inquiries — the examining attorney controls the direction of examination under FRE 611(a), and unresponsive volunteering can be stricken regardless of whether the testimony could be admitted if properly elicited.

Stephen's attorney asked Hannah when she first retained legal counsel. That is a narrow, date-oriented question. Hannah's explanation of why she delayed — that settlement talks were ongoing — was never asked for. The attorney who elicits testimony has the right to control what the witness covers. The settlement testimony may well be admissible if the plaintiff's attorney elicits it on redirect to rebut the delay argument, but the defendant's attorney did not ask for it and may have it struck as nonresponsive.

**Silver Key:** When a stem has a precise cross-examination question AND a witness who volunteers extra information, always check whether the volunteered material answers the question asked. If it goes beyond the scope of the question, the motion to strike should be granted regardless of what the testimony might prove if it were properly elicited.

## 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice C — No, admissible to explain delay (DOMINANT TRAP):**
This answer correctly identifies a true legal rule — FRE 408 does not bar settlement evidence offered for a purpose other than proving or disproving the validity of the claim, and explaining a delay in filing suit is exactly such a purpose. Students who know the limited-admissibility exception conclude the motion must fail. The trap works by conflating two separate doctrines: admissibility and responsiveness. Even evidence that is admissible for a limited purpose can be struck when the witness volunteered it without being asked. The plaintiff's attorney can elicit the settlement explanation on redirect; the defendant's attorney was not required to open that door on cross.

**Choice D — No, settlement proves fault:**
This is the most straightforwardly wrong answer. FRE 408 specifically prohibits using settlement offers or settlement discussions to prove or disprove the validity or amount of a disputed claim, including inferences about fault. A student who misremembers FRE 408 as permitting this inference picks D. The rule exists precisely to encourage settlements by ensuring that an offer cannot later be used as an admission of liability.

**Choice A — Yes, policy rule bars all settlement evidence:**
This answer reaches the right outcome for the wrong reason. FRE 408's exclusionary policy does not bar all settlement evidence — it only bars evidence offered to prove liability or the claim's validity. Evidence of settlement talks offered to explain delay, or for bias or another permissible purpose, is not barred. If the student picks A thinking FRE 408 categorically excludes settlement evidence, the answer misstates the rule. The correct reason to grant the motion is nonresponsiveness, not an across-the-board exclusion.

**Gold Key callout on Choice C:** Choice C inverts the analysis — it knows FRE 408's limited-purpose exception but misses that this exception is irrelevant to the responsiveness question. The Gold Key: admissibility and responsiveness are separate. The settlement testimony might be admissible if properly elicited; it is still nonresponsive because it was not asked for.

**Silver Key callout on Choice C:** Silver Key SK-EV-NONRESPONSIVE-CLASH-01 fires here: the stem has a precise question ("when did you retain counsel?") and volunteered extra content (why she delayed). Spot that mismatch — the volunteered content is nonresponsive regardless of its admissibility.

## 9. Black-Letter Verification

**Tested rule:** FRE 611(a) grants the trial court authority to exercise reasonable control over the mode and order of examining witnesses to make the examination effective for ascertaining truth and to avoid wasting time. Under this provision, a court may strike testimony that is nonresponsive to the question asked, even if the testimony would otherwise be admissible for some purpose.

**Nonresponsive answers:** An answer is nonresponsive when it goes beyond the scope of the question. The questioning attorney's motion to strike is the proper remedy. The court has discretion to grant or deny, but when the answer clearly exceeds the question's scope, the motion should be granted. Admissibility for some other purpose does not defeat a nonresponsiveness motion.

**FRE 408:** Settlement offers, settlement discussions, and statements made during negotiations are inadmissible to prove or disprove the validity or amount of a disputed claim or to impeach by prior inconsistent statement or contradiction. The rule does NOT exclude such evidence when offered for another purpose — e.g., proving witness bias, negating a claim of undue delay, or establishing bad faith. Therefore FRE 408 would not categorically bar the settlement testimony if it were properly elicited to rebut the delay argument.
