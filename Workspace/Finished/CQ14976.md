### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. Lydia and Naomi held Bethany Olive Grove as joint tenants with right of survivorship. Lydia's will did not transfer any present interest during Lydia's life, and a will speaks only at death. Gold Key: a joint tenant can sever by a lifetime transfer or other effective severing act, but a mere devise in a will does not sever because the right of survivorship takes effect at death before the devise can move the joint-tenancy interest. Silver Key: do not stop at the first true phrase in a "because" answer; ask whether the listed act is the severing act. Because Lydia's only act was a will, Naomi takes the whole property by survivorship.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
**A.** This choice sounds probate-fluent because ademption is a real wills concept. The breaker is that the land never became a probate asset for Lydia's will to dispose of, so ademption is the wrong frame for the call.

**C.** This choice sounds property-fluent because after-acquired title and estoppel by deed are real doctrines. The breaker is that no one conveyed land before owning it and later acquired title; the fight is about survivorship versus a will.

**D.** This choice is the dominant trap because it grabs a true idea: a joint tenant can often sever without the other joint tenant's consent. Gold Key: the act still has to be an effective lifetime severing act; a will is not one because it does not transfer the interest until death, when survivorship has already operated.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Ownership, Concurrent Estates, Joint tenancy, and the verified outline code is `82010301`. The credited outcome is unchanged: the surviving joint tenant owns the whole property because the deceased joint tenant's will did not sever the joint tenancy.

Authority floor: Georgia Code section 44-6-190 annotations summarize *Harbin v. Harbin*, 261 Ga. App. 244, 582 S.E.2d 131 (2003), for the rule that a will transfers property only after probate following the testator's death and therefore does not qualify as a lifetime transfer capable of severing a joint tenancy. Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/2021/title-44/chapter-6/article-8/section-44-6-190/. The same survivorship principle appears in *Estate of Emmet Awtry v. Commissioner*, 221 F.2d 749 (8th Cir. 1955), explaining that property held in joint tenancy passes to the survivor by operation of law and is not capable of testamentary devise by the deceased joint tenant. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/221/749/273635/. California Civil Code section 683.2, discussed in *Raney v. Cerkueira*, confirms the related modern statutory frame that an effective severance must occur before death to eliminate the other joint tenant's survivorship right. Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2019/a152549.html.

On the transformed facts, Lydia made only a will. That instrument did not operate as a lifetime conveyance or other effective severance before Lydia died. When Lydia died, Naomi's survivorship right took effect and Naomi became sole owner. Barnabas receives nothing in Bethany Olive Grove under Lydia's will. No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is triggered.
