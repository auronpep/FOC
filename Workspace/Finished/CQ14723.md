Daniel, a teenager, and two friends from his youth worship band were driving home late after a Scripture-memory fundraiser. Their van sideswiped a pickup driven by an elderly beekeeper. Two companions watched a fatal beating and urged the attacker to run only after the victim fell; are they accomplices to manslaughter?

### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

A is the best answer. The companions did not help, solicit, encourage, plan, guard, supply a weapon, act as lookouts, or otherwise promote the manslaughter before or during the beating. Their visible conduct is watching, followed by urging flight after the victim fell. Gold Key: accomplice liability needs intentional help or encouragement for the crime charged before or during its commission; mere presence or post-offense flight advice does not make a bystander an accomplice to that crime. Silver Key: lock the timing and the charge; this is accomplice-to-manslaughter, not accessory-after-the-fact.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

B is attractive because the companions look morally blameworthy for standing there while a vulnerable person was beaten. The breaker is that criminal accomplice liability is not a general duty-to-rescue rule; absent a legal duty or intentional aid, nonintervention and presence do not make them accomplices.

C is the dominant trap because "run" sounds like helping Daniel after violence has occurred. Gold Key: the help must promote or facilitate the manslaughter itself. A post-offense flight prompt may point toward a different after-the-fact theory, but it does not prove they aided or encouraged the killing.

D is attractive because a hung jury against Daniel feels like the companions' case should fail with his. The breaker is that modern accomplice liability does not require the principal actor to be convicted first; the companions' liability turns on their own assistance and intent.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged. Under Model Penal Code sec. 2.06(3)(a), a person is an accomplice if, with the purpose of promoting or facilitating the offense, the person solicits, aids, agrees to aid, or attempts to aid another in planning or committing it. The source item tests that requirement: watching a fatal beating, then urging escape after the victim falls, is not aid or encouragement in committing manslaughter. The principal's unresolved trial does not by itself bar a later accomplice case under the modern approach, but that does not help the prosecution because these companions did not assist the charged offense.

Authority floor: Model Penal Code sec. 2.06(3)(a). Secondary confirmation checked the standard accomplice rule that mere presence, even with flight after the crime, is insufficient without aid, encouragement, intent, or a legal duty.
