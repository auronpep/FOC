### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

Lydia's testimony is admissible as evidence of trade usage. Under the UCC (§ 1-303) and common law, evidence of usage of trade is admissible to give meaning to contract terms. A witness who is familiar with the trade may testify to what a term means within that industry. Here, Lydia testifies that in the leather-tanning trade, "curing solution" means a mixture containing 40 percent oak bark extract. This is precisely the kind of evidence that trade usage doctrine exists to admit — it helps the court understand the meaning of specialized contract language as understood by participants in the relevant market.

**Gold Key:** Trade usage (industry-wide meaning of a term) is distinct from business habit (a particular organization's routine practice). Trade usage is admissible to interpret contract terms; business habit is admissible to prove conduct on a particular occasion. The witness here describes what the term means across the trade, not what her company customarily does.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**D (business habit) — DOMINANT TRAP:**
This choice is attractive because Lydia is testifying about industry practice — she says what "curing solution" means "in the leather-tanning trade." Students hear "in the trade" and think "business habit" or "routine practice." But business habit (FRE 406) is about a particular person or organization's regular conduct offered as evidence that they acted in conformity with that habit on a specific occasion. Lydia is not describing her workshop's routine practice; she is describing what the term means across the entire industry. That is trade usage, not business habit. **Gold Key:** Trade usage = what a term means industry-wide. Business habit = what a particular entity routinely does. The witness describes the former.

**A (hearsay):**
This choice tempts students who mechanically associate witness testimony with hearsay analysis. Hearsay is an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted. Lydia's testimony is her own in-court statement — she is the witness, and she is testifying live. There is no out-of-court statement being offered. The hearsay rule simply does not apply to a witness's own testimony.

**B (self-serving statement):**
This choice appeals to students' intuition that a party's witness is biased. But there is no rule of evidence that excludes testimony simply because it is self-serving. Party-witness testimony is routinely admitted. The remedy for self-serving testimony is cross-examination and jury evaluation, not exclusion.

### 9. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing rule:** UCC § 1-303(c) defines "usage of trade" as "any practice or method of dealing having such regularity of observance in a place, vocation, or trade as to justify an expectation that it will be observed with respect to the transaction in question." Evidence of such usage is relevant to the meaning of a contract. *See also* FRE 401/402 (relevant evidence is generally admissible); 2 McCormick on Evidence § 221 (8th ed.).

**Trade usage vs. business habit:** Trade usage (UCC § 1-303) concerns the meaning of terms within an industry. Business habit/routine practice (FRE 406) concerns the regular conduct of a particular person or organization, admissible to prove action in conformity therewith on a specific occasion. These are distinct doctrines with distinct purposes. *See* FRE 406; *see also* 2 McCormick on Evidence § 162.

**Self-serving statements:** No rule of evidence categorically excludes self-serving testimony. *See* 1 McCormick on Evidence § 10 (8th ed.) ("The objection that a witness's testimony is 'self-serving' is not a valid ground for exclusion.").

**Hearsay:** Hearsay is "a statement that: (1) the declarant does not make while testifying at the current trial or hearing; and (2) a party offers in evidence to prove the truth of the matter asserted in the statement." FRE 801(c). A witness's own in-court testimony is not hearsay.

#### Wrong-Answer Recovery Paths

**If you picked D (business habit):** You heard Lydia describe what the term means "in the trade" and associated it with habit/routine practice. But business habit (FRE 406) is about a particular entity's regular conduct — what *this company* routinely does. Lydia described what the term means across the *entire industry*. That's trade usage. Recovery: ask — is the witness describing industry-wide meaning or organizational routine? Industry-wide = trade usage. Organizational = business habit.

**If you picked A (hearsay):** You may have mechanically applied hearsay analysis to any out-of-court statement. But Lydia is testifying *in court* — she is the witness. Hearsay requires an out-of-court statement. Recovery: always check — is the statement being offered an out-of-court statement, or is the witness testifying live? Live testimony ≠ hearsay.

**If you picked B (self-serving statement):** You recognized that Lydia is Timothy's business partner and her testimony benefits his side. But there is no rule excluding self-serving testimony. Recovery: remember — bias goes to weight, not admissibility. The remedy for self-serving testimony is cross-examination, not exclusion.
