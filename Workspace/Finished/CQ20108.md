## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

**B is correct.** The common-law and Model Penal Code duress defense
requires that the defendant be subjected to a threat of unlawful
force that a person of reasonable firmness would have been unable to
resist. The defense is unavailable, however, when the defendant
**recklessly or voluntarily placed herself in a situation where
coercion was likely**. Esther knowingly joined a crew planning to run
untaxed cigarettes by fishing boat under cover of night — a covert,
illegal operation in which violent enforcement of the captain's
orders is plainly foreseeable. She therefore brought the duress
situation on herself. Even though Marcus's knife threat was real,
and even though Esther's compliance was compelled in the moment, the
created-risk limitation defeats the duress claim.

> **Gold Key:** the *voluntary-association* limitation is the duress
> defense's quietest element and its most-tested on the bar. The
> question is **never** simply "was the threat serious enough?" — it
> is "did the defendant bring the risk of coercion on herself?" A
> yes to that second question collapses the defense.

> **Silver Key:** on this 2-vs-2 array (A & D say "duress succeeds";
> B & C say "duress fails"), find the "fails" choice that **names the
> actual rule** — the recklessly-joined limitation — and the "fails"
> choice that **invents a rule** (accomplice threats never count).
> The one that names the actual rule is credited; the one that
> invents a rule is a false-law trap.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations (one per distractor)

### Choice A — "Duress succeeds because Esther did not plan to steer the boat."

The cognitive move this sells is the **role-specific framing**: the
trap says Esther "didn't sign up for *this part*," so the duress
should work. The breaker is that the duress rule does not ask whether
the defendant planned a specific role. It asks whether the defendant
voluntarily or recklessly entered a situation where coercion was
likely. Esther knowingly joined the entire crew and the entire
criminal plan; whether she planned to be the helmsman is irrelevant
to the recklessly-joined element. C3 cut: the rule is about the
defendant's overall entry into the criminal enterprise, not her
specific task on the night of the offense. Filter broken: **NOT_TRUE**.

### Choice C — "Duress fails because threats by accomplices never count as human threats."

The cognitive move this sells is the **categorical rule invention**:

threats" for duress purposes. The breaker is that accomplice threats
*are* human threats under the duress rule — the *source* of the
threat is not a limit on the defense. The actual limit is the
defendant's voluntary entry into the criminal setting. C3 cut: the
choice invents a rule (accomplice threats never count) that the
duress framework does not state. Filter broken: **NOT_TRUE**.

### Choice D (dominant trap) — "Duress succeeds because Marcus threatened Esther with a fishing knife."

This is the highest-pull wrong answer on the mechanic, and the only

conclusion. The cognitive move it sells is the **threat-element

**created-risk limitation**: duress is unavailable to a defendant

and the no-voluntary-association half — and the question is decided
by the second half on these facts.

> **Gold Key:** a serious threat from a co-felon does not, by itself,
> make duress available. The defense fails when the defendant
> voluntarily placed herself in a setting where exactly that kind of
> threat was likely. This is the **voluntary-association limitation**
> on duress, codified in Model Penal Code § 2.09(2)(c).

## 9. Black-letter verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

**The rule.** Common-law duress is an affirmative defense requiring
(1) a threat of unlawful force, (2) of such severity and imminence
that a person of reasonable firmness would have been unable to
resist, (3) that is causally connected to the defendant's criminal
conduct, and (4) that the defendant did not voluntarily or recklessly
bring upon herself. The last element is the **voluntary-association
limitation**: a defendant who knowingly joined a criminal enterprise
in which coercion was foreseeable cannot later invoke duress to
escape liability for the resulting crimes. The Model Penal Code
§ 2.09 codifies this principle — the defense is unavailable to a
defendant "who knowingly placed himself in a situation in which it
was probable that he would be subjected to duress." The limitation
is a well-settled bar on duress claims by participants in violent
criminal enterprises.

**Application to Esther's conduct.** Esther knowingly joined a
midnight cigarette-smuggling operation run by fishing boat. The
covert, illegal nature of the plan — and the foreseeable role of the
captain in enforcing the crew's compliance — make violent
coercion by a co-felon a foreseeable risk of participation. When
Marcus drew the fishing knife and ordered Esther to steer close to
shore, the threat was indeed real and immediate. But the
voluntary-association limitation defeats the duress claim: Esther
brought the coercive situation upon herself by joining the crew.
The fact that the underlying compliance (steering close to shore)
was not the role she originally planned is irrelevant; the duress
rule asks about her overall entry into the criminal enterprise, not
her specific task.

**Authority floor (lawyer confirmation).**

- *Authority:* **Model Penal Code § 2.09** (Duress) — the modern
  codification of the duress defense, including the
  voluntary-association limitation in § 2.09(2)(c): the defense is
  unavailable to a defendant "who knowingly placed himself in a
  situation in which it was probable that he would be subjected to
  duress." (Authority class: rule-number / model code. Low
  fabrication risk; widely cited.)
- *Common-law roots:* the voluntary-association limitation is
  embedded in the common-law duress doctrine as a limit on the
  reasonableness inquiry — a court asks whether a *reasonable*
  person would have yielded, and a person who voluntarily entered
  a criminal setting has, by that act, expanded the scope of
  coercions he is reasonably expected to bear. I do not cite a
  specific case for the common-law formulation to avoid citation
  risk; the principle is well settled and a treatise citation would
  not raise the primary-law floor under the QA gate. Flag for
  attorney review if a specific case anchor is needed downstream.

**Doctrinal-currency check.** The duress / voluntary-association
limitation is not on the doctrinal-currency hotlist (it does not
implicate FRE 702, the Confrontation Clause, Kennedy v. Bremerton,
Bruen, Dobbs, Mallory, Cedar Point, Ramos, Erlinger, or Counterman).
No currency concerns.
