### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** The lineup identification is out because Daniel was already indicted and the lineup was conducted without counsel. But that does not automatically erase every later identification. Mary had a source for recognizing the robber that came before the lineup: several minutes face-to-face, bright lighting, and a detailed description given before police showed her the lineup. The in-court identification may be admitted on that independent-source basis.

**Gold Key:** A suppressed uncounseled post-indictment lineup does not automatically suppress an in-court identification; the courtroom ID may come in if the prosecution shows it rests on the witness's independent observation of the crime. **Silver Key:** In a two-yes/two-no array, do not stop at the result word. Test the reason attached to each result.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. Automatic suppression of all later identifications.** This choice is attractive because the lineup was unconstitutional and its identification was suppressed. The breaker is the word "automatically." **Gold Key:** The later courtroom identification can survive if the witness's recognition has an independent source, and Mary's detailed pre-lineup observation facts are built for that exception.

**B. Harmless because Mary testified live.** This choice reaches the right yes/no result, which makes it dangerous. The reason is the trap. Live testimony does not itself cleanse the lineup problem. The reason the identification may be permitted is that Mary can identify Daniel from what she saw during the robbery, not merely because she is available on the stand. **Silver Key:** The two "Yes" answers clash on the reason; choose the yes that explains source, not cure.

**D. In-court identifications always excluded.** This choice overreacts to the suggestiveness concern. Courtroom identifications can be challenged, but they are not always excluded. After a tainted lineup, the specific question is whether the in-court identification rests on an independent source.

### 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source: after a post-indictment uncounseled lineup, the lineup identification is suppressed, but an in-court identification may still be admitted if the prosecution can show an independent source for the witness's ability to identify the defendant.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), treats a post-indictment lineup as a critical stage and applies an independent-source inquiry to later in-court identifications. Wade identifies factors such as the witness's prior opportunity to observe the crime, discrepancies or consistency in the pre-lineup description, prior identifications or failures to identify, time lapse, and lineup conduct. *Gilbert v. California*, 388 U.S. 263 (1967), likewise distinguishes direct testimony about the illegal lineup from later in-court identifications and permits the state an opportunity to establish an independent source. The Library of Congress Constitution Annotated summary confirms the Wade-Gilbert rule for post-indictment lineups and right to counsel.
