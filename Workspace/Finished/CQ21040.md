The innkeeper then called the local constabulary and handed over the silver. Barnabas moves to suppress the silver under the Fourth Amendment. Should the silver be suppressed on Fourth Amendment grounds?

A) Yes, because the innkeeper searched a private satchel without a warrant.
B) Yes, because the constabulary later received the evidence.
C) No, because the search was purely private and not attributable to the government.
D) No, because satchels have no reasonable expectation of privacy.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
The innkeeper acted on his own initiative with no police request, encouragement, or agency relationship. The Fourth Amendment exclusionary rule does not apply to purely private searches.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
A: The Fourth Amendment restricts government action. A private actor acting independently is not bound by it.
B: Later receipt by police does not convert an already completed private search into government action.
D: Satchels can carry privacy interests. The reason suppression fails is lack of government action, not lack of privacy interest.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification
Fourth Amendment protection requires government action; private actors are not bound unless acting as government agents. The innkeeper's independent action does not trigger the exclusionary rule.

The variation preserves the original legal rigor by maintaining the core issue of government action required for Fourth Amendment applicability in a private search context.
