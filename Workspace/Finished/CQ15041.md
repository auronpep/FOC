## 1. Final Question
Twelve years ago, Martha bought a brick studio lot for a Scripture-printing shop from a seller. Martha financed the purchase price with a loan from Cedar Bank that was secured by a mortgage on the lot. Nine years ago, Martha paid the Cedar Bank loan in full. Five years ago, Martha sold the studio lot to Timothy by a general warranty deed with no exceptions. ## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
B is correct. Martha bought owner-side title coverage when she bought the studio lot. Gold Key: an owner's title policy can continue after the insured conveys the land when the insured is later liable on warranties of title in that conveyance; paying off the loan ends the lender's stake, not the owner's warranty-liability coverage. Silver Key: when two answers say Martha wins, choose the reason tied to the insurer's policy promise, not a side fact about the later claimant. ## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Titles, Deeds, and Conveyancing, and the verified outline code is `84040000`. The title-insurance rule is policy-based: an owner's policy protects the insured against covered defects, liens, and encumbrances existing at the policy date, and it can continue after the insured conveys title when the insured has liability for warranties given in that conveyance. The ALTA 2021 Owner's Policy of Title Insurance, Condition 2, states that coverage continues for an insured who retains an interest, owns a purchase-money mortgage obligation, or has liability for warranties given in a transfer of title; Condition 8 describes the policy as a contract of indemnity for actual monetary loss from matters insured against by the policy. See https://floir.gov/docs-sf/property-casualty-libraries/title-insurance-2021-forms/2021-alta-owners-policy.pdf. `Point of Rocks Ranch, LLC v. Sun Valley Title Insurance Co.`, 146 P.3d 677 (Idaho 2006), applies the same continuation-of-insurance language and recognizes warranty-liability coverage as one continuation path after conveyance. See https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/point-of-rocks-ranch-890705604. `Chicago Title Insurance Co. v. Cochran Investments, Inc.`, 602 S.W.3d 895 (Tex. 2020), confirms that deed warranties allocate liability for title failures, with special warranties limiting that liability and general warranties carrying broader title-risk liability. See https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/supreme-court/2020/18-0676.html. Research did not contradict the supplied official key. Martha is the owner-policy insured, the old mortgage was an undisclosed covered defect existing at the policy date, and Martha suffered actual monetary loss because her general warranty deed exposed her to Lydia's successful title-covenant claim.
