## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Martha leased a small lakeside cottage from Peter for one year so she could spend weekends writing hymn arrangements. Three months into the lease, a tornado destroyed the cottage completely. The lease said nothing about storms, casualty loss, rebuilding, or repair obligations. Peter told Martha that because she had possession under the lease, she had primary responsibility for the cottage and had to rebuild it. Martha refused. Peter sued to compel Martha to rebuild the cottage.

Should the court rule for Peter?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Must a tenant rebuild leased premises completely destroyed by a storm when the lease is silent?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. No, because a tenant's repair duty is limited to minor repairs needed to prevent waste.

B. Yes, because a tenant has a duty to repair while the lease is in effect.

C. No, because a tenant has no duty to make any repairs.

D. Yes, because the lease did not address storm damage.

### 5. Correct answer

A (original key C).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. A tenant has a duty not to commit waste and may have to make minor protective repairs, but that is not a duty to rebuild a cottage completely destroyed by a tornado. Gold Key: tenant repair duty prevents waste; it does not silently become a duty to reconstruct the premises after a casualty. Silver Key: when an answer says "repair," check whether the facts ask for a minor protective fix or a complete rebuild.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

B is attractive because tenants do have duties during the lease. The trap pushes a real repair duty past its boundary. A duty to prevent waste does not make the tenant the insurer of the building after a natural disaster.

C is attractive because Peter's demand is plainly too broad. The breaker is the word "any." A tenant can have some repair duties, especially minor repairs needed to avoid waste, so the absolute no-duty answer overcorrects.

D is attractive because the lease is silent, so a student may hunt for a default rule against Martha. Silence does not create a rebuilding covenant. The default duty is much narrower than Peter's requested remedy.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is unchanged: the landlord should lose because the tenant's default duty is not a duty to rebuild premises destroyed by casualty. The tested issue is tenant repair duty as part of landlord-tenant law and waste.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Restatement (Second) of Property: Landlord and Tenant section 13.1 recognizes tenant liability for waste-type harm to the leased property. That repair concept is limited; secondary discussions applying the Restatement explain that the tenant's duty is ordinary repair or waste prevention, not replacement or substantial reconstruction absent a lease covenant. See, for example, Western Assets Corp. v. Goodyear Tire & Rubber Co., 759 F.2d 595, 602 (7th Cir. 1985), discussed at https://www.greenbaumlaw.com/insights-publications-Repair-Covenants-The-Jekyll-and-Hyde-of-Leases.html. This is a lawyer-confirmation layer, not the student's first-pass reason.

Authority note: the web-confirmed authority for the precise no-rebuild formulation is partly secondary/case-discussion material, so attorney_review is true for the authority floor even though the exam rule and supplied key are not in doubt.

The variation preserves rigor because the answer array still tempts three overreads: repair means rebuild, silence means tenant pays, and no rebuild means no repair duty at all.
