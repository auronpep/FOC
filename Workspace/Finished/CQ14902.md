## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** Mary is not trying to prove the contents of a writing, recording, or photograph. She is describing a physical puppet she personally handled. **Gold Key:** the best evidence rule starts with a scope gate; physical objects are outside that gate. **Silver Key:** test the scope gate before walking down the exception ladder. Since the puppet is not the kind of item governed by the rule, Mary need not make a bad-faith showing, give a notice showing, or introduce a photograph.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells a procedural-sounding notice step. The breaker is that the notice idea is downstream of the original-document framework, and the puppet never enters that framework.

**C.** This is the dominant trap. The stem says the object was discarded, so the bad-faith-destruction prerequisite feels fact-engaged. **Gold Key:** that prerequisite matters only after the item is within the best-evidence/original-document rule. A hand-sewn puppet is outside the scope gate.

**D.** This choice sells the everyday idea that a photograph is the “best” proof of how something looked. The breaker is that the legal rule is not a general “best available evidence” rule; it does not require a photo of a physical object before a firsthand witness may describe it.
