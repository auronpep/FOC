### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. The issue is whether a conspiracy to commit a crime merges into the completed target crime under common law. The credited answer remains no.

Lawyer-confirmation layer: the governing rule is that conspiracy and the completed substantive offense are ordinarily separate crimes. In Callanan v. United States, 364 U.S. 587 (1961), the Supreme Court affirmed separate punishment for conspiracy and the completed offense under the Hobbs Act and described the distinct social danger of group criminal agreement. In Iannelli v. United States, 420 U.S. 770 (1975), the Court again stated that conspiracy and the completed offense are traditionally treated as separate crimes and that conspiracy generally does not merge into the substantive offense upon proof of completion, subject to narrow Wharton's Rule analysis not implicated by ordinary robbery. The supplied key is consistent with those authorities.
