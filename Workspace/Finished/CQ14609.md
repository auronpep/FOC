### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The source row supplies QID 14609, the original traffic-stop/passenger-exit fact pattern, the pick rates, and the official explanation that the correct answer is “No as to both” because pretext does not invalidate an objectively justified traffic stop and passengers may be ordered out during a lawful traffic stop. 

The right-answer legal rule is grounded in **Whren v. United States**, where the Supreme Court held that a temporary traffic detention based on probable cause to believe a traffic violation occurred does not violate the Fourth Amendment even if a reasonable officer would not have stopped the motorist absent another law-enforcement objective; the Court also stated that subjective intentions play no role in ordinary probable-cause Fourth Amendment analysis. ([Justia Law][1])

The passenger-exit rule is grounded in **Maryland v. Wilson**, where the Supreme Court held that an officer making a traffic stop may order passengers out of the car pending completion of the stop. The Court treated the passenger’s additional intrusion as minimal once the vehicle has already been lawfully stopped. ([Justia Law][2])
