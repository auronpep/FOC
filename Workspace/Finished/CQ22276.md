## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**C is correct.** Rape is a general-intent crime, but "general intent" still means the
defendant must intend the *act* — here, the act of intercourse. A person so intoxicated
that he does not realize he is engaging in intercourse lacks even that minimal intent, and
without it there can be no conviction.

> **Gold Key:** Rape requires intent to do the act of intercourse; intoxication deep enough
> that the defendant doesn't realize he is having intercourse negates that intent.
> **Silver Key:** Among four "so drunk" facts, only the one where the *defendant* is unaware
> of the *act itself* acquits — it is the single fact that destroys his own intent.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B (dominant trap).** It feels like a clean mens-rea defense: if Stephen honestly thought
Lydia was willing, surely he lacked the guilty mind for rape — and his drunkenness seems to
make the mistake forgivable. The breaker: rape is general intent, so a reckless or
criminally negligent mistake about consent still convicts, and voluntary intoxication is no
defense to a general-intent crime — the belief leaves the act fully intended.
> **Gold Key:** A drunken (even honest) belief in consent does not acquit a general-intent
> rape; only loss of intent to do the act does (choice C).

**A.** It mirrors the credited answer almost word-for-word — "so drunk she didn't realize
intercourse was happening" — and a student riding the momentum of the correct mechanic
grabs it. The breaker: this fact is about the *victim*, and a victim too intoxicated to know
what is happening cannot consent, which makes the intercourse *more* clearly rape, not less.
> **Silver Key:** This is a wrong-party clone — the mechanic acquits only when it kills the
> *defendant's* intent; attached to the victim it cuts for the prosecution.

**D.** The husband detail whispers "consent" and "mistaken identity," tempting the reader to
treat her changed state of mind as curing the encounter. The breaker: the change of mind
comes *after* intercourse has already begun, and rape is complete at penetration without
consent — a completed crime is not undone by later or mistaken consent.
> **Gold Key:** Rape completes at penetration; the victim's subsequent or mistaken consent
> cannot reach back and unmake it.

## 9. Black-letter verification + right-answer legal reasoning (lawyer-confirmation)

Common-law rape is the unlawful carnal knowledge (sexual intercourse) of a woman, not the
defendant's spouse, by force and without her consent. It is classified as a **general-intent**
crime: the prosecution need not prove a specific purpose beyond the intent to perform the
act of intercourse, and a reckless or criminally negligent mistake as to consent will
support conviction (LaFave, *Substantive Criminal Law* § 17.4). Because it is general
intent, **voluntary intoxication is no defense** to the consent element (Model Penal Code
§ 2.08(2); *People v. Hood*, 1 Cal.3d 444, 462 P.2d 370 (1969) (specific/general-intent line
governs availability of the intoxication defense); see also *Montana v. Egelhoff*, 518 U.S.
37 (1996) (a State may bar the voluntary-intoxication defense altogether)). General intent
nonetheless requires intent to do the proscribed *act*; intoxication so extreme that the
defendant does not realize he is engaging in intercourse negates that act-intent, which is
why **C** alone acquits. A victim's incapacity to consent (unconscious or intoxicated)
renders the intercourse without consent and therefore rape, defeating **A**; and rape is
complete upon penetration without consent, so the victim's later or mistaken consent does
not undo it, defeating **D** (Perkins, *Criminal Law*).

## Gold Key(s)

- **GK-CRIMINAL-GENINTENT-01** (distinction): Rape is general intent — the defendant need
  only intend the act of intercourse, and a reckless/negligent (or voluntarily intoxicated)
  mistake about consent does not exonerate; but he must intend the act itself, so
  intoxication that leaves him unaware he is having intercourse negates that intent and
  defeats the charge. *Unlocks:* B; identifies C. *Authority (lawyer-confirmation only):*
  MPC § 2.08(2); *People v. Hood*, 1 Cal.3d 444 (1969); LaFave § 17.4.
- **GK-CRIMINAL-CONSENT-02** (rule): A victim's incapacity to consent means the intercourse
  is without consent (more clearly rape, not less), and rape is complete at penetration, so
  later or mistaken consent cannot undo it. *Unlocks:* A and D. *Authority:* LaFave § 17.4;
  Perkins, *Criminal Law*.
