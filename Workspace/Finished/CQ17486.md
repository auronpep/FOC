### 1. Final question

After Paul is indicted for robbing Ruth's private Christian lampstand shop, police conduct a live lineup without notifying Paul's lawyer or obtaining a waiver. The witness identifies Paul at the lineup. Before the lineup, the witness had seen the robber only for one second from behind in a dim service alley and had given no useful description. At trial, the prosecution asks the witness to identify Paul in court.

Should the in-court identification be suppressed?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

After an uncounseled post-indictment lineup, should a later in-court identification be suppressed when the witness had only a weak pre-lineup view and lacks an independent source?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is the best answer. The lineup happened after indictment without counsel or waiver, so the lineup was unconstitutional. The later courtroom identification is not automatically saved. Gold Key: if the witness's courtroom identification is tainted by an uncounseled post-indictment lineup, the prosecution must prove an independent source; a one-second view from behind in a dim alley with no useful description is not enough. Silver Key: do not stop at the first suppressed item when the call asks about a later in-court identification.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A sells the lineup-only remedy. It correctly notices that the lineup ID is suppressible, but it cuts the remedy short. The Gold Key kills this trap: the courtroom ID can also be suppressed when it derives from the illegal lineup and lacks an independent source.

C sells a categorical reliability attack. The witness's identification is weak here, but the reason for suppression is not that all eyewitness evidence is inadmissible. The reason is specific taint from an uncounseled post-indictment lineup plus no independent source.

D sells the open-court shortcut. A witness does not get an automatic courtroom reset after a constitutional lineup violation. A courtroom identification can be excluded if it is not independently sourced.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule is unchanged. A post-indictment live lineup is a critical stage for Sixth Amendment purposes, and conducting it without counsel or waiver violates the right to counsel. A later in-court identification by the same witness is admissible only if the prosecution establishes that the courtroom identification rests on an independent source rather than on the unconstitutional lineup. When the witness's pre-lineup observation was too weak to supply that source, the in-court identification is suppressible.

Authority floor: United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/388/218/, frames the issue as whether courtroom identifications must be excluded after a post-indictment lineup conducted without counsel, and requires an independent-source inquiry. Gilbert v. California, 388 U.S. 263 (1967), https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep388/usrep388263/usrep388263.pdf, applies the Wade rule to uncounseled post-indictment lineups and in-court identifications. DOJ Criminal Resource Manual section 239, https://www.justice.gov/archives/jm/criminal-resource-manual-239-power-order-lineup-right-counsel, confirms that the Sixth Amendment lineup right applies at or after formal adversary proceedings. The supplied key matches these authorities.
