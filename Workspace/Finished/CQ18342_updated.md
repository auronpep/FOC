# CQ18342 - Criminal Law - Divergent Variant

## Pass-1 Report

**Final Question:**
A youth pastor, Pastor Aaron, is indicted for embezzlement of church funds. During the pre-trial phase, police obtain a court order requiring him to provide a handwriting exemplar for comparison against church donation records. Pastor Aaron is not provided counsel during the exemplar production. He argues the handwriting sample must be suppressed because counsel was absent. What is the best answer?

**Distilled Core Question:**
Is the compelled production of a handwriting exemplar by an indicted defendant, without counsel present, a Sixth Amendment violation?

**Final Choices (Post-Shuffle):**
A: The sample is not suppressible because providing a handwriting exemplar is not a critical stage requiring counsel.
B: The sample must be suppressed because every post-indictment police procedure requires counsel.
C: The sample must be suppressed because handwriting is testimonial.
D: The sample is admissible only if Pastor Aaron chose the sentences himself.

**Correct Answer:** A

**Letter Map:**
A: Correct
B: Distractor (Mechanic: Misapplication of Sixth Amendment scope)
C: Distractor (Mechanic: Misclassification of evidence type)
D: Distractor (Mechanic: Irrelevant condition)

**Right-Answer Explanation:**
Providing a handwriting exemplar is considered physical evidence rather than a "critical stage" of the prosecution. Therefore, the Sixth Amendment right to counsel is not triggered by the absence of an attorney during this event.

**Wrong-Answer Explanations:**
B: The Sixth Amendment right to counsel attaches only at critical stages of prosecution where the absence of counsel could result in the loss of a fair trial. Exemplar production is not such a stage.
C: Handwriting exemplars are generally considered non-testimonial physical characteristics, similar to fingerprints or blood samples, and do not fall under the privilege against self-incrimination or the need for counsel as testimonial content does.
D: The requirement for counsel does not depend on the selection of text for the exemplar. The nature of the event itself (exemplar collection) is the determining factor.

**Black-Letter Verification with Authority:**
Compelling a defendant to provide a physical exemplar, such as handwriting, is not a critical stage requiring counsel, even after formal charges have been filed. *Gilbert v. California*, 388 U.S. 263 (1967).

**Rigor/Difficulty Note:**
Core. Requires application of Sixth Amendment "critical stage" doctrine to specific types of evidence collection.

**C3 Walkthrough:**
*   **CUT:** The core legal issue is whether exemplar collection is a "critical stage."
*   **CLASH:** The clash is between the defendant's right to counsel at all critical stages and the limited scope of this right regarding physical evidence collection.
*   **CALL:** The call requires identification of the correct doctrinal limitation (not a critical stage).

**Divergence Audit:**
*   Original Stem Summary: Indicted defendant compelled to provide handwriting exemplar without counsel, Sixth Amendment claim.
*   Variant Stem Summary: Indicted youth pastor compelled to provide handwriting exemplar without counsel, Sixth Amendment claim.
*   Divergence: Contextual (Pastor/Church funds vs Forgery).

**Review Truth:**
A handwriting exemplar is physical evidence, not a critical stage of prosecution requiring counsel.
