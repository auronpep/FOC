**Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)**

B is correct. The Fourth Amendment generally requires a warrant for a search, but a warrantless search is valid if consented to by a third party with authority to consent. A guest in a hotel — or, by the same logic, a guest at a church retreat center — is entitled to exclusive possession of the room during the stay. The retreat center director, like a hotel clerk, does not have the power to consent to a search of a guest's room, even if the guest is behind on the retreat fee. The motion in B would be granted.

Silver Key: Third-party consent is not all-or-nothing. A car owner can consent to a search of the car over a passenger's objection (the owner has authority over the chattel). But a hotel or retreat center operator CANNOT consent to a search of a guest's room (the guest has exclusive possession during the stay). The key distinction: exclusive possession of the space being searched.

**Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer**

Tested rule: The Fourth Amendment prohibits unreasonable searches and seizures. A warrantless search is presumptively unreasonable, but several exceptions apply: (1) consent by a party with authority, (2) search incident to a lawful arrest, (3) inventory search of an impounded vehicle, (4) exigent circumstances, (5) plain view, (6) automobile exception, and (7) Terry stop-and-frisk. For third-party consent, the third party must have authority over the property being searched. A car owner has authority over the car and can consent to a search over a passenger's objection (United States v. Matlock, 415 U.S. 164 (1974)). A hotel clerk, however, does NOT have authority to consent to a search of a registered guest's room because the guest has exclusive possession of the room during the stay (Stoner v. California, 376 U.S. 483 (1964)). The same logic applies to a church retreat center operator: the guest has exclusive possession of the room during the stay, and the operator cannot consent to a search even if the guest is behind on the retreat fee.
