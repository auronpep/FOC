### 6. Full right-answer explanation
Choice C tracks the rule the item is really testing. A printed newspaper or periodical authenticates itself, so the prosecution does not need the article's author or a publisher representative just to clear the authenticity step. `Gold Key:` FRE 902(6) takes newspapers and periodicals out of the ordinary "bring in a witness to identify it" workflow. `Silver Key:` when one answer names a self-authentication rule and the competing answers merely demand extra foundation, follow the rule that removes the whole objection.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations
**A.** This is attractive because "party-opponent admission" is a familiar evidence phrase, and students often lunge at a hearsay label when they see the prosecution offering an article against the defendant. The stem never says Lydia made the article's statement, adopted it, or was quoted in it, so that doctrine is a neighboring issue rather than the authentication issue this question asks you to solve.

**B.** This sells the instinct that a published writing must come in through some formal newspaper-office or publisher witness. The breaker is that Rule 902(6) already supplies the authentication foundation for printed newspapers and periodicals, so an official designee is an invented extra requirement.

**D.** This is the dominant trap because author testimony sounds like classic, sensible authentication. `Gold Key:` the whole point of the periodical rule is that you do not need the author to authenticate printed material that purports to be a newspaper or periodical, so this answer denies admission on a requirement the Rules do not impose.

### 8. Full black-letter verification with authority
The credited outcome remains unchanged. The row frames the fight as an authentication question, and on that question the governing rule is FRE 902(6): "Printed material purporting to be a newspaper or periodical" is self-authenticating. FRE 901(a) supplies the ordinary baseline that the proponent must offer evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims, but Rule 902 creates exceptions to that ordinary foundation requirement. Because newspapers and periodicals sit inside Rule 902(6), the prosecution does not need the article's author or an official publisher designee merely to authenticate the printed article. That is why choice C is the only answer that states the correct rule. Choice A still fails because the stem does not supply the facts needed to make the article a party-opponent statement. Authority checked against FRE 902(6) and FRE 901(a). Sources: Cornell LII Rule 902, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_902; Cornell LII Rule 901, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901.
