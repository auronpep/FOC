### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**A is correct.** Solicitation is complete when the defendant intentionally asks, hires, commands, or encourages another person to commit a crime. Stephen asked Daniel to arrange a killing. That request completed common-law solicitation even though Daniel was an undercover officer and even though Stephen later canceled before payment.

**Gold Key:** Common-law solicitation is complete at the criminal request. Later withdrawal may matter under some renunciation statutes or MPC-style rules, but it does not erase the completed common-law solicitation.

**Silver Key:** Do not import attempt language into solicitation. Solicitation asks whether the criminal request was made, not whether substantial steps toward the target crime occurred.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B. acquitted, because he withdrew before payment and commission of the act.**
This choice sells the attractive idea that a timely change of heart should wipe out an inchoate crime. The breaker is the Gold Key: common-law solicitation was already complete when Stephen asked Daniel to arrange the murder.

**C. convicted, because the undercover officer agreed to commit the offense.**
This is the dominant trap. It treats Daniel's apparent agreement as the event that creates liability, but solicitation does not require acceptance by the person solicited.

**D. acquitted, because no substantial acts were performed.**
This choice borrows the attempt frame and asks whether enough steps were taken toward murder. The Silver Key steers off it: solicitation requires the request itself, not a substantial step toward the completed crime.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

Legal-confirmation layer only: the common-law bar rule is that solicitation is complete once a person, with the required intent, requests or encourages another to commit a crime. No agreement by the solicited person is required, and the target crime need not be attempted or completed. Model Penal Code section 5.02 states the solicitation framework and also recognizes a renunciation defense when the actor, after soliciting another, persuades the person not to commit the crime or otherwise prevents it under circumstances showing complete and voluntary renunciation.

The source explanation credits conviction because the request happened before withdrawal. The source note also flags a possible MPC renunciation wrinkle if "called the plan off" is treated as stopping the solicited party. This packet therefore preserves the official common-law outcome while marking `bank_validation_verdict: FORK_OR_SPLIT` for the MPC renunciation ambiguity.
