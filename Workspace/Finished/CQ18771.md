# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

Esther owned a small lakeside cottage. To pay for a custom harp she wanted for a worship-music recording, Esther borrowed $80,000 from Barnabas Bank and gave Barnabas a note and mortgage on the cottage for that amount. Barnabas did not record the mortgage.

Six years later, to launch a Scripture-memory stationery business, Esther borrowed $35,000 from Lydia Finance and gave Lydia a note and deed of trust on the same cottage for that amount. Lydia recorded the deed of trust immediately.

Three years later, Esther defaulted on both notes. In a foreclosure proceeding brought jointly by Barnabas and Lydia, a court approved the sale of Esther's cottage for $80,000. The jurisdiction has a recording statute that provides, "No transfer of an interest in real property shall be good against subsequent transferees for consideration and without notice unless it is recorded."

If Barnabas and Lydia both claim a right to the foreclosure-sale proceeds, whose claim should receive priority?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

An earlier mortgage was not recorded, and a later deed of trust was taken for value without notice and recorded before foreclosure; under a notice recording statute, which lender has priority to the foreclosure proceeds?

## 3. Final Answer Choices

A. Lydia Finance, because Barnabas Bank had constructive notice of the deed of trust in favor of Lydia Finance.

B. Barnabas Bank, because the priority of real-property security interests is not governed by recording statutes.

C. Lydia Finance, because the mortgage held by Barnabas Bank was not recorded.

D. Barnabas Bank, because the mortgage held by Barnabas Bank was a purchase-money mortgage.

## 5. Correct Answer

C. Lydia Finance, because the mortgage held by Barnabas Bank was not recorded. Original key: A.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

C is correct. The statute protects a later transferee who gives value and takes without notice of the earlier unrecorded interest. Lydia gave value, took a deed of trust, immediately recorded it, and the facts do not show that Lydia knew of Barnabas's earlier mortgage when Lydia took its interest. Barnabas's mortgage was not recorded, so Barnabas cannot use that unrecorded mortgage to outrank Lydia under the statute.

Gold Key: A notice recording statute asks whether the later taker paid value and lacked notice of the earlier unrecorded interest when the later taker acquired the interest.

Silver Key: When two answers give the same winner, separate the winner from the reason. The winning party is Lydia, but only the reason tied to Barnabas's unrecorded mortgage answers the call.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because "constructive notice" sounds like the recording-system word the question wants. The breaker is direction and timing: the question is not whether Barnabas later had notice of Lydia's recorded deed of trust; the statute protects Lydia only if Lydia was a subsequent transferee for value without notice of Barnabas's prior unrecorded mortgage. Silver Key: split the same-winner pair by asking whose notice matters at the acquisition moment.

B is attractive because mortgages feel like a special creditor-priority system outside ordinary deed recording. The breaker is that a mortgage and deed of trust are real-property security interests, and the given statute covers a "transfer of an interest in real property." The choice overclaims that recording statutes do not govern this priority fight.

D is attractive because purchase-money mortgages can receive special priority, and the first loan came first in time. The breaker is the Gold Key for the purchase-money trap: a purchase-money mortgage secures credit used to acquire the mortgaged real estate. Esther borrowed from Barnabas to buy a custom harp, not to buy the cottage.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. The foreclosure-sale proceeds are claimed by competing real-property security holders, so the proceeds follow the priority of the security interests. Under Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages section 7.4, foreclosure surplus and terminated interests are handled by priority; the American Law Institute describes the Restatement as covering real property used as security and including chapters on priorities and foreclosure. See https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-third/property-mortgages.

The operative priority rule here comes from the hypothetical notice recording statute in the stem: an unrecorded transfer of an interest in real property is not good against a subsequent transferee for consideration and without notice. A later deed-of-trust beneficiary is a real-property security transferee for value. Because Barnabas did not record before Lydia took and recorded, and because the facts do not show Lydia had actual or constructive notice of Barnabas's mortgage when Lydia took, Lydia has priority to the proceeds.

Research did not reveal a contradiction with the supplied official key. No hotlist doctrinal update is implicated.
