### 1. Final question

Ruth sues Daniel over the sale of a restored hand press used for printing hymn-study booklets. Ruth offers a printed screenshot of a message thread in which Daniel allegedly admitted that he still owed the final payment for the press. The court excludes the screenshot on authentication grounds. Outside the jury's hearing, Ruth's lawyer states on the record the date of the message, the sender, the recipient, and the exact admission the message contains. Daniel argues that Ruth failed to preserve error because the phone containing the original message thread was not produced during the offer of proof. Is Daniel correct?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Ruth preserved the exclusion issue by putting the substance of the excluded message on the record outside the jury's hearing. The point of an offer of proof is to let the court and a reviewing court know what the excluded evidence would have shown. `Gold Key:` the offer of proof is a record-preservation device, not the admission of the document itself, so the offer does not have to satisfy the original-writing rule. `Silver Key:` keep the call on preservation. The screenshot's ultimate admissibility is a separate question from whether Ruth made an adequate record after exclusion.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

- **A.** This choice is attractive because students may think a lawyer's summary is too informal when the excluded evidence is a message. The breaker is the absolute word "never." Rule 103 allows the substance of excluded evidence to be made known by an offer of proof, and the court can direct a different form if needed.
- **B.** This choice pulls toward the duplicate-evidence lane, and screenshots can make students start fighting about originals and copies. The breaker is the call: Daniel is arguing preservation, not whether the screenshot should ultimately come into evidence. `Silver Key:` answer the preservation question before chasing the duplicate rule.
- **D.** This is the dominant trap because the excluded item is a writing and the answer sounds like careful best-evidence discipline. The breaker is the `Gold Key:` an offer of proof tells the court what the excluded evidence would have shown; it is not itself the document being admitted to prove its contents.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source item. Federal Rule of Evidence 103(a)(2) preserves a claim of error after evidence is excluded when the party informs the court of the evidence's substance by an offer of proof, unless the substance was apparent from context. Rule 103(c) also recognizes the court's control over the character or form of the offer of proof, including question-and-answer form, which confirms that the offer is a record-making device rather than admission of the excluded item itself. Rule 1002 requires an original writing, recording, or photograph only when a party is proving the content of that item. Here, Ruth was not admitting the message through the offer of proof; she was making a record of what the excluded message would have shown. Daniel's original-writing objection to preservation therefore fails, and the official key remains correct.
