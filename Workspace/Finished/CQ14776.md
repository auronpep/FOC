### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. The prosecutor is not trying to admit the contents of the detention files. The prosecutor is using a records witness to prove that a diligent search found no public record of the defendant being held there. Gold Key: FRE 803(10) permits testimony, or a Rule 902 certification, that a diligent search failed to disclose a public record when offered to prove the record does not exist or that a recorded matter did not occur. Silver Key: when the stem says the search found no entry, the lane is absence of a public record, not a summary of many records.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because the file set sounds large and the witness is describing a search through many records. The breaker is the purpose of the evidence: the testimony proves no booking entry was found, not the summarized contents of voluminous writings. Silver Key: a "no record found" fact points to an absence exception, not a Rule 1006 summary.

B is attractive because the testimony reports what the public records did not contain, so a student may stop at the hearsay label. The breaker is the Gold Key: FRE 803(10) supplies a hearsay exception for testimony that a diligent search failed to disclose the public record or recorded matter.

D is the dominant trap because it sounds like the original-document rule or a completeness instinct: if records matter, the records should come in. The breaker is the rule's own method of proof. Gold Key: FRE 803(10) allows the absence to be proved by testimony or certification; it does not require offering the full booking file first.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule is unchanged. The source item and the variation both ask whether a government records witness may testify that a diligent search of public detention records disclosed no record supporting the defendant's alibi.

Authority floor: Federal Rule of Evidence 803(10) covers the absence of a public record. It allows testimony, or a Rule 902 certification, that a diligent search failed to disclose a public record or statement when offered to prove that the record or statement does not exist or that a matter did not occur or exist if a public office regularly kept such records. Because the variation uses live testimony from the records supervisor, the criminal-case notice rule for certifications is not doing the work. Rule 1006 is different: it permits summaries, charts, or calculations to prove the contents of voluminous admissible writings, recordings, or photographs that cannot be conveniently examined in court. This witness is not summarizing the contents of the booking records; she is proving the absence of a record.
