## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final Question

Lydia paid for a Saturday Scripture-lettering workshop at Martha's privately owned art studio. After the class ended, Lydia walked out of the studio and stepped onto the city-owned plaza. A stranger who had never attended the class, had no connection to the studio, and had caused no earlier trouble there suddenly knocked Lydia down and hurried away. The studio had not promised or provided plaza security and had no control over the stranger.

Lydia sues the studio for negligence for failing to prevent the shove. Is Lydia likely to establish that the studio owed a negligence duty to control the stranger?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Did the studio owe a duty to control a stranger when it had no relationship with him, no undertaking to provide security, no prior warning, and no control over the public plaza?

### 3. Final Answer Choices

A. No, because the studio had no recognized relationship or undertaking that made the stranger's sudden act controllable.

B. No, because a business can never owe a duty arising from another person's intentional act.

C. Yes, because merchants must stop every sudden criminal act within sight of their doorway.

D. Yes, because the studio is vicariously liable for the stranger's tort once Lydia was still nearby.

### 5. Correct Answer

A (original key B).

### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

A is correct. The answer stays tied to the facts that matter: no relationship with the stranger, no assumed security role, no earlier disturbance, and no control over the public plaza. Gold Key: a business is not an insurer against every random third-party act; duty to control or protect requires a recognized relationship, an undertaking, or conduct that made the risk legally controllable. Silver Key: cut the two absolute answers first, then ask which remaining answer tracks the relationship-and-undertaking facts in the stem.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

B is attractive because it reaches the same no-duty result. The breaker is the word "never." Gold Key: duties for third-party acts can arise through a special relationship, an undertaking, or conduct that creates or controls the risk, so this answer wins the result while losing the rule.

C is the dominant trap because it sounds like the business must protect paying participants near its entrance. The breaker is the absolute "every sudden criminal act." The stem gives no prior disturbance, no security undertaking, no relationship with the stranger, and no control over the plaza, so the merchant-insurer version overclaims the duty rule.

D is attractive because vicarious liability is a familiar business-liability phrase. The breaker is the frame. The stranger was not an employee, agent, or person acting for the studio, so vicarious liability does not answer whether the studio itself owed a direct duty to control a third person.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: a defendant generally has no affirmative duty to control a third person to prevent harm to another absent a special relationship, an undertaking, or conduct/risk that makes the harm legally controllable. The credited answer is correct because the transformed facts deny every duty trigger: the stranger had no connection to the studio, there was no prior disturbance or notice, the studio did not undertake plaza security, and the plaza was city-owned rather than controlled by the studio.

Authority floor: Restatement (Second) of Torts Section 315 states the no-duty-to-control baseline with special-relationship exceptions. Restatement (Third) of Torts: Liability for Physical and Emotional Harm Sections 37 and 40-43 organize the modern affirmative-duty framework around risks not created by the actor, special relationships, and undertakings. Research sources used: H2O Open Casebook page for Restatement (Second) Section 315, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/4643-tobia-torts-2022/resources/8.3.2-restatement-2d-315-general-principle/; Restatement (Third) table of contents showing Sections 37 and 40-43, https://euro.ecom.cmu.edu/program/law/08-732/Types/Restatement3dTOC.pdf; ALI discussion noting the Section 315 no-duty principle, https://www.ali.org/news/articles/us-supreme-court-cites-torts-2d-and-torts-3d.
