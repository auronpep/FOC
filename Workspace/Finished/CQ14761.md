# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

At an autumn harvest festival, Hannah stopped at a woodworking booth run by Barnabas. A tall display ladder he had leaned against the booth frame slipped and dropped onto her foot, breaking two of her toes. Barnabas rushed over and said to her, "Please, let me give you this $250 — I feel just awful that this happened to you." Hannah has since sued Barnabas for the personal injuries she suffered. At trial, Hannah offers evidence of Barnabas's statement. Is the statement admissible?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

Right after accidentally injuring someone — and before any claim is disputed — a person apologizes and offers the injured person money. The injured person sues and offers that statement against the speaker. Admissible?

- **B.** Yes, as the statement of a party-opponent.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**B is correct.** Barnabas's words are his own, made out of court, and Hannah offers them *against* him. A statement offered against an opposing party that the party made himself is defined as not hearsay — a statement of an opposing party (the classic "admission by a party-opponent"). His contrition plus a money offer tends to show he was at fault, so it is relevant to liability, and nothing excludes it.

**Gold Key:** FRE 408 protects compromise offers only once a claim is *disputed* as to validity or amount. Here the words came moments after the injury, before any claim existed or was contested — so 408 never attaches, and the statement comes in as a party's own admission.

**Silver Key:** In a 2×2 "Yes/No × rationale" array, let the *rationale* do the cutting: the only choice that admits the evidence on a ground that needs no extra fact and no exception-fit is the party-opponent ground. Lock it and don't get pulled by the sympathetic "No" answers.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A — Yes, as a present sense impression.** This choice rewards a student hunting for *some* hearsay exception to justify admission, and "he said it right after it happened" pattern-matches to the immediacy in FRE 803(1). The breaker: a present sense impression must *describe or explain* the event or condition perceived; an apology and an offer of money describe nothing about how the ladder fell — so the doctrine is not in play, even though the timing looks right.

**C — No, because it is not relevant to the issue of liability.** This choice sells the instinct that an apology is just politeness, not proof of fault. The breaker: relevance only requires a *tendency* to make a fact more or less probable (Rule 401), and "I feel awful, let me pay you" plainly tends to show Barnabas thought he was responsible — so it clears the low relevance bar easily.

**D — No, because it was an offer of compromise.** *(Dominant trap.)* This is the strongest pull: money + contrition looks exactly like a settlement gesture, and students know settlement offers are excluded. The breaker: **Gold Key** — FRE 408 reaches offers to compromise a *disputed* claim, and at the moment Barnabas spoke there was no dispute at all; he was conceding, not negotiating, so 408 does not apply and his words stay in as a party admission.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are **unchanged** from the original: a party's own out-of-court statement, offered against him, is admissible as a statement of an opposing party, and is not knocked out by the compromise rule because no claim was yet in dispute.

- **Right answer:** FRE 801(d)(2)(A) — a statement offered against an opposing party that was made by the party in an individual capacity is not hearsay. Relevance satisfied under FRE 401 (any tendency to prove a consequential fact).
- **Dominant-trap rule:** FRE 408 — bars compromise offers/statements only when made to compromise a claim that is *disputed* as to validity or amount; a pre-dispute apology and payment offer fall outside it.
- **Distractor A rule:** FRE 803(1) — present sense impression requires a statement *describing or explaining* an event/condition made during or immediately after perceiving it.

Authority (lawyer-confirmation layer only): FRE 801(d)(2)(A); FRE 408(a); FRE 803(1); FRE 401. All are real, current rule numbers; no doctrinal-currency hotlist item is implicated. Verdict: **PASS** — C3 residual equals the official key.
