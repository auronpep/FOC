## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**C is correct.** The commerce power reaches almost any economic activity that could
conceivably affect — even secondarily — the stream of interstate commerce. Honey is
manufactured, distributed, and sold nationwide; a statute aimed at the manufacture,
distribution, and sale of that product across the country sits squarely inside the
commerce power, and Congress need only have a rational basis to conclude the regulated
class of activity affects interstate commerce. **Gold Key:** *any nationwide commercial
market — the making, moving, or selling of goods — is regulable under the Commerce
Clause; aggregate economic activity with a substantial effect on interstate commerce is
enough.* **Silver Key:** *"most easily justify" tells you to pick the broadest catch-all
power, not to hunt for a clever narrow fit — for commercial regulation that default is
always Commerce.*

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

- **A — The General Welfare Clause (dominant trap).** The stem foregrounds "public-health
  problems," so the phrase "general welfare" feels like a direct, purpose-matched grant of
  power. The breaker: the General Welfare Clause is not an independent source of
  regulatory authority — it only qualifies Congress's power to tax and spend. **Gold Key:**
  *the General Welfare Clause lets Congress tax and spend for the general welfare; it does
  not grant a freestanding power to regulate.*
- **B — Fourteenth Amendment enforcement power.** "Enforcement power" sounds like broad
  federal muscle to attack a nationwide problem, and the health-and-safety framing feels
  rights-adjacent. The breaker: the §5 enforcement power reaches only *state* action, and
  this statute regulates private (and public) manufacturers and sellers, so the power
  cannot stretch to cover it. **Gold Key:** *Section 5 reaches only state action — never
  purely private conduct.*
- **D — The spending power.** The spending power is genuinely broad, and a student who
  knows Congress funds national programs reaches for it reflexively. The breaker: the
  spending power operates only by attaching conditions to the receipt of federal funds —
  there is no federal money or funding condition anywhere in this statute, so the power is
  not in play. **Gold Key:** *the spending power regulates only by conditions on federal
  dollars; no funds, no spending-power hook.*

## 9. Black-letter verification + legal reasoning (lawyer-confirmation layer)

**Tested rule (unchanged):** Congress's Commerce Clause power (U.S. Const. art. I, §8,
cl. 3) extends to the channels and instrumentalities of interstate commerce and to
intrastate activities that, in the aggregate, substantially affect interstate commerce,
reviewed for a rational basis. A comprehensive federal statute regulating the manufacture,
distribution, and sale of a product traded nationwide is the paradigm commerce-power
exercise. Authority: *Wickard v. Filburn*, 317 U.S. 111 (1942); *Gonzales v. Raich*,
545 U.S. 1 (2005); *United States v. Lopez*, 514 U.S. 549 (1995) (economic-activity
limit, satisfied here).

- **General Welfare Clause** is not a standalone regulatory grant; conventional doctrine
  reads art. I, §8, cl. 1 as conferring only the spending power. Authority: *United States
  v. Butler*, 297 U.S. 1 (1936).
- **Spending power** conditions federal funds and is not implicated absent a funding
  condition. Authority: *South Dakota v. Dole*, 483 U.S. 203 (1987).
- **Fourteenth Amendment §5** enforcement power reaches only state action and is limited by
  congruence-and-proportionality. Authority: *City of Boerne v. Flores*, 521 U.S. 507
  (1997); *Civil Rights Cases*, 109 U.S. 3 (1883).
