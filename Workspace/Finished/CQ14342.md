## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**Gold Key:** A federal excise tax that appears to raise revenue is usually valid under Article I, Section 8 when geographically uniform; a high rate or deterrent economic effect does not by itself make it unconstitutional.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are preserved. Article I, Section 8, Clause 1 gives Congress power to lay and collect taxes, duties, imposts, and excises to pay debts and provide for the common defense and general welfare, with duties, imposts, and excises required to be uniform throughout the United States. The official Constitution Annotated overview states that this clause gives Congress broad federal taxing authority. Source: https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artI-S8-C1-1-1/ALDE_00013387/

The uniformity requirement for an indirect tax is geographic uniformity, not equal economic effect across activities or states. Constitution Annotated summarizes that indirect taxes satisfy uniformity when the tax operates with the same force wherever the subject is found. Source: https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artI-S8-C1-1-3/ALDE_00013389/. Fernandez v. Wiener, 326 U.S. 340 (1945), likewise treats the excise-tax uniformity requirement as geographic. Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/326/340/
