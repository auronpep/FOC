### 6. Student black-letter-law answer explanation
C is correct. The Supreme Court can review a final state-court judgment when the case is at the highest state court in which a decision could be had. That does not always mean the court named "state supreme court." If state law gives Daniel no appeal from the city municipal court, that municipal court is the last available state court for this case. Because Daniel raised a federal constitutional objection and no state appellate court can review the judgment, the Supreme Court has jurisdiction to review the municipal-court decision.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation
C is correct because federal law looks to the highest state court in which review could be had, not simply to the court's formal rank. Daniel lost in a city municipal court, and state law permits no appeal from that court's judgments. That makes the municipal court the final available state court for this case. Gold Key: for state-court certiorari, "highest court" means the highest state court available in that case; a trial-level municipal court can qualify when no state appellate route exists. Silver Key: when the choices fight over court labels, ask whether any higher state court can still decide the case.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because it sounds like the formal hierarchy rule: Supreme Court, federal courts, and the state's top court. The breaker is the statutory phrase "highest court of a State in which a decision could be had." Article III does not itself limit review to named state supreme courts, and a lower state court can be the highest available court when state law provides no state appeal.

B is attractive because Daniel did raise a federal constitutional objection, and Supreme Court review of state judgments requires a federal-law issue. The breaker is that a federal issue alone does not answer the timing and court-level question. Gold Key: the judgment must be final from the highest state court in which review could be had, so if a state appeal were available the case would not yet be ready for Supreme Court review.

D is attractive because state law controls whether Daniel can appeal within the state court system. The breaker is that state law does not have to authorize Supreme Court review. State law only tells us whether another state court can still hear the case; Congress supplies the Supreme Court's review authority.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Constitutional Law, Judicial Review, Jurisdiction of Courts, and the verified outline code is `43020200`.

Article III gives the Supreme Court appellate jurisdiction subject to exceptions and regulations made by Congress. Congress currently regulates Supreme Court review of state-court judgments through 28 U.S.C. Section 1257(a), which allows review by writ of certiorari from final judgments rendered by the highest state court in which a decision could be had when the case presents a qualifying federal question. Official source: https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/1257

The Constitution Annotated explanation confirms the operational rule for this item: the relevant state court is often the state's court of last resort, but it may be an intermediate appellate court or a trial court when the judgment is final under state law and no state appellate court can review it. Official source: https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/artIII-S2-C2-5/ALDE_00001223/

Those authorities confirm the official key. Daniel raised a federal constitutional objection in the municipal court, the city municipal court entered the judgment, and state law provides no state appeal. For Section 1257 purposes, the municipal court is the highest state court in which a decision could be had. The credited outcome is unchanged: the Supreme Court has jurisdiction to review the municipal-court decision.
