### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Peter’s statement shows willingness to go forward with a sale on the known price-and-delivery terms. **Gold Key:** When the earlier buyer letter makes delivery the required way to accept, a later statement that “I’ll bring it tomorrow” is not itself the invited delivery; it is best treated as a new offer to complete the deal. **Silver Key:** Read the answer array as four legal labels for the same sentence. Cut the labels that require an act already completed, then keep the label that fits a future-tense commitment.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. Ratification trap.** This choice sells the idea that the earlier exchange already produced an acceptance that can later be confirmed. The breaker is that Lydia’s earlier letter did not create an acceptance waiting to be ratified; it made performance by delivery the operative route.

**B. Commencement trap.** This choice is attractive because Peter is talking about carrying the puppet set and even names the cargo tricycle he plans to use. The breaker is grammar-level: “I’ll bring it tomorrow” is future-tense planning, not delivery or tender now.

**D. Acceptance trap.** This choice is the dominant doctrinal pull because Peter’s words sound like agreement. **Gold Key:** If the invited acceptance is delivery, a promise to deliver later is not the invited acceptance; it leaves Peter’s words as an offer, not an acceptance.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The credited outcome is unchanged: the seller’s statement is classified as an offer, not acceptance, ratification, or commencement of performance. The Restatement defines an offer as a manifestation of willingness to enter a bargain that justifies the other party in understanding that assent will conclude it. ([Open Casebook][1]) Restatement §30 recognizes that an offer may require acceptance by promise or by performance, and Restatement §50 defines acceptance as assent made in the manner invited or required by the offer. ([Verkerke Contracts One][2]) Where an offer invites acceptance by performance and not by promise, Restatement §45 treats tender or beginning of the invited performance as the operative step; the duty remains conditional on completion or tender of the invited performance. ([Open Casebook][3]) Because this variation uses goods, UCC §2-206 is also checked: it invites reasonable modes of acceptance unless the language or circumstances unambiguously indicate otherwise, and the buyer’s delivery-conditioned wording is the controlling indication here. ([Legal Information Institute][4])
