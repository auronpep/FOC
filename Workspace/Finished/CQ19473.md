### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning
The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row. Conspiracy requires an agreement to commit an unlawful act and the required intent; in most MBE framing, once the conspiracy is complete, later withdrawal is not a defense to the conspiracy itself. It may terminate liability for later acts of co-conspirators and may matter to limitations, but it does not negate the prior agreement.

Authority layer: in *Smith v. United States*, 568 U.S. 106 (2013), the Supreme Court treated withdrawal as an affirmative defense that does not negate an element of conspiracy and explained that withdrawal terminates liability for post-withdrawal acts while the defendant remains guilty of conspiracy. Source reviewed: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/568/106/ and https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/11-8976.
