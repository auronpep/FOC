Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer:
The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The Fifth Amendment provides that private property may not be taken for public use without just compensation. United States v. Causby, 328 U.S. 256 (1946), is the governing low-overflight authority. There, frequent low military flights over a chicken farm, with noise and glare that killed chickens and destroyed the practical use of the property as a commercial chicken farm, could constitute a taking even though the government did not seize the ground.

The same rule controls Lydia's apiary. The Air Force is a government actor. Its repeated low helicopter passes directly and immediately interfered with Lydia's ability to use her land as an apiary and forced that use to close. The compensable-taking theory does not depend on the private ownership of the heliport, does not require destruction of every possible land use, and does not rest on Lydia owning all airspace above the farm.
