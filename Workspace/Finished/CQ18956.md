## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

After indictment for taking a donor's camera from a private retreat center, police schedule a live lineup. Officers tell Naomi that she has the right to have counsel present at the lineup and that the lineup can wait until counsel arrives. Naomi says she understands and signs a written waiver. The victim identifies Naomi.

Is the identification automatically excluded because counsel was absent?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Does a valid waiver prevent automatic exclusion of a post-indictment live-lineup identification when counsel is absent?

### 3. Final answer choices

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Gold Key: the post-indictment lineup right to counsel is real, but it can be waived if the waiver is knowing, voluntary, and intelligent. Naomi was told counsel could attend and that the lineup could wait for counsel. She said she understood and signed a written waiver. On those facts, the identification is not automatically excluded merely because counsel was absent.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A pulls because trial is the most familiar counsel setting. The breaker is that waivers are not confined to trial; counsel rights at critical stages can be waived if the waiver is valid.

B pulls because an accurate identification feels like the practical reason to admit the evidence. The breaker is that the counsel question is resolved by waiver, not by after-the-fact accuracy.

D pulls because a post-indictment live lineup is a critical stage. Gold Key: the right exists, but the "never" overclaim fails because the defendant may waive the right knowingly, voluntarily, and intelligently.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged. A post-indictment lineup is a critical stage at which the Sixth Amendment right to counsel applies. United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), states that counsel's presence is required at such a lineup absent an intelligent waiver. Gilbert v. California, 388 U.S. 263 (1967), supplies the exclusion consequence for a post-indictment lineup conducted without counsel and without a valid waiver. Kirby v. Illinois, 406 U.S. 682 (1972), confirms the attachment line by distinguishing pre-indictment identification from post-charge identification. The general waiver standard is knowing, voluntary, and intelligent. Those authorities confirm the source key: because Naomi was advised of the right, told the lineup could wait, said she understood, and signed a written waiver, the identification is not automatically excluded for counsel's absence.
