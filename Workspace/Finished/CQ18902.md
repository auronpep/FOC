### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** Foreclosure proceeds move through a strict payment waterfall. First, the sale expenses are paid. Next, the foreclosed mortgage debt is paid, including accrued interest on that mortgage. Only after that does any surplus move to junior mortgagees, and junior mortgagees are paid in order of priority. If the money runs out before a lower-priority junior mortgage is reached, that junior mortgagee receives nothing from the sale proceeds.

Here, the sale produced $240,000. The foreclosure expenses take $18,000, leaving $222,000. The accrued interest on the first mortgage takes $7,000, leaving $215,000. The $180,000 first mortgage principal is then paid, leaving $35,000. Bethany Credit Union's second mortgage is next in priority and receives the remaining $35,000. Nothing remains for Antioch Fund's third mortgage.

**Gold Key:** Foreclosure-sale proceeds pay sale expenses, then the foreclosed mortgage debt including interest, then junior liens in priority order, and only then the owner if any surplus remains.

**Silver Key:** Treat every answer choice like a ledger. Run the same dollars through the waterfall top to bottom, and stop the moment the money runs out.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A** attracts the student who correctly sees that junior mortgagees come after the foreclosed first mortgage, but then treats the remaining $35,000 as a pool to divide between both junior lenders. The breaker is priority. Bethany Credit Union is second in line, so it must be paid before Antioch Fund receives anything. Because $35,000 remains and Bethany is owed exactly $35,000, the entire remaining amount goes to Bethany and the third mortgage receives nothing. **Silver Key:** a foreclosure waterfall is not a sharing pool; once a priority level is reached, pay it before moving down.

**B** looks close because it pays foreclosure expenses, the first mortgage principal, and the second mortgage before sending a small remainder to the third mortgage. The missing step is accrued interest. Interest on the foreclosed mortgage is part of the foreclosed debt and must be paid before junior mortgagees receive proceeds. This choice pays the third mortgage with dollars that should have paid the first mortgage's accrued interest. **Gold Key:** the foreclosed mortgage debt includes principal and accrued interest.

**D** sells the idea that all recorded mortgage principal should be paid before interest or that junior mortgagees can be protected by trimming the first mortgage principal. The breaker is seniority. ### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule.** Foreclosure sale proceeds are applied first to costs and expenses of the sale, then to the judgment or mortgage debt being foreclosed, and then to junior liens or interests terminated by the foreclosure in order of priority. Any remaining surplus goes to the holder of the equity of redemption. Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages section 7.4 states the junior-lien surplus rule: when the foreclosure sale price exceeds the mortgage obligation, the surplus is applied to terminated junior interests in priority order and the remaining balance goes to the equity holder. Courts applying the Restatement describe the same rule. In *Fidelity Bank v. King*, 136 P.3d 465 (Kan. 2006), the court described foreclosure proceeds as generally going to costs and taxes, then the foreclosing judgment, then priority lienholders before any debtor surplus. In *Perry v. Blum*, 629 F.3d 1 (1st Cir. 2011), the court likewise recognized that junior lienholders have an equitable lien on foreclosure surplus under Restatement section 7.4.

**Application.** The first mortgage foreclosure produced $240,000. Costs and expenses total $18,000. The foreclosed first mortgage debt includes both $7,000 accrued interest and $180,000 principal. After those payments, exactly $35,000 remains. Bethany Credit Union, the second mortgagee, is the next junior lienholder in priority and is owed $35,000. The remaining proceeds therefore fully pay Bethany. No proceeds remain for Antioch Fund, the third mortgagee.
