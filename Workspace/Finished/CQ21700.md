## 1. Final Question
King Solomon places a deadly hemlock extract in a silver goblet intending to kill his rival, Adonijah. Before Adonijah arrives at the banquet hall, a palace steward mistakenly drinks from the goblet and dies. Solomon is charged with the murder of the steward. Is Solomon guilty of the murder of the steward?

A) Yes, but only of involuntary manslaughter because the steward drank by mistake.
B) Yes, because Solomon's intent to kill Adonijah transfers to the steward's completed homicide.
C) No, because hemlock requires the intended victim to consume it.
D) No, because Solomon intended to kill Adonijah, not the steward.

Solomon intended to kill Adonijah and used hemlock for that purpose. The steward died from the hemlock. Transferred intent supplies malice for the steward's murder.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

D: In completed homicide, intent to kill the intended victim transfers to the actual victim who dies.

Intent to kill transfers to an unintended victim in a completed homicide (Model Penal Code § 2.03(2)(a)). Solomon's intent to kill Adonijah is sufficient to support a murder charge for the death of the steward.
