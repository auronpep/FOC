### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. Lydia's owed money under the completed contract, and Peter assigned that payment right to Ruth. Gold Key: an obligor who pays the assignor before receiving notice that the payment right was assigned is discharged to that extent; after notice, payment must go to the assignee. Silver Key: in an assignee's collection suit, separate facts about the assigned right from facts about the underlying work. The fact that actually helps Lydia's is the one showing that Lydia's already paid the person it was still allowed to pay before notice arrived.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
**A. The agreement between Peter and Lydia's did not contain any clause allowing Peter to assign the right to payment.**  
This is attractive because students often treat assignment as something that requires advance permission. The breaker is that a contract right to payment is generally assignable unless an effective restriction or special burden problem makes the assignment invalid. A silent contract does not give Lydia's a judgment against Ruth.

**C. The agreement between Peter and Lydia's stated, "This agreement may not be modified except in a writing signed by both parties."**  
This is attractive because the word "assigned" can feel like a later change to the deal. The breaker is that an assignment transfers a right to receive performance; it does not modify Peter's promised work or Lydia's payment duty. The no-oral-modification clause attacks the wrong legal move.

**D. Ruth was not trained in carving or painting cedar Bible-story displays.**  
This is the dominant trap because the contract was for skilled work and Ruth is a stranger to that skill. The breaker is that Ruth is suing to collect money after Peter finished the work, not trying to perform the carving herself. Gold Key: assigning a right to receive money is not delegating the work duty, so Ruth's lack of carving skill does not defeat her assigned payment claim.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue is assignment of contract rights, specifically an obligor's discharge when it pays the assignor before notice of the assignment. The verified outline code is `53070200` for Contracts, Third-Party Rights, Assignment of Rights. The source row listed `56050200`, but the tested rule is not contract modification; the deepest listed node that covers the rule is `53070200`.

Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 317 states that an assignment transfers the assignor's right to performance to the assignee, and that contract rights are generally assignable unless the substitution materially changes the obligor's duty, materially increases burden or risk, impairs expected return performance, or is barred by statute, public policy, or a valid contract term. Restatement section 317 comment d confirms that when the duty is to pay money, changing the person to whom payment is made is ordinarily not a material change. Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 338(1) provides the notice rule: until the obligor receives notice that the right has been assigned and performance is to be rendered to the assignee, the assignor retains the power to discharge the obligor's duty by receiving performance. UCC section 9-406(a) states the same commercial-payment principle for account debtors: payment to the assignor discharges the obligation until, but not after, effective notification of the assignment.

Sources checked: https://advance.lexis.com/open/document/openwebdocview/-317-Assignment-of-a-Right/ ; https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/2nd%2BSemester/LAW%2B506-002%2B%E2%80%93%2BContracts%2BII/R2C%2B%C2%A7%2B338 ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-406

The credited outcome is unchanged. If Lydia's paid Peter before notice of Peter's assignment to Ruth, Lydia's payment discharged the duty to that extent, and Ruth's collection claim against Lydia's fails. The other added facts do not discharge Lydia's payment duty: Ruth's skill is irrelevant to a completed-work payment assignment, silence about assignment does not create a permission requirement, and a no-written-modification clause does not bar an assignment of the payment right.
