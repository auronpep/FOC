## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**A is correct.** The traditional common-law approach treats statutory rape as strict liability with respect to the complainant's age. The prosecution must prove the sexual intercourse and the fact that the complainant was below the age of consent; it does not have to prove that Lydia knew Timothy was 15 or was unreasonable about his age. Timothy was actually 15, and the jurisdiction's age of consent is 16. The elements are met.

**Gold Key:** Under the traditional common-law rule, statutory rape is strict liability as to age. A reasonable mistake about age, even one supported by the minor's statement and an ID check, does not defeat guilt.

**Silver Key:** Lock the call first. The call asks for the traditional common-law approach, so the fairness facts about reasonable reliance do not get weighed until the strict-liability rule has been applied.

## 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer

The tested issue is unchanged: whether, under the traditional common-law approach to statutory rape, a defendant's reasonable mistake about the complainant's age is a defense when the complainant is below the statutory age of consent. The credited outcome is unchanged: guilt.

Traditional statutory rape doctrine treats the age element as strict liability. The prosecution need not prove mens rea as to age, and reasonable mistake about age is not a defense under that traditional rule. A leading modern example is **Garnett v. State, 632 A.2d 797 (Md. 1993)**, where the Maryland high court held that the relevant statutory-rape provision imposed strict liability and made no allowance for a mistake-of-age defense. The older common-law-style authority **Regina v. Prince, L.R. 2 C.C.R. 154 (1875)** likewise rejected a reasonable mistake about age defense under an age-protective statute. The Model Penal Code takes a more nuanced approach in **MPC section 213.6(1)**, but the question expressly asks for the traditional common-law approach, so that modern compromise does not change the answer.
