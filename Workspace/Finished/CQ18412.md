### 7. Full right-answer explanation
C is correct. Timothy is a child doing an ordinary child activity, not operating a motor vehicle or doing a licensed adult task. Gold Key: a child in ordinary child activity is measured against a reasonably careful child of like age, intelligence, and experience, not against the adult reasonable person. Silver Key: when a standard-of-care question gives you both "child" and "not an adult activity," the clash is child standard versus adult standard; the ordinary-play fact sends the answer to the child standard.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because it offers a bright-line age cutoff and seems protective of young children. The breaker is that the law does not create a blanket no-standard rule for every child under 12; the child standard still applies unless the child is too young to be capable of negligence under the governing rule.

B is attractive because the child standard does consider the child's age, intelligence, and experience, so a personal-capacity answer sounds close. The breaker is that the test is not Timothy's private sincere best effort; it is still an objective child standard measured against a reasonably careful child of like age, intelligence, and experience.

D is attractive because adult reasonable person is the default negligence phrase students hear first. Gold Key: the adult standard does not swallow child defendants in ordinary child activities; here, Timothy was not using a motorized vehicle or doing an adult-only activity, so the child standard controls.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Torts, Negligence, Standard of Due Care, and the verified outline code is `61020201`. The governing rule is that a child engaged in ordinary child activity is judged by the care reasonably expected of a child of like age, intelligence, and experience under similar circumstances. Restatement of Torts section 283 comment e states the child-standard rule, and Restatement (Second) of Torts section 283A is the usual modern formulation. Research also confirms the adult-standard exception for activities normally undertaken only by adults and requiring adult qualification or licensing. See Restatement of Torts section 283, comment e, https://advance.lexis.com/open/document/openwebdocview/Restatement-of-the-Law-Torts-283/?pdcomponentid=432501&pddocfullpath=%2Fshared%2Fdocument%2Fanalytical-materials%2Furn%3AcontentItem%3A5KB9-PYG0-02JF-N0BS-00000-00; WIS JI-Civil 1010 citing Restatement (Second) of Torts section 283A and the adult-activity exception, https://wilawlibrary.gov/jury/files/civil/1010.pdf. Because Timothy is a child doing ordinary nonmotorized play, the credited child-standard answer is unchanged from the source item.
