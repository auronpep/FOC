### 7. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. Timothy owned a life estate measured by Timothy's own life. When he quitclaimed all of his interest to Lydia, he transferred only what he had: possession for the rest of Timothy's life. Gold Key: a life tenant's transferee takes a life estate pur autre vie, measured by the original life tenant's life, not by the transferee's life. Silver Key: build the title snapshot in two slots, current possession first and future fee-simple possession second; the permission fact does not enlarge or erase either slot. Lydia therefore holds a life estate pur autre vie, and Ruth keeps the remainder in fee simple.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because Ruth is the named remainderman and will eventually own the cabin outright. The breaker is timing: Timothy is still alive, so Ruth's future interest has not become possessory and Ruth does not yet have fee simple absolute possession.

C is the dominant trap because it keeps Lydia as the current holder and Ruth as the fee-simple remainderman, so it feels almost complete. The breaker is the measuring life. Gold Key: Lydia's estate is not measured by Lydia's own life; it is a life estate pur autre vie measured by Timothy's life because Timothy could convey only his own life estate.

D is attractive because Naomi's lack of consent tempts students to treat the quitclaim as if it failed and scrambled the title back toward the original owner. The breaker is that Naomi had already conveyed the fee into a life estate plus remainder, and Timothy's deed did not give Ruth a present life estate or restore a fee-simple remainder to Naomi.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Nature and Ownership of Land, Ownership, Life estates, and the verified outline code is `82010103`. A conveyance "to Timothy for life, remainder to Ruth" gives Timothy a life estate and gives Ruth a vested remainder in fee simple. A life tenant may transfer the life estate during life, but the transferee receives no greater estate than the life tenant had. The transferee's estate is therefore a life estate pur autre vie, measured by the original life tenant's life. Cornell Wex states that a life-estate holder may transfer the interest during life, and a third-party transferee must surrender the property to the remainderman when the original life tenant dies: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/life_estate. Cornell Wex also defines a life estate pur autre vie as one measured by a life other than the holder's life: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/life_estate_pur_autre_vie. As a real authority check, `Smith v. Smith`, 60 S.E.2d 347 (W. Va. 1950), recognizes estates pur autre vie and treats a vested remainder as one capable of taking possession whenever the prior estate ends: https://law.justia.com/cases/west-virginia/supreme-court/1950/10216-2.html. West Virginia Code section 44-1-21 also recognizes an "estate for the life of another" as a legal property interest: https://code.wvlegislature.gov/44-1-21/. These authorities confirm the supplied official key's substance: Lydia holds a life estate pur autre vie measured by Timothy's life, and Ruth holds the remainder in fee simple. No researched authority contradicted the official key.
