### 1. Final question

Ruth ran a private booth called Psalm Market, where she sold coffee and Scripture-verse cards at weekend craft fairs. ### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Ruth could reject the cart because the missing locking drawer made the tender nonconforming. That first move did not end the contract. Daniel's company still had until April 15 to make a conforming delivery, and it seasonably offered to install the drawer on Monday morning within that contract time. Gold Key: under the UCC, perfect tender gives the buyer an initial rejection right, but a seller who seasonably offers to cure before the contract deadline gets the chance to cure. Silver Key: split "reject" from "cancel"; the best answer keeps both pieces instead of choosing only one side.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is the dominant trap because the missing drawer sounds easy to fix. The breaker is that cure does not force Ruth to accept the nonconforming cart as delivered; it gives Daniel's company a chance to make a conforming tender within the contract time.

C sells the substantial-impairment phrase from nearby UCC territory. The breaker is lane control: this is an initial rejection of a single tender of goods, not revocation after acceptance or an installment-contract rejection, so Ruth did not need to show substantial impairment before rejecting.

D sells the loudest version of perfect tender. Ruth's rejection was proper, but the seller's timely cure offer blocked immediate cancellation because the delivery deadline had not passed.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

**Student black-letter-law answer explanation:** In a sale of goods, a buyer may reject a tender that fails to conform to the contract. The missing promised drawer made the cart nonconforming, so Ruth could refuse the cart as delivered. But because the contract deadline had not expired and Daniel's company offered to fix the defect by that deadline, Ruth could not simply cancel and buy elsewhere without allowing the cure.

**Lawyer-confirmation authority:** UCC 2-601 states the perfect tender rule: if goods or tender fail in any respect to conform to the contract, the buyer may reject the whole, accept the whole, or accept any commercial unit and reject the rest. UCC 2-508(1) gives the seller a cure right when a nonconforming tender is rejected and the time for performance has not yet expired, so long as the seller seasonably notifies the buyer and makes conforming delivery within the contract time. UCC 2-608 uses substantial impairment for revocation of acceptance, which confirms why the substantial-impairment answer is the wrong lane for an initial rejection. Sources checked: https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-601, https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-508, and https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-608.
