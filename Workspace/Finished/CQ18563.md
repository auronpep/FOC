## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

After arresting Timothy for robbing a cash table at a private Christian homeschool expo, officers placed him in a lineup with five other men. Witnesses had reported that the robber said, "Set the cash box beside the hymnals." The officers required Timothy to repeat that sentence aloud so the witnesses could compare his voice. Timothy refused, saying the Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination barred the officers from making him say the sentence.

Does compelling Timothy to repeat the sentence for voice identification violate the Fifth Amendment privilege?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. The officers are not asking Timothy to confess, admit knowledge, or assert that the sentence is true. They are using the sound and characteristics of his voice so witnesses can compare it with the robber's voice. Gold Key: the Fifth Amendment bars compelled testimonial communication, but it does not bar compelled physical identifying evidence such as fingerprints, handwriting exemplars, lineup appearance, or voice exemplars. Silver Key: separate the words' content from the physical trait being measured. If the government wants the voice sound, not the truth of the sentence, the answer is no Fifth Amendment violation.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is the dominant trap because "against his will" sounds like the heart of compulsion. The breaker is the overclaim. The privilege does not block every compelled evidence-gathering step; it blocks compelled testimonial self-incrimination.

C is attractive because custody and interrogation are familiar Fifth Amendment signals. That frame is too narrow. The privilege can apply outside Miranda settings when testimonial compulsion is present, but this voice-comparison lineup is not testimonial compulsion.

D is attractive because Timothy is forced to repeat words associated with the robbery. Gold Key: the privilege turns on whether Timothy is being forced to communicate testimonial content, not whether the words sound incriminating in the abstract. The officers want the voice characteristics, not an admission that the sentence is true.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, credited outcome, and trap structure are unchanged. The Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination protects a person from being compelled to provide testimonial or communicative evidence against himself. It does not prevent the government from compelling a suspect to provide real or physical identifying evidence.

Authority: In United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), the Court held that requiring a bank-robbery suspect in a lineup to speak words allegedly used by the robber did not compel testimonial evidence; the suspect used his voice as an identifying physical characteristic. GovInfo lists Wade at https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USREPORTS-388/USREPORTS-388-218. United States v. Dionisio, 410 U.S. 1 (1973), confirms that compelled voice exemplars used for identification do not violate the Fifth Amendment because they are not used for testimonial content: https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/united-states-v-dionisio-893794276. Schmerber v. California, 384 U.S. 757 (1966), draws the core distinction between compelled communications and compelled real or physical evidence: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/384/757/. The DOJ Criminal Resource Manual summarizes the same voice-exemplar rule: https://www.justice.gov/archives/jm/criminal-resource-manual-256-voice-exemplars-self-incrimination.

Here, Timothy is compelled to speak only so witnesses can compare the sound and identifying characteristics of his voice. The government is not using the truth of his words. Compelling the voice sample therefore does not violate the Fifth Amendment privilege. Outline note: the source row carried `73030400`, a robbery code. The tested rule is criminal procedure identification evidence, so the verified deepest matching code is `75080200`, which appears verbatim in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md` as "Fifth and Sixth Amendments—Statements, Confessions, and Identifications > Identifications."
