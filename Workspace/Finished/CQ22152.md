Ruth hurt her shoulder when her neighbor's goat slipped through an open gate and knocked her into a stack of hymn folders she was carrying to a home Bible study. Ruth had a history of shoulder problems, but she claimed that a recent surgery had left her completely pain-free before the goat incident. ### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is admissible because Ruth made the statement to her doctor during a medical follow-up about her shoulder symptoms. Gold Key: FRE 803(4) covers statements made for medical diagnosis or treatment that describe medical history, symptoms, pain, sensations, or the general cause when reasonably pertinent to diagnosis or treatment. Silver Key: when the statement is made in the treatment room to a medical provider, test the medical-purpose exception before treating "hearsay" as the end of the analysis.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because Ruth is the plaintiff and can be cross-examined at trial. The breaker is that cross-examination alone is not the reason this out-of-court statement comes in; the admissibility route is the medical-treatment hearsay exception.

D is attractive because the doctor's testimony repeats Ruth's out-of-court statement. The breaker is that the statement is hearsay but not inadmissible hearsay if it fits FRE 803(4). Gold Key: medical-purpose symptom statements are a Rule 803 exception.

B is attractive because some hearsay exceptions depend on unavailability. The breaker is that this one does not; Rule 803 exceptions apply regardless of whether the declarant is available.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule is unchanged: the patient's out-of-court statement to a doctor is offered through the doctor, so it needs a hearsay exception. Federal Rule of Evidence 803(4) covers statements made for medical diagnosis or treatment that describe medical history, past or present symptoms, pain, sensations, or the inception or general cause when reasonably pertinent to diagnosis or treatment. Rule 803 applies regardless of whether the declarant is available. Ruth's statement that she had no shoulder pain at a routine post-surgery follow-up described her symptoms for treatment purposes, so the doctor's testimony is admissible under FRE 803(4). Authority: FRE 803(4), https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803.
