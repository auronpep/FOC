## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Silas and Martha, siblings, inherited a sprawling 50-acre property known as "Covenant Creek" from their father, a local deacon. The property included a large historic farmhouse. The father's will left the property to both children as tenants in common but did not address who had the right to occupy the farmhouse or any potential rental payments. Martha chose to move into the farmhouse to maintain the family heritage, while Silas remained in his apartment in a nearby city. 

The fair market rental value of the farmhouse was determined to be $12,000 per year. Silas requested that Martha pay him $6,000 per year for her use of the property. Martha refused to pay, but she sent Silas a letter stating, "You are my brother and a co-owner; you and your family are welcome to move into the guest wing of the farmhouse whenever you wish." Silas declined the offer and sued Martha for his share of the rental value.

Is Silas entitled to receive rent from Martha?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

Does a tenant in common who is in sole possession of the property owe rent to a co-tenant who is not in possession, if the possessory co-tenant has not excluded the other?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. No, because Martha has not wrongfully excluded Silas from the property.

B. No, because the father’s will was silent regarding the payment of rent.

C. Yes, because Martha is in sole possession of the farmhouse.

D. Yes, because Silas and Martha own equal undivided interests in the property.

### 5. Correct answer

A (original key B).

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct because under the majority rule, a co-tenant in possession has the right to occupy the entire premises and does not owe rent to non-possessory co-tenants unless there has been an ouster. Gold Key: A co-tenant in possession is only liable for rent if they have "ousted" (wrongfully excluded) the other co-tenant. Since Martha explicitly invited Silas to live in the farmhouse, she has not excluded him, and therefore no rent is due. Silver Key: Occupancy alone, even of the entire property, does not create a duty to pay rent to co-tenants.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

B is a trap that focuses on the silence of the will. While the will is indeed silent, the legal duty to pay rent is determined by property law principles (ouster), not by the absence of a provision in the testamentary instrument. This is a "moral common sense" trap where students look for the grantor's intent instead of the governing legal test.

C is the dominant trap. It reflects a common student misconception that sole possession of a shared asset requires payment to the non-using owners. While Martha is occupying the entire home, this fact does not trigger liability without the additional element of ouster.

D is an irrelevant truth. It is true that Silas and Martha own equal undivided interests, but that interest grants them both the right to possess the whole. It does not mean they must pay each other for exercising that right unless one is prevented from doing so.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The majority rule at common law is that each co-tenant (whether a tenant in common or a joint tenant) has an undivided right to possess and enjoy the entire property. Consequently, a co-tenant in possession is not liable to a co-tenant out of possession for rent or for the value of the use and occupation of the premises, provided there has been no ouster.

Primary Authority: Restatement (First) of Property § 161 (Majority rule: co-tenant in possession has no duty to pay rent to co-tenant out of possession absent ouster).
Black-letter rule: Ouster is the wrongful exclusion of a co-tenant from the property. Without ouster or an agreement to the contrary, the possessory co-tenant keeps the benefits of their possession.
Verification: The outcome is consistent with the supplied official key. Martha's offer to Silas to move in affirmatively disproves ouster.
