## 1. Final question

Lydia ran a private Christian theater-supply workshop. After a cancelled Bible-pageant season, eight pallets of foam-and-wood Jericho-wall panels were crowding her rented warehouse. Lydia told Peter's set-building company that it could have the panels for free for use in its stage construction if Peter's company brought a truck and hauled them away. The panels turned out to be defective, and Peter's company sued Lydia for breach of contract. Lydia argued that she merely made a gift of the panels on the condition that Peter's company pick them up.

Can Peter's company successfully sue for breach of contract?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A person offers unwanted materials for free if the recipient hauls them away, because the person wants removal and wants to avoid disposal costs. Is the pickup enough consideration to support a contract?

D. Yes, because Peter's company relied on Lydia's promise to its detriment.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Lydia did not simply decide to make a generous gift. She wanted the panels out of her warehouse and wanted to avoid paying someone else to haul them away. Peter's company supplied that removal performance. **Gold Key:** a free transfer can still be supported by consideration when the promisor sought the recipient's act as the price of the promise. **Silver Key:** when the array fights over "gift" versus "bargain," ask whether pickup was merely how the recipient collected the item or the very performance the promisor wanted.

Here, hauling the panels away was the performance Lydia wanted. That makes the exchange bargained for, even though no money changed hands.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This answer sells a formal version of bargaining: no price, no negotiation, no contract. The breaker is that consideration does not require haggling or a dollar price. Lydia sought the hauling as the return performance, and Peter's company gave that performance.

**B.** This is the dominant trap. The word "free" makes the transfer sound like a gift, and the pickup condition sounds like a mere condition of receiving the gift. **Gold Key:** the pickup was not just a delivery condition; it was the removal benefit Lydia wanted. **Silver Key:** ask whether Lydia would have cared about the pickup even if she were not giving the panels away. She would, because she wanted the warehouse cleared and wanted to avoid a junk-haul bill.

**D.** Reliance offers a tempting fairness path to a yes answer. The breaker is that the stem gives no separate reliance fact, such as buying special equipment or turning down another job because of Lydia's promise. The stronger answer is ordinary consideration: Lydia's promise and Peter's hauling induced each other.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged: the issue is whether a "free" transfer is supported by consideration when the promisor wanted the recipient's pickup/removal performance. The source row supplied outline code **52040100**, but the Contracts outline places the tested rule under **55020201 - Formation > Consideration > Bargain and exchange**. That verified code is used throughout.

Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 71 provides the governing consideration rule: consideration requires a bargained-for performance or return promise, and performance is bargained for when the promisor seeks it in exchange for the promise and the promisee gives it in exchange. Restatement section 81 supports the same result by making clear that motive or additional inducement does not defeat consideration when the exchange element is present. The H2O Open Casebook reproductions of sections 71 and 81 are linked below for verification. [1] [2]

Lydia's promise induced Peter's company to haul away the panels, and the hauling induced Lydia's promise because she wanted the panels removed without paying a hauler. The legal issue is not whether the panels had a cash price. It is whether Lydia sought Peter's performance as the price of her promise. She did. Research does not contradict the supplied official key.
