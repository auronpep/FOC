### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Lydia needs a fact that attacks the paper as a deed. Gold Key: a deed conveying real property must be signed by the grantor or by an authorized agent for the grantor; without that signature, the paper does not convey the grantor's interest. Silver Key: sort the array into deed validity, deed construction, and recording/priority. The missing grantor signature is the validity defect, so it gives Lydia the strongest response.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because deed formalities feel strict, and a missing estate label sounds like a missing legal term. The breaker is that an otherwise valid deed that does not specify the estate conveyed is generally read to pass the grantor's transferable interest unless the instrument shows a lesser intent.

C is attractive because recording sounds like the final step that makes a deed official. The breaker is the Silver Key: recording protects priority and gives notice to later claimants; it is not what makes a delivered deed valid between the grantor and grantee.

D is the dominant trap because students often drag contract consideration into a deed question. The breaker is the Gold Key's lane discipline: a deed can be a gift conveyance, so no payment or other consideration does not defeat a properly signed and delivered deed.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested issue is unchanged: whether the alleged deed has the formal signature needed to convey the landowner's interest. The credited outcome is unchanged because a paper not signed by the grantor or an authorized agent does not effectively convey the grantor's real-property interest.

Authority floor: California Civil Code section 1091 states the common conveyancing rule that an estate in real property can be transferred only by operation of law or by a written instrument subscribed by the party disposing of the interest or by an agent authorized in writing. Estate of Stephens, 28 Cal.4th 665 (2002), applies that rule to agent execution and confirms that authority to execute a deed must be in writing. California Civil Code section 1105 confirms the construction rule for the estate-conveyed distractor: a grant is presumed to pass fee simple unless a lesser estate appears. Colorado Revised Statutes section 38-35-109 reflects the recording rule: unrecorded instruments are ineffective against certain later protected claimants, except between the parties and those with notice. Bonner v. Oklahoma Rock Corp., 863 P.2d 1176 (Okla. 1993), states the general property-law point that a conveyance of land as a gift can be as effective as one supported by consideration.
