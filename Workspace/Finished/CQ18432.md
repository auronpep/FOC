## 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The governing authority floor is the Restatement (Second) of Contracts. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 242 explains when a party's uncured material failure to perform or offer performance discharges the other party's remaining duties. Section 242(3) states that a material failure to perform or offer performance on a stated day does not of itself discharge the other party unless the circumstances, including the language of the agreement, indicate that performance or an offer to perform by that day is important. Section 241 supplies the materiality factors, including the injured party's expected benefit, compensation, forfeiture, likelihood of cure, and good faith.

Here, the language of the agreement and the circumstances both indicate importance: the contract required closing "on or before September 30," stated that "time is of the essence," and identified Daniel's immediate move-in need as the reason for that date. **Signal:** A land-sale contract has a stated closing date, an immediate move-in reason, and time-is-of-the-essence language.

**Student move:** Tie the buyer's right to reject to the essential date, not to fault or damages.

**Tiny rule:** Essential date missed means late tender fails.

**Trap:** Phrase alone, reasonable effort, or no damages.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-REPROP-LAND-TIME-ESSENCE-01:** In a land-sale contract, a closing date can be essential because of the surrounding circumstances, and a "time is of the essence" clause confirms that timing matters rather than replacing the factual reason.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-REPROP-LAND-TWO-DANIEL-CHOICES-01:** When two choices give the buyer the win, compare the reasons; choose the reason tied to the essential closing date, not the shortcut phrase, fault, or damages.
