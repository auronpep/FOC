### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule:** Under *Stone v. Powell*, 428 U.S. 465 (1976), a state prisoner may not obtain federal habeas relief on the ground that illegally seized evidence was introduced at trial when the state provided an opportunity for full and fair litigation of the Fourth Amendment claim. The rule is a habeas limitation tied to the exclusionary rule's deterrence rationale.

**Statutory setting:** 28 U.S.C. Section 2254 governs habeas applications by persons in custody under state-court judgments. Section 2254(b)(1) requires exhaustion or the absence/ineffectiveness of state corrective process before habeas relief may be granted, but exhaustion alone does not establish entitlement to relief.

**Certiorari point:** A denial of certiorari does not express the Supreme Court's view on the merits. *Maryland v. Baltimore Radio Show*, 338 U.S. 912 (1950), explains that denial means fewer than four Justices voted to grant review and carries no merits implication.

**Application:** Barnabas raised the Fourth Amendment objection at trial and pursued it through the highest state court. The federal petition asks the district court to use habeas as a second suppression forum. Stone forecloses that relief.
