# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

Lydia signed a valid written contract to purchase a small lakeside parcel called **Prayer Garden Lot** from Stephen for its fair market value of **$87,000**. The contract allowed Lydia to assign her rights.

Before closing, Lydia directed Stephen to convey title to Lydia and to Ruth, an old Bible-study friend whom Lydia had not contacted in many years. Later, Lydia learned that Ruth would have to sign closing papers. Lydia asked her brother Daniel to come to closing and pretend to be Ruth.

At closing, Stephen executed a deed in proper form purporting to convey Prayer Garden Lot to Lydia and Ruth as tenants in common. Daniel pretended to be Ruth and signed Ruth’s name on all required closing documents. Lydia paid the full $87,000 purchase price. The deed was promptly and properly recorded.

Unknown to Lydia and Daniel, Ruth had died several months before closing. Ruth’s duly probated will devised “all my real estate to my nephew Timothy” and left the residue of her estate to Lydia. Lydia and Timothy dispute the status of Prayer Garden Lot. Timothy brings an appropriate action against Stephen and Lydia to quiet title to an undivided one-half interest in Prayer Garden Lot.

The court should hold that legal title to Prayer Garden Lot is vested:

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A buyer has a deed made to herself and a dead friend as tenants in common, using an impersonator to sign for the friend. Who owns title after the deed is recorded?

## 3. Final answer choices

B. One-half in Lydia and one-half in Stephen.
C. All in Stephen.
D. One-half in Lydia and one-half in Timothy.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** The deed worked as to Lydia, the living named grantee who paid the price and appeared at closing. It did not work as to Ruth because Ruth was already dead and could not take or accept the conveyance.

**Gold Key:** A deed needs a grantee who can take title. A deed to a dead co-grantee fails as to that share.

**Silver Key:** Split the deed by grantee. Do not treat the forged/dead-grantee problem as destroying the whole deed or transferring the failed share to the buyer.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. All in Lydia.** This is attractive because Lydia paid the entire purchase price and arranged the transaction. The breaker is that paying all the money does not make the failed half-interest slide over to Lydia when the deed names two tenants in common.

**C. All in Stephen.** This is attractive because the closing was tainted by impersonation and a dead named grantee. The breaker is that the defect only kills Ruth’s half; Lydia’s half still has a living grantee, consideration, deed, delivery, and recording.

**D. One-half in Lydia and one-half in Timothy.** This is attractive because Ruth’s will gave all her real estate to Timothy. The breaker is that Ruth never acquired the Prayer Garden Lot interest, so there was no half-interest for Timothy to inherit.

## 9. Black-letter verification and legal reasoning

A valid deed requires a grantee capable of taking title, plus effective delivery and acceptance. The California Department of Real Estate reference book states that a deed grantee must be a person in existence and capable of taking title, and that a deed to a dead person is void. A real-property practice source similarly states that a valid deed must be delivered to and accepted by the grantee. ([California Department of Real Estate][1])

The original tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are preserved: the conveyance succeeds as to the living buyer’s one-half tenancy-in-common interest and fails as to the dead co-grantee’s one-half interest, which remains in the grantor. The devisee takes nothing because the dead friend never acquired the interest.

## 10. Rigor / difficulty preservation

Difficulty is preserved because the dominant trap remains the same: students want to give all title to the person who paid the full price. The item still requires separating payment, deed validity, co-grantee capacity, and inheritance.

**CUT D:** Needs the Gold Key. Timothy’s devise only matters if Ruth first acquired an interest. She did not.
