## 6. Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**Gold Key:** Solicitation requires (1) an intentional communication that (2) asks, commands, encourages, or counsels another person to commit a crime, with (3) the intent that the crime be committed. Jonah expressed a wish. He didn't ask. He didn't encourage. He didn't offer payment or give instructions. Venting anger about a rival is not solicitation. The actus reus is missing entirely.

Jonah said "I wish someone would break my rival's windows." That's a statement of desire, not a request directed at the friend. No ask, no command, no encouragement, no counsel, no payment, no instructions. The friend said nothing and left. The actus reus of solicitation — the intentional communicative act seeking another's criminal action — is completely absent. Not guilty.

## 7. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**New A (original B): "Jonah is guilty because the friend heard a statement about criminal damage"** — This is the half-truth distortion. Yes, the friend heard something. But hearing a statement about a desired bad outcome is not the same as being solicited. The law requires an intentional request or encouragement directed at another person to commit a crime. Jonah didn't direct anything at the friend. Mentioning a wish for vandalism is not soliciting vandalism.

**New B (original D): "Jonah is not guilty only because vandalism is a misdemeanor"** — This is a flat misstatement. The grade of the underlying offense has absolutely nothing to do with whether Jonah's words constituted solicitation. The defect here isn't the severity of vandalism — it's the complete absence of a solicitation act. This choice distracts with an irrelevant factor.

**New C (original A — CORRECT):** Explained above.

**New D (original C — DOMINANT TRAP): "Jonah is guilty because solicitation does not require agreement"** — This is the most dangerous distractor. It's true that solicitation doesn't require the other person to agree — that's a correct statement of law. But it's not responsive to what's missing here. The issue isn't whether the friend agreed — it's whether Jonah ever made a solicitation at all. No request, no encouragement, no counsel = no solicitation act = not guilty, regardless of the agreement rule. Students who remember the no-agreement rule and stop there will pick this and miss the real issue.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Rule:** Solicitation requires (1) an intentional communication (2) that asks, commands, encourages, or counsels another person to commit a crime, with (3) the specific intent that the crime be committed. Mere expression of a wish, fantasy, anger, or venting — without a directed request or encouragement — does not satisfy the actus reus of solicitation.

**Authority:**
- **Model Penal Code § 5.02(1):** A person is guilty of solicitation to commit a crime if, with the purpose of promoting or facilitating its commission, he commands, encourages, or requests another person to engage in specific conduct that would constitute such crime or an attempt to commit such crime, or would establish the other's complicity in its commission or attempted commission.
- **Common law:** Solicitation merges into the completed crime if the crime is committed. The actus reus is the soliciting communication itself; the mens rea is the intent that the crime be committed. *See* LaFave, *Criminal Law* § 12.1.
- **Key distinction:** A statement of desire ("I wish someone would...") without a directed request is not solicitation. The communication must seek the other person's criminal action. *See* *State v. Cotton*, 790 P.2d 1050 (N.M. Ct. App. 1990) (letters to third parties expressing desire for harm, without directing the recipient to act, did not constitute solicitation).

#### Wrong Answer Recovery Paths

**If student chose A (half-truth):** "The friend heard something, yes. But hearing a statement about a desired bad outcome is not the same as being solicited. Solicitation requires an intentional request or encouragement directed at another person to commit a crime. Jonah didn't direct anything at the friend."

**If student chose B (flat misstatement):** "The grade of vandalism — misdemeanor or felony — has nothing to do with whether Jonah's words were solicitation. The issue is whether Jonah made a request or encouragement. He didn't. That's the whole case."

**If student chose D — dominant trap (no-agreement overclaim):** "You remembered correctly that solicitation doesn't require agreement. But that rule only matters AFTER a solicitation has occurred. The threshold question is: did Jonah ever solicit anyone? No request, no encouragement, no command = no solicitation act. The agreement question never comes up."

#### Outline Mastery Map

- **Placement:** Criminal Law > Inchoate Offenses > Solicitation (71040100)
- **This item teaches:** The actus reus of solicitation — intentional communicative act requirement
- **Fills:** Gap between "solicitation doesn't require agreement" and "but it does require a request"
- **Adjacent to:** Conspiracy (requires agreement), attempt (requires substantial step), merger doctrine

#### Crossover Intersection Map

- **Criminal + Criminal:** Solicitation merges into the completed crime if the crime is committed — you can't be convicted of both solicitation and the completed offense
- **Within Criminal:** Solicitation vs. attempt — solicitation is the request; attempt requires a substantial step toward commission
- **Criminal + ConLaw:** First Amendment limits — solicitation of crime is not protected speech; but mere advocacy or abstract discussion may be protected under *Brandenburg v. Ohio*

#### Review Truth

Solicitation requires an intentional communication that asks, commands, encourages, or counsels another person to commit a crime; mere fantasy or venting without a request does not constitute solicitation.
