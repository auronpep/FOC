## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The governing law is common-law Contracts, specifically delegation of duties. The verified outline placement is `53070300`, listed in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md` under Contracts / Third-Party Rights / Delegation of Duties.

Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 318 states the delegation rule. An obligor may generally delegate performance unless barred by public policy or the promise, but a promise requires performance by a particular person to the extent the obligee has a substantial interest in that person's performance or control. The same section also provides that delegation does not discharge the delegating obligor unless the obligee agrees otherwise. Source checked: https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/2nd%2BSemester/LAW%2B506-002%2B%E2%80%93%2BContracts%2BII/R2C%2B%C2%A7%2B318

UCC section 2-210 uses the same substantial-interest formulation for Article 2 delegation and confirms the general principle that a party may perform through a delegate unless the other party has a substantial interest in original performance or control. Source checked: https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/28%3A2-210

This item is a service/custom-art contract, so the common-law Restatement rule supplies the direct authority.
