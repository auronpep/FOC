### 7. Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** Naomi transferred all of her remaining interest in the lease to Stephen. Because she transferred everything — not just part — this was an assignment, not a sublease. **Gold Key (Sublet ≠ Assign):** Subletting means transferring only part of the remaining lease term while the original tenant retains a reversionary interest. Assignment means transferring all of the remaining interest with no reversion retained. The lease prohibited *subletting*. Because courts strictly construe restraints on alienation, a clause prohibiting subletting applies only to subleases — not to assignments. Caleb's restriction is limited to exactly what it says. The transfer to Stephen was not prohibited, so it was valid. Caleb cannot remove Stephen. **Silver Key (Two-No Clash):** When C and D both say "No," ask: would Caleb win if the missing fact D implies were present? If the lease *had* contained an express termination clause, could Caleb remove Stephen? No — because Naomi never breached the no-subletting clause (she assigned, not sublet). D's reason is affirmatively wrong, not just incomplete. Cut D and pick C.

### 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**B — Yes, because the lease prohibited alienation of the leasehold interest.**

B is the dominant trap. A student reads "the lease contained a clause prohibiting subletting" and hears "the lease restricted the tenant's ability to transfer the leasehold." Assignment is a transfer. The cognitive move: if the tenant was restricted from transferring and she transferred anyway, the landlord wins. **Gold Key (Sublet ≠ Assign):** The clause prohibited subletting — a specific form of transfer — not "alienation" broadly. Restraints on alienation are strictly construed; "no subletting" reaches only subleases. B rewrites the clause in language broader than the stem supports. The substitution of "alienation of the leasehold interest" for "subletting" is the fatal expansion — the choice adds doctrine the clause does not contain.

**A — Yes, because in the absence of a specific agreement to the contrary, a tenant may not transfer the entire balance of a leasehold interest without the landlord's express permission.**

A sells the intuition that tenants need permission to do anything with someone else's property. It sounds protective of landlord interests. But A reverses the baseline rule. A tenant has the power to assign a leasehold absent a restriction in the lease; the rule runs the opposite direction from what A states. A specific agreement *restricting* transfer — not one *permitting* it — is what changes the tenant's default freedom. Absent any covenant, assignment is permitted.

**D — No, because the lease did not contain an express reservation of Caleb's right to terminate the leasehold in the event of a breach.**

D reaches the right direction (No) but gives a legally wrong reason. A student who flagged the "no express termination reservation" language in the stem sees it as the factual key and builds the answer around it. The cognitive move: since there's no termination clause, Caleb cannot terminate, so the court won't rule for him. **Silver Key (Two-No Clash):** Test the premise: even if the lease *had* contained an express termination clause, Caleb still could not remove Stephen — because Naomi never committed a breach. She assigned the lease rather than subletting, so the no-subletting clause was never triggered. D implies Caleb would win with that clause, which is affirmatively false. The controlling reason Stephen stays is strict construction of the restraint, not the absence of a termination provision.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** Contractual restraints on alienation of leasehold interests are strictly construed. A covenant prohibiting subletting does not encompass assignment, because assignment (transfer of all remaining interest) and subletting (transfer of a partial interest, with the original tenant retaining a reversion) are legally distinct transfers. Absent a specific covenant restricting assignment, a tenant may freely assign a leasehold.

**Legal issue:** Whether a no-subletting clause in a written lease bars the tenant's assignment of the full remaining leasehold term.

**Credited outcome unchanged:** Caleb cannot remove Stephen. The assignment was valid because the no-subletting clause, strictly construed, does not reach assignments.

**Authority (lawyer-confirmation layer):**
- Restatement (Second) of Property (Landlord and Tenant) § 15.2 (1977): absent restriction, a tenant may transfer all or any part of a leasehold; consent requirements are strictly construed against the landlord.
- Ernst v. Conditt, 390 S.W.2d 703 (Tenn. Ct. App. 1964): distinguished sublease (partial transfer, reversion retained) from assignment (all interest transferred); held a no-sublet clause did not bar assignment.
- General common law: courts favor free alienability of property interests; covenants restricting alienation are construed narrowly.

**Key disagreement check:** None. The official key (D → new C) is consistent with governing rule and leading authority. Verdict: PASS.
