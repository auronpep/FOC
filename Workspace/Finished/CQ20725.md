**Silver Key:** Bracket the answer array. “No contract” is too narrow. “Every foreseeable purchaser” is too broad. The named-recipient answer is the middle lane.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice is attractive because foreseeability feels like the normal tort boundary. The breaker is the word **every**: this answer blows the reliance class open beyond the named recipient or limited group.

**C.** This choice is attractive because knowledge of falsity sounds like the safer mental-state requirement for imposing liability. The breaker is the call itself: this is **negligent** misrepresentation, and the stem gives **careless** false information, not a fraud-only knowledge requirement.

The transformed item preserves the original legal issue and outcome: liability for negligent misrepresentation by a professional information supplier to a specifically known recipient expected to rely in a business transaction. Restatement (Second) of Torts §552 imposes liability when a person in business or a profession supplies false information for others’ business guidance and fails to use reasonable care, with liability limited to the person or limited group for whose benefit the information is supplied or whom the supplier knows the recipient intends to reach.
