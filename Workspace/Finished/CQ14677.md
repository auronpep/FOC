### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C (not guilty of murder or manslaughter) is correct.**

Hannah raises a mistake-of-fact defense. She believed the rifle was a theatrical prop that could fire only blanks. Mistake of fact is a defense when it negates the state of mind required for the crime. Murder and manslaughter are both general-intent (or malice) crimes. For such crimes, the mistake of fact must be both honest and reasonable. Here, the jury believes Hannah's testimony and finds her mistake was reasonable. That reasonable mistake negates the mens rea for both murder and manslaughter, so she is not guilty of either.

**Gold Key (Mistake-of-Fact Gate):** For specific intent crimes, any genuine mistake of fact (even unreasonable) negates the specific intent. For general intent or malice crimes, the mistake must be both honest and reasonable. For strict liability crimes, mistake of fact is never a defense.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (guilty of murder) is incorrect.** This choice ignores the mistake-of-fact defense entirely. A reasonable mistake that negates the required mental state is a complete defense to murder — a malice/general-intent crime. **D (guilty of manslaughter) is incorrect.** This is the dominant trap. **C in the post-shuffle (guilty of either murder or manslaughter) is incorrect.** This is the false-compromise distractor. ### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule:** Mistake of fact is a defense to criminal liability when it negates the mens rea required for the offense. The scope of the defense depends on the type of crime charged:

1. **Specific intent crimes:** Any honest mistake of fact (reasonable or unreasonable) that negates the specific intent is a defense. *See* People v. Navarro, 205 Cal. App. 3d 1106 (1988) (genuine belief negates specific-intent theft regardless of reasonableness).
2. **General intent / malice crimes:** The mistake must be both honest *and* reasonable — i.e., the kind of mistake a reasonable person would have made under the circumstances. *See* CALCRIM 3406; Model Penal Code § 2.04(1)(a) (mistake negatives a material element of the offense).
3. **Strict liability crimes:** Mistake of fact is never a defense.

**Application:** Murder (malice aforethought) and manslaughter (general intent / unlawful act or criminal negligence) are both general intent or malice crimes. Hannah's honest and reasonable belief that the rifle was a prop negates the mental state for both. The defense is complete — not a reduction.

**Authority:** Model Penal Code § 2.04(1)(a); CALCRIM 3406; People v. Navarro, 205 Cal. App. 3d 1106 (1988) (specific-intent contrast); 2 W. LaFave, Substantive Criminal Law § 5.6 (general-intent reasonable-mistake requirement).
