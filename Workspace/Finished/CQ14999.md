## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**B is correct.** Hannah's deed used only purpose language: the land was conveyed
to the Good News Literacy Center "for the purpose of" constructing a
Scripture-copying studio. That phrase explains why Hannah made the conveyance,
but it does not say "so long as," "until," "on condition that," "provided that,"
or anything else that would make the Center's estate defeasible. Nor does it say
that the Center must hold the land in trust or that the land will revert if the
studio is not built. The Center therefore took fee simple absolute. Because the
Center owned Cedaracre in fee simple absolute, it could convey Cedaracre for valid
consideration to Barnabas Builders LLC. **Gold Key:** a bare "for the purpose of"
phrase is motive language, not a title limitation or trust duty. **Silver Key:**
run the title chain before probate: if Hannah already conveyed the fee, neither
her heir nor her will beneficiary receives that land at Hannah's death. The court
should quiet title in Barnabas Builders LLC.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A (Ruth - DOMINANT TRAP).** Ruth is attractive because Hannah's will sounds
broad: "all my estate, real and personal." The breaker is the title-chain timing.
At Hannah's death, Cedaracre was no longer part of Hannah's estate because she had
already deeded it to the Good News Literacy Center in fee simple absolute. **Gold
Key:** purpose words alone do not keep a slice of title in the grantor; if the
deed did not reserve a reverter, condition, or trust duty, the will has no land to
grab. **Silver Key:** always ask what the testator owned at death before giving
effect to "all my estate."

**C (the state attorney general).** The attorney general's theory is tempting
because the grantee is a charitable nonprofit and the deed states a religious
educational purpose. The breaker is that an official's power to enforce a true
charitable trust is not title, and no charitable trust was created by this deed.
The instrument lacks trust language, trustee duties, a mandatory use command, a
reverter, or a condition. **Gold Key:** a charitable purpose can explain a gift
without turning the grantee into a trustee. The state official therefore has no
title to quiet.

**D (Daniel).** Daniel is tempting because he is Hannah's sole heir at law. That
fact matters only if property passes by intestacy. Here, two separate blockers
defeat him. First, Hannah had a valid will leaving her estate to Ruth. Second,
and more importantly, Cedaracre was not in Hannah's estate when she died because
she had already conveyed it in fee simple absolute. Daniel's heir status answers
the wrong question.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

**Tested rule and issue.** The item tests deed construction and present estates:
whether a conveyance "to [grantee] for the purpose of [a stated use]" creates a
fee simple absolute or instead creates a defeasible fee, retained future interest,
or charitable trust. The credited rule is unchanged: absent clear limiting,
conditional, reverter, or trust language, a purpose statement does not cut down a
fee simple conveyance.

**Authority floor.** In *Koprivec v. Rails-to-Trails of Wayne County*,
2018-Ohio-465, the Supreme Court of Ohio treated a deed's "forever for the
purpose of constructing and using thereon a Rail Road" language as describing the
reason for the conveyance rather than conditioning the grantee's right to hold the
estate, and it relied on Restatement (First) of Property section 34 for the
default fee-simple-absolute principle. Source:
https://www.supremecourt.ohio.gov/rod/docs/pdf/0/2018/2018-ohio-465.pdf.
*Olcott v. Gabert*, 86 Tex. 121, 23 S.W. 985 (1893), likewise states the
common-law principle that a mere declaration of the use to which land is to be
applied ordinarily does not create a condition; it specifically discusses church
use language and the grantee's power to convey. Source:
https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/5914cf4aadd7b04934820856. For the trust
point, Uniform Trust Code section 402 requires an indication of intent to create a
trust; purpose language alone does not supply that missing intent. Source:
https://www.uniformlaws.org/HigherLogic/System/DownloadDocumentFile.ashx?DocumentFileKey=9b0ead4f-451d-81a1-290c-113e10097f2a&forceDialog=0.

**Application.** Hannah's deed did not use durational language ("so long as"),
conditional language ("on condition that" or "provided that"), a right of entry,
a possibility of reverter, or trust language. The phrase "for the purpose of
constructing a Scripture-copying studio" is a statement of motive. The Good News
Literacy Center therefore received fee simple absolute and later validly conveyed
the same title to Barnabas Builders LLC. Hannah's will did not transfer Cedaracre
to Ruth because Hannah no longer owned Cedaracre at death. Daniel took nothing as
heir for the same reason and because there was a valid will. The state attorney
general took no title because no charitable trust existed.

## distilled_core_question

A landowner deeds land to a Christian literacy nonprofit "for the purpose of"
building a studio, later dies with a son and a will to a friend, and the nonprofit
never builds the studio but sells the land to a buyer. Does the purpose phrase
leave title in the buyer, the heir, the will beneficiary, or the state official?

## call_and_prediction

The call asks who owns title now. Predict the current title-chain owner: the
buyer's title is good if the purpose phrase did not create a defeasible fee or
trust.

## trigger_facts

- Hannah deeded the land before death.
- The deed says only "for the purpose of" constructing a studio.
- The deed contains no condition, special limitation, reverter, or trust duty.
- Hannah's will is broad, but it operates only on property she owns at death.
- The Center later conveyed to Barnabas Builders for valid consideration.

## final_student_script

Start with the deed, not the will. The deed says "for the purpose of" building a
studio. It does not say "so long as," "until," "on condition that," "provided
that," "reverts," or "in trust." So the Center got fee simple absolute. The
Center sold to Barnabas Builders. Hannah's will and Daniel's heir status do not
move Cedaracre because Hannah no longer owned it when she died. Pick **B**.

## remediation_card

**RC-RP-PURPOSE-WORDS-FSA-14999 - Purpose Words Do Not Cut Down Title**

- Signal: A deed says land is conveyed "for the purpose of" a project.
- Student move: Look for actual limiting words before cutting down the grantee's
  title.
- Tiny rule: Purpose language is motive unless the instrument adds condition,
  reverter, limitation, or trust duty.
- Trap: choosing the will beneficiary or state official because the purpose
  sounds charitable.
- Confidence: ANCHOR_ASSISTED.

## Gold Key(s)

- **GK-REAL_PROPERTY-PURPOSE-FSA-01** (phrase): A deed phrase such as "for the
  purpose of" normally states motive; without clear words of limitation,
  condition, reverter, or trust duty, the grantee receives fee simple absolute.
  Unlocks the charitable-purpose and will-devisee traps. Authority:
  *Koprivec v. Rails-to-Trails of Wayne County*, 2018-Ohio-465; *Olcott v.
  Gabert*, 86 Tex. 121; Restatement (First) of Property section 34 as cited in
  *Koprivec*.
- **GK-REAL_PROPERTY-PROBATE-OWNED-AT-DEATH-01** (rule): A will transfers only
  property the testator owns at death; land already conveyed away in fee simple
  absolute is not part of the probate estate. Unlocks the will-devisee and heir
  traps. Authority: basic probate/title-chain principle applied to the fee simple
  conveyance.

## Silver Key(s)

- **SK-REAL_PROPERTY-TITLECHAIN-BEFORE-PROBATE-01** (call): In a quiet-title
  call with a deed before death and a will after it, run the deed first; probate
  can distribute only what remains after the lifetime conveyance.
