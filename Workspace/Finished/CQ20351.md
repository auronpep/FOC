## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct. Gold Key:** mutual mistake does not get you rescission if the party seeking rescission bore the risk of the mistake. The contract did that work here: Ruth accepted the course “as is,” including hidden subsurface drainage defects, whether known or unknown. **Silver Key:** when the answer set splits between “both were mistaken” and “risk was assigned,” read the as-is clause as the locking fact. ## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells a protective instinct: hidden defects feel too serious to be covered by boilerplate. The breaker is the word **always**; it turns a risk-allocation clause into a clause that can never allocate the very risk it names.

**B.** This choice sells a proof-condition story: Barnabas should have to prove Ruth personally checked every tunnel before she can bear the risk. The breaker is the invented prerequisite; the contract can allocate hidden-defect risk without a separate every-tunnel inspection showing.

**C.** This is the dominant trap. It sells the classic mutual-mistake move: both sides were wrong about an important existing fact. **Gold Key:** mutual mistake is not enough when the contract assigned that mistake-risk to the buyer.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The legal issue and credited outcome are unchanged from the original row: the supplied explanation says a genuine mutual mistake does not permit avoidance when the adversely affected party bears the risk, and the contract expressly assigned unknown hidden structural defects to the buyer. 

Lawyer-confirmation layer: Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 152 states that a mutual mistake can make a contract voidable only unless the adversely affected party bears the risk under § 154; § 154 states that a party bears the risk when it is allocated to that party by agreement. ([Open Casebook][1]) Cornell’s Wex summary states the same MBE-level element: mutual material mistake requires that the adversely affected party not bear the risk of the mistake under Restatement § 154. ([Legal Information Institute][2])

Application: Ruth is the adversely affected party, but the agreement expressly allocates hidden subsurface drainage defects to her. That defeats rescission.

## review_truth

A mutual mistake does not support rescission when the contract assigned that mistake-risk to the party seeking rescission.
