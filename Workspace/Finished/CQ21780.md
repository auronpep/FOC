## Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)
The court should admit neither. Under FRE 412(a), in any criminal proceeding involving alleged sexual misconduct, evidence offered to prove that a victim engaged in other sexual behavior or to prove a victim's sexual predisposition is not admissible. This includes reputation or opinion evidence that the victim "was thought of as a prostitute." The narrow exceptions in FRE 412(b)(1) — specific instances to prove someone other than the defendant was the source of semen/injury, specific instances with the defendant to prove consent, or evidence required by the defendant's constitutional rights — are not triggered here. The defense is alibi (identity), not consent, and the stem contains no facts about semen, injury source, or a constitutional claim that would require admission of this evidence.

The certified court record of the victim's prior misdemeanor prostitution conviction is likewise inadmissible. It constitutes evidence offered to prove the victim engaged in other sexual behavior (the act of prostitution), which FRE 412(a)(1) prohibits. Separately, even if analyzed under FRE 609 for impeachment, a misdemeanor conviction is admissible to attack credibility only if the crime involved dishonesty or false statement (FRE 609(a)(2)). Prostitution does not involve dishonesty or false statement as an element; it is not a crimen falsi. Therefore the conviction is inadmissible for impeachment. Because the stem raises only alibi and contains none of the 412(b) exception facts, both offers are properly excluded upon objection. The correct choice is C.

**Gold Key (for the 609/conviction component):** A misdemeanor conviction is admissible for impeachment under FRE 609(a)(2) only if the crime involved dishonesty or false statement. Prostitution lacks any element of deceit or false statement and therefore does not qualify, regardless of how "bad" the conduct sounds.

**D (court record only).** Attractive because prior convictions feel like classic impeachment material in any credibility fight, and the conviction is for the same type of conduct as the charged offense, making it seem especially probative of the victim's character or motive to lie. Prostitution does not. **Gold Key:** The 609 dishonesty/false-statement threshold defeats any pure-impeachment theory for this misdemeanor.

## Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested black-letter rule is the scope of FRE 412 (rape shield / exclusion of victim's past sexual behavior and predisposition evidence) in criminal sexual misconduct prosecutions, together with the parallel application of FRE 609 impeachment limits to a misdemeanor conviction that does not qualify as crimen falsi. The legal issue is whether reputation evidence of the victim as a "prostitute" and/or a certified record of the victim's prior misdemeanor prostitution conviction are admissible when the defense is alibi (no consent or source facts alleged).

Governing authority:
- Fed. R. Evid. 412(a)(1)–(2): In a criminal proceeding involving alleged sexual misconduct, evidence offered to prove that a victim engaged in other sexual behavior or to prove a victim's sexual predisposition is not admissible. Reputation and opinion evidence fall squarely within the prohibition.
- Fed. R. Evid. 412(b)(1)(A)–(C): The three narrow criminal-case exceptions (source of semen/injury/physical evidence from someone other than defendant; specific instances with the accused to prove consent; constitutional rights) are not satisfied on the facts given. Alibi is an identity defense, not consent. No semen, injury, or source facts are mentioned. No constitutional-compulsion argument is raised in the offer.
- Fed. R. Evid. 609(a)(2): Evidence of a criminal conviction for a misdemeanor is admissible to attack a witness's character for truthfulness only if the crime "involved dishonesty or false statement." Prostitution is a crime of moral turpitude in many jurisdictions but does not require proof of an element of deceit or falsification; therefore it does not qualify. (This limitation is long-standing and unaffected by recent amendments to other evidence rules.)

## Gold Key(s)
**GK-EVIDENCE-FRE412-01 (rule)**  
In a criminal case involving alleged sexual misconduct, FRE 412(a) prohibits evidence offered to prove that a victim engaged in other sexual behavior or a victim's sexual predisposition, including reputation evidence. The narrow exceptions in 412(b)(1) require specific facts (source of semen/injury, consent with the accused, or constitutional compulsion) that are absent when the only defense is alibi.

**GK-EVIDENCE-FRE609-01 (threshold)**  
Under FRE 609(a)(2), a misdemeanor conviction is admissible to attack a witness's character for truthfulness only if the crime involved dishonesty or false statement. Prostitution is a crime of moral turpitude in many places but does not contain an element of deceit or false statement and therefore does not qualify.

## Silver Key(s)
**SK-EVIDENCE-412-CUT-01 (cut)**  
When the call is "upon proper objection, which should the court admit?" in a sexual misconduct case and the first offer you see is reputation evidence that the victim "was thought of as a prostitute," immediately CUT every choice that contains that testimony before you analyze the conviction. 412(a)(2) is a total bar on predisposition/reputation evidence unless exception facts are present in the stem.
