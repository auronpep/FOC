# Pass-1 Transform Report — Q20580 (Criminal Law and Procedure · Solicitation)

## 1. Final Question

Paul asks Timothy to break into Ruth's lakeside cottage after midnight and steal a cedar chest containing handwritten Bible-study notes. Timothy says, "I will think about it," but does not commit. An hour later, Paul panics, calls Timothy, and says, "Do not go. I was wrong to ask." Timothy stays home and never goes near the cottage.

Paul is charged with solicitation to commit burglary. Under common law, what is the best result?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A person asks another to commit burglary, then withdraws before the other person agrees or acts. Is the solicitor guilty of common-law solicitation?

## 3. Final Answer Choices (Post-Shuffle)

**B.** Paul is not guilty because he withdrew before Timothy acted.

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**D is correct.** Common-law solicitation is complete when the defendant, with the purpose that a crime be committed, asks, encourages, or induces another person to commit that crime. Paul crossed that line when he asked Timothy to break into Ruth's cottage and steal the cedar chest. Timothy did not have to agree, travel to the cottage, or enter the cottage. Paul's later call may have prevented the burglary, but it did not undo the completed solicitation.

**Gold Key:** For common-law solicitation, freeze the offense at the communicated criminal request. Later remorse, no agreement, and no completed target crime do not erase the solicitation.

**Silver Key:** In an inchoate-crime timing question, identify the charged inchoate offense first. Solicitation lives at the request; attempt lives at a later step; burglary lives at completion.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### Choice A

**Why it attracts:** Timothy's "I will think about it" sounds like no deal, and students often import conspiracy's agreement requirement into solicitation. The breaker is that solicitation is a request crime, not an agreement crime. The solicited person's assent is not an element.

### Choice B — Dominant Trap

**Why it attracts:** Paul's callback feels morally important because it happens before Timothy does anything. The breaker is the timing of the completed offense. **Gold Key:** common-law solicitation is complete at the communicated criminal request, so later withdrawal does not erase the completed solicitation even though it may prevent the burglary.

### Choice C

**Why it attracts:** The answer ties guilt to the underlying burglary, which feels concrete because no one went near the cottage. The breaker is that solicitation is an inchoate offense. **Silver Key:** do not require the target crime's completion when the charge is solicitation; the legal event is the request.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer

The tested rule is unchanged: under common law, solicitation requires an intentional request, command, encouragement, or inducement that another person commit a crime, and the offense is complete when the solicitation is communicated. Agreement by the solicited person and completion of the target crime are not required. Later withdrawal or remorse does not undo the completed common-law solicitation.

Authority check: *State v. Keen*, 25 N.C. App. 567, 214 S.E.2d 242 (1975), recognizes that solicitation to commit a felony is complete with the solicitation even without acquiescence by the person solicited. *State v. Tyner*, 50 N.C. App. 206, 272 S.E.2d 626 (1980), likewise treats solicitation as complete with the act of solicitation and distinct from attempt. *State v. Thomas*, 01-863 (N.C. Ct. App. 2002), citing *State v. Davis*, 110 N.C. App. 272, 429 S.E.2d 403 (1993), describes solicitation as requiring counseling, enticing, or inducing another with intent that the crime be committed. Research sources reviewed: https://law.justia.com/cases/north-carolina/court-of-appeals/1975/7428sc965-1.html ; https://law.justia.com/cases/north-carolina/court-of-appeals/1980/8026sc587-1.html ; https://law.justia.com/cases/north-carolina/court-of-appeals/2002/01-863-6.html .

The official key is confirmed. Paul's request to Timothy supplies the actus reus. His purpose that Timothy commit burglary supplies the mens rea. Timothy's nonagreement, nonaction, and Paul's withdrawal all fail to defeat liability for the completed solicitation.
