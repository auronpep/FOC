## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question — revised stem + call

Before Lydia played handbells at a community hymn festival, her physician, Stephen, prescribed a prescription-only wrist nerve stimulator to control hand tremors. The stimulator carried a rare risk of finger numbness that could make fine hand movement difficult. Before Stephen ordered it, Barnabas Biotech sent Stephen an updated professional safety notice that clearly described the risk, symptoms, and alternative treatments. Stephen read the notice, discussed the risk with Lydia, and ordered the stimulator because alternatives were unsuitable. Lydia later suffered finger numbness and sued Barnabas Biotech, arguing that Barnabas did not send the same warning directly to her. The jurisdiction follows the learned-intermediary rule.

**Call:** Is Lydia likely to prevail on a strict products liability claim for failure to warn against Barnabas Biotech?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A prescription-only product had a clear risk warning sent to the prescribing physician, who read it and discussed it with the patient. The patient suffered the warned-of risk and sues because the manufacturer did not warn her directly. Does the failure-to-warn claim succeed?

### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.** Barnabas warned the prescribing physician before the device was ordered. Stephen read the warning, discussed the risk with Lydia, and ordered the stimulator only after considering the alternatives. **Gold Key:** In a learned-intermediary jurisdiction, a prescription-product manufacturer can satisfy its warning duty by giving an adequate warning to the prescribing physician or other appropriate healthcare provider. The claim fails because the proper warning audience was warned.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells a clean injury story: the warning named finger numbness, and Lydia suffered finger numbness. **Silver Key:** On a failure-to-warn call, “the warned-of risk happened” proves injury; it does not prove a warning defect.

**B.** This choice sells a total-immunity shortcut: prescription products feel special, so the student may treat them as outside products liability altogether. The breaker is the word **all**. Prescription products are not categorically exempt; this claim fails because the warning duty was satisfied.

**C.** This is the dominant trap. It sells the intuitive patient-safety frame: the patient used the product, so the patient must always receive the manufacturer’s warning directly. **Gold Key:** Learned intermediary cuts that “always” move. In this jurisdiction, an adequate warning to the prescribing physician can satisfy the manufacturer’s duty.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The source row identifies QID 19434 as a Torts products-liability learned-intermediary item, gives the official key as B, and states the rule reference: a prescription-product manufacturer may satisfy its warning duty by giving adequate warnings to the prescribing physician as learned intermediary. 

Legal verification supports the same result. Restatement (Third) of Torts: Products Liability § 6(d) treats prescription drugs and medical devices as not reasonably safe due to inadequate warnings when reasonable warnings are not provided to prescribing and other healthcare providers positioned to reduce the risk; it also recognizes direct-patient warning duties only in special circumstances where providers are not positioned to reduce the risk. ([Biotech Law][1]) Courts applying the learned-intermediary doctrine state the same core rule: adequate warnings to prescribing physicians generally discharge the manufacturer’s duty to warn the patient directly. ([Justia][2])

The transformed item keeps the tested rule, issue, and credited outcome unchanged. The product changed from a prescription medication to a prescription-only medical device, but the learned-intermediary rule applies to both prescription drugs and medical devices under Restatement § 6(d). ([Biotech Law][1])

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-TORTS-LEARNED-INTERMEDIARY-01**

For prescription products, an adequate warning to the prescribing healthcare provider can satisfy the manufacturer’s warning duty; the manufacturer is not automatically required to warn the patient directly.

Type: `distinction`.

Trigger: prescription-only product + warning to prescriber + jurisdiction follows learned-intermediary rule.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-TORTS-WARNED-RISK-01**

On a failure-to-warn call, an answer saying the warned-of risk happened proves injury, not warning defect.

Type: `trap_spotting`.

Trigger: answer points to injury by the described risk instead of the adequacy or audience of the warning.
