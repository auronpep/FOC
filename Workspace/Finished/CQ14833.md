### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**Gold Key:** Under FRE 602, a witness is competent to testify about a matter if there is evidence sufficient to support a finding that the witness has personal knowledge of it — and the witness's own testimony about what they observed is itself enough to establish that foundation. FRE 408 excludes evidence of settlement conduct only when offered to prove or disprove the validity or amount of a disputed claim; it does not bar a witness from testifying about facts they personally observed just because those facts were learned during mediation.

Paul personally inspected the soundboard at mediation. That observation gave him personal knowledge of the crack pattern. The fact that the inspection happened during settlement talks is irrelevant to whether Paul is competent to testify about what he saw. The soundboard's disappearance doesn't change the competency analysis either — Paul can testify from present recollection of his observation. The testimony is admissible.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (dominant trap):** This choice is attractive because FRE 408 is a heavily tested rule, and students instinctively want to apply it whenever "settlement negotiations" appear. The trap is conflating two separate evidentiary gates: the admissibility of compromise offers/statements to prove a claim's validity (FRE 408) versus the competency of a witness to testify from personal knowledge (FRE 602). Paul isn't offering the settlement conduct to prove liability — he's testifying about what he personally saw. **Gold Key:** FRE 408 does not bar a witness from testifying about facts personally observed just because the observation occurred during mediation.

**B:** This choice invents a requirement that doesn't exist. There is no rule requiring a witness to prove they weren't at fault for a piece of physical evidence to disappear before they can testify about what they observed. The competency question under FRE 602 turns solely on whether the witness has personal knowledge, not on chain-of-custody or fault for loss.

**D:** This choice reaches the right outcome (admissible) but for the wrong reason. Whether Peter's expert examined the soundboard has zero bearing on Paul's competency to testify. A witness's personal knowledge is established by their own perception, not by whether someone else also examined the item.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**FRE 602 (Need for Personal Knowledge):** "A witness may testify to a matter only if evidence is introduced sufficient to support a finding that the witness has personal knowledge of the matter. Evidence to prove personal knowledge may consist of the witness's own testimony." (LII, Cornell)

**FRE 601 (General Competency):** Every person is competent to be a witness unless these rules provide otherwise. The competency gate is low — personal knowledge under FRE 602 is the primary filter.

**FRE 408 (Compromise Offers and Negotiations):** Excludes evidence of settlement conduct only when offered "to prove or disprove the validity or amount of a disputed claim." It does not bar testimony about facts a witness personally observed during mediation when offered for another purpose (here, the witness's own perception of a physical item).

**Authority:** Federal Rules of Evidence 601, 602, 408 (LII, Cornell). The Advisory Committee Notes to Rule 602 confirm that personal knowledge "may consist of what the witness thinks he knows from personal perception" and that the witness's own testimony can furnish the foundation.
