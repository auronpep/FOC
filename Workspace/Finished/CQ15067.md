### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Titles, adverse possession requirements, and the verified outline code is `84040101`. Adverse possession requires possession that is actual, open and notorious, exclusive, hostile or adverse, and continuous for the statutory period. In [Nome 2000 v. Fagerstrom, 799 P.2d 304 (Alaska 1990)](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/nome-2000-v-fagerstrom-894899707), the court stated the familiar adverse-possession elements and emphasized that the nature of the required acts depends on the character of the land. [Lessee of Ewing v. Burnet, 36 U.S. 41 (1837)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/36/41/) confirms that color of title can support adverse possession only when paired with visible acts of ownership and actual adverse possession for the limitations period. [Mannillo v. Gorski, 54 N.J. 378 (1969)](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-jersey/supreme-court/1969/54-n-j-378-0.html) supports the point that mistaken boundary possession can still be hostile when the possessor claims the occupied land.

Applied here, Manna Meadow fails because Daniel's conduct was too sporadic, nonexclusive, and noncontinuous. The invalid deed gave him color of title, but not the missing possession. The eight-foot strip succeeds because Daniel visibly occupied and excluded others from the strip with trees and a fence, did so under a claim up to the fence, and maintained that condition for 18 years, longer than the 15-year statutory period.
