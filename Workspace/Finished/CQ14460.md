### 1. Final question

Before Mary laid a tile, Timothy, Peter’s choir friend and an avid collector of Christian art, said in front of both Peter and Mary that he would not approve Mary’s sketch. That rejection mattered because Peter and Mary had already signed a detailed writing: Mary would cover the side of Peter’s privately owned vintage camper with a fish-and-loaves mosaic according to Mary’s attached sketch, and Peter would pay $18,000 at completion. Peter’s sister Ruth was not a party to the agreement and owned no interest in the camper. At Peter’s insistence, the writing said that neither party would be bound unless Timothy approved Mary’s sketch. After Timothy rejected the sketch, Peter nevertheless told Mary to go ahead with the mosaic. Mary reluctantly began and completed 45% of the work. Peter then repudiated any duty to pay the contract price or any part of it.

If Mary now sues Peter for damages for breach of contract, which concept best supports Mary’s claim?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** Peter’s duty to pay was tied to Timothy’s approval, but Peter chose to go forward after Timothy rejected the sketch. That conduct waived the approval condition. Mary then materially relied on that waiver by completing 45% of the mosaic. **Gold Key:** a protected party who orders performance after an express condition fails has waived the condition, and after material reliance the waiver cannot be revoked. **Silver Key:** lock the call before the labels: this is a breach-of-contract claim on a signed writing, so the contract-lane concept beats backup recovery labels.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. Promissory estoppel.** This choice attracts the student who sees Mary relying on Peter’s instruction to continue. The breaker is the call: Mary is suing for damages for breach of contract on a signed writing, so reliance matters here because it makes the waiver of the condition irrevocable, not because the case needs promissory estoppel as the main theory. **Silver Key:** when the stem gives a contract and the call asks for breach, do not jump to a backup reliance label unless the contract lane is blocked.

**C. Unjust enrichment.** This is the dominant trap because it feels fair: Peter received part of a fish-and-loaves mosaic and then refused to pay. The breaker is the contract lane. The writing gives Mary a breach-of-contract route, and unjust enrichment is not the best support for that assigned claim.

**D. Substantial performance.** This choice attracts the student who sees partial work and reaches for a performance doctrine. The breaker is the condition lane: the problem is not whether 45% completion is enough performance; the problem is that Timothy’s approval failed and Peter then ordered Mary to proceed anyway. **Gold Key:** the work after the failed approval condition matters because it is reliance on waiver, not because it is substantial performance.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The source row tests a Contracts question on Performance, Breach, and Discharge; it supplies the official credited concept, pick rates, and explanation that the owner waived the condition by ordering work after the condition failed and that reliance made the waiver nonrevocable. 

Lawyer-confirmation layer: Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 84 treats a promise to perform a conditional duty despite non-occurrence of a condition as binding, with reinstatement limited where it would be unjust because of material change of position; § 246 treats acceptance or retention of performance with knowledge of a failed condition as a promise to perform under § 84. ## Gold Key(s)

**GK-CONTRACTS-WAIVER-CONDITION-01**
A party protected by an express condition can waive that condition by ordering performance after the condition fails; after the other party materially relies, the waiver cannot be revoked. Authority: Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§ 84, 246; Clark v. West. ([Law Insider][1])

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-CONTRACTS-WAIVER-CONDITION-01**
Lock the call before the labels: the question asks for the concept supporting a breach-of-contract claim, so backup recovery labels move behind the contract-lane answer unless the contract lane is blocked.
