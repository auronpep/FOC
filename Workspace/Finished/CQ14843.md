### 1. Final question

During litigation on a federal claim arising from a dispute over a private Christian board-game expo, Peter had the burden of proving that Martha received a written notice cancelling her exhibit slot for the “Manna Mail” booth. ### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.** Peter’s proof of proper mailing supports a presumption of receipt. Martha’s denial supplies rebuttal evidence. After that, the jury is not forced to find receipt and is not forced to find nonreceipt. The jury may credit the mailing inference and find receipt.

**Gold Key:** In federal civil cases, a presumption shifts only the burden of producing rebuttal evidence; it does not shift the burden of persuasion.

**Silver Key:** Read the modal words first. The array has “must find receipt,” “must find nonreceipt,” “burden shifts,” and “may find receipt.” The only answer that leaves credibility with the jury is the permissive “may” answer.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells the strongest trap: the idea that a presumption makes the other side disprove the presumed fact. The breaker is the word **persuade**. The presumption can require Martha to come forward with rebuttal evidence, but it does not move the burden of persuasion to her. **Gold Key:** production may shift; persuasion stays with the party who had it.

**B.** This choice gives the mailing presumption conclusive force. The breaker is **must**. Once Martha testifies to nonreceipt, the factfinder may still infer receipt from proper mailing, but the jury is not required to find receipt.

**C.** This choice treats rebuttal as an automatic win for Martha. The breaker is again **must**. Rebuttal prevents a forced receipt finding, but it does not force the opposite finding. The jury may still credit the mailing evidence.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

Federal Rule of Evidence 301 states that, in civil cases, a presumption shifts the burden of producing evidence to rebut the presumption, but does **not** shift the burden of persuasion, which remains with the party who originally had it. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) The Supreme Court has recognized the mailbox presumption: proof that a properly directed letter was placed in the post office creates a presumption that it reached its destination and was received in the ordinary time. ([Justia Law][2]) The Advisory Committee and conference materials reflected in Rule 301 also preserve the distinction between a required presumption and a permissible inference after contrary evidence is introduced; the factfinder may infer the presumed fact but is not forced to do so. ([Legal Information Institute][1])
