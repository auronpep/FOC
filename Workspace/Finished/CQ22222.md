## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

The objection should not be sustained. At common law, the spousal communications privilege protects confidential communications made between spouses during a valid marriage. The privilege survives the dissolution of the marriage, so former spouses can still invoke it to protect marital communications made while they were married.

However, the spousal communications privilege contains a critical exception: it does **not** apply when the former spouses are parties in a civil action against each other. Here, Rachel and David are litigation former spouses engaged in a custody dispute — a civil action between them. Because the action is between the spouses, the privilege is defeated, and Rachel's statements are admissible.

**Silver Key:** When a privilege question has a general rule and a specific exception that applies to the facts, spot the exception first — the exam tests the exception, not the rule.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing rule:** The spousal communications privilege protects confidential communications made between spouses during a valid marriage. The privilege belongs to both spouses and prevents either from being compelled to disclose such communications. *See* 8 J. Wigmore, Evidence § 2332 (McNaughton rev. 1961); Cornell LII Institute, Spousal Privilege (last reviewed April 2021).

**Survival after divorce:** The spousal communications privilege generally survives the end of the marriage. Former spouses may invoke the privilege to protect communications made during the marriage. *See* 8 J. Wigmore, Evidence § 2332; *In re Fisher's Estate*, 114 N.Y.S.2d 836 (N.Y. Sur. Ct. 1952).

**Exception — action between spouses:** The spousal communications privilege does not apply when the spouses are suing each other in a civil case or when one spouse initiates a criminal proceeding against the other. *See* 8 J. Wigmore, Evidence § 2340 (limiting the privilege to actions not between the spouses); *State v. Smith*, 333 P.3d 1174 (Or. Ct. App. 2014) (spousal communications privilege inapplicable in litigation between spouses); *People v. Mills*, 2012 IL App (3d) 110553 (no privilege where spouses are adverse parties in civil action).

**Verification of credited outcome:** The original key (A) is correct. The spousal communications privilege is defeated by the interspousal-suit exception. The custody battle is a civil action between the former spouses. The credited outcome is **unchanged**.
