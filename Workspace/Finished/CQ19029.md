## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question

Martha owned a vacant city lot where she wanted to open a ticketed Scripture-themed garden walk for families. After negotiation, Martha hired Timothy, a builder, to construct a prayer path, teaching pavilion, lighting stations, and a tall entry arch on the land according to specifications furnished by Martha. Martha and Timothy entered into a written contract by which Timothy agreed to begin construction on March 3, to be finished with everything but the entry arch by June 10, and to complete construction of the entry arch by July 25. The contract price was $96,000, to be paid as follows: $32,000 on March 3, $32,000 upon completion of everything but the entry arch, and the balance of $32,000 upon completion of the entry arch.

Timothy began work on March 3, after receiving $32,000 from Martha. By June 10, Timothy completed construction of everything but the entry arch in accordance with the specifications. On June 10, Timothy demanded that Martha pay him $32,000, but Martha refused to do so.

Which of the following statements is most correct concerning Timothy's rights?

## 6. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** Timothy completed the milestone that triggered Martha's next payment duty. Martha then refused to make the payment the contract required at that point. That breach excuses Timothy from going forward with the remaining entry-arch work, so Timothy may refuse further performance without becoming the breaching party.

**Gold Key:** A material failure to perform a due exchange duty excuses the other party's remaining exchange performance.

**Silver Key:** When the call asks for the contractor's rights after a missed progress payment, cut exact-dollar answers that lack cost-to-complete facts before resolving the stop-work clash.

## 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This is the dominant trap. It sells the familiar "finish first, sue later" instinct in a construction contract. The breaker is that Martha's progress payment was already due after Timothy completed the first milestone. **Gold Key:** a material missed payment can excuse Timothy's remaining work; he does not have to build the entry arch before treating Martha's refusal as a breach.

**C.** This answer is attractive because $32,000 is the installment Timothy demanded and Martha refused to pay. The breaker is the word **limited**: Timothy's damages are not automatically capped at the missed installment, because a nonbreaching builder may also seek losses caused by the breach, including profit on the unfinished work if proved.

**D.** This answer is attractive because $64,000 is the unpaid balance of the contract price. The breaker is that the unpaid balance is not necessarily the damages measure. Any award for the unfinished work must account for costs Timothy avoided by not having to complete that work. **Silver Key:** exact-dollar damages choices are suspect when the stem gives no cost-to-complete facts.

## 8. Full black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

The governing law is common-law contracts, not UCC Article 2, because the agreement is for construction services and improvements on land. The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged: when one party materially fails to perform a due contractual duty, the other party's remaining exchange performance is excused or suspended.

Authority floor: Restatement (Second) of Contracts section 237 states the core rule that each party's remaining duties are conditioned on there being no uncured material failure by the other party to render an earlier due performance. Public source checked: https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/2nd%2BSemester/LAW%2B506-002%2B%E2%80%93%2BContracts%2BII/R2C%2B%C2%A7%2B237. Restatement section 240 supports the installment structure when part performances are agreed equivalents. Public source checked: https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/2nd%2BSemester/LAW%2B506-002%2B%E2%80%93%2BContracts%2BII/R2C%2B%C2%A7%2B240. Restatement section 241 supplies the materiality factors. Public source checked: https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/2nd%2BSemester/LAW%2B506-002%2B%E2%80%93%2BContracts%2BII/R2C%2B%C2%A7%2B241.

Martha's refusal to pay the second installment after Timothy completed the corresponding construction milestone is a material failure to render performance due at an earlier time. Timothy is therefore excused from continuing with the entry-arch work and may refuse further performance without liability for breach. The damages distractors are also unstable under Restatement section 347, which measures expectation damages by the loss in value plus other loss, less costs or losses avoided.
