## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question
Paul owns a small Christian woodworking shop that makes olive-wood display stands for Scripture-memory cards. In a signed writing, Paul contracted with Lydia's private retreat-bookstore business to sell 60 identical display stands, 30 stands to be delivered on July 10 and the remaining 30 on August 10. The writing did not say where delivery would occur or when or where Lydia had to pay. Which of the following statements is correct?

### 2. Distilled Core Question
When an Article 2 sales contract leaves the delivery place and payment terms blank, where is the first lot tendered and when must the buyer pay for that lot?

### 3. Final answer choices
A. Paul must deliver the 30 stands on July 10, and Lydia must pay the price for those stands within a reasonable time after delivery.

B. Paul must deliver the 30 stands on July 10, but Lydia's payment is due only when all 60 stands have been delivered.

C. Paul must tender the 30 stands to Lydia at Lydia's retreat-bookstore business on July 10, but does not have to turn them over until Lydia pays the price for those 30 stands.

D. Paul has no duty to tender the 30 stands on July 10 at Paul's woodworking shop unless Lydia tenders the price for those 30 stands on that date.

### 5. Correct answer
D (original key: B)

### 7. Full right-answer explanation
D is correct. The writing leaves two different blanks: delivery place and payment terms. Gold Key: in an Article 2 sale with no delivery place, the default place is the seller's place of business; when payment time and place are not stated, payment is due when and where the buyer receives the goods, and tender of payment is a condition to the seller's duty to complete delivery. Silver Key: test the choices in order, delivery place first and payment timing second. D is the only choice that keeps the first lot at Paul's shop and makes Lydia's payment tender the condition for completing that July 10 delivery.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because "within a reasonable time" is a familiar gap-filler phrase, and it sounds fair to let Lydia inspect the stands before paying later. The breaker is that this is the wrong gap-filler for payment: the Article 2 payment default is tied to the time and place where the buyer receives the goods, not to some later reasonable-time window. Gold Key: the specific open-payment rule beats the generic reasonable-time instinct.

B is attractive because the two delivery dates can make the contract feel like one indivisible bargain, with payment waiting until the whole 60-stand order is complete. The breaker is that the writing calls for delivery in lots, and the first lot's payment is not postponed until the second lot arrives. The payment duty for the July 10 lot is tied to the July 10 receipt/tender, not to final completion in August.

C is attractive because it preserves the seller-protective idea that Paul need not hand over the stands without payment. The breaker is the delivery place. Silver Key: a half-right answer still loses when it moves the tender to Lydia's business even though the default delivery place is Paul's place of business.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Contracts, Contract Content and Meaning, Gap-Fillers, and the verified outline code is `52040100`. This is a sale of goods governed by Article 2. UCC 2-308(a) provides that, absent agreement, the place for delivery is the seller's place of business. UCC 2-310(a) provides that, absent agreement, payment is due at the time and place where the buyer is to receive the goods. UCC 2-511(1) makes tender of payment a condition to the seller's duty to tender and complete delivery, and UCC 2-507(1) makes the seller's tender a condition to the buyer's duty to accept and pay. Because the agreement requires delivery in two lots, UCC 2-307 also supports demanding the apportioned price for the lot when that lot is tendered. The authorities checked were UCC 2-308, UCC 2-310, UCC 2-511, UCC 2-507, and UCC 2-307 at Cornell LII: https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-308, https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-310, https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-511, https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-507, and https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-307. Those rules confirm that the credited outcome is unchanged: Paul need only tender the first lot at his own shop, and Lydia's tender of the price for that lot is due as part of the July 10 exchange.
