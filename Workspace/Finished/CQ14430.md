### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B best supports Timothy’s suit. The $75 condition means Timothy did not yet have a binding option before September 15. If Timothy had not learned of Daniel’s lease to Naomi before Daniel received Timothy’s payment-and-acceptance letter, Timothy can argue that he accepted the still-open offer within the stated time.

**Gold Key:** A failed option is not the same thing as a revoked offer; the offeror may have the power to revoke, but the offeree’s power of acceptance ends only when revocation is received or the offeree receives reliable information of an inconsistent act.

3. **True/responsive version:** “The $75 payment was actually received before Daniel leased the Psalm Room to Naomi.”
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Traditional option doctrine requires a binding basis for irrevocability; where the writing expressly conditions option status on actual receipt of consideration, an oral reassurance or unpaid recital does not make the option already binding. Berryman v. Kmoch is consistent with the traditional bar-exam rule. ([Justia][2])

### Choice B

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** B gives the notice fact. It answers the revocation-before-acceptance problem.
2. **Student label:** true and responsive residual.
3. **True/responsive version:** Already true and responsive.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Under Restatement §43, an inconsistent sale or lease terminates the offeree’s power of acceptance only when the offeree acquires reliable information of that fact before accepting. ([Open Casebook][1]) Berryman applies the same sale-plus-knowledge principle in an unpaid land-option setting. ([Justia][2])

### Choice C

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** C tells you what the parties do for work; it does not tell you whether Timothy accepted before revocation.
2. **Student label:** professional-status misfit.
3. **True/responsive version:** “This was a signed merchant offer for goods under UCC §2-205.” That still would not fit this lease.
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** UCC §2-205 applies to merchant offers to buy or sell goods. This is a common-law real-property lease formation problem, so professional status does not make the offer irrevocable. ([Legal Information Institute][3])

### Choice D

1. **Student-accessible C3 signal:** D shows Timothy was preparing privately; it does not show Daniel received acceptance or that Timothy lacked notice of revocation.
2. **Student label:** reliance-preparation wrong element.
3. **True/responsive version:** “Timothy mailed or delivered an acceptance before receiving reliable information that Daniel had leased the studio to Naomi.”
4. **Lawyer confirmation:** Preparatory financing is not itself acceptance, and it is not the substantial, foreseeable reliance needed to make an unaccepted offer binding as an option. The official explanation for the original row treats this reliance theory as insufficient.

## 12. remediation_card

**Card:** CON-FORM-OPTION-REVOCATION-01
**Title:** Revocable is not revoked.
**Signal:** Unpaid option consideration plus later acceptance.
**Student move:** Separate option status from offer status.
**Tiny rule:** No option means the offer can be revoked; it does not mean it already ended.
**Trap:** Treating failed irrevocability as automatic revocation.
**Confidence:** ANCHOR_ASSISTED.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-CONTRACTS-PSALM-ROOM-01 — distinction**
A failed option is not the same as a revoked offer; an offeree may still accept a revocable offer before effective revocation. Trigger this when the option payment was not made but the offeree later sends payment and acceptance within the stated time.

**GK-CONTRACTS-PSALM-ROOM-02 — threshold**
When option status is expressly conditioned on actual payment, an unpaid recital does not make the option already binding. Trigger this when a choice says the recital makes the deal binding despite a payment-before-option condition.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-CONTRACTS-PSALM-ROOM-01 — call_focus**
Do not stop at “no option.” Ask the next call question: was the revocable offer actually revoked before acceptance?
