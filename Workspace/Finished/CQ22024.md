# CQ22024 — Hannah's Bethel House and Lot (Class Gifts / Rule of Convenience)

## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question (stem + call)

When Hannah married her husband Daniel, Daniel already had a son, Timothy, from a previous marriage, and had also adopted a son, Stephen, and was raising him as his own. After their marriage, Hannah and Daniel had a son together, Samuel. When Hannah died, her will left a house and lot in the town of Bethel:

> "to all the children of my husband Daniel, including any child he may adopt and whether born of our marriage or of a former one."

At the time of Hannah's death, Daniel was living and had the three children described above.

**Call:** At the time of Hannah's death, which of the following best describes the interest in the Bethel property held by Samuel, the child born to both Hannah and Daniel?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A will makes an immediate outright gift of land to "all the children of [a living person]." At the testator's death, at least one child of that person is already alive and ascertained. How is the interest of one of those existing children classified?

### 3. Final answer choices (A–D, post-shuffle)

- **C.** Vested subject to partial divestment, since Daniel may have more children in his lifetime.
- **D.** Contingent, since Daniel may subsequently adopt other children already living at the time of Hannah's death.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**A is correct.** This is an immediate, outright class gift — no life estate in front of it — so it takes effect the instant Hannah dies. **Gold Key:** under the rule of convenience, a class gift closes as soon as it takes effect if at least one member is then alive and ascertained. At Hannah's death, Daniel already had three living, ascertained children (Timothy, Stephen, Samuel), so the class closed immediately; no one born or adopted later can join. With the class closed, Samuel's share is fixed and cannot shrink — **absolutely vested**.

**Silver Key:** Samuel is a living, ascertained taker facing no stated condition, so his interest is plainly *vested* — that alone kills "contingent" and "void," leaving only the open-vs-closed question, which the rule of convenience answers.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

- **C (dominant trap).** It tracks the most natural instinct: Daniel is alive and could father more children, so the class "must" stay open and existing shares are vested subject to partial divestment. That is exactly right *for an open class* — and exactly why students pick it. The breaker is the **Gold Key**: because a member was alive and ascertained when the gift took effect at Hannah's death, the rule of convenience closed the class then and there, so no later child can divest Samuel.
- **D.** It feels lawyerly: membership looks unsettled because Daniel might later adopt other children already living, so the interest seems "contingent" on who turns out to be in the class. The breaker is the **Silver Key** plus the **Gold Key**: an interest is contingent only if *this* taker faces a condition precedent or is unascertained — Samuel is neither. The possibility that *others* might join a class never makes an ascertained member's share contingent (and here the class is closed anyway).
- **B.** It borrows the language of perpetuities — "a person not in being may affect the right, so it is void" — which sounds like a real invalidating rule. The breaker is that no such rule exists here: the chance that future people affect a class makes a share *subject to open* at most, never void; and the class closed immediately regardless, so no afterborn matters.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning (lawyer-confirmation layer)

**Tested rule (unchanged from the original):** the **rule of convenience** (class-closing rule). A class gift "to the children of X" admits every class member in being and ascertained when the gift takes effect in possession; once at least one member is then entitled to take, the class closes and later-born (or later-added) members are excluded. For an immediate testamentary gift, the gift takes effect at the testator's death.

**Application:** Hannah's devise is immediate and outright. At her death, Daniel had three living, ascertained children, all within the expressly defined class. The class therefore closed at Hannah's death; Samuel is a member, takes a presently possessory, fixed share, and — because the class is closed — his interest is not subject to open. It is absolutely vested.

**Authority floor (real):** *Andrews v. Partington* (1791), 3 Bro. C.C. 401 — the leading statement of the rule of convenience: a class closes when a member first becomes entitled to call for distribution. Supported by the Restatement (Third) of Property: Wills and Other Donative Transfers (class-closing rules). Vested-vs-contingent classification: an interest is contingent only if subject to a condition precedent or held by an unascertained person.

**Credited outcome:** UNCHANGED. Absolutely vested (class closed immediately). (Original key B ≡ post-shuffle A.) C3 residual agrees with the official key → **PASS**.
