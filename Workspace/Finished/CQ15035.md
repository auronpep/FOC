## 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

**Pass-1 Step 1 — tested black-letter rule.** Misuse, excessive use, or expanded use of an easement generally does not terminate or forfeit the easement. The ordinary remedy is an injunction against misuse or damages, unless the unauthorized use is inseverable, willful, and substantial enough that no lesser remedy can protect the servient estate.

The source row’s official explanation states the same rule: increased use of the easement on one day of the week does not terminate the easement, and the servient holder’s remedy for excessive use is injunction or damages, not forfeiture. 

Legal research confirms the rule. *Vieth v. Dorsch* states that the servient owner’s rights can be protected by enjoining misuse without forfeiting legitimate use, and it describes injunction as the proper and adequate remedy for unauthorized, excessive, or misused easement use. ([Justia Law][1]) *Sommer v. Misty Valley, LLC* likewise quotes the general rule that misuse or excessive use is not sufficient for abandonment or forfeiture, and states that termination is an extreme remedy used only when misuse cannot be severed and prohibited. ([Justia Law][2])
