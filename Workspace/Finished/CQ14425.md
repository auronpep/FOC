### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**Gold Key:** Under UCC § 2-206(1)(b), shipment of nonconforming goods is *normally* treated as both an acceptance and a breach — but if the seller **seasonably notifies** the buyer that the shipment is offered **only as an accommodation to the buyer**, the shipment is **not an acceptance** and therefore creates **no contract**. It operates instead as a **counteroffer** (an offer to sell the goods as shipped).

Timothy's note — "Oak is back-ordered... sending you 50 board-feet at the same price of $200. Hope it works for your project" — is the classic accommodation notification. It is seasonable (same day as delivery), it identifies the nonconformity (maple vs. oak), and it frames the shipment as something offered for the buyer's use, not as an acceptance of Barnabas's order. Because Timothy seasonably notified Barnabas that the maple was sent only as an accommodation, the shipment did **not** accept Barnabas's offer, and Timothy is **not** in breach.

The result: Barnabas may accept Timothy's counteroffer by keeping the maple (paying the $200 price) or reject the maple — but since no contract was formed, he has **no breach remedies** against Timothy.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Distractor A (original C, 38% — DOMINANT TRAP):**
*What makes it attractive:* Students see nonconforming goods shipped and instinctively apply the default UCC § 2-206(1)(b) rule: nonconforming shipment = acceptance + breach, with buyer entitled to damages and seller retaining a right to cure. This is the "master rule" before the exception. The distractor is well-constructed — it includes "subject to Timothy's right to cure," which is the precise language of UCC § 2-508, making it sound lawyerly and complete.
*Why it fails:* The accommodation notification exception negates the premise. Timothy's note explicitly stated the maple was being sent as a substitute, not as an acceptance of the oak order. The Gold Key unlocks this: notification + "only as an accommodation" means no acceptance occurred, so no breach occurred, so no cure right applies.

**Distractor B (original A, 5%):**
*What makes it attractive:* This distractor also assumes a breach occurred (allowing recovery for "total breach of contract") but frames the buyer's rejection remedy as more aggressive — rejecting and suing for damages. Students who know a contract was breached but miss the accommodation nuance might gravitate here.
*Why it fails:* It repeats the core false premise that Timothy's shipment was an acceptance and breach. Because the accommodation exception applies, Timothy never breached. No contract = no breach = no damages.

**Distractor D (original D, 2%):**
*What makes it attractive:* This one adds a cover requirement — the buyer "may reject the maple **provided that he promptly covers** by obtaining conforming oak from another supplier." Cover and reject language mirrors the remedy structure students see in breach-of-contract courses (UCC § 2-712). It sounds like a sophisticated buyer-protection answer.
*Why it fails:* Cover presupposes a breach. Under UCC § 2-712, a buyer may cover only when the seller has breached. Timothy's accommodating shipment was a counteroffer, not a breach, so the cover obligation never arises.

### 9. Black-Letter Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing authority:** UCC § 2-206(1)(b):

> An order or other offer to buy goods for prompt or current shipment shall be construed as inviting acceptance either by a prompt promise to ship or by the prompt or current shipment of conforming **or non-conforming goods**, but such shipment of non-conforming goods **does not constitute an acceptance if the seller seasonably notifies the buyer that the shipment is offered only as an accommodation to the buyer**.

**Rule breakdown:**
1. **Default rule:** An order to buy goods for current shipment invites acceptance by shipping the goods — even nonconforming ones. Under the default, a nonconforming shipment = acceptance + simultaneous breach.
2. **Accommodation exception:** If the seller **seasonably notifies** the buyer that the shipment is offered **only as an accommodation**, the shipment is **not an acceptance** and therefore not a breach.
3. **Effect of the exception:** The nonconforming shipment operates as a **counteroffer** (an offer to sell the goods as delivered). The buyer may accept or reject, but has **no breach claim**.

**Application:** Barnabas sent an order for "50 board-feet of oak, delivered by end of week, at $200" — an offer to buy goods for prompt shipment under § 2-206(1)(b). Timothy's text ("Oak is back-ordered. Maple is what I have on hand — sending you 50 board-feet at the same price of $200. Hope it works for your project") satisfies every element of the accommodation exception: (a) it was seasonable (delivered simultaneously with the goods), (b) it identified the substitution, and (c) it framed the shipment as an offer for the buyer's use, not as performance of the original order. Timothy made a counteroffer to sell maple for $200. Barnabas may accept or reject freely.

**Authority:** UCC § 2-206(1)(b) (verified at Nebraska Legislature, https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/ucc.php?code=2-206, and FindLaw / N.Y. UCC § 2-206, https://codes.findlaw.com/ny/uniform-commercial-code/ucc-sect-2-206/). No split doctrine located.

**Credited outcome unchanged from original.** Timothy's accommodating shipment = counteroffer, not acceptance. Buyer may accept or reject; no breach remedies. Correct.

This item tests your ability to apply the accommodation-notice exception within UCC § 2-206(1)(b) — a formation-of-contracts rule under the MBE Contracts subject. The tested move is spotting that a seller who seasonably notifies the buyer that a nonconforming shipment is offered only as an accommodation has made a counteroffer, not an acceptance, meaning no breach-of-contract remedies arise.

**2. Student Access Test**

Can a student who has completed the C3 lessons, subject overlays, and taught tiny anchors identify each choice's problem without outside law? Yes — with the Gold Key. The accommodation-notice exception is a taught rule that unlocks the item. Without it, the item is an anchor_gap.

**3. Question Data**

Barnabas texts Timothy: "Need 50 board-feet of oak, delivered by end of week, at $200." Timothy is out of oak. Timothy delivers 50 board-feet of maple with a note: "Oak is back-ordered. Maple is what I have on hand — sending 50 board-feet at $200. Hope it works for your project." Barnabas receives both the delivery and the note on Tuesday afternoon. Which of the following best describes their rights and duties?

**4. Distilled Core Question**

A buyer offers to buy goods for prompt shipment. The seller ships different (nonconforming) goods and leaves a note saying they're sent as a substitute. What are the parties' rights?

**5. Call and Prediction**

Call: "Which of the following best describes the rights and duties of Barnabas and Timothy?" — This is asking you to identify the correct legal consequence of an accommodating shipment of nonconforming goods under UCC Article 2. Before looking at the choices, predict: did Timothy accept Barnabas's offer? Or did Timothy make a new offer? Predict that Timothy's note ("Oak is back-ordered... Hope it works") is an accommodation notice, which means Timothy made a counteroffer — and Barnabas can accept or reject, but cannot sue for breach.

**6. Trigger Facts**

- **Barnabas ordered 50 board-feet of oak for $200** — establishes an offer to buy goods for prompt shipment under UCC § 2-206(1)(b).
- **Timothy is out of oak** — explains the nonconforming shipment.
- **Timothy delivered maple instead of oak** — nonconforming goods.
- **Timothy included a note: "Oak is back-ordered... Hope it works for your project"** — the accommodation notice. This is the decisive fact.
- **Note delivered with the shipment (same day, Tuesday)** — seasonable.

**7. Governing C3 Lane**

CUT → (no CLASH) → (no CALL needed). The C3 lane is a clean CUT: eliminate choices that assume Timothy breached or imposed any duty on Barnabas. Only the choice that recognizes the counteroffer frame survives.

**8. Choice-by-Choice Walkthrough**

**Choice A:** *Barnabas may accept the maple, in which case he must pay Timothy the full price, less any damages sustained because of the nonconforming shipment, or he may reject the maple and recover from Timothy for total breach of contract, subject to Timothy's right to cure.*

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:** This choice brings in breach and cure. But when a seller seasonably notifies the buyer that a nonconforming shipment is offered only as an accommodation, no acceptance has occurred and therefore no breach has occurred. The "right to cure" is irrelevant — you can't cure a breach that never happened.

**(2) Student label:** "Breach-and-cure answer" — applies the wrong legal framework.

**(3) What a TRUE-and-responsive version would look like:** A true version of this choice would require a scenario where Timothy shipped maple without any accommodation notice — in which case the default rule (nonconforming shipment = acceptance + breach) would apply.

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:** Gold Key — UCC § 2-206(1)(b) accommodation exception. Timothy's note expressly frames the maple as a substitute offered for Barnabas's use ("Hope it works for your project"). This is a seasonable accommodation notice. The shipment is a counteroffer, not an acceptance. No breach. No cure right under UCC § 2-508.

---

**Choice B:** *Barnabas may accept the maple, in which case he must pay Timothy the full price, less any damages sustained because of the nonconforming shipment, or he must reject the maple and recover from Timothy for total breach of contract.*

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:** This choice also assumes Timothy breached — it lets Barnabas "recover from Timothy for total breach of contract." Same fundamental error as A: the accommodation notice negates the breach premise.

**(2) Student label:** "Breach-without-cure answer."

**(3) What a TRUE-and-responsive version would look like:** Same as A — this would be correct if Timothy shipped nonconforming goods without an accommodation notice.

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:** Timothy's accommodation notice (same-day, identifies substitution, uses "hope it works" language) is seasonable and explicit under UCC § 2-206(1)(b). No acceptance. No breach. No damages.

---

**Choice C:** *Barnabas may accept the maple, in which case he must pay Timothy the full price, or he may reject the maple, in which case he has no further rights against Timothy.*

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:** This is the only choice that correctly frames the situation as a counteroffer — Barnabas can take it (pay the price) or leave it (no further rights). No breach, no damages, no duties. This matches the accommodation-exception framework.

**(2) Student label:** "Counteroffer (correct)."

**(3) What a TRUE-and-responsive version would look like:** This IS the true-and-responsive choice. Timothy made a counteroffer by shipping maple with an accommodation notice. Barnabas may accept the counteroffer by keeping and paying, or reject it with no liability on either side.

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:** UCC § 2-206(1)(b). Timothy's seasonable notice that the maple was offered as an accommodation to Barnabas means the shipment is not an acceptance. It operates as a counteroffer. Barnabas may accept or reject freely.

---

**Choice D:** *Barnabas may accept the maple, in which case he must pay Timothy the full price, less any damages sustained because of the nonconforming shipment, or he may reject the maple provided that he promptly covers by obtaining conforming oak from another supplier.*

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:** "Provided that he promptly covers" — this makes rejection conditional on cover. Cover is a remedy that only exists when the seller has breached (UCC § 2-712). And even in breach scenarios, cover is the buyer's *option*, not a *condition* of rejection. So this choice gets two things wrong: (1) it assumes Timothy breached (he didn't), and (2) even if he had, cover is not a prerequisite to rejection.  

**(2) Student label:** "Cover-required answer."

**(3) What a TRUE-and-responsive version would look like:** This would need a scenario where Timothy breached the contract and Barnabas wanted to reject and simultaneously purchase substitute goods. Even then, the "provided that" framing would still be wrong — cover is optional.

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:** UCC § 2-712 (cover) is available only after a seller's breach. Timothy's accommodating shipment is a counteroffer, not a breach. Even if Timothy had breached, the buyer may reject without covering; cover is a separate optional remedy.

**16. Crossover Intersection Map**

- **UCC § 2-712 (Cover):** Cover is a buyer's remedy after seller's breach. The accommodation exception means no breach occurred, so cover is never available.
