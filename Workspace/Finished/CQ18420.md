Should the federal court dismiss the indictment?

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
B is right because the state and federal governments are separate sovereigns. The Double Jeopardy Clause bars the same sovereign from prosecuting a defendant twice for the same offense, but a state prosecution and a federal prosecution are treated as prosecutions for offenses against different sovereigns. The state acquittal therefore does not require dismissal of the later federal indictment.

Gold Key: Under the dual sovereignty doctrine, state and federal governments may each prosecute the same conduct because each sovereign defines its own offense.

Silver Key: Before using same-conduct, collateral-estoppel, or Blockburger tools, identify who is prosecuting the second case; different sovereigns usually decide the question first.

A is attractive because collateral estoppel sounds like the right label after a jury acquittal. The breaker is that the federal government was not the same prosecuting sovereign in the state case, and the separate-sovereigns rule controls before issue preclusion can do the work this answer wants.

C is attractive because Blockburger is a real double-jeopardy test and federal-officer assault likely has a distinct element. The answer reaches a neighboring test instead of the controlling one.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning
The credited answer is correct under the Fifth Amendment Double Jeopardy Clause as interpreted by the Supreme Court's dual sovereignty doctrine. In United States v. Lanza, the Court recognized that the federal government and a state are separate sovereigns that may each punish offenses against their own peace and dignity. In Gamble v. United States, the Court reaffirmed the separate-sovereigns doctrine and held that state and federal prosecutions for the same conduct are not prosecutions for the same offense for Double Jeopardy purposes.

That rule controls here. Paul's state acquittal ended the state's case. It did not make the federal government the same sovereign, and it did not disable Congress's separate federal offense for assaulting a federal officer. The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome remain unchanged: the federal court should not dismiss the indictment on double jeopardy grounds.
