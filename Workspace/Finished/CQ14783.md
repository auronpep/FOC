# PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

Ruth sued Peter for negligence after she was badly stung during a honeybee demonstration at a Christian harvest fair. Ruth called Barnabas, an apiary-safety specialist, as an expert witness. During Barnabas's direct testimony, Barnabas identified a beekeeping safety manual as a reliable authority in the field. Barnabas seeks to read to the jury passages from the manual on which he relied in forming his opinion about the proper standard of care for opening hives near fair guests. The passages are

**A.** admissible as a basis for Barnabas's opinion, but not as substantive evidence of the proper standard of care.

**B.** inadmissible, because the manual should be received as an exhibit instead of read to the jury by Barnabas.

**C.** admissible as a basis for Barnabas's opinion and as substantive evidence of the proper standard of care.

**D.** inadmissible, because Barnabas's credibility cannot be supported unless it has been attacked.

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** The passages satisfy the learned-treatise exception. Barnabas is testifying as an expert, he relied on the manual during direct examination, and he identified it as a reliable authority in apiary safety. **Gold Key:** When a learned treatise is relied on by an expert on direct examination and established as a reliable authority, its relevant statements may be read into evidence for their truth. They are not limited to explaining the expert's basis. The passages are therefore admissible both as part of Barnabas's opinion basis and as substantive evidence of the proper standard of care.

**Silver Key:** When the answer pair says "basis only" versus "basis plus substantive," make the CLASH about purpose. If the stem gives both direct-exam reliance and reliable-authority foundation, the "only as basis" answer loses.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is attractive** because it captures a real expert-evidence idea: experts may rely on professional materials in forming opinions. The breaker is the word "not." The learned-treatise rule does more than explain Barnabas's basis; once the manual is established as reliable and relied on during direct testimony, the relevant statements may be used substantively. **Gold Key:** FRE 803(18) is a hearsay exception, not merely an expert-basis rule.

**B is attractive** because it notices the special procedure for learned treatises and tries to make the jury receive the manual itself. The breaker is that the procedure runs the other way. The relevant statements may be read into evidence, but the publication itself is not received as an exhibit under the learned-treatise exception.

**D is attractive** only if the student misreads the passage as character or credibility support for Barnabas. The breaker is the purpose. Ruth is not offering the manual to bolster Barnabas's truthfulness; she is using it with an expert to prove the professional standard of care. That makes credibility-support doctrine nonresponsive.

## 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The governing rule is Federal Rule of Evidence 803(18), the learned-treatise exception to hearsay. A statement in a treatise, periodical, or pamphlet on a subject of science, art, medicine, history, or another learned field is not excluded by the hearsay rule if it is called to an expert's attention on cross-examination or relied on by the expert on direct examination, and the publication is established as a reliable authority by expert testimony, admission, other expert testimony, or judicial notice. If admitted, the statement may be read into evidence but the publication itself may not be received as an exhibit.

Here, Barnabas relied on the manual during direct examination and identified it as a reliable authority. The passages are relevant to the proper standard of care. Under FRE 803(18), the passages may be read to the jury and used substantively. They also may explain the basis for Barnabas's expert opinion. The credited outcome is therefore unchanged: admissible as a basis for the expert opinion and as substantive evidence of the standard of care.
