### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** The attenuation doctrine breaks the causal chain between an initial police illegality and a later confession when sufficient intervening circumstances dissipate the taint. Here, four key facts support attenuation: (1) Lydia was released from custody, (2) four days passed, (3) she consulted with an attorney, and (4) she voluntarily returned to the station on her own. None of these facts involve continued police pressure. Under the attenuation analysis, these intervening events — particularly the combination of release, time, legal consultation, and voluntary return — are sufficient to break the causal chain between the illegal arrest and the confession. The confession is therefore admissible.

**Gold Key (GK-CRIM-ATTENUATION-01):** When you see "fruit of the poisonous tree" + a later voluntary statement, look for the attenuation pillars: (1) release from custody, (2) passage of time, (3) consultation with counsel, (4) voluntary return, and (5) no new police pressure. The more pillars present, the stronger the attenuation argument.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**Choice A (dominant trap):** This choice sells the intuitive overclaim that an illegal arrest permanently taints everything that follows — "fruit forever." It's attractive because the exclusionary rule *does* require suppression of evidence obtained through police illegality, and students often extend this to mean *all* downstream evidence without limit. **The breaker:** The fruit-of-the-poisonous-tree doctrine includes the attenuation exception. Voluntary intervening acts — especially release, time, counsel, and voluntary return — can break the causal chain. **Gold Key GK-CRIM-ATTENUATION-01** is the unlock: the attenuation pillars are all present here, so "fruit forever" is wrong.

**Choice B:** This choice tempts students who fixate on Miranda warnings as the gateway to admissibility of any confession. It's attractive because Miranda is heavily tested and students associate it with all confession issues. **The breaker:** Miranda governs custodial interrogation. Here, the confession was voluntary and not the product of custodial interrogation. The admissibility question turns on attenuation, not Miranda. The illegal arrest is the challenged conduct, and the issue is whether the taint was broken — not whether warnings were given during the earlier arrest.

**Choice D:** This choice attracts students who conflate the legality of the arrest with the admissibility of the confession. It's attractive because probable cause *is* the standard for a lawful arrest, and students want to "fix" the illegal arrest by asking whether probable cause existed. **The breaker:** The question assumes the arrest was illegal. The attenuation analysis does not retroactively validate the arrest — it asks whether the causal chain was broken *despite* the illegality. Probable cause for the original arrest is irrelevant to the attenuation question.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing rule:** The Fourth Amendment exclusionary rule prohibits the use of evidence obtained through unconstitutional government conduct (fruit of the poisonous tree). However, the Supreme Court has recognized several exceptions, including the **attenuation doctrine**. Under this doctrine, evidence is admissible if "the connection between the illegal action and the challenged evidence has 'become so attenuated as to dissipate the taint.'" *United States v. Crews*, 445 U.S. 463, 471 (1980) (quoting *Wong Sun v. United States*, 371 U.S. 471, 487–88 (1963)). The Court considers factors including: (1) the time elapsed between the illegality and the evidence, (2) the presence of intervening circumstances, and (3) the purpose and flagrancy of the official misconduct. *Brown v. Illinois*, 422 U.S. 590, 603–04 (1975). Voluntary consent or a voluntary act by the defendant after release from custody is a powerful intervening circumstance. *Wong Sun*, 371 U.S. at 491.

**Application:** Lydia was released (not held), four days passed, she consulted an attorney, and she voluntarily returned. These are textbook attenuation factors. The confession was not the product of continued detention, coercion, or new police pressure. Under *Brown* and *Wong Sun*, the taint is dissipated.

**Verdict:** The credited answer (admissible — attenuation) is correct under controlling doctrine. The legal issue, tested rule, and credited outcome are **unchanged** from the original. `bank_validation_verdict: PASS`.

2. **Student label:** The "fruit forever" overclaim.
3. **What a true/responsive version would look like:** "The confession may be suppressed if the taint of the illegal arrest was not broken by intervening circumstances." This would be a qualified, conditional claim — not an absolute.

**Choice B: "The confession must be suppressed unless Lydia received Miranda warnings during the illegal arrest."**
