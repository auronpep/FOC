B is correct. The statement attacked the quality and safety of Esther's product, not merely Esther's feelings or general popularity. The loss is also concrete: three identified buyers canceled pending orders totaling a specific dollar amount. Gold Key: injurious falsehood, often called trade libel when it targets goods or business quality, protects economic interests in products and requires specific pecuniary loss. ### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because the word "libel" tempts students to collapse every false market-harming statement into ordinary defamation. The breaker is the target of the statement: it attacked the product's quality and safety and produced canceled orders. Gold Key: product disparagement plus specific pecuniary loss points to injurious falsehood or trade libel, not defamation only.

C is attractive because competition can sound like a hard commercial arena where rivals are expected to criticize each other. The breaker is the word "knowingly": competition does not create a permission slip for false product-quality claims that cause specific monetary loss.

D is attractive only if the student treats any indirect business harm as enough for an intentional tort label. The breaker is visible from the choice itself: battery is a contact tort, and this stem is about words to buyers and canceled orders.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Torts, Other Torts, Economic Torts, Injurious falsehood (trade libel), and the verified outline code is `62050303`. The credited outcome is unchanged. Injurious falsehood imposes liability for a false statement harmful to another's pecuniary interests when the publisher intends or recognizes likely pecuniary harm and knows the statement is false or acts in reckless disregard of truth. Restatement (Second) of Torts section 623A states the general injurious-falsehood rule, and section 626 applies that rule to disparagement of the quality of land, chattels, or intangible things likely to cause pecuniary loss through third-party conduct. The American Law Institute identifies injurious falsehood as Chapter 28 in Restatement (Second) of Torts, Volume 3: https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-second/torts. California's CACI No. 1731 collects the same trade-libel elements and quotes Restatement sections 623A and 626, including false product-quality disparagement, knowledge or reckless disregard, third-party reliance, and direct financial harm: https://www.justia.com/trials-litigation/docs/caci/1700/1731/. The variant satisfies the rule because Paul knowingly made a false statement about Esther's product quality to third-party purchasers, and those purchasers canceled identified orders totaling $26,400. No researched authority contradicted the supplied official key.
