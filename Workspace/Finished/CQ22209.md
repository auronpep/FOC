## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

The correct answer is **C**. The evidence is being offered in a rape prosecution to suggest that Mary was sexually willing, so the threshold question is whether the condoms count as evidence of Mary's other sexual behavior or sexual predisposition. They do. Once that threshold is locked, FRE 412(a) bars the evidence unless one of the narrow criminal-case exceptions applies. None does here.

**Gold Key:** In a criminal sex-misconduct case, FRE 412 blocks evidence of the alleged victim's other sexual behavior or sexual predisposition unless the evidence fits one of three narrow doors: another source of physical evidence, prior sexual behavior with the accused to prove consent, or constitutional necessity.

**Silver Key:** When two answers say "No," do not reward the one that sounds like general relevance if the item is really a rape-shield problem. Lock the FRE 412 threshold first, then check the short exception list.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning

The credited answer remains correct under current law. FRE 412(a) bars evidence offered to prove that an alleged victim engaged in other sexual behavior or had a sexual predisposition in a criminal case involving alleged sexual misconduct. FRE 412(b)(1) then gives only three criminal-case exceptions: evidence of another source of semen, injury, or other physical evidence; evidence of prior sexual behavior between the alleged victim and the accused offered on consent; and evidence whose exclusion would violate the defendant's constitutional rights. Source checked against Cornell's text of Rule 412: https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_412

Application here is straightforward. Peter is not offering the condoms to show another source of physical evidence. He is not offering prior sexual behavior between Mary and Peter. And nothing in the stem suggests constitutional necessity. Instead, he is offering the condoms to suggest that Mary was willing to accompany him and willing to have sex. That is classic other-sexual-behavior or predisposition reasoning, so the evidence is barred.
