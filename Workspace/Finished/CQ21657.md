## 7. Full right-answer explanation

A is correct. Stephen intentionally used a heavy cast-iron skillet as a deadly weapon and struck Timothy twice in the head. That supports malice and an intentional killing. But first-degree murder requires more than intent to kill. It requires premeditation and deliberation: some cool reflection before the killing. The stem gives the opposite signal. The whole outburst lasted less than thirty seconds, began with a sudden argument, and contains no evidence that Stephen planned or expected the confrontation.

Gold Key: intent to kill plus a deadly weapon can prove malice, but it does not by itself prove first-degree murder; first-degree requires premeditation and deliberation. Silver Key: on a "most serious homicide offense" call, climb only to the highest offense whose extra element is supported by the facts.

Voluntary manslaughter is too low because knocking a loaf of bread to the floor after being shoved is not adequate provocation for a reasonable person to kill. Involuntary manslaughter is too low because Stephen's conduct was intentional, not merely negligent or accidental. The most serious proper conviction is second-degree murder.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Murder is an unlawful killing with malice aforethought; express malice includes a deliberate intention to unlawfully take life. See California Penal Code sections 187 and 188. First-degree murder requires a willful, deliberate, and premeditated killing, while "all other kinds of murders are of the second degree." See California Penal Code section 189. Manslaughter is an unlawful killing without malice; voluntary manslaughter is tied to sudden quarrel or heat of passion, and involuntary manslaughter covers non-intent killings committed through unlawful acts or gross negligence-type conduct. See California Penal Code section 192. These current statutory formulations track the MBE distinction used here: an intentional killing with malice, but without premeditation and without legally adequate provocation, is second-degree murder.
