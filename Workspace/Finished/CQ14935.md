## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

A is correct. A seller who promises marketable title cannot force the buyer to close when the property is already in violation of zoning and that violation exposes the buyer to litigation or enforcement. Gold Key: the existence of zoning restrictions is different from a present zoning violation; the violation is what can make title unmarketable. Silver Key: in a win/lose-because call, the outcome and the because-clause both have to fit. Esther loses because the current zoning violation creates the marketable-title defect.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

B sells a notice move: zoning ordinances are public, so Stephen sounds charged with knowing them. The breaker is that the call is not about awareness of the ordinance. The defect is the present violation of the ordinance, which creates the title risk.

C sells the right litigation result with a blame-based reason. The breaker is the Silver Key: a correct winner with a non-dispositive because-clause still fails. Marketable title turns on the present violation and resulting risk, not on whether Esther or a prior owner did the remodeling.

D is the dominant trap. It separates "title" from "building use" and sounds attractive because Stephen inspected the building and Esther can deliver a deed. Gold Key: a present zoning violation is not treated like a harmless physical defect; it can expose the buyer to enforcement or litigation and make the promised marketable title unavailable.

## 9. Black-Letter Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are preserved. In a land-sale contract requiring marketable title, the seller must be able to deliver title that is free from reasonable doubt and does not expose the buyer to a substantial risk of litigation. A zoning ordinance by itself does not make title unmarketable, but a present violation of zoning can.

The leading authority used for the lawyer-confirmation layer is Lohmeyer v. Bower, 170 Kan. 442, 227 P.2d 102 (1951), available at https://law.justia.com/cases/kansas/supreme-court/1951/38-110-0.html. Lohmeyer treats an existing violation of a municipal ordinance and restrictions as making title doubtful and unmarketable because the purchaser would face the hazard of litigation. Cornell Wex similarly defines marketable title as title free from claims, disputes, or threatened litigation and lists zoning-law violations as encumbrances that can make title unmarketable: https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/marketable_title.

The fire-code fact is not the decisive ground. The credited answer rests on the present zoning violation and the resulting enforcement or litigation risk. Research did not contradict the supplied official key. No doctrinal-currency hotlist issue is implicated.
