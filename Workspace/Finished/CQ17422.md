## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

A is correct. Federal Rule of Evidence 611(a) gives the trial court reasonable, not complete, control over the mode and order of examining witnesses, and that control must remain effective for determining the truth. Completely barring meaningful cross-examination of the only witness on a contested signature fact is not a reasonable exercise of that control and is likely an abuse of discretion. Gold Key: FRE 611(a)'s "reasonable control" is the load-bearing phrase. ## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

B. The offer-of-proof answer is attractive because students remember that an offer of proof is required to preserve an evidentiary complaint. The breaker is the wrong element: FRE 103's offer-of-proof rule protects a record when specific evidence is excluded, but here the trial judge did not exclude a piece of evidence; the judge eliminated the entire cross-examination. C. The complete-discretion answer is attractive because trial judges do have broad trial-management authority and a courtroom-control instinct says "the judge runs the room." The breaker is the rule's actual language: FRE 611(a) uses "reasonable" control, not complete discretion, and reasonableness must keep the procedure effective for determining the truth. Gold Key: when a choice claims a judge has "complete" control over a trial procedure, that word is almost always too broad.

D. The credibility-only answer is attractive because students remember that cross is supposed to be limited. The breaker is the scope rule itself: cross goes to the subject matter of the direct and matters affecting credibility, and FRE 611(b) lets the court go further "as if on direct." None of that authorizes a judge to bar cross entirely of the key witness on a contested fact. The rule limits the scope of cross; it does not eliminate cross.

## 9. Black-Letter Verification and Legal Reasoning

The credited outcome remains that the trial court's blanket denial of cross-examination of the only witness on a contested fact is likely an abuse of discretion under FRE 611(a). Federal Rule of Evidence 611(a) provides that the court should exercise reasonable control over the mode and order of examining witnesses and presenting evidence so as to (1) make those procedures effective for determining the truth, (2) avoid wasting time, and (3) protect witnesses from harassment or undue embarrassment. That reasonableness standard is the floor on which a trial court's trial-management discretion rests; the rule does not grant "complete" or unreviewable discretion. Federal Rule of Evidence 611(b) sets the scope of cross to the subject matter of the direct and matters affecting the witness's credibility, with discretion to allow additional matters; that rule limits what cross may cover, not whether cross may occur. Federal Rule of Evidence 103 addresses offers of proof, which are required to preserve a record when specific evidence is excluded, but a wholesale denial of cross-examination is a different problem and is not cured by a failure to make an offer of proof.

This intersects with FRE 611(b) (scope of cross) and FRE 103 (offers of proof), but the controlling lane is FRE 611(a) (control by the court).

## review_truth

Under FRE 611(a) the trial court has reasonable, not complete, control over the mode and order of examining witnesses, and that control cannot be used to eliminate meaningful cross-examination of a key witness on a contested fact.
