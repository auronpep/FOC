## 7. Right-Answer Explanation

Naomi's original statement to the auction organizer was admitted into evidence as hearsay (without objection — the prosecution presented it as an excited utterance). Because that hearsay statement is in evidence, Naomi becomes an impeachable hearsay declarant under FRE 806. FRE 806 allows the defendant to attack that declarant's credibility by introducing a prior inconsistent statement — here, Naomi's later recantation to Timothy. Critically, FRE 806 expressly provides that the prior inconsistent statement may be admitted "regardless of when it occurred or whether the declarant had an opportunity to explain or deny it." The ordinary explain-or-deny prerequisite of FRE 613(b) is waived for hearsay declarants. Timothy's testimony comes in — but strictly for impeachment, not as substantive proof.

**Gold Key (GK-EVID-806-01):** FRE 806 authorizes impeachment of an unavailable hearsay declarant with a prior inconsistent statement and specifically waives the FRE 613(b) explain-or-deny requirement. "No opportunity to explain or deny" is never a valid objection under FRE 806.

**Silver Key (SK-EVID-806-01):** When an unavailable hearsay declarant's statement has been admitted and the opponent offers a prior inconsistent statement to impeach — reach for FRE 806, not FRE 613(b). FRE 806 is the governing provision; it overrides the explain-or-deny requirement that traps students who apply the ordinary witness rule.

## 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**B — No, because Naomi will be afforded no opportunity to explain or deny the later statement.**

The pull: When a witness is impeached with a prior inconsistent statement, she normally must be given a fair opportunity to explain or deny it. Naomi cannot explain or deny because she is unavailable. A student who knows the explain-or-deny rule but doesn't know FRE 806's carve-out will reach this conclusion.

The breaker: FRE 806 exists precisely for this situation. When the declarant made the original hearsay statement — not the later inconsistent one — and is now unavailable, FRE 806 specifically provides that the prior inconsistent statement is admissible "regardless of when it occurred or whether the declarant had an opportunity to explain or deny it." The explain-or-deny requirement of FRE 613(b) applies to testifying witnesses; it does not apply to hearsay declarants being impeached under FRE 806.

**Gold Key (GK-EVID-806-01):** FRE 806 expressly waives the explain-or-deny requirement for hearsay declarants. This choice applies FRE 613(b)'s rule for testifying witnesses to a context FRE 806 was written to address. It is the dominant trap.

**Silver Key (SK-EVID-806-01):** This is the dominant trap: recognize the FRE 806 pattern (unavailable hearsay declarant + prior inconsistent statement) and switch from FRE 613(b) to FRE 806. FRE 806 makes the objection fail.

**C — No, because the prosecutor will be afforded no opportunity to confront Naomi.**

The pull: The Sixth Amendment guarantees the right to confront witnesses. Naomi cannot be cross-examined because she is unavailable. A student who conflates prosecutorial interests with the Confrontation Clause will be drawn here.

The breaker: The Sixth Amendment Confrontation Clause belongs exclusively to criminal defendants — not to the government. A prosecutor has no constitutional right to confront witnesses. This choice simply misstates constitutional doctrine. And even setting that aside, it was the prosecutor who offered Naomi's original hearsay statement, so the prosecutor can hardly claim prejudice from inability to cross-examine Naomi.

**Gold Key (GK-EVID-806-02):** Only the accused — never the government — has the Sixth Amendment right to confront witnesses. A prosecutor cannot exclude evidence on confrontation grounds; only a criminal defendant can invoke the Confrontation Clause.

**D — Yes, because it is substantive proof that Paul did not steal the donation money.**

The pull: Naomi later said she was mistaken. If that is true, it proves Paul is innocent. Why shouldn't it count as proof of innocence?

The breaker: The direction of admissibility is right (yes, it comes in), but the purpose is wrong. Naomi's later statement is an out-of-court statement offered, if admitted for its truth, to prove Paul did not steal — that is textbook hearsay. There is no applicable hearsay exception for this statement. FRE 806 makes the statement admissible only to impeach Naomi's credibility as to her original hearsay statement; it does not lift the hearsay bar on the statement's substantive use.

**Gold Key (GK-EVID-806-03):** Under FRE 806, the prior inconsistent statement of a hearsay declarant is admissible only to attack the declarant's credibility — not as substantive proof of the facts asserted. If offered for truth, the statement remains hearsay without an applicable exception.

## 9. Black-Letter Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** Federal Rule of Evidence 806 provides: "When a hearsay statement . . . has been admitted in evidence, the declarant's credibility may be attacked, and then supported, by any evidence that would be admissible for those purposes if the declarant had testified as a witness. The court may admit evidence of the declarant's inconsistent statement or conduct, regardless of when it occurred or whether the declarant had an opportunity to explain or deny it."

**Applied:** Naomi's original statement to the auction organizer was admitted as hearsay (excited utterance). Naomi is unavailable. Paul offers Timothy's testimony about Naomi's later recantation to attack Naomi's credibility — the paradigm case for FRE 806. The explain-or-deny requirement of FRE 613(b) is expressly waived. The statement is admissible for impeachment only.

**Why not B (no opportunity to explain/deny):** FRE 806 texts the explain-or-deny requirement out of existence for hearsay declarants. The rule was enacted specifically to solve the problem that arises when the declarant cannot explain or deny because she is unavailable. B applies FRE 613(b), the ordinary witness rule, to a situation governed by FRE 806.

**Why not C (prosecutor's confrontation right):** The Sixth Amendment Confrontation Clause guarantees "the accused" the right to confront witnesses. U.S. Const. amend. VI. It runs against the government, protecting defendants. Prosecutors have no correlative confrontation right.

**Why not D (substantive use):** If admitted for truth (Paul did not steal), the statement is hearsay — an out-of-court assertion offered to prove the matter asserted. FRE 806 does not create a hearsay exception; it permits impeachment use only. No other hearsay exception (FRE 803, 804, 807) covers a declarant's recantation of her own prior hearsay statement in these facts.

**Authority:** FRE 806 (text); U.S. Const. amend. VI (Confrontation Clause — defendant only); FRE 613(b) (ordinary witness explain-or-deny rule); Christopher B. Mueller & Laird C. Kirkpatrick, Federal Evidence § 8:44 (FRE 806 impeachment of hearsay declarants).

**Choice A — Yes, but only as a prior inconsistent statement to impeach Naomi's credibility.**

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:** FRE 806: "The court may admit evidence of the declarant's inconsistent statement or conduct, regardless of when it occurred or whether the declarant had an opportunity to explain or deny it." The statement impeaches Naomi as the hearsay declarant. **Gold Keys GK-EVID-806-01 and GK-EVID-806-03** confirm both the admissibility basis and the purpose limitation.

---

**Choice B — No, because Naomi will be afforded no opportunity to explain or deny the later statement.**

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:** The explain-or-deny requirement is real (FRE 613(b)) and applies when a testifying witness is impeached with a prior inconsistent statement. But this is not that situation — Naomi is a hearsay declarant who never testified. FRE 806 governs, and FRE 806 was written precisely to deal with the impossibility of giving an unavailable hearsay declarant a chance to explain. The rule overrides the ordinary requirement.

**Silver Key (SK-EVID-806-01):** This is the dominant trap. The moment you see "hearsay declarant + unavailable + prior inconsistent statement," switch to FRE 806. FRE 806 expressly waives the explain-or-deny requirement. Any "No because no chance to explain or deny" answer is wrong.

**(2) Student label:** CUT (NOT_RESPONSIVE — bait_doctrine: applies FRE 613(b) to a hearsay declarant context governed by FRE 806)

**(3) What a true/responsive version would need:** A valid "No" based on the explain-or-deny rule would require facts showing the declarant IS a testifying witness who has not been given the required opportunity under FRE 613(b). For hearsay declarants under FRE 806, the requirement simply does not apply.

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:** FRE 613(b) requires that a testifying witness be given the opportunity to explain or deny a prior inconsistent statement used to impeach her. FRE 806 specifically provides that when a hearsay declarant is impeached, the court "may admit evidence of the declarant's inconsistent statement or conduct, regardless of . . . whether the declarant had an opportunity to explain or deny it." **Gold Key GK-EVID-806-01** defeats this dominant trap.

---

**Choice C — No, because the prosecutor will be afforded no opportunity to confront Naomi.**

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:** The choice asserts that the prosecutor has a right to confront witnesses. But the Sixth Amendment right to confrontation runs in favor of the accused — not the government. A prosecutor cannot exclude evidence by claiming a right to confront. The legal premise is simply wrong.

**(2) Student label:** CUT (NOT_TRUE — flat_misstatement: prosecutors have no Sixth Amendment confrontation right)

**(3) What a true/responsive version would need:** A valid confrontation-based exclusion would require the defendant to argue that admission of hearsay evidence against her violated her confrontation rights — the opposite posture. Here, it is the defendant who is trying to get Naomi's recantation IN, not out.

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:** U.S. Const. amend. VI: "the accused shall enjoy the right . . . to be confronted with the witnesses against him." The Confrontation Clause protects defendants; it provides no right to the government. Additionally, it was the prosecutor who introduced Naomi's original hearsay statement, so the prosecutor can hardly claim a right to confront a declarant of her own evidence. **Gold Key GK-EVID-806-02** defeats this distractor. See Crawford v. Washington, 541 U.S. 36 (2004).

---

**Choice D — Yes, because it is substantive proof that Paul did not steal the donation money.**

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:** The ruling direction is correct (admissible), but the basis is wrong. The choice says the statement is "substantive proof" — that it actually proves Paul's innocence. FRE 806 does not say that. The statement comes in only to attack Naomi's credibility as a hearsay declarant. If offered for its truth (Paul didn't steal), it is hearsay with no applicable exception. Getting the outcome right with the wrong reason gives the wrong full answer.

**(2) Student label:** CUT (NOT_TRUE — flat_misstatement: the statement is not admissible as substantive proof; FRE 806 permits impeachment use only)

**(3) What a true/responsive version would need:** A valid substantive-proof Yes would require either (a) an applicable hearsay exception covering the recantation, or (b) FRE 806 itself providing for substantive use — but FRE 806 contains neither. The statement's probative value under FRE 806 is only to undermine Naomi's credibility as the hearsay declarant.

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:** FRE 806 permits the prior inconsistent statement to attack credibility — not to prove the facts in it. If offered for truth (Paul did not steal), the statement is hearsay under FRE 801(c): it is an out-of-court statement offered to prove the matter asserted. No hearsay exception in FRE 803, 804, or 807 covers a declarant's out-of-court recantation of her own prior hearsay statement on these facts. **Gold Key GK-EVID-806-03** defeats this distractor.

**Title:** FRE 806: Impeaching the Unavailable Hearsay Declarant

**Signal:** Hearsay statement admitted into evidence; declarant is unavailable; the opponent of that hearsay now offers a prior inconsistent statement of the same declarant.
