Ruth, a homeless woman, broke into the back room of a church's community hall after hours and fell asleep. A volunteer security attendant found her, woke her, and demanded that she leave. 7) Full right-answer explanation (student-facing)

Ruth's use of the extinguisher was a response to an attack. Self-defense, when reasonable and proportionate, is a complete defense to assault, including aggravated forms that require intent to cause serious bodily injury. The facts show Ruth acted to repel an ongoing beating; therefore the prosecution cannot prove the required culpable state for aggravated assault, nor the intent element for burglary (no intent to commit a felony upon entry is shown). The most serious provable offense therefore is trespass.

Gold Key: GK-CRIM-ruth-01 — "Self-defense as a complete bar" — If an actor reasonably uses force to repel an ongoing unlawful attack, that force negates criminal liability for defensive uses of force, including where injury results. (Authority: Model Penal Code §3.04 — use for attorney confirmation.)

Black-letter: Self-defense permits use of reasonable force to repel an unlawful attack; when reasonable, it is a complete defense to assaultive offenses. Burglary requires breaking and entering the dwelling (or structure specified by jurisdiction) of another at night (or jurisdictional variant) with intent to commit a felony therein; intent is an element that must be proved. Because the facts show a defensive response to a violent assault, the elements for aggravated assault or burglary are not met.
