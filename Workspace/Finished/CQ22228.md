## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct.** Under FRE 702, the court acts as a gatekeeper to determine whether a witness qualifies as an expert. But once the expert is admitted, the jury decides what weight to give the expert's testimony — and that includes evaluating the expert's credibility. The jury cannot assess credibility without knowing the expert's qualifications. Even though the defendant stipulated to Timothy's expertise and the court found him qualified, the plaintiff's attorney may still question Timothy about his credentials so the jury can weigh them. This is especially important because the jury will likely hear competing expert opinions and must compare qualifications to decide whom to believe.

**Gold Key (GK-EVIDENCE-EXPERT-CRED-01):** The court decides *admissibility* (is this person an expert?), but the jury decides *weight* (how credible is this expert?). Expert qualifications remain relevant to the jury even after the court's gatekeeping role is satisfied.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification

**Governing framework:** FRE 702 (Testimony by Expert Witnesses) establishes the court's gatekeeper role: the court must determine that the witness is qualified by knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education. The court may accept a stipulation from the parties as the basis for this determination.

**Jury's role:** Once expert testimony is admitted, the jury evaluates its weight and credibility. The jury's assessment of an expert's credibility necessarily includes consideration of the expert's qualifications — the jury cannot weigh expert testimony without knowing the expert's background. This is especially critical when competing experts testify.

**Key authorities:**
- **FRE 702** — Court determines whether witness qualifies as expert; stipulation is a permissible basis.
- **FRE 104(a)** — Preliminary questions of admissibility (including expert qualification) are decided by the court.
- **FRE 702, Advisory Committee Notes** — The court's gatekeeping role is about admissibility, not weight.
- **General principle:** The jury determines the weight and credibility of admitted testimony. See *Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc.*, 509 U.S. 579 (1993) (court's gatekeeper role is about reliability of methodology, not credibility weighting).

**Verification:** The tested rule (jury may consider expert qualifications for credibility even when expertise is conceded), the legal issue (whether proponent may continue questioning after stipulation), and the credited outcome (yes, because jury considers qualifications for credibility) are all unchanged from the original.
