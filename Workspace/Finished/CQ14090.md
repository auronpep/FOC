### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)
C is correct. The stem presents a diversity case in which the defendant federal employee asserts a federal common law tort defense arising from official duties on federal military property. Post-Erie, federal common law survives in narrow circumstances involving unique federal interests. Gold Key: when a federal common law defense is necessary to avoid a significant conflict between federal interests and state law, and there is a unique or peculiar federal interest (here, federal employees on federal military property during official duty), that defense may apply in diversity after balancing federal and state interests. The facts of official duty on restricted federal military property supply the unique federal interest that makes the defense available. ### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The credited answer and legal issue are unchanged from the original row. The tested black-letter rule is that, after Erie Railroad v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64 (1938), federal courts sitting in diversity apply state substantive law but may still apply federal common law in narrow circumstances where there is a unique federal interest that would be significantly frustrated by application of state law, using a balancing test that weighs federal interests against state interests (Byrd v. Blue Ridge Rural Electric Coop., Inc., 356 U.S. 525 (1958)). In the specific context of federal common law defenses, Boyle v. United Technologies Corp., 487 U.S. 500 (1988), holds that a federal common law defense may preempt a conflicting state law when (1) there is a unique or peculiar federal interest and (2) the state law is in direct conflict with the federal interest. Here, the defense concerns federal employees performing official duties on federal military property—a context presenting a strong federal interest in the management, security, and operations of federal military installations. The court is therefore likely to find that the federal interest is important enough to support application of the federal common law defense in this diversity case. The original row's additional_info and explanations correctly identify this balancing analysis and the Boyle/Byrd framework; the transform preserves that exact legal substance and outcome.
