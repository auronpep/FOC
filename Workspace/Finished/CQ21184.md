While navigating a dense crowd in a bustling Jerusalem marketplace, Silas carefully slid a small leather pouch containing silver coins out of a merchant’s loosely hanging sash. The merchant, distracted by a haggling customer, did not notice the movement and was neither touched nor threatened during the encounter.

What is Silas's most likely common-law offense?

### 2. Distilled Core Question
Silas stealthily takes a coin pouch from a merchant in a crowd without the merchant noticing. What is his most likely crime?

### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation
Larceny is the trespassory taking and carrying away of the personal property of another with the intent to permanently deprive the owner of it. Here, Silas took the merchant's pouch without permission and carried it away. Because he did so stealthily, without using force or placing the merchant in fear, the crime is larceny rather than robbery. The merchant's lack of awareness does not provide a defense to larceny; it merely prevents the crime from escalating to robbery.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
- **A:** This choice is attractive because it correctly identifies that the property was taken from the victim's person or presence. However, robbery requires more than just proximity; it requires that the taking be accomplished by force or intimidation. Since the merchant was unaware and no force was used, this is not robbery.
- **C:** Extortion (or blackmail) involves obtaining property through future threats of harm. In this case, there was no threat made to the merchant; Silas relied on stealth rather than coercion.
- **D:** This choice is attractive to students who confuse the elements of robbery with larceny. While a victim's lack of awareness prevents a robbery conviction (which requires force or fear), it is not a defense to larceny. The trespassory taking of property is a crime regardless of whether the owner realizes it immediately.

### 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification
**Authority:** *Commonwealth v. Jones*, 462 Mass. 211 (2012); Restatement (Second) of Torts § 18 (cmt. g).
Common-law larceny requires a trespassory taking and asportation with intent to steal. Robbery is larceny plus the additional elements of taking from the person or presence by force or intimidation. Stealthy pickpocketing, where the victim is unaware of the taking and no force is used beyond that necessary to lift the item, constitutes larceny but lacks the "force or fear" required for robbery. The transform preserves the rule, issue, and credited outcome.
