### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Gold Key: after formal adversary proceedings begin, the Sixth Amendment right to counsel applies at live corporeal identification confrontations, including lineups and showups, unless counsel is validly waived. Daniel was arraigned and counsel had been appointed, so the right had attached. The police then physically displayed Daniel alone to an eyewitness without telling counsel. That is the critical-stage problem. Silver Key: do not let the answer set change the frame from "live accused displayed to witness after attachment" into "multi-person lineup only," "conversation," or "witness certainty."

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A pulls because one-way glass makes the procedure feel less like questioning. The breaker is that the Sixth Amendment identification rule protects counsel's presence at the corporeal confrontation itself; the witness does not need to speak to the accused.

B pulls because certainty sounds like a reliability answer. The breaker is that certainty belongs to the due process reliability lane, not the Sixth Amendment counsel-at-critical-stage lane. It does not erase the absence of counsel after attachment.

D pulls because Wade is famous as a lineup case. Gold Key: the rule is not limited to multi-person lineups; a live one-person showup after attachment can also be a corporeal critical stage requiring counsel.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged. The Sixth Amendment right to counsel applies at critical stages after adversary judicial proceedings have begun. United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), held that a postindictment lineup is a critical stage requiring counsel. Kirby v. Illinois, 406 U.S. 682 (1972), identifies formal charge, preliminary hearing, indictment, information, or arraignment as the start point for adversary judicial proceedings. Moore v. Illinois, 434 U.S. 220 (1977), confirms that after such proceedings have begun, a lineup or showup implicates the right to counsel. The Department of Justice Criminal Resource Manual section on lineups states the same rule for a lineup or showup at or after initiation of adversary criminal proceedings. Those authorities confirm the source key: Daniel's post-arraignment one-person corporeal showup without counsel gives him a Sixth Amendment suppression argument.
