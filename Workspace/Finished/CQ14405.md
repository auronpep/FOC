# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final Question

On April 10, Lydia mailed Barnabas, the owner of a small worship-music supply shop, an order requesting prompt shipment of the specified "Psalm 150 Deluxe" 25-note handbell set at Barnabas's current catalog price. On April 11, Barnabas mailed Lydia a signed note accepting the order and assuring her that the set would be shipped on April 12. ## 2. Distilled Core Question

## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** Barnabas accepted Lydia's order on April 11 by mailing a signed note promising shipment of the exact Psalm 150 Deluxe handbell set. Once that contract existed, the April 12 shipment of a different set was a nonconforming tender, not a new contract and not a clean counteroffer.

Gold Key: under UCC § 2-206, a nonconforming shipment can be treated as an accommodation only when the shipment is the acceptance and the seller seasonably notifies the buyer that it is offered only as an accommodation; a prior prompt promise has already accepted the buyer's offer. Silver Key: read the events in order and lock the first acceptance event before letting the accommodation label control the answer.

Because the shipped set did not conform to the contract, Lydia may reject the whole, accept the whole, or accept any commercial units and reject the rest under UCC § 2-601. If she rejects, UCC § 2-711 preserves buyer remedies. If she accepts with notice of breach, UCC §§ 2-607 and 2-714 preserve damages for the nonconforming tender. So, on April 14, Lydia may accept or reject the substitute handbell set and still recover any damages caused by the breach.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### A. Accommodation/counteroffer trap - 22% inherited

**What makes it attractive.** This choice hears the word "accommodation" and reaches for the UCC exception. It feels precise because Barnabas mailed a separate notice and the substitute goods arrived with the acceptance note.

**The breaker.** The Gold Key defeats the trap: the accommodation exception matters when the nonconforming shipment is the seller's acceptance. Here, Barnabas had already accepted the original order by mailing the April 11 promise to ship the exact set. The later substitute shipment breached that existing contract. The accommodation notice, even if timely mailed, cannot turn the already-formed contract into a counteroffer.

### B. Substitute-goods contract trap - 1% inherited

**What makes it attractive.** This choice uses the shipment as the contract-forming event and follows the shipped goods to the wrong contract object. Students who remember that shipment can be acceptance may stop one step too early.

**The breaker.** The contract was not for whatever goods Barnabas happened to ship. The April 11 note accepted Lydia's order for the Psalm 150 Deluxe set. A shipment of different goods is nonconforming tender under that contract, not a new contract for the substitute set.

### D. Rejection-waiver trap - 5% inherited

**What makes it attractive.** This choice treats rejection as a harsh election: if Lydia sends the goods back, she must give up damages. That sounds orderly, but it flips the UCC remedial structure.

**The breaker.** Rightful rejection does not waive breach remedies. UCC § 2-711 gives the buyer remedies when the buyer rightfully rejects. Rejection changes what happens to the goods; it does not erase the seller's breach.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer

**Tested rule.** Under UCC § 2-206(1)(b), an order for prompt shipment invites acceptance by either a prompt promise to ship or prompt/current shipment. A shipment of nonconforming goods is not an acceptance if the seller seasonably notifies the buyer that the shipment is offered only as an accommodation, but that accommodation rule does not undo a contract already formed by a prior prompt promise. Under UCC § 2-601, if goods fail in any respect to conform to the contract, the buyer may reject the whole, accept the whole, or accept any commercial unit and reject the rest. UCC §§ 2-607, 2-711, and 2-714 preserve remedies after acceptance or rightful rejection.

## wrong_answer_recovery_paths

- A recovery: ask "what accepted first?" If the answer is the promise, the accommodation label loses.
- B recovery: ask "what goods did the order identify?" The contract follows the ordered goods, not the substitute goods.
- D recovery: ask "does rejection punish the buyer?" Rightful rejection preserves remedies.

## review_truth

When a seller has already accepted a buyer's order, later substituted goods are nonconforming tender, so the buyer may accept or reject them and still keep UCC breach remedies.
