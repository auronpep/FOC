### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**Gold Key:** Under FRE 405(a), when a criminal defendant "opens the door" by calling a character witness to testify about the defendant's reputation for a pertinent character trait, the prosecution may cross-examine that witness by inquiring into relevant specific instances of the defendant's conduct — not as substantive evidence of character, but to test the basis and adequacy of the witness's knowledge of the defendant's reputation.

**B is correct.** Timothy called Lydia to testify about his reputation for honesty. That single act opened the door. The prosecutor's question about the grain-elevation accusation is a specific instance of conduct that, if Lydia was unaware of it, would suggest her knowledge of Timothy's reputation is shallow. If she was aware of it but still calls him honest, that tells the jury something different. Either way, the question is a proper method of impeachment under FRE 405(a). The question is permitted not to prove Timothy's guilt, but to probe whether Lydia really knows what she claims to know.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (Original B) — The Dominant Trap.** This choice sells the misconception that only a conviction can support a cross-examination question about specific acts. The attraction is intuitive: students know that evidence of other acts is often restricted, and they overgeneralize to require a conviction. But FRE 405(a) imposes no such requirement. An arrest, an accusation, or even a rumor can be raised on cross-examination because the purpose is not to prove the act happened but to test whether the character witness knew about it. The conviction requirement is a red herring — it simply does not apply to this form of impeachment.

**C (Original D).** This choice reaches the right outcome (the question should be permitted) but for the wrong reason. The prior accusation is not being offered to show that Timothy is the kind of person who commits forgery — that would be the prohibited propensity inference under FRE 404(a)(1). The question is permitted for a narrow impeachment purpose: to test Lydia's basis of knowledge. A student who picks this answer understands that the question should be allowed but conflates two distinct evidentiary pathways. The Gold Key distinction between impeachment-of-witness and substantive-propensity is what separates the right reason from the wrong one.

**D (Original A).** This choice reflects a misunderstanding of how a defendant places character "at issue." Students associate "putting character at issue" with the defendant personally testifying. But under FRE 404(a)(2)(A), a defendant places character in issue by calling a qualified witness to testify about the defendant's reputation or opinion for a pertinent trait. Timothy did exactly that when he called Lydia. The defendant need not take the stand himself. By opening the door through Lydia, Timothy invited the prosecution to test her knowledge on cross-examination.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification & Legal Reasoning

**Governing Rule:** Federal Rule of Evidence 405(a) — Methods of Proving Character.

> (a) BY REPUTATION OR OPINION. When evidence of a person's character or character trait is admissible, it may be proved by testimony about the person's reputation or by testimony in the form of an opinion. **On cross-examination of the character witness, the court may allow an inquiry into relevant specific instances of the person's conduct.**

**Authority:** Fed. R. Evid. 405(a). See also Fed. R. Evid. 404(a)(2)(A) (defendant may offer evidence of a pertinent character trait; if admitted, the prosecutor may offer evidence to rebut). LII, Cornell Law School, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_405.

**Application:** Timothy called Lydia to testify about his reputation for honesty. Under FRE 404(a)(2)(A), this is a permissible way for a defendant to place a pertinent character trait in evidence. Once the defendant does so, FRE 405(a) expressly authorizes the prosecution to cross-examine the character witness by asking about relevant specific instances of conduct. The purpose is not to prove the conduct occurred as substantive evidence of character (which would run afoul of FRE 404(a)(1)'s propensity bar), but to test the witness's basis of knowledge. The question need not involve a conviction — any relevant specific instance of conduct is fair game on cross-examination.

**Verification:** The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the original. The official key (C) is correct. Research confirms the rule. No doctrinal-currency concerns — FRE 405 has not been amended on this point. **Bank validation verdict: PASS.**

This item tests the intersection of FRE 404(a) and FRE 405(a) — specifically, what happens when a criminal defendant "opens the door" by calling a character witness, and the prosecution responds on cross-examination. This is a C3_SOLVE item — every distractor can be eliminated through visible structural analysis.

**Call:** Should the court permit the question?
