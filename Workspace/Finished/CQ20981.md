## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**A is correct.** The Establishment Clause analysis for a longstanding religiously expressive public monument or display is context-sensitive. The court should look at history, setting, and coercive effect rather than apply a rule that automatically invalidates every religious symbol on public property or automatically validates every old display.

**Gold Key:** A religious symbol on public property is not automatically unconstitutional, and age alone is not automatically dispositive. For longstanding public monuments, symbols, and practices, the analysis is history-focused and context-sensitive.

**Silver Key:** Three answers try to end the case with an automatic command. In this answer array, the responsive choice is the one that refuses an automatic rule and keeps the court in the context inquiry.

Here, the fountain is old, commemorative, surrounded by other civic memorial material, and not tied to compelled religious participation. Those facts do not mechanically decide the case by themselves, but they make the context-sensitive answer the only accurate statement.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**B.** This choice sells a half-right move: the fountain's age is relevant. The breaker is that age is not a complete constitutional test. A court still asks what the display is, how it is presented, what history surrounds it, and whether the government is coercing religious exercise.

**C.** This choice sounds evenhanded because it imagines equal representation as the cure. The breaker is that the Constitution does not require a city to add symbols from every major faith before a longstanding civic display can remain. The choice invents an all-symbols requirement.

**D.** This is the dominant trap. It correctly notices the government actor and the Christian symbol, then turns that observation into an automatic rule. **Gold Key:** the presence of a religious symbol on public property does not by itself establish an Establishment Clause violation. **Silver Key:** the word "automatically" is the cut signal; the answer skips the history-and-context inquiry the call requires.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing rule.** The First Amendment provides that government may not make a law respecting an establishment of religion. In modern Establishment Clause cases, especially after *Kennedy v. Bremerton School District*, 597 U.S. 507 (2022), the Court has rejected the old Lemon/endorsement framework as the controlling test and asks whether the government action is consistent with historical practices and understandings. In *American Legion v. American Humanist Association*, 588 U.S. 29 (2019), the Court treated a longstanding cross-shaped public war memorial as requiring attention to history, context, and the role the monument had taken on over time rather than an automatic religious-symbol rule.

**Authority.** U.S. Const. amend. I; *American Legion v. American Humanist Association*, 588 U.S. 29 (2019), https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/588/17-1717/; *Kennedy v. Bremerton School District*, 597 U.S. 507 (2022), https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/597/21-418/.
