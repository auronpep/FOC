### 7. Full right-answer explanation
A is correct. Attempt is an intent crime: the defendant must intend to commit the target offense and take a sufficiently close step toward it. Gold Key: an attempted unintentional homicide does not fit that structure, because involuntary manslaughter punishes an unintended killing caused by criminal negligence, recklessness, or an unlawful act. Silver Key: when the call says "attempted" before the target crime, test the target offense's mental state before letting near-death facts or rescue facts do the work. Daniel may have acted recklessly, but he did not intend to kill anyone, and no one died. That defeats attempted involuntary manslaughter.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
B is attractive because it sounds like a complete attempt analysis: Daniel finished the dangerous act, the harm was almost fatal, and rescue interrupted the fatal chain. The breaker is that completion-plus-rescue proves proximity and causation pressure, not the specific intent needed for attempt. Gold Key: an attempt charge still needs intent to commit the target offense, and involuntary manslaughter is not an intended killing.

C is attractive because it reaches the same not-guilty result and uses a homicide-classification phrase that sounds technical. The breaker is that lesser-included status does not answer the charge. Daniel is not guilty of attempted involuntary manslaughter because attempt and an unintended killing do not line up, not because prosecutors must charge murder first or cannot charge lesser homicide offenses.

D is attractive because the stem is morally alarming and Daniel's reckless stunt nearly killed three people. The breaker is that recklessness may support involuntary manslaughter if a death occurs, but it does not supply the intent required for an attempt conviction. Silver Key: do not let the severity of the injuries replace the mental state required by the charged inchoate offense.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Criminal Law and Procedure, General Principles, Elements of Crimes, Mens rea, and the verified outline code is `72010302`. Attempt requires an intent to commit the target offense plus conduct sufficiently close to commission. Model Penal Code section 5.01 states the attempt framework in terms of purposeful conduct directed toward commission of the crime, and common-law MBE doctrine treats attempt as requiring specific intent to commit the target crime. See Model Penal Code section 5.01, reproduced at Open Casebook, https://opencasebook.org/casebooks/6141-tanaka-criminal-law-casebook/resources/10.2.1.1-model-penal-code-mpc-501-criminal-attempt/ and Criminal Law Web, https://www.criminallawweb.net/web1/MPC/PART1/snippets/inchoate.htm. Involuntary manslaughter is, by definition, an unintended homicide. Because the target offense does not include an intended killing, a defendant cannot be guilty of attempting involuntary manslaughter under the ordinary bar-exam rule. The credited outcome remains unchanged: the defendant is not guilty because he lacks the mens rea required for attempt.
