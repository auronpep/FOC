### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam-Register)

**Gold Key: NECESSITY FAULT-CREATION BAR** — Necessity requires that the defendant did not create the emergency through his or her own fault. A defendant who negligently or recklessly created the conditions requiring the criminal choice cannot claim necessity, regardless of how compelling the actual emergency became.

Naomi's necessity defense fails because she created the emergency through her own negligence. She deliberately led her flock across a clearly marked private pasture during a posted flash-flood warning. This volitional act of ignoring explicit warnings and closure signs is the very reason she and her animals ended up trapped by rising water. The fault-creation bar defeats the defense even though the danger to life was real and immediate. The law looks at how the defendant got into the situation, not just what options existed once the emergency arose. Because Naomi's own negligent conduct produced the peril, necessity is unavailable as a defense to trespass and property destruction.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**B (new D) — "Yes, because Naomi had no other reasonable alternative once the creek blocked her path."**

**C (new A) — "No, because necessity is never available as a defense to property crimes."**

### 9. Black-Letter Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Governing Rule:** Necessity (choice of evils) requires that (1) the harm avoided is greater than the harm caused, (2) no reasonable lawful alternative existed, and (3) the defendant did not create the emergency through his or her own fault. The defense is unavailable if the defendant negligently or recklessly created the situation requiring the criminal choice.

**Authority:**
- Model Penal Code § 3.02(1)(a): Conduct the actor believes necessary to avoid harm or evil is justifiable provided the harm avoided is greater than that sought to be prevented by the law defining the offense. (MPC does not codify the fault-creation bar in the same provision, but it is a well-established common-law limitation.)
- *People v. Lovercamp*, 43 Cal. App. 3d 823 (1974): The court recognized that necessity requires the defendant not have been at fault in creating the emergency. The defendants who escaped prison under threat of assault could raise necessity only because they did not create the conditions of their peril.
- *State v. Holmes*, 2010: The choice-of-evils defense generally requires that the harms be the product of nature or circumstances beyond the defendant's control.
- Texas Penal Code § 9.22: Necessity is unavailable if the actor created the harm through recklessness or negligence.
- Wex (Cornell LII): "The actor did not substantially contribute to the emergency" is a required element of the necessity defense.

**Application:** Naomi's conduct satisfies the first two elements — the harm avoided (death by drowning) is greater than the harm caused ($2,800 in property damage), and once the creek rose, no reasonable alternative existed. But she fails the third element. She deliberately led her flock across a clearly marked private pasture during a posted flash-flood warning. This volitional, negligent act created the emergency. The fault-creation bar renders necessity unavailable.

#### Wrong-Answer Recovery Paths

**If student picked A:** The student assumed necessity has subject-matter limits. Recovery: remember that necessity can apply to property crimes. The question is whether the elements are met, not what type of crime is charged.

**If student picked B:** The student ranked the harms correctly but forgot the fault-creation bar. Recovery: harm-ranking is necessary but not sufficient. Always check whether the defendant created the emergency before concluding the defense applies.

**If student picked D:** The student correctly identified the no-alternative element but missed the fault-creation bar. Recovery: satisfying one element of a multi-element defense is not enough. The fault-creation bar is an independent requirement that defeats the defense even when other elements are met.

#### Outline Mastery Map

- **Placement:** CRIMINAL > General Principles > Defenses > Justification Defenses > Necessity
- **This item teaches:** The fault-creation bar as an independent requirement of the necessity defense
- **Fills:** Understanding of when necessity fails despite compelling circumstances
- **Adjacent to:** Duress (also has a self-creation limitation under MPC § 2.09), Self-defense (also has an initial-aggressor limitation)

#### Crossover Intersection Map

- **Duress:** Both necessity and duress have self-creation limitations. Under MPC § 2.09, duress requires the defendant not have been negligent or reckless in placing himself in the situation.
- **Self-defense:** Both necessity and self-defense have limitations when the defendant is the initial wrongdoer. An initial aggressor cannot claim self-defense unless he withdraws.
- **Choice of evils (statutory):** Many states have codified the choice-of-evils defense (e.g., Texas Penal Code § 9.22), which explicitly includes the fault-creation bar.

#### Review Truth

A defendant who negligently or recklessly creates the emergency requiring the criminal act cannot claim necessity as a defense.
