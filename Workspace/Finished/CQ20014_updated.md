# CQ20014 - Evidence - Divergent Variant

## Pass-1 Report

**Final Question:**
In a federal civil trial regarding a church property dispute, a lifelong member of the church, Elias, who is ninety-five years old, is called to testify about the boundaries of the church cemetery as they existed decades ago. Elias occasionally struggles with recent names and events, but he states clearly that he remembers the fence line placement from the era in question and understands the solemn obligation to tell the truth in court. The opposing party objects, arguing that Elias's age and memory gaps make him incompetent as a witness. How should the court rule?

**Distilled Core Question:**
Does advanced age or memory weakness render a witness incompetent to testify under the Federal Rules of Evidence?

**Final Choices (Post-Shuffle):**
A: Sustain the objection because memory weaknesses automatically render a witness incompetent.
B: Sustain the objection unless Elias can provide the exact date and time of the fence construction without hesitation.
C: Overrule the objection only if the opposing party cannot produce any other witnesses regarding the cemetery boundaries.
D: Overrule the objection because Elias's memory lapses are matters for the jury to consider in weighing his credibility, not for determining his basic competency.

**Correct Answer:** D

**Letter Map:**
A: Distractor (Mechanic: Misidentifies memory weakness as competency defect)
B: Distractor (Mechanic: Invented foundational requirement of exactness)
C: Distractor (Mechanic: Misstates requirements for admissibility)
D: Correct

**Right-Answer Explanation:**
Under FRE 601, every person is competent to be a witness unless the rules provide otherwise. The threshold for competency is very low; if the witness understands the duty to tell the truth and has personal knowledge of the matter, ordinary memory lapses go to the weight of the testimony and are matters for the trier of fact (the jury) to evaluate, not the court's determination of competency.

**Wrong-Answer Explanations:**
A: Competency is not defeated by memory weaknesses, which are issues of credibility.
B: Precise memory of dates and times is not a prerequisite for witness competency.
C: The existence of other evidence or witnesses does not affect the competency of a witness who has personal knowledge.

**Black-Letter Verification with Authority:**
FRE 601.

**Rigor/Difficulty Note:**
Core. Tests the fundamental principles of witness competency versus credibility.

**C3 Walkthrough:**
*   **CUT:** The core legal issue is whether memory lapses or age defeat witness competency.
*   **CLASH:** The clash is between the opponent's demand for absolute memory precision and the liberal competency standard of FRE 601.
*   **CALL:** The call requires applying the competency standard to determine if the witness may testify.

**Divergence Audit:**
*   Original Stem Summary: Ninety-year-old witness, memory lapses, intersection accident, competency objection.
*   Variant Stem Summary: Ninety-five-year-old witness, memory lapses, cemetery boundary, competency objection.
*   Divergence: Contextual (Cemetery boundary vs intersection accident).

**Review Truth:**
A witness who understands the duty to tell the truth and has personal knowledge is competent, regardless of age-related memory lapses.
