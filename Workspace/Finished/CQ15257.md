### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**D is correct.** Peter is the injured rescuer. The defect in the safety device caused Daniel to fall, and a rescue attempt is a foreseeable response to a climber stranded on a ledge. **Gold Key:** In a defective-product case, do not stop at the buyer/user. A commercial seller can be liable for physical harm to a foreseeable non-user, including a rescuer, when the product defect creates the danger that leads to the rescue. **Silver Key:** Lock the plaintiff in the call. Peter, not Daniel, is suing; the answer must explain why Peter’s injury is within the product-risk chain.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A is incorrect.** This answer uses a negligence-style inspection frame. It focuses on whether Lydia’s Outdoor Goods could have discovered the manufacturing defect. That is not the product-liability path this question is testing. **Gold Key:** Retailer inspection is not the gate in a strict products-liability claim; the key is defective product + commercial seller + harm within the risk.

**B is incorrect.** This answer uses a reliance / representation frame. Peter does not need to be the buyer or to rely on an implied representation of safety if his injury is a foreseeable result of the defective product. **Gold Key:** Foreseeable non-users and rescuers are not cut out merely because they did not buy or rely on the product.

**C is incorrect.** This answer reaches the correct result but for the wrong reason. Daniel’s lack of negligence in failing to test the device is not the reason Peter recovers from the retailer. **Silver Key:** When the call is Peter v. retailer, cut answers that make Daniel’s conduct the deciding reason unless the stem makes Daniel’s conduct the legal gate.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

The tested rule and outcome are unchanged from Q15257. The original row keys **D** and explains that the rescuer succeeds because injury to someone in the rescuer’s position was foreseeable if the defective safety device failed. 

Lawyer-confirmation layer: Restatement (Third) of Torts: Products Liability § 1 states the modern product-liability frame as commercial sellers or distributors being liable for harm caused by defective products; ALI describes the Restatement as covering liability of commercial sellers and distributors for harm caused by products. ([Open Casebook][1]) Cornell’s Wex summary states the strict-liability point directly: if a defective product causes harm, the supplier’s great care is irrelevant. ([Legal Information Institute][2]) Vandermark v. International Railway supplies the rescue principle: danger invites rescue, and the wrong that imperils the victim is also a wrong to the rescuer. ([Justia Law][4])

## review_truth

When a defective product foreseeably creates a rescue, the rescuer is inside the risk chain; do not get trapped by inspection, reliance, or the buyer’s conduct.
