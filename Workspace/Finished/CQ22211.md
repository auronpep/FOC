### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

The receipt is admissible as a business record under FRE 803(6). The business records exception allows a record to be admitted as an exception to the hearsay rule if it was made at or near the time of the event by (or from information transmitted by) a person with knowledge, kept in the course of a regularly conducted business activity, and it was the regular practice of that business to make such a record — provided the source of information and the method of preparation indicate trustworthiness. Here, Lydia made the receipt immediately upon receiving the information from Marcus, who had a business duty to inspect and report accurately. The fact that Lydia lacked personal knowledge of the shipment's contents does not disqualify the record; FRE 803(6) permits the entrant to receive the information from an inherently reliable source within the business chain. The receipt was kept in the regular course of Lydia's cloth trade and has been authenticated by the person who made it.

**Gold Key:** Under FRE 803(6), the entrant need not have personal knowledge of the underlying facts — information from a person with a business duty to report qualifies as an inherently reliable source. This is what separates business records from past recollection recorded.

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**D (past recollection recorded) — DOMINANT TRAP:**
This choice is attractive because Lydia did make a written record and now lacks independent recollection — the classic fact pattern students associate with past recollection recorded. The trap is that PRC under FRE 803(5) requires the record to have been made from the witness's *own* personal knowledge. Here, Lydia never inspected the shipment herself; she received the information from Marcus. Because the record was not based on her personal knowledge, it cannot qualify as past recollection recorded. **Gold Key:** PRC requires the witness's own knowledge — when the entrant lacked personal knowledge, the record can still qualify under the business records exception, which expressly permits information from a reliable source within the business.

**A (best evidence rule):**
This choice tempts students who see an original document being offered and think the best evidence rule is the governing doctrine. The best evidence rule (FRE 1002) is not a hearsay exception and does not make otherwise inadmissible evidence admissible. It merely requires the original writing when proving its contents. The receipt must independently qualify under a hearsay exception to be admitted; the best evidence rule neither supplies nor replaces that analysis.

**B (present recollection refreshed):**
This choice draws attention because Lydia's attorney showed her a document before she testified. However, present recollection refreshed (FRE 612) allows a witness to testify from refreshed recollection — the document itself is *not* admitted into evidence. Here, Lydia stated she has no independent recollection whatsoever, and the attorney is offering the receipt as an exhibit, not using it to prompt live testimony. That is the hallmark of a hearsay exception, not FRE 612.

### 9. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing rule:** FRE 803(6) — Records of Regularly Conducted Activity (Business Records Exception).

A memorandum, report, record, or data compilation of acts, events, conditions, opinions, or diagnoses is not excluded by the hearsay rule if: (A) the record was made at or near the time of the matter recorded; (B) by (or from information transmitted by) a person with knowledge; (C) kept in the course of a regularly conducted business activity; (D) it was the regular practice of that business to make the record; and (E) the source of information and the method of preparation indicate trustworthiness. FRE 803(6) does not require the entrant to have personal knowledge — information from a person with a business duty to report qualifies as an inherently reliable source. *See* FRE 803(6)(B); *see also* 2 McCormick on Evidence § 286 (8th ed.).

**FRE 803(5) — Past Recollection Recorded** requires that the record was made or adopted by the witness when the matter was fresh in the witness's memory and that the witness *now* lacks sufficient recollection — critically, the record must reflect the witness's own knowledge. The original witness (Lydia) lacked personal knowledge of the shipment contents; she received the information from Marcus. This disqualifies PRC.

**FRE 612 — Present Recollection Refreshed** permits a witness to testify from refreshed memory after reviewing a document; the document itself is not received as an exhibit. Here, the document is offered as an exhibit and the witness has no recollection to refresh.

**FRE 1002 — Best Evidence Rule** requires the original writing to prove its contents but is not a hearsay exception and does not independently render a document admissible.
