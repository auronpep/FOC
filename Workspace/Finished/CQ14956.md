# Pass-1 Transform Report - Q14956 (Real Property - Mortgages)

## 1. Final Question

Barnabas owned a small storefront building where he ran the Daily Bread bakery. To buy the building, Barnabas signed a loan note secured by a mortgage on the building, and Barnabas was personally liable on the note. Barnabas later sold the building to Lydia. ## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**D is correct.** Lydia did more than buy the building subject to an existing mortgage. She expressly assumed the mortgage debt. As between Barnabas and Lydia, that made Lydia the party who should bear the mortgage obligation. Barnabas remained personally liable to the lender unless the lender released him, but that continuing personal liability did not block reimbursement. It made him a surety after Lydia assumed the debt. When Lydia missed due installments and Barnabas paid them to prevent default and foreclosure, suretyship gave Barnabas a right to reimbursement from Lydia for the amounts he paid.

**Gold Key:** When a purchaser expressly assumes an existing mortgage, the purchaser becomes the principal obligor as between buyer and seller, and the original mortgagor is treated as a surety. If the original mortgagor pays a due installment that the assuming purchaser should have paid, the original mortgagor may seek reimbursement from the purchaser.

**Silver Key:** Separate the lender's rights from the buyer-seller reimbursement question. The lender may still hold the original borrower personally liable, but between the original borrower and the assuming buyer, the assuming buyer is the one who should pay.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

### Choice A - Dominant Trap

**Why it is attractive:** Barnabas signed the note and stayed personally liable. A student may read that fact as ending the case: if Barnabas owed the lender, then paying the installments was just paying his own debt.

**The breaker:** Personal liability is not the reason Barnabas loses. It is the reason suretyship has work to do. Lydia expressly assumed the mortgage debt, so as between Lydia and Barnabas, Lydia became the principal obligor. Barnabas remained liable to the lender, but his liability was secondary as between the parties. **Gold Key:** the assuming purchaser owes the debt as principal between buyer and seller; the original mortgagor who pays a due installment can seek reimbursement.

### Choice B

**Why it is attractive:** The answer gets the yes/no outcome right, and it sounds like the sale shifted the mortgage burden to Lydia. Students often compress "Lydia assumed the mortgage" into "Barnabas is off the hook."

**The breaker:** A sale with assumption does not, by itself, release the original mortgagor from personal liability to the lender. Release requires the lender's release, novation, or another arrangement discharging Barnabas. Barnabas wins for a different reason: he remained liable to the lender but became a surety as between himself and Lydia.

### Choice C

**Why it is attractive:** Foreclosure is the familiar mortgage remedy, so the answer tempts the student to think the mortgage process must happen before any reimbursement action. It also sounds procedurally cautious.

**The breaker:** Barnabas is not trying to foreclose. He is suing Lydia for reimbursement after he paid due installments that Lydia had assumed. Suretyship reimbursement does not require Barnabas to initiate or wait for foreclosure proceedings before seeking repayment from the principal obligor.

## 9. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing mortgage-assumption rule:** Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages Section 5.1 treats assumption of liability as a promise by the transferee of mortgaged real estate to perform the obligation secured by the mortgage. When mortgaged real estate is transferred with assumption of liability, the mortgage remains effective against the real estate, and the mortgagee may proceed against the transferee personally to the extent of the transferee's assumption liability while preserving recourse according to the parties' respective liabilities. A federal court applying the Restatement quotes and applies Section 5.1 in this form in *Thompson v. Florida Wood Treaters, Inc.*, Civil No. 2006-224 (D.V.I. Dec. 6, 2009), available at https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-vid-3_06-cv-00224/pdf/USCOURTS-vid-3_06-cv-00224-0.pdf. The ALI publication page for the Restatement is https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-third/property-mortgages.

**Governing suretyship reimbursement rule:** Restatement (Third) of Suretyship & Guaranty Section 22 provides the reimbursement rule for a secondary obligor who pays the principal obligor's debt. The paying surety's right of reimbursement arises when the underlying obligation is due and the secondary obligor pays. The ALI publication page is https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-third/suretyship-and-guaranty. A Real Property, Trust and Estate Law Journal article summarizes the same Restatement rule and identifies Section 22 as the reimbursement provision: https://www.dwt.com/-/media/files/publications/2016/03/2016_hulse_after-the-guarantor-pays.pdf.

**Application:** Barnabas was personally liable on the note. Lydia expressly assumed the mortgage debt when she bought the building. As between Barnabas and Lydia, Lydia became the principal obligor for the assumed mortgage debt, while Barnabas's continuing exposure to the lender put him in a surety position. Lydia missed due installments. Barnabas paid those overdue installments. Because the underlying installments were due and Barnabas paid them, he may seek reimbursement from Lydia under suretyship.
