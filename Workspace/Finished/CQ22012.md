## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

D is correct. The phrase "for so long as" is durational language. It gives Ruth House a fee simple determinable and leaves Peter with an automatic future interest if the stated use stops. Gold Key: a fee simple determinable leaves the grantor a possibility of reverter, not a reversion or a right of reentry. Silver Key: when the stem asks for the donor's interest, sort the answer by the trigger: automatic end plus uncertain event equals possibility of reverter.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because the deed contains future-looking conditions and a gift over to another charity. The breaker is the call. Peter's retained interest is a grantor interest, and the Rule Against Perpetuities does not void a grantor's possibility of reverter.

B is attractive because the deed has conditional language and the property might return if the use stops. The breaker is automatic operation. "For so long as" does not require Peter to enter or sue to retake possession.

C is attractive because the land can return to Peter, so "reversion" feels close. The breaker is inevitability. A reversion follows an estate certain to end, but Ruth House's estate may last forever if the parcel keeps being used for the stated residence.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is unchanged: Peter retained a valid possibility of reverter. A conveyance using durational language such as "for so long as" creates a fee simple determinable. If the stated use ends, title automatically returns to the grantor. Because the estate might never end, the grantor's retained future interest is a possibility of reverter, not a reversion. Because the interest is retained by the grantor, the Rule Against Perpetuities does not invalidate it.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: Cornell Wex states that a possibility of reverter is the future interest retained by the grantor of a fee simple determinable and that durational language such as "until" or "as long as" creates it. Cornell Wex's fee-simple-determinable entry likewise states that ownership automatically returns to the grantor and that the grantor's future interest is a possibility of reverter. The same rule is reflected in Restatement (First) of Property section 44's formulation of fee simple determinable as an estate that expires automatically on a stated event. The RAP point is also standard: the Rule Against Perpetuities does not apply to interests retained by the grantor, including possibilities of reverter.
