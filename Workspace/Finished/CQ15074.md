# Pass-1 Transform Report — Q-15074

## 1. Final Question

Barnabas owned five adjacent parcels of farmland. He borrowed $40,000 from a credit union and gave the credit union a promissory note and a deed of trust encumbering all five parcels. The deed of trust was promptly and properly recorded. After Barnabas repaid $8,000, the credit union executed and delivered to him a valid release of Parcel 3. Barnabas altered the release document to also include Parcel 5, then recorded the altered instrument. He thereafter sold Parcel 5 to Lydia, an innocent purchaser for value. When the credit union discovered the alteration, it brought an appropriate action against Barnabas and Lydia to set aside the release as to Parcel 5. Barnabas did not defend, but Lydia did. The jurisdiction's recording act provides: "No unrecorded conveyance or mortgage of real property shall be good against subsequent purchasers for value without notice, who shall first record."

The court should rule for

A: Lydia, because the credit union was negligent in failing to verify the recorded release before the sale.

B: Lydia, because she was entitled to rely on the recorded release.

C: the credit union, because the alteration of the release was ineffective.

D: the credit union, because Lydia could have discovered the alteration by reasonable inquiry.

## 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**C is correct.** The recording act in the jurisdiction is a race-notice statute: it protects subsequent purchasers for value without notice who first record. But the recording act only protects against *unrecorded* prior interests. A forged or fraudulently altered instrument is **void ab initio** — it is a legal nullity that conveys no interest whatsoever. Because the release was validly executed only as to Parcel 3, Barnabas's unauthorized addition of Parcel 5 was a fraudulent alteration. The altered release is void as to Parcel 5. A void instrument does not become effective merely by being recorded, and no subsequent purchaser — however innocent — can acquire rights through a void instrument. Lydia therefore cannot claim the protection of the recording act, and the credit union's deed of trust remains enforceable against Parcel 5.

**Gold Key:** A forged or fraudulently altered instrument is void ab initio — it is a legal nullity that conveys no title or interest, and recording does not cure the defect. A bona fide purchaser cannot prevail through a void instrument under any type of recording act.

## 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is attractive** because it feels unfair that the credit union didn't catch the alteration earlier — the lender seems careless, and students want to assign blame where negligence appears plausible. The breaker: the recording system does not impose a duty on lenders to police the records for fraud after a valid release is executed. Constructive notice runs to purchasers searching the record, not to lenders monitoring it. The credit union had no legal obligation to check whether Barnabas would later tamper with the release. **Gold Key:** The recording act allocates risk to subsequent purchasers (who must search), not to prior interest-holders (who must police the records).

**B is the dominant trap** because the recording act explicitly protects subsequent purchasers for value without notice, and Lydia did exactly what the statute contemplates — she searched the record, found a recorded release, and relied on it. The pull is strong: the statute's plain language seems to be her shield. The breaker: the recording act only protects against *unrecorded* prior interests. A forged/altered instrument is void ab initio — it is not merely unrecorded, it is a legal nullity. Recording a void instrument does not give it life. Lydia's status as a BFP cannot cure a void instrument. **Gold Key:** A forged instrument is void ab initio — it conveys no interest, and no recording act protects a purchaser through a void instrument, regardless of good faith.

**D is attractive** because it reaches the right party (the credit union) and sounds like it involves a reasonable-inquiry standard that seems plausible in a fraud context. Students who sense B is wrong but can't articulate why may land on D as the "next best" answer. The breaker: constructive notice under the recording act means searching the recorded instruments in the chain of title — it does not require a purchaser to investigate whether each recorded instrument is genuine or has been altered. Imposing a duty to inspect for forgery would destroy the reliability of the recording system. **Gold Key:** Constructive notice = searching the record, not auditing the record for fraud.

## 8. Black-Letter Law Verification

**Governing rule:** A forged or fraudulently altered instrument affecting real property is void ab initio — a legal nullity that conveys no title or interest. Recording a void instrument does not validate it. A subsequent bona fide purchaser cannot acquire rights through a void instrument under any type of recording act (race, notice, or race-notice).

**Authority:** The majority rule, followed in most U.S. jurisdictions, holds that a forged deed or instrument is void, not merely voidable. *See, e.g.*, Florida (forged deed is void ab initio — conveys no title even to a good-faith purchaser); Texas Prop. Code § 13.001 (notice statute; recording protects against unrecorded instruments, not void ones). The Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages § 5.4 treats forged mortgage releases as ineffective. Patton on Titles § 8 and 4 Am. Law of Property § 17.10 confirm that recording acts presuppose a valid instrument — a void instrument gains nothing from recording.

**Application:** The jurisdiction's recording act is a race-notice statute (key language: "without notice, who shall first record"). The act protects subsequent BFPs against prior *unrecorded* interests. But the altered release is not merely unrecorded — it is void as to Parcel 5 because Barnabas fraudulently altered it without the credit union's authorization. A void instrument is not within the scope of recording-act protection. Lydia, despite being an innocent purchaser for value, cannot prevail through a void instrument. The credit union's deed of trust remains enforceable against Parcel 5.
