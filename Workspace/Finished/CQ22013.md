## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Naomi sold her terraced hillside olive grove to Stephen. The grove ran to 60 acres, of which a stone press house and a small almond stand sat on one corner and the rest was planted in old olive trees. The price was $180,000. Stephen paid $90,000 in cash at closing, and the remaining $90,000 was to be paid in full 12 years after closing, with interest of 6 percent per year paid to Naomi on a monthly basis until then.

After taking title, Stephen moved onto the grove with his wife Lydia, paid the monthly interest to Naomi as agreed, and paid the real estate taxes of $1,500 per year as they came due. Stephen worked the grove for eleven years and then died. His will devised the grove to Lydia for life, remainder to his brother Barnabas. After Stephen's death, Lydia stayed on the grove and kept making the monthly interest payments to Naomi. The next year the $90,000 balance of the purchase price became due, and Lydia received a $1,500 tax bill from the county assessor.

As between Lydia and Barnabas, which of the following correctly states their respective obligations for the real estate taxes and the principal balance owed under Stephen's purchase?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

As between a life tenant and a remainderman, who pays the property taxes and who pays the principal of the debt encumbering the land?

### 3. Final answer choices

B. Barnabas is obligated to pay the real estate taxes, but Lydia is obligated to pay the $90,000 balance to Naomi.

C. Lydia is obligated to pay the real estate taxes, but Barnabas is obligated to pay the $90,000 balance to Naomi.

D. Lydia is obligated to pay the real estate taxes and to pay the $90,000 balance to Naomi.

### 5. Correct answer

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. A life tenant owes the current carrying charges of the property and the remainderman owes the capital debt. Property taxes that fall due during the life tenancy are a current carrying charge, so Lydia pays them. The unpaid $90,000 purchase balance is the principal of an encumbrance, and principal is a charge against the inheritance, so Barnabas pays it. Gold Key: as between a life tenant and a remainderman, the life tenant pays property taxes and interest on an encumbrance, while the remainderman pays the principal of the encumbrance. Silver Key: read the answer set as a taxes-column by principal-column grid, decide each column on its own, and reject any choice that dumps both obligations on one party.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because Barnabas is the one who ends up owning the grove outright, so it feels right that the eventual owner shoulders the whole burden, taxes included. The breaker is timing: real estate taxes accrue during Lydia's possession, and current taxes follow the present possessor, not the future owner.

B is attractive because it splits the two duties between the two people, which looks like the careful, balanced answer. The breaker is direction: it assigns the recurring tax to the future owner and the capital debt to the present possessor, which reverses both rules at once.

D is attractive because Lydia lives on the grove, collects the bills, and is already paying the monthly interest, so it seems she owes whatever comes due, including the maturing balance. The breaker is the Gold Key: a life tenant pays taxes and interest but never the principal of an encumbrance, which is the remainderman's charge.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is unchanged: the life tenant pays the taxes and the remainderman pays the principal balance. A life tenant is entitled to current possession and is correspondingly bound to bear the ordinary current charges of the property. That duty covers property taxes accruing during the life tenancy and the interest on any encumbrance on the land. The principal of an encumbrance is a capital obligation that runs with the inheritance and is borne by the remainderman, because retiring the principal preserves and enriches the future estate rather than the present possessory enjoyment. Applying that to the facts, the $1,500 tax bill and the monthly 6 percent interest are Lydia's, and the $90,000 maturing principal balance is Barnabas's.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: the allocation of current charges to the life tenant — taxes (to the extent of the property's income or rental value) and interest on encumbrances — is stated in the Restatement (First) of Property §§ 129–131 (duties of a life tenant as to current charges). The companion rule, that the principal of a mortgage or other encumbrance is a charge against the remainder and is borne by the remainderman, is settled black-letter property doctrine (see Stoebuck & Whitman, The Law of Property § 4.3, life tenant pays interest and carrying charges while the remainderman bears principal). This is not on the doctrinal-currency hotlist, and the research agrees with the supplied official key, so PASS is available.
