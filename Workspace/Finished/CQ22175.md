## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question
Martha owned a three-level private marketplace building where several small businesses rented studio space. Lydia rented the entire third floor for a Bible-lettering workshop. People visiting Lydia's studio could ride the inside elevator or use a narrow outside stairway bolted to the rear wall of the building, running from the courtyard to the third floor only. Hannah attended a workshop there. As she left and walked down the outside stairway, she fell and suffered serious injuries. Hannah sued Martha, alleging that the stairway was dangerous because it was too steep, had no handrail, and had narrow treads. Martha denied that the stairway was dangerous. She also asserted as an affirmative defense that she did not control the stairway because it had been leased to Lydia as part of the third-floor studio. At trial, Hannah called Stephen, who had been Martha's building caretaker at the time of the accident but was now unemployed. Stephen testified that two days after the accident, Martha told him to install a handrail on the stairway and to post a sign reading, "CAUTION: Steep and narrow studio stair." Martha's attorney objected to the testimony and moved to strike it. Which is Hannah's most effective response?

### 2. Distilled Core Question
In a negligence case where the defendant denies control of the accident area, may the plaintiff use the defendant's later repair and warning to prove control?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation
C is correct. The testimony describes a later repair and warning, so it cannot be used simply to prove that Martha was careless or that the stairs were dangerous. Gold Key: under FRE 407, later remedial measures may be admitted for another purpose, including disputed ownership or control. Silver Key: when a public-policy exclusion is in play, label the purpose of the evidence before arguing relevance. Martha made control a live issue by saying the stairway was part of Lydia's lease, and Martha's instruction to add the rail and warning sign supports the inference that Martha controlled the stairway.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because awareness feels like a cleaner purpose than saying "the stair was dangerous." The breaker is that awareness of the dangerous condition still points the jury toward fault for the accident. Gold Key: FRE 407 lets the later measure in for disputed control, not as a back-door way to prove culpability.

B is attractive only if the student imagines an employment-status rule about whether Stephen may testify against Martha. The breaker is that Stephen's current job status does not answer the Rule 407 objection. A former caretaker with personal knowledge can still testify; the fight is over the purpose for which the later repair and warning are offered.

D is attractive because the handrail and warning sign seem to say the stairs needed fixing. The breaker is the exact public-policy bar. Silver Key: do not stop at "relevant"; ask whether the stated purpose is an allowed purpose. Dangerousness is the forbidden fault-purpose, not the control exception.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue is Evidence, Privileges and Public Policy Exclusions, Public Policy Exclusions, Subsequent remedial measures, and the verified outline code is `35030301`. The source row's supplied code `31010500` points to impeachment, but the operative rule is FRE 407, so the deeper matching outline node is `35030301`.

Federal Rule of Evidence 407 bars evidence of measures taken after an injury when offered to prove negligence, culpable conduct, a product defect, or a need for a warning. The same rule permits the court to admit the evidence for another purpose, including impeachment or, when disputed, proving ownership, control, or feasibility of precautionary measures. Current rule sources confirm that Rule 407 is the governing authority: the U.S. Courts 2024 Evidence Rules compilation includes Rule 407, and Cornell LII's rule text and advisory-committee notes describe the same control exception. Sources: https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-evidence-dec-1-2024_0.pdf and https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_407.

The credited answer remains unchanged. Martha expressly denied control over the stairway. Her instruction two days later to install a handrail and post a warning sign is not offered to prove the stairs were dangerous or that she was negligent; it is offered to prove that she exercised control over the stairway. That is an allowed FRE 407 purpose because control is disputed.
