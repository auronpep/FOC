### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

**D**. A Rule 30 notice of deposition is sufficient to compel an adverse party's attendance. Fed. R. Civ. P. 30(a)(1) allows a party to depose any other party without a subpoena; a subpoena under Rule 45 is required only to compel a non-party witness's attendance. Mark is a party-defendant, so Paul's notice of deposition was sufficient. **Gold Key:** the party-vs-non-party distinction in Rule 30 controls — subpoenas are for witnesses, not for adverse parties.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. No, because the songwriter did not subpoena the singer.**

This is the dominant trap. The student imports the witness-subpoena rule into a party-deposition context. The breaker is the **Gold Key**: Rule 30(a)(1) permits depositions of any other party by notice alone; a Rule 45 subpoena is required only for non-party witnesses. Visible from the stem: Mark is a named defendant, not a non-party witness.

**B. No, because the singer will likely be called to testify at trial.**

The trial-testimony mislabel. The student's intuition is that a party who will testify has no need to be deposed. The breaker is that Rule 30 expressly contemplates deposing parties; whether the party will also testify at trial is irrelevant to whether a deposition may be compelled. Visible from the call: the question is whether the notice suffices, not whether the deposition is useful.

**C. Yes, because there is likely substantial need for the singer's deposition.**

The fabricated-requirement trap. The student imports the work-product / non-parties rule into a party context. The breaker is that Rule 30(a)(1) imposes no substantial-need showing for a party deposition; a party may be deposed on any non-privileged matter.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The supplied row tests QID 19022, a Civil Procedure item on whether a Rule 30 notice of deposition alone is sufficient to compel a party-defendant's attendance. The official key is A: yes, because the singer is an adverse party.

Fed. R. Civ. P. 30(a)(1) provides that "a party may, by oral questions, depose any person, including a party, without leave of court except as provided in Rule 31(a)(2)." A party-deponent need not be subpoenaed; service of the written notice under Rule 30(b)(1) on the party's attorney of record is sufficient. Rule 45 subpoenas are required only to compel the attendance of non-party witnesses at a deposition. See, e.g., 8A Charles Alan Wright et al., Federal Practice and Procedure § 2103 (3d ed.) (Rule 30 allows a party to depose any other party by notice alone). The transformed outcome is identical: Paul's notice was sufficient to compel Mark's deposition appearance, and a subpoena was not required.
