## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation
C is correct. Mercy Bank advanced the money Daniel used to acquire the warehouse lot and took a mortgage on that lot as part of the same purchase. Gold Key: a purchase-money mortgage is senior to a lien that arose against the buyer before the buyer acquired the property. Silver Key: separate lien attachment from lien priority. Lydia's judgment may attach under the statute, but the call asks which lien is first, and purchase-money status puts the bank ahead.

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations
A is attractive because the numbers are clean: Daniel paid $32,000 down, and Lydia's judgment was only $14,000. The breaker is the priority frame. The relative size of the down payment and the judgment debt does not decide which lien is first.

B is attractive because seller-financed purchase-money mortgages are familiar, and Mercy Bank was not the seller. The breaker is the Gold Key: purchase-money priority can also belong to a third-party lender when the lender's money is used to buy the land and the mortgage is part of that purchase.

D is the dominant trap. It is attractive because Lydia's judgment was properly filed before Daniel bought the lot, and Mercy Bank had constructive notice of that filing. The breaker is the Silver Key: notice and attachment do not answer the whole call. Purchase-money priority defeats the ordinary recording-notice pull here, so Lydia's judgment lien is not first.

## 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification AND Legal Reasoning for the Right Answer
The tested issue remains Real Property, Mortgages, Purchase-money mortgage priority, and the verified outline code is `83050102`. A purchase-money mortgage secures funds used to acquire the mortgaged real property. When the mortgage is part of the acquisition transaction, it takes priority over liens and claims that arose against the buyer before the buyer acquired title, including a previously filed judgment lien that attaches to after-acquired property.

The authority floor is satisfied by Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages section 7.2(b), which gives a purchase-money mortgage priority over liens or claims arising against the purchaser-mortgagor before acquisition of title. The American Law Institute identifies chapter 7 of the Restatement as the priorities chapter for real-property security, and secondary reporting quotes section 7.2(b) on the exact prior-lien rule. Wisconsin's published statute and annotations state the same rule in concrete statutory form: a purchase-money mortgage includes one given to a third person who advances purchase money, and a purchase-money mortgage executed with the delivery of title has priority over existing judgments against the buyer. See Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages section 7.2(b); https://www.ali.org/publications/restatement-law-third/property-mortgages; https://www.nlrg.com/legal-content/the-lawletter/bid/68046/property-vendor-s-purchase-money-mortgage-priority-over-third-party-purchase-money-mortgage; https://law.justia.com/codes/wisconsin/chapter-708/section-708-09/. No researched authority contradicted the supplied credited answer.

The credited outcome is unchanged. Lydia's judgment lien attached under the after-acquired-property statute, but that only gets Lydia into the priority contest. Mercy Bank's loan funded the purchase price, and the bank's mortgage was recorded immediately after the deed as part of the closing. Mercy Bank's purchase-money mortgage is senior to Lydia's earlier judgment lien. Lydia's motion should not be granted.
