# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question — revised stem + call

Daniel agreed to create and lead a private Scripture-themed lantern-and-handbell evening for Lydia’s family Easter celebration for **$48,000**. Daniel reasonably expected to spend **$31,000** on lantern rentals, music assistants, and supplies. Before Daniel rented anything or began preparing, Lydia repudiated the contract without excuse. Daniel had no other losses and avoided all performance costs.

**What is Daniel’s proper expectation recovery?**

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A service provider had a $48,000 contract, expected $31,000 in performance costs, and the client repudiated before any costs were incurred. What expectation damages does the provider recover?

## 3. Final answer choices

A. **$17,000**, because Daniel recovers the profit he would have earned after subtracting costs saved.
B. **$48,000**, because Lydia repudiated the contract before Daniel began preparing.
C. **Nothing**, because Daniel had not yet begun preparing when Lydia repudiated.
D. **$31,000**, because Daniel expected to spend that amount performing.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**A is correct.** Daniel’s expectation recovery is the benefit of the bargain, not the gross contract price and not the costs he never had to spend. The contract price was $48,000. The performance costs Daniel avoided were $31,000. His expected net profit was therefore **$17,000**.

**Gold Key:** In a service contract repudiated before performance, expectation damages are the contractor’s lost profit when all performance costs are avoided: contract price minus costs saved, plus any other proven loss.

**Silver Key:** Read the answer array as a damages-measure array: gross price, expected cost, net profit, and zero. The only answer that both uses the contract price and subtracts the avoided costs survives.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B.** This choice sells the instinct that repudiation unlocks the whole contract price. The breaker is the saved-cost fact: Daniel avoided $31,000 in performance costs, so awarding $48,000 would overpay him.

**C.** This choice sells the no-work myth: no preparation feels like no damages. **Gold Key:** lost profit can exist even before work begins, because the breach deprived Daniel of the bargain’s net benefit.

**D.** This choice sells the precision of the cost number. The breaker is that expected costs were not Daniel’s loss; they were costs he avoided and therefore subtracts.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

The source row identifies Q#20271 as a Contracts expectation-damages item testing common-law expectation damages with a cost-saved offset, with the credited outcome equal to contract price minus avoided performance costs.  The revised item preserves that rule and moves only the surface story. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 347 states the expectation measure as loss in value plus other loss, less cost or other loss avoided by not having to perform; that supports the $48,000 − $31,000 = $17,000 recovery.
