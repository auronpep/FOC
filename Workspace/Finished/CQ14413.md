### 6. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** The sign was a unilateral offer: the bookstore asked for a completed recitation in exchange for $30 in store credit. Peter did not earn the credit until he finished the recitation, but once he began the invited performance, the bookstore could not revoke the offer out from under him.

**Gold Key:** In a unilateral-contract offer, beginning the invited performance creates an option contract; the offer becomes temporarily irrevocable, while the offeror's duty to pay remains conditional on completion.

**Silver Key:** Separate the two time points. Beginning performance freezes the offer; completing performance earns the promised payout.

Peter began the requested recitation before Lydia cancelled the promotion, and he later finished it correctly. The store credit was therefore enforceable.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A. No, because the bookstore's sign was only a friendly gift-style promotion.** This choice is attractive because the sign sounds cheerful, promotional, and generous. The breaker is the visible exchange language: "recite Psalm 23 ... and we will give you $30." That is a requested performance for a stated reward, not a no-strings gift.

**C. Yes, because Peter's public recitation gave the bookstore notice that he had accepted.** This choice reaches the right yes/no result but sells the wrong legal engine. The breaker is the Gold Key: beginning performance made the offer irrevocable; it was not a notice rule, and Peter's acceptance was completed by finishing the requested recitation.

**D. No, because the bookstore cancelled the offer before Peter finished the requested recitation.** This is the dominant trap because it remembers one real point: a unilateral contract is accepted by completing the requested performance. The breaker is the Silver Key. Completion earns the payout, but beginning performance freezes the offer first, so Lydia's mid-recitation cancellation was ineffective.

### 8. Black-letter verification and legal reasoning

**Pass-1 step (1) - black-letter rule tested.** The item tests the modern common-law unilateral-contract rule for revocation after the offeree has begun invited performance.

Restatement (Second) of Contracts §71 confirms that a performance is consideration when it is sought in exchange for the promise.
