## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Martha signed a written agreement to sell Timothy a narrow vineyard path parcel that she used for quiet prayer walks beside her family print shop. The agreement did not say what estate or interest Martha would convey. Later, Martha delivered a deed for the parcel to Timothy. Which of the following statements best describes the effect of the deed?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. Gold Key: an otherwise complete deed that does not state the estate conveyed is presumed to pass the grantor's interest at delivery unless the deed itself shows an intent to pass a lesser estate. Silver Key: once the call asks about the deed's effect, measure the interest at deed delivery, not at the earlier contract. Martha's deed therefore conveyed whatever interest Martha had when she delivered it.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is the dominant trap because it turns the silence into a stronger promise than the deed made. A silent deed can pass the grantor's interest, but it does not automatically enlarge that interest into a fee simple absolute or create damages merely because the grantor held less.

B sells a formal-defect story. The deed should identify the estate, but when it is otherwise complete and has no contrary limiting language, the law supplies a presumption instead of treating the conveyance as a nullity.

D is attractive because the sale agreement came first. Silver Key: the deed is the conveyance instrument, so the operative measuring point is delivery of the deed, not formation of the earlier contract.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule is unchanged: when a real-property conveyance is otherwise complete but silent about the estate or interest conveyed, the deed is presumed to pass the grantor's estate or interest at the time of delivery unless the instrument shows an intent to convey a lesser estate. The credited outcome is unchanged because the buyer receives whatever interest the seller held when the deed was delivered.

Authority floor: New York Real Property Law sec. 245 states that a grant of real property passes all the grantor's estate or interest unless a lesser intent appears from the grant, and that a greater estate does not pass than the grantor possessed or could lawfully convey at delivery. ORS 93.120 states the same general conveyancing rule: a conveyance passes the grantor's estate unless a lesser intent appears by express terms or necessary implication. Research did not contradict the supplied official key.

Sources: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/245 and https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_93.120.
