## 7. Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

The exclusionary rule suppresses evidence that is the "fruit of the poisonous tree" — derivative evidence obtained as a result of illegal police conduct. However, a new voluntary crime committed during or after an unlawful stop is an independent intervening act that severs the causal connection between the police illegality and the evidence of that new crime. Stephen's push was a new criminal act, not derivative evidence of the unlawful stop. The state may introduce testimony about the push in the assault prosecution.

**Silver Key:** When the defendant commits a new crime in response to police illegality, ask: "Is this derivative evidence of the original illegality, or is it an independent act?" An independent voluntary crime severs the taint.

**Gold Key:** The attenuation doctrine provides that evidence is admissible if the connection between the illegal police conduct and the evidence is sufficiently attenuated. A new voluntary criminal act by the defendant is the strongest form of attenuation — it breaks the causal chain entirely.

## 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A — Yes, because the exclusionary rule immunizes all conduct after police illegality.** The attraction: students may overgeneralize the exclusionary rule into a blanket protection — once the police act illegally, everything that happens afterward is tainted. The breaker: the exclusionary rule does not grant immunity for new crimes. It suppresses derivative evidence obtained through the illegality, but it does not give a suspect a license to commit independent crimes without consequence. The suspect remains criminally accountable for voluntary acts of violence.

**C — No, because unlawful stops are valid if the suspect later commits a crime.** The attraction: this reaches the correct outcome (No, not suppressed), and students who are unsure about the reasoning may grab any "No" answer. The breaker: the later crime does NOT retroactively make the original stop lawful. The stop remains unlawful. The reason evidence of the push is admissible is not that the stop retroactively became valid, but that the push is an independent act not derived from the stop.

**D — Yes, because the push happened during an unlawful stop. (Dominant Trap)** The attraction: the push literally occurred while the unlawful stop was ongoing — what could be a stronger temporal connection? Students see the proximity and assume the fruit-of-the-poisonous-tree doctrine makes the evidence of the push inadmissible. The breaker: temporal proximity alone does not make evidence derivative. The push was a new voluntary crime — an independent act that broke the causal chain. The exclusionary rule suppresses evidence that would not have been obtained "but for" the illegality, but a new crime committed by the suspect is not "obtained" through the stop; it is an independent act with independent consequences.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Authority:** The exclusionary rule and the fruit-of-the-poisonous-tree doctrine are governed by Fourth Amendment case law. *Wong Sun v. United States*, 371 U.S. 471 (1963), established the attenuation doctrine — evidence is admissible if the connection between the illegal police conduct and the evidence is sufficiently attenuated to dissipate the taint. An independent intervening act by the defendant is the clearest form of attenuation.

*United States v. Ceccolini*, 435 U.S. 268 (1978) (attenuation analysis considers the voluntariness of the intervening act). The attenuation factors include: (1) the time between the illegality and the evidence, (2) the presence of intervening circumstances, and (3) the purpose and flagrancy of the official misconduct.

A new voluntary crime committed by the defendant is an intervening act that severs the causal connection. *See also Hudson v. Michigan*, 547 U.S. 586 (2006) (attenuation doctrine applies when the causal link between police illegality and evidence is weak). The rule is well established: the exclusionary rule does not suppress evidence of a new independent crime committed in response to police illegality.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-CRIMPROC-ATTENUATION-01** (rule): The attenuation doctrine provides that evidence is admissible if the connection between illegal police conduct and the evidence is sufficiently attenuated. A new voluntary criminal act by the defendant is the strongest form of attenuation — it breaks the causal chain entirely.

**GK-CRIMPROC-SCOPE-01** (rule): The exclusionary rule does not immunize a suspect from prosecution for new crimes committed in response to or during police illegality. It suppresses derivative evidence, not independent criminal conduct.

**SK-CRI…E-01** (cut): When the defendant commits a new crime in response to police illegality, ask: "Is this derivative evidence of the original illegality, or is it an independent act?" An independent voluntary crime severs the taint.
