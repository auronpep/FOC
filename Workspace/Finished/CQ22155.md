# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question (revised stem + call)

Hannah was injured while riding a small harvest train at a privately owned Christian-themed family park. Hannah sued the park for negligence. During trial, Hannah sought to call the Chief Mechanical Inspector of the State Ride Safety Bureau.

Hannah wanted the inspector to testify about an official report the inspector made after the accident. Specifically, Hannah wanted the inspector to read a quote from the train attendant, who said Hannah was injured because the attendant had forgotten to replace a worn brake cable.

Is the report admissible?

## 2. Distilled Core Question

A civil plaintiff offers a public inspector's accident report that includes the defendant park employee's statement admitting a missed repair within the employee's job. Is the report admissible over hearsay?

## 3. Final answer choices (A-D, post-shuffle)

- **B:** No, because there is no indication that the attendant is unavailable.
- **C:** Yes, because the attendant's statement was included in an admissible public record.
- **D:** Yes, because the attendant's statement is a past recollection recorded.

## 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam register)

**C is correct.** This is a two-layer hearsay problem. The attendant's quote is one out-of-court statement. The inspector's report is another. **Gold Key:** hearsay inside hearsay can be admitted only when each layer has its own admissibility route. The attendant's statement is offered against the park and concerns a repair within the attendant's employment, so it is an opposing-party employee statement under FRE 801(d)(2)(D). The inspector's official accident report is a public record under FRE 803(8). **Silver Key:** do not stop after spotting hearsay; count the layers and clear them one by one.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A - No, because the attendant's statement is inadmissible hearsay.** This answer has the strongest pull because the quote is an out-of-court statement offered for its truth. The breaker is the second move. **Gold Key:** the employee's work-duty statement is treated as the park's opposing-party statement, and the public report supplies the report layer.

**B - No, because there is no indication that the attendant is unavailable.** This answer borrows a real hearsay concept, but it uses the wrong gate. Public records under FRE 803 and opposing-party statements under FRE 801(d)(2) do not require the declarant to be unavailable.

**D - Yes, because the attendant's statement is a past recollection recorded.** Past recollection recorded sounds plausible because the statement is in a written report. But that doctrine is for a witness who cannot fully recall and uses a record made when the matter was fresh. The train attendant is not being called as the memory-impaired witness, so this is the wrong route.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer (lawyer-confirmation layer)

**Tested rule:** FRE 805 provides that hearsay within hearsay is not excluded if each part of the combined statements conforms with an exception or exclusion. FRE 801(d)(2)(D) excludes from hearsay a statement offered against an opposing party that was made by the party's agent or employee on a matter within the scope of that relationship while it existed. FRE 803(8) excepts qualifying public records, including records of a public office that set out legally required observations or factual findings from a legally authorized investigation in a civil case, unless the opponent shows untrustworthiness.

**Authority:** FRE 805, FRE 801(d)(2)(D), and FRE 803(8). Current rule text is available at https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_805, https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_801, and https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_803.

**Confirmation:** The credited outcome is unchanged from the source: the report is admissible. The legal issue is unchanged: a public official's accident report containing a defendant employee's statement creates multiple hearsay, and each layer has an admissibility route. The dominant trap is unchanged: treating the embedded employee quote as inadmissible hearsay and stopping before the FRE 801(d)(2)(D) and FRE 803(8) analysis. Not on the doctrinal-currency hotlist. Authority floor satisfied by FRE 805, 801(d)(2)(D), and 803(8).
