## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (exam register)

**Credited answer: B — burglary, robbery, and murder.**

Paul is guilty of all three crimes.

**Burglary:** Paul broke and entered the farmhouse — a dwelling — at 11:00 p.m. (nighttime). Martha opened the door to a "delivery" invitation, but Paul pushed in at gunpoint. Common law burglary requires a breaking, defined as any force used to gain entry; pushing through an opened door by threat of deadly force satisfies it. At the moment of entry, Paul intended to commit theft (the cash in the desk drawer). All five common law elements are present.

**Robbery:** After entering, Paul used the gun to compel Martha to open the locked drawer and hand over the cash, then tied her up. The taking of the cash from the victims' presence by force or intimidation is robbery — larceny plus the force element.

**Murder (Felony Murder):** John died of a cardiac arrest while still bound and struggling during the commission of these inherently dangerous felonies. Under the felony murder rule, a death caused during the commission or attempted commission of a violent felony — here burglary and robbery — is murder, regardless of intent to kill. The death is a foreseeable consequence of home-invasion violence, and Paul's felonious conduct set the chain of events in motion.

**Gold Key:** The Gold Key "Burglary Elements" confirms: pushing through an already-opened door by threat of force satisfies the common law "breaking." Without that key, a student might miss the burglary count.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification (Lawyer-Confirmation Layer)

**Burglary (Common Law):** Burglary at common law requires (1) breaking — any use of force to gain entry, including pushing through a door opened by another when the entrant lacks permission; (2) entering; (3) a dwelling house; (4) of another; (5) at nighttime; (6) with the intent to commit a felony therein. *R. v. Culkin* (setting the common law definition). The farmhouse is a dwelling, 11:00 p.m. qualifies as nighttime, and the intent to steal cash is the requisite felony intent. Many state statutes have expanded these elements, but the MBE tests the common law baseline unless a modern code is specified.

**Robbery:** Larceny from the person or presence of another by force or intimidation. *Common law robbery.* The cash was taken from the desk drawer in the victims' presence while Paul held them at gunpoint — force through intimidation.

**Felony Murder:** A killing — even an unintended one — that occurs during the commission or attempted commission of an inherently dangerous felony is murder at common law and in most states. *Common law felony murder.* Burglary and robbery qualify as inherently dangerous felonies. The victim's cardiac arrest from stress during the felony is a foreseeable consequence; proximate causation is satisfied. No intent to kill is required. Some jurisdictions require the death be "in furtherance" of the felony, which is satisfied when the victim dies while bound as a direct result of the felony's restraint.
