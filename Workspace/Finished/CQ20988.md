Police arrest Timothy on suspicion of robbing Lydia's privately owned Scripture print shop, but no complaint, information, or indictment has been filed, and no preliminary hearing or first appearance has occurred. Was Timothy's Sixth Amendment right to counsel violated?

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

C is the best answer. The Sixth Amendment lineup right turns on attachment. Gold Key: the right to counsel at a lineup applies after formal adversary proceedings have begun, such as by formal charge, preliminary hearing, indictment, information, arraignment, or comparable first judicial appearance. Timothy had been arrested, but none of those formal steps had occurred before the lineup. Silver Key: when the call asks Sixth Amendment, check the procedural starting line before checking whether the lineup was fair.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A sells the arrest-equals-attachment shortcut. Arrest is serious, but it is not the same as the formal start of adversary judicial proceedings. The Gold Key kills this trap: the Sixth Amendment right to counsel has not attached merely because the suspect is under arrest.

B sells a broad lineup-counsel rule. Wade protects post-attachment corporeal lineups; it does not say every lineup always requires counsel no matter when it happens. The timing fact is the breaker.

D sells the wrong safety valve. The Sixth Amendment claim fails because the right had not attached, but that does not mean lineups can never be challenged before trial. A suggestive and unreliable precharge identification can still raise due process issues.

### 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule is unchanged. The Sixth Amendment right to counsel is offense-specific and attaches only when formal adversary judicial proceedings begin. Once attached, a corporeal lineup can be a critical stage requiring counsel or waiver. Before attachment, an uncounseled lineup does not violate the Sixth Amendment, although a defendant may still challenge an unnecessarily suggestive and unreliable identification on due process grounds.

Authority floor: United States v. Wade, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/388/218/, recognizes the post-indictment lineup right-to-counsel rule. Kirby v. Illinois, 406 U.S. 682 (1972), https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/406/682/, confirms that the Sixth Amendment counsel right attaches only at or after initiation of adversary judicial proceedings. Rothgery v. Gillespie County, 554 U.S. 191 (2008), https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/554/191/, explains attachment at the first appearance before a judicial officer where the defendant is told of the formal accusation and liberty restrictions are imposed. DOJ Criminal Resource Manual section 239, https://www.justice.gov/archives/usam/criminal-resource-manual-239-power-order-lineup-right-counsel, summarizes that lineup counsel applies at or after formal charge, preliminary hearing, indictment, information, or arraignment. The supplied key matches these authorities.
