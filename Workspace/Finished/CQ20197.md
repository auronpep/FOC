## 6. Full right-answer explanation

**A is correct.** Ruth intentionally asked Daniel to break into the rival shop and steal the mailing list. That request completed the solicitation even though Daniel was an undercover detective and never intended to commit the burglary. **Gold Key:** solicitation turns on the solicitor's intentional request that another person commit a crime; the solicited person's undercover status, refusal, or lack of criminal intent does not defeat the offense. **Silver Key:** when one answer points to the defendant's request and another points to the officer's lack of intent, name the axis as **whose intent matters**.

## 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B.** This is the dominant trap. It makes Daniel's undercover feigned willingness feel like the whole issue because he never meant to commit the burglary. The breaker is the **Gold Key:** solicitation uses Ruth's intentional request, not Daniel's private intent.

**C.** This choice sounds official because warrants belong to police work. The breaker is the **Silver Key:** lock the call before reacting to police facts; the question is Ruth's solicitation, not Daniel's search authority.

**D.** This choice borrows the two-guilty-parties idea from the conspiracy neighborhood. The breaker is the **Gold Key:** solicitation does not require the solicited person to be guilty, willing, or even capable of being convicted.

## 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The tested rule is criminal solicitation. Under the common MBE framing, solicitation is complete when the defendant, with the required purpose or intent, commands, encourages, requests, or otherwise asks another person to commit the target crime. The target crime need not occur, and the solicited person's true willingness or criminal intent is not an element of the defendant's solicitation.

The authority floor is satisfied by two sources. **Model Penal Code § 5.02**, as quoted in *People v. Saephanh*, defines solicitation as commanding, encouraging, or requesting another person to engage in specific conduct that would constitute the crime or an attempt. **18 U.S.C. § 373(c)** states the same tested principle in the federal solicitation context: it is not a defense that the solicited person could not be convicted because the person lacked the required state of mind, was immune, or was not subject to prosecution. The source item's credited outcome is therefore unchanged. Ruth made the request with the criminal purpose; Daniel's undercover status and lack of intent do not defeat the offense.
