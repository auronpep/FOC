B is correct. Naomi should not be found guilty of attempt because attempt requires intent to commit the prohibited act. Gold Key: attempt is intent-sensitive even when the completed regulatory offense might not require specific intent. ### 9. Full Black-Letter Verification And Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Criminal attempt requires intent to commit the target offense plus conduct sufficiently close to commission. At common law, attempt includes a specific intent requirement for the unlawful act. Braxton v. United States, 500 U.S. 344 (1991), states that common-law attempt requires specific intent to commit the unlawful act. Model Penal Code section 5.01 likewise defines attempt around the actor's culpability and the actor's belief about the circumstances, including purposeful conduct that would be criminal if the circumstances were as the actor believed them to be.

Naomi did sell during the prohibited period in actual time, so she might have exposure under the completed ordinance if that offense is strict or negligence-based. But the charge presented is attempt. Because Naomi believed the time was 11:05 P.M., she did not intend to sell during the prohibited window. That missing intent defeats attempted liability. The credited answer remains no guilt for attempt.
