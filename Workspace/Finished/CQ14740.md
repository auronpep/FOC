D. Attempted larceny is tempting because Ruth intended to steal and did not obtain the bookmark. Silver Key: the call asks for the most serious listed crime, so completed burglary outranks any attempted theft label.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. Common-law burglary is breaking and entering the dwelling of another at night with intent to commit a felony inside. Taylor v. United States, 495 U.S. 575 (1990), describes the classic common-law definition as breaking and entering a dwelling at night with intent to commit a felony. See https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-supreme-court/495/575.html. Modern entry authorities also confirm that burglary entry may be partial. People v. Valencia, 28 Cal. 4th 1 (2002), discusses the rule that any partial entry by a body part or controlled instrument can satisfy entry. See https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/28/1.html.

Ruth pushed open the study window, crossed the inner boundary of the dwelling with part of her body, and acted after midnight intending to steal valuable property. That completes burglary. Attempted burglary and attempted larceny are lower or incomplete labels; no-crime conflicts with the completed entry.
