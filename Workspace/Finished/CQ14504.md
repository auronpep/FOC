### 1. Final Question

Naomi and Timothy stood together on a small vineyard plot that Naomi wished to sell. They orally agreed on a price of $12,000 and orally traced the boundaries as "the row of olive trees on the east, the stone wall running north from them, the creek bed on the far side, and the road along the fourth edge."

Which of the following situations best supports the remedy of reformation?

**A.** Timothy reasonably believed the oral agreement included two acres on the far side of the stone wall, but Naomi did not. As later reduced to writing, the deed reflected their original spoken boundaries and did **not** include the two extra acres.

**B.** Timothy reasonably believed the oral agreement required Naomi to remove an old dilapidated press house near the stone wall before closing, but Naomi did not. The written deed said nothing about the press house.

**C.** As later reduced to writing, the deed by clerical mistake described the boundary as running to a fence that lay two acres beyond the stone wall, rather than stopping at the stone wall as both parties had agreed.

**D.** Timothy reasonably thought that the price orally agreed upon was $11,000, but Naomi did not. As later reduced to writing, the deed said $12,000.

### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**Gold Key:** Reformation exists to correct a *defective writing*, not to fix a defective *agreement*. The prerequisite is that both parties actually reached a shared meeting of the minds on a term, and the scribe then wrote it down wrong. If there was no prior meeting of the minds to begin with, reformation has nothing to attach to.

**C** is correct because both Naomi and Timothy orally agreed the boundary was the stone wall. The written deed, by clerical mistake, described a line running to a fence two acres beyond the stone wall. This is the textbook case for reformation: a mutual prior agreement that the writing inadvertently misstates. The court can reform the deed to match what both parties actually agreed to.

Notice the writing *added* land the parties never agreed to include — the very evil reformation is designed to remedy. The Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 155 confirms that reformation is available when a writing fails to express the parties' prior agreement due to a mutual mistake of integration (here, a clerical/transcription error).

### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A (Gold Key callout — the trap this Gold Key defeats):** Timothy alone believed the extra two acres were part of the deal. When only one party holds a belief about a term and the other doesn't, there is no meeting of the minds on that term — and without a prior mutual agreement, reformation has no target. The written deed actually *correctly* omitted the two acres because Naomi never agreed to include them. There is no "mistake in the writing" to fix. This is a unilateral mistake about a term the parties never mutually agreed upon, and reformation is unavailable. **Gold Key:** reformation corrects the writing, not the negotiation.

**B:** This distractor tempts the student who remembers "reformation fixes mistakes in the contract" but forgets the prerequisite of a prior mutual agreement. Timothy alone thought the press house had to go. Naomi never shared that understanding. Since there was no meeting of the minds on the press house removal, the written deed's silence on the point is not a clerical error — it accurately reflects the parties' actual (limited) agreement. No mutual agreement, no reformation. The unilateral-mistake mechanic survives, re-skinned from a shed to a press house, preserving the original 5% distractor signal.

**D:** This is the price variant of the unilateral-mistake trap. Timothy alone thought the price was $11,000. Naomi thought it was $12,000 — and that's what the deed says. This is not a clerical error; it's a unilateral misunderstanding about what the parties were agreeing to. There was no shared meeting of minds on $11,000, so there's nothing for reformation to "correct" the writing to. Reformation cannot supply an agreement the parties never reached. Timothy simply misunderstood what he was agreeing to, and the writing reflects what Naomi actually agreed to — there is no discrepancy between the shared understanding and the writing to remedy.

### 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** Reformation is an equitable remedy available when the parties to a contract have reached a mutual agreement on the terms, but the written instrument, due to a clerical or transcription error (a "mistake of integration"), fails to reflect their shared understanding. The court reforms the writing to match the parties' actual prior agreement.

**Authority:**

- **Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 155** (1981): when a writing that evidences an agreement fails to express the agreement because of both parties' mistake about the contents or effect of the writing, the court may reform the writing to express the agreement, subject to protection for third-party good-faith purchasers.

- **Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 152**: A contract is voidable if both parties made a material mistake about a basic assumption on which the contract was made — but reformation is distinct: it addresses the writing, not the contract itself.

- **Corbin on Contracts § 614**: "Reformation is not a proper remedy for the enforcement of terms to which the defendant never assented."

- **LII / Cornell Law - Wex - Reformation and Mistake**: Reformation requires an antecedent agreement and either mutual mistake or unilateral mistake plus inequitable conduct; mutual mistake generally requires both parties to share the mistaken belief. This item involves the clean mutual-writing-error version.

**Application:** In Choice C, both parties agreed on the stone-wall boundary. The scribe wrote "fence beyond the stone wall" — a clerical mistake in the integration of the agreement into the writing. The writing departs from the actual mutual agreement. Reformation is the precise remedy.

**Contrast with distractors:** Choices A, B, and D all present unilateral misunderstandings with no shared agreement on the term and no inequitable conduct by the other party. Without a meeting of minds on that term, there is no prior agreement for the writing to misstate. Reformation is unavailable.

**Confirmed:** The tested rule (reformation for clerical errors in integrating a mutual agreement), legal issue (distinguishing clerical errors from unilateral misunderstandings), and credited outcome (C = correct, matching original A = correct) are all unchanged from the original.
