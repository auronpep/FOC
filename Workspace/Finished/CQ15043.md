### 6. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. A deed becomes operative when the grantor delivers it with present intent to transfer the interest. Samuel did more than sign papers for a future plan. He physically handed the deed to Ruth and said, "The orchard is yours now." Those words show present transfer intent. His request that Ruth delay recording did not make recording a condition of validity.

Gold Key: delivery with present intent, not recording, is the title-transfer event. Once Samuel delivered the deed to Ruth with present intent, later regret, later silence in the records, and later family pressure did not keep title in Samuel's estate.

Silver Key: freeze the first legal transfer moment. If the deed was validly delivered when handed over, later facts must undo that transfer. Delayed recording and later remorse do not undo it.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because it reaches the right winner and points to a public-record event. The breaker is that recording is notice machinery, not the act that made this deed valid between Samuel and Ruth. Ruth wins because Samuel delivered the deed with present intent, not because she recorded it after he died.

C is attractive because the deed stayed unrecorded while Samuel was alive, and the recording happened only after death. The breaker is the Gold Key: a deed delivered to the grantee with present intent transfers title when delivered. Samuel's death before recording did not strip the deed of effect.

D is the dominant trap. It sells the fairness reaction that Ruth should lose because she lied about burning the deed. The breaker is timing: the deed had already been delivered before that lie. Silver Key: freeze the first legal transfer moment before reacting to later bad facts. Ruth's later dishonesty did not retransfer title to Samuel, make the earlier delivery vanish, or make Timothy the owner.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested issue remains Real Property, Titles, Delivery and acceptance. The verified outline code is 84040203, which appears verbatim in OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md under Titles, Deeds, and Conveyancing > Conveyance by Deed > Delivery and acceptance.

Governing rule: a deed must be delivered to be operative, and delivery turns on the grantor's intent that the deed take effect as a present conveyance. Physical transfer to the grantee plus words of present ownership strongly supports delivery. Recording is not required to transfer title between the parties. A later oral instruction not to record until after death does not defeat an otherwise present delivery.

Primary authority check: Montana Code Annotated 2025 section 70-1-510 states the common rule that delivery to the grantee is necessarily absolute and that the instrument takes effect on delivery, discharged of any condition on which delivery was made. Source: https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0700/chapter_0010/part_0050/section_0100/0700-0010-0050-0100.html

California Civil Code sections 1054 and 1056 provide the same rule structure: a grant vests only upon delivery by the grantor, and a grant cannot be delivered to the grantee conditionally. Section 1058 also confirms that redelivering or canceling a real-property grant does not retransfer title. Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2005/civ/1052-1059.html

The source credited outcome is confirmed. Samuel's deed was in proper form. He handed it to Ruth and used present-transfer words. The delayed-recording request did not postpone title. The later request to return the deed, Ruth's false statement, Samuel's intestacy, and the post-death recording do not defeat the completed delivery. Ruth is the owner.
