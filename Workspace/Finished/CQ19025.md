### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

This sells a burden-of-proof story. A student may see a harsh public accusation and reach for the criminal-law burden as a fairness safeguard. The breaker is that this is not a criminal prosecution and the threshold issue is not whether the board can prove truth beyond a reasonable doubt. **Silver Key:** the first question is whether there is a protected due process interest at all.

**C. Ruth wins because due process regulates false accusations by private and public speakers alike.**

This sells the broad fairness move. A false accusation feels like something law should regulate no matter who speaks. The breaker is that due process limits government deprivation of protected liberty or property interests; it is not a general defamation code, and private speakers are not state actors for due process purposes.

**D. Ruth has a due process liberty claim whenever government speech damages reputation.**

This is the dominant trap. It captures the student's sympathy for a contractor publicly branded as dishonest by the state. The Constitution Annotated describes the doctrine as requiring reputation plus loss of a statutory entitlement or similar right. Source: https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/essay/amdt14-S1-5-2/ALDE_00013748/
