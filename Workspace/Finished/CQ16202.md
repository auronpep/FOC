# A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

## 1. Final question
A grand jury was investigating the theft of a local church’s mission fund. The only information known to the prosecutor was a rumor that Peter might have been involved. The grand jury subpoenaed Peter. He refused to answer questions about the theft and was granted use immunity. He then testified that he and his friend Andrew had taken the funds. The grand jury indicted both Peter and Andrew for the theft. The prosecutor had no evidence as to the identity of the thieves except the testimony of Peter and Andrew.

At Peter’s trial, his objections to Andrew’s being permitted to testify should be:

## 2. Distilled Core Question
Does use immunity prevent the government from using the testimony of an accomplice who was discovered only because of the defendant’s own immunized testimony?

## 3. Final answer choices
A: sustained, because Andrew’s testimony was acquired as a result of Peter’s grand jury testimony.
B: sustained, because the prosecutor may not bargain away the rights of one codefendant in a deal with another.
C: overruled, because a witness cannot be precluded from testifying if his testimony is given voluntarily.
D: overruled, because the authorities suspected Peter even before he testified in the grand jury hearing.

## 6. Full right-answer explanation
(A) is the best response, because it correctly identifies that the accomplice's testimony is inadmissible as "derivative use" of Peter's immunized testimony. When the government grants use and derivative use immunity (the standard "use immunity"), it is prohibited from using the compelled testimony or any evidence derived from that testimony (the "fruits") against the witness in a subsequent criminal prosecution. Here, the facts specify that the prosecutor had no evidence of the accomplice’s identity until Peter testified under the grant of immunity. Thus, Andrew's testimony is a direct fruit of Peter's immunized statement. To use such evidence, the prosecution would bear the heavy burden of proving that Andrew was discovered via a "legitimate source wholly independent of the compelled testimony." Since the prosecutor's only lead was Peter's testimony, that burden cannot be met.

## 7. Full wrong-answer explanations
(B) is incorrect because it misstates the law regarding prosecutorial discretion. While Peter has rights regarding his own testimony, a prosecutor is generally free to make deals with one co-defendant (like Andrew) to testify against another. The reason the objection is sustained here is not a general prohibition on "bargaining away rights," but rather the specific Fifth Amendment bar on using evidence derived from Peter’s immunized statement.

(C) is incorrect because it mischaracterizes the nature of the testimony. While a witness who testifies voluntarily can generally be heard, Peter's testimony here was not voluntary; it was compelled by a subpoena after he initially refused to answer, made possible only by the grant of immunity. Furthermore, the issue is not Andrew's willingness to speak, but the government's use of Andrew as a "fruit" of Peter's compelled words.

(D) is incorrect because mere suspicion does not constitute an "independent source." While the authorities had a "rumor" or suspicion about Peter, they had *no* evidence regarding Andrew's identity or involvement until Peter spoke. For the independent source exception to apply, the government must show they actually possessed or would have inevitably discovered the evidence through a path completely unrelated to the immunized testimony. A rumor is not a source.

## 8. Black-letter-law verification
**Authority:** *Kastigar v. United States*, 406 U.S. 441 (1972). The Supreme Court held that the government may compel testimony by granting "use and derivative use" immunity, which is coextensive with the Fifth Amendment privilege. This immunity "prohibits the prosecutorial authorities from using the compelled testimony in any respect, and it therefore imposes on the prosecution the affirmative duty to prove that the evidence it proposes to use is derived from a legitimate source wholly independent of the compelled testimony." *See also Murphy v. Waterfront Commission*, 378 U.S. 52 (1964).

#### 12. remediation_card
**5th Amendment Immunity**: Use and derivative use immunity prevents the government from using a witness's compelled testimony, or any evidence derived from it, against that witness in a criminal case.

#### 13. Gold Key(s)
**Derivative Use Immunity**: The Fifth Amendment requires that any grant of immunity must be "coextensive" with the privilege it replaces. This means the government cannot use the "fruits" of the statement (derivative use).

#### 14. Silver Key(s)
**Trap Spotting**: Be wary of choices that offer "prior suspicion" as an "independent source." To admit evidence under the independent source exception, the government must prove they *actually* had a source wholly independent of the illegal/protected act.
