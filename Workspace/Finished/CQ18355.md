### 6. Full Right-Answer Explanation

**D is correct.** The lineup identification is out because Esther had already been indicted and police conducted the lineup without notifying her appointed lawyer. But the later courtroom identification is not automatically out. The court must ask whether Naomi can identify Esther from a source independent of the tainted lineup, such as her observations during the robbery.

**Gold Key:** A post-indictment lineup is a critical stage; if it is conducted without counsel, direct testimony about the lineup identification is suppressed, but a later in-court identification may survive on an independent source. **Silver Key:** Separate the two identification events before choosing: lineup testimony and courtroom testimony do not necessarily travel together.

### 7. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. Automatic fruit for both identifications.** This choice is attractive because the lineup violated the right to counsel and the word "fruit" sounds like the normal exclusionary-rule path. The breaker is the automatic sweep. Direct testimony about the lineup identification is excluded, but the courtroom identification may still be admitted if it rests on an independent source. **Gold Key:** The Wade-Gilbert rule creates a split remedy, not a total automatic bar.

**B. Confidence as reliability.** This choice sells the everyday idea that a confident eyewitness must be reliable. The breaker is that confidence does not answer the constitutional problem. The lineup occurred after indictment without counsel, so the lineup identification is inadmissible regardless of the witness's confidence.

**C. Implied waiver by silence.** This choice shifts the burden to Esther and makes silence do too much work. The breaker is waiver. A waiver of the Sixth Amendment right to counsel must be knowing, voluntary, and intelligent; a defendant's failure to request counsel for a lineup she was not told about is not a waiver.

### 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source: a post-indictment lineup is a critical stage of the prosecution. If police conduct it without notice to and presence of counsel, testimony that the witness identified the accused at that lineup is inadmissible. A later in-court identification is treated separately and may be admitted if the prosecution establishes an independent source for the witness's courtroom identification.

Lawyer-confirmation authority: *United States v. Wade*, 388 U.S. 218 (1967), holds that a post-indictment lineup is a critical stage and that in-court identification after an uncounseled lineup must be excluded unless independent origin or harmless error is established. *Gilbert v. California*, 388 U.S. 263 (1967), confirms that in-court identifications require an independent-source determination, while direct testimony that witnesses identified the defendant at the illegal lineup is treated as the direct product of the illegal procedure. The official key is consistent with those authorities.
