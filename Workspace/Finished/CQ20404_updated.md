## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** The cash-load records are admissible because they make Mary’s claim more probable: Stephen said he received only $3,200 for the season, while the records show $18,600 in same-season cash loads. **Gold Key:** relevance is an “any tendency” threshold, not a conclusive-proof requirement. **Silver Key:** in a relevance array, cut answers that demand “conclusive,” “always,” or “only”; the surviving answer states what the evidence tends to show. Stephen may argue gifts or family loans, but that attacks weight, not admissibility.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells the fear that suspicious financial records will inflame the jury. The breaker is the word **always**: it turns a balancing question into an automatic exclusion.

**C.** This choice sells the idea that evidence must close every innocent explanation before the jury may hear it. **Gold Key:** relevant evidence does not need to be conclusive; a cash-load gap can still make the receipt-taking claim more probable.

**D.** This choice sells a half-safe use: “let it in only to contradict Stephen.” **Silver Key:** do not accept an “only” purpose limit when the same fact also tends to prove the claim itself.

## 9. Black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

Federal Rule of Evidence 401 defines relevant evidence by a low threshold: it must have any tendency to make a consequential fact more or less probable. Rule 402 makes relevant evidence admissible unless another rule or law excludes it, and Rule 403 permits exclusion only when probative value is substantially outweighed by listed dangers such as unfair prejudice. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) The Advisory Committee notes also treat relevance as a matter of logical, circumstantial reasoning and reject a stricter probability requirement. ([Legal Information Institute][2])

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. The records do not prove theft by themselves, but they make receipt-taking more probable because they show funds far beyond Stephen’s stated pay during the same season. The alternate explanations are jury arguments.

### review_truth

A clue can be admissible even when it is not a confession; weakness usually goes to weight, not admissibility.
