Hannah's ex-partner threatens to hurt her unless she delivers stolen packages by midnight. He leaves her alone for six hours with her phone and car; a police station is two blocks away. Hannah is afraid but does not call police or leave; she delivers the packages and is charged with theft.

A. Yes, because the threat involved serious harm
B. Yes, because fear alone makes alternatives unreasonable

D. No, because duress applies only to threats made at gunpoint

7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)

Duress requires that the defendant reasonably lacked an opportunity to avoid the threatened harm by lawful means. Here Hannah was left alone for six hours with a phone, a car, and a police station two blocks away; a reasonable person would have had a legal opportunity to seek help or escape before committing the theft. That available alternative defeats the duress defense.

8. Full wrong-answer explanations

A (dominant trap): Serious threats are necessary but not sufficient for duress; the defense also requires no reasonable legal alternative. The presence of a phone, car, and nearby police shows Hannah had an available alternative.

B: Fear alone does not render alternatives unreasonable. The issue is whether a reasonable person had a legal route to safety; fear does not automatically eliminate that requirement.

D: This is a categorical misstatement. Duress does not require the threat be at gunpoint; it requires imminent serious bodily harm or death and no reasonable legal alternative.

9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning (lawyer-confirmation layer)

Authority floor: Model Penal Code §2.09 (Duress) and common-law duress principles — duress requires that the actor reasonably believed the threat would be carried out and that there was no reasonable opportunity to avoid the threatened harm through legal means. Given the facts (hours alone, means to contact police, nearby station), the defense likely fails. No authoritative conflict with the source key identified.

10. Note on preserved rigor/difficulty

The variation preserves the core doctrinal test — imminence and lack of reasonable legal alternatives — and thus preserves difficulty and the intended skill tested.

11. C3 elimination walkthrough — CUT → CLASH → CALL

CUT: D is immediately false; duress is not limited to gunpoint threats.

CLASH: B and A compete by emphasizing fear and severity respectively. Both offer partial true propositions but fail to address the legal alternative requirement. The clash resolves by testing whether a reasonable alternative existed.

CALL: The deciding question is whether Hannah had a reasonable legal alternative. Because she had a phone, a car, and a nearby police station over a six-hour period, the defense is defeated and C is correct.
