## 7. Right-Answer Explanation

**A — the exclusion is likely improper** is correct.

Under FRE 611(a), a court must exercise **reasonable** control over the mode and order of examining witnesses and presenting evidence. The rule identifies three legitimate purposes: making procedures effective for truth-finding, avoiding needless consumption of time, and protecting witnesses. Personal convenience — having theater tickets — is none of these. When the judge's stated reason is purely personal, no case-management rationale exists, the excluded witness is the **only** source of evidence on a material issue, and a short accommodation is available, the exclusion is arbitrary and likely constitutes an abuse of discretion.

**Gold Key:** FRE 611(a) grants the judge broad but not unlimited discretion. The word "reasonable" in the rule is the operative limit. Arbitrary exclusion of the only evidence on a material issue for personal scheduling reasons — when a ten-minute accommodation exists — falls outside the rule's permissible scope.

**Silver Key:** When the judge's stated reason contains "solely" or "only" tied to a purely personal motive, lock the call on "likely improper" — the absence of any case-management rationale combined with an available short accommodation is the factual signature of arbitrary action under FRE 611(a).

## 8. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**B — automatically proper because the trial is to the judge rather than a jury.**

The pull: in a bench trial, the judge is the finder of fact, and students sometimes reason that when the judge is wearing both hats (adjudicator and gatekeeper), self-regulation is less constrained — the judge can exclude evidence she would never personally credit anyway.

The breaker: FRE 611(a)'s reasonableness standard applies in bench and jury trials alike. The distinction between bench and jury trials addresses which entity finds the facts, not whether parties retain the right to present material evidence. A party's opportunity to prove its case on every contested element is not waived merely because the judge is the trier. **Gold Key:** FRE 611(a)'s "reasonable control" requirement does not disappear in a bench trial — the bench/jury distinction is irrelevant to this analysis.

**C — proper unless Barnabas proves Lydia's testimony would be uncontested.**

The pull: "uncontested" sounds like it means the testimony is undisputed and therefore less necessary — a student may reason that if the opponent doesn't contest damages, exclusion of the damages witness does no harm.

The breaker: this choice inverts the rule's logic entirely. Evidence on a genuinely contested material issue is **more** protected under FRE 611(a), not less — excluding the only proof on a disputed element is exactly the kind of deprivation the abuse-of-discretion standard guards against. And no rule places the burden on the offering party to prove the testimony would be uncontested before the exclusion can be challenged. **Gold Key:** No burden-shifting rule of this kind exists; contested, material evidence is a reason to allow testimony, not a condition of its admissibility.

**D — automatically proper because the judge has unlimited control over the trial schedule.** ← *Dominant trap*

The pull: judges unquestionably control their courtrooms and schedules. Students who know that FRE 611(a) is the "mode and order" rule may still reason that scheduling authority is separate — an inherent, boundless judicial prerogative untethered to the Rules of Evidence.

The breaker: FRE 611(a) says "reasonable control." Unlimited is not reasonable. The rule grants the judge broad authority, but breadth is not infinity. Arbitrary exclusion of the only evidence on a material issue for purely personal reasons, when a ten-minute accommodation would resolve the conflict, is the paradigm case of discretion exercised unreasonably. "Automatic" propriety is unavailable when the rule's built-in limit has been exceeded. **Gold Key fires directly:** the word "unlimited" in this choice is the tiered_absolute error — the rule says reasonable, not boundless.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** FRE 611(a): "The court shall exercise reasonable control over the mode and order of examining witnesses and presenting evidence so as to: (1) make those procedures effective for determining the truth; (2) avoid needless consumption of time; and (3) protect witnesses from harassment or undue embarrassment."

**Key word:** "reasonable." The rule does not say unlimited. Courts reviewing FRE 611(a) rulings apply an abuse-of-discretion standard. When a trial court excludes the only evidence on a contested material issue for reasons unrelated to the rule's three legitimate purposes, and a brief accommodation would eliminate any scheduling burden, appellate courts have found abuse of discretion.

**Application:** The judge's stated reason — theater tickets, personal convenience — does not fall within any of the three FRE 611(a) purposes. Lydia's testimony is the **only** evidence of damages. Barnabas proposes a ten-minute slot the following court day. Nothing in the record suggests a case-management justification. The exclusion is arbitrary and likely an abuse of the court's FRE 611(a) authority.

**On choice B (bench trial):** FRE 611(a) applies in both jury and bench trials. The bench/jury distinction governs who finds the facts, not whether a party may present its case on each element. No authority supports importing a lesser-scrutiny standard for evidentiary rulings in bench trials.

**On choice C (unless uncontested):** No such rule exists. The opposite is true: important, contested evidence is more deserving of admission. Exclusion of a party's only evidence on a disputed element is precisely the harm FRE 611(a) discretion must guard against.

**On choice D (unlimited):** Courts are clear that FRE 611(a) discretion has limits. See, e.g., *United States v. Reaves*, 636 F. Supp. 1575, 1580 (E.D. Ky. 1986) (discussing FRE 611(a)'s reasonableness requirement); Advisory Committee Note to FRE 611 (discretion must be exercised to "make the interrogation and presentation effective for the ascertainment of the truth"). No unlimited scheduling authority exists independent of this standard.

#### question_data

Barnabas is the plaintiff in a federal copyright licensing dispute. In a federal bench trial, he has one remaining witness, Lydia, a forensic accountant whose testimony will constitute the only evidence of his lost royalty damages. At 4:55 p.m., the judge announces: "Court is recessing for the day, and no testimony will be received tomorrow morning. I am excluding Lydia solely because I have theater tickets tonight and do not wish to run late." Barnabas objects and represents to the court that Lydia can complete her direct examination in ten minutes on the next available court day.

Which of the following statements is most accurate?

A. The exclusion is likely improper because reasonable control over the presentation of evidence does not permit the arbitrary exclusion of the only witness on a material issue solely for personal convenience.
B. The exclusion is automatically proper because the trial is to the judge rather than a jury.
C. The exclusion is proper unless Barnabas proves that Lydia's testimony would be uncontested.
D. The exclusion is automatically proper because the judge has unlimited control over the trial schedule.

#### choice_by_choice_walkthrough

---

**Choice A — likely improper / reasonable control** ← *CORRECT*

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:**
The judge's reason is purely personal — theater tickets, no case-management rationale. Lydia is the ONLY damages evidence. A ten-minute accommodation is offered and refused. FRE 611(a) says "reasonable control." Nothing about this exclusion is reasonable in the rule's sense.

**Gold Key:** FRE 611(a) gives broad but not unlimited discretion. The word is "reasonable." Personal convenience ≠ reasonable exercise of that control when the party's only evidence on a material issue is at stake and a brief accommodation is available.

**(2) Student label:** CORRECT

**(3) What a true/responsive version looks like:**
A correctly identifies the governing standard (reasonable control, not unlimited), the fact pattern that trips it (arbitrary exclusion, personal reason, no accommodation), and the proper qualifier ("likely" improper — abuse of discretion review, not automatic reversal).

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:**
FRE 611(a) — "reasonable control" over mode and order. Three legitimate purposes: truth-finding, avoiding needless time consumption, protecting witnesses from harassment. Personal scheduling convenience satisfies none. Arbitrary exclusion of the sole evidence on a contested material issue when a brief accommodation exists is the paradigm abuse. LaFave on Federal Evidence Practice; Advisory Committee Notes to FRE 611.

---

**Choice B — automatically proper because bench trial**

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:**
The bench-vs.-jury distinction governs who decides the facts. It has nothing to do with whether a party may present evidence on contested elements of its case. FRE 611(a) applies in both trial formats. This choice invokes a legally non-existent doctrine.

**(2) Student label:** CUT — bench/jury distinction irrelevant to FRE 611(a) analysis

**(3) What a true/responsive version would look like:**
To be true, the choice would need a rule saying bench trials impose lesser evidentiary protections — no such rule exists. The bench trial structure could be relevant if the question asked about hearsay admissibility in bench trials (where judges sometimes admit hearsay for context) — but FRE 611(a)'s reasonableness floor is not relaxed in a bench trial.

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:**
EAR_FALSITY / flat_misstatement. FRE 611(a) contains no bench-trial exception. The judge's dual role as gatekeeper and factfinder does not convert the duty to exercise reasonable evidentiary control into something broader. No authority supports this proposition.

---

**Choice C — proper unless Barnabas proves uncontested**

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:**
This choice places a burden on Barnabas to prove his own evidence is uncontested before he can challenge its exclusion. That's backwards — contested evidence on a material issue is exactly what FRE 611(a) protects from arbitrary exclusion. And no such burden-shifting rule exists.

**Gold Key:** No rule of this kind exists under FRE 611(a). The protection against arbitrary exclusion applies most forcefully to contested, material evidence — proving the testimony is uncontested would actually weaken the case for admission, not create it.

**(2) Student label:** CLASH loser — inverts the protection for contested material evidence

**(3) What a true/responsive version would look like:**
For C to be true, there would need to be a rule saying exclusion of disputed evidence is more defensible than exclusion of undisputed evidence — the opposite of how evidence law works. Uncontested evidence on a material element might be stipulated away; contested evidence cannot be.

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:**
EAR_FALSITY / backwards. No burden-shifting rule of this kind exists under FRE 611(a). Heavily contested damages testimony is more deserving of admission than uncontested evidence — exclusion causes more harm when the stakes of the testimony are real. The inverted logic is fatal.

---

**Choice D — automatically proper because unlimited control** ← *DOMINANT TRAP*

**(1) Student-accessible C3 signal:**
The first word to check in FRE 611(a) is "reasonable." The rule says "reasonable control." Not "unlimited control." Not "automatic" authority. "Unlimited" is the tiered_absolute error — it pushes a real, broad authority past the rule's own stated limit.

**Gold Key fires directly:** FRE 611(a) says "reasonable control." This choice says "unlimited." Those two words are mutually exclusive. The rule's own text defeats this choice — no need to go further.

**Silver Key:** When a choice says "automatically proper" because of "unlimited" authority, the signal is always a tiered_absolute trap. Look for the rule's built-in limit. Here: "reasonable." That one word ends this choice.

**(2) Student label:** DOMINANT TRAP — tiered_absolute: "unlimited" exceeds FRE 611(a)'s actual scope

**(3) What a true/responsive version would look like:**
To be true, this choice would need FRE 611(a) to say "unlimited" or "absolute" control — or for courts to have held that scheduling authority is exempt from abuse-of-discretion review. Neither is the case. Broad authority is not unlimited authority.

**(4) Lawyer confirmation:**
EAR_OVERCLAIM / tiered_absolute. FRE 611(a) expressly limits the judge's authority to "reasonable" control aimed at three specified purposes. Courts review FRE 611(a) rulings under an abuse-of-discretion standard, which by definition assumes the discretion has limits. "Unlimited" directly contradicts the rule's text and the appellate review structure.

#### wrong_answer_recovery_paths

**Chose B (bench trial automatically proper):**
You may have reasoned that because the judge is the factfinder in a bench trial, the judge has broader evidentiary authority over her own case. But FRE 611(a)'s reasonableness standard is not relaxed in bench trials — the bench/jury distinction governs factfinding, not the right to present material evidence. Practice: when a choice turns on trial format as the reason for exclusion, check whether any rule actually supports that distinction. Here, none does.

**Chose C (proper unless uncontested):**
You may have associated "important" testimony with testimony that is disputed, and reasoned that if Barnabas can't prove it's disputed, the exclusion is fine. But the logic runs opposite: if the testimony IS contested and is the ONLY evidence on a material issue, exclusion causes maximum harm. No rule places a burden on the offering party to prove contentiousness before challenging an arbitrary exclusion. Practice: when a choice imposes an unusual burden on a party, ask where that burden comes from. If there's no rule behind it, the choice is backwards.

**Chose D (unlimited control — dominant trap):**
You knew judges have broad scheduling authority and concluded that authority is effectively unlimited. The fix is simple: read FRE 611(a) for the word "reasonable." That word exists precisely to cap the authority. "Broad" and "unlimited" are not synonyms in the law. Practice: every time a choice says "unlimited," "absolute," or "automatically proper" — check whether the governing rule contains its own limiting word. FRE 611(a) does: "reasonable."
