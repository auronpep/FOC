### 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation

B is correct. The live gate is not Ruth's acceptance, Ruth's recording, or Naomi's spoken approval. The live gate is whether Timothy had written authority to sign the deed for Naomi, or whether Naomi later signed a written adoption or ratification. Gold Key: when a deed is signed by an agent for a landowner, oral authority and oral ratification do not satisfy the writing gate. ### 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

A is attractive because recording feels like the act that makes a deed official. The breaker is that recording is priority and notice machinery; it does not create authority that was missing when the deed was signed.

C is the dominant trap because ratification is a real legal idea, and Naomi did say she stood behind Timothy's signature. Gold Key: for this deed-authority problem, ratification must satisfy the same writing gate that original authority would have needed. Naomi's oral approval does not transfer title.

D is attractive because reliance sounds fair after Ruth accepted and recorded the deed. The breaker is that the choice invents a reliance exception to the deed-authority writing requirement. The stem gives no signed writing by Naomi and no fact that replaces that writing gate.

### 9. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification And Legal Reasoning For The Right Answer

The tested rule, issue, and credited outcome are unchanged. A deed conveying real property must be executed by the owner or by an agent whose authority satisfies the required writing/formality rule. Under the equal-dignity principle, if the law requires written authority for an agent to bind the principal in a land conveyance, oral authorization is not enough. A later ratification must satisfy the same formality when it is being used to validate the agent's unauthorized deed execution. Recording a deed gives notice and can affect priority among competing claimants, but it does not cure an invalid execution caused by missing authority.

Lawyer-confirmation authorities: Restatement (Third) of Agency section 3.02 states the formal-requirements rule for agent authority when the law requires a signed writing or record; representative statutes and cases reflect the same equal-dignity result for land transactions, including Ga. Code section 10-6-2 and its annotations on written authority and oral ratification, Ariz. Rev. Stat. section 33-401(A), and Estate of Stephens, 28 Cal. 4th 665 (2002). Research did not contradict the source key.
