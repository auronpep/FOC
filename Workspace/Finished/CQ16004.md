## 7. Right-Answer Explanation (post-shuffle A)

**Gold Key:** A taking is not trespassory when the property's actual owner authorizes or consents to the taking — even if the taker does not know the authorizer is the owner. ## 9. Black-Letter Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Larceny — trespassory element:**
Common-law larceny requires: (1) a trespassory (2) taking and (3) carrying away (4) of personal property (5) of another (6) with specific intent to permanently deprive. The trespassory element demands that the taking violate the owner's possessory rights. Where the owner consents to or authorizes the taking, the taking is not trespassory and larceny fails. *See* Wayne R. LaFave, *Substantive Criminal Law* § 19.2(b) (3d ed.) (owner's consent to taking negates the trespassory element). MPC § 223.2 similarly requires the taking be "unlawful" — an authorized taking is not unlawful. Here, Mary owns the bag and directed Timothy to take it → authorized taking → not trespassory → no larceny. Timothy's subjective belief that he was stealing from a stranger is irrelevant to the objective owner-consent question.

**Conspiracy — bilateral specific intent:**
Common-law conspiracy requires: (1) an agreement between two or more persons (2) with specific intent to commit a crime (3) to do so. The majority of modern jurisdictions (and MPC § 5.03) additionally require an overt act in furtherance. Critically, every party to the agreement must harbor specific intent to commit the targeted crime. If one "co-conspirator" knows the planned act is not criminal — here, because they own the property and have consented to its taking — that party lacks the requisite specific criminal intent and no valid conspiracy forms. *See* LaFave, *Substantive Criminal Law* § 12.2(b). Mary knew the bag was hers; she could not intend to steal what she already owned and had authorized Timothy to take. No bilateral intent → no conspiracy.

**Verification:** The tested rule (trespassory element of larceny; bilateral specific intent for conspiracy), the legal issue (whether either crime is established on these facts), and the credited outcome (neither) are unchanged from source row 16004.
