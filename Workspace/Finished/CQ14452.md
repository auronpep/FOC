On April 3, Galilee Lampworks, a commercial maker of decorative olive-oil lamps, received a purchase-order form from Lydia's private Christian gift shop. The form ordered 360 hand-glazed olive-oil lamps for delivery no later than July 12 at a delivered total price of $18,600, as quoted in Galilee's current catalog. ### 6. Full right-answer explanation
B is correct. Galilee's form was a definite acceptance: it said the order was accepted and repeated Lydia's quantity, price, and delivery terms. The added liability-cap clause does not prevent contract formation under the UCC battle-of-the-forms rule. Between merchants, an added term can enter the contract unless an exception applies. A clause capping the seller's liability for any breach at $250 materially changes an $18,600 sale, so it does not become part of the contract without Lydia's express agreement.

**Gold Key:** under UCC Section 2-207, a definite acceptance with added boilerplate can still form a contract; the separate question is whether the added term joins the deal. **Silver Key:** split the answer array into two questions: contract formed, then term included. Material alteration answers the second question, not the first.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations
A is attractive because Lydia never consented to Galilee's $250 cap, and consent feels like a formation requirement. The breaker is that lack of express consent keeps the added cap out; it does not erase the matching acceptance. The Gold Key separates contract formation from term inclusion.

C is the dominant trap. It sells a smart-sounding UCC shortcut: if the Code permits agreed damages limits, the printed liability cap should ride into the merchant contract. The breaker is the Gold Key. UCC permission for remedy or damages terms does not skip UCC Section 2-207; a material added term in an acceptance does not become part of the contract without express agreement.

D is attractive because it correctly spots that the $250 cap is a material alteration. The breaker is the Silver Key. Material alteration knocks out the added clause, not the whole contract, when the response is otherwise a definite acceptance.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Contracts, Formation of Contracts, Acceptance, and the verified outline code is `55020102`. The source row's official key is confirmed by UCC Section 2-207.

UCC Section 2-207(1) provides that a definite and seasonable expression of acceptance can operate as an acceptance even though it states additional or different terms, unless the acceptance is expressly made conditional on assent to those terms. UCC Section 2-207(2) provides that additional terms are proposals for addition to the contract and, between merchants, become part of the contract unless the offer limits acceptance to its terms, the terms materially alter the contract, or timely objection occurs. Source: Cornell LII, Uniform Commercial Code Section 2-207, https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-207.

The UCC comments confirm that materially altering terms do not enter unless expressly agreed to, and that material alteration turns on surprise or hardship if incorporated without express awareness. Source: D.C. Law Library, D.C. Code Section 28:2-207 Uniform Commercial Code Comment, https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/28%3A2-207.

UCC Sections 2-718 and 2-719 recognize agreed liquidated-damages and remedy-limitation terms, but those sections do not make an unagreed material term part of a contract when Section 2-207 excludes it. Sources: Cornell LII, Uniform Commercial Code Sections 2-718 and 2-719, https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-718 and https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-719.

Here, Galilee's form was not expressly conditional on Lydia's assent to the liability cap; it affirmatively accepted the order and matched the central deal terms. The liability cap is a material additional term because it would sharply reduce Galilee's exposure for any breach of an $18,600 goods contract. The enforceable contract therefore exists, but the cap is excluded. No researched authority contradicts the supplied key. The transform preserves the tested rule, legal issue, credited outcome, and answer-array mechanics.
