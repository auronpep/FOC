## 7. Full Right-Answer Explanation (Exam Register)

**C is correct** (was D pre-shuffle). Under the UCC, a usage of trade is a practice or method of dealing having such regularity of observance in a place, vocation, or trade as to justify an expectation that it will be observed with respect to the transaction in question. UCC § 1-303(c). Under UCC § 2-202, trade usage may explain or supplement a written contract term even if the writing appears complete, so long as the usage can be reasonably construed as consistent with the express terms. UCC § 1-303(d), (e).

Here, the contract specifies delivery "on or before October 15," but the trade usage allows a 25-day leeway. Paul tendered on November 8 — 23 days late. Construed in light of the usage, Paul's tender was within the permitted window, so Timothy's rejection was a breach. The fact that the usage is typically negated by express language if a strict deadline is intended reinforces that the usage reflects the parties' likely intent.

**Gold Key:** Under the UCC, trade usage and course of dealing can supplement or explain a written term even in a fully integrated contract — they are admissible not to *contradict* the writing but to *explain or supplement* it, and they are enforceable whenever reasonably consistent with the express terms. UCC § 2-202(a); § 1-303(d)–(e).

## 8. Full Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A is incorrect.** This is the dominant trap. The choice appeals to commercial common sense — if the buyer wasn't harmed, why should rejection be allowed? But under the UCC perfect tender rule (§ 2-601), a buyer may reject goods that fail in any respect to conform to the contract. Materiality of the delay is irrelevant; even an immaterial delay in delivery constitutes non-conformity entitling the buyer to reject. The "no material harm" fact does nothing to help Paul recover.

**B is incorrect.** Paul's subjective belief that Timothy would accept late tender cannot override the contract's objective terms. Contract interpretation under the UCC focuses on the agreement's express terms, course of dealing, course of performance, and trade usage — not on one party's unilateral assumptions about the other's willingness to be flexible. Paul's private belief is legally irrelevant.

**C is incorrect.** A lumber mill workers' strike does not establish impracticability under UCC § 2-615. Impracticability requires that the non-occurrence of the event (here, a strike) was a "basic assumption on which the contract was made." The mere possibility of labor disruption in the lumber industry is a foreseeable commercial risk, not a supervening event that destroys a basic assumption of the contract. Paul assumed the risk of ordinary supply-chain disruptions.

## 9. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

**Tested rule:** UCC contract interpretation — trade usage as a supplement to express contract terms.

**Governing authority:**

- **UCC § 1-303(c):** Defines "usage of trade" as any practice or method of dealing having such regularity of observance in a place, vocation, or trade as to justify an expectation that it will be observed with respect to the transaction in question.
- **UCC § 1-303(d):** Provides that usage of trade is relevant in ascertaining the meaning of the parties' agreement, may give particular meaning to specific terms, and may supplement or qualify the terms.
- **UCC § 1-303(e):** Express terms and applicable usage of trade must be construed whenever reasonable as consistent with each other. If unreasonable to construe them as consistent, express terms prevail.
- **UCC § 2-202:** Terms set forth in a writing intended as a final expression of the agreement may not be contradicted by prior or contemporaneous oral agreements but may be explained or supplemented by usage of trade (among other things).
- **UCC § 2-601 (Perfect Tender Rule):** The buyer may reject goods that fail in any respect to conform to the contract.

**Affirmative reasoning:** The trade usage here — a 25-day leeway on delivery dates — is a regularly observed practice in the custom furniture trade. It supplements the express delivery term. Because the usage can be reasonably construed as consistent with the "on or before October 15" language (it doesn't contradict it; it explains what "on or before" means in this trade context), the usage is enforceable. Paul's November 8 tender falls within the 25-day window, so it was conforming, and Timothy's rejection was wrongful.

**Confirmed:** The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the original. The official key (trade usage controls) is correct.
