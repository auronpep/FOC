### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**A is correct.** The transcript is a two-layer evidence item. The first layer is Peter’s earlier testimony, offered for its truth. **Gold Key:** in a transcript problem, each hearsay layer needs its own path. Peter’s statement concerns his work for Bethany Banners and was made while he was still employed, so it can be used against Bethany Banners as an employee/opposing-party statement. The second layer is the court transcript itself. A court transcript records what was said in court as part of an official court function, so the transcript layer fits the public-record path.

**Silver Key:** split the answer into two layers before you pick an exception name. One choice must account for both the speaker’s statement and the transcript that carries it. A is the only answer that does that.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**B.** The recorded-recollection label is attractive because the evidence is a “record” and the speaker is unavailable. The breaker is that this is not Peter’s memory record being used because he cannot recall; the transcript is a court record of sworn testimony, so the recorded-recollection route is the wrong exception.

**C.** This choice catches the first danger: the transcript and the earlier testimony are out-of-court layers. The breaker is that “hearsay” is only the start of the analysis; **Gold Key:** a combined hearsay item is admissible if each layer has its own admissibility path.

**D.** Former testimony is attractive because Peter testified at an earlier trial and is now unavailable. The breaker is that **Gold Key:** former testimony needs the party now opposing the evidence, or a civil predecessor, to have had an opportunity and similar motive to develop that testimony; Bethany Banners was not a party to Peter’s criminal trial.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and right-answer legal reasoning

The governing doctrine is unchanged from the original. FRE 805 states that hearsay within hearsay is not excluded if each part conforms to an exception or exclusion. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) FRE 801(d)(2)(D) treats a statement offered against an opposing party as nonhearsay when made by the party’s agent or employee on a matter within the scope of that relationship while it existed. ([Legal Information Institute][2]) FRE 803(8) covers qualifying public records, including records of a public office that set out office activities or matters observed under a legal duty to report, absent a trustworthiness showing by the opponent. ([Legal Information Institute][3])

The former-testimony distractor remains wrong because FRE 804(b)(1) requires that the testimony now be offered against a party, or in a civil case a predecessor in interest, who had an opportunity and similar motive to develop the testimony by direct, cross-, or redirect examination. ([Legal Information Institute][4]) The recorded-recollection distractor remains wrong because FRE 803(5) requires a record on a matter the witness once knew about, made or adopted when fresh, and accurately reflecting the witness’s knowledge; if admitted, it is read into evidence and received as an exhibit only if offered by an adverse party. ([Legal Information Institute][3])
