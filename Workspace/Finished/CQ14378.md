### 1. Final question

Barnabas sells custom prayer journals to Lydia, who runs a private Christian bookstore. During the last five years, they have made hundreds of separate journal-supply contracts. Each time Barnabas delivered a shipment, Lydia signed an invoice listing the agreed price for that shipment. The invoices never mentioned any discount for early payment.

In the devotional-goods wholesale trade, the regular custom is to allow a 2% discount from the invoice price if payment is made within 10 days after delivery. In every prior transaction between Barnabas and Lydia, Lydia paid 15 days after delivery and deducted 5% from the invoice price. Barnabas accepted those payments without objection.

Barnabas and Lydia recently entered into a contract for one shipment of leather-bound study journals at a price of $84,000. They used the same delivery procedure and the same invoice form as before. Barnabas delivered the journals, and Lydia signed the invoice. Three days after delivery, Lydia received this note from Barnabas: "Payment in full in accordance with the signed invoice is due immediately. No discounts permitted. s/Barnabas."

Which of the following statements concerning these facts is most accurate?

### 6. Full right-answer explanation

C is correct. This is a sale of goods. The signed invoice was silent about any discount. Under the UCC, a final written expression may still be explained or supplemented by the parties' course of dealing, usage of trade, or course of performance. Here, Barnabas and Lydia's own repeated pattern was stronger than the general trade custom: Lydia had always paid 15 days after delivery and deducted 5%, and Barnabas had accepted that pattern without objection. When course of dealing and trade usage conflict, course of dealing controls. Lydia is therefore entitled to the 5% discount if she pays within 15 days.

Gold Key: Under UCC Sections 2-202 and 1-303, course of dealing and trade usage can explain or supplement a final goods writing, and a conflicting course of dealing prevails over trade usage.

Silver Key: When the invoice is silent and two outside sources point different ways, rank the sources by closeness to these parties. Their own repeated course of dealing beats the broader trade custom.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

A is attractive because Barnabas's note uses command language and appears to end the prior pattern. The breaker is the lane: this question is about the meaning of a silent invoice term, not a waiver of an inconsistent express term. A unilateral note after delivery does not turn the parties' repeated course of dealing into nothing. The Gold Key keeps the focus on UCC interpretation: course of dealing is a source for the agreement's meaning.

B sounds protective of the signed invoice and uses the right doctrine label, but it leaves out the UCC exception that decides the question. The parol evidence rule blocks contradiction of a final writing by prior or contemporaneous agreements; it does not bar course-of-dealing or usage evidence from explaining or supplementing a silent goods invoice. Silver Key: the invoice's silence matters. There is no discount term to contradict, so the outside commercial sources can fill the gap.

D is the dominant trap. It sells a real UCC source: trade usage can explain or supplement a goods contract. The breaker is priority. Once the parties' own repeated course of dealing conflicts with the broader trade custom, the course of dealing is the closer and controlling source.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The verified outline code is `52040300`, listed in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md` as Contracts, Contract Content and Meaning, Gap-Fillers, Interpretation, and the Parol Evidence Rule > Parol Evidence Rule.

The governing authority is UCC Section 2-202 and UCC Section 1-303. Section 2-202 permits a final written expression in a goods contract to be explained or supplemented by course of dealing, usage of trade, and course of performance. Section 1-303 defines course of dealing and usage of trade, makes them relevant to the meaning of the agreement, and provides the hierarchy when they cannot reasonably be read consistently: express terms prevail first, course of performance next, course of dealing next, and usage of trade last. Sources checked: https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-202 and https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/1/1-303
