## 6. Right-Answer Explanation

**B is correct.** Esther took the vineyard **subject to** the mortgage but did not assume the mortgage debt. That means the mortgage lien still burdens the vineyard, and City Bank may protect its security interest, but Esther did not become personally liable for Daniel's debt. Her voluntary payments did not convert the transfer into an assumption.

Gold Key: **GK-RP-MORTGAGE-SUBJECT-TO-01** - Taking property "subject to" a mortgage keeps the lien on the land; only an assumption makes the transferee personally liable for the debt.

Silver Key: **SK-RP-MORTGAGE-LIABILITY-AXIS-01** - In a mortgage-transfer question, separate the property lien from personal liability before reacting to words like due-on-sale or privity.

## 7. Wrong-Answer Explanations

**A. City Bank, because Daniel's transfer violated the due-on-sale clause.** This choice sells the strongest "violation means lender wins" instinct. The due-on-sale clause may allow City Bank to accelerate the loan against Daniel, but it does not make Esther personally liable when she did not assume the debt.

**C. Esther, because she is not in privity of estate with City Bank.** This choice lands on the right party but gives the wrong reason. The issue is not privity of estate; the controlling fact is Esther's express non-assumption of Daniel's mortgage debt.

**D. City Bank, because Esther is in privity of estate with City Bank.** This is the dominant trap. It uses mortgage vocabulary to make personal liability sound automatic, but privity of estate is not the mechanism that makes a transferee personally liable on a mortgage debt. Gold Key: **GK-RP-MORTGAGE-SUBJECT-TO-01** kills this trap because personal liability requires assumption, not merely taking title subject to the mortgage.

## 8. Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

Governing rule: When a mortgagor transfers property subject to an existing mortgage and the transferee does not assume the debt, the transferee takes the property encumbered by the mortgage but does not become personally liable for the mortgage debt. The original mortgagor remains personally liable.

Authority floor: Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages secs. 5.1-5.2 addresses transfers with and without assumption of mortgage liability. The due-on-sale clause layer is also consistent with 12 U.S.C. Sec. 1701j-3, which permits enforcement of due-on-sale clauses but does not itself turn a nonassuming transferee into a personally liable borrower. Teaching sources checked during QA state the same exam rule: a transferee who does not assume takes "subject to" the mortgage and has no personal liability on the debt.

Legal reasoning for the credited answer:

- Daniel was the borrower-mortgagor.
- Esther received the vineyard by deed subject to City Bank's mortgage.
- Daniel and Esther expressly agreed that Esther would not assume or agree to pay Daniel's debt.
- City Bank accepted several voluntary payments from Esther, but acceptance of those payments is not an assumption agreement.
- The due-on-sale clause may give City Bank a remedy against Daniel or the property, but it does not create personal liability against Esther.
- Judgment in this action for delinquent payments should be for Esther.
