## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question

Naomi, who raised lavender on her private farm for Scripture-themed sachets, signed a writing with Barnabas agreeing to sell him "all lavender stems from the psalm rows this summer." The writing did not state a cutting or pickup date. Barnabas argues that Article 2 rules cannot apply because the lavender was still rooted and uncut when the writing was signed.

Which answer is best?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A writing sells all identified growing crops from a named area for the season, but omits the pickup date. Can Article 2 apply before the crops are cut?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. Barnabas is correct because an omitted cutting or pickup date makes the agreement governed by common law.

B. Barnabas is correct because growing plants become goods only after they are cut.

C. Barnabas is wrong because the agreement can cover future goods, including identified growing crops to be cut and sold.

D. Barnabas is wrong only if the lavender had already been cut and tendered to him.

### 4. Correct answer

### 6. Full right-answer explanation

C is the best answer. **Gold Key:** Article 2 can govern identified growing crops and future goods before harvest or delivery; harvest is not the entry ticket. Naomi and Barnabas identified the crop by kind, rows, and season: all lavender stems from the psalm rows this summer. That is enough to keep the deal in the Article 2 lane.

**Silver Key:** Run the call in order: first decide whether the deal is in Article 2, then treat the missing pickup date as an open term instead of a source-law switch. The omitted cutting or pickup date may need a reasonable-time gap filler, but it does not turn a crop sale into a common-law contract.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested issue remains Article 2 source law for future goods / identified growing crops, with an omitted pickup date as a non-source-law timing gap. The transformed answer preserves the original rule, issue, and credited outcome: the buyer is wrong to argue that Article 2 cannot apply merely because the crops were not harvested.

UCC section 2-105 defines goods to include growing crops and also recognizes future goods; a purported present sale of future goods operates as a contract to sell. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-105

UCC section 2-107(2) confirms that a contract for the sale apart from the land of growing crops, capable of severance without material harm, is a contract for the sale of goods within Article 2 even before severance. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-107

The missing cutting or pickup date does not defeat Article 2 treatment. UCC section 2-204(3) allows a sale-of-goods contract with one or more open terms if the parties intended a contract and there is a reasonably certain basis for a remedy; UCC section 2-309(1) supplies a reasonable time when shipment, delivery, or another action is not agreed. Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-204 and https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-309

**GK-CONTRACTS-OPEN-TIME-01:** A missing delivery or pickup time does not move a goods contract to common law; Article 2 can supply a reasonable time when the parties intended a contract.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-CONTRACTS-SOURCE-THEN-GAP-01:** Run the call in order: first decide whether the deal is in Article 2, then treat the missing pickup date as an open term instead of a source-law switch.
