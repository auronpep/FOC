### 6. Full right-answer explanation

B is correct. Barnabas is not offering the phone recording or trying to prove the exact words on it. He is testifying from his own hearing of the caller's voice and explaining why the voice sounded like Mary's. His inability to be positive does not keep the testimony out; it gives Mary a point for cross-examination and argument about weight. Gold Key: voice identification can be made by opinion based on hearing the voice under circumstances connecting it with the alleged speaker. Gold Key: the original-recording rule applies when a party is proving a recording's contents, not when a witness testifies from personal perception about who was speaking. Silver Key: separate identity from content before choosing between the voice-identification answer and the recording answer.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

A. The recording answer is attractive because the stem tells you a recording exists and was not brought to court. The breaker is the offered purpose: Barnabas is not proving the recording's words or asking the jury to accept a substitute copy of the recording. He is giving voice-identification testimony from what he heard. Gold Key: the original-recording rule is a contents rule, so it does not bar testimony offered to identify the caller's voice from the witness's own perception.

C. The certainty answer sells a clean-sounding foundation rule: if the witness cannot be positive, the testimony must stay out. Gold Key: voice identification does not require courtroom certainty before admission. Barnabas's uncertainty is exactly the kind of weakness the jury can weigh after hearing the testimony.

D. The waiver answer reaches the correct admissibility result through the wrong procedural story. A defendant's failure to subpoena a known recording is not the reason Barnabas may testify. The testimony comes in because the original-recording rule is not triggered by this offered purpose, and the voice-identification foundation is enough for admissibility.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer

The credited outcome is legally sound. FRE 901(a) requires evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims, and FRE 901(b)(5) specifically recognizes voice identification by opinion based on hearing the voice at any time under circumstances connecting it with the alleged speaker. FRE 602 also supports the personal-knowledge layer because a witness may testify to a matter from the witness's own perception. Barnabas heard the call and had heard Mary's voice before in circumstances connecting the distinctive cadence and accent to Mary. That is enough to let the testimony reach the jury; the lack of certainty affects weight and cross-examination, not admissibility.

The unproduced phone recording does not change the result. FRE 1002 requires an original writing, recording, or photograph only when a party is proving its contents. Here, Barnabas is not trying to prove the precise words or contents of the saved recording through secondary evidence. He is testifying to his own perception of the caller's voice. The original-recording rule therefore does not bar his testimony, and Mary's failure to subpoena the recording is not a waiver theory needed to admit it.
