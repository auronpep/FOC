## A) PASS-1 TRANSFORM REPORT

### 1. Final question - revised stem + call

Lydia completed a six-week private tutoring package for Naomi, teaching her how to hand-letter Scripture cards for Naomi's family Bible-study group. Naomi owed Lydia $2,400 for the lessons. The statute of limitations later barred Lydia from suing to recover the unpaid tutoring bill.

Naomi then sent Lydia a signed note stating, "I promise to pay you $800 if you will mark the old tutoring debt satisfied." Lydia agreed.

Is Naomi's promise to pay Lydia $800 enforceable?

### 2. Distilled Core Question

A person owes an old contract debt that can no longer be sued on because limitations ran, then signs a new promise to pay part if the creditor marks it satisfied. Is the new promise enforceable?

### 3. Final answer choices

A. Yes, because Naomi's promise gave Lydia a practical benefit.

B. Yes, because a promise to pay part of an antecedent debt that would be enforceable except for the statute of limitations is binding without new consideration.

C. No, because Naomi did not separately promise that she would waive the statute of limitations defense.

D. No, because Lydia gave no new consideration for Naomi's new promise.

### 5. Correct answer

B. Original key: D.

### 7. Full right-answer explanation

B is enforceable. Naomi made a fresh written promise to pay part of an old contractual debt. The old tutoring bill could not be recovered in court only because the statute of limitations had run. In that narrow old-debt setting, the law treats the new promise as binding without requiring Lydia to give new consideration.

**Gold Key:** A new promise to pay all or part of a statute-barred antecedent debt is one of the narrow contracts rules where the promise can be binding without new consideration.

**Silver Key:** When one answer gives the broad no-consideration rule and another gives a narrow stale-debt exception, decide the exception first. The stem is built around an old debt, limitations, and a fresh promise to pay part.

### 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells a practical-benefit shortcut: Lydia gets money she otherwise could not collect in court, so the result feels fair. The breaker is that benefit alone is not the legal reason. **Gold Key:** the enforceability comes from the special rule for a new promise to pay a statute-barred antecedent debt, not from a free-floating benefit theory.

**C.** This choice sells a technical waiver trap. The breaker is that Naomi's signed promise to pay part of the old debt is enough; she did not also have to use separate statute-of-limitations waiver language. The statute-defense statement is one possible way to show the promise, not the only way.

**D.** This choice sells the dominant trap: the general rule that a promise needs consideration. The breaker is the old-debt exception. **Silver Key:** the answer array gives you a broad rule and a narrower exception; the narrower statute-barred-debt answer controls this stem.

### 9. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The verified outline placement is `55020204`, which appears in the project outline as Contracts -> Formation of Contracts -> Formation -> Consideration -> Preexisting duty.

Lawyer confirmation: Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 82(1) provides that a promise to pay all or part of an antecedent contractual or quasi-contractual indebtedness is binding if the indebtedness is still enforceable or would be enforceable except for the statute of limitations. That rule directly fits Naomi's signed promise to pay $800 of Lydia's old tutoring debt. A Hawaii appellate decision, First Hawaiian Bank v. Zukerkorn, 2 Haw. App. 383, 633 P.2d 550 (1981), states the same stale-debt revival principle while holding that disputed facts about whether a new promise was made prevented summary judgment. Here, unlike Zukerkorn, the new promise is express and undisputed in the stem. Sources checked: [Restatement Second of Contracts § 82 outline](https://matthewminer.name/law/outlines/1L/1st%2BSemester/LAW%2B505-002%2B%E2%80%93%2BContracts%2BI/R2C%2B%C2%A7%2B82) and [First Hawaiian Bank v. Zukerkorn](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/5914918badd7b0493458a2ea).

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged: a debtor's fresh promise to pay part of an antecedent debt barred by limitations is enforceable without new consideration. The research does not contradict the supplied official key.
