**D is wrong.** This is the dominant trap. It sounds powerful because it proves that the stimulant can cause uncontrollable rage. ### 8. Black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

The supplied official explanation states the tested rule: common-law battery is a general-intent crime; voluntary intoxication is not a defense to a general-intent crime; the best fact for the instruction is that the defendant was tricked into taking an intoxicating substance he believed was aspirin.  That rule is consistent with mainstream criminal-law treatment: voluntary intoxication is generally not a defense to general-intent crimes, while involuntary intoxication may operate as a defense if it creates the required incapacity. ([Justia][1]) The Model Penal Code’s intoxication provision likewise treats non-self-induced intoxication as a potential affirmative defense when it produces substantial incapacity, and defines self-induced intoxication around knowingly introducing a substance with a known intoxicating tendency. ([Open Casebook][2]) The Supreme Court’s *Montana v. Egelhoff* materials confirm the historical baseline that voluntary intoxication was not treated as an excuse or justification at common law.
