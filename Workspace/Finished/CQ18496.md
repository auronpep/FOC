## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**B is correct.** The merger question is not whether Timothy caused a death. It is whether the predicate felony is merely the assaultive act that became the homicide, or whether it has an independent felonious purpose. **Gold Key:** felony-murder merger bars assaultive predicates that are the homicide itself; an independent felony such as kidnapping does not merge when the death occurs during the continuing felony. **Silver Key:** on a merger call, name the axis as independent purpose versus homicide-integral assault. Here, Timothy kidnapped the captain to question him later, and the death occurred while that restraint was still continuing.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This choice sells the familiar mental-state objection: Timothy did not mean for the captain to die. **Gold Key:** felony murder does not require an intent to kill. The predicate felony supplies the murder mental-state substitute when death occurs during the felony transaction.

**C.** This choice reaches the same no-merger result but gives a false reason. The breaker is the because-clause: heat exposure is not arson, and a correct-looking conclusion cannot survive an invented predicate.

**D.** This is the dominant trap. It remembers that merger exists, then inflates it into a universal rule. **Gold Key:** merger does not say every felony that causes death merges; it blocks assaultive predicates that are integral to the homicide, while independent felonies such as kidnapping remain available predicates.
