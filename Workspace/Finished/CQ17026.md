### 6. Full right-answer explanation

C is the credited answer. **Gold Key:** In a goods-dominant mixed transaction, incidental services do not pull the whole deal out of UCC treatment. **Gold Key:** Under the UCC meaning-source ladder, the court uses agreement sources before default gap-fillers; prior course of dealing between the same parties can explain or supplement an undefined term if reasonably consistent.

**Silver Key:** Read the array as a source-order ladder. First ask whether UCC/source law is available; then compare private course of dealing against general trade usage; only after that consider default gap-fillers.

Here, the kits are the main economic object of the contract. The painting and short assembly lesson are attached services, but the price allocation points to goods as the main transaction. The disputed phrase is undefined. The prior contracts are between the same parties and used the same phrase. That makes the prior-contract evidence a course-of-dealing source that may explain the term.

### 7. Full wrong-answer explanations

**A.** This answer sells the “gap means gap-filler” reflex. The breaker is source order: the missing definition does not send the court straight to defaults when party-specific meaning evidence is available.

**B.** This answer sells the service-frame trap. The painting and lesson are real services, but the contract’s center of gravity is still the puppet-stage kits; incidental services do not make the entire transaction a services contract.

**D.** This answer sells the broad-industry-confidence trap. General trade usage matters, but it does not automatically outrank the parties’ own prior course of dealing; the Gold Key hierarchy would have steered away from this choice.

### 8. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning

UCC Article 2 applies to “transactions in goods.” ([Legal Information Institute][1]) For mixed goods-and-services contracts, the dominant bar-exam test is the predominant-purpose test: the question is whether the transaction’s main thrust is services with goods incidental, or a sale with labor/services incidental; *Bonebrake v. Cox* states that mixed status alone does not exclude Article 2. ([OpenJurist][2])

UCC 2-202 allows a final writing to be explained or supplemented by course of dealing, usage of trade, or course of performance, so long as the evidence does not contradict the writing. ([Legal Information Institute][3]) UCC 1-303 defines course of dealing as prior transactions between the parties and states the hierarchy: express terms first; course of performance; course of dealing; then usage of trade. ([Legal Information Institute][4])

Student move: do not jump to trade usage or gap-fillers.

Tiny rule: same-party course of dealing can explain the term; defaults are later.

Trap: source-order reversal.

Confidence: ANCHOR_ASSISTED.

## Gold Key(s)

**GK-CONTRACTS-MANGER-BUNDLES-01:** A mixed goods/services contract follows the goods side when the sale-of-goods purpose predominates; small setup, painting, configuration, or training services do not automatically make the whole deal a services contract.

**GK-CONTRACTS-MANGER-BUNDLES-02:** For UCC meaning evidence, use agreement sources before defaults: express terms, course of performance, course of dealing, usage of trade, then gap-fillers. Course of dealing beats general usage of trade if they cannot reasonably be read together.

## Silver Key(s)

**SK-CONTRACTS-MANGER-BUNDLES-01:** Read this array as a source-order ladder: source law first, private party history second, general trade usage third, defaults last.
