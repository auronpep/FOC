## 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Esther's email did not ask Timothy to promise that he would make the cards. It asked for completed performance. Timothy began that invited performance while Esther was alive and with Esther's knowledge. That beginning created temporary protection for the offer, and Esther's later death did not wipe out the protected chance to finish. Timothy completed the cards by the deadline, so he accepted by completion and may recover from the estate.

**Gold Key:** When a unilateral offer invites acceptance only by performance, beginning the invited performance creates an option contract. The offeror's duty remains conditional on timely completion, but death after protected performance begins does not automatically terminate the offeree's power to finish and accept.

**Silver Key:** Freeze the timeline before choosing: offer -> work begins -> death -> timely completion. The death answer loses because death came after the performance protection attached.

## 8. Full wrong-answer explanations

### A

A is attractive because death is a real offer-termination event, and the stem makes Esther's death feel decisive. The breaker is the sequence. Timothy had already begun the invited performance before Esther died. **Gold Key:** beginning the invited performance created an option contract, so death after that point did not automatically end Timothy's protected power to complete.

### B

B is attractive because Timothy did spend time and supplies after Esther died, so reliance sounds like the fairest measure. The breaker is the invented exact-match rule. Recovery turns on completing the requested performance under the protected unilateral offer, not on whether expenses equal the offered price.

### D

D is attractive because Esther died before receiving the finished cards, and an estate may feel like it should choose whether to be generous. The breaker is the exchange structure. Esther offered money for a completed card set; that is not a mere gift promise waiting on estate grace.

## 9. Full black-letter-law verification and legal reasoning for the right answer

The tested rule, legal issue, and credited outcome are unchanged from the source row: when an offer invites acceptance by performance and does not invite a promissory acceptance, the offeree's beginning or tender of the invited performance creates an option contract. The offeror's duty remains conditional on completion of the invited performance. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 45 supplies that rule. Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 37 then confirms that the power of acceptance under an option contract is not terminated by the offeror's death or incapacity unless a contractual duty is discharged. Ordinary death-as-termination rules apply to unprotected offers, not to this protected unilateral-offer sequence.

Here, Esther invited Timothy to accept by completing the Scripture-memory cards, not by promising to complete them. Timothy began the requested work before Esther died and completed it by the deadline. The estate therefore cannot avoid payment merely because Esther died after the option protection attached. The official key is consistent with Restatement (Second) of Contracts §§ 37 and 45.
