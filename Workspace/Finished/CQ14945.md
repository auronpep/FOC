### 7. Full right-answer explanation (exam-register)
D is correct. Cedar Bank bought the note and mortgage first and promptly recorded that assignment. That recording is the priority fact. Lydia's later delivery of the original papers, payment of value, and lack of actual knowledge do not beat a prior recorded assignment. The note also signals that it is subject to a same-day contract, so Lydia cannot use a holder-in-due-course shortcut to ignore Cedar Bank's recorded claim. Because the debt and the mortgage travel together, Cedar Bank owns both the note and the mortgage. **Gold Key:** when the first note-and-mortgage assignment is recorded, the later buyer from the same mortgagee is charged with the record and cannot win merely by holding the papers. **Gold Key:** do not split the note from the mortgage; the mortgage is security for the debt and ordinarily follows it. **Silver Key:** read the answers as a 2x2 grid, cut the split-ownership answers first, then choose between the two all-in owners by asking who had the recorded assignment first.

A is attractive because it gives Lydia the paper she physically received while leaving Cedar Bank the real-property security that Cedar Bank recorded first. ### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue is Real Property, Mortgages and Security Devices, Transfers of Interest > Transfers by mortgagee. The verified outline code is `83050402`, which appears verbatim in `OUTLINE_CODES_COMPLETE.md`.

The governing exam rule has three parts. First, recording-act principles protect the assignee who first records an assignment of a mortgage interest against later purchasers charged with the record. A representative primary-law formulation appears in S.C. Code Sections 30-7-10, 30-7-40, and 30-7-60: written instruments affecting real-property interests and assignments of mortgage interests become effective against later purchasers by recording, and later purchasers lose protection when the competing assignment was already recorded. Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t30c007.php

Second, the note here is not a clean negotiable-instrument shortcut for Lydia. UCC Section 3-106(a) provides that a promise is conditional if it states that it is subject to or governed by another record, while Section 3-106(b) preserves negotiability only for a reference to collateral, prepayment, acceleration, or a fund/source of payment. Daniel's note does both things: the mortgage-reference language alone would not defeat negotiability, but the separate "subject to the terms of a contract" language does. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/3/3-106. A person who is not a holder in due course takes subject to a property or possessory claim to the instrument under UCC Section 3-306. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/3/3-306

Third, the mortgage follows the debt. Carpenter v. Longan, 83 U.S. 271 (1872), states the classic rule that the note and mortgage are treated as inseparable, with the mortgage as incident to the debt. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/83/271. Modern sources state the same point: Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages Section 5.4(a) provides that transfer of an obligation secured by a mortgage also transfers the mortgage unless the parties agree otherwise, and UCC Section 9-203(g) attaches the security interest in the mortgage when the secured right to payment is transferred. Sources: https://www.courts.maine.gov/courts/sjc/lawcourt/2020/20me033.pdf and https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-203

Those authorities confirm the supplied official key. Cedar Bank took a written assignment first and recorded it. Martha Mortgage Company retained the note and mortgage only as collecting agent, not as owner free to resell them. Lydia later paid value and received the papers, but she bought from a mortgagee whose recorded assignment had already divested the note-and-mortgage package. The tested rule, legal issue, credited outcome, dominant trap, and 2x2 answer-array geometry are unchanged from the source row.
