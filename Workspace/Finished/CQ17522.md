### 8. Full Black-Letter-Law Verification and Legal Reasoning

The tested rule and credited outcome are unchanged: a homicide victim's statement about the cause or circumstances of death, made while believing death is imminent, is admissible as a dying declaration, and the Confrontation Clause objection does not defeat the statement on this exam-bank rule.

FRE 804(b)(2) covers a "Statement Under the Belief of Imminent Death" in a homicide prosecution or civil case when the declarant believed death was imminent and spoke about its cause or circumstances. Official 2024 rule PDF: https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/federal-rules-of-evidence-dec-1-2024_0.pdf.

Crawford v. Washington, 541 U.S. 36 (2004), bars testimonial hearsay against a criminal defendant unless the declarant is unavailable and the defendant had a prior opportunity to cross-examine, but it expressly flagged testimonial dying declarations as a possible historical exception. U.S. Reports PDF: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep541/usrep541036/usrep541036.pdf.

Giles v. California, 554 U.S. 353 (2008), described dying declarations by a speaker aware of impending death as a historic exception distinct from forfeiture by wrongdoing. U.S. Reports PDF: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep554/usrep554353/usrep554353.pdf.

Michigan v. Bryant, 562 U.S. 344 (2011), supplies the nearby primary-purpose doctrine for non-testimonial emergency statements, but that doctrine is not the credited basis here because the call frames the issue as a Crawford objection to a testimonial identification. Justia opinion: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/562/344/.
