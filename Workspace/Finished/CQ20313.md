### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**C is correct.** Lydia was not asking Paul to help her carry out a fraud. She was asking whether the planned description would be lawful and stated that she would not use it if it was fraudulent. That makes the communication compliance-seeking legal advice.

**Gold Key:** Crime-fraud is purpose-based, not topic-based. A suspicious topic does not defeat privilege unless the client is using legal advice to commit, continue, or cover up a crime or fraud.

**D.** This choice treats Paul’s advice against the advertisement as if it destroys privilege. The breaker is direction: advice against the act reinforces the compliance-advice frame rather than the fraud-furthering frame.

### 9. Black-letter verification and right-answer legal reasoning

Under Federal Rule of Evidence 501, privilege questions in federal evidence law are governed by common law principles unless a constitution, federal statute, Supreme Court rule, or state-law rule of decision supplies otherwise. ([Legal Information Institute][1]) The attorney-client privilege exists to encourage candid legal consultation, and the crime-fraud exception does not apply merely because a client discusses illegal or suspicious conduct; it applies when the communication is made for the purpose of obtaining advice to commit or further crime or fraud. ([Justia Law][2])

The credited answer remains the same legal substance as the source row: protected attorney-client communication, crime-fraud exception not triggered. ([Justia Law][2])
