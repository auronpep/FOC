### 9. Full black-letter-law verification AND legal reasoning for the right answer
The tested issue remains Criminal Law and Procedure, Other Crimes, Burglary, and the verified outline code is `73031000`. Common-law burglary requires breaking and entering the dwelling house of another at night with intent to commit a felony therein; modern burglary statutes commonly expand the place or time elements but still require intent to commit a crime, felony, or theft inside. Model Penal Code section 221.1(1) states the same target-offense idea in modern terms: entry into a building or occupied structure with purpose to commit a crime therein. Cornell Wex summarizes the common-law and MPC formulations at https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/burglary and https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/breaking_and_entering; the FBI UCR definition likewise classifies burglary as unlawful entry of a structure to commit a felony or theft, https://ucr.fbi.gov/crime-in-the-u.s/2018/crime-in-the-u.s.-2018/topic-pages/burglary. The attempt distractor tracks legal-impossibility doctrine: when the completed intended act would not be criminal, the traditional common-law rule treats attempt liability as unavailable; see the discussion of legal impossibility and People v. Jaffe, 185 N.Y. 497, 78 N.E. 169 (1906), collected at https://okcca.net/ouji-cr/2-15/. Nothing in the research contradicts the supplied key. Daniel intended a noncriminal display scramble, so the burglary charge fails even though he believed the conduct was a felony.
