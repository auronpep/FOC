### 7. Full right-answer explanation

**A** is the only answer that keeps the two crime labels separate.

**Gold Key:** Common-law forgery has a legal-efficacy gate. A fake writing is not enough. The writing must be the kind that could matter legally if genuine. **Gold Key:** False pretenses can rest on a material lie about source or provenance. Lydia did more than stay silent. She said the leaf came from a private Cyprus collector who wanted anonymity. That was a false statement about an existing or past fact, and Barnabas paid money because of the story.

**Silver Key:** Read the answer array as a two-axis grid: forgery yes/no, then false pretenses yes/no. The correct box is **no forgery / yes false pretenses**.
